# Lesson plan — Oxides, acids, bases and salts

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Oxides, acids, bases and salts
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — the first compound family and its children (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** oxides — an element joined with oxygen — come in four characters (basic, acidic, amphoteric, neutral); basic oxides with water make bases, acidic oxides with water make acids, and acid + base gives salt + water: neutralisation (አግልሎት)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Elements, compounds and mixtures — compounds are known; now the first family of them is followed into daily life.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Elements, compounds and mixtures. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an oxide is and name its four characters with an example formula each.
2. Say how acids and bases are made from oxides and water, and how indicator paper tells them apart.
3. State neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water — and name acid/base pairs from daily life, with the safety rule: wash a spill of strong acid or base at once with plenty of clean water; never taste or touch them.

## What you need
Indicator paper if the class has it; a lemon, vinegar, ash and soap for the home-shelf sort. Strong acids and bases are NEVER handled — the safety rule is part of the lesson.

## If the learner is stuck
- Tastes to test → the book's warning stands: tasting bases is dangerous, and strong acids and bases are never tasted or touched. Indicator paper does the telling.
- Mixes the indicator colours → acid turns BLUE paper RED (think of a sour lemon's red bite); base turns RED paper BLUE.
