# Lesson plan — Chemical change: burning and gas-making

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Chemical change: burning and gas-making
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — the chemical family at work: two of the book's numbered experiments (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a chemical change makes a new body — proved by the book's Experiment 2 (paper burned to ash) and Experiment 3 (lemon and yeast filling a balloon with gas)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Properties that identify a thing — chemical properties were defined there; here they are proved by experiment, with an adult, the book's safe way.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Experiment 2 — paper, then ash** — describe, predict, burn with tongs (adult), compare: the ash is a new body
2. **Experiment 3 — the balloon that fills itself** — lemon juice and yeast make gas; the balloon over the bottle's neck inflates — gas forming is a chemical change

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Properties that identify a thing. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a chemical property is: a property seen when a body changes into a new body — burning, rusting, gas forming.
2. Tell Experiment 2: paper described, then burned with tongs, and the ash compared with the prediction.
3. Tell Experiment 3: lemon juice and yeast make a gas that inflates a balloon over the bottle's neck.

## What you need
For watching and telling, nothing. For doing: an adult, tongs, a small piece of paper, a plastic bottle, lemon juice, yeast and a balloon — and the book's care: burning is an adult's work; children watch from a safe place.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls the ash 'burned paper, still paper' → test it against the description: can it fold, carry writing, tear like paper? The new body fails every property of the old.
- Thinks someone blew the balloon up → the neck is sealed over the bottle; the gas was made inside, by the lemon and yeast together.
