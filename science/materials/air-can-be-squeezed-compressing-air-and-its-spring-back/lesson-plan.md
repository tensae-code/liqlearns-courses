# Lesson plan — Air can be squeezed — compressing air and its spring back

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Air can be squeezed — compressing air and its spring back
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials (CURRICULUM.md Unit 4), following Air is there — air takes up space and can be pushed in the same Gabon air thread, four paliers of schooling later: air first proved to exist, now proved elastic
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** air, alone among the everyday states near us, can be squeezed into a much smaller space: seal air in a syringe and push — the air compresses, resisting more and more, and when you let go it springs back to its first volume, because air is elastic; water and solids refuse this squeezing almost completely — which is why pumps, cushions, tyres and spray bottles all work on air's spring

## Sub lessons
1. **Squeezing air — it gives, and resists** — air compresses: sealed in a syringe, it can be pushed into half its space or less, because air's particles are far apart and can be crowded closer; the more you squeeze, the harder the air pushes back against the plunger — the resistance you feel growing is the compressed air pressing on every side
2. **Air springs back — it is elastic; water is not** — release the plunger and the compressed air expands, driving the plunger back to its starting place: air is elastic, always returning to its former volume when the squeeze ends; try the same with the syringe full of water — the plunger will not move at all, because water's particles are already close and cannot be crowded; this spring of air is put to work in pumps, air cushions, tyres, sprayers and the brakes of great vehicles

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Air is there — air takes up space and can be pushed**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Air compresses: sealed air can be pushed into a much smaller space — its particles are far apart, and squeezing closes the gaps.
2. Say, in their own words: The more air is compressed, the harder it pushes back — crowded air's pressure grows as its volume shrinks.
3. Say, in their own words: Air is elastic: released, it expands and springs back to its first volume, driving the plunger back.
4. Say, in their own words: Water and solids do not compress (for any hand purpose): their particles are already close-packed.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A plastic syringe with its tip sealable by a finger (no needle — the bare syringe only), a second syringe or bottle of water for the comparison, and a bicycle pump if available.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the plunger moved in, then travelled back alone. Explain those two travels and you have the lesson.
- Thinks the air vanished when squeezed → it only crowded closer. The spring-back proves every bit of it was still in there.
- Thinks water failed because the syringe was weak → the same syringe, same hand, same push: the water refused. Particles already close have no gaps to give.
