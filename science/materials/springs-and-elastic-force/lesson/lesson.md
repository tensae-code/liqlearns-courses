# Springs and elastic force

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — elasticity: the spring that stretches under a load and pulls back — met in a ballpoint pen, then measured by hanging weights. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — The spring in a ballpoint pen

China's Grade 3 book opens the topic by taking a **ballpoint pen** apart: inside sits a small **spring**, the part that makes the refill spring in and out at a click. The spring is stretched and pressed by hand — pulled longer, squeezed shorter — and each time it is let go, it returns to its own length.

That return is the spring's nature: a spring pushed or pulled out of shape **pulls back** toward its first shape. The force of that pulling back is its **elastic force**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hanging weights — the spring lengthens

Hung up with weights hung on it, the spring shows its rule in numbers the eye can read: **hang more weight, and the spring lengthens more**; take weight off, and it shortens back. Stretch follows load, step for step.

This measured stretch is elasticity at work — the same property that runs the pen's click, and that the spring force meter (the next lesson in the book's order) is built on: a spring whose stretch reads the force hung on it.

## The key ideas

- A **spring** pulled or pressed out of shape **pulls back** to its first shape — that pull-back is its **elastic force**.
- **Elasticity** is the property behind it: stretch under a load, recover when the load is removed.
- Hang more weight and the spring **lengthens more**, step for step — the measured stretch the spring force meter is built on.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take apart a click ballpoint pen with an adult's permission and find its spring. Draw the pen and label the spring's job.
2. Stretch a safe spring or elastic band gently with different small loads and record how its length changes with each load.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — why a spring always pulls back.
