# Lesson plan — Oxides — basic, acidic and in-between

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Oxides — basic, acidic and in-between
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Classification of Compounds (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** oxides — compounds of oxygen with one other element — split into basic oxides (metal oxides that form bases), acidic oxides (non-metal oxides that form acids), amphoteric oxides that act both ways, and neutral oxides that do neither
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the primary lesson Oxides, acids, bases and salts, which meets the four characters at first depth; this Grade 8 lesson proves each character with the book's two burning experiments. It stands alone for a direct learner who knows what a compound is.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Classify an oxide as basic, acidic, amphoteric or neutral, with an example of each.
2. Write the equations for magnesium and sulphur burning and say what each product does in water.
3. Write one oxide-plus-water equation each for a base formed and an acid formed.

## What you need
No home burning: magnesium and sulphur are bench experiments to read and watch, not to repeat at home.

## If the learner is stuck
Sorts oxides by the element's look → use the rule: metal oxide → basic, non-metal oxide → acidic; then place Al₂O₃/ZnO (both ways) and CO/H₂O (neither) as the two extra classes.
