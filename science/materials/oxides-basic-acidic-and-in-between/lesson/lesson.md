# Oxides — basic, acidic and in-between

## Do it first

Magnesium ribbon burns with a brilliant white flame. Sulphur burns and its gas is caught. Before reading: guess what each ash or gas does in water — makes it basic, or makes it acidic? Keep your guesses.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four characters of oxides

An **oxide** is oxygen joined with **one other element**. **Basic oxides** are **metal oxides**: with water they form **bases** — CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂. **Acidic oxides** are **non-metal oxides**: with water they form **acids** — CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃, SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃. **Amphoteric oxides** (Al₂O₃, ZnO) react with **both** acids and bases. **Neutral oxides** (CO, H₂O) react with **neither**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two experiments that prove it

**Experiment 3.2:** magnesium ribbon burns in air with a brilliant white flame, leaving white ash — **2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO**; the ash in water turns indicator toward base: MgO is a **basic oxide**. **Experiment 3.1:** sulphur burns, S + O₂ → SO₂; the gas **sulphur dioxide** dissolves in water and the solution tests **acidic**: SO₂ is an **acidic oxide**. Metal burns to a base-maker; non-metal burns to an acid-maker — the pattern of Sub lesson 1, proved on the bench.
