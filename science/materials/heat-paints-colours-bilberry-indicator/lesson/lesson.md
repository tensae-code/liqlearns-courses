# Heat paints colours — bilberry indicator

## Do it first
Before you read: the same bilberry juice is dropped into a cup of warm water and a cup of cold water. Do you think the juice will show the same colour in both cups, or a different colour? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Finland's way in — its Luova LUMA activity bank teaches it with a local berry: heat paints colours, as bilberry juice changes colour in warm and cold water.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bilberry juice in warm water

Make **bilberry juice** — Finland's local berry, crushed so its dark juice runs.

An adult prepares a cup of **warm water**. Put bilberry juice into the warm water and watch: the juice **changes colour** as it meets the warmth. Look closely and record the colour the juice shows in the warm cup — that record is half the experiment.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bilberry juice in cold water

Now put the **same bilberry juice** — from the same batch — into a cup of **cold water**.

Watch again. The colour the juice shows in the cold cup is **not the same** as in the warm cup. Same juice, same amount, two waters: the only difference between the cups is **warm against cold**.

So **heat paints colours**: bilberry juice changes colour in warm and in cold water, and the colour difference between the two cups is heat's painting, read with the eyes.

## The key ideas

- **Bilberry juice changes colour** in warm water and in cold water.
- The fair test is the **same juice** in two cups — the only difference is **warm against cold**.
- **Heat paints the colour**: the difference between the cups is heat's work, read by eye.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the warm cup first, then the cold cup and the comparison. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What was the same in both cups, and what was different?" Answer with the juice and the heat, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. With an adult handling the warm water, set up the two cups: same bilberry juice in warm water and in cold water.
2. Draw both cups and colour each drawing to match what you saw. Write warm and cold under the right drawing.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the same juice, the two waters, and how heat painted the difference.
