# Heat paints colours — bilberry indicator — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is bilberry juice, and why is it Finland's own way into this lesson?
**Answer:** It is the juice of the bilberry, a local berry of Finland; the activity uses that local berry as the colour that answers to warm and cold water.

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Describe the warm-cup step.
**Answer:** Bilberry juice is put into a cup of warm water (an adult handles the warm water), and the colour the juice shows there is watched closely and recorded.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What is compared in this lesson, and what does the comparison prove?
**Answer:** The same bilberry juice in warm water against the same juice in cold water; the colours differ, and since only the water's warmth differed, heat is what paints the colour difference.

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the juice in both cups come from the same batch?
**Answer:** So the test is fair: if the juices differed, a colour difference could come from the juice instead of the heat; with the same juice, warm against cold is the only difference left.
