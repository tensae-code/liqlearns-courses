## Sub lesson 1 — The stretched skin on a liquid's surface

Yemen's Grade 9 Science book teaches the surface of a liquid as a stretched skin: a needle and a razor blade — both denser than water — can be floated on water, held up by that skin, though left to themselves they would sink.

## Sub lesson 2 — Walking on the film — and breaking it

Pond insects walk on the film without breaking through it, living on the skin's strength. Soap films stretched on a wire ring show the same skin in another form: a film that pulls itself tight across the ring.

## Sub lesson 3 — The pull, measured

The skin's pull is a real force and can be measured: surface tension is counted in newtons per metre — the pull along each metre of the surface's edge. A property you can float a needle on is a property you can put a number on.
