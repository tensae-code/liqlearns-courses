# Acids and bases — definitions and naming

## Do it first

Three bottles, labels hidden: hydrochloric, sulphuric, sulphurous. Their anions are chloride, sulphate, sulphite. Can you rebuild each name from its anion alone? Try before you read the machine.

## Sub lesson 1 — What acids and bases are

An **acid** is a substance that releases **hydrogen ions, H⁺**, when dissolved in water — in water the ion rides on a water molecule as **H₃O⁺**. A **base** releases **hydroxide ions, OH⁻**. A base that **dissolves** in water is called an **alkali** — so every alkali is a base, but not every base is an alkali. Hydrochloric acid, HCl, and sodium hydroxide, NaOH, are the bench pair used through this unit.

## Sub lesson 2 — The naming machine — and the oxoacid ladder (teacher guide — evidence)

Read the acid's **anion** and turn its ending: **-ide → hydro-…-ic** (chloride → **hydrochloric** acid); **-ate → -ic** (sulphate → **sulphuric** acid); **-ite → -ous** (sulphite → **sulphurous** acid). One formula, two names by state: HCl is **hydrogen chloride** as a gas or pure liquid, **hydrochloric acid** once dissolved. The teacher guide adds the **oxoacid ladder**: oxoacids (hydrogen + oxygen + a central element) are named from reference acids (carbonic, phosphoric, chloric, sulfuric, nitric); one oxygen **more** than the -ic acid gives **per-…-ic**, one **less** gives **-ous**, two less gives **hypo-…-ous** — the chlorine family runs perchloric, chloric, chlorous, hypochlorous. Anions follow: -ic acid → **-ate**, -ous acid → **-ite**. Correctly paired, the phosphoric family runs: H₃PO₄ phosphate PO₄³⁻; H₂PO₄⁻ **dihydrogen phosphate**; HPO₄²⁻ **hydrogen phosphate**.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: acids release H⁺ in water (carried as H₃O⁺) and bases release OH⁻ — soluble bases are alkalis — and an acid's name is built from its anion: -ide → hydro-…-ic, -ate → -ic, -ite → -ous, with the guide's oxoacid ladder (per- / hypo-) completing the machine. Your companion asks one question; answer it from the lesson, not from memory of anything else.
