| Human-made (artificial) materials (مواد اصطناعية): materials invented to meet our renewed needs do not occur in nature but are made from mixtures of things found in nature; plastics (اللدائن) are made mostly from oil chemicals and come in kinds matched to uses — light and heat-resistant for food packaging and dishes, hard for furniture covers, flexible for plastic bags and food film, transparent sometimes used instead of glass; ceramics (الخزف) are an artificial material people make from clay and other materials, hardened by heating in a kiln until hard and strong — pottery, bricks and tiles (sidebar: ceramic tiles cover the space shuttle, protecting it from the great heat when it re-enters the atmosphere); metals are important natural materials in the Earth (iron, aluminium, silver, tin, copper, zinc, gold) but pure metals are usually too soft to use, so alloys (السبائك) were invented — a mixture of two or more metals, sometimes with non-metals such as carbon and silicon, harder and stronger than the pure metals: steel (iron + carbon — car bodies, ships, cooking tools), bronze (copper + tin — statues, medals, bells), brass (copper + zinc — ornaments, musical instruments, locks, electric wires); sidebar: gold purity is measured in carats, pure gold being 24 carats (Libya, G6 SB T2) — placement filed as NEW LESSON NEEDED — artificial-materials-plastics-ceramics-and-alloys | Libya, *Science, Grade 6, Student Book, Term 2*, printed pp. 72–74 |
