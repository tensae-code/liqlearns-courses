## Sub lesson 1 — Human-made materials, and plastics

Some materials are **natural**: they are found in nature — wood, stone, the metals in the Earth. But our needs change, and natural materials no longer suit every job. So people **invent** new materials. These **human-made (artificial) materials** do not occur in nature; they are made from mixtures of things found in nature.

**Plastics** are the book's first family: they are made mostly from **oil chemicals**, and they come in kinds matched to their uses — light and heat-resistant kinds for food packaging and dishes, hard kinds for furniture covers, flexible kinds for plastic bags and food film, and transparent kinds sometimes used instead of glass. One invented family, many jobs — because each kind is made with its job in mind.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ceramics and alloys

**Ceramics** are a second invented family: people make them from **clay and other materials**, hardened by heating in a **kiln** until they are hard and strong — pottery, bricks and tiles. Ceramic tiles even cover the **space shuttle**, protecting it from the great heat when it re-enters the atmosphere.

Metals themselves are natural materials — iron, aluminium, silver, tin, copper, zinc, gold — but pure metals are usually **too soft** to use. So people invented **alloys**: a mixture of two or more metals, sometimes with non-metals such as carbon and silicon, **harder and stronger** than the pure metals. **Steel** is iron + carbon (car bodies, ships, cooking tools); **bronze** is copper + tin (statues, medals, bells); **brass** is copper + zinc (ornaments, musical instruments, locks, electric wires). And gold's purity is measured in **carats** — pure gold is 24 carats.

## The key ideas

- **Artificial materials** are invented by people, made from mixtures of natural things, to meet needs natural materials no longer meet.
- **Plastics** are made mostly from oil chemicals, in kinds matched to their uses.
- **Ceramics** are clay and other materials hardened by kiln heat — pottery, bricks, tiles, even space-shuttle tiles.
- **Alloys** are mixtures of metals (sometimes with non-metals), harder and stronger than pure metals: steel, bronze, brass.
