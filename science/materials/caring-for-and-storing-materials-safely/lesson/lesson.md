# Caring for and storing materials safely

## Do it first
Before you read: think of a tool or a school material that was spoiled because it was left out, put away wet, or never cleaned. What went wrong — the thing itself, or how it was kept? Say your answer. Then read on: this is Rwanda's way in — its SET books return to keeping and maintaining materials properly in year after year.

## Sub lesson 1 — Keeping materials properly — at home and at school

Rwanda's Primary 2 book teaches **keeping and maintaining home materials properly**, and then the same care for **school materials**: materials that are kept properly stay ready for their work and last; materials left carelessly are lost, broken or spoiled.

## Sub lesson 2 — Maintaining and storing agricultural tools

In Primary 4 the same care reaches the farm: **maintenance of agricultural tools** and **storage of agricultural tools** are taught as their own topics — a tool is maintained so it keeps working, and stored in its proper place so it is safe and can be found.

## Sub lesson 3 — Maintaining the objects we make and learn with

The care continues for the things learners make themselves: **maintenance of utility and learning objects** is taught in Primary 4, Primary 5 and Primary 6 alike — what you have made is kept working and kept whole by maintaining it, not by making it again.

## The key ideas

- **Keeping materials properly — at home and at school** — keeping home and school materials properly.
- **Maintaining and storing agricultural tools** — maintaining and storing farm tools.
- **Maintaining the objects we make and learn with** — maintaining made objects.
