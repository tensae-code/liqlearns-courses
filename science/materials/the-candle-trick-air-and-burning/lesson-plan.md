# Lesson plan — The candle trick — air and burning

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** The candle trick — air and burning
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — appended to Unit 4 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the candle trick — a flame put under a glass does not keep burning: burning needs air, and when the air trapped under the glass is used up the flame goes out — Finland's own way, in its Luova LUMA activity bank, is the trick itself: watch what happens to a flame under a glass
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **A flame under a glass** — A lit candle is covered with a glass and the class watches the flame — first burning, then changing as the trapped air is used.
2. **Burning needs air** — The flame goes out under the glass because burning needs air; with the trapped air used up, the burning cannot continue.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: A flame under a glass.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Burning needs air.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so — here, a candle and a glass, and the candle part is adult work only: an adult lights and handles the candle while learners watch from a safe place.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks the flame went out because the glass pushed it or frightened it → return to the trapped air: the glass traps a small store of air, the flame uses it, and burning stops when it is gone.
