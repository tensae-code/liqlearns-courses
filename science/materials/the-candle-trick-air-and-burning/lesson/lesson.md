# The candle trick — air and burning

## Do it first
Before you read: a candle is burning. An adult puts a glass over it, upside down, so no new air can get in. What do you think will happen to the flame — keep burning, grow, or go out? Say your answer out loud, and say why. Then read on: this is Finland's way in — its Luova LUMA activity bank teaches it as the candle trick: what happens to a flame under a glass.

## Sub lesson 1 — A flame under a glass

An adult lights a **candle** and, when the flame is steady, puts a **glass over it**.

Watch the flame. It does not stay as it was. Trapped under the glass, it changes — and then it **goes out**. Nothing blew on it. Nothing touched it. Only the air around it changed: the flame was left with the small store of air trapped under the glass, and no new air could get in.

## Sub lesson 2 — Burning needs air

A flame is **burning**, and burning needs **air**.

Under the glass the flame uses the trapped air. When that air is **used up**, the burning cannot continue — so the flame goes out. Lift the glass, give the candle new air, and an adult can light it again: with air, it burns; trapped without new air, it stops.

That is the whole trick, and it is not magic: **burning needs air**.

## The key ideas

- A **flame under a glass** goes out — nothing blew on it and nothing touched it.
- **Burning needs air**: the flame uses the air trapped under the glass.
- When the trapped air is **used up** and no new air can get in, the burning stops.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the flame under the glass first, then why it went out. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What did the flame run out of?" Answer with the air, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Predict first: before an adult does the candle trick, draw the flame as you expect it to look after the glass goes on, and write one sentence saying why.
2. Watch an adult do the trick. Draw the flame three times — burning freely, under the glass, and after — and label what changed.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the glass, the trapped air, and why the flame went out.
