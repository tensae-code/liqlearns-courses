# Lesson plan — Matter — the particle theory

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Matter — the particle theory
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 7, Unit 2 "Matter in Our Surroundings" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** matter occupies space and has mass, and everything is made of tiny particles that attract each other, have spaces between them, always move, and move faster when heated
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the primary Materials lessons (Describing things; Elements, compounds and mixtures, where matter and atoms were first named) — here the particle theory is stated in full, with its proofs

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What matter is — and what is not matter** — grind sugar finer and finer, dissolve it, and taste proves it is still there; matter occupies space and has mass; sound, light and perfume-smell are sorted out as non-matter
2. **The six particle postulates** — matter is tiny particles; particles attract each other; spaces lie between them; particles always move; temperature speeds them; each substance's particles are its own — with Democritus, who first had the idea (teacher guide — evidence)
3. **Two proofs — spaces and endless dilution** — sand-plus-water falls short of the sum of volumes, and Experiment 2.1 dilutes permanganate/ink colour beaker by beaker — the colour fades but never quite goes

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Elements, compounds and mixtures. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what matter is — it occupies space and has mass — and name the three states it exists in.
2. Sort sound, light and perfume-smell out as non-matter, and say why.
3. Recite the six particle postulates.
4. Tell both proofs: the sand-and-water shortfall, and Experiment 2.1's endless dilution.
5. Name Democritus as the first thinker of the particle idea (teacher-guide evidence).

## What you need
Sugar and a grinder or mortar; sand, water and a measuring cylinder; potassium permanganate or ink, beakers and water for Experiment 2.1.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks dissolved sugar is gone → taste is the witness: if it tastes sweet, it is there — as particles too small to see.
- Calls perfume-smell matter → the smell is carried BY matter (air particles), but the smell itself has no mass and takes no space.
