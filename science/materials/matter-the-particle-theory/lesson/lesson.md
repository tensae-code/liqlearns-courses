# Matter — the particle theory

## Do it first
Grind a spoon of sugar. Grind it finer. Finer still. Now stir the powder into water and watch it vanish. Taste the water. Sweet! The sugar has gone from sight — but it has not gone. Where is it? This lesson answers with the oldest big idea in science: everything is made of tiny particles.

## Sub lesson 1 — What matter is — and what is not matter
**Matter** is anything that **occupies space and has mass**. It exists in three states: **solid, liquid and gas**. Now sort carefully: a stone, water, air — matter. But **sound, light and the smell of perfume are NOT matter** — they take up no space of their own and have no mass, even though your senses find them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The six particle postulates
1. All matter is made of **tiny particles**.
2. The particles **attract each other**.
3. There are **spaces between** the particles.
4. The particles are **always moving**.
5. **Heat makes particles move faster** — temperature speeds them.
6. **Each substance's particles are its own** — water's particles are not iron's particles.

The idea is very old: the ancient Greek thinker **Democritus** first said that all things are tiny particles. Scientists have tested the idea ever since — and it holds.

## Sub lesson 3 — Two proofs — spaces and endless dilution
**Proof of spaces:** mix sand and water and measure. Together they take **less room than the two volumes added** — the water has slipped into the spaces between the sand's particles.

**Experiment 2.1 — the endless dilution. Aim:** to show that a coloured substance is made of particles too small to see. **Materials:** potassium permanganate (or ink), beakers, water. **Steps:** colour one beaker of water deep purple; pour a little into a second beaker of clear water and fill it up; repeat beaker by beaker. **Observation:** each beaker is paler than the one before — yet even after many dilutions the colour **never quite goes**. The colour is still there, spread as particles too tiny to see. If matter were not particles, the colour would vanish all at once; instead it thins without end.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the six postulates in your own words, then both proofs. Your companion asks: "Is light matter? Is air?" — answer with the test: space and mass.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the sugar test at home: grind, dissolve, taste. Write one sentence: what does the taste prove?
2. Predict, then test: will 50 mL of sand plus 50 mL of water measure 100 mL? Measure and explain the shortfall with particles and spaces.
3. Run your own dilution: one drop of ink, beaker by beaker. How many beakers until you cannot see the colour — and is it really gone?
