# Textiles — what clothes are made of

## Do it first
Pick up the shirt or dress you are wearing. What is it made of? Where did that material come from before it was cloth? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 1 *Estudo do Meio* book ends its clothing run with the textile materials themselves, and Grade 2 returns to sort clothing kinds by their fabrics.

## Sub lesson 1 — Textile materials — what cloth is made from

Angola's Grade 1 book names the **textile materials** (Os materiais têxteis): the materials cloth is made from, before any cutting or sewing. Every piece of clothing begins as a material — fibres worked into thread, thread woven or knitted into cloth, cloth cut and sewn into clothes. The material is the first thing: it decides whether the cloth is soft or rough, warm or cool, strong or quick to tear.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kinds of clothing and their fabrics

The Grade 2 book comes back to clothing and sorts the **different kinds of clothing by their fabrics**: this garment from one textile material, that garment from another. Reading a piece of clothing, in Angola's way, means naming two things — the kind of clothing it is, and the fabric it is made of — because the fabric is what the body feels and what washing and wear act on.

## The key ideas

- A **textile material** is a material cloth is made from — fibres worked into thread, thread into cloth, cloth into clothes.
- The material decides how the cloth behaves: soft or rough, warm or cool, strong or quick to tear.
- Angola's Grade 2 way reads every garment twice: the **kind of clothing**, and the **fabric** it is made of.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What two things do you name when you read a piece of clothing, in Angola's Grade 2 way?" Answer from the lesson: The kind of clothing it is, and the fabric — the textile material — it is made of.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take three pieces of clothing at home. For each, name the kind of clothing and guess the textile material it is made from; say which feels warmest and which coolest, and why the material explains it.
2. Find one garment whose label names its material. Read the label aloud and say what the named material tells you about the cloth.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
