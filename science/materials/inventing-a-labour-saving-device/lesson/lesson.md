# Inventing a labour-saving device

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — designing and making a device that saves labour — joining the simple machines to do a job with less effort — then testing it and improving it. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Designing and making the device

China's Grade 5 book closes its simple-machines unit with an invention: **design and make a labour-saving device** — a device whose whole purpose is to do a job with less effort. The inventor's materials are the machines the unit has taught — the lever, the pulley, the wheel and axle, the inclined plane — used alone or joined.

The design names the job first: what work is to be saved, and which machine or machines will save it. Then the making: the device built from what is at hand, in the design's order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Testing the device and improving it

An invention is not finished when it is built — it is finished when it is **tested**. The book's second half tests the device at its job: does it really save labour? Where does it strain, slip or waste effort?

Each failure found in testing becomes an improvement: a longer lever, a better-placed pulley, a joint remade. The device the class ends with is the tested, improved one — invention taught as a circle: design, make, test, improve.

## The key ideas

- A **labour-saving device** does a job with less effort; its parts are the simple machines — lever, pulley, wheel and axle, inclined plane — alone or joined.
- The design **names the job first**: what work is to be saved decides which machines the device needs.
- An invention is finished by **testing and improving**: each failure the test finds becomes a change, and the improved device is tested again.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Designing and making the device, Testing the device and improving it. Your companion may stop you once and ask one question about the hardest part; answer from the lesson, not by repeating it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Choose a job at home that takes effort. Design a labour-saving device for it on paper, labelling which simple machines it uses and what each one does.
2. Build a simple version of your device from safe materials and test it at its job. Write what the test showed.
3. Improve one thing the test exposed, test again, and write whether your device now saves more labour than before.
