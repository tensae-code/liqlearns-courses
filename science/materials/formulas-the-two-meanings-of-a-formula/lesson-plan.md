# Lesson plan — Formulas — the two meanings of a formula

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Formulas — the two meanings of a formula
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a formula speaks two ways at once: qualitatively it names which elements are present, quantitatively its coefficients and subscripts count molecules and atoms
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Formulas — polyatomic ions and their compounds (same unit). The Chemistry title counts with formulas again at secondary depth; this is the first-depth lesson, new here, cross-referenced not copied.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the qualitative reading of a formula: which elements are present.
2. Give the quantitative reading: total atoms of each element.
3. Separate the jobs of coefficient (whole units) and subscript (atoms inside).
4. Count correctly through brackets: Ca(OH)₂ = 1 Ca, 2 O, 2 H.

## If the learner is stuck
Multiplies the coefficient only into the first element → the coefficient counts WHOLE units: in 5H₂O it multiplies the O as well as the H. Ignores brackets → open brackets first, count inside, then multiply.
