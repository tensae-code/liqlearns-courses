# Formulas — the two meanings of a formula

## Do it first

Write down everything 5H₂O tells you — everything. Then do the same for 2HBr. Compare your two lists at the end of the lesson and count what you missed.

## The qualitative reading

Read **qualitatively**, a formula tells you WHICH elements are present, nothing more. HNO₃ contains hydrogen, nitrogen and oxygen. Ca(OH)₂ contains calcium, oxygen and hydrogen. This reading answers: what is it made of?

## The quantitative reading

Read **quantitatively**, the numbers count. The **subscript** counts atoms inside one molecule or unit; the number in front — the **coefficient** — counts whole molecules or units. In 5H₂O: five water units, each 2 H and 1 O — 10 hydrogen atoms and 5 oxygen atoms in all.

## Dissecting formulas

2HBr — 2 units, each 1 H and 1 Br: 2 H, 2 Br in all. 3Cl₂ — 3 molecules of two chlorines: 6 Cl. 4Br₂ — 8 Br. Ca(OH)₂ — the bracket doubles everything inside it: 1 Ca, 2 O, 2 H. HNO₃ — 1 H, 1 N, 3 O.

## The bracket rule, again

A subscript outside brackets multiplies EVERYTHING inside them. Miss that and Ca(OH)₂ loses an oxygen and a hydrogen. Always open the brackets in your count before totalling.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: a formula speaks two ways at once: qualitatively it names which elements are present, quantitatively its coefficients and subscripts count molecules and atoms. Your companion asks one question; answer it from the lesson, not from memory of anything else.
