# The properties of water

## Do it first
Pour water into a glass, then into a bottle. What stayed the same about the water, and what changed? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 5 *Ciências da Natureza* book, inside the water run, stops to set out the physical properties of water (As propriedades físicas da água) as their own lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Water's physical properties

Pure water has properties that name it anywhere: it is **colourless**, it has **no taste and no smell of its own**, and it is a **liquid** — it **flows** and **takes the shape of the vessel that holds it**, keeping its own amount while its shape changes. Angola's Grade 5 book teaches these **physical properties of water** (As propriedades físicas da água) as a set: the marks by which water is known as water.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the properties in daily life

The properties explain what we see every day. Water poured from pot to cup to bucket keeps its amount and changes its shape — the vessel's shape. Water that is coloured, tasting or smelling carries something in it — which is why these properties are also a first test: water for drinking should show water's own marks, nothing added. The book sets the properties inside its water run, between water in nature and the waters people use, so the learner reads every later water by them.

## The key ideas

- Pure water is **colourless**, with **no taste and no smell of its own**.
- Water is a liquid: it **flows** and **takes the shape of its vessel**, keeping its own amount.
- Coloured, tasting or smelling water **carries something in it** — the properties are a first test of water.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Water is poured from a pot into a cup. What changed and what stayed the same?" Answer from the lesson: Its shape changed — it took the cup's shape; its amount stayed the same. That is water behaving as a liquid.

## Do It Yourself
1. Pour the same water between three different vessels. Say at each pour what changed (shape) and what did not (amount).
2. Test three waters by the lesson's marks — look, smell, taste only if an adult says it is safe drinking water — and say which shows water's own properties.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
