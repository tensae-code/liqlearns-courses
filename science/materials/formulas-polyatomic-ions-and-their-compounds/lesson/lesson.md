# Formulas — polyatomic ions and their compounds

## Do it first

You can criss-cross single elements. Here is a group that travels together: CO₃, valence 2 — the carbonate ion. With sodium (valence 1), try to write the compound's formula. Keep your try; brackets are coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ions and their valences

A **polyatomic ion** is a group of atoms that travels together carrying one charge. Learn them as cards. Valence 1: OH⁻ hydroxide, CN⁻ cyanide, HCO₃⁻ hydrogen carbonate, NO₃⁻ nitrate, and the positive one, NH₄⁺ ammonium. Valence 2: CO₃²⁻ carbonate, SO₄²⁻ sulfate. Valence 3: PO₄³⁻ phosphate. The charge number IS the valence you criss-cross with.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building and naming their compounds

Criss-cross exactly as with binary compounds: Na (1) with CO₃ (2) gives **Na₂CO₃**; K (1) with NO₃ (1) gives **KNO₃**; Na (1) with OH (1) gives **NaOH**. When the ion group must repeat, wrap it in **brackets** and put the count outside: Al (3) with SO₄ (2) gives **Al₂(SO₄)₃**. Naming puts the metal — or ammonium — first, then the ion's name, with a Roman numeral when the metal varies: CuCO₃ copper(II) carbonate, Fe₂(SO₄)₃ iron(III) sulfate. The **ion card game** drills it: 100 cards of ions and metals; players build neutral compounds and name them, competing for correct builds.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: polyatomic ions are charged groups of atoms with their own formulas and valences; their compounds criss-cross like binary ones, with brackets when a group repeats. Your companion asks one question; answer it from the lesson, not from memory of anything else.

2. Write Ca(OH)₂, Al₂(SO₄)₃ and Fe₂(SO₄)₃ slowly, saying for each: which group repeats, and why the brackets are there — do the activity 'Bracket practice' in hands-on.md.
