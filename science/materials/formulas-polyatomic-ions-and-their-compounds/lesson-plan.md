# Lesson plan — Formulas — polyatomic ions and their compounds

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Formulas — polyatomic ions and their compounds
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** polyatomic ions are charged groups of atoms with their own formulas and valences; their compounds criss-cross like binary ones, with brackets when a group repeats

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The ions and their valences** — A **polyatomic ion** is a group of atoms that travels together carrying one charge.
2. **Building and naming their compounds** — Criss-cross exactly as with binary compounds: Na (1) with CO₃ (2) gives **Na₂CO₃**; K (1) with NO₃ (1) gives **KNO₃**; Na (1) with OH (1) gives **NaOH**.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Unit 3 lessons in order — symbols, then formulas, then reactions. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the nine common polyatomic ions with formulas and valences.
2. Criss-cross an ion with a metal to a correct formula (Na₂CO₃, KNO₃, NaOH).
3. Use brackets when an ion group repeats: Al₂(SO₄)₃, Ca(OH)₂.
4. Name ion compounds, with Roman numerals where the metal varies (CuCO₃, Fe₂(SO₄)₃).
5. Play the ion card game: build and name neutral compounds against a partner.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and cards for the ion game (paper scraps work).

## If the learner is stuck
Writes Al₂SO₄₃-style formulas → a repeating ion group goes in brackets with the count outside: Al₂(SO₄)₃. Forgets ammonium is positive → NH₄⁺ is the one positive ion in the set; it pairs with negative ions like a metal.
