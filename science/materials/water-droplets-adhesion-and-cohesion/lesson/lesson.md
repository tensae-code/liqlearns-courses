# Water droplets — adhesion and cohesion

## Do it first
Put one drop of water on a coin and one on a sheet of foil. They do not sit the same way. What decides a drop's shape — the water, or what it lands on? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Samoa's Year 10 way in — its Book 2 answers with two forces pulling in opposite directions.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two forces in every drop

Water pulls on itself: **cohesive** forces hold water molecules to each other. Water also pulls on the surface it touches: **adhesive** forces hold water to other materials. Every drop is a contest between the two. The book proves the pair with bench sights: drops sitting on foil and on a coin, small drops running together into one bigger drop when they touch (cohesion winning inside the water), and two glass plates stuck fast together by a thin film of water between them (adhesion holding plate to water to plate) (printed pp. 51–52).

## Sub lesson 2 — Round or flat — and the detergent test

When cohesion beats adhesion, the drop pulls itself into the roundest shape it can — a **round** drop. When adhesion beats cohesion, the drop spreads — a **flat** drop. The deciding test: touch a round drop with a drop of **detergent**, and watch its shape collapse and spread — the detergent weakens the water's hold on itself, and the contest's winner changes in front of your eyes (printed pp. 51–52).
