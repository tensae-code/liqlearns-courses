Today: **Water droplets — adhesion and cohesion** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**Two forces in every drop.** Water pulls on itself: cohesive forces hold water molecules to each other. Water also pulls on the surface it touches: adhesive forces hold water to other materials. Every drop is a contest between the two. The book proves the pair with bench sights: drops sitting on foil and on a coin, small drops running together into one bigger drop when they touch (cohesion winning inside the water), and two glass plates stuck fast together by a thin film of water between them (adhesion holding plate to water to plate) (printed pp. 51–52).

**Round or flat — and the detergent test.** When cohesion beats adhesion, the drop pulls itself into the roundest shape it can — a round drop. When adhesion beats cohesion, the drop spreads — a flat drop. The deciding test: touch a round drop with a drop of detergent, and watch its shape collapse and spread — the detergent weakens the water's hold on itself, and the contest's winner changes in front of your eyes (printed pp. 51–52).
