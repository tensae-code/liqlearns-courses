Today: Solutions and dissolving.

Remember this one thing: when a solid dissolves in water it spreads through the water as particles too small to see, making a solution — the solid has not vanished, it is still there; substances that will not dissolve are insoluble, and warming and stirring make dissolving faster and greater.

Stir salt or sugar into water and the grains disappear — but taste the water and the solid is still there, spread evenly through every drop. That even mixture is a solution: the dissolved solid is the solute, the water is the solvent.

Sand, stones and oil do not dissolve: stirred into water they stay themselves, sinking, floating or separating out again. Insoluble is a property of the pair — this solid in this liquid — not a failure of stirring.

A solution that will take no more solid is saturated, but warming the water usually lets it dissolve more, and stirring and crushing make dissolving faster. Cooling a hot saturated solution can bring the solid back out as crystals — proof it was there all along.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
