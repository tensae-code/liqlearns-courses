# Solutions and dissolving

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Solutions and dissolving. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
South Sudan's Primary 3 Science pupil's book gives dissolving a whole run of Unit 4 lessons: dissolving solids in water, substances that refuse to dissolve, where the dissolved solid goes, and how to make more of it dissolve.

In your mind's eye: when a solid dissolves in water it spreads through the water as particles too small to see, making a solution — the solid has not vanished, it is still there; substances that will not dissolve are insoluble, and warming and stirring make dissolving faster and greater. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Dissolving makes a solution.** Stir salt or sugar into water and the grains disappear — but taste the water and the solid is still there, spread evenly through every drop. That even mixture is a solution: the dissolved solid is the solute, the water is the solvent.

**Some substances are insoluble.** Sand, stones and oil do not dissolve: stirred into water they stay themselves, sinking, floating or separating out again. Insoluble is a property of the pair — this solid in this liquid — not a failure of stirring.

**More can dissolve.** A solution that will take no more solid is saturated, but warming the water usually lets it dissolve more, and stirring and crushing make dissolving faster. Cooling a hot saturated solution can bring the solid back out as crystals — proof it was there all along.
