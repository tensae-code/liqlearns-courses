# Solutions and dissolving — writing

Write your answer out in full sentences.

**1.** Write two or three sentences explaining solutions and dissolving, using the lesson's key facts.
**Model answer: When a solid dissolves in water it spreads through the water as particles too small to see, making a solution — the solid has not vanished, it is still there; substances that will not dissolve are insoluble, and warming and stirring make dissolving faster and greater. Stir salt or sugar into water and the grains disappear — but taste the water and the solid is still there, spread evenly through every drop. That even mixture is a solution: the dissolved solid is the solute, the water is the solvent. Sand, stones and oil do not dissolve: stirred into water they stay themselves, sinking, floating or separating out again. Insoluble is a property of the pair — this solid in this liquid — not a failure of stirring. A solution that will take no more solid is saturated, but warming the water usually lets it dissolve more, and stirring and crushing make dissolving faster. Cooling a hot saturated solution can bring the solid back out as crystals — proof it was there all along.**
