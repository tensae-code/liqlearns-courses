# Lesson plan — Materials and their properties — hard, strong, flexible

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Materials and their properties — hard, strong, flexible
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials (CURRICULUM.md Unit 4), beside Properties that identify a thing and How materials behave — stretchy, breakable, shapable: this is the systematic Gabon 4e année pass through the physical and mechanical properties, one property-word at a time
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every material has properties — inborn ways it behaves — and three of the most useful are mechanical: hardness (resisting scratches and dents), strength (resisting breaking under load or pull), and flexibility (bending without breaking, and sometimes springing back); we test each property, and we choose materials by matching the property to the job — a bridge needs strength, a floor needs hardness, a ruler that survives a schoolbag needs flexibility
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Properties that identify a thing is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Hardness — resisting scratches and dents** — hardness is a material's resistance to being scratched, dented or worn: we test it by scratching one material with another (the harder scratches, the softer is scratched) and by pressing; diamond-hard and chalk-soft are the two ends, and hardness is the property we ask for in floors, tools and knife edges
2. **Strength — resisting breaking** — strength is a material's resistance to breaking under a pull or a load: a strong thread carries a heavy stone, a weak one snaps; strength is tested by loading a sample until it fails, and it is the property that keeps bridges, ropes, shelves and chair legs trustworthy
3. **Flexibility — bending without breaking** — flexibility is a material's ability to bend without breaking: rubber and many plastics bend far and some spring back (elastic), wood bends a little, glass and dry clay do not bend at all — they are rigid and brittle; flexibility is chosen where things must survive knocks and movement, rigidity where they must hold an exact shape

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Properties are a material's inborn ways of behaving — the same treatment gets different answers from different materials.
2. Say, in their own words: Hardness: resisting scratches and dents — tested by scratching: the harder scratches the softer.
3. Say, in their own words: Strength: resisting breaking under pull or load — tested by loading until the sample fails.
4. Say, in their own words: Flexibility: bending without breaking; elastic materials also spring back. Rigid materials hold shape; brittle ones snap.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Coins and a nail for scratching (with care), samples: wood, plastic, rubber, metal, stone, card, fabric; a small weight or books for the strength test.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: three things, one bend, three different answers. A property is just a material's answer, given every time.
- Mixes up hard and strong → a plate is hard but not strong (it scratches little, breaks easily); a rope is strong but not hard. Different contests, different properties.
- Thinks flexible means weak → a rubber band bends without breaking where a 'harder' dry twig snaps. Bending can be the strongest answer of all.
