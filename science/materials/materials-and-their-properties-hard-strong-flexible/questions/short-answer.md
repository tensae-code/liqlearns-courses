# Materials and their properties — hard, strong, flexible — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why are axe edges and drill bits chosen from hard materials?
**Answer: Their jobs are daily contests against wood, metal and stone: a softer edge would be scratched, dented and worn blunt at once. Hardness is resistance to exactly that wear.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A plate is hard but a rope is strong. Explain how both statements can be true.
**Answer: They name different properties. The plate resists scratches (hard) but snaps under a blow or load (not strong). The rope is easily scratched (not hard) but carries heavy loads without breaking (strong).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Name a job that needs flexibility and one that needs rigidity, with your reasons.
**Answer: Flexibility: a schoolbag ruler or a spring — it must bend (and spring back) instead of breaking under knocks. Rigidity: a window pane or a shelf board — it must hold an exact shape under its load.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the lesson's choosing-rule in one sentence.
**Answer: Name the property the job needs first — hardness, strength or flexibility — then pick the material that owns that property.**
