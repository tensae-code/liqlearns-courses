# Describing things: matter and its properties

## Do it first
Salt or sugar? Orange or lemon? They look the same — until you use all your senses. Everything around us is matter, and every thing has its own set of properties. This lesson teaches you to read them.

## Everything is matter; telling things apart

Everything around us is **matter** — the Grade 2 book's word is ቁስ, the Grade 3 book's ቁስአካል. Objects agree and differ in their **properties**: **colour, shape, size, taste and smell** — and the Grade 3 book adds **soft or rough, heavy or light, sweet or bitter, long or short**.

Properties are how we tell alike things apart. **Salt** and **sugar** are both white grains — taste parts them. A **clay jebena** and a **metal pot** are both for the kitchen — weight, feel and sound part them. **Oranges** and **lemons** are both round and yellow-orange — taste parts them at once.

The Grade 3 book's game makes the point: one pupil lists an object's properties — white, dusty, writes on the board — and the other names the object: **chalk**. Properties, listed truly, name the thing.

## Say it to your companion
Describe an object to your companion by its properties only — no name allowed. Your companion guesses it, the book's own game. Then swap: their object, your guess.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort ten things from home into groups by one property — colour, then again by another — size or roughness — and say what changed.
2. Describe your favourite cup so exactly that a friend could pick it from five cups: colour, size, feel, weight.
3. List the properties of air: can you see it? Feel it? The lesson says air is matter too — defend that.
