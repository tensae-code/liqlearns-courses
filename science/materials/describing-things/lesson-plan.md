# Lesson plan — Describing things: matter and its properties

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Describing things: matter and its properties
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — the fourth title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** everything around us is matter, and we tell things apart by their properties: colour, shape, size, taste, smell, soft or rough, heavy or light, sweet or bitter, long or short
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Human Body — the five senses are the tools this lesson uses: properties are what the senses report.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say that everything around us is matter (ቁስ / ቁስአካል) — chair, stone, book, oil, even air.
2. Describe an object by its properties: colour, smell, soft or rough, heavy or light, sweet or bitter, long or short.
3. Tell things apart that look alike — salt from sugar, oranges from lemons — by taste and smell as well as sight.
4. Play the book's guessing game: one learner lists an object's properties, the other names the object.

## What you need
Objects from home and classroom to sort: a clay jebena and a metal pot, an orange and a lemon, salt and sugar (to look at — tasting only what a teacher gives).

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses only the eyes → properties live in all five senses: a lemon and an orange part company at taste and smell, not at sight.
- Thinks air is 'nothing' → the books count air as matter; it can be felt on the skin and it fills a balloon.
- This lesson's next door: How materials behave — what things do when you pull, drop and beat them.
