# Building new objects from a small set of pieces

## Do it first
A tower built from ten blocks comes down — and the same ten blocks become a bridge. Nothing was added, nothing thrown away. So where does the new object come from? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 2 book teaches the parts of a structure as material that can be reshaped into new objects.

## Sub lesson 1 — Objects are made of a small set of pieces

Start by seeing the **pieces**: many objects are **made of a small set of pieces** joined in one arrangement (Grade 2, 2.3.3, printed p. 98). The object is not one solid lump of stuff — it is pieces, plus the way they are put together. Name the pieces and you have the object's whole material.

## Sub lesson 2 — Disassemble and reshape — a new object, a new function

The pieces can come apart: **disassemble** the object, and the same set of pieces can be **reshaped into a new object with a different function**. The tower's blocks become the bridge. Matter is conserved — the pieces are unchanged — while arrangement and function change completely. That is the lesson's model, and the book has pupils model it with their own hands.

## The key ideas

- **Objects are made of a small set of pieces** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Disassemble and reshape — a new object, a new function** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A model house built from a set of pieces is taken apart and the pieces become a model boat. What stayed the same, and what changed?" Answer: The pieces — the material — stayed the same; the arrangement changed, and with it the object and its function.
