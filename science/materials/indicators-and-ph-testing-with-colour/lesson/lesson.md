# Indicators and pH — testing with colour

## Do it first

Three clear liquids look exactly alike: an acid, water, a base. You may not taste or touch them. How could colour alone tell you which is which? Name a coloured plant juice that might do it, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Indicators — colour that answers

An **indicator** is a substance that **changes colour** in acid and in base, so the eye can sort what taste and touch must never test. In **Experiment 3.3** common acids and bases are tested against indicators and each pair's colour is recorded — the same indicator shows **one colour in acid, a different colour in base**. A universal indicator shows a **range** of colours, one for each strength, which is what makes the pH scale readable.

## Sub lesson 2 — The pH scale — and Ethiopia's own plant indicators

The **pH scale runs 0 to 14**: **below 7 is acid** (the lower, the stronger), **7 is neutral**, **above 7 is base** (the higher, the stronger). Household substances sit along it — lemon juice and vinegar low, pure water at 7, soap and ash water high. Bought indicators are not the only way: **local plant indicators — beetroot and petunia** among them — change colour in acid and base too, and testing with them is the **eco-friendly** way, using what grows nearby instead of bought chemicals.
