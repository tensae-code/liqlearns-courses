# Lesson plan — Indicators and pH — testing with colour

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Indicators and pH — testing with colour
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Classification of Compounds (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indicators change colour in acid and in base, and the pH scale 0–14 fixes the strength: below 7 acid, 7 neutral, above 7 base — with local plant indicators (beetroot, petunia) as the eco-friendly test
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Acids and bases — definitions and naming (same unit): the things defined there are tested here. It stands alone for a direct learner who can say what an acid and a base are.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Indicators — colour that answers** — An **indicator** is a substance that **changes colour** in acid and in base, so the eye can sort what taste and touch must never test.
2. **The pH scale — and Ethiopia's own plant indicators** — The **pH scale runs 0 to 14**: **below 7 is acid** (the lower, the stronger), **7 is neutral**, **above 7 is base** (the higher, the stronger).
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Unit 3 lessons in order. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an indicator does and why colour, not taste, does the testing.
2. Read a pH number: acid below 7, neutral 7, base above 7; stronger toward the ends.
3. Name two local plant indicators and why using them is eco-friendly.

## What you need
If available: beetroot juice or petunia petals, vinegar, soap water, three clear cups. Adult present; nothing is tasted.
