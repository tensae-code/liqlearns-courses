# Reactions — chemistry in everyday life

## Do it first

List every chemical reaction that happened in your home before you left it this morning. Fire? Cooking? A lamp? Write the list — the lesson will grade it.

## Reactions at work

Burning fuels gives energy for cooking, light and travel. Cooking itself is chemistry. Iron is smelted, glass and pottery are fired, cheese is made and drinks are brewed by reactions. Rocks, air, oceans and living bodies run on reactions too — chemistry is the basis of energy supply, technology and culture.

## The home energy audit

Gather it as a group audit: every home energy reaction you can find — the fire, the stove, the lamp, the candle — each with its reactants and what it gives: heat, light, cooked food, ash and smoke.

## Making tella — a whole process (teacher-guide evidence)

The teacher guide works tella-making as the unit's complete everyday-chemistry anchor. Barley is soaked about 24 hours to malt (bikil), sun-dried and ground. Gesho leaves and stems are dried and ground. The two are mixed with water in a smoked vessel (insera) and fermented two days into tejet. Dough of millet, sorghum or teff is baked as unleavened ye-tella kita, sliced in and sealed for anaerobic fermentation into tenses. Maize is soaked about 3 days, dried, roasted and ground dark as asharo, which sets the tella's colour. Last, difdif is diluted in, the tella rests 5–6 hours, is filtered and served. Raw materials: barley, wheat, maize, millet, sorghum, teff and gesho. The guide's verdict: fermentation is a chemical change — new substances form, and no filtering can un-form them.
