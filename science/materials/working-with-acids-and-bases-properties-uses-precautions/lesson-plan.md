# Lesson plan — Working with acids and bases — properties, uses, precautions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Working with acids and bases — properties, uses, precautions
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Classification of Compounds (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** acids react with active metals to release hydrogen, taste sour, conduct and serve in batteries, food and industry; bases feel soapy — and the safety rules (the student book's three, the guide's eight) are content, not a footnote
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Indicators and pH — testing with colour (same unit): what was tested there is handled and used here. It stands alone for a direct learner who knows what acids and bases are.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Properties and uses** — **Acids** taste **sour** (in foods — never taste a laboratory acid), **conduct** electricity in solution, and react with **active metals to release hydrogen**: **Experiment 3.
2. **Precautions — the rules are part of the science (teacher guide — evidence)** — The student book prints **three safety rules** for all handling; the teacher guide keys **eight**: **eye protection**; **protective clothing / apron**; bottles **kept stoppered** when not in use; **spillages wiped up at once** with a wet cloth; **shoes that cover the feet**; **never smell a chemical directly**; **never taste a chemical** — and no food, drink or chewing gum in the laboratory; and **never add water to concentrated acid — add acid to water, carefully**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write the zinc + hydrochloric acid equation and name the gas.
2. List acid and base uses from daily life.
3. Recite the eight safety rules and the eye-splash first aid.

## What you need
Nothing to handle at home: this lesson's bench work is read and watched; the doing is the safety booklet and the rule drill.
