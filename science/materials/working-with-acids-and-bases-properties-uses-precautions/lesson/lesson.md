# Working with acids and bases — properties, uses, precautions

## Do it first

A piece of zinc drops into dilute hydrochloric acid and the tube fizzes hard. Before reading: what gas is that fizz? And which three rules would you set before any pupil touches the bench? Keep both answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Properties and uses

**Acids** taste **sour** (in foods — never taste a laboratory acid), **conduct** electricity in solution, and react with **active metals to release hydrogen**: **Experiment 3.4** — Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂, the fizz in the tube. Acids work in **car batteries, food** (vinegar, citrus) and **industry**. **Bases** feel **soapy** to the touch and turn indicators toward their base colour; in **Experiment 3.5 ammonia** is tested against indicators and shows basic. Bases work in soap, cleaning and treating acid soils (the next lesson).

## Sub lesson 2 — Precautions — the rules are part of the science (teacher guide — evidence)

The student book prints **three safety rules** for all handling; the teacher guide keys **eight**: **eye protection**; **protective clothing / apron**; bottles **kept stoppered** when not in use; **spillages wiped up at once** with a wet cloth; **shoes that cover the feet**; **never smell a chemical directly**; **never taste a chemical** — and no food, drink or chewing gum in the laboratory; and **never add water to concentrated acid — add acid to water, carefully**. Pupils compile the eight into their **own safety booklet**. First aid from the same key: a chemical **splash in the eye is washed with plenty of water** before medical treatment.
