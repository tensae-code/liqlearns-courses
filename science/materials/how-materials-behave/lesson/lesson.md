# How materials behave: stretchy, breakable, shapable

## Do it first
Pull it — it stretches back. Drop it — it shatters. Beat it — it flattens into a sheet. Materials have behaviours, and knowing them keeps things whole and hands safe. This lesson sorts the behaviours.

## Sub lesson 1 — Things that break easily

Some materials **break easily**: a **bottle**, a **drinking glass**, a clay **jebena**. The Grade 2 book's activity is a sorting: children bring objects from home and sort them into **breakable** and **not breakable**. The point of the sort is care: breakable things are carried with two hands, kept from table edges, and never thrown. We do not test them by dropping — a breakable thing is known by its kind, not by its crash.

## Sub lesson 2 — Things that stretch

Other materials **stretch**: **rubber and plastics** — a **balloon**, the elastic of a **money band**, **waist elastic**, **socks**. Children in the book pull plastic and rubber in class, and sort stretchy from non-stretchy things brought from home. The teacher guide gives the behaviour its proper name: **elasticity (ተለጣጭነት)** — a material's ability to stretch and **spring back**. That springing back is why children bounce on a car tyre: the tyre stretches under them and pushes back.

## Sub lesson 3 — Shapable and brittle materials

The teacher guide adds two more behaviours. Some metals can be **drawn out into thin wire** — that is **ductility (ተጠመዝማዥነት)** — and some can be **beaten into flat sheet** — **malleability (ተጠፍጣፊነት)**. The guide's examples for the shapable metals: **gold, silver, copper, iron, aluminium and tin** — the goldsmith beating a sheet and drawing a wire is using both.

Against them stands **brittleness (ተሰባሪነት)**: materials that neither stretch nor flatten, but snap or shatter — **charcoal, stone, glass, pencil lead, bottle, ice, brick and ceramic**. One pull tells you the family: it stretches back, it shapes, or it breaks.

[Picture 12124 — a goldsmith beating a flat sheet and drawing a wire]

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion the three behaviour sorts with one example each from your own home: a thing that stretches, a thing that breaks, a thing that could be shaped. Your companion adds one more of each.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort twenty things from home into BREAKABLE and NOT BREAKABLE, the Grade 2 book's own sorting, and lay the breakable ones where they are handled carefully.
2. Stretch test: pull a rubber band gently, let go, watch it spring back. Try the same with a strip of paper. Say why one is called stretchy and the other is not.
3. Bounce question: the book asks why children bounce on a car tyre. Answer it with the stretch word, in a full sentence.
