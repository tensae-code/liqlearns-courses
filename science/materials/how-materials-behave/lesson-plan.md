# Lesson plan — How materials behave: stretchy, breakable, shapable

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** How materials behave: stretchy, breakable, shapable
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — after Describing things (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** when we pull, drop and beat materials, they behave in kinds: some stretch and spring back, some break, some can be drawn into wire or beaten into sheet
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Describing things — properties first; behaviour is what a material does when it is used.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Things that break easily** — bottle, drinking glass, jebena — sorted, and handled carefully
2. **Things that stretch** — rubber and plastics stretch and spring back; the guide's word is elasticity
3. **Shapable and brittle materials** — the guide's ductility and malleability, and brittleness — the counter-sort

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Describing things: matter and its properties. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort objects into breakable and not breakable: bottle, drinking glass and jebena break easily.
2. Show what stretchy materials do: rubber and plastics stretch — balloon, money-band elastic, waist elastic, socks — and spring back.
3. Say the teacher guide's three shaping words: elasticity, ductility (drawn into wire) and malleability (beaten into sheet), with gold, silver, copper, iron, aluminium and tin as examples.
4. Say brittleness: materials that neither stretch nor flatten — charcoal, stone, glass, pencil lead, bottle, ice, brick, ceramic.

## What you need
Things brought from home to sort: a rubber band, a balloon, socks, a bottle and a glass (handled carefully, never dropped on purpose), a stone, a piece of charcoal.

## If the learner is stuck
- Wants to test 'breakable' by dropping → the books sort breakables by knowledge, not by breaking: a thing known to break is handled carefully, never dropped to prove it.
- Calls every soft thing stretchy → stretchy means it stretches AND springs back, like the band; a sock stretches, clay only changes shape.
- The shaping words (ductility, malleability) rest on teacher-guide evidence — labelled so in the sources.
