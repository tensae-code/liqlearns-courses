# What matter is

## Sub lesson 1 — Everything around us is matter

**Matter** is everything that has mass and takes up space. The chair, the water in the cup, the book and your own body are matter. Even the air — which you cannot see — is matter: blow up a balloon and the air inside pushes the rubber out, taking up space.

Kuwait's Grade 1 Term 2 book opens its Matter and Energy unit here: before states of matter or changes, the child first sorts the world into matter — the stuff everything is made of.

## Sub lesson 2 — Matter and not-matter

Not everything is matter. Light is not matter: it has no mass and takes up no space, though it travels through matter and shows it to us. Heat, sound and shadows are not matter either — they are things matter does or things that happen to it.

The test the book gives is simple enough for a first-year child: can you weigh it, and does it take up room? If yes to both, it is matter. Everything else in this course's Materials title — states, particles, changes — is built on this one sorting.

## The key ideas

- Matter has mass and takes up space.
- Solids, liquids, gases — and even invisible air — are matter.
- Light, heat, sound and shadows are not matter.
- The test: can it be weighed, and does it take up room?
