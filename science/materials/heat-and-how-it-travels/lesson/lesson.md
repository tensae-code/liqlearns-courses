# Heat and how it travels

## Do it first
Two spoons, one pot of hot water — and only one handle burns your fingers. Heat travels, but not always the same way. This lesson names its three roads.

## Heat on the move — three ways
**Heat is energy flowing from a hotter body to a colder one** when they touch; **a temperature difference is required** for the flow. It travels **three ways: conduction, convection and radiation**.

[Picture 13424 — one pot shown three ways — conduction, convection, radiation]

## Conduction and convection
In **conduction** the heated end's molecules move more, collide with their neighbours and hand the energy along the body. **Conductors**: iron, aluminium, nickel and copper. **Non-conductors**: wood and plastic. The book's test: candle wax fixed along a metal rod melts in sequence, from the hot end. **Convection** happens in **liquids and gases only**: the hot part rises and the cold part sinks, circulating until the whole pot of water — or the teakettle — is hot.

[GIF 13425 — the circulation loop turning in the pot]

## Radiation — and Experiment 8
**Radiation** is heat travelling through air and **even vacuum** — the Sun's heat reaching Earth across emptiness. **Experiment 8** focuses sunlight with a hand lens onto paper until it browns, smokes and burns; heat spreads in all directions from its source. **This experiment is filed as a teacher demonstration only** — adult hands, away from eyes, never looking at the Sun — and never as a pupil task.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the three ways with one example each — spoon handle, pot water, sunshine — and the one condition every flow needs: a temperature difference.

## Do It Yourself
1. Spoon test (adult-led): metal and wooden spoons stood in one hot pot. After a minute, which handle is hot? Name the way and the families: conductor, non-conductor.
2. Kettle watching: watch a teakettle or pot heat (from a safe distance, with an adult). Where does the water move first — and what loop does the lesson say it follows?
3. Sun on two stones: a black stone and a white stone in the sun. Both are warmed by the same way — radiation. Which warms faster is your own observation to record.
