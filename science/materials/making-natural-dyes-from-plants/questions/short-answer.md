# Making natural dyes from plants — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What does the dye-making investigation find first, and how?
**Answer:** Which plant parts make colour for materials — by trying parts one by one and keeping the finding part by part: this part gives a colour, that part gives none, another gives a different colour.

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is a plant part that gives no colour still a useful result?
**Answer:** Because the investigation sorts plant parts into colour-makers and non-colour-makers; learning that a part gives no colour is a finding exactly like learning that one does.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Describe how a plant part's colour becomes a dye.
**Answer:** The part is put in water; its colour leaves the part and dissolves in the water, spreading through the whole cup until the water itself is coloured — and that coloured water is the dye that colours materials.

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does this lesson sit inside the mixtures unit?
**Answer:** Because the dye is a mixture made on purpose: colour mixed into water. Finding the colour-makers and dissolving their colour is mixture-making, taught in the unit that teaches mixtures.
