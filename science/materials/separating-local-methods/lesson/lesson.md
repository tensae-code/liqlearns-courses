# Separating mixtures — local methods

## Do it first
Before any laboratory, the harvest was already being separated: grain tossed into the wind, coffee settling clear in the jebena, tella clarifying in its vessel. This lesson names the science inside the home's own methods.

## Sub lesson 1 — Winnowing and sieving — the harvest methods
**Aim:** to recognise and use the community's own separation methods — sieving, winnowing, decanting and clarifying — and name the science in each
**Materials:** a winnowing tray with grain and chaff, a sieve, a jebena with brewed coffee, a vessel of tella or tej
**Steps:**
1. Toss the grain-and-chaff mixture from the tray into moving air, again and again.
2. Collect what fell straight down and what blew aside, separately.
3. Shake a mixed sample through the sieve and inspect both sides.
4. Let brewed coffee stand in the jebena, then pour the clear coffee off the settled grounds.

## Sub lesson 2 — What you observe, and what it proves
**Observation:** In winnowing, the dense grain falls nearly straight while the light chaff is carried aside by the air — separation by density. The sieve parts by size. The jebena's rest-and-pour is decantation; tella and tej clarify by settling; the local still is distillation. The home kit and the laboratory kit are one kit.

## Say it to your companion
Take your companion through a home separation tour: winnowing, sieving, the jebena, the tella vessel. For each, they name the laboratory twin: decantation, filtration, distillation.

## Do It Yourself
1. Winnow a handful of grain (or watch it done). Sketch the two paths: falling and blowing.
2. Pour coffee from a jebena slowly. What separation are you doing, and what stays behind?
3. List every separation used in your home this week, each with its laboratory name.
