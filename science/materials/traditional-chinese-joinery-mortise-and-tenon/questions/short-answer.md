# Traditional Chinese joinery — mortise and tenon — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** Name the two parts of the joint and say which each one is.
**Answer:** The tenon — a tongue of wood that sticks out of one piece — and the mortise — a hole or slot cut in the other piece to match it.

**2.** How does the joint hold without nails?
**Answer:** By fit: the tongue pushes into the hole of exactly its shape, and the two parts grip each other — the strength is in the shape and the fit, not in any added fastener.

**3.** Where does China's Grade 3 book show this joinery at work?
**Answer:** In old Chinese buildings and bridges, where beam fits into post and rail into upright, each joint cut to its place.

**4.** Why is this joint taught inside a building-materials unit?
**Answer:** Because before steel and reinforced concrete, this was the way China's builders joined timber — the joint shows how a material was worked, not only what it is.
