# Lesson plan — Traditional Chinese joinery — mortise and tenon

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Traditional Chinese joinery — mortise and tenon
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — appended to Unit 4 (Materials) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the mortise-and-tenon joint — a wooden tongue fitting a wooden hole — the joinery of old Chinese buildings and bridges, China's own craft way
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The two parts of the joint** — A **mortise-and-tenon** joint joins two pieces of wood with no nails and no screws.
2. **Joinery in old Chinese buildings and bridges** — China's Grade 3 book meets this joint where it has worked for centuries: in **old Chinese buildings and bridges**.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: The two parts of the joint.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Joinery in old Chinese buildings and bridges.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: kitchen and household materials only where the hands-on says so, with adult help where the hands-on says so.
