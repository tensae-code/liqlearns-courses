# Lesson plan — Salts — preparation, names and uses

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Salts — preparation, names and uses
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Classification of Compounds (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** salts are made three ways (acid + base, acid + metal, acid + carbonate), named from their acid (binary-acid salts end -ide; oxyacid salts end -ate — teacher guide), and used everywhere from table salt to fertiliser
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Neutralisation — cancelling acid with base (same unit): one of this lesson's three preparation routes is that lesson's reaction. It stands alone for a direct learner who can write acid + base → salt + water.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Making salts — three routes** — A **salt** is what an acid leaves behind when its hydrogen is replaced.
2. **Naming salts — and the six-jar uses table (teacher guide — evidence)** — A salt's surname comes from its acid (teacher guide's completion of the naming machine): salts of **binary acids** drop 'hydro-' and turn '-ic acid' into **'-ide'** — **chloride, bromide, iodide**; salts of **oxyacids** turn '-ic acid' into **'-ate'** — **nitrate, chlorate, sulphate, phosphate**.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Unit 3 lessons in order. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the three salt-preparation routes, one with a balanced equation.
2. Name a salt from its acid: -ide for binary acids, -ate for oxyacids.
3. Match the six use-table salts to their jobs.
