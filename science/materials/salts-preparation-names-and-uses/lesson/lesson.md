# Salts — preparation, names and uses

## Do it first

Six jars: NaCl, CaCO₃, NaHCO₃, CaSO₄, KNO₃, (NH₄)₂HPO₄. Before reading: which jar seasons food, which builds walls, which feeds a field? Guess all six — the lesson marks your guesses.

## Sub lesson 1 — Making salts — three routes

A **salt** is what an acid leaves behind when its hydrogen is replaced. Salts are **binary** (two elements, like NaCl) or **ternary** (three, like CaCO₃). Three preparation routes: **acid + base** (neutralisation), **acid + metal** (Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂ — the salt zinc chloride stays in solution), and **acid + carbonate** (CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ — it fizzes as the carbonate breaks). **Experiment 3.6 prepares common salt** by the acid + base route and recovering the solid. A class project tests **local carbonate rocks with vinegar** — fizz means carbonate.

## Sub lesson 2 — Naming salts — and the six-jar uses table (teacher guide — evidence)

A salt's surname comes from its acid (teacher guide's completion of the naming machine): salts of **binary acids** drop 'hydro-' and turn '-ic acid' into **'-ide'** — **chloride, bromide, iodide**; salts of **oxyacids** turn '-ic acid' into **'-ate'** — **nitrate, chlorate, sulphate, phosphate**. The first name is the metal or ammonium. The uses table runs six salts: **NaCl** table salt; **CaCO₃** limestone and chalk; **NaHCO₃** baking soda; **CaSO₄** plaster and gypsum; **KNO₃** fertiliser; **(NH₄)₂HPO₄** ammonium hydrogen phosphate, fertiliser — from table to field.
