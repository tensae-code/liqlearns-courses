# Lesson plan — Organic and inorganic compounds

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Organic and inorganic compounds
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Classification of Compounds (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** carbon is the dividing line: organic compounds are carbon compounds — with named exceptions (carbon oxides, carbonates and a few others counted inorganic); inorganic compounds cover everything else, including all the acids, bases and salts that follow
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Molecules — atoms joined (Unit 2): compounds are known as joined atoms; now compounds are sorted by whether carbon is in them. The primary lesson Oxides, acids, bases and salts touches the inorganic families at first depth; this is the Grade 8 sorting rule that organises them.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort a substance as organic or inorganic using the carbon rule.
2. Name the exceptions: carbon oxides and carbonates count as inorganic.
3. Tell the life-force story and what Wöhler's 1828 urea synthesis proved.

## What you need
Paper cards or a list of household substances to sort.
