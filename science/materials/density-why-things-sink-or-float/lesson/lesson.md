# Density: why things sink or float

## Do it first
A bucket of water. Drop in a small iron ball — down it goes, straight to the bottom. Drop in a block of sponge — it bobs on top and will not sink. Same water, same bucket. Why does the water treat them so differently? The answer has a name: density.

## Packed matter: the sink-or-float property
**Density** is **the amount of matter packed into a body's volume** — how much stuff is squeezed into the space the body takes up.

The iron ball packs a great deal of matter into its small volume, so it is **dense** — and it **sinks**. The sponge packs very little matter into its volume — its spaces are full of air — so it is **not dense**, and it **floats**.

That is why the book says density **tells what a body is made of**: two things of the same size can behave oppositely in water, because what is packed inside them differs.

## Grade 5 — density's formula, and pressure
The Grade 5 book gives density its formula: **density = mass ÷ volume**, world unit **kg/m³**; pure water's density is **1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³**. Solids are generally denser than liquids — which answers the opener: **oil and water in one glass separate, the oil on top**. Then the twin idea: **pressure = force ÷ the area it lands on**, world unit the **pascal**; with the same force, **a bigger area means less pressure and a smaller area more**. The opener experiments: pressing a **pen's two ends into the two palms** (which palm hurts?) and a **goat against a donkey on soft ground** — whose hoof sinks deeper? Uses follow: horses', elephants' and oxen's broad feet, tractors' wide tyres, and why boats float.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the bucket story: what sank, what floated, and what density means — matter packed into volume.

## Do It Yourself
1. Float-or-sink trials: test five safe things in the bucket — a leaf, a coin, a cork or dry wood, a stone, an orange. Record sink or float for each, the book's way.
2. Same size, different fate: find two things of nearly the same size where one sinks and one floats. Density, not size, decided.
3. Sponge science: squeeze the floating sponge under the water and let go — it bobs back up. Say why, using the word density.
