# Dissolving — when solids disappear into liquids — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Water and other liquids as solvents.
**Answer:** Some solids **dissolve** in a liquid: they disappear into it, spread through it, and are still there — the sweet taste proves the sugar.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: The first races — fine against coarse, stirred against still.
**Answer:** Serbia's book settles what changes the speed by racing pairs: **fine sugar against cubes** — the fine, with more surface touching the water, against the lump; and **stirring against still** — the stirred glass raced against the glass left alone.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: The last race — warm water against cold.
**Answer:** The final pair races the water itself: the same solid dissolved in **warm water against cold**.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Water and other liquids as solvents → The first races — fine against coarse, stirred against still → The last race — warm water against cold — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
