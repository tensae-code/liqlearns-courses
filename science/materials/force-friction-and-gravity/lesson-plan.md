# Lesson plan — Force, friction and gravity

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Force, friction and gravity
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — the influences that start, stop and turn things (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a force is a push or pull — contact or non-contact; force changes shape, direction and speed; friction fights motion between touching surfaces (static greater than kinetic); and gravity is the Earth's pull toward its centre, under which — without air resistance — all bodies fall together, as Galileo taught
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Motion, distance and speed — motion is described; now what causes it to change is named.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Motion, distance and speed. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Separate contact forces from non-contact forces with the book's examples (well-rope and bicycle against gravity and the magnet).
2. Recite force's three effects: changes shape and size; changes a moving body's direction; changes speed.
3. Explain friction — roughness, static against kinetic — and weigh its benefits against its harms; state gravity and Galileo's falling-bodies point.

## What you need
A magnet and a nail, clay or plasticine, a lemon, a ball and a stone — the book's own test kit; a magnifier if the class has one.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks force must touch → the magnet and gravity act across a gap; those are the non-contact family.
- Thinks friction is only a harm → without it shoes slip, brakes fail and matches never light; the lesson weighs both sides, as the book does.
