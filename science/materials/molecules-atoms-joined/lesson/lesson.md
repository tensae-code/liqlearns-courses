# Molecules — atoms joined

## Do it first

Here are three drawings in words: a single atom alone; two atoms joined as a pair; a cluster of several atoms joined. And here is a question to carry into the lesson: a drop of water can be divided into smaller drops, and smaller — what is the smallest piece that is still water? Guess now, and check your guess at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Atoms join — element molecules

Atoms do not always travel alone. Atoms **join into molecules**. When the joined atoms are all of **one element**, the molecule sorts by how many atoms it holds:

- **Monoatomic** — one atom: the molecule is a single atom, as with the noble gases.
- **Diatomic** — two atoms joined: oxygen in the air travels as O₂, two oxygen atoms together.
- **Polyatomic** — many atoms joined in one molecule.

[Picture 33704 — single atoms, pairs and clusters sorted into the three molecule types]

## Sub lesson 2 — Compound molecules — and the smallest particle of a substance

When atoms of **different elements** join, the molecule is a **compound molecule**. Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom join into one molecule of **water**: different elements, one joined particle, a substance with its own behaviour — water behaves as water, not as hydrogen and not as oxygen.

[GIF 33705 — two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom joining into a water molecule]

That gives the definition the lesson turns on: **the molecule is the smallest particle of a substance that still behaves as that substance**. Divide a water molecule and you no longer have water at all — you have hydrogen and oxygen atoms. So the answer to the opening question is: the smallest piece that is still water is **one water molecule**.
