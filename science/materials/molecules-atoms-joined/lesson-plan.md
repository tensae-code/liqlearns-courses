# Lesson plan — Molecules — atoms joined

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Molecules — atoms joined
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 8, Unit 2 "Composition of Matter" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** atoms join into molecules — monoatomic, diatomic or polyatomic — compound molecules join different elements, and the molecule is the smallest particle of a substance that still behaves as that substance
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Materials lessons Inside the atom — protons, neutrons, electrons and Formulas — reading what a compound is made of (Grade 7 depth), whose short 'molecules of elements' part names the same three words inside formula-reading; here the Grade 8 book teaches the molecule itself, element and compound molecules together, as its own lesson. The Chemistry title treats molecules again at secondary depth; cross-referenced not copied.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Atoms join — element molecules** — atoms join into molecules; a molecule of one element only can be monoatomic (one atom), diatomic (two atoms) or polyatomic (many atoms)
2. **Compound molecules — and the smallest particle of a substance** — molecules of different elements joined are compound molecules; the molecule is the smallest particle of a substance that still behaves as that substance

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Isotopes — and why atomic masses are not whole numbers. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort molecules as monoatomic, diatomic or polyatomic, with an example of each kind.
2. Tell an element molecule from a compound molecule.
3. State what a molecule is: the smallest particle of a substance that still behaves as that substance.
4. Describe the joining in the water case: two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom join into one water molecule.

## What you need
Bottle tops or stones in two colours, for building molecules by hand.

## If the learner is stuck
Counts a lone atom and a molecule as the same thing → for substances that travel joined (oxygen as O₂), the joined pair is the particle of the substance; a lone atom of it is not how the substance exists. Thinks joining melts atoms into one blob → the GIF keeps each atom whole and distinct inside the molecule; joining is linking, not melting.
