# Lesson plan — Air is there — air takes up space and can be pushed

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Air is there — air takes up space and can be pushed
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials (CURRICULUM.md Unit 4), beside Solids, liquids and gases and Describing things — matter and its properties: air is the first gas the learner proves is real matter — it takes up space
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** air is real stuff, not nothing: although we cannot see it, air takes up space — a cup pushed straight down into water stays dry inside because the air inside it will not let the water in — and air can be pushed and made to push: squeezed or blown, it moves water, fills balloons and tyres, and does work
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Solids, liquids and gases is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Air takes up space** — air is matter: it fills every 'empty' space — the cup pushed into water keeps its paper dry because the air trapped inside occupies the cup and the water cannot enter where the air already is; tip the cup, the air escapes as bubbles, and the water rushes in at once
2. **Air can be pushed — and pushes back** — air moves when it is pushed and pushes what is in its way: blowing fills a balloon and stretches it, a pump forces air into a tyre until the tyre is hard with it, moving air — wind — turns sails, carries seeds and dries wet clothes; we use air's push every day without seeing the pusher

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Solids, liquids and gases**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Air is matter — invisible, but real: it takes up space, like water and stone do.
2. Say, in their own words: A cup pushed straight into water keeps paper dry inside: the trapped air will not let the water in.
3. Say, in their own words: Tip the cup and the air escapes as bubbles — water rushes in: space is filled by one thing or another.
4. Say, in their own words: Air can be pushed, and pushed air pushes back — balloons, tyres and pumps all work on this.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A clear cup or glass, a basin of water, a small piece of paper or a dry leaf, a balloon, and a bicycle pump if one is near.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the dry paper. Nothing visible kept it dry — start from that nothing-that-is-something.
- Thinks 'empty' means nothing inside → the room, the bottle and the cup are all full of air. Empty of water is not empty of everything.
- Thinks air cannot push because it is light → light is not weak: a tyre is made hard by air alone, and wind can push a whole boat.
