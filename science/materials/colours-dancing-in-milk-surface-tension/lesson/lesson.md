# Colours dancing in milk — surface tension

## Do it first
Before you read: drops of colour are put on a dish of milk. They sit almost still. Then one drop of soap touches the milk. What do you think the colours will do — stay still, sink at once, or move? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Finland's way in — its Luova LUMA activity bank teaches it as colours dancing in milk: soap breaks the milk's surface film and chases the fat.

## Sub lesson 1 — The film on milk

Pour **milk** into a shallow dish and let it settle. Put drops of **colour** on the surface.

The colours do not spread at once. They rest on the **surface film** of the milk — the skin-like top of the liquid — sitting where they were dropped, barely moving. The film is holding the surface together, and the colours sit on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Soap breaks the film

Now touch the milk with a little **soap**.

The soap **breaks the milk's surface film** — and **chases the fat** in the milk. The surface, no longer held in one piece, pulls and swirls — and the colours resting on it **dance**: they race, curl and mix as the broken surface moves.

So the dancing is not the soap painting. It is the **film breaking**: soap breaks the surface film and chases the fat, and the moving surface carries the colours with it.

## The key ideas

- Drops of colour rest on milk's **surface film**, barely moving.
- **Soap breaks** the milk's surface film and **chases the fat**.
- The colours **dance** because the broken surface pulls and swirls, carrying them with it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the colours resting on the film first, then the soap breaking it. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What did the soap break?" Answer with the surface film, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Set up the milk dish with adult help: drops of colour on still milk. Draw the dish before any soap touches it.
2. Add one touch of soap and draw the dish again. Label what broke and what moved.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the film, the soap, and why the colours danced.
