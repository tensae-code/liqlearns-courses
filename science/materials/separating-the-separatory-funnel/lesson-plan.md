# Lesson plan — Separating mixtures — the separatory funnel

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Separating mixtures — the separatory funnel
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 7, Unit 2 "Matter in Our Surroundings" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two liquids that refuse to mix are parted in a stoppered funnel: settle into layers, then drain the lower layer through the stopcock and stop exactly at the boundary
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Separating mixtures — why and how we choose; here the funnel method for unmixing liquids is done for real

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Experiment 2.9 — shaking, venting and settling in the funnel** — oil and water are shaken in the stoppered funnel, vented, and left to settle into two layers
2. **What you observe, and what it proves** — the stopcock is opened, the lower (water) layer drains into a beaker, and the tap is closed exactly when the boundary arrives — the oil is caught separately

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Mixtures and how to separate them. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Run or describe Experiment 2.9 in order: fill, stopper, shake, vent, settle.
2. Drain the lower layer through the stopcock into a beaker.
3. Close the stopcock exactly at the boundary so the layers stay parted.
4. Say which mixtures the funnel cannot separate (solutions with no layers).

## What you need
a separatory funnel with stopper and stopcock, oil, water, a beaker, a stand

## If the learner is stuck
- Drains everything into one beaker → the skill is the stop: watch the boundary fall and close the tap the moment it arrives.
- Tries the funnel on salt water → a solution has no layers; there is no boundary to stop at.
