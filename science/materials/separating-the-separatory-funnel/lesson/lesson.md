# Separating mixtures — the separatory funnel

## Do it first
Shake oil and water hard in a bottle. For a moment they blur together — then, watch: they part into two layers again, oil riding on water. They refuse to mix. This lesson parts them cleanly, with the funnel built for exactly this job.

## Sub lesson 1 — Experiment 2.9 — shaking, venting and settling in the funnel
**Aim:** to separate oil and water — two liquids that do not mix — using a separatory funnel (Experiment 2.9)
**Materials:** a separatory funnel with stopper and stopcock, oil, water, a beaker, a stand
**Steps:**
1. Pour oil and water into the separatory funnel and put the stopper in.
2. Shake the funnel gently, then vent it — briefly lift the stopper (or open the tap away from faces) to release pressure.
3. Set the funnel in its stand and leave it until the two liquids settle into two clear layers.
4. Place a beaker under the stem and open the stopcock so the lower layer — the water — drains down.

## Sub lesson 2 — What you observe, and what it proves
**Observation:** The liquids settle into two layers, oil above water. Opened, the stopcock lets the water drain into the beaker; closed exactly when the boundary reaches the tap, it keeps the oil in the funnel, to be caught separately. Timing the close is the whole skill.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the funnel's whole story: shake, vent, settle, drain, stop at the boundary. Your companion asks: what goes wrong if you close the tap too late?

## Do It Yourself
1. Shake oil and water in a clear bottle. Time how long the layers take to part.
2. Practise the boundary judgement with coloured water under oil in a glass: say 'close NOW' at the moment the lower layer would run out.
3. Name two other pairs of liquids this method could part — and one pair it cannot (a solution). Say why not.
