# Lesson plan — Soluble and insoluble substances

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Soluble and insoluble substances
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — appended to Unit 4 (Materials) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** which substances dissolve in water (soluble) and which never dissolve however long you stir (insoluble) — and that dissolving is a physical change, the substance still there — Bhutan's own way, in its Class IV and Class V Science textbooks
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Soluble substances — they dissolve and disappear into the water** — Stir salt or sugar into water and the grains vanish.
2. **Insoluble substances — they stay themselves however long you stir** — Stir sand into water and stir as long as you like: the sand is still sand.
3. **Dissolving is a physical change (Bhutan, Class V)** — Bhutan's Class V book asks: is dissolving a physical change? Yes.
