# Lesson plan — Solids, liquids and gases

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Solids, liquids and gases
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — the states of matter, after the behaviour lessons (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** matter exists in three states: solid keeps shape and volume; liquid keeps volume but takes the container's shape; gas has neither and spreads to fill its container — and heat can change a state without making any new matter
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Describing things — the same matter, now sorted by state, and changed by heat.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Solids keep their shape** — fixed shape and fixed volume: pencil, stone, paper, metal, wood, ice
2. **Liquids take the container's shape — the pouring experiment** — Experiment 1: bottle to bowl — fixed volume, no fixed shape
3. **Gases spread out** — the balloon proof: no fixed shape or volume; air has weight and takes up space
4. **Changes that make nothing new** — physical change: ice to water to vapour; breaking and melting make no new matter

5. **Boiling, melting and the candle — the Grade 4 depth** — boiling/boiling point and melting/melting point defined through water; the candle melting and re-solidifying; the local boiling point measured with a thermometer (adult help)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Describing things: matter and its properties. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three states of matter: solid, liquid, gas.
2. Say the solid rule: fixed shape and fixed volume (pencil, stone, paper, metal, wood, ice).
3. Do the book's pouring experiment and state the liquid rule: fixed volume, no fixed shape — it takes the container's shape.
4. Say the gas rule from the balloon: no fixed shape or volume; it spreads to fill its container, takes up space, and has weight.
5. Say what a physical change is: breaking wood or melting ice changes shape or state, not the substance — no new matter is created; water runs ice → water → vapour with heat.

6. Define boiling, boiling point, melting and melting point through water, and give the candle as a second melt-and-set example (Grade 4 depth).
## What you need
A plastic bottle of water, a bowl and a cup for the pouring experiment; a balloon; ice if available, or the picture of it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the water in the bowl is 'more water' → pour it back into the bottle: same volume, new shape. The rule is shape changes, volume does not.
- Thinks air weighs nothing → the filled ball or balloon is heavier; gas has weight and takes up space, as the book teaches.
- Thinks melting makes new matter → the book's physical-change rule: state changed, substance did not; no new matter is created.
