# Making things — choosing materials

## Do it first
Before you read: look at three things near you — a ruler, a cooking pot, a chair. Each is made of the material it is made of for a reason. Say out loud what would go wrong if each were made of paper. Then read on: this is Iran's way in — its books start every maker with the same two questions: what do I want to make, and what should it be made of?

## Sub lesson 1 — What do I want to make?

Every making starts with the thing itself, named first: Iran's Grade 1 book opens the making lesson by asking "what do I want to make?", and its Grade 2 book has pupils choose something to make by looking around them — the made thing comes first, the material second.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the material that fits the job

A material is chosen because of what the thing must do. A ruler must be straight and stiff; a cooking pot must stand heat and not melt; a shelter must keep out weather and danger — for thousands of years humans used natural materials like wood and stone for shelter, as Iran's Grade 7 book tells it, and choosing still follows the job. Even a chocolate is shaped by choice: Iran's Grade 2 makers draw the shape they want and make a mould for it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Design it, build it, test it

Iran's Grade 6 book runs the full maker's round in its "let us design and build" lesson: look at the kinds there are, decide what yours will do, build it, and watch how it goes. A design is a choosing plan; building tests the choices; what fails sends you back to choose again.

## The key ideas

- Making starts from the thing you want to make, named first — then the material is chosen.
- A material fits a job by its properties: stiff for a ruler, heat-standing for a pot, weather-keeping for a shelter.
- A mould gives a made thing the exact shape its maker chose.
- Designing, building and testing is one round: what fails in the test sends the maker back to choose again.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why is the pot not made of wood?" Answer: because the material must fit the job — a pot must stand heat without burning or melting, and wood would burn; the maker chooses the material by what the thing has to do.

## Do It Yourself
1. Choose a thing to make, name it, and list two materials you could make it from — say which fits its job better, and why.
2. Draw the shape you would give a chocolate of your own, and say what you would make its mould from.
3. Teach the maker's round — want, choose, design, build, test — to someone at home in five sentences.
