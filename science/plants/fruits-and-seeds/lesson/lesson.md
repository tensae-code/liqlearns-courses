# Fruits and seeds

## Do it first
Cut an apple open, in your mind. What do you find at its centre, in a neat row? And why would a plant wrap those things in something sweet and bright? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Albania's way in — its Grade 5 guide teaches fruits and seeds (Frutat dhe farat) as a learning situation of their own, straight after plant reproduction.

## Sub lesson 1 — The fruit holds the seeds

A **fruit** is the part of a flowering plant that holds its **seeds**.

After the flower's work (stocked in `plants/flowers-and-how-plants-reproduce`), the flower's base grows and changes into the fruit, and the seeds form inside it. Apple, bean pod, grain and nut are all fruits in the science sense: coverings built around seeds.

The covering is not decoration. It guards the seeds while they ripen.

## Sub lesson 2 — The seed — the next plant packed

Inside the fruit sits the **seed**: the plant's offspring packed for travel and waiting.

A seed holds the start of a new plant and a store of food for its first growth. When the fruit ripens, the seeds are ready — to be carried away, to land in soil, and, when warmth, light and moisture come, to germinate (germination is stocked in `plants/what-a-plant-needs-to-grow`, where Albania's guide also stocks it). Fruit and seed belong together: the fruit is the packing, the seed is the passenger.

## The key ideas

- A **fruit** is the covering a flowering plant builds around its **seeds** — apple, pod, grain and nut all count.
- The fruit guards the seeds while they ripen.
- A **seed** packs the start of a new plant plus a store of food for its first growth.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the fruit holds the seeds first, then the seed — the next plant packed. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is a bean pod a fruit?" Answer: Yes — in the science sense a fruit is the covering built around seeds, and the pod is exactly that covering.

## Do It Yourself
1. Open three different fruits and draw the seeds inside each. Write how each fruit holds and guards them.
2. Sort a handful of foods into the science's fruits and non-fruits. Defend one hard case out loud.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — fruit as packing, seed as passenger.
