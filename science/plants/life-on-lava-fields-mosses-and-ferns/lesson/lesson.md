# Life on lava fields — mosses and ferns first

## Do it first
A fresh lava field is black, bare rock where nothing grows. Years later it wears a soft green coat. What kind of plant could possibly live first on bare rock, with no soil at all? Say your guess out loud. Then read on: Iceland's guide teaches the lava chapter of its nature book — and the first settlers are mosses and ferns.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mosses and ferns — the first settlers of new lava

New lava is **colonised first by mosses and ferns**. The guide names the moss: **hraungambri, the woolly fringe-moss**, settling on the lava, with ferns beside it — small, seedless plants that can take hold where flowering plants cannot yet live. The seedless plants themselves (mosses, liverworts and ferns as kinds) are stocked in `plants/seedless-plants-mosses-and-ferns`; this lesson's one thing is their work as lava's first settlers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Counting the capsules — the fern frond activity

The guide's lava activity is a counting one: **count the spore capsules on one fern frond**. A fern makes its next generation in those little capsules, and counting them turns the colonising story into a number a pupil holds: one frond, this many capsules, this many chances for new ferns on new ground.

## The key ideas

- New lava is colonised first by mosses and ferns — hraungambri, the woolly fringe-moss, among them.
- Mosses and ferns are small seedless plants that can take hold on bare lava where flowering plants cannot yet live.
- The guide's activity: count the spore capsules on one fern frond.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Mosses and ferns — the first settlers of new lava first, then Counting the capsules — the fern frond activity. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why are mosses and ferns the first settlers, and not trees?" Answer: They are small seedless plants that can take hold on bare rock with no soil — trees need what the first settlers only later help to build.
