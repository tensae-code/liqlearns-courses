# Plants — seedless plants: mosses, liverworts and ferns

## Do it first

A moss cushion and a fern stand side by side in a damp corner. Both are green, neither flowers. Write one way they are the same and one way they differ.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bryophytes — mosses and their kin

Bryophytes — mosses, liverworts and hornworts — are small plants of moist places. They have only root-like, stem-like and leaf-like parts, not true roots, stems and leaves, and they reproduce by spores.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ferns, and the comparison (doing)

Pteridophytes (ferns) have true root, stem and leaves plus vascular tissue — xylem and phloem. The stem runs underground as a rhizome, the leaves are fronds, and the fronds' undersides carry spore patches. Ferns still have no seeds and no flowers. Practical 5.5 compares collected mosses, ferns, junipers and flowering plants trait by trait. Field safety (teacher guide — evidence, item 11, guide printed pp. 84–108): some organisms may sting or bite or carry disease — never collect with bare hands, use gloves or a container, watch where you step, wash hands afterwards, and release every specimen alive and unharmed where you found it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: bryophytes such as mosses are small moist-place plants with only root-like, stem-like and leaf-like parts reproducing by spores, while ferns have true roots, stems, leaves and vascular tissue — and still no seeds or flowers. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
