# Keeping local seeds and adapted varieties

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's subject — keeping local seeds and adapted varieties. Then read on: this is Honduras's way in — its *Ciencias Naturales* books teach this thing at the places named in Sources, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 22

Lesson 22: seed kinds — traditional seed selected and saved by farmers each year; hybrid seed from crossing varieties (DICTA's improved hybrids: Dicta-660 rice, VMR sorghum, rust-resistant Ihcafé-90 and Lempira coffee, Dicta Sequía maize, Carrizalito and Deorho beans) whose seed must be bought every sowing and needs agrochemicals; and transgenic (GM) seed made in laboratories, whose consequences science cannot yet fully predict.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why save creole (criolla) seeds

Why save creole (criolla) seeds: bought or gathered once, self-sufficiency, strong resistant plants selected year by year, stronger flavour, and endless diversity of size, shape and colour — Honduras's dry-corridor trial of Brazilian heat-resistant vegetable seed in Choluteca, Valle and Comayagua opens the lesson.
