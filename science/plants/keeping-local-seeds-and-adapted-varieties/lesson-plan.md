# Lesson plan — Keeping local seeds and adapted varieties

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Plants · **Lesson:** Keeping local seeds and adapted varieties
**Place in the curriculum:** Plants — appended to Unit 8 (Plants) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Lesson 22: seed kinds — traditional seed selected and saved by farmers each year; hybrid seed from crossing varieties (DICTA's improved hybrids: Dicta-660 rice, VMR sorghum, rust-resistant Ihcafé-90 and Lempira coffee, Dicta Sequía maize, Carrizalito and Deorho beans) whose seed must be bought every sowing and needs agrochemicals; and transgenic (GM) seed made in laboratories, whose consequences science cannot yet fully predict.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
1. **Lesson 22** — Lesson 22: seed kinds — traditional seed selected and saved by farmers each year; hybrid seed from crossing varieties (DICTA's improved hybrids: Dicta-660 rice, VMR sorghum, rust-resistant Ihcafé-90 and Lempira coffee, Dicta Sequía maize, Carrizalito and Deorho beans) whose seed must be bought every sowing and needs agrochemicals; and transgenic (GM) seed made in laboratories, whose consequences science cannot yet fully predict.
2. **Why save creole (criolla) seeds** — Why save creole (criolla) seeds: bought or gathered once, self-sufficiency, strong resistant plants selected year by year, stronger flavour, and endless diversity of size, shape and colour — Honduras's dry-corridor trial of Brazilian heat-resistant vegetable seed in Choluteca, Valle and Comayagua opens the lesson.
