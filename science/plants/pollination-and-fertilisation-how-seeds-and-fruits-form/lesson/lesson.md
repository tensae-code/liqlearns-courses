# Pollination and fertilisation — how seeds and fruits form

## Do it first
A flower opens — and later there is a fruit full of seeds. What happened in between? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Sudan's way in — its Grade 6 Science teaches sexual reproduction in flowering plants as two steps: pollination, then fertilisation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pollination — pollen reaches the stigma

**Pollination** is pollen carried to the **stigma**. The carriers, as Sudan's book gives them, are **wind, insects, water and humans** — and the journey is either in the **same flower** or **across flowers**, from one flower to another.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fertilisation — the pollen grain joins the ovule

**Fertilisation** follows: the **pollen grain joins the ovule inside the ovary**. Pollination brings the pollen to the stigma; fertilisation is the joining itself, inside the ovary — the two steps are different, and the order never swaps.

## Sub lesson 3 — Then seeds and fruits form

After fertilisation, the **ovary grows into the fruit** and the **fertilised ovules inside it become the seeds**. The fruit the plant carries is the grown ovary, and every seed in it was an ovule that a pollen grain joined.

## The key ideas

- **Pollination — pollen reaches the stigma** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Fertilisation — the pollen grain joins the ovule** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Tell pollination and fertilisation apart." Answer: Pollination is pollen carried to the stigma — by wind, insects, water or humans, in the same flower or across flowers. Fertilisation is the pollen grain joining the ovule inside the ovary, after which the ovary becomes the fruit and the ovules the seeds.
