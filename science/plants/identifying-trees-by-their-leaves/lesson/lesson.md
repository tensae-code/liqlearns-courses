# Identifying trees by their leaves

## Do it first
Two trees stand side by side in a wood. No flowers, no fruit — only leaves. Could you name which tree is which? What would you look at? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Iceland's forest chapter sends pupils out with a tree key to do exactly that.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forest trip — naming trees where they stand

On a **forest trip**, trees are named where they stand — not from a picture in a book, but from the living tree in front of you. The part the guide trusts for the naming is the **leaf**: leaf by leaf, the species gives itself away.

## Sub lesson 2 — The tree key — Ég greini tré

The naming is done with a **tree-key handbook, Ég greini tré** ("I identify trees"): a key asks about the leaf step by step, and each answer narrows the trees down until one species is left. Key in hand, leaf in the other — that is Iceland's way of teaching tree identification.

## The key ideas

- Trees on a forest trip are named where they stand, by their leaves.
- A tree key (Ég greini tré) asks about the leaf step by step, narrowing the trees down to one species.
- Leaf in one hand, key in the other — Iceland's way of identifying trees.
