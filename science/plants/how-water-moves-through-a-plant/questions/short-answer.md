# How water moves through a plant — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** Trace water's whole journey through a plant, part by part.
**Answer:** In through the roots from the soil, up along the stem to the parts above ground, and out through the leaves into the air.

**2.** What evidence shows the water climbing the stem?
**Answer:** A plant standing in coloured water carries the colour up its stem — the colour marks the water's road for the eye to follow.

**3.** Why call the plant a through-road for water?
**Answer:** Because water passes through it on a journey — soil to sky — entering at one end (the roots) and leaving at the other (the leaves), with the stem as the road between.

**4.** What would happen to the leaves if the roots could take in no water?
**Answer:** No water would climb the stem to supply them: the journey starts at the roots, so a plant cut off there cannot keep its leaves supplied.
