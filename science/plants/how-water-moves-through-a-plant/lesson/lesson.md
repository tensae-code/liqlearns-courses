# How water moves through a plant

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — water's journey through a plant: in through the roots, up along the stem, and out through the leaves into the air. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — In through the roots, up along the stem

A plant drinks through its **roots**: water in the soil enters the root and begins a journey upward. China's Grade 3 book follows the water in its balsam lessons: from the roots the water travels **up along the stem**, the plant's own rising path, toward every part above ground.

The journey is not a guess — the book's own tests show it: a plant standing in coloured water carries the colour up its stem, marking the water's road for the eye to follow.

## Sub lesson 2 — Out through the leaves into the air

The journey ends at the **leaves**. Water that has climbed the stem leaves the plant through its leaves, passing **out into the air** — the plant is a through-road for water, from soil to sky.

In, up, out: roots take in, stem carries up, leaves let out. The whole movement is one journey, and it runs whenever the plant lives — the silent traffic that keeps every leaf supplied.

## The key ideas

- Water enters the plant **through the roots** and travels **up along the stem** — coloured water standing in the stem proves the road.
- The journey ends at the **leaves**, where water passes **out into the air**.
- The plant is a **through-road for water**: in at the roots, up the stem, out at the leaves — soil to sky.

## Do It Yourself
1. Stand a pale-stemmed plant or leafy stalk in coloured water (with an adult's knowledge) and draw it after some hours: where has the colour climbed?
2. Draw a whole plant and mark water's journey with three arrows — in, up, out — labelling the part each arrow passes through.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how soil water ends up in the air.
