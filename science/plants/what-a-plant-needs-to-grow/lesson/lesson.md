# What a plant needs to grow

## Do it first
Four tins. Four bean seeds, sown the same morning. One tin has nothing. One has soil but no water. One has water but no soil. One has both. A week later, only one plant is standing tall. This lesson runs the book's great experiment — and the tins teach the needs themselves.

## The four needs and the four tins

What does a plant need to grow? The book answers with four: **soil, water, air and sunlight**. First each need is tested by picture comparisons of seedlings in tins. Then the **full class experiment**: bean seeds sown **the same day** in **four tins** — one **empty**, one with **soil only**, one with **water only**, one with **soil + water** — all given **equal sun and air**, their growth **observed and reported**.

Read the tins at the end. The empty tin: nothing to stand in, nothing to drink. Soil only: a stand, but thirst. Water only: a drink, but no stand and no food from soil. Soil + water, with sun and air over all: the plant. No single need is enough — growth is the four together.

## Say it to your companion
Set your companion before your four tins mid-experiment. Make them predict the finishing order and defend it need by need. At the end, let the tins mark their answer.

## Do It Yourself
1. Run the full experiment: label the tins, sow the same day, give equal sun and air, water only where water belongs. Record growth every two days.
2. Give a houseplant the four needs as a checklist audit: soil? water? air? light? Fix the one it is missing.
3. Sow two extra seeds: one in soil + water kept in darkness (the Light lesson's tin). Say before it grows which need it lacks.
