# Lesson plan — Fallen leaves feed the soil; plants hold the soil

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Plants · **Lesson:** Fallen leaves feed the soil; plants hold the soil
**Place in the curriculum:** Plants — the last plant lesson in this band (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fallen leaves and grass rot into the topsoil and raise its fertility; plants stop soil erosion, shelter wild animals and give recreation — and cutting and clearing plants does the opposite harm
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What a plant needs to grow — soil was the first need; this lesson shows where rich soil itself comes from: from plants.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the rot cycle: fallen leaves and grass rot into the topsoil and raise fertility.
2. Say how plants hold the soil: roots grip it, cover shields it, so erosion is stopped.
3. Name plants' other shelters: they shelter wild animals and give recreation.
4. Say the harm of cutting and clearing plants.

## What you need
A handful of leaf litter and the dark soil under a tree; a slope to look at in rain if the season gives one.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sweeps every leaf as 'dirt' → the book teaches the opposite ledger: fallen leaves are the soil's food; sweeping them away starves the topsoil.
- Thinks erosion is only the river's work → rain on bare soil is the thief here; plants are the lock.
