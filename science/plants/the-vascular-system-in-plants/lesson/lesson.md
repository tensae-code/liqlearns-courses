## Sub lesson 1 — Pipes inside the plant

A plant has transport pipes inside it, and together they are its **vascular system**. The **xylem** carries water and dissolved minerals upward, from the roots through the stem to the leaves. The **phloem** carries food made in the leaves to every other part — down to the roots, up to the flowers and fruits.

Kuwait's Grade 7 book teaches the vascular system as the plant's answer to a size problem: a tall plant's roots and leaves are metres apart, and diffusion alone could never join them. The pipes are the joining.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two pipes, two directions

The two pipes are told apart by what they carry and which way it flows: xylem — water, upward, roots to leaves; phloem — food, both ways from the leaves to wherever it is needed or stored.

Cut a stem and the pipes can be found in bundles; stand a white flower or a celery stalk in coloured water and the xylem shows itself, carrying the colour up into the petals and leaves — the book's own demonstration, and this lesson's hands-on.

## The key ideas

- The vascular system is the plant's transport pipes.
- Xylem carries water and minerals up from roots to leaves.
- Phloem carries food from the leaves to all parts.
- Coloured water rising in a stem shows the xylem at work.
