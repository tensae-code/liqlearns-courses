Today: **The vascular system in plants** — Kuwait's way: the vascular system in plants — the xylem carrying water up from the roots and the phloem carrying food from the leaves to every part.

A plant has transport pipes inside it, and together they are its vascular system. The xylem carries water and dissolved minerals upward, from the roots through the stem to the leaves. The phloem carries food made in the leaves to every other part — down to the roots, up to the flowers and fruits. Kuwait's Grade 7 book teaches the vascular system as the plant's answer to a size problem: a tall plant's roots and leaves are metres apart, and diffusion alone could never join them. The pipes are the joining. The two pipes are told apart by what they carry and which way it flows: xylem — water, upward, roots to leaves; phloem — food, both ways from the leaves to wherever it is needed or stored. Cut a stem and the pipes can be found in bundles; stand a white flower or a celery stalk in coloured water and the xylem shows itself, carrying the colour up into the petals and leaves — the book's own demonstration, and this lesson's hands-on.
