# Lesson plan — Plants that heal

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Plants · **Lesson:** Plants that heal
**Place in the curriculum:** Plants — the medicinal gift (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the local medicinal plants the Grade 3-level list names — garlic, tena adam, dama kese, haregresa, feto, grawa, moringa (shiferaw), kebericho, tosign, eret, gizawa, ginger — with garlic easing coughs and colds as the book's example; pupils bring local medicinal plants to class
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Kinds of plants and their parts — the healer must first know the plant exactly; a wrong plant is no medicine. Medicine at home follows elders and health workers; this lesson teaches the book's list and its respect.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Plants — Kinds of plants and their parts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the medicinal plants the book lists: garlic, tena adam, dama kese, haregresa, feto, grawa, moringa (shiferaw), kebericho, tosign, eret, gizawa, ginger.
2. Give the book's worked example: garlic eases coughs and colds.
3. Bring a local medicinal plant to class, as the book assigns, and tell what the elders use it for.

## What you need
A brought plant (with an elder's leave and name for it); garlic and ginger to see and smell.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats the list as permission to dose → the lesson teaches the book's list and respect for it; using medicine belongs to elders and health workers, and the learner's task is knowing and naming, not dosing.
- Mixes the names up → learn them on the plant, by leaf and smell, the way the book's bring-to-class activity does.
- This lesson's next door: Fallen leaves feed the soil — the plant's gift that continues after it falls.
