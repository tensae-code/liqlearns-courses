# Plants that heal

## Do it first
Garlic is for the pot — and for the cough. Ginger is for the tea — and for the cold. Our area's plants are a pharmacy that grows. This lesson learns the book's list of healing plants, with respect: these are elders' knowledge, written into the school book.

## The healing list of our area

The book's list of our area's **medicinal plants**: **garlic, tena adam, dama kese, haregresa, feto, grawa, moringa (shiferaw), kebericho, tosign, eret, gizawa and ginger**. These are the plants the community reaches for in sickness, and the book brings them into the classroom: pupils **bring local medicinal plants to class** and learn them by sight and smell.

Its worked example is the kitchen's own: **garlic eases coughs and colds**. The lesson's posture is the book's posture — this knowledge lives with the elders first; the school honours it by naming it exactly, plant by plant, and by knowing its limits: naming is the learner's work; healing is the elders' and the health workers'.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion five names from the list with the plant, picture or smell for each. Your companion gives the book's example back to you: which plant eases coughs and colds?

## Do It Yourself
1. Bring-and-tell: bring one medicinal plant from home (with leave), name it, and report what your elders use it for — the book's own assignment.
2. Smell school: garlic, ginger, moringa leaves — learn each by smell with eyes shut, as healers learn them.
3. Garden corner: with a teacher, mark where moringa or another listed plant grows near school or home, and learn its parts on the living plant.
