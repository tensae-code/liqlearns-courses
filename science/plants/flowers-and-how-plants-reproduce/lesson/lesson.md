# Flowers — and how plants reproduce

## Do it first
A plant cannot walk to another plant, and it cannot pick up its babies and plant them. Yet every year new plants like it appear. Which part of the plant does that work? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Albania's way in — its Grade 5 guide gives plant reproduction (Riprodhimi i bimëve) its own learning situation, built on the flower.

## Sub lesson 1 — The flower's parts

The **flower** is the plant's seed-making part, and it is built of parts with jobs.

Outside sit the **petals**, the flower's coloured flags. Inside stand the **stamens**, which make the fine dust called **pollen**, and the **pistil**, in whose base the future seeds are held. Naming the parts — petal, stamen, pistil — is the first half of the lesson: reproduction has machinery, and the flower is it.

## Sub lesson 2 — From pollen to seed

Reproduction runs in order. **Pollen** must travel from a stamen to a pistil — carried by wind or by visiting insects — and that travelling is **pollination**.

When pollen arrives, the making of seeds can begin inside the pistil's base; the seeds formed there are the plant's offspring, each carrying the start of a new plant like its parent. Flower first, pollen's journey second, seed last: that road is how a flowering plant reproduces.

## The key ideas

- The **flower** is the plant's seed-making part: petals outside, **stamens** making **pollen**, the **pistil** holding the future seeds.
- **Pollination** is pollen travelling from stamen to pistil, by wind or by insects.
- After pollination, seeds form — each seed the start of a new plant like its parent.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the flower's parts first, then from pollen to seed. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A bee leaves a flower dusty with pollen and flies to another flower. What job is it doing?" Answer: Pollination — carrying pollen from stamen to pistil, the journey reproduction depends on.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find a flower and draw it. Label petal, stamen and pistil, and write the job of each in one line.
2. Watch a flower for ten minutes: record every visitor and say what job the visitor might be doing.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how a flower makes seeds.
