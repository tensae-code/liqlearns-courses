# Hands-on — Indigenous plants of The Bahamas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The wild-plant walk
Walk a short route you know well — a roadside, a shore path, a piece of wild ground. Count the flowering plants you pass that are growing wild, not in a planted bed. Choose one, and stand with it for five full minutes: flower, leaf, stem, fruit if there is any, and the ground under it.

## The description card
On paper, write the chosen plant's description card: flower (colour, size, arrangement), leaf (shape, size, edge), stem (woody or soft, how tall), place (the ground and setting, exactly). No name is needed if you do not know it — the description is the science.

## The finding test
Trade description cards with a partner (or give yours to someone at home). Each person must find the other's plant from the card alone. Where the finder hesitates, the card — not the finder — is wrong: add the missing feature and write the card again.
