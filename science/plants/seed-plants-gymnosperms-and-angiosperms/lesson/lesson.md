# Plants — seed plants: gymnosperms and angiosperms

## Do it first

Hold a pine cone and an apple in your mind. In one, the seeds sit open; in the other, hidden inside fruit. Which plant is which kind? Decide before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The plant plan and gymnosperms

Plants make their own food by photosynthesis — they are autotrophs. They stay in one place, their cells have cellulose walls, and they reproduce with or without seeds. Gymnosperms bear naked seeds, often in cones; they have no flowers and are mostly trees — pine and juniper. A field enquiry studies Ethiopia's indigenous gymnosperms: their habitats, uses and protection.

## Sub lesson 2 — Angiosperms — flowering plants

Angiosperms are the flowering plants. Their seeds are enclosed in fruit. They are the most diverse plant group and supply nearly all human plant food — teff, corn, grasses, fruit trees and roses among them.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: plants are photosynthesising autotrophs with cellulose cell walls; seed plants split into gymnosperms with naked seeds, often in cones, and angiosperms whose seeds are enclosed in fruit. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
