# Living things live where their needs are met

## Do it first
Before you read: a spruce tree grows in the forest, and a tulip grows in a garden bed. Swap them in your mind — the tulip deep in the dark forest, the spruce in the small flower bed. Would each one do well in the other's place? Why, or why not? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Sweden's way in — its copy pages open with a two-page garden-and-forest scene and one question: "Var ska växterna vara?" — *Where should the plants be?*

## Sub lesson 1 — Each plant has its place

The plants around us **do not all live in the same place**.

Some belong to the **forest**: the spruce and pine, the wood anemone and liverleaf on the forest floor, the bilberry and lingonberry bushes. Some belong to the **garden**: the tulip, rose and daffodil grown for beauty, the apple and pear trees, the strawberry bed.

This is not an accident of the picture. **A living thing lives where its needs are met.** The forest plant finds in the forest the light, space, soil and company it needs; the garden plant is planted and cared for where its needs are met in the garden. Put a plant where its needs are not met, and it does not thrive.

## Sub lesson 2 — Placing the cut-outs — prove it by sorting

Sweden's proof of this idea is done **with the hands**. The cut-out plants from the sheets — the trees (spruce, pine, apple tree, oak, birch, pear tree), the berry bushes and flowers — are stuck, one by one, into the garden-and-forest scene, **each plant in the place it belongs**.

Every placing is a small judgement: name the plant, name its place, and stand by it. A plant placed in the wrong side is not a decoration mistake — it is the idea itself got wrong, because the lesson's rule is the placing rule: **living things live where their needs are met**.

The cut-out sheets themselves are stocked where they belong in this course — the trees and flowers with `plants/kinds-of-plants-and-their-parts`, the berry bushes with `plants/plants-give-us-things-and-food` — and this scene is where they are used.

## The key ideas

- **Living things live where their needs are met** — each plant has its place: forest plants in the forest, garden plants in the garden.
- Sweden teaches it as a **sorting task**: cut-out plants stuck into a two-page garden-and-forest scene, each in the place it belongs — "Var ska växterna vara?" (Where should the plants be?).
- A plant put where its needs are **not** met does not thrive — the wrong placing is the lesson's own test.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — each plant has its place first, then the placing of the cut-outs. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why does the spruce belong in the forest?" Answer with its needs, not by repeating the picture.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw your own two-place scene: garden on one side, forest on the other.
2. Cut out four plants and stick each in its place, saying its name and its place out loud as you place it.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — why a living thing lives where its needs are met.
