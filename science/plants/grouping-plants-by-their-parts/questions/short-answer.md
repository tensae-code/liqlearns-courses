# Grouping plants by their parts — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Grouping by roots.
**Answer:** Papua New Guinea's Grade 3 textbook groups plants first by their **roots**: look at the root a plant has, and plants with the same kind of root go in one group. The root is underground, so the grouping starts by carefully looking at the whole plant, not only the part above the ground.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Grouping by stems.
**Answer:** The same plants can be grouped again by their **stems**: the stem a plant has — its thickness, its strength, whether it stands or climbs — puts it in a different group from the root grouping. One plant can belong to a root group and a stem group at the same time, because each grouping answers a different question.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Grouping by leaves — and a flower collection.
**Answer:** The third grouping is by **leaves**: leaf shape and form sort the plants a third way. The Standards Based Grade 3 guide extends the sorting to flowers and stems too, and sets a My Flower Collection — its background notes name Papua New Guinea plants such as **hibiscus, bougainvillea, orchid and aloe vera** — the country's own plants are the material the grouping is practised on.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Grouping by roots → Grouping by stems → Grouping by leaves — and a flower collection — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
