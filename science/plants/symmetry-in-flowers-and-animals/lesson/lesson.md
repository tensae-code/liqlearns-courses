# Symmetry in flowers and animals

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's one thing — symmetry — matching halves — read first in flowers and then in animal shapes, in the two readings of Iraq's Grade 5 pupil's book. Say what you already know about it out loud. Then read on: this is Iraq's way in — its Ministry of Education *Science* books (the Primary Science Book Series) teach this thing at the places named in this lesson's sources.

## Sub lesson 1 — Symmetry in flowers

After its flowering and non-flowering plant lessons, Iraq's Grade 5 book sets a reading: **symmetry in flowers**. A flower shows symmetry when its parts are arranged so that the flower matches itself across a middle — one side the mirror of the other.

The reading teaches the eye before the word: look at the flower, find the middle, and check the matching. Where the halves match, the flower is symmetrical.

## Sub lesson 2 — Symmetry in animal shapes

After its vertebrate and invertebrate lessons, the book sets the matching reading: **symmetry in animal shapes**. The same check, on animals: an animal's shape can match across its middle — the two sides of the body answering each other.

Flower and animal, one idea: symmetry is a pattern of living shapes, and the book reads it in the plant unit and the animal unit alike — the same lesson, two kingdoms.

## The key ideas

- **Symmetry** is matching across a middle — one side the mirror of the other.
- Iraq's book reads symmetry first in **flowers**, then in **animal shapes**.
- One idea in two kingdoms: symmetry is a pattern of living shapes.
