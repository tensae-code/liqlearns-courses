# Forests and what they give

## Do it first
Stand at the edge of a forest — a real one if you can, the picture if you cannot. Birds are nesting in it. Its wood built houses you know. Somewhere in a forest like this, visitors are paying to glimpse a walia. One living crowd does all of that. But first: what IS a plant, exactly? The Grade 4 book defines it before it celebrates it.

## What a plant is — and what the forest gives
**Plants are living things that make their own food, stay in one place, grow and reproduce.** That is the book's definition — four marks: living, food-making, rooted in one place, growing and multiplying.

Gathered as **forests**, plants give people: **building material, paper, furniture** — and they give **shelter to wild animals**. They also earn **tourism income**: visitors come for the rare animals of the forests and parks — the **walia, the red fox, rare birds** — while some forest and grassland animals (the book names **qoqqo, sass and dikula**) give **meat and skins** for shoes and goods.

A forest is not empty land waiting. It is a working crowd of living food-makers, housing animals and employing people who never touch an axe.

## Grade 5 — vegetation zones; forests and the Green Legacy
Natural vegetation (plants that grow without being planted) is distributed chiefly by altitude in Ethiopia: **(1) mountain vegetation** above 3,000 m; **(2) forests**; **(3) savanna grassland** — tall grasses, acacias and thorny trees, mostly 250–2,300 m; **(4) semi-desert vegetation** — short thorny trees and shrubs that endure drought. **Church forests** are credited with preserving Ethiopia's oldest plant kinds. Forests (450–3,500 m) split into **highland forests** — broadleaf at 1,500–1,800 m (wanza, weyra, blackwood, kerero, bisana, birbra, forest coffee; thick in Ilubabor, Kefa, Wollega) and coniferous **tid and zigba** at 1,800–2,200 m — and **lowland forests** below 1,500 m (Genale, Wabi Shebele and Awash riverbanks). Then the numbers story: fifty years ago **30–40%** of Ethiopia was dense forest; it fell to about **3%** (cleared for farms, fuel, settlement); since the **Green Legacy** programme, cover has recovered to **11–15%**.

## Grade 6 — the region's vegetation; deforestation's numbers; protecting the forest
The region's kinds match: **highland vegetation (1,300–4,000 m)** — bamboo forest at 2,000–2,500 m, zigba, tsid, weyra and koso, asta and jibra above 3,000 m; **savanna grassland (250–2,300 m)** — Kenya's **Masai Mara (about 1,150 km²)** and Tanzania's Serengeti pictured; **desert and semi-desert** where rainfall falls under **250 mm** — xerophyte plants, in Somalia's coast, Kenya's Nyika and Ethiopia's Afar (Chalbi and Guban deserts pictured); and **mangrove/wetland vegetation (ረግረጋማ)** on river and lake shores — greatest in the **Rufiji delta (Tanzania)** and the **Zambezi delta (Mozambique)**. The warning in numbers: **Uganda lost more than half its forest between 1990 and 2015 — cover fell from 24% to 12.4%**, at up to 3% a year; at this rate it loses all its forest within **25 years**. Tanzania's cover could vanish entirely within **50–80 years** without correction. The root cause named: ever-growing population clearing for food, farms, export products and fuelwood. The book's three protections: **afforestation and reforestation; controlling forest fire; protection laws, enacted and enforced** — with the communities near forests playing the key role, national parks serving forest and animal resources together, and the youth taught continuously.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the plant definition — all four marks — and then the forest's five gifts: building, paper, furniture, shelter, tourism.

## Do It Yourself
1. Forest gifts hunt: name one building, one paper thing and one piece of furniture you used today. All three began as forest.
2. Church forest visit: with family, visit a church or mosque forest. List the animals and birds it shelters — the forest's tenants.
3. Definition check: is a mushroom-coloured stone a plant? Is a rooted green shrub that never moves? Test both against the four marks.
