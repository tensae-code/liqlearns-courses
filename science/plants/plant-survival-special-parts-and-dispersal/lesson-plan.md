# Lesson plan — Plant survival — special parts and dispersal

**Course:** Science · **Title:** plants · **Lesson:** Plant survival — special parts and dispersal
**Place in the curriculum:** Plants — appended to Unit 8 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** plant survival — how plants protect themselves from enemies, adapt to climate and season, and spread their seeds and fruit so the species lives on
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Protecting themselves, and suiting the climate** — Malaysia's Grade 5 book teaches how plants **protect themselves from enemies: thorns, stinging hairs, poison, thick bark and bad smells**, each catalogued with examples. Plants also **adapt to climate and season change: waxy leaves (keladi), needle leaves and thick bark in cold climates, and water-storing stems**.
2. **Seed and fruit dispersal — travelling to new ground** — A plant species survives by spreading. The book teaches dispersal **by water** — fruits that float and travel on water; **by animals and humans** — hooked, sticky and eaten fruits; and **by explosive mechanism and by wind**, all compared in a summary chart. The unit's summary and questions gather the protection features and the dispersal ways together.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks a plant's only defence is thorns → return to sub lesson 1: stinging hairs, poison, thick bark and bad smells are defences too.
- Mixes the dispersal ways up → return to sub lesson 2: water, animals and humans, explosion, wind — say one fruit for each.
