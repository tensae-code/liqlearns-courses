Today: **Root kinds — tap, fibrous and storage** — Seychelles' way, from its Integrated Science unit booklets (Unit 4).

Seychelles' Unit 4 Topics 3–4 sort plant roots into two great kinds. A tap root grows one main root downward with smaller roots branching from it; fibrous roots are many thin roots of about the same size, with no single main root. The kind goes with the plant's other parts: most monocots have narrow parallel-veined leaves and fibrous roots, and most dicots have wide net-veined leaves and tap roots.

Roots work for the plant: they hold it in the soil and support it — which is why weeds must be pulled out by their roots, or they grow again. The unit's own example is the banana plant, which has small short roots. Some roots also store food for the plant. Looking at a whole pulled-up plant — leaves, veins and roots together — tells you which kind you have.
