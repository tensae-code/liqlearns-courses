# Plants give us things and food

## Do it first
The table you eat from. The paper you write on. The wall behind you. All of them were growing once. This lesson traces our things and our food back to the plants they came from.

## From plant to table, chair and page

Count the gifts. **Houses, clothes, chairs, tables and paper come from plants** — wood cut and shaped, fibres woven, pulp pressed into sheets. Then **food**: plants feed **people** — cabbage, orange, maize and all the crop groups of the kinds lesson — and plants feed **animals**, the grass and leaves of every grazing beast. And at the smallest scale, **bees and butterflies feed from flowers**, carrying the flowers' dust as they go — the plants feeding even the insects, and the insects serving the plants in return.

## Say it to your companion
Take your companion on the trace-back tour: pick any three made things and walk each one back to its plant. Then three foods. Your companion must name the plant for each, or catch you where you cannot.

## Do It Yourself
1. Room audit: list everything in one room that comes from a plant — wood, paper, cotton, food. The list is longer than anyone expects.
2. Meal trace: take today's meals and trace each dish to its plant. Where a dish has no plant in it, look again — what did the animal eat?
3. Flower watch: sit by a flowering plant for ten minutes. Count its visitors — bees, butterflies — and say what each came for.
