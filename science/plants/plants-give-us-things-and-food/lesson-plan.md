# Lesson plan — Plants give us things and food

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Plants · **Lesson:** Plants give us things and food
**Place in the curriculum:** Plants — the uses begin (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** houses, clothes, chairs, tables and paper come from plants; plants feed people — cabbage, orange, maize — and animals, and bees and butterflies feed from flowers
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Kinds of plants and their parts — the plant known part by part; now its gifts are counted.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Plants — Kinds of plants and their parts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Trace the made things back to plants: a wooden table, a sheet of paper, a house wall.
2. Name plant foods for people: cabbage, orange, maize — and plants as feed for animals.
3. Say who else feeds from plants: bees and butterflies feed from flowers.

## What you need
Your own room to audit: everything in it that began as a plant.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts only food as plant gifts → the book's list starts with things: houses, clothes, chairs, tables, paper.
- Forgets the animals → plants feed animals too, and the flower-feeders — bees and butterflies — are part of the lesson.
