# Seed dispersal — how seeds travel

## Do it first
A plant is rooted in one spot for life. Its seeds, though, are found far away — across the field, down the river, over the hill. How did they get there, with no legs and no one planting them? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Albania's way in — its Grade 5 guide teaches seed shapes and dispersal (Forma e farave; shpërndarja e farave) and proves the flying kind with paper-flyer seed models the pupils make themselves.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why seeds travel

**Seed dispersal** is the travelling of seeds away from the plant that made them.

A seed that simply drops lands in its parent's shade, competing with the parent for light, water and soil. A seed that travels can land in open ground of its own. Dispersal is how a rooted plant still spreads its kind across the land.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shapes fit ways of travelling — and the paper flyers

Seeds travel by their shape. **Winged and parachute seeds** ride the wind; **hooked seeds** catch on fur and clothing; **seeds in tasty fruit** ride inside animals; some seeds **float** on water.

Albania's guide proves the wind road with the pupils' hands: **paper-flyer seed models** — paper seeds shaped like the flying seeds — are made, dropped and compared. The model that spins or glides longest shows what the real seed's shape is for: shape is not decoration, it is the ticket for the journey.

## The key ideas

- **Seed dispersal** is seeds travelling away from their parent — escaping the parent's shade to find open ground.
- Seeds travel by shape: wings and parachutes on the wind, hooks on fur, fruit inside animals, floating on water.
- Albania's proof is made by hand: **paper-flyer seed models**, dropped and compared — the shape is the ticket.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — why seeds travel first, then shapes fit ways of travelling — and the paper flyers. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A seed is shaped like a tiny parachute. How does it travel, and why does that shape help?" Answer: By wind — the parachute holds it in the air longer, so the wind carries it farther from its parent.

## Do It Yourself
1. Collect or draw seeds of three shapes and write the way of travelling each shape fits.
2. Make two paper-flyer seeds with different wing shapes, drop both from the same height, and record which flies longer.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how a seed's shape is its ticket.
