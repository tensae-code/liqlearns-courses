# Wild plants and sown plants

## Do it first
Think of a plant growing where nobody planted it, and a plant in a garden or field that someone sowed. What is the difference in how each came to be there? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 2 *Estudo do Meio* book, inside the plant run, sorts plants into the wild-growing and the sown (Plantas espontâneas e semeadas).

## Sub lesson 1 — Wild plants — growing by themselves

**Wild plants** (plantas espontâneas — spontaneous plants) grow **by themselves**: no one sowed them. Their seeds arrived by wind, water or animals, and where the seed landed and found what it needed, the plant grew. The grass by the path, the tree in open land, the flowers of the field are wild in this sense — they belong to a place without being planted in it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sown plants — planted by people

**Sown plants** (plantas semeadas) are **planted by people**: someone chose the seed and the place, sowed it, and tends it as it grows. The plants of the garden and the field — the family's and the farmer's crops — are sown plants. The sort of a plant, in Angola's Grade 2 way, is read from how it came to be growing there: by itself, or by a sower's hand.

## The key ideas

- **Wild plants** (espontâneas) grow by themselves — nobody sowed them.
- **Sown plants** (semeadas) are planted by people, who choose the seed and the place and tend them.
- The sort is read from **how the plant came to be growing there**, not from how it looks.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A plant is growing by the path and nobody planted it. Which sort is it, and why?" Answer from the lesson: It is a wild plant — an espontânea — because it grows by itself: its seed arrived and grew where it landed, with no sower.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find three plants near home. Sort each one: wild or sown. Say the evidence you used for each.
2. Name one sown plant your family or a farmer nearby tends, and say what the sower does for it.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
