# Life stages of a plant

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the oldest plant you know — a tree, a bush, a plant in a pot. Was it always that size? What was it at the very beginning? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Djibouti's way in — its Grade 3 guide teaches the great stages of a plant's life, and compares them with the stages of a human life.

## Sub lesson 1 — The great stages of a plant's life

A plant's life has **great stages**. It begins — from its seed or other beginning — small and new. It **grows**: stem, leaves, the whole green body building up. It reaches its **full grown form**, the stage at which it flowers and makes its own seeds — the beginnings of the next round. And in time its life completes, as every living thing's does.

Stage is not a small word here: at each great stage the plant looks different, is a different size, and does a different job. Telling a plant's life means naming its stages in order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Compared with the stages of a human life

Djibouti's guide teaches the plant's stages **compared with the stages of a human life** — and the comparison does real work. You know your own stages first-hand: you began small, you grew, you are growing still, toward your full grown form.

Set beside the plant, the pattern shows itself: a life is not one fixed shape but a road of stages. The plant's road and the human road are not the same road — but both are told stage by stage, and each stage prepares the next.

## The key ideas

- A plant's life runs through **great stages** — beginning, growing, full grown form, and the completing of its round.
- At each stage the plant is a different size and shape, doing a different job.
- Djibouti's guide compares the plant's stages with **the stages of a human life** — two different lives, both told in stages.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is a plant always the size it is now?" Answer no, and tell its life in stages — then say how the guide compares it with a human life.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw one plant's life in four boxes: its beginning, its growing, its full grown form, and its own seeds beginning the next round. Label each box with its stage.
2. Write your own life so far in stages, beside the plant's stages. Where do the two lists match in pattern? Where do they differ?
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — a plant's life told in stages.
