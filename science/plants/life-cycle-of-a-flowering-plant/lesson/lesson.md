# Life cycle of a flowering plant

## Do it first
Before you read: hold a dry bean in your mind. Inside it, something is asleep — and given the right things, it wakes, grows, flowers, and makes more beans. What is inside the seed, and what does it ask for before it will start? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Bulgaria's way in — its Grade 4 book follows one bean through the whole circle of a flowering plant's life.

## Sub lesson 1 — The seed and its waking — germination

Bulgaria's Grade 4 book's Lesson 39 opens the **bean seed** and names its parts: the **embryo** — the young plant itself, folded and waiting; the **coat** that protects it; and the **stored food** that feeds it at the start.

Waking the seed is **germination**, and germination has conditions: the seed needs **warmth, moisture and air**. Give all three and the embryo grows out on its stored food; miss one and the seed waits.

## Sub lesson 2 — Growth, flowering and new seeds — the circle closes

The germinated plant **grows**, then **flowers** — and the flowers make **new seeds**, each built like the seed the plant began from: embryo, coat and stored food.

That is why it is a **life cycle** and not a line: the annual plant's life ends by making the beginnings of the next round. Seed → germination → growth → flowering → new seeds → and round again.

## The key ideas

- The bean seed holds an **embryo**, a protective **coat**, and **stored food**.
- **Germination** needs **warmth, moisture and air** — all three.
- The cycle closes: growth → **flowering** → **new seeds**, each the start of the next round.
