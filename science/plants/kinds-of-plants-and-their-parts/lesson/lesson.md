# Kinds of plants and their parts

## Do it first
A wanza tree, a shrub, a tuft of grass. Three sizes — but one plan: every one of them has roots below and leaves above. This lesson sorts plants by kind, then takes one apart, part by part.

## Kinds of plants, and the parts of a plant

Our woreda's plants come in **kinds by size**: **big trees** — grar, warka, eucalyptus, wanza; **medium shrubs**; and **small plants** — grass, teff and their kin.

Take any one of them and it has the same **parts**: the **root** below ground, the **stem** — a **trunk** in a tree — rising up, the **leaf**, and the **flower**; the teacher guide's labelled picture adds the **fruit** where the flower was. Learn the parts on a small plant first, where the whole plant fits in your hands, then find the same parts on the biggest wanza: root, trunk, leaf, flower — the plan does not change with the size.

The crops we grow sort by their use into four groups: **cereals, pulses, garden vegetables and fruits** — the fields of the woreda arranged by what they feed us.

## Grade 5 — seedless plants: algae, fungi, mosses, ferns
Living things group into **five kingdoms**, of which the Grade 5 book treats plants and animals; plants divide into **seedless** plants (reproduction hidden) and **seeded** plants (held for Grade 6). **Algae**: the simplest seedless plants — mostly water-living, with **no true root, stem or leaf**, yet able to make their own food. **Fungi cannot make their own food**; they feed as parasites, symbionts or decomposers (saprophytes) — moulds, mushrooms and yeast named; uses: food, medicine, raising bread and injera, returning fertility to soil. **Yeast** is singled out: a **single-celled fungus** that turns sugary food into alcohol and carbon dioxide. **Bryophytes — mosses (ሳረንስቶች) and liverworts (ጉብ እፆች)** — live in damp, shady places, have no true root, stem or leaf, and act as **pioneers**: the first plants on bare ground, holding it against erosion and adding fertility; their life alternates between the large, long-lived, food-making **gametophyte** and the small, short-lived **sporophyte**. **Ferns** have true roots, stems and leaves; under their leaves sit **brown spore cases** holding millions of spores — and like all seedless plants they need water to reproduce. The Grade 5 teacher guide's plant-types subtopic (teacher guide — evidence) pictures the same strip: grasses, shrubs, trees, crops.

## Grade 6 — seeded plants: cones, flowers, pollination, seeds
Seeded plants split by their seed's covering. **Gymnosperms (ግልብ ዘር — "naked seed")** bear visible seed with **no flower and no fruit**; their reproductive organ is the **cone (ቅንብብ)**; mostly tall woody trees with thin, evergreen leaves, never food plants — tsid, zigba, pine, cypress. **Angiosperms (ክንንብ ዘር — "covered seed")** carry seed **inside a fruit** and reproduce through a **flower**; they are the great food sources and the most widespread plants on Earth — eucalyptus, sensel, mango, maize, teff, noug. The **flower** is the sexual organ, in four ring-arranged parts: **sepals (አበባ አቃፊ)**, **petals (መልካ አበባ)**, the male **stamen (ወንዴ ጽጌ)** and the female **pistil (ሴቴ ጽጌ)**. **Pollination** is pollen moving from anther to stigma — **self-pollination** within one plant's flowers, **cross-pollination** between different plants — by agents living (insects, birds, mammals) or non-living (water, wind). After fertilisation the **seed** forms and the **fruit** is the covering around it; seeds sort by their food leaves (cotyledons) into **two-cotyledon** seeds (broad bean, pea, chickpea, lentil) and **one-cotyledon** seeds (maize, sorghum, wheat, barley).

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion to three plants. Name each one's kind by size, then take the smallest apart in words: root, stem, leaf, flower. Your companion checks your parts against a real plant.

## Do It Yourself
1. Collect a fallen leaf, a twig and a flower (fallen, not picked). Lay a whole plant's portrait with them and label every part.
2. Crop sorting at home: list what your family grows or buys and sort it into the book's four groups — cereals, pulses, vegetables, fruits.
3. Measure three plants near home — tallest, middle, smallest — and record their kinds. The tape can be a string.
