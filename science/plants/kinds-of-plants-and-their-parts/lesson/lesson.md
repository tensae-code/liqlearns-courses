# Kinds of plants and their parts

## Do it first
A wanza tree, a shrub, a tuft of grass. Three sizes — but one plan: every one of them has roots below and leaves above. This lesson sorts plants by kind, then takes one apart, part by part.

## Kinds of plants, and the parts of a plant

Our woreda's plants come in **kinds by size**: **big trees** — grar, warka, eucalyptus, wanza; **medium shrubs**; and **small plants** — grass, teff and their kin.

Take any one of them and it has the same **parts**: the **root** below ground, the **stem** — a **trunk** in a tree — rising up, the **leaf**, and the **flower**; the teacher guide's labelled picture adds the **fruit** where the flower was. Learn the parts on a small plant first, where the whole plant fits in your hands, then find the same parts on the biggest wanza: root, trunk, leaf, flower — the plan does not change with the size.

The crops we grow sort by their use into four groups: **cereals, pulses, garden vegetables and fruits** — the fields of the woreda arranged by what they feed us.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion to three plants. Name each one's kind by size, then take the smallest apart in words: root, stem, leaf, flower. Your companion checks your parts against a real plant.

## Do It Yourself
1. Collect a fallen leaf, a twig and a flower (fallen, not picked). Lay a whole plant's portrait with them and label every part.
2. Crop sorting at home: list what your family grows or buys and sort it into the book's four groups — cereals, pulses, vegetables, fruits.
3. Measure three plants near home — tallest, middle, smallest — and record their kinds. The tape can be a string.
