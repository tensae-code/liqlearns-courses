Today: **The flower and the fruit** — Nicaragua's way, from its book (Nicaragua, Grade 9 — *Ciencias Naturales, Noveno grado*, printed pp. 36–39).

Nicaragua's Grade 9 book teaches the flower's **four whorls**: the **calyx of sepals** (gamosepalous when the sepals are joined), the **stamens free or fused** (monadelphous, diadelphous and more), and the rest of the flower's plan. It sorts flowers **by ovary position — epigynous, perigynous, hypogynous** — and names the **inflorescences: spike, raceme, umbel, panicle** — the ways flowers are gathered on a plant.

The same book teaches the **fruit as the transformed ripe ovary**: **dry indehiscent fruits** (achene, samara, the caryopsis of wheat, rice and maize), **dehiscent** fruits (capsule, legume, silique), **aggregate fruits** (strawberry) and **multiple fruits**. Inside is the **seed — the angiosperms' main dispersal organ** — holding the embryo, the cotyledons and the testa (seed coat).

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
