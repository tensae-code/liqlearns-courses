# Wetland plants — sedges and horsetails

## Do it first
At a wetland's edge stand grasses, sedges and horsetails, all green, all upright, all easy to confuse at a glance. A botanist sorts them without hesitation. What do you think she looks at first? Say your guess out loud. Then read on: Iceland's wetland chapter teaches the sorting itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sedges — telling them from other plant kinds

**Sedges** are one of the wetland's plant kinds, and the chapter teaches **telling sedges apart from other plant kinds** — knowing a sedge when you meet one at the wet ground's edge, not lumping every green upright plant together as 'grass'.

## Sub lesson 2 — Horsetails — and vetrarkvíðastör

**Horsetails** are the second kind the chapter sorts out from the rest. It names a horsetail relative of the wet ground: **vetrarkvíðastör**, the wintergreen horsetail relative **with its long seed spikes** — the named plant by which the sorting is practised.

## The key ideas

- Sedges are a wetland plant kind, told apart from other plant kinds — not lumped in as 'grass'.
- Horsetails are a second wetland kind, sorted out from the rest in the same way.
- The chapter's named example: vetrarkvíðastör, the wintergreen horsetail relative with long seed spikes.

## Say it to your companion
