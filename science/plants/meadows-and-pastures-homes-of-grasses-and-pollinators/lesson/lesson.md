# Meadows and pastures — homes of grasses and pollinators

## Do it first
Stand at the edge of a grass field in spring. What lives there besides the grass? Flowers, and over the flowers, wings. Say one winged visitor you would expect. Then read on: this is Serbia's way in — meadows and pastures.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meadows and pastures — grass lands at work

**Meadows** and **pastures** are the grass lands: meadows are kept as **hay meadows**, grass grown and cut for hay, and pastures are grass land where animals graze. One grass community, two uses — cutting and grazing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Medicinal plants of the meadow

Among the meadow grasses grow **medicinal plants** — plants people gather for healing. The meadow is not grass alone: its useful plants are part of what the community gives.

## Sub lesson 3 — Butterflies and bees — the pollinators

Over the meadow flowers fly **butterflies and bees**, and their visiting is work: they **pollinate the flowers** as they move from flower to flower. No pollinators, no new flowers — the winged visitors hold the meadow's future.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Does this part name the grass land, a plant it gives, or an animal that works in it?" Answer: The grass land itself is meadow and pasture; the medicinal plants are what it gives people; the butterflies and bees are the pollinators whose visiting lets the flowers make new plants.
