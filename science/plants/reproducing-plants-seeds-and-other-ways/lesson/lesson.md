# Reproducing plants — seeds and other ways

## Do it first
Before you read: think of a plant you have seen growing somewhere new — in a crack, in a pot, in a garden row. How did it start there? From a seed — or some other way? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Djibouti's way in — its Grade 3 guide's Module 3 teaches the different ways to grow a new plant, seed and non-seed together.

## Sub lesson 1 — The seed way

The way everyone knows first is the **seed**. A plant makes seeds, the seeds are sown, and — with what a seed needs to germinate — each seed starts a new plant of the same kind.

The seed way is real, and Djibouti's guide teaches it. But the module's title is wider than the seed: it is about **the different ways to grow a new plant** — and the seed is only the first of them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Other ways — bulb and cutting

A plant can also develop **without starting from a seed**. Djibouti's guide names the other ways its pupils meet: from a **bulb** — the packed underground store that sends up a new plant — and from a **cutting** — a piece cut from a living plant that, planted and cared for, grows into a whole new plant.

So reproduction in plants is not one road but a family of roads: seed, bulb and cutting all end at the same place — a new plant — but they start from different beginnings.

## The key ideas

- A new plant can grow from a **seed** — the seed way.
- A new plant can also grow **without a seed**: from a **bulb**, or from a **cutting** — a piece of a plant that grows whole.
- Seed, bulb and cutting are one family of ways: different beginnings, the same end — a new plant.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Can a new plant only start from a seed?" Answer no, and name the guide's other ways: bulb and cutting.

## Do It Yourself
1. Plant two beginnings side by side if you can: a seed in one pot, and a cutting (a piece of a plant that grows easily) in another. Watch both for two weeks and write what each one does.
2. Find a bulb — an onion will do. Look at it closely and write how a new plant could start from it without any seed.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the ways a new plant can start.
