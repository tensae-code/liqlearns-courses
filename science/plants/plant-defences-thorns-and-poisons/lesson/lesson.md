# Plant defences — thorns and poisons

## Do it first
A sheep walks up to two bushes. One is soft and leafy; the other is covered in sharp thorns. Which one gets eaten? Say your answer and your reason out loud. Then read on: Iceland's grassland chapter teaches the thorn as a plant's defence — and notes how rare that defence is on its own island.

## Sub lesson 1 — Thorns — a defence against grazers

Plants grown **with thorns are unpopular with grazers**: the thorn is a **defence** — a sharp guard that makes the plant costly to eat, so grazing animals pass it by for softer food. A plant cannot run; the thorn is how it stands its ground.

## Sub lesson 2 — Few such wild species in Iceland

The guide adds Iceland's own note: **few such wild species grow in Iceland**. The thorn defence, common in warmer grazing lands, is rare in the island's wild flora — a country's plant defences read off its own plant list, which is how the guide teaches it.

## The key ideas

- Thorns are a plant defence: plants grown with thorns are unpopular with grazers.
- A plant cannot run — the thorn is how it stands its ground against being eaten.
- Few such thorn-defended wild species grow in Iceland.
