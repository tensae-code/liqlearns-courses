# School and family gardens — growing your own food — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** What do Honduras's books teach about a huerto (garden plot) defined?
**Answer: a huerto (garden plot) defined: a usually fenced piece of land for growing vegetables, fruit trees, medicinal plants or forest nurseries; vegetables (hortalizas) are small, delicate, fast-growing plants, most harvested between one and five months, and important in the diet for their high nutritive value, especially vitamins.**

**2.** What do Honduras's books teach about the three garden types?
**Answer: the three garden types: urban gardens (in soil or in containers, preferably reusing disposable packaging; in homes, terraces, balconies, gardens, abandoned places and public parks and squares), family gardens (a home area for the family's daily vegetables and fruits — they favour self-consumption, family unity, healthy eating and care of the environment) and school gardens (a small plot at the school; city schools build them on large cultivation tables, and where there is no land, balconies, terraces, used tyres or boxes serve)**

**3.** What do Honduras's books teach about the FAO's five benefits of school gardens?
**Answer: the FAO's five benefits of school gardens: (1) health and education — students who do not eat well grow badly, achieve less, suffer anaemia and leave school early, while gardens improve the diet; (2) fruits and vegetables rich in vitamins and minerals raise the nutritional value of school meals; (3) learning — students learn sowing, germination and growth directly, gaining farming skills, self-sufficiency and a way to earn a living; (4) entrepreneurial aptitudes — gardens can generate resources and microenterprises for selling and conserving produce; (5) environment — they conserve soil, protect the environment and use recyclable materials.**

**4.** Name this lesson's one thing.
**Answer: School and family gardens — growing your own food.**
