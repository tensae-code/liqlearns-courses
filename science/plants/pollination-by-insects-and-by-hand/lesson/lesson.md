# Pollination — by insects and by hand

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — pollination — by insects and by hand. Then read on: this is Seychelles' way in — the National Institute of Pedagogy's Integrated Science unit booklets (Unit 6).

## Sub lesson 1 — Insects carry the pollen

Seychelles' Unit 6 Topic 3 defines pollination: pollen reaching the stigma of a flower. Insects do the carrying without knowing it — bees and moths visit flowers to collect nectar, and the pollen's hooked grains catch in their fur, ride to the next flower, and rub off on its stigma. The flower feeds the insect; the insect pollinates the flower.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pollinating by hand

People can do the same job by hand: for pumpkin or passion fruit flowers, pollen is picked up on a brush and placed on the stigma. Hand pollination is used when insects are few or a grower wants the cross made for certain. Either way the event is the same — pollen on a stigma — and without it there is no fruit and no seed from that flower.
