Today: **Vision defects and correcting them** — Kuwait's way: defects of vision — short sight and long sight, where the eye's image falls in front of or behind the retina — and how lenses correct them.

Seeing works when the eye's lens focuses the image exactly on the retina. In short sight (myopia), the image of distant things falls in front of the retina: far things blur, near things stay clear. In long sight (hyperopia), the image would fall behind the retina: near things blur first. Kuwait's Grade 9 book teaches the defects right after its Eye and Vision lesson, inside the physical-sciences unit but general in content: the eye is an optical instrument, and like any instrument it can be out of focus. Each defect has its correcting lens. Short sight is corrected by a concave (diverging) lens, which spreads the rays so the image moves back onto the retina. Long sight is corrected by a convex (converging) lens, which gathers the rays so the image moves forward onto the retina. Glasses and contact lenses are those correcting lenses worn at the eye. The lesson's rule is the optician's rule: test the eye, name the defect, choose the lens that puts the image where it belongs — on the retina.
