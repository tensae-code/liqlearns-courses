## Sub lesson 1 — When the image misses the retina

Seeing works when the eye's lens focuses the image exactly on the **retina**. In **short sight** (myopia), the image of distant things falls in front of the retina: far things blur, near things stay clear. In **long sight** (hyperopia), the image would fall behind the retina: near things blur first.

Kuwait's Grade 9 book teaches the defects right after its Eye and Vision lesson, inside the physical-sciences unit but general in content: the eye is an optical instrument, and like any instrument it can be out of focus.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lenses put the image back

Each defect has its correcting lens. Short sight is corrected by a **concave** (diverging) lens, which spreads the rays so the image moves back onto the retina. Long sight is corrected by a **convex** (converging) lens, which gathers the rays so the image moves forward onto the retina.

Glasses and contact lenses are those correcting lenses worn at the eye. The lesson's rule is the optician's rule: test the eye, name the defect, choose the lens that puts the image where it belongs — on the retina.

## The key ideas

- Short sight: distant image falls in front of the retina; far things blur.
- Long sight: image would fall behind the retina; near things blur.
- Concave lenses correct short sight; convex lenses correct long sight.
- Glasses are correcting lenses chosen by testing the eye.
