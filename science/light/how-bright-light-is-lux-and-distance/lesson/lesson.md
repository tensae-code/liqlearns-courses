# How bright light is — lux and distance

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — the brightness of light — measured in lux with a luxmeter, falling as you move away from the source, and compared across a candle, a lamp and an LED. Hold it in your mind, say what you already know about it out loud. Then read on: this is North Macedonia's way in — its Grade 5 book teaches this thing at printed pp. 142–143.

## Sub lesson 1 — Brightness has a measure — the lux

Light can be brighter or dimmer, and North Macedonia's Grade 5 book gives that brightness a measure: the **lux**, read with a **luxmeter**. Brightness stops being a feeling ('this seems bright') and becomes a number two people can agree on, because both can read the same meter in the same place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Further from the source, dimmer

The book's second step: **brightness falls as you move away from the source**. The same lamp lights the page close to it strongly and the far corner weakly — the light spreads out as it travels, so each step away takes some of the brightness with it. The luxmeter proves it: read near, walk away, read again, and the number drops.

## Sub lesson 3 — Candle, lamp and LED — and where brightness matters

The book compares sources — a **candle**, a **lamp** and an **LED** — by **reading distance**: how far away you can still read by each one's light. And it closes with where brightness is a working tool, not a curiosity: in **greenhouses**, where plants are grown under measured light, and in **film work**, where a scene must be lit to a chosen brightness. One measure, the lux, runs through all of it.
