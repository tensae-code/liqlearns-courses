# Seeing colours — why things look the colour they do

## Do it first
Why is a yellow ball yellow — and what colour would it look through green glass? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Sudan's way in — its Grade 6 Science teaches seeing coloured bodies in its Light unit, by what each body lets pass, takes in and reflects.

## Sub lesson 1 — The disc gathers the colours back into white

Spin a disc painted with the **seven colours** and they **gather back into white**: the colours that a prism splits white light into (the lesson before this one) are all still there in the spinning disc, mixing in the eye back into white.

## Sub lesson 2 — See-through bodies — the colour they let pass

A **see-through coloured body looks the colour of the light it lets pass**: the glass holds back the other colours and passes its own, so what reaches the eye is the colour that passed through.

## Sub lesson 3 — Solid bodies — the colour they reflect

A **solid body looks the colour it reflects**: a **yellow ball takes in every colour and reflects yellow**; **white bodies reflect all colours**; **black bodies take all in and reflect none**. The book's test: a **red ball looked at through red, green and blue glass in turn** — each glass passes only its own light, so the ball's look changes with the glass.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why does a yellow ball look yellow?" Answer: It takes in every colour and reflects yellow — a solid body looks the colour it reflects, as white bodies reflect all colours and black bodies reflect none.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the two rules from memory — see-through bodies: the colour they let pass; solid bodies: the colour they reflect — then check.
2. Predict, then say, what a red ball looks like through red glass, green glass and blue glass in turn, using the two rules.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
