# The pinhole camera — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: A box, a tiny hole and a screen.
**Answer:** Eritrea's Grade 6 book (printed p. 68) teaches the **pin-hole camera** as its own topic, inside its light run — after eclipses and before reflection. The camera is the simplest picture-maker there is: a closed box, dark inside, with one tiny hole in one side and a screen — plain or translucent paper — facing the hole on the inside of the opposite side. Point the hole at a bright scene and the picture appears on the screen, made by light alone: no lens, no film, nothing between the scene and the screen but the hole and the dark.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: The upside-down picture.
**Answer:** The picture on the screen is **upside down** — and left-right swapped — and the book's light run has already given the reason: light travels in straight lines. A ray from the top of the scene passes straight through the hole and lands low on the screen; a ray from the bottom lands high. Because every ray goes straight and they all cross at the hole, the picture arrives turned over. The pinhole camera is the straight-line rule you can hold in your hands — and the ancestor of every camera since.

**Answer:** A box, a tiny hole and a screen → The upside-down picture — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
