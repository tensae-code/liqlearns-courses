# Lesson plan — Sources of light: lamps people make and light from nature

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Sources of light: lamps people make and light from nature
**Place in the curriculum:** Light — after the passing and blocking lessons (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** light comes from two families of sources: natural — sun, moon and stars; and human-made — kuraz lamp, bulb, candle, torch, fanos — and the learner sorts any source into its family
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Light and dark and the two material families — the learner knows light can be present or absent; this lesson asks where it comes from.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the human-made sources: kuraz lamp, bulb, candle, torch, fanos.
2. Ask parents how people got light and fire in the old days, as the book assigns.
3. Name the natural sources: sun, moon and stars.
4. Sort a mixed list of sources into natural and human-made, in a table, without a mistake.

## What you need
The light sources of your own home to list; an older person to ask about light in the old days.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls the moon a light-maker like the sun → the book lists moon and stars among nature's light sources as they appear to us; keep the book's own sort: they are the natural family's night members.
- Forgets air's cousin here: a source is not a passer — glass passes light but makes none.
- The guide's light-properties content (rays travelling in straight lines) rides in the sources as evidence; the shadow lesson already shows it in action.
