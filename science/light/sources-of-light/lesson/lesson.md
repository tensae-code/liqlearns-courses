# Sources of light: lamps people make and light from nature

## Do it first
When the sun goes down, your home still has light. Who made it? And the light of the day — who made that? Every light has a source, and sources come in two families. This lesson sorts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lamps people make

People make light. The Grade 2 book's human-made list: the **kuraz lamp**, the electric **bulb**, the **candle**, the **torch**, and the **fanos** lantern. Behind them stands an older story the book sends children home to collect: **how people got light and fire in the old days** — asked of parents. Every lamp on the list is an answer to the same problem the old-days methods solved: darkness, pushed back by something people kindle, carry and switch.

## Sub lesson 2 — Light from nature, and the great sorting

Nature's sources are the **sun**, the **moon** and the **stars**: the sun rules the day, the moon and stars the night. The book's closing task is a **sorting**: a mixed list of sources laid into a two-family table — **natural** on one side, **human-made** on the other. Do it until it is easy: sun — natural. Torch — human-made. Stars — natural. Bulb — human-made. The sort is the lesson: every light you will ever see belongs to one family or the other.

## Say it to your companion
Lay the two families before your companion: five human-made, three natural. Then quiz them with mixed names — fanos? stars? bulb? moon? — until the sorting is instant.

## Do It Yourself
1. The old-days enquiry: ask a parent or grandparent how people got light and fire before bulbs came to your area. Bring back one method and tell it in class words.
2. Night inventory: after dark, walk your home and list every source that is on. Sort the list into the two families.
3. Draw the same village twice: by day under the sun, by night under moon and stars and lamp-light. Label every source.
