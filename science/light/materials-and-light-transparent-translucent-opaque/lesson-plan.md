# Lesson plan — Materials and light — transparent, translucent, opaque

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Materials and light — transparent, translucent, opaque
**Place in the curriculum:** Light — Unit 7, Physical Phenomena, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a semi-guided investigation — predict first, then devise your own test — sorts everyday materials into the three classes the sorting itself discovers: opaque (nothing through), translucent (some through, blurred) and transparent (through clearly, a medium for light)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Light — sources, and the straight-line rule: light travels straight until it meets a material; now materials are sorted by what they do to it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts by what the material IS instead of what light DOES → the classes are about light passing: test, do not guess from the name.
- Calls frosted glass opaque because no picture is seen through it → hold it to the torch: light comes through, blurred — that is translucent.
