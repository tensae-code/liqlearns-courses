# Materials and light — transparent, translucent, opaque

## Do it first

Here is a tray of ordinary things — a drink bottle, cardboard, newspaper, grease, a CD case, wood, tissue, a cement bag, chalk, a spoon, a stone. A torch will shine at each one. Before any shining: write your prediction for every item — all the light through, some through, or none? Your predictions are part of the work; keep them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Predict, then devise your own test

This is a semi-guided investigation: the question is given, the method is yours. Decide as a group how you will test each material fairly — same torch, same distance, same way of judging what comes through — then test every item on the tray and record what actually happens beside your prediction. Where prediction and result disagree, the result wins, and the disagreement is the interesting part: write why you think you predicted wrong.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three classes your sorting discovers

The tested materials fall into three classes:

- **Opaque** — lets **nothing** through: a wall, wood, cardboard, stone.
- **Translucent** — lets **some** light through, but blurred: bathroom glass, water, tissue, grease-spotted paper.
- **Transparent** — lets light through **clearly**: air, window glass — and so a transparent material can serve as a **medium** for light, the stuff light travels through.

Sort the whole tray into the three classes from your own results, and be ready to defend any item a classmate sorts differently — by re-testing it, not by arguing louder.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three classes with one tested example of each, and the one item where your prediction was wrong and what the test showed.

## Do It Yourself

1. Home sort: find five materials at home (curtain, cup, bottle, book cover, plastic bag). Predict, test with a torch at night, sort. Bring the list.
2. The grease test: put one drop of oil or grease on newspaper. Hold it to the light. Which class does the spot join, and why?
3. Design question: a bathroom window must let light in but stop anyone seeing through. Which class must its glass be, and why do the other two classes fail the job?
