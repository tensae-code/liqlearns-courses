# Waves and how they carry energy

## Do it first
Australia's Grade 9 book spent its last chapter on energy — heat, sound and light. Now it opens a whole chapter with one word: waves. What is a wave — and if nothing seems to travel from a loudspeaker to your ear, what does? Say your answer out loud. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Waves — a travelling disturbance

A **wave** is a disturbance that travels. Drop a stone in still water and rings spread out from the splash: the rings move, while the water itself mostly stays where it is, bobbing up and down as each ring passes. That is the wave's signature everywhere it appears: something travels — and what it travels through stays, on the whole, in place.

## Sub lesson 2 — How waves carry energy

What travels in a wave is **energy**. The stone's splash gives energy to the water, and the rings carry that energy outward across the pond — energy that can rock a floating leaf far from the splash, though no water flowed there to push it. Carrying energy from place to place, without carrying the material along, is the wave's one job.

## Sub lesson 3 — Waves around us — the step back

The book's own order is the frame: its energy chapter met heat (4.1), sound (4.2), light (4.3) and hearing and seeing (4.4); the waves chapter now steps back and names what sound and light travel as — **waves**. From here the chapter goes on to the visible spectrum and to radiation of low and high frequency: all of it waves, sorted. Australia's way is to meet the travellers first, then step back and name the travelling.

## The key ideas

- **Waves — a travelling disturbance** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **How waves carry energy** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Waves around us — the step back** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What travels in a wave — the water, or something else?" Answer: Energy travels; the water mostly stays, bobbing as the wave passes — the wave carries energy, not material.
