# Low- and high-frequency radiation around us

## Do it first
The radio plays, the phone connects, the sun warms — and can also burn. Australia's book sorts the radiation around us by one measure: frequency. What could 'low' and 'high' frequency radiation mean? Say your answer out loud. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Radiation sorted by frequency

**Frequency** is how often a wave repeats in a second. Radiation — energy travelling as waves — comes in a whole range of frequencies, and Australia's book sorts that range into two great families: **low-frequency radiation** (5.3) and **high-frequency radiation** (5.4). The sorting itself is the first idea: one family of waves, divided by how fast they repeat.

## Sub lesson 2 — Low-frequency radiation — the long, gentle waves

Low frequency means the wave repeats slowly, and its waves are long. This is the radiation of everyday signals and warmth around us — the low end of the range, carrying the least energy in each wave. Australia's book teaches it first (5.3), as the home end of the range: the radiation we live inside all day.

## Sub lesson 3 — High-frequency radiation — the short, energetic waves

High frequency means the wave repeats very fast, and its waves are short — and a high-frequency wave carries more energy than a low-frequency one. That is why this end of the range is treated with more care: the same family of waves, at high frequency, is strong enough to do harm as well as good. Australia's book closes the pair with it (5.4): low and high are not two different things, but two ends of one range, told apart by frequency alone.
