# Lesson plan — Low- and high-frequency radiation around us

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Low- and high-frequency radiation around us
**Place in the curriculum:** Light — appended to Unit 5 (Light) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** radiation sorted by frequency into its two families — low-frequency radiation (5.3) and high-frequency radiation (5.4) — and what tells them apart, Australia's own way in its Grade 9 Pearson Science 9
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Radiation sorted by frequency**
2. **Low-frequency radiation — the long, gentle waves**
3. **High-frequency radiation — the short, energetic waves**

## Before this lesson
- No required first lesson. Recommended, never forced: the neighbouring Light lessons that touch the same ground.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Radiation sorted by frequency.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Low-frequency radiation — the long, gentle waves.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: High-frequency radiation — the short, energetic waves.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes the two families up → return to the one measure: frequency — repeats slowly is low, repeats fast is high, and fast carries more energy.
