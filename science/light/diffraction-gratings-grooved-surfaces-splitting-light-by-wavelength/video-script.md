Today: **Diffraction gratings** — how a surface covered in tiny grooves takes light apart.

A CD looks silver, but its surface is a forest of fine grooves, spaced about as finely as light's own wavelength. Light bouncing off it comes back as thousands of copies — one from every groove.
And copies of a wave play a sorting game: for each colour — each wavelength — there is an angle where all the copies arrive in step and blaze out strongly. A different colour, a different angle.
So white light fans into a spectrum — not by bending, as in a prism, but by teamwork among the grooves. You meet it with a torch and a CD in a dark room — exactly as a pupil practical runs it.
