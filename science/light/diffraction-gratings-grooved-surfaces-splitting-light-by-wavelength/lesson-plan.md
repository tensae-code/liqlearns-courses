# Lesson plan — Diffraction gratings — grooved surfaces splitting light by wavelength

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Diffraction gratings — grooved surfaces splitting light by wavelength
**Place in the curriculum:** Light — after Dispersion — splitting and remixing white light (CURRICULUM.md Unit 5): the second instrument that takes white light apart, and the sharper one
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a surface ruled with thousands of fine, evenly spaced grooves — a diffraction grating (a CD is one) — splits light differently from a prism: each groove reflects or passes the light, the many copies interfere, and each wavelength is sent out strongly in its own direction, so white light fans into a spectrum ordered purely by wavelength

## Sub lessons
1. **A forest of grooves — many copies of the same wave** — a grating is a surface covered in parallel grooves spaced on the scale of a wavelength of light; light falling on it is reflected (or passed) by every groove at once, so one incoming wave becomes thousands of copies, slightly offset from groove to groove — and copies of a wave can strengthen or cancel each other.
2. **Each wavelength its own angle — the CD rainbow** — for one particular direction the copies of one wavelength all arrive in step and reinforce; a different wavelength comes into step at a different angle: tilt a CD under a torch and the reflected colours sweep across the wall — white light fanned out, wavelength by wavelength, exactly as Finland's pupil practical observes.
3. **Gratings beside prisms — two ways to take light apart** — a prism splits light by bending — each colour refracted by a different amount; a grating splits it by interference — each wavelength reinforced at its own angle; gratings spread the spectrum wider and order it strictly by wavelength, which is why the instruments that read the light of stars and lamps are built around gratings.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Dispersion — splitting and remixing white light** and **Light — a wave you can see**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a diffraction grating is and why a CD works as one.
2. Explain the splitting in wave terms: many groove-copies of the light, reinforcing only where they arrive in step.
3. Say why each wavelength leaves at its own angle, and why tilting the CD changes the colours you see.
4. Contrast grating and prism: interference against bending, and name the ordering both agree on — by wavelength.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A CD or DVD (one you may take into the dark with a torch), a torch or phone light, a darkened room and a pale wall; paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: a plain silver disc, a torch, a dark room — and a rainbow on the wall. The disc did that; the rest of the lesson is how.
- Thinks the CD is painted with colours → tilt it: the colours move and change with the angle. Paint does not do that; an angle-dependent wave effect does.
- Confuses the grating with a prism → the prism bends light inside glass; the grating never enters the light — its grooves copy the wave many times, and the copies sort the wavelengths by where they reinforce.
