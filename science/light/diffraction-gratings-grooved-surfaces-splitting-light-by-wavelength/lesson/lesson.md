# Diffraction gratings — grooved surfaces splitting light by wavelength

## Do it first
Before you read: darken the room. Take a CD — an ordinary music disc you are allowed to use — and shine a torch at it, catching the reflection on a pale wall. Colours appear. Tilt the disc slowly: the colours sweep and shift. The disc is not painted; nothing was projected through it. Write down, before reading on, what you think the disc's surface did to the light. Then read on: its surface is covered in grooves finer than you can see — and they are a machine for sorting light.

## Sub lesson 1 — A forest of grooves — many copies of the same wave

A **diffraction grating** is a surface ruled with a great number of fine, straight, **evenly spaced grooves** — thousands of them. A CD is a grating you already own: its music is stored along a spiral track whose turns sit side by side like grooves, spaced on the scale of a **wavelength of light** itself — hundreds of nanometres apart.

That spacing is the whole trick. When light falls on the grating, **every groove reflects it** (in a see-through grating, every slit passes it). One incoming wave leaves as **thousands of copies**, one from each groove, each copy slightly offset from its neighbours. And waves that travel together obey a rule you met in the wave lessons: copies arriving **in step** — crest on crest — **reinforce** each other and grow strong; copies arriving **out of step** cancel. The grating's question, for every direction, is: for which wavelength do the copies arrive in step *here*?

## Sub lesson 2 — Each wavelength its own angle — the CD rainbow

The answer changes with direction, and that is the spectrum. For any one wavelength there are particular angles at which the groove-copies all arrive in step and the light blazes out strongly. A **different wavelength** — longer or shorter — comes into step at a **different angle**. So the grating does not send white light out as one beam: it fans it out, **each wavelength at its own angle**, violet at one edge of the fan, red at the other, every colour between in wavelength order.

That is your Do it first. The torch's white light struck the CD's grooves; the wall received the fan — colours thrown wide because a wall catches what a glance at the disc only hints at. And when you tilted the disc, you changed the angles, so the colours swept: which wavelength arrives in step toward your eye depends on the geometry, and tilting rewrites the geometry. The pupil practical built on exactly this is simple: groups in a darkened room, a torch, a CD, and the colours on the wall — then the answer that names the disc a **diffraction grating**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gratings beside prisms — two ways to take light apart

You already own one light-splitter: the **prism**, from the dispersion lesson. It splits by **bending** — white light enters glass, and each colour is refracted by a slightly different amount, so the fan opens inside the bending. The grating splits by **interference** — the light never enters anything; the grooves copy the wave, and the copies sort the wavelengths by where they reinforce.

Both end at the same order — short wavelengths at one end, long at the other — but the grating spreads its spectrum **wider and more evenly, strictly by wavelength**. That is why the instruments that must read light precisely — picking apart the light of a lamp, a star, a distant galaxy to learn what it is made of — are built around gratings, not prisms. The prism showed you white light is a mixture; the grating is the mixture's measuring ruler.

## The key ideas

- A **diffraction grating**: a surface of many fine, evenly spaced grooves — a CD's track makes it one — spaced on the scale of a wavelength of light.
- Each groove returns a **copy** of the wave; copies **reinforce** where they arrive in step and cancel where they do not.
- Each **wavelength** reinforces at **its own angle** — so white light fans out into a spectrum ordered purely by wavelength.
- Tilting the grating changes the geometry, so the colours **sweep**.
- Prism: splits by **bending**. Grating: splits by **interference** — wider, evenly ordered, the scientist's measuring ruler.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the CD rainbow to your companion in your own words: the grooves, the copies, and the angle each wavelength chooses. If your companion can say how a grating's trick differs from a prism's, the lesson has landed.
