# Using wave patterns to send information

## Do it first
Two sailors, too far apart to shout, flash lights at each other in patterns — and a message crosses the water. No object travelled between them. What carried the message? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 4 book teaches information transfer as a design problem solved with wave patterns.

## Sub lesson 1 — Patterns can carry information — light signals and codes

A wave on its own just repeats; a **patterned** wave — flashes long and short, in an agreed order — can stand for letters and words: **light signals and codes** (Grade 4, 4.3.3, printed p. 126). The information is not in the light itself but in the pattern imposed on it, and the receiver who knows the code can read the message off the pattern.

## Sub lesson 2 — Designing a solution to an information-transfer problem

Utah's task is a design task: **design a solution to an information-transfer problem using wave patterns** — choose the wave, design the code, and make the pattern clear enough to be read at the far end. The solution is judged the way all designs are: does the message arrive, correctly, where it must?

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Where does the information live in a light-signal message — in the light, or somewhere else?" Answer: In the pattern imposed on the light — the agreed code of flashes. The light carries it; the pattern is it.
