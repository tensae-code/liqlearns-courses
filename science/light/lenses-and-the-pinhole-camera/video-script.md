Today: **Lenses — and the pinhole camera** — Papua New Guinea's way, from its Department of Education science books (see lesson/sources.md for the exact books and pages).

Papua New Guinea's Standards Based Grade 7 guide teaches the **convex lens** first by its properties and function: a lens thick in the middle, which gathers light passing through it and can form a picture of what stands before it. The lens's job is done by its shape.

Beside the lens, the guide sets the **pinhole camera**: a box with one tiny hole, which also forms a picture — with no lens at all. The two are taught together so the learner can compare them: the lens gathers light to make its picture; the pinhole lets through only a thin thread of light from each point.

The guide closes with **applications of convex lenses** — the places lenses are put to work, in instruments that magnify and in cameras. The run is: what the lens is, the camera with no lens to compare it against, then where lenses serve.
