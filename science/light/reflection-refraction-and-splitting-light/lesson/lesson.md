# Reflection, refraction and splitting light

## Do it first
A pencil in water looks snapped in two. A coin hides in an empty cup — and walks into view as the water rises. Light is not lying; it is bending. This lesson names the bend, the bounce and the split.

## Reflection and its two laws
On a smooth, shiny surface most light bounces back. The geometry is named: the **incident ray (እርፍ ጨረር)**, the **reflected ray (ጽብርቅ ጨረር)** and the **normal line**, with the angle of incidence and angle of reflection measured from the normal. **Experiment 7** tests it with a mirror, and the two laws are stated: **(1)** the incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in **one plane**; **(2)** the **angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection**.

## Refraction — light bends at a boundary
Crossing the boundary between two transparent media changes light's speed, and so **bends its direction**. Two activities make it visible: a **coin hidden at the edge of an empty coffee cup comes into view when the cup is filled with water**, and two identical pencils — one in a water-filled glass, one in an empty one — look different seen from the side: the wet one looks **broken at the water line**.

## Dispersion — white light is seven colours
**White light is a mixture of seven colours.** A cardboard **Newton's disc** painted blue, red and green spins fast and looks whitish; a **prism** splits sunlight into the colours because each colour's speed changes by a different amount across the boundary; the **rainbow** is set as the class question — nature's own dispersion, in the sky.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the two reflection laws, then refraction's two proofs (coin, pencil), then the three dispersion witnesses — disc, prism, rainbow.

## Do It Yourself
1. The coin cup: the book's activity exactly — coin at the cup's edge, step back until it just hides, then have a partner pour water slowly. The coin returns. Whose light bent?
2. Two pencils: one in water, one in air, side by side. Draw exactly what you see at the water line — the lesson's picture, from your own eyes.
3. Disc spin: paint a cardboard disc blue, red and green, spin it fast on a pin. The colours merge toward white — dispersion run backwards.
