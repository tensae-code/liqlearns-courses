# Lesson plan — Reflection, refraction and splitting light

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Reflection, refraction and splitting light
**Place in the curriculum:** Light — what happens where light meets a boundary (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on a smooth surface light reflects under two laws (the rays and the normal in one plane; angle of incidence = angle of reflection, Experiment 7); crossing into another transparent medium it refracts — bends — as the hidden-coin and broken-pencil activities show; and white light disperses into seven colours, by prism, rainbow and the spinning Newton's disc
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Light travels in straight lines — the ray runs straight until it meets a boundary; now the three meetings are named.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State reflection's geometry (incident ray እርፍ ጨረር, reflected ray ጽብርቅ ጨረር, the normal) and its two laws, as Experiment 7 tests them.
2. Explain refraction at a boundary and prove it with the book's two activities: the appearing coin and the broken-looking pencil.
3. Say what dispersion shows — white light is seven colours — by prism, by rainbow and by the Newton's disc spinning to white.

## What you need
A mirror, a coffee cup and a coin, a glass of water and two pencils, a prism if the class has one (or sunlight and water spray for a rainbow), a cardboard disc painted blue, red and green.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says the pencil really breaks in water → lift it out: whole. The light bent at the boundary; the pencil never did.
- Measures reflection angles from the surface → the book measures from the NORMAL — the dashed line standing square to the mirror.
