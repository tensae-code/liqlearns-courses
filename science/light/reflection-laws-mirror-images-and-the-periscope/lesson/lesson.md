# Reflection — laws, mirror images and the periscope

## Do it first

Stand a candle before a mirror. The flame you see in the glass — reach behind the mirror for it. There is nothing there. Yet it has a place, a size and a left-right of its own. This lesson measures the bounce that makes it, states its two laws, tests the image's four properties on your own face, and ends by building the instrument that sees over walls.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two kinds of reflection, two laws

Light rays travel out in all directions; at a boundary some light is absorbed and the rest is turned back — **reflection**. A polished surface gives **regular (specular) reflection**: the reflected rays stay parallel. A rough surface gives **diffuse reflection**: the rays scatter — yet the laws hold for both, because each single ray still meets its own local angle.

On the mirror diagram the vocabulary is fixed: the **incident ray** arriving, the **reflected ray** leaving, and the **normal** — the line standing square to the surface where the ray strikes — with the angles θi (incidence) and θr (reflection) measured from the normal. The two laws: **(1) the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection; (2) the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in one plane.**

## Sub lesson 2 — The mirror image — and the periscope you build

The plane mirror's image is **virtual**: the rays never actually pass through it, so no screen held behind the mirror can catch it. It is **erect**, the **same size** as the object, **laterally inverted** (left and right swapped), and **as far behind the mirror as the object is in front** — test all four on your own face. Lateral inversion is why AMBULANCE is written reversed on the vehicle: a driver ahead sees it in a mirror, where the reversal is undone and it reads correctly (teacher evidence). Cash the laws out in a build: a **periscope** — two mirrors, set parallel at 45° in a tube — bounces light down (or up) so you can see over a wall or round a corner; draw its ray path and explain each bounce by the laws.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two laws, the four properties of a mirror image, and how the periscope's two mirrors each obey the first law.

## Do It Yourself

1. Face test (Activity 7.6): before a mirror, raise your right hand — the image raises its left. Measure: stand 30 cm from the mirror; where does your image stand? Write all four properties with your evidence.
2. Build the periscope (Activity 7.7): two mirrors at 45° in a cardboard tube. See over a wall or a high shelf. Draw the ray path with normals and equal angles marked.
3. Rough or polished? Shine a torch at a mirror and at a sheet of rough paper in a dark room. Where does the bright patch land in each case? Name the kind of reflection each time.
