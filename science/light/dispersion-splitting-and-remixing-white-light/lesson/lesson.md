# Dispersion — splitting and remixing white light

## Do it first

After rain, the sky sometimes paints a bow of colours from plain sunlight. A CD tilted in the sun flashes the same colours. A soap bubble wears them too. Plain white light goes in — colours come out. Where were the colours hiding? Build the answer, then run it backwards.

## Sub lesson 1 — The prism splits white light

Through a **prism**, white light splits into a band of **seven colours — violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red (VIBGYOR)**. The split happens because **each colour refracts by a different amount** — **violet bends most, red least** — so the mixture fans out. The conclusion is the lesson: **sunlight is a mixture** of the seven colours, and the **rainbow is nature's dispersion** — sunlight split by raindrops the way the prism splits it in the hand. Soap bubbles and CDs show the same family of colours for the same reason: white light, separated.

## Sub lesson 2 — Newton's disc — remixing the colours

Run the split backwards as a build: paint a disc in **seven segments**, one for each colour in order, mount it on a pencil or an enzirt, and spin it fast. The seven colours blur together and the disc looks **whitish** — the remix proves the mixture: what the prism took apart, fast spinning puts back together. If your disc looks greyish rather than whitish, check the segments: equal sizes, the true seven colours, in VIBGYOR order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the seven colours in order, which bends most in the prism and why the fan opens at all, and what the spinning disc proves.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build Newton's disc: seven equal segments, VIBGYOR in order, spun fast on a pencil or enzirt. Record what you see at slow, medium and fast spin.
2. CD colours: tilt a CD in sunlight and list the colours you can pick out. Compare your list with VIBGYOR.
3. Rainbow watch: after rain with sun, find a rainbow and write which colour sits on its outer edge and which on its inner edge.
