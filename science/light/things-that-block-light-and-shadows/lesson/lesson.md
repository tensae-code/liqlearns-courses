# Things that block light, and shadows

## Do it first
Stand in the sun. Look down — a dark you follows you on the ground. That shadow is light, blocked by your body. This lesson teaches the blocking family: wall, wood, book — and every shadow ever made.

## Wall, table, book — and every shadow

The blocking family is the Grade 2 book's **ብርሃን ከል (light-blocking)** list: **wall, wooden table, book**. Hold any of them to the light and the light stops — and behind the blocker lies its shadow side.

Two everyday mysteries fall out of this one rule. A **house is dark inside at night** because its walls and roof block light and the sun, its light-maker, is gone. And **standing in the sun makes a shadow** because your body is a blocker: the dark shape on the ground is the sunlight that never arrived. **Leafy trees are shade** for the same reason — a thousand leaf-blockers overhead.

The sun moving makes the shadow move and lengthen: morning's long shadow shortens to midday and stretches out again towards evening — the blockers stand still; the light's angle does the dancing.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion: stand them in sunlight, point to their shadow, and say what made it. Then hold up a book to a torch beam and show the same thing small: blocked light, dark behind.

## Do It Yourself
1. Watch your shadow at morning, midday and late afternoon. The book's shadow lengthens as the sun moves — draw all three.
2. Find shade at midday: stand under a leafy tree. The leaves blocked the light. Name five other shady places and what blocks the light in each.
3. At night, list why your house is dark inside: walls block the outside light, and inside lights are off. Say it in the lesson's words.
