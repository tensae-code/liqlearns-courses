# Bioluminescence — living things that glow

## Do it first
Every light has a source. Lamps are sources people make; the sun, moon and stars are nature's sources. But is every natural source a thing that is not alive? Jordan's Grade 2 book adds an enrichment that breaks that guess: **some living things make their own light**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Living light

**Bioluminescence** (المضيئات الحيوية — the living light-makers) is light made by a living thing's own body. The source is not a lamp someone built, and it is not the sun: the light-maker is alive.

So the families of light sources grow by one. There are the sources **people make** — lamp, bulb, candle, torch. There are nature's sources that are **not alive** — sun, moon and stars. And there are nature's sources that **are alive** — the glowing living things of this enrichment. When you sort a light, ask two questions: who or what makes it — and is the maker alive?

## The key ideas

- **Bioluminescence** is light produced by a living thing's own body.
- A glowing living thing is a **natural** light source — and, unlike the sun, moon and stars, it is **alive**.
- Jordan's book teaches it as an enrichment inside its light unit, right after the light-sources lesson.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: what bioluminescence is, and how a glowing living thing is different from both a lamp and the sun. Your companion may stop you once and ask one question; answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the three families of light sources and one example for each family you have seen yourself.
2. Explain, in two sentences, why a glowing living thing does not belong in the lamps family.
3. Teach someone at home the word bioluminescence and what it means.
