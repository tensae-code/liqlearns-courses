# Lesson plan — Colour mixing — primary and secondary colours

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Colour mixing — primary and secondary colours
**Place in the curriculum:** Light — appended to Unit 5 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** mixing two primary colours (red, yellow, blue) invents a secondary colour; white lightens and black darkens; and a mixed colour cannot be unmixed — Cabo Verde's own way, in its Grade 1 activity guide, is the experiment called "The colour inventor", where the child invents the new colours with plasticine
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Primary colours, and the secondary colours they make** — Red, yellow and blue are the starting colours; mixing two of them invents a secondary colour: orange, green, purple.
2. **White lightens, black darkens — and no unmixing** — White makes a colour lighter, black makes it darker, and once colours are mixed they cannot be unmixed.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: `light/dispersion-splitting-and-remixing-white-light` — the colours inside white light; this lesson is the other road, mixing colours in plasticine and paint. Recommended, never forced.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: plasticine or paint in red, yellow, blue, white and black, where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks a secondary colour is a starting colour → ask which two primaries made it: every secondary colour has two primaries inside it.
