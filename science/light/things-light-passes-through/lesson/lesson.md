# Things light passes through

## Do it first
Shine the torch. At the glass, the light lands on the wall behind it. At the book, it stops dead. Materials greet light in two ways — this lesson teaches the first: the ones that let it through.

## Glass, water, air — and the torch test

Some materials **let light through**: the Grade 2 book names **glass, water and air**. Its test is exact: shut the door and windows, and shine a **torch** through an **unpainted glass window** — the light comes through and shows on the other side. The teacher guide sorts the same behaviour with two family words: materials that transmit light, and materials that block it — the transparent and the opaque. This lesson is the first family: hold the torch to glass, and the beam arrives; the glass is a door for light.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion the test: torch, glass, book. Let them predict before each shine, then explain the result with the words 'passes through' and 'blocks'.

## Do It Yourself
1. Test five things at home with a torch in a dark corner: window glass, water in a glass, a plastic sheet, a book, a wooden door. Record PASS or BLOCK for each.
2. Sit by a glass window at midday. The sunlight in the room crossed the glass to reach you — trace its path with your hand.
3. Find one place where passing light matters: a window. Say why the window is glass and not wood.
