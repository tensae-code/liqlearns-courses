# Light travels in straight lines

## Do it first
Here is a candle, lit by an adult. Look at its flame through this tube — bent like an elbow. Can you see it? Now through the straight tube. There it is. The candle did not move. Your eye did not move. Only the tube changed. What does that tell you about the road light takes?

## Experiment 4 — the straight tube and the bent tube
**Light is a form of energy**, and its road is always the same: **light travels only in straight lines**.

**Experiment 4** is the proof. A candle's light is seen **through a straight plastic tube** — the straight road runs unbroken from flame to eye. Bend the tube, and the flame **cannot be seen**: there is no straight line from the flame through the bend to your eye, and light will not turn the corner.

And this is how seeing itself works: **we see things when light bounces off them into our eyes**. The light strikes the thing, bounces, and runs straight into the eye — the straight-line rule is why you can see at all.

## Grade 6 — Experiment 6: the three-card test
The Grade 6 book proves the straight line with a stricter test. **Experiment 6**: a candle is viewed through **aligned holes in three 20 × 20 cm cardboards** (the alignment checked with a dry thin wire): the flame is visible **only while the holes line up**; shifting the middle card hides it — **light travels in a straight line**. The chapter's frame: light is **energy**; we see by it; it travels in a straight line (a **light ray** marks its path) through transparent media and through vacuum, at different speeds in different media; meeting a body, light can **bounce back, be absorbed, or pass through**.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the experiment and the rule: why the straight tube shows the flame, why the bent tube hides it, and how light reaches your eye from anything you see.

## Do It Yourself
1. Tube test at home: a torch instead of the candle, one straight tube (or a rolled paper tube) and one bent. Repeat the proof and say the rule.
2. Bounce hunt: name three things you can see right now. For each, trace the light's road: source → thing → eye, all in straight lines.
3. Corner problem: can light from the next room reach your eye around a corner with no mirror? Answer with the rule.
