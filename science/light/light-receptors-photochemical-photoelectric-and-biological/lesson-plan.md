# Lesson plan — Light receptors — photochemical, photoelectric and biological

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Light receptors — photochemical, photoelectric and biological
**Place in the curriculum:** Light (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 — Physics expansion) — a new lesson built for the Mauritania placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** light does not only travel and reflect — it is also received. A light receptor is anything that takes light in and answers it with a change, and Mauritania's Grade 8 manual sorts receptors into three families by the kind of change: photochemical (light drives a chemical change), photoelectric (light frees electric charge) and biological (a living sense organ answers)

## Sub lessons
1. **Receptors — where light arrives** — Every light story ends somewhere: light leaves a source, crosses a medium, and is received. A receptor is the receiver — the thing in which light produces an effect. Mauritania's manual teaches receptors as a family of their own, right after the sources: sources give light out, receptors take it in and change because of it. Naming the receiver completes the chain source → journey → receptor that the whole optics unit uses.
2. **Photochemical and biological receptors** — In a photochemical receptor, light's energy drives a chemical change: photographic film darkens where light falls, because the light rearranges its silver salts — the picture is the chemical record of the light. Biological receptors are living: the eye's retina answers light with nerve signals (that is seeing), skin answers with warmth and tanning, and a green leaf answers sunlight with photosynthesis. The change differs, the pattern is one: light in, a transformation inside the receptor.
3. **Photoelectric receptors — light makes electricity** — In a photoelectric receptor, light frees electrons: a photovoltaic cell (solar panel) turns light straight into an electric current, and a photocell changes its conduction when light falls on it — the working receptor inside automatic lamps and alarms. Mauritania's Grade 8 book names solar panels as its photoelectric example: the same sunlight the leaf receives chemically, the panel receives electrically.

5. Sources give light out; receptors take it in — the two ends of every optics story
