# Lesson plan — Sunlight keeps everything alive, and what light is used for

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Light · **Lesson:** Sunlight keeps everything alive, and what light is used for
**Place in the curriculum:** Light — the last light lesson in this band; the plant lessons take it further (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the sun is our main light source: plants make their food with sunlight, animals eat plants, people eat both — without sunlight nothing lives; sunlight also gives heat and sight, dries clothes, and can make electricity
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Sources of light — the sun already named first among nature's sources; this lesson gives its full importance, with the two-tin experiment.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Light — Sources of light: lamps people make and light from nature. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the sunlight chain: plants make food with sunlight → animals eat plants → people eat both → without sunlight, nothing lives.
2. Run Experiment 2: seeds in two tins, one in light, one in darkness, both watered daily; compare growth after days and weeks.
3. Name the guide's other uses of light and sunlight: sight, heat, drying clothes, plants preparing food, solar electricity for lighting, vehicles and water heating, and drawing water up from rivers and lakes.

## What you need
Two tins of soil and bean seeds for Experiment 2; a dark box or cupboard for the second tin; water for daily watering.

## If the learner is stuck
- Waters the tins unevenly and 'proves' nothing → the book's fairness rule: same soil, same seed day, same water; only the light may differ.
- Thinks light is only for eyes → the chain: plants eat light, animals eat plants, people eat both; and the guide adds heat and solar power.
- The uses part rests on the Grade 3 teacher guide — evidence, labelled in the sources; the experiment and the chain are the student book's own.
