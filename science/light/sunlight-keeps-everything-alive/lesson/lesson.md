# Sunlight keeps everything alive, and what light is used for

## Do it first
Turn off the sun for a year, in your mind. First the plants fail. Then the animals that eat them. Then us. Light is not only for seeing — it is the first link of all food. This lesson proves it with two tins of seeds.

## Sub lesson 1 — The sunlight chain and the two-tin experiment

The sun is our **main light source**, and the Grade 3 book gives the reason it matters most: **plants make their food with sunlight; animals eat plants; people eat both — without sunlight, nothing lives.** Light also gives **heat** and **sight**, but the chain is the crown: every meal begins in sunlight.

**Experiment 2** proves the plant link. Sow seeds in **two soil tins** on the same day. Keep one **in light**, one **in darkness**. **Water both daily**, the same. After days and weeks, compare: the light-kept seedling grows green and strong; the dark-kept one stays pale and weak. Same seed, same soil, same water — light was the difference.

## Sub lesson 2 — What light is used for

The Grade 3 teacher guide gathers light's uses in one place. Light comes from natural and man-made sources — the sun, the moon, a lit lamp, a lit candle — and **without light we cannot see things**. **Sunlight is a free source of energy**: it **dries clothes**, lets **plants prepare their food**, and can **generate electricity** for lighting, vehicles and water heating. The guide adds the sky's own work: solar energy also **draws water up from rivers and lakes** — evaporation, in the guide's words — the first step of the rain the weather lessons watch falling.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion the chain link by link, then show them your two tins and let the tins argue: which seedling proves what sunlight does? Your companion states the experiment's rule.

## Do It Yourself
1. Run Experiment 2 fully: sow the same day, water both tins daily, keep one dark. Measure both seedlings weekly and keep the record — the pale, weak one is the argument.
2. Stand in morning sun with wet clothes on a line: name sunlight's three gifts at once — light, heat, drying.
3. Find one solar thing in your area — a panel, a solar lamp, a water heater — and say which of the guide's uses it serves.
