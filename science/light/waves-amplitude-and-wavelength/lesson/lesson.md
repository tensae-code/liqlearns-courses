# Waves — amplitude and wavelength

## Do it first
Shake a rope and a wave runs down it. Shake harder, and the wave looks different; shake faster, and it looks different again. Two different changes — so how are they named and measured? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 4 book models waves in water and rope and describes their regular patterns with two words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Waves have a regular pattern — water and rope models

A **wave** is a **regular pattern** that travels: ripples crossing water, a shake running down a rope (Grade 4, 4.3.1, printed p. 110). The models matter — in water and in rope the pattern can be seen, repeated and compared, and it is the pattern, repeating the same shape again and again, that the lesson describes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Amplitude — how big the wave is

**Amplitude** describes how big the wave's swing is — the height of the pattern from its resting line to its crest. Shake the rope harder and the amplitude grows while the pattern's spacing can stay the same: amplitude is the wave's size, measured on the model.

## Sub lesson 3 — Wavelength — how long the pattern is

**Wavelength** describes how long one full repeat of the pattern is — crest to crest. Shake the rope faster and the repeats crowd closer: the wavelength shortens. Amplitude and wavelength together describe any wave pattern the models make — how big, and how long.

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A rope wave is made taller without changing its spacing. Which measure changed?" Answer: Amplitude — the size of the swing. Wavelength, the crest-to-crest length of one repeat, stayed the same.
