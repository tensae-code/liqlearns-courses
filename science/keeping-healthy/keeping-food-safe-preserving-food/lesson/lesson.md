# Keeping food safe — preserving food

## Do it first
Think of a food your family keeps for many days without it spoiling. How is it kept? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 2 *Estudo do Meio* book teaches preserving food (A conservação dos alimentos) among the food-care lessons, and the Grade 5 *Ciências da Natureza* book teaches it again at its own depth.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why food must be preserved

Food left as it is **spoils**: it changes, smells and tastes bad, and can make us ill. **Preserving food** — the conservation of foods (A conservação dos alimentos) — is the set of ways people hold that spoiling back, so food stays safe and good for longer than the day it was made or harvested. Angola teaches it first in Grade 2, inside the run of lessons about care with food.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ways of preserving food

The ways work by taking away what spoiling needs. Food is **dried** in sun and air, **salted**, **smoked**, kept **cold**, or **covered and stored** away from dirt and insects — each way slows the change that ruins food. The Grade 5 book returns to the same conservation of foods and deepens it: the older child chooses the way that fits the food and the place, and checks preserved food before eating, as with all food care.

## The key ideas

- **Preserving food** holds back spoiling, so food stays safe and good for longer.
- The ways — drying, salting, smoking, keeping cold, covering and storing — work by **taking away what spoiling needs**.
- Angola teaches preserving food in **Grade 2** and returns to it in **Grade 5** at a deeper level.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How do the ways of preserving food work, in one sentence?" Answer from the lesson: Each way takes away something spoiling needs — water, warmth, dirt or insects — so the food changes much more slowly and stays safe longer.
