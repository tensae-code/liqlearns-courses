# Protecting yourself: sexual violence and saying no

## Do it first
Some lessons protect you from germs. This one protects you from a person. The book speaks about it plainly, in print, because silence protects only the attacker. Learn the words, the doors, and the answer — no.

## What it is; causes and effects
**School-related sexual violence** covers sexual, physical and psychological attacks **on the way to and from school as well as at school**; it drives pupils out of school and kills ambition. It is defined as **imposing one's own desire by force on another without consent**. **Causes** listed: social conditions, peer influence, economic conditions, sexual desire, and sometimes cultural support for the act. **Effects**: bodily injury, anxiety, self-isolation, feelings of inferiority, social rejection, unwanted pregnancy, and sexually transmitted infections including HIV/AIDS — with effects reaching the family too. It is committed against both sexes but mostly against females, with early marriage, forced rape, beating and domestic-labour exploitation named among the reasons. The three kinds of gender-based violence at school: **sexual** (unwanted touching, coercion, rape), **physical** (any force that causes pain) and **psychological** (insults, belittling and threats by peers or teachers).

## Worknesh's answer (plot summary only)
Pupil **Worknesh** refuses fellow pupil Addisu's repeated pressure to "be my girlfriend": she has decided to remain a virgin until marriage; if he loved her he would not ask this of her now; her answer is **no**; she wants to finish her education. The book praises her **purposeful, polite escape** from the pressure. (Placed as a plot summary only.)

## Protecting yourself — the book's advice lists
Three printed lists. **Preventing forced rape**: do not move alone in deserted places; trust your inner feeling; do not stay alone with a suspected person; **report suspicion to family, teachers, police, the women-and-children's affairs office or the kebele administration**; do not linger in improper places. **Resisting peer pressure**: do not rush into a boyfriend/girlfriend relationship; respect the opposite sex; resist even close friends' pressure; stay away from friends of bad conduct; **learn to say no**; do not be bought by gifts, money or novelty. **If an attempted rape happens**: do not submit to threats; do not panic; **scream continuously; fight to the end to save yourself**.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the definition, the three kinds, and the five doors — family, teacher, police, women-and-children's office, kebele — then Worknesh's answer in her spirit: firm, polite, final.

## Do It Yourself
1. The five doors drill: write the five reporting doors on five slips. A partner names a worry; you answer with the nearest door. Every worry has a door.
2. Worknesh lines: in pairs, practise a firm polite refusal aloud — 'My answer is no. I want to finish my education.' — until it can be said calmly, looking up.
3. Safe route map: draw your way to school and mark the deserted places the book warns of, and the staffed, busy places. Walk the safe line in your head, then in life.
