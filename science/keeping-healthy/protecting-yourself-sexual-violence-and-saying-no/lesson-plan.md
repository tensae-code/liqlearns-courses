# Lesson plan — Protecting yourself: sexual violence and saying no

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Protecting yourself: sexual violence and saying no
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — safety from violence, in the book's plain words (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** school-related sexual violence is imposing one's own desire by force on another without consent — sexual, physical or psychological; its causes and effects are listed plainly; and the book's protection is practical: do not move alone in deserted places, trust your inner feeling, report suspicion to family, teachers, police, the women-and-children's affairs office or the kebele administration, resist peer pressure, learn to say no — and if an attack is attempted: do not submit to threats, do not panic, scream continuously, fight to save yourself
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What disease is and Accidents and staying safe — harm and its prevention; now harm done by a person, and its prevention.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define sexual violence in the book's words: imposing one's own desire by force on another without consent.
2. Name the three kinds of gender-based violence at school: sexual, physical, psychological.
3. Recite the protection lists: preventing, resisting peer pressure (Worknesh's answer as the model), and what to do if an attempted rape happens — and the five doors to report to.

## What you need
Only the lesson, taught seriously. Any pupil disclosure goes at once to a trusted adult and the school's safeguarding route — the lesson's own reporting list is the route.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks reporting is betraying → the book's list makes reporting a duty of protection: family, teachers, police, the affairs office, the kebele. Silence protects the attacker, never the victim.
- Thinks 'no' must be rude to work → Worknesh's no is praised as purposeful AND polite. Firm is enough.
