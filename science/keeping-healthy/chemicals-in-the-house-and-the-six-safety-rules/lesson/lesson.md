# Chemicals in the house and the six safety rules

## Do it first
Think of your home. The berekina under the sink. The fertiliser sack. The medicines, the insect spray, the cleaning things. Your home is full of chemicals — most useful, some poisonous. The danger is not that they exist. It is how they are handled — and who can reach them. The book answers with six rules.

## Sub lesson 1 — The home's chemical shelf — and the child's special danger
The chemicals live with us: the book names **berekina (bleach)** for cleaning, **fertilisers, food salt, sugar** and the like — and sets pupils a **fifteen-item sorting table**: vinegar (acetic acid), laundry chemicals, berekina, flit insecticide, laundry soap, toothpaste, salt, drinking alcohol, sugar, aspirin, DDT, kitchen-utensil cleaner, glass cleaner, crop-pest killer and cosmetics — marking **which harm through careless handling, and which do not**.

Some cleaning chemicals are **poisonous**. And the book warns of the child's special danger plainly: **children who touch chemicals and put their hands to their mouths poison easily**. That is why chemicals are **kept where children cannot reach**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The six safety rules
The book prints **six safety rules** — learn them as a set:

1. **Buy only what will be used directly.**
2. **Buy only the amount needed for the time.**
3. **Read and follow the caution label on the container before use.**
4. **Handle chemicals with proper care.**
5. **Store them out of children's reach, and dispose of unwanted chemicals carefully.**
6. **Teach children how to handle chemicals safely.**

Six rules: two about buying, one about reading, one about handling, one about storing and disposing, one about teaching. A home that keeps them keeps its chemicals as servants, not dangers.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the child's special danger first — touch, hands to mouth, poison — then the six rules in order, without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Shelf survey (looking only): with an adult, look at where chemicals stand at home. Are they out of small children's reach? Report to the family what the book's fifth rule says.
2. Label reading: with an adult, read one container's caution label aloud together. That reading is rule three, practised.
3. Teach one rule: choose one of the six and teach it to a younger child in the house — rule six is teaching itself.
