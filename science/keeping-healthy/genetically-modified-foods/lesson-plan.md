# Lesson plan — Genetically modified foods

**Course:** Science · **Title:** keeping-healthy · **Lesson:** Genetically modified foods
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — appended to Unit 14 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** genetically modified (transgenic) foods — foods from plants given added genes so they resist pests, herbicides and drought — and the argument about their risks
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Transgenic foods — genes added for resistance** — Nicaragua's Grade 6 book teaches **transgenic foods**: genes are added so plants **resist pests, herbicides and drought**. The common ones it names are **maize, soya, cotton, potato, rice and tomato**.
2. **The argument — health-risk claims and environmental impact** — The same book carries the other side as part of the lesson: **Greenpeace's health-risk claims**, and the **environmental impact of unnatural cross-breeding** — the new combinations do not occur in nature, and what they do to health and to the environment is argued about. The book teaches the foods and the argument together.

## Before this lesson
- No required first lesson. Recommended, never forced: the neighbouring keeping-healthy lessons that touch the same ground.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Transgenic foods — genes added for resistance.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: The argument — health-risk claims and environmental impact.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks transgenic means sprayed with chemicals → return to sub lesson 1: the change is in the plant's genes — genes added so the plant itself resists pests, herbicides and drought.
- Takes one side as the whole lesson → return to sub lesson 2: the book teaches the claims and the impact argument alongside the foods themselves.
