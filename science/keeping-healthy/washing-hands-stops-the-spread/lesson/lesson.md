# Washing hands stops the spread

## Do it first
Before you read: look at your hands. They look clean — but can you see everything on them? Microbes are too small to see. If those hands now touch your mouth, or your food, or a friend's hand, what travels with them? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Peru's way in — its Grade 1 Ciencia y Tecnología ficha, *Las enfermedades y su cuidado* (diseases and their care), closes by making the child explain, from the drawings, how cleanliness and good food prevent disease.

## Sub lesson 1 — Clean hands break the route

Microbes reach our body on **dirty hands** — that is the true route the ficha marks, not clean hands.

**Washing hands** breaks that route. Cleanliness — **aseo** in the ficha's Spanish — removes what the hands are carrying before the hands reach the mouth, the food, or another person. The spread is stopped at the hands, before the disease starts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Good food, and explaining it from the drawings

The ficha names **two preventers together**: **cleanliness (aseo)** and **good food**.

Good food helps the body stay strong. Clean hands keep the microbes from arriving. Together they prevent disease — one guards the door, the other strengthens the house.

And Peru's own closing test is not a written definition: the child **explains from the drawings** how cleanliness and good food prevent disease. If you can point at each drawing and say what it prevents, and why, you have the lesson.

## The key ideas

- Microbes reach the body on **dirty hands**; **washing hands** breaks that route.
- **Cleanliness (aseo)** and **good food** are the ficha's two preventers, named together.
- The proof of the lesson is explaining, **from the drawings**, how those two prevent disease.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — clean hands breaking the route first, then good food. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What does washing stop?" Answer with the route, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Wash your hands now, slowly: wet, soap, scrub palms and between the fingers, rinse.
2. Draw the two drawings: dirty hands reaching for food, and clean hands beside good food. Write one sentence under each.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — explaining from your drawings, as the ficha asks.
