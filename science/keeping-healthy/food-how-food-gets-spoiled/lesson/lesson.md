# Food — how food gets spoiled

## Do it first

Picture a plate of good food left uncovered on a table all afternoon. What could reach it? What could it do to the person who eats it tonight?

## Sub lesson 1 — The causes of spoiling

Food gets fouled and spoiled in printed ways: flies, rats and cockroaches fouling the food; dirty water; dirty utensils; spoiled or expired ingredients; dirty preparation places and dirty hands; and eating raw food that should not be eaten raw.

## Sub lesson 2 — The diseases that follow, and our friend and the doctor

The diseases that follow fouled food are named: diarrhoea, amoeba, typhoid and cholera.

The book's printed story, "Our friend and the doctor", is told here as a plot summary only: a boy rushes in from play and eats unwashed fruit bought at the market. He falls ill. At the doctor's, the questions trace the cause — what did you eat? Did you wash your hands? Was the food cooked, or raw, and was it washed? The answers point back to the unwashed fruit and the unwashed hands.

## Say it to your companion

List the causes of spoiling from memory, name two diseases that follow, then retell the doctor story's plot in three sentences — the play, the unwashed fruit, the doctor's questions.

## Do It Yourself

1. Walk your own kitchen: find one place where flies, dirty water or dirty utensils could foul food, and fix it or report it.
2. Write the doctor's three questions from memory.
3. Make a cover for one food container at home, the way the story teaches.
