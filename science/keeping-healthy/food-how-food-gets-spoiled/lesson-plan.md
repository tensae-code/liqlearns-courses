# Lesson plan — Food — how food gets spoiled

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Food — how food gets spoiled
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how food gets spoiled and makes us sick — the causes list — and the story of our friend and the doctor, told as a plot summary
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Food — what food is; this lesson teaches what happens when food is fouled.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The causes of spoiling** — flies, rats and cockroaches fouling food, dirty water, dirty utensils, spoiled or expired ingredients, dirty preparation places and hands, eating raw food
2. **The diseases that follow, and our friend and the doctor** — diarrhoea, amoeba, typhoid and cholera follow fouled food; the printed story is summarised

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the printed causes of food spoiling.
2. Name the diseases that follow fouled food: diarrhoea, amoeba, typhoid, cholera.
3. Retell the plot of 'Our friend and the doctor' as a summary.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a food cover or cloth to practise with.

## If the learner is stuck
- The causes are forgotten → group them: creatures (flies, rats, cockroaches), dirt (water, utensils, places, hands), and the food itself (spoiled ingredients, raw food).
- The story is retold word for word → ask for the plot only: what happened, in order, in the learner's own words.
