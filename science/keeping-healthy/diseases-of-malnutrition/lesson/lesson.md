# Diseases of malnutrition

## Do it first
Two children eat every day, yet one keeps losing weight and falling sick. If the meals are there, what else could be missing? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 6 book opens its health unit with the diseases of malnutrition, going back over the younger years' teaching and deepening it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Malnutrition — food that fails the body

Mauritania's Grade 6 *Sciences Naturelles* book opens with **malnutrition**: the state of a body whose food fails it — too little food, or food that lacks what the body needs — over time. Its first named sign in the book's deepening run (printed pp. 9–16) is **weight loss**: the body, not fed what it needs, draws on itself and grows lighter and weaker.

Malnutrition is not one missed meal. Like the harmful habits of the younger book, it is a pattern — a continuing failure of food — and its diseases grow out of that pattern.

## Sub lesson 2 — Deficiency diseases — what is missing, named

The Grade 6 lessons **revisit and deepen** the deficiency diseases of the third and fourth years: a **deficiency disease** is a sickness caused by the lack of one needed thing in the food — the body cannot make the missing part itself, so the lack, continued, becomes disease.

Deepening means the older child now ties the chain together: the missing nutrient, the body's failing work, and the disease that follows — and sees the balanced diet and the day's food rations, taught next in the same book, as the answer that prevents it.

## The key ideas

- **Malnutrition** is the state of a body whose food fails it — too little, or missing what it needs — over time; **weight loss** is its named first sign.
- A **deficiency disease** is caused by the continued lack of one needed thing in the food.
- Mauritania's Grade 6 book **revisits and deepens** the third and fourth years' teaching, and answers it with the balanced diet and the day's rations.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How is a deficiency disease different from an infectious disease?" Answer from the lesson: A deficiency disease comes from inside the diet — a needed thing missing from the food over time — not from a germ passed between people; its cure and prevention begin with the food itself.

## Do It Yourself

2. Look at one day's meals at home and name what each part gives the body; say what would be missing if one part were dropped for months.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
