# Lesson plan — Keeping our bodies clean

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Keeping our bodies clean
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — near the top of the Science ladder; the body learned in The Human Body, now cared for (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** keeping the whole body clean as a daily habit — washing, teeth, nails, hair and clothes — and knowing why it matters
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Parts of the body and the five senses — the learner already knows the parts they are now learning to care for.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The daily cleaning routine** — washing the body, face, hands, hair and clothes; teeth with a meswak; cutting nails
2. **Lice, fleas and why we wash** — the pests that live on an unclean body, hair and clothes
3. **Keeping clean for everyone's sake** — what uncleanliness does to the people around us

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Human Body — Parts of the body and the five senses. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Wash hands, face, feet and body with clean water and soap, the way the books teach.
2. Clean the teeth, cut the nails, wash and comb the hair, and wash clothes.
3. Wash hands with soap or ash before and after eating, after the latrine, and after touching animals.
4. Say what lives on an unclean body: lice and fleas — and why we wash them away.

## What you need
Clean water, soap (or ash, as the books also teach), a meswak chewing stick for teeth, nail cutter or scissors used carefully, clean clothes.

1. **Do it first** — the learner looks at their own hands, nails, hair and clothes and judges what needs cleaning today, before any routine is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 12102]: a picture of a child washing hands with soap, and the morning cleaning things beside them — the student follows the picture step by step and does the same with their own hands.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 12103]: a picture of hands being washed — wet, soap, scrub, rinse — the student watches the order, then washes their own hands in the same order.
   - ADD AUDIO: the hygiene song (ጽዳት — “Cleanliness”), sung as a sing-along: wash the whole body, face, eyes, ears, nose, hair, teeth, hands and feet, cut nails, wash clothes, so health is kept (theme as the book prints it; the song is named and summarised, not transcribed) — the student listens and joins in.

## If the learner is stuck
- The routine feels long → do it by the song: one verse, one part of the body. The song is the order.
- No soap in the house → the books themselves teach ash as the stand-in for hand-washing; clean water and rubbing still do the work.
- This lesson's next door: What disease is, and how it spreads — why this routine is a shield, not just a habit.
