# Keeping our bodies clean

## Do it first
Look at your hands. Look at your nails. Is your hair washed? Are your clothes clean? A body is cleaned a little every day, not once in a long while. This lesson gives the daily routine, part by part.

## Sub lesson 1 — The daily cleaning routine

Keeping clean means washing regularly, every day, not only when we look dirty. Wash the **hands, feet and face** with water and soap every day. Wash and comb the **hair** regularly. Bathe the whole **body** at least twice a week, as the Grade 1 guide teaches. Keep the **nose and ears** clean. **Cut the nails** short — dirt hides under long nails.

Clean the **teeth** every day. The Grade 1 book teaches cleaning teeth with a **meswak** — a chewing stick: chew one end until it frays into a brush, then brush the teeth with it, stroke by stroke. Wash your **clothes** too — a clean body in dirty clothes is only half clean.

Wash hands at the special times: **before and after eating, after the latrine, and after touching animals** — with soap, or with ash where the book teaches ash is what people use.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lice, fleas and why we wash

There is a reason for all this washing. On a body, hair and clothes that are not washed, small pests come to live: **lice** in the hair and **fleas** in clothes and bedding. They bite, they itch, and they make a person miserable — and they pass from person to person where people crowd together unwashed.

The answer is the routine itself: washed hair, washed clothes and a washed body are a home lice and fleas do not want. That is why the Grade 1 book puts the pests and the washing side by side: wash, and the pests have nowhere to live.

## Sub lesson 3 — Keeping clean for everyone's sake

Cleanliness is not only about yourself. The Grade 3 guide teaches it plainly: if we do not keep clean, other people are exposed to problems — sickness passes to them, and people cannot happily come near us, stay with us, play, learn or eat with us. The Grade 1 guide's answers say the same: an unclean person falls sick, passes sickness to others, and people avoid them.

So the morning wash is a kindness as well as a habit: every morning on rising, wash the face and hands well with water and soap — for your own health, and for everyone who will sit beside you that day.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole morning routine in order — face, hands, teeth, hair, nails, clothes — and why each one is done. Your companion asks: what happens if we skip it?

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the full hand-washing routine now, slowly: wet, soap, scrub palms and between the fingers, rinse.
2. Make a seven-day cleaning chart for your home: body, teeth, nails, hair, clothes. Tick each day you do each one.
3. Sing the cleanliness song as you wash: wash the whole body, face, eyes, ears, nose, hair, teeth, hands and feet, cut the nails, wash the clothes — so health is kept.
