# Diarrhoea and worm infestations

## Do it first
Before you read: a younger child at home has diarrhoea — runny stool again and again. Where could that sickness have come from — what went into the child's mouth before it started? And what drink can be given at home, before anything else? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Uganda's way in — its Primary Four home-study booklet puts diarrhoea and worms together, because both arrive through what is swallowed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diarrhoea diseases — and ORS/SSS at home

Diarrhoea diseases have causes a child can name: **contaminated water and contaminated food**. What is swallowed carries the sickness in.

The booklet's home answer is **ORS/SSS** — the rehydration drink used at home. It is given **in amounts by age**: the amount for a small child is not the amount for an older one, and the booklet gives the amounts age by age. Home treatment starts at home, with the right amount for the child's age.

## Sub lesson 2 — Worm infestations — threadworm and other worms

Worms live in the body as **infestations**. The booklet's named example is the **threadworm**, with other worms beside it.

The lesson teaches the worms' whole path: **how they enter** the body, and the **signs** that show they are there. A child who knows how worms enter, and what signs to watch for, can spot an infestation instead of only suffering it.

## The key ideas

- Diarrhoea diseases come from **contaminated water and contaminated food**.
- **ORS/SSS** is the home rehydration drink, given **in amounts by age**.
- **Threadworm** and other worms enter the body and live as infestations; each has **signs** to watch for.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — diarrhoea and its home drink first, then worms. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Where does diarrhoea come from?" Answer with the causes, not by repeating the title.

## Do It Yourself
1. Name three ways water or food at home can become contaminated, and one guard against each.
2. Say the ORS/SSS rule aloud: the drink is given at home, and the amount follows the child's age — then explain why a baby and a ten-year-old do not get the same amount.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the signs of a worm infestation.
