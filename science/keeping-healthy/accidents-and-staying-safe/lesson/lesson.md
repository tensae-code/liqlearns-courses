# Accidents and staying safe

## Do it first
Look around one room of your home — in your mind will do. The open fire. The sharp knife left on the edge. The river path after rain. Harm does not always walk in slowly like sickness; sometimes it arrives in a second. The Grade 4 guides teach children to see those seconds coming — by naming the things that can cause harm before they cause it.

## Sudden accidents and the things that cause harm (teacher guide — evidence)
The two regional teacher guides carry this teaching in their cross-cutting unit: **accidents and vulnerability** (SNNP) and **sudden accidents and the things that can cause harm** (Oromia). (Teacher guide — evidence.)

An **accident** is a **sudden** harmful event — not a slow sickness but a moment: a burn, a cut, a fall, a flood. **Vulnerability** is how exposed a person or place is to that harm: the toddler near the open fire is more vulnerable than the adult across the room.

The SNNP syllabus names **fire and flood** among the hazards pupils must identify. The work of the lesson is identification: walk the home and the road with new eyes, name every thing that can suddenly cause harm, and move what can be moved, guard what cannot, and teach the small children away from all of it.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion what an accident is, what vulnerability means, and three hazards you can name at home — fire and flood from the book's list, and one of your own finding.

## Do It Yourself
1. Hazard walk: with an adult, walk home and name every sudden-harm thing. Count them. That count is your home's accident map.
2. Guard one: choose one hazard you may help with — lids on pots turned, tools put away — and guard it for a week.
3. Vulnerable first: name who in your home is most vulnerable to each hazard you found. Safety is planned around them first.
