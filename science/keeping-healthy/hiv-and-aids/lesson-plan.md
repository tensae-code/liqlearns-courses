# Lesson plan — HIV and AIDS: how it spreads and how it does not

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** HIV and AIDS: how it spreads and how it does not
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — the urgent-issues chapter of the Grade 3 book (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** AIDS is caused by the HIV virus; it spreads through blood and sometimes from mother to child — and it does NOT spread by touching, handshakes, sharing a latrine, eating together, or breathing
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What disease is, and how it spreads — the learner knows germs travel; this lesson corrects the fears about one sickness that travels only in particular ways.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Keeping Healthy — What disease is, and how it spreads. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what AIDS is: caused by the HIV virus, passing only person to person, weakening the whole body.
2. Say how it spreads: through blood by needles, razors and sharp instruments; sometimes from mother to child, including by breastfeeding, as the book teaches.
3. Say how it does NOT spread: touching, shaking hands, sharing a latrine, eating and drinking together, breathing.
4. Say why it matters to the community, and name prevention: never share the sharp things.

5. Add the Grade 4 fullness: unpack HIV and AIDS letter by letter; say a carrier can look healthy for years yet pass the virus on; name the three transmission roads and the printed preventions, including the mother's medicine and care; and keep the does-not-pass list complete — workplaces, toilets, sneezing, handshakes, one table, insect bites, one glass.
## What you need
Nothing but care in speaking: this lesson is taught kindly, the way the books teach it — a person with HIV is a neighbour, not a danger at the table.

## If the learner is stuck
- Fears eating with or touching a person with HIV → the book's list is explicit: it does NOT spread by touching, shaking hands, sharing a latrine, eating and drinking together, or breathing.
- Thinks it travels like flu, by air → it passes only person to person through blood (needles, razors, sharp instruments) and sometimes from mother to child.
- This lesson's next door: Road safety — the other urgent issue in the books' final chapters.
