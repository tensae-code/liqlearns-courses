# Signs, lights and crossing rules

## Do it first
Roads speak. Their words are signs and coloured lights. Red, yellow, green — each colour is a command. This lesson teaches you to read the road.

## Signs, lights and the rules table

The Grade 2 book prints **traffic signs** and asks what each one means — signs are the road's standing orders, there even when no person is. Then the **lights**: the traffic light runs **red — yellow — green**, and the pedestrian light runs **red — green**; the book sets both in a meaning table — red commands stop and wait, green commands go (for the walker: cross, after looking).

Then the **rules table**, with the benefit of each rule: cross at the **zebra crossing**; walk on the **left line** of the road; **never cross from behind a parked vehicle**; **look left and right and cross** without lingering; **never cross holding hands in a chain**. Learn the table as one thing: signs tell, lights command, rules protect.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion the lights: traffic light and pedestrian light, colour by colour. Then read three signs to them and let them say what each sign commands.

## Do It Yourself
1. Play traffic light with colour cards on the schoolyard zebra: red — wait at the edge; green — look both ways and cross calmly on the zebra.
2. Draw the signs you pass on your walk to school. Say each one's meaning aloud to whoever walks with you.
3. Make the rules table as a poster: the five rules, one picture each. Hang it by the door.
