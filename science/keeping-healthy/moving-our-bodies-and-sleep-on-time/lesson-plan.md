# Lesson plan — Moving our bodies and sleep on time

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Moving our bodies and sleep on time
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — the active half of health (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** exercise and sport keep people active, strong and healthy; enough sleep, on time, keeps learning and work strong
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the body lessons — the learner knows the parts; here the parts are used, and rested.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons

2. **Enough sleep, on time** — going to bed and rising on time; late nights spoil learning and work

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Keeping Healthy — Keeping our bodies clean. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the exercises and sports the book teaches: walking, running, football, rope-skipping, wrestling, gena.
2. Say what sport does: it makes people active, strong and healthy.
3. Say the sleep rule: go to bed and rise on time; staying up with television and play spoils learning and work.

## What you need
A rope for skipping if you have one; a clock or a drawn clock face for bedtime.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks rest is laziness → the book puts the two together: movement by day, sleep on time by night; each one makes the other work.
- Stays up late with television → name what it spoils, in the book's words: learning and work the next day.
