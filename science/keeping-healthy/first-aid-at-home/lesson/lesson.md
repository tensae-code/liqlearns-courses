# First aid at home

## Do it first
Before you read: someone at home has just hurt themselves — a cut finger, a small burn, a fall. Before any medicine, before any hospital, there are things the people standing right there can do in the first minutes. What comes first? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Gabon's way in — its Grade 5 Super Efficace guide gives the first-aid gestures their own lesson, because the first help is given at home, by the people who are there.

## Sub lesson 1 — First: stop, look, and call for help

**First aid** is the first help given to a hurt or suddenly ill person, before fuller help arrives. Its first gesture is not touching at all: it is **stopping and looking**.

Stop and look: what happened? Is the place safe to help in? How hurt is the person? Then **call for help** — an adult, a neighbour, the health worker — because first aid is a bridge to real care, not a replacement for it. A calm helper who has looked and called does more good than a rushing helper who has done neither.

## Sub lesson 2 — The first gestures — comfort, clean water, pressure, rest

With help on its way, the first gestures are simple ones, done gently:

- **Comfort and keep the person still and warm** — fear and movement can make a hurt worse; a calm voice is itself a gesture.
- **Clean water for a small cut or a burn** — clean water washes dirt away and cools a burn; dirty water and home mixtures do the opposite.
- **Pressure on bleeding** — a clean cloth pressed on a bleeding cut slows the blood while help comes.
- **Rest** — a hurt person rests; first aid does not send them back to play or work.

Each gesture is small. Together, done in the first minutes and in the right order — stop, look, call, then comfort, clean, press, rest — they are what first aid means at home.

## The key ideas

- **First aid** is the first help given before fuller help arrives — a bridge to real care, not a replacement for it.
- The first gestures are **stop, look, and call for help** — in that order.
- Then come the gentle gestures: **comfort and keep still, clean water for small cuts and burns, pressure on bleeding, and rest**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — first stop, look and call, then the first gestures. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is the very first gesture of first aid?" Answer: stopping and looking, before touching — and then calling for help.

## Do It Yourself
1. Order drill: write the seven gestures — stop, look, call, comfort, clean, press, rest — on seven slips, mix them, and put them back in the lesson's order. Say aloud why the first three come before the rest.
2. Home map: with an adult, find where clean water, clean cloths and the nearest help are in your home. That map is part of first aid, learned before it is needed.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — why first aid begins with stopping and looking, not with touching.
