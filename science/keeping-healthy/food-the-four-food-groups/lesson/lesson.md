# Food — the four food groups

## Do it first

Lay out, in words or pictures, the foods you ate yesterday. How many different kinds can you make of them? This lesson makes four.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where food comes from, and the Feeders poem

Food comes from animals and from plants. The book's printed poem መጋቢዎች — "Feeders" — sings of exactly this, and here it is named and summarised only: roots, stems and leaves are washed, cooked and eaten; bees circle the flowers to make honey; animals are kept so that we get milk and meat.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four groups in a web

The four basic food groups, drawn as a web with food at the centre:

- **Vegetables and fruits** — they protect the body from disease.
- **Grains and pulses** — they give energy and heat, and they build.
- **Milk and milk products** — they give energy and strength.
- **Meat and eggs** — they build the body.

## Sub lesson 3 — Eating from all four — and growing your own

Eat from all four groups. If we do not, the body suffers: the book names bleeding gums, stunted growth and thin bones. A hard-to-get food can be substituted — beans can stand in for meat. And food can be produced at home: a garden, chickens, bees, a dairy cow. The book's project: grow vegetables or fruit in containers — a cut jerrycan, a bucket, a tin — and show the class what grows.

## Say it to your companion

Name the four groups and what each one does, then say which group your own last meal missed — and what could stand in for it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the four-group web: food in the middle, four arms, one group on each arm with its job.
2. Start the container project: plant a vegetable or fruit in a cut jerrycan, bucket or tin, and care for it.
3. Plan one day of eating that touches all four groups.
