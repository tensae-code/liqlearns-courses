# Parasites — inside and outside the body

## Do it first
Before you read: some living things do not make their own food and do not hunt — they live on or in another living thing and feed off it, while that other living thing grows weak. What would you call such a living thing, and does it matter whether it lives inside the body or outside it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Afghanistan's way in — its Grade 5 book teaches parasites in a run of three lessons: parasites, then internal parasites, then external parasites.

## Sub lesson 1 — Parasites — living on or in a host

A **parasite** is a living thing that lives **on or in another living thing** — called its **host** — and **feeds off the host**.

The feeding is one-way: the parasite gains food and a place to live; the host loses food and strength, and can become **weak or sick**. That one-way harm is what makes it a parasite and not just a visitor.

Parasites split by the one question this lesson is built on: **where do they live?** Inside the body, or outside it? The next two sub lessons take the two answers in the book's own order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Internal parasites — inside the body

**Internal parasites** live **inside the body** — in places like the gut (the intestines), where the body's own digested food passes.

From inside, they take food the body has eaten, so the host can grow weak and sick even while eating. Worms living in the gut are the familiar kind. They get in from outside — with unclean food and water and on dirty hands — which is why clean food, clean water and washed hands are the defence taught all through this book's health lessons.

## Sub lesson 3 — External parasites — outside the body

**External parasites** live **on the outside of the body** — on the **skin**, in the **hair**, or in the **clothes**.

From outside, they bite and feed there; their biting makes the skin **itch**, and some of them can **carry disease** from one person or animal to another (this same book's next lessons name the louse as a disease carrier). Lice, fleas and ticks are the familiar kinds. The defence is the outside mirror of the inside one: a clean body, clean hair, and clean clothes and bedding.

## The key ideas

- A **parasite** lives on or in a **host** and feeds off it, harming it — the host can become weak or sick.
- **Internal parasites** live **inside** the body, in places like the gut, taking the body's food.
- **External parasites** live **outside** — on skin, hair or clothes — biting, causing itching, and sometimes carrying disease.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — parasites and the host first, then inside, then outside. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is the one question that sorts a parasite as internal or external?" Answer: Where it lives — inside the body is internal; on the outside of the body is external.
