# Lesson plan — What disease is, and how it spreads

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** What disease is, and how it spreads
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — the body cared for in Keeping our bodies clean; here, what happens when sickness comes anyway (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what disease is, how sickness travels — germs, flies and mosquitoes, dirt — and how cleanliness stops it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Keeping our bodies clean — the learner already has the routine; this lesson gives the reason underneath it.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What disease is** — disease named plainly: germs and dirt are the roots of sickness
2. **How disease travels, and how we stop it** — germs, flies and mosquitoes carry sickness; cleanliness stops it
3. **Sicknesses from poor hygiene, and things we never share** — the named sicknesses, and the personal things that stay personal

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Keeping Healthy — Keeping our bodies clean. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what disease is, in a Grade 1 child's words: germs and dirt are the roots of sickness.
2. Name how sickness travels: germs, flies and mosquitoes.
3. Name the sicknesses the books tie to poor hygiene: trachoma, stomach illness, itch/scabies, diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid, typhus and amoeba.
4. Say which things must never be shared: razor, scissors, needle, knife, nail cutter, underclothes, handkerchiefs and toothbrushes.
5. Say how we stop sickness: the cleaning routine, covered food, clean water, clean latrines.

## What you need
A food cover, clean water to drink, and the cleaning things from the hygiene lesson.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks sickness comes from cold air or bad luck alone → go back to the carriers: germs too small to see, carried by flies to uncovered food, by mosquitoes, and by dirty hands.
- Shares a toothbrush 'because it is family' → the rule has no family exception in the books: personal sharp and washing things are never shared.
- This lesson's next door: Healthy food and spoiled food — the sickness that rides on food itself.
