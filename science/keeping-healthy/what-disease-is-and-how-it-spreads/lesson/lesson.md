# What disease is, and how it spreads

## Do it first
Think of a time you were sick in bed. You could not play. You could not learn. What is that thing called sickness — where does it come from, and how does it travel from one person to another? That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What disease is

**Disease** is sickness — the body stopped from working well. The Grade 1 book opens its disease section by defining it for a young child and naming its causes at their level: **germs** and **dirt** are the roots of sickness. Germs are living things too small to see. Where dirt gathers — unwashed hands, uncovered food, dirty water, a dirty latrine — germs gather with it, and sickness follows.

[Picture 12106 — a well child playing and the same child sick in bed, drawn kindly]

## Sub lesson 2 — How disease travels, and how we stop it

Sickness travels. The Grade 1 book names its carriers: **germs** themselves, **flies**, and **mosquitoes**. A fly walks on dirt, then walks on your food. A mosquito bites a sick person, then bites a well one. Dirty hands carry germs to the mouth.

And sickness can be stopped — the same chapter ties it back to the cleaning routine: washed hands, covered food, clean water, washed clothes, cut nails. The fly that finds food covered finds no landing place. The hands that are washed carry nothing to the mouth.

[Picture 12107 — a fly landing on uncovered food, and the same food covered as the answer]

## Sub lesson 3 — Sicknesses from poor hygiene, and things we never share

The Grade 2 and Grade 3 books name the sicknesses that ride on poor hygiene: **trachoma** — the eye sickness, spread where faces are unwashed and flies gather, which the Grade 3 book warns can cause blindness; **stomach illness and diarrhoea**; **itch and scabies**; colds and vomiting; and in Grade 3 also **cholera, typhoid, typhus and amoeba**. These sicknesses pass easily from person to person.

That is why some things are **never shared**: sharp and personal things — **razor, scissors, needle, knife, nail cutter** — and **underclothes, handkerchiefs and toothbrushes**. What touches one person's skin, blood or mouth stays with that person. Face-washing morning and evening and clean latrines are the Grade 3 book's own answer to trachoma: washed faces give the flies nothing.

[Picture 12108 — flies gathering at an unwashed child's face, with washed children beside]

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion what disease is, name the three travellers that carry it, and list the things we never share. Your companion adds one way we stop sickness for each traveller you named.

## Do It Yourself
1. Cover food at home today with a clean cover or lid, and say why, in the lesson's words, to the person who cooks.
2. Make a never-share card: draw the razor, needle, toothbrush and handkerchief, and write NEVER SHARE under them. Put it where your family keeps these things.
3. Wash a fruit the book's way before eating it — and say what the washing removed.
