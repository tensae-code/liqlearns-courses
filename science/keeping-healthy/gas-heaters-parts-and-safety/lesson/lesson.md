# Gas heaters — parts and safety

## Do it first
A gas stove sits in the kitchen. Before anyone lights it, what are its parts — and which part does what? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its primary books teach gas appliances by their parts, taking heating appliances apart in Grade 4 and naming the stove's parts in Grade 5.

## Sub lesson 1 — Parts of a gas stove — burners, ignition, knobs

Mauritania's Grade 5 *Sciences Naturelles* book names the gas stove's parts (printed p. 91): the **burners**, where the gas burns; the **ignition system**, which lights the gas; and the **control knobs**, which open, close and govern the gas to each burner.

Each part has its job, and the jobs run in order: the knob lets the gas through, the ignition lights it, the burner holds the flame. A user who knows the parts knows what they are commanding when they cook.

## Sub lesson 2 — Taking a gas heating appliance apart — and naming its parts

The Grade 4 book's experimentation lesson goes further by hand (printed p. 65): **dismantling gas heating appliances and naming their parts**. Taken apart — safely, unlit and disconnected — the appliance stops being a sealed mystery: each part is seen, named and understood in its place.

That is the safety ground of the whole lesson: gas is handled safely by people who know what each part does, what must be closed, and what must never be forced. The Grade 5 teacher's guide carries worked answers for the gas stove, butane and charcoal lessons (guide evidence).

## The key ideas

- A gas stove's named parts: **burners**, the **ignition system** and the **control knobs** — gas let through, gas lit, flame held, in that order.
- Mauritania's Grade 4 book teaches **dismantling gas heating appliances and naming their parts** — an appliance understood part by part is an appliance handled safely.
- Knowing the parts is the ground of gas safety: what each part does, what must be closed, what must never be forced.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why do the books teach a gas appliance's parts before its safe use?" Answer from the lesson: Because safe use is commanding the parts — letting gas through with the knob, lighting it with the ignition, holding the flame at the burner — and a user cannot command safely what they cannot name.

2. Say the order of jobs when a burner is lit — knob, ignition, burner — and what each part does in that order.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
