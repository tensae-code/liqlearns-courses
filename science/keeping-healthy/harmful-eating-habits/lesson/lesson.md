# Harmful eating habits

## Do it first
Think of yesterday's meals and snacks. Was there anything you ate, or any way you ate it, that you would not want to do every day? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 3 book gives the habits that harm the body a lesson of their own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Eating habits that harm the body

Mauritania's Grade 3 *Sciences Naturelles* book teaches food in a run: where food comes from, the nutrients, the day's meals and healthy eating habits — and then, as its own lesson, **the eating habits that harm the body**.

A habit is a thing done again and again, until it is done without thinking. A harmful eating habit is one whose repetition works against the body: the harm is not in one meal but in the pattern, repeated day after day. That is why the book teaches habits, not single meals — what a child does daily is what shapes the body.

## Sub lesson 2 — Learning the harmful side by name

The lesson stands right after healthy eating habits in the book (printed p. 16 teaches the healthy habits; printed p. 17 this one), and the pairing is the teaching: a habit is judged against the healthy pattern the child has just learned — the day's meals in their order, food that gives the body what it needs.

Naming the harmful habits is the protection. A child who can say which of their own eating habits harm the body can change that one habit, and a changed habit changes every day that follows it.

## The key ideas

- A **habit** is a thing done again and again — its harm or good is in the repetition, not in one meal.
- **Harmful eating habits** are the eating patterns that work against the body, taught as their own lesson in Mauritania's Grade 3 book.
- The lesson is judged against the **healthy eating habits** taught just before it in the same book.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why does the book teach harmful habits as a lesson of their own, and not only the healthy ones?" Answer from the lesson: Because a habit harms by repetition: a child who can name the habits that harm the body can change them, and the book pairs the harmful lesson directly with the healthy-habits lesson before it.

## Do It Yourself
1. List your own eating habits for one day, in order. Mark each one: healthy, or harmful to the body.
2. Pick the one harmful habit from your list and write what you will do instead, using the healthy pattern from the day's meals.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
