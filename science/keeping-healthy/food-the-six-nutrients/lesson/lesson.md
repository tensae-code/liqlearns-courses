# Food — the six nutrients

## Do it first

You are mostly water — about two parts in three. Before reading: guess which part of your food builds you, which part gives energy, and which part is water itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The energy and building nutrients

The six nutrients are: **carbohydrates**, **fats and oils**, **protein**, **minerals**, **vitamins** and **water**. Each one does its own work in the body, and each is carried by its own foods. Carbohydrates and fats and oils are the body's energy carriers; protein builds the body. Learn each nutrient together with the foods that carry it — a nutrient without its foods is only a word.

## Sub lesson 2 — Minerals, vitamins and water

Minerals and vitamins complete the six: small in amount, real in their work of keeping the body right. And water stands in the list as a nutrient in its own right — water alone is about **67% of the body**, two-thirds of what you are. That is why drinking water is not separate from eating: water is one of the six.

## Say it to your companion

Name the six nutrients in order, give one job and one carrying food for each, and finish with the water fact: about 67% of the body.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a six-row table: nutrient | its job | a food that carries it. Fill every row.
2. Drink a glass of water and say which of the six nutrients you have just taken.
3. Find one food at home that carries two nutrients, and name both.
