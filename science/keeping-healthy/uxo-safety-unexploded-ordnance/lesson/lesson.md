# UXO safety — unexploded ordnance

## Do it first
Before you read: this lesson teaches one thing — UXO safety — the unexploded ordnance left in the ground in Laos: what it is, how people get hurt by it, and what to do in risky areas — as placed from Laos’s Grade 1–3 Science and Environment books. What do you already know about it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Laos's way in — its Science and Environment books teach this as its own topic, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — The bombs still in the ground

Laos’s books open with the thing itself: bombs and unexploded ordnance (UXO) left in Laos from the war years are still in the ground. The Grade 2 book teaches the **kinds of bombs**; the Grade 3 book teaches their **shapes and colours** and the **places where they still remain**.

Knowing what these things look like — their kinds, shapes and colours — and where they still lie is the first safety knowledge: a thing you can recognise is a thing you can keep away from. The Grade 3 book even practises the danger safely, bursting a flour/rice-filled balloon as a safe simulation of what a real bomb does — the lesson learned with no real bomb anywhere near.

## Sub lesson 2 — How people get hurt

The Grade 2 book (Ch. 2) names the causes of injury and death from UXO plainly: **digging** in ground where a bomb lies, **fires** set over buried ordnance, **dismantling** a found bomb, and **clearing grass** where one is hidden.

Every named cause is an ordinary day’s work meeting a hidden bomb. That is why this lesson belongs to everyday safety, not to history: the danger arrives while people farm, clear land and make fires, so the protection must be known by the people doing those ordinary things — children included.

## Sub lesson 3 — What to do — in risky areas, and when you see one

The Grade 2 book teaches **what to do in risky areas**; the Grade 1 teacher guide adds the clearest rule of all, as guide evidence: if you see a bomb or a bomb-like thing, **do not touch it** — the teacher instructs this clearly (guide p. 148).

Laos’s own way of teaching this is itself part of the lesson: the Grade 2 teacher guide (guide evidence) runs a **UXO role-play** with a box of fruit and bomb pictures, and the class sings the song **"Bombs are dangerous"** — the rule carried by play and song, so the youngest children hold it. The rule itself never changes: do not touch, keep away, and follow what the books teach for risky areas.
