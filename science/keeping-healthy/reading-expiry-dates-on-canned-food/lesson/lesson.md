# Reading expiry dates on canned food

## Do it first
Picture a tin of food in a cupboard at home. Somewhere on it, small printed numbers carry a decision: eat, or do not eat. Where would you look, and what would the numbers tell you? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 4 book teaches reading the date on tinned food as a lesson of its own.

## Sub lesson 1 — The date printed on the tin

Mauritania's Grade 4 *Sciences Naturelles* book teaches food keeping step by step: preserving foods so they keep, telling spoiled food from good food — and then this lesson: tinned foods carry a **use-by date** (expiry date), printed on the tin by the maker, that says until when the food inside is safe to eat.

The date is part of the food's keeping. Preserving stretches food's life; it does not stretch it forever, and the printed date is where the maker states the limit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Read before you eat

The habit the lesson builds is an order of actions: **find the date, read the date, decide** — before the tin is opened, not after the food is tasted. A tin past its date is treated as spoiled food is treated in the lesson before: not eaten.

That is the whole lesson, and it is meant to be carried into every kitchen and shop: the numbers on the tin are read first, every time.

## The key ideas

- Tinned foods carry a printed **use-by (expiry) date** — the maker's stated limit for safe eating.
- **Preserving** stretches food's life but not forever; the date is where the stretch ends.
- The safe order is: **find the date, read the date, decide** — before opening or tasting.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why must the date be read before the tin is opened, and not judged by tasting?" Answer from the lesson: Because the printed date is the maker's stated limit for the food's safe keeping; past it, the food is treated as spoiled — and tasting spoiled food is the harm the lesson exists to prevent.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find three tinned or packaged foods at home and read each one's date aloud. Say for each: still in date, or past it.
2. Write the lesson's order of actions — find, read, decide — on a card for the kitchen cupboard.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
