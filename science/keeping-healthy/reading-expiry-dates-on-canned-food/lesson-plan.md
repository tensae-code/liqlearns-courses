# Lesson plan — Reading expiry dates on canned food

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Reading expiry dates on canned food
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — appended to Unit 14 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading the use-by (expiry) date printed on tinned foods before eating them — Mauritania's Grade 4 way of closing its food-preservation lessons: preserved food keeps only until its date
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The date printed on the tin** — Mauritania's Grade 4 *Sciences Naturelles* book teaches food keeping step by step: preserving foods so they keep, telling spoiled food from good food — and then this lesson: tinned foods carry a **use-by date** (expiry date), printed on the tin by the maker, that says until when the food inside is safe to eat.

The date is part of the food's keeping.
2. **Read before you eat** — The habit the lesson builds is an order of actions: **find the date, read the date, decide** — before the tin is opened, not after the food is tasted.
