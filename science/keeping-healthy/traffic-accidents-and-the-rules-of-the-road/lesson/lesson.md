# Traffic accidents and the rules of the road

## Do it first
Every day you cross roads. Cars, bajajs, carts — and children get hurt on roads when crossing goes wrong. This lesson first names the danger, then teaches the rules that keep a crossing safe.

## Sub lesson 1 — Traffic accidents — how children are hurt

Roads are useful and roads are dangerous. The Grade 2 book opens its urgent-issues chapter with **traffic accidents**: the crashes and knock-downs in which people — and children among them — are hurt on roads. It places the topic first among urgent issues because it is the danger children meet every single day, on the ordinary walk to school. The rest of the road lessons exist because of this one page: accidents are not fate; most happen where a rule was broken or a look was not taken.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rules of the road

The Grade 3 book states the **traffic laws** for people on foot. **Pedestrians keep to the left** on the pedestrian way; vehicles keep to the drivers' way. **Cross only at the zebra crossing** — calmly, **looking right and left** and checking that no fast vehicle is coming. **Never dash** into the zebra carelessly. **Never cross screened by a parked vehicle** — the driver cannot see you, and you cannot see the road. **Adults hold children's hands** to cross.

The book also prints a figure of traffic-law violations to identify: crossing outside the zebra, dashing, crossing from behind a vehicle. Learn to spot each one — in the picture, and then on your own street.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion the crossing routine as if they were a younger child: where to walk, where to cross, how to look, what never to do. Your companion plays the younger child and asks 'why?' at every step — answer from the rules.

## Do It Yourself
1. Practise the crossing routine at a real zebra with an adult: stop, look right, look left, look right again, cross calmly, hand held.
2. Draw your walk to school. Mark every crossing. Mark the dangerous ones — where a parked vehicle hides you, where there is no zebra — and plan the safer way.
3. Teach the rules to a younger child this week, holding their hand at the crossing the way the book shows.
