**The one thing this lesson teaches:** autoimmunity: normally the immune system combats viruses, bacteria or any other infectious organism threatening health, but when a failure occurs the same system designed to protect the body can turn against it: when the immune system does not work adequately it cannot distinguish the body's own cells from foreign ones, and instead of fighting external antigens, the immune system's cells or the antibodies they produce can go against their own cells and tissues by error; the components involved in the offence are called autoreactive lymphocytes or autoantibodies (guide evidence)

## Sub lessons
1. **Autoimmunity** — autoimmunity: normally the immune system combats viruses, bacteria or any other infectious organism threatening health, but when a failure occurs the same system designed to protect the body can turn against it: when the immune system does not work adequately it cannot distinguish the body's own cells from foreign ones, and instead of fighting external antigens, the immune system's cells or the antibodies they produce can go against their own cells and tissues by error; the components involved in the offence are called autoreactive lymphocytes or autoantibodies (guide evidence)
2. **Examples named by the guide** — examples named by the guide: lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, myositis, coeliac disease (intolerance to gluten), dermatomyositis, type 1 diabetes and chronic thyroiditis; an autoimmune disease can occasion destruction of body tissue, abnormal growth of an organ and changes in the functioning of organs; the causes are still being investigated — probably several factors are implicated, including viruses and environmental factors, certain chemical compounds and some medicines, all able to damage or change the body's cells (guide evidence)
3. **In many cases the first symptoms are fatigue, muscle** — in many cases the first symptoms are fatigue, muscle pains and fever, and the classic symptom of an autoimmune disease is inflammation, which can cause redness, heat, pain and swelling (guide evidence)
