# Cleaning tools and what each one does

## Do it first
Before you read: this lesson teaches one thing — cleaning tools and what each one does — as placed from South Sudan's Primary Science books (see lesson/sources.md for the exact books and pages). What do you already know about it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is South Sudan's way in — its Ministry of General Education and Instruction books teach this as its own topic, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tools for cleaning our bodies

South Sudan's Primary 2 book names the cleaning items themselves: **toothbrush and toothpaste, soap, towel, comb and handkerchief** — and which of them are **personal items never to be shared**. Each item has its own job on the body, and the lesson is learning which item does which job.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tools for cleaning our surroundings

The Primary 4 book names the tools for cleaning the environment: **broom, bucket, mop, rake, cloth, brush, slasher, hoe, panga and wheelbarrow** — and where each is used: the rake to the compound, the mop to the classroom, the wheelbarrow to the areas around the school; the slasher keeps grass short against mosquitoes and rodents.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing the right tool for the job

The two runs teach one rule together: a cleaning job is done with **its own tool** — the body's tools are personal and are not shared, and the surroundings' tools are matched to the place being cleaned. Naming the tool and its job, before the cleaning starts, is the lesson's one thing.
