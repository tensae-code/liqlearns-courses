# Water safety and harbour safety equipment

## Do it first
Picture a pier: boats below, water all around the edges, people walking where a slip means the sea. What is kept on a pier to help someone who falls in? Say one thing you would hope to find there. Then read on: this is Iceland's way in — its Grade 3 safety chapter sends children to get to know the safety equipment on piers and docks.

## Sub lesson 1 — Piers and docks — busy places beside deep water

A **pier** or **dock** is where boats come in and people work and walk — right at the edge of the water. That edge is the danger: a slip or a fall there is a fall into the sea, so harbour places are fitted with safety equipment on purpose.

## Sub lesson 2 — The safety equipment — know it before you need it

The harbour's **safety equipment** is the gear kept on piers and docks to help when someone is in the water or in danger there. Iceland's aims sheet makes getting to know that equipment itself a chapter aim: the safe child is the one who already knows what the equipment is and where it stands, before any emergency.

## The key ideas

- A pier or dock is a busy place right at the water's edge — a fall there is a fall into the sea.
- Safety equipment is kept on piers and docks to help when someone is in the water or in danger there.
- Iceland's way: children get to know the harbour safety equipment itself, before any emergency.
