# My first 1000 days

## Do it first
Before you read: a baby cannot choose its food, its air or its care. Do those first days matter only while they pass — or can they still matter when the baby is a grown adult? Say your answer out loud, and one reason for it. Then read on: this is the Cook Islands way in — its Grade 8 book *Ko Au e Toku Aorangi: Kai No te Oraanga Meitaki (Me, Myself, My Environment: Nutrition)* teaches this through a real discovery story: **Toku Tai Tauatinianga Ra Mua — My First 1000 Days**.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first 1000 days shape later health

The **first 1000 days** of a life — from the very start of life, through birth, the baby months and the toddler years — **shape later health**.

They are not a small beginning that the rest of life simply overwrites. How a child grows in those days is carried forward: the body built then is the body the adult lives in. That is why the book gives these days a lesson of their own, inside a book about food and health: what happens first matters for everything after.

## Sub lesson 2 — Professor Barker's discovery: the Hertfordshire birth records

How was this found out? The book tells it through **Professor Barker's discovery**.

In **Hertfordshire**, in England, detailed **birth records** had been kept — records of babies' births and their early growth. Barker studied those records and did the patient scientific thing: he **followed what happened to those recorded babies later in life**, comparing how their lives began with how their health went as adults.

Old paper, carefully kept, became evidence — because the records let a scientist join the start of a life to its later health, person by person.

## Sub lesson 3 — The surprise finding, and the questions still open

Inside the records sat a **surprise finding**: the earliest days and later health were **linked** — how a life began showed up again in the health of the adult, in a way nobody had expected.

A surprise like that does not end a science story; it **opens** one. If the first 1000 days shape later health, then exactly what in those days matters most, how the shaping works, and how much can still be changed later are live questions — **questions scientists still work on**, as the book says plainly. The lesson you take away is double: care for the first 1000 days, and keep asking how their long shadow works.

## The key ideas

- The **first 1000 days** of a life **shape later health** — the start is a foundation, not a warm-up.
- **Professor Barker** discovered the link by studying the **Hertfordshire birth records** and following the recorded babies into adult life.
- The records held a **surprise finding** — earliest days and adult health linked — and it opened **questions scientists still work on**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the shaping first, then Barker and the records, then the surprise and the open questions. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How can old birth records prove anything about adults?" Answer with the following-into-later-life step, not by repeating.
