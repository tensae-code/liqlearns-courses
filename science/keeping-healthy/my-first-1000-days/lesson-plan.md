# Lesson plan — My first 1000 days

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** My first 1000 days
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — appended to Unit 14 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how the first 1000 days of a life shape later health — taught the Cook Islands book's way, through Professor Barker's discovery: the Hertfordshire birth records, the surprise finding inside them, and the questions scientists still work on
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The first 1000 days shape later health** — A life's first 1000 days — from the very start of life through the baby and toddler years — are not a throwaway start: how those days go shapes the health of the whole later life.
2. **Professor Barker's discovery — the Hertfordshire birth records** — Professor Barker studied old birth records from Hertfordshire that recorded babies' births and early growth, and followed what happened to those people later in life.
3. **The surprise finding, and the questions still open** — The records held a surprise: the earliest days and later health were linked in a way nobody had expected — a finding that opened questions scientists still work on today.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks the story is only history → return to the open questions: the finding is still being worked on, and the first-1000-days idea is about babies being born now.
