# Hands-on — My first 1000 days

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Draw the 1000-days line
Draw a long line on paper and mark it: the start of life, birth, first steps, first words, and the end of the first 1000 days. Under the line, write three things a baby needs across those days to grow well. Say out loud why the book calls these days a foundation, not a warm-up.

## Retell the records
Tell Barker's story to someone at home in four steps, in order: old records in Hertfordshire — what the records held — following the people into later life — the surprise. Your listener must be able to say back what the surprise was.

## One open question
Write one question scientists might still work on about the first 1000 days — for example what in those days matters most, or how much later health can still be changed. Under it, write what kind of records or study could help answer it.
