| drugs defined with the WHO: any substance that, introduced into a living organism, can modify one or more of its functions; drugs are natural or artificial substances that alter the perception, mood, conduct, knowledge and physical performance of whoever consumes them; every civilisation has consumed them — for religious, ritual, medicinal, habitual or recreational motives, as the shamans and priests of ancient cultures intoxicated themselves with hallucinogenic plants (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 21 |
| drugs classified by legal status: licit (used freely — tobacco and alcohol), for medical use (prescription drugs tied to treating mood and sleep disorders, painful diseases or achieving lucidity — psychopharmaceuticals, minor stimulants, methadone) and illicit (whose commerce is illegal, varying by country — cannabis derivatives, heroin, cocaine) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 21 |
| drugs classified by their effect on the central nervous system: depressants (inhibit functions or activity of some brain region), stimulants (temporary improvements of neurological or physical activity), hallucinogens (changes in perception, consciousness or emotion — psychedelics, dissociatives, which block the conscious mind's signals to other parts of the brain and can produce sedation, respiratory depression, analgesia, anaesthesia, ataxia and loss of mental faculties and memory, and deliriants) and opioids (binding to opioid receptors in the CNS and gastrointestinal tract) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 21 |
| psychoactivity is the common characteristic of all drugs — the capacity to reach the brain and alter its normal functioning; the effects of the commonest drugs are tabulated short-term and long-term: tobacco (short: relaxation, false concentration, reduced lung capacity, premature fatigue, dulled taste and smell, bad breath, yellow fingers and teeth, cough; long: grave damage to the respiratory and circulatory systems, cancers of the lung, larynx, oesophagus, kidney and bladder, sexual impotence) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 22 |
| alcohol, short-term (disinhibition, relaxation, euphoria, difficulty speaking and associating ideas, motor uncoordination, in grave cases ethyl coma and even death) and long-term (damage to the digestive tube, liver and pancreas, heart diseases, sexual impotence, alcoholic dementia); marijuana, short-term (euphoria, altered perception and memory, increased appetite, dry mouth, red shining eyes, tachycardia, sweating, lack of coordination) and long-term (disorders of memory, concentration and learning, changes of personality and humour, apathy, drowsiness, lack of motivation); inhalants, short-term (slurred speech, drunken or dizzy appearance, hallucinations, delirium, hostility, impaired judgement, loss of consciousness, strong headaches) and long-term (muscle weakness, disorientation, grave damage to heart, liver, kidneys, lungs and brain, memory deterioration, reduced intelligence, hearing loss, death by heart failure or asphyxia); cocaine, short-term (inhibited appetite, tachycardia, sweating, raised blood pressure, contracted blood vessels, raised body temperature) and long-term (perforation of the nasal septum, respiratory diseases, high risk of myocardial infarction and cerebral accident, sexual disorders, impotence, menstrual alterations, infertility) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 22 |
| addiction (drogadicción) defined as the imperious need to consume alcoholic drinks or any other drug: addictions can produce pleasure, relief and other compensations short-term, but provoke pain, disaster, desolation and many problems medium-term; the negative consequences affect health, family, society and work — deteriorated relationships, isolation, aggression, public disorder, marginalisation, loss of self-esteem and elevated guilt (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 23 |
| alcohol's social damage in Honduras, and the IHADFA evidence: alcohol is one of the principal factors of family disintegration, domestic violence, traffic accidents, work absenteeism, corruption and damage to public and private property, early school abandonment and youth vagrancy through alcoholic parents' neglect; the Honduran Institute against Drug Addiction (IHADFA, 2011) found 49.0% of students in public and private institutes of both sexes had consumed alcoholic drinks at some time in their lives (48.2% of boys, 49.7% of girls), with current or recent consumption declared by 9.6% (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 23 |
