Today: **Uric acid and sore joints** — Nepal's way, from its Curriculum Development Centre books.

Uric acid: the acid left when purines from our cells and food break down, normally cleared by the kidneys; when it builds up in the blood the joints ache deeply, redden and swell until walking is hard; bringing it down — drink plenty of water a little at a time, eat cherries (10 to 40 a day as printed, their anthocyanins easing the inflammation), and avoid fatty red meat, sea food and lentils.

Uric acid explained from both sides (guide evidence; lesson first proposed in the Grade 10 student book above): it is necessary — it clears plaque from vessels and is needed to build DNA and RNA — and only the excess harms, when urate crystals settle in the joints; purine-rich food, diabetes, weak kidneys and drinking little water named as causes; cherries and baking soda named among the controls, as printed.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
