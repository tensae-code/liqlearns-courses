# Lesson plan — Food — a balanced diet

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Food — a balanced diet
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a balanced diet: eating all the nutrient groups in the right mix — taught through the bread-and-tea child and a family meal-plan table the learner builds and reports on
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the six nutrients; this lesson mixes them rightly across a family's day.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The right mix** — eating all nutrient groups in the right mix is a balanced diet
2. **The bread-and-tea case** — the case study of a child fed only bread and tea
3. **The family meal-plan table (teacher guide — evidence)** — the meal-plan table the pupil builds and later reports on; the two regional guides' food-and-balanced-diet teaching stands as labelled evidence

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title in the Science course, in order. Recommended, never forced — a learner who lands here directly can start here.
- Nothing else is required; every term used is taught inside this lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a balanced diet is: all nutrient groups in the right mix.
2. Explain the bread-and-tea case: what it lacks and why it fails the body.
3. Build a family meal-plan table and report on it.

## What you need
Paper for the meal-plan table; the family's real meals to plan from.

## If the learner is stuck
- 'Balanced' is heard as 'a lot' → it is the mix of groups, not the size of the pile.
- The plan copies the book instead of the family → plan the family's real foods, sorted into the groups.
