# Food — a balanced diet

## Do it first

A child eats only bread and tea, every day. Bread and tea are real food — so what is wrong? Hold your answer until the case is read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The right mix

A balanced diet is eating all the nutrient groups in the right mix. No single food, and no single group, carries everything: the mix is the lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — The bread-and-tea case

The book's case study: a child fed only bread and tea. Bread and tea are not poisons — but alone, day after day, they leave out the groups that build, protect and strengthen. The case teaches the mix by showing its absence: a diet can fill the stomach and still fail the body.

## Sub lesson 3 — The family meal-plan table (teacher guide — evidence)

Now build it: a family meal-plan table for a day, with foods from every nutrient group in the right mix — and later report back on how the plan went at home. The two regional teacher guides teach this same ground — what food is, the nutrients food must contain, and the balanced diet — and their rows stand in this lesson's sources as labelled teacher-guide evidence.

## Say it to your companion

Say what a balanced diet is, retell the bread-and-tea case in two sentences, and present your own family meal-plan table group by group.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build a one-day family meal-plan table covering every nutrient group.
2. Carry the plan out for one day at home and report what changed.
3. Find one meal your family eats that misses a group, and name what could be added.
