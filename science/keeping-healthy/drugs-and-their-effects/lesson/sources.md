| §7-3 to §7-6, Misuse of drugs; the harmful effects of drug misuse; how to avoid drug addiction; inhalant misuse (إساءة استخدام العقاقير / الآثار الضارة لإساءة استخدام العقاقير / كيف نتجنب إدمان العقاقير؟ / إساءة استخدام المستنشقات): if drugs are so useful, how can they be harmful at the same time? Using drugs when they are not needed at all causes serious problems — misuse results when a person takes drugs intensively and without a doctor's prescription, and taking drugs in this way is very harmful to the body and may lead to death; the commonly misused drugs are classified into four main classes (Table 7-1): depressants (عقاقير مهبطة) — sleeping and calming drugs used as sleeping tablets to overcome insomnia and treat epilepsy, they "slow down" the central nervous system, calm the body and help the user sleep; stimulants (عقاقير منبهة) — amphetamine and cocaine, they stimulate the central nervous system and are used to fight depression, prevent tiredness and fight hunger; hallucinogens (عقاقير مهلوسة) — marijuana, they make the user feel hallucination, delusion, distorted images and a "high" feeling; narcotics/opiates (عقاقير مسكنة (الأفيونات)) — heroin, extracted from opium (the poppy plant), they relieve pain, calm and anaesthetise body and mind and create a false feeling of euphoria, and are usually injected as a solution, addicts often sharing the same syringe and needle; §7-4: tolerance (تحمل العقار) — the user is forced to take bigger and bigger doses to reach the same feeling; addiction (الإدمان) — the user cannot do without the drug, needing a continuous supply and often losing interest in anyone or anything else; when a person dependent on a drug stops taking it he feels ill and suffers strong withdrawal symptoms — anxiety, sweating, cramps, vomiting, diarrhoea, trembling and hallucination — and sudden withdrawal from certain drugs can cause death; the chapter summary adds that drug misuse can transmit deadly diseases such as AIDS through shared needles, and leads to crime; §7-5 avoiding addiction: teenagers try drugs for many reasons — pressure from bad companions, wanting to look grown-up, curiosity, boredom, or escaping reality; practical suggestions: avoid situations where people use drugs — if a friend encourages you to use drugs he truly does not care about your health, and you must leave him at once; make excuses to leave the situation; be firm in your stand against drugs, remembering that misusing them is against religion and the law and its punishment, and the punishment for trafficking in them, is severe; if you know someone misusing drugs, find a way to help him through one of the bodies named (the family; the Arab Union for the Prevention of Addiction — raising parents' awareness, encouraging young people towards hobbies and sport, teaching prevention by community participation, giving addicts useful skills, helping them find jobs and medical advice, cooperating with official bodies and families; the National Centre for Combating Communicable and Endemic Diseases — treatment and rehabilitation supervision and awareness campaigns; the Anti-Narcotics Agency جهاز مكافحة المخدرات — combating trafficking and use, following up treatment rehabilitation centres, awareness campaigns); §7-6 inhalant misuse (إساءة استخدام المستنشقات): materials such as glue, thinners and paint contain solvents that evaporate when exposed to air, and these vapours, called inhalants, can make a person addicted when inhaled; repeated inhalant misuse can result in brain damage and may lead to death; signs and symptoms: a nose that is always running, sores on the mouth and nose, bloodshot eyes, confusion and disorientation, and an unkempt personal appearance (Libya, G8 SB T1) — placement filed as NEW LESSON NEEDED — drug-misuse-tolerance-addiction-and-inhalant-abuse;
