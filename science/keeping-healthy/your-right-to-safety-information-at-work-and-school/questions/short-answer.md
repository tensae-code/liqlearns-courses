# Your right to safety information at work and school — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: a right to know.
**Answer: People who work or learn in a place have the right to know what dangers that place holds — which substances are hazardous, which machines can hurt, where the fire equipment and exits are — and to be taught how to protect themselves. Nobody has to guess about a hazard that others already know about.**

**2.** Explain: labels and instructions are part of the right.
**Answer: Hazard signs, warning labels on containers, and safety instructions are how the right is delivered. Reading them before handling a substance or starting a task is using the right; a container with no label, or a task with no instruction, is the right not being met — and that should be reported, not silently accepted.**

**3.** Explain: asking is exercising the right.
**Answer: If the information is missing, the safe and proper move is to ask — a teacher, a supervisor, a safety officer — before starting. Asking about a hazard is never wrong, and being refused safety information is a wrong done to the person who asked.**
