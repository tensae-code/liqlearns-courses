# Video script — Your right to safety information at work and school

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: Your right to safety information at work and school.

Remember this one thing: every person at work or at school has the right to be told about the hazards around them and how to stay safe from them — safety information is a right, not a favour.

People who work or learn in a place have the right to know what dangers that place holds — which substances are hazardous, which machines can hurt, where the fire equipment and exits are — and to be taught how to protect themselves. Nobody has to guess about a hazard that others already know about.

Hazard signs, warning labels on containers, and safety instructions are how the right is delivered. Reading them before handling a substance or starting a task is using the right; a container with no label, or a task with no instruction, is the right not being met — and that should be reported, not silently accepted.

If the information is missing, the safe and proper move is to ask — a teacher, a supervisor, a safety officer — before starting. Asking about a hazard is never wrong, and being refused safety information is a wrong done to the person who asked.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
