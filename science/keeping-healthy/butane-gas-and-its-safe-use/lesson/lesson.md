# Butane gas and its safe use

## Do it first
The gas in a cooker bottle has no colour, yet it can be poured like a liquid when it is squeezed into its bottle. What gas is it, and where else does it work in the home? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 5 book teaches butane as its own lesson, between the gas stove's parts and charcoal.

## Sub lesson 1 — Butane — colourless, and easily liquefied

Mauritania's Grade 5 *Sciences Naturelles* book defines **butane** by its two handling facts (printed p. 93): it is **colourless** — a gas you cannot see — and it is **easily liquefied** — squeezed, it becomes a liquid, which is how so much of it fits inside a cooker bottle or a lighter.

Those two facts are the safety facts too: a gas you cannot see must be handled by knowledge, not by sight, and a gas stored as liquid under pressure lives in its bottle until its valve lets it out as gas.

## Sub lesson 2 — Butane at work — and gas preferred over charcoal

The book names butane's work: it is **used in cookers and lighters** — the flame in the kitchen and the flame in the hand both burn it.

And it closes with a choice: **gas is preferred over charcoal to protect the environment**. Charcoal, the book's next lesson teaches, is made by burning wood away from air — every charcoal fire begins with cut wood. Cooking with gas instead spares that wood, and the book teaches the preference as part of the science.

## The key ideas

- **Butane** is a **colourless** gas that is **easily liquefied** — stored as a liquid under pressure in bottles and lighters.
- Butane's work: **cookers and lighters**.
- Mauritania's Grade 5 book **prefers gas over charcoal to protect the environment** — charcoal begins with cut wood.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why must butane be handled by knowledge rather than by sight?" Answer from the lesson: Because it is colourless — a gas you cannot see. Its handling facts (colourless, easily liquefied, stored under pressure) are what the user must know and act on.
