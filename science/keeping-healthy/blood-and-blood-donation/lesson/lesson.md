# Blood and blood donation

## Do it first
Your heart beats and substances travel around your body — the circulation you met in the heart lesson. Now suppose a person loses a lot of that travelling blood. Where could replacement blood come from? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Cuba's way in — right after circulation, its Grade 6 book teaches the importance of blood donations.

## Sub lesson 1 — Blood — the body's carrier

The book's order is the lesson's order: first circulation — **the heart distributes substances around the body through the blood** — and only then donation. Blood is the body's carrier: what it carries keeps every part alive, which is why losing much of it is dangerous, and why blood itself can be given.

## Sub lesson 2 — Donating blood — Cuba's own way

A healthy person can **give a part of their blood** — a **blood donation** — and the body replaces what was given. The given blood goes to people whose lives depend on receiving it. Cuba's book teaches this as an important act in its own right: in Cuba, donating blood is presented to Grade 6 learners as something that matters — a gift of the carrier itself, from one person's body to another's.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why can blood be donated, and why does it matter?" Answer: Because blood is the body's carrier and a healthy giver's body replaces what was given; the donated blood can keep alive a person who has lost much of their own.
