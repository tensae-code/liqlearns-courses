# Sanitation — clean water and waste

## Do it first
Think of the water your home drinks, and of where your home's rubbish and dirty water go. What keeps them from meeting? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 4 *Estudo do Meio* book teaches basic sanitation (Saneamento básico) right before its lesson on water itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Basic sanitation — clean water kept clean

**Basic sanitation** (Saneamento básico) is the set of arrangements that keep water and waste apart so people stay healthy. Its first half is **clean water**: water for drinking and the home must be drawn, carried and stored so that dirt and germs cannot get back into it — the subject Angola's Grade 4 book sets immediately before its water lesson, because sanitation is what makes water safe to use.

## Sub lesson 2 — Waste and dirty water — taken away safely

The second half is **waste**: rubbish, dirty water and the latrine must be placed and kept so they cannot reach the water people drink or the ground children play on. Waste left anywhere returns — in flies, in smells, in sickness. Basic sanitation is a whole: clean water in, waste safely away, and the ground and air of home and neighbourhood kept between them.

## The key ideas

- **Basic sanitation** keeps water and waste apart so people stay healthy.
- Clean water must be drawn, carried and stored so dirt and germs **cannot get back in**.
- Waste — rubbish, dirty water, the latrine — must be kept where it **cannot reach** drinking water or play ground.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is basic sanitation, in one sentence?" Answer from the lesson: It is the set of arrangements — clean water kept clean, and waste taken safely away — that keep a home and neighbourhood healthy.

## Do It Yourself
1. Walk your home's water path: where drinking water is drawn, carried and stored. Name one place dirt could get in, and how it is stopped.
2. Walk your home's waste path: rubbish, dirty water, latrine. Say whether each is kept away from water and play ground, and what you would move if not.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
