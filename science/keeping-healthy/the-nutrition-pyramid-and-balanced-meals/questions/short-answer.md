# The nutrition pyramid and balanced meals — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Eating a balanced, healthy diet.
**Answer:** Vietnam's Grade 4 *Khoa học 4* opens the lesson by judging three days of real menus: a balanced diet mixes the food kinds across the day, and plant and animal protein and fat must be combined — neither side alone gives the body everything.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Vietnam's Nutrition Pyramid for children aged 6–11.
**Answer:** The book sets out Vietnam's own Nutrition Pyramid (National Institute of Nutrition, 2021) for children aged 6–11: daily amounts for each layer, from **cereals at the wide base** up to **salt at the narrow top — under 4 g a day** — with **1,300–1,500 ml of water** and **60 minutes of physical activity** a day standing beside the pyramid.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Judging real meals against the pyramid.
**Answer:** Learners judge real school and home meal trays against the pyramid's layers and amounts. Vietnam's own warning closes the lesson: dipping sauces — nước chấm and soup powder — quietly add much salt to meals, so a tray can break the pyramid's top rule without looking salty.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Eating a balanced, healthy diet → Vietnam's Nutrition Pyramid for children aged 6–11 → Judging real meals against the pyramid — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
