# The nutrition pyramid and balanced meals

## Do it first
Before you read: say, in one sentence, what you think this lesson's one thing is, from its title alone — **The nutrition pyramid and balanced meals**. Then read on: this is Vietnam's way in — its books teach this thing at the places cited in the sources, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Eating a balanced, healthy diet

Vietnam's Grade 4 *Khoa học 4* opens the lesson by judging three days of real menus: a balanced diet mixes the food kinds across the day, and plant and animal protein and fat must be combined — neither side alone gives the body everything.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vietnam's Nutrition Pyramid for children aged 6–11

The book sets out Vietnam's own Nutrition Pyramid (National Institute of Nutrition, 2021) for children aged 6–11: daily amounts for each layer, from **cereals at the wide base** up to **salt at the narrow top — under 4 g a day** — with **1,300–1,500 ml of water** and **60 minutes of physical activity** a day standing beside the pyramid.

## Sub lesson 3 — Judging real meals against the pyramid

Learners judge real school and home meal trays against the pyramid's layers and amounts. Vietnam's own warning closes the lesson: dipping sauces — nước chấm and soup powder — quietly add much salt to meals, so a tray can break the pyramid's top rule without looking salty.
