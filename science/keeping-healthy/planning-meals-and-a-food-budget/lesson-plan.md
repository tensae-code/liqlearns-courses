# Lesson plan — Planning meals and a food budget

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Planning meals and a food budget
**Place in the curriculum:** appended to Unit 14 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** planning meals and a food budget — meal planning and budgeting as Zimbabwe's Grade 4 and 5 guides teach the unit
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Meal planning** — Zimbabwe's Grade 4 and Grade 5 guides both teach **meal planning** as a named unit: meals are planned ahead, not decided by chance at the cooking fire.
2. **Budgeting for food** — The unit's own full title is meal planning **and budgeting**: the plan must fit the money there is, and the budgeting is taught as part of the same skill.
3. **A plan that feeds well** — Planning and budgeting join in one test: the planned meals must still feed the family well. That test is the lesson's whole point — a cheap plan that starves is not a plan.
