# Fire safety and escape

## Do it first
Before you read: this lesson teaches one thing — fire safety and escape — putting out burning clothes you are wearing, escaping from a fire, and the safe place to flee to — as placed from Laos’s Grade 3 Science and Environment book, Ch. 3. What do you already know about it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Laos's way in — its Science and Environment books teach this as its own topic, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — If your clothes catch fire

Laos’s Grade 3 book (Ch. 3, printed pp. 13–16) opens its fire teaching with the person, not the building: **putting out burning clothes you are wearing**. When the fire is on your own clothes, the first danger is to you, and the first job is to put that fire out.

The book teaches this as a named skill of the chapter — a thing to know before it is ever needed, because burning clothes give no time to work out what to do.

## Sub lesson 2 — Escaping from a fire — and the safe place

The chapter continues: **escaping from a fire place**, and **the safe place to flee to**. Escape is not just getting out — it is getting out to a known safe place, chosen before any fire: fleeing has a destination.

Together the chapter’s three parts are one order of safety: put out the fire on the person, get out of the fire place, and go to the safe place. Laos teaches all three in one chapter, in that order — the person first, then the place, then the refuge.
