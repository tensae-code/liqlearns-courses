# Reading food labels — nutrition information

## Do it first
Before you read: say, in one sentence, what you think this lesson's one thing is, from its title alone — **Reading food labels — nutrition information**. Then read on: this is Indonesia's way in — its book teaches this thing at the place cited in the sources, in the order below.


## Sub lesson 1 — The label on the packet

Packaged food carries its story on the packet: the **nutrition information** — the printed panel that says what is inside the food and how much of each thing one portion holds. Indonesia's Grade 6 book (Bab 1, nutritious food for your digestion) does not teach this from a picture of a label: pupils collect **snack packaging from around the school** and read the real labels on the food they actually meet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sugar and salt — the figures to watch

A label holds many numbers, and the book gives its guidance on where to look first: **sugar and salt**. Those two are the figures a reader checks before eating — how much sugar the snack carries, and how much salt — because both are easy to take far too much of without ever noticing, packet by packet.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing from what the label says

Reading the label is for **choosing**: two snacks side by side can be compared by their printed figures, and the reader picks knowing what is inside instead of guessing from the packet's front. That is the lesson's whole point — the nutrition information is there to be read, and a reader who checks sugar and salt first eats with open eyes.
