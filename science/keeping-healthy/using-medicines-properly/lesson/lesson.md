# Using medicines properly

## Do it first
A medicine is a help with a measure. Too little, and it does not do its work. Too much, guessed bravely, and a help can become a harm. The Oromia teachers' guide gives this its own subtopic — using medicines properly — and this short lesson keeps its title and its point.

## Properly — the guide's word (teacher guide — evidence)
The **Oromia Grade 4 teacher guide** names the subtopic **መድሃንቶችን በአግባብ መጠቀም — using medicines properly**. (Teacher guide — evidence.) Its picture is the point in one scene: **a medicine bottle with its dose measured by a health worker**.

Proper use, held to what the guide's subtopic and picture give: the **dose is the health worker's measure** — medicines are taken **as directed**, in the amount directed, for the person directed. A medicine measured for one person, one sickness and one body is not a guess to be copied by another.

Hold this beside the stimulants lesson and do not confuse them: stimulants are harms to keep away from; medicines are helps to use **properly**.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the guide's title in your own words, what 'properly' guards (dose, direction, the right person), and how this lesson differs from the stimulants lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Direction reading: at home, with an adult, read one real medicine's directions or label together — read-only, hands off the contents.
2. Two-column sort: HELPS USED PROPERLY / HARMS TO KEEP AWAY. Place: a health worker's measured medicine; chat; a prescribed syrup taken as directed; tobacco. Defend each place.
3. Ask the health worker: on a clinic visit, ask how a dose is decided. One sentence of the answer is the lesson from a real voice.
