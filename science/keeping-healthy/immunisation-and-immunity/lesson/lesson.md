# Immunisation and immunity

## Do it first
Before you read: how can a child be protected from a disease the child has never had — protected before the disease ever arrives? And why do clinics write every visit on a card the mother keeps? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Uganda's way in — its Primary Five home-study booklet opens its whole science course with immunisation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Immunity and immunisation — how vaccines make the body resistant

**Immunity** is the body's resistance to a disease. **Immunisation** builds that resistance before the disease arrives: a **vaccine** makes the body resistant, so the disease cannot take hold.

The booklet's big claim is plain: immunisation **cuts child deaths** — children who are immunised do not die of diseases that kill the unimmunised.

## Sub lesson 2 — Childhood immunisable diseases — measles and the immunisation table

Some childhood diseases can be prevented by immunisation — they are the **immunisable diseases**. The booklet's worked example is **measles**: a **virus** disease, with **sores and a rash**; its vaccine is given **by injection in the left upper arm**.

The booklet's **immunisation table** lays the childhood immunisations out together — which vaccine, and when — so a family can see the whole course of protection at once.

## Sub lesson 3 — Other immunisable diseases — cholera prevention — and the child health card

The booklet widens the list to other immunisable diseases, and for **cholera** it gives the prevention a home can practise alongside: **boiled water** and **covering food**.

Every immunisation is written down on the **child health card** — the booklet closes by teaching what is recorded at immunisation, so the card itself becomes the child's record of protection.

## The key ideas

- **Immunisation** uses **vaccines** to make the body **resistant** — protection built before the disease arrives — and it **cuts child deaths**.
- **Measles** is a virus disease (sores, rash); its vaccine is given **by injection in the left upper arm**; the **immunisation table** lays out the childhood course.
- **Cholera** prevention at home is **boiled water** and **covering food**; every immunisation is recorded on the **child health card**.
