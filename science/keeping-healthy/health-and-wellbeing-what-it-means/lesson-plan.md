# Lesson plan — Health and wellbeing — what it means

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Health and wellbeing — what it means
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — appended to Unit 14 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what health and wellbeing means — Oraanga e Pitoenua in the Cook Islands book's own words — that opinions about what it means differ from person to person, and that you find out what your community thinks by asking them — the Cook Islands way in, at the opening of its Grade 8 health-science inquiry book
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Oraanga e Pitoenua — what health and wellbeing means** — Health and wellbeing is the state the whole book's class research project is about: being well in body and in life, not only not being sick.
2. **Opinions differ** — Ask different people what health and wellbeing means and the answers differ — each person names what matters to them.
3. **Asking the community** — The book's way to find out is to ask the community itself: prepare questions, ask people, and listen to what they say.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Oraanga e Pitoenua — what health and wellbeing means.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Opinions differ.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: Asking the community.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks health is only "not being sick" → return to the book's own frame: wellbeing is wider than sickness, and people's own answers show how wide.
