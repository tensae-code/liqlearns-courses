# Healthy food and spoiled food

## Do it first
Here are two plates. One keeps a body strong. One harms it slowly. Which is which — and how can you tell by looking, smelling, and thinking about what is in it? That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Healthy and unhealthy food

The Grade 2 teacher guide sorts food into healthy and unhealthy inside its weather subtopic — recorded here as the book prints it, not tidied away. **Healthy food** is uncontaminated, fresh, and low in sugar, salt and fat. **Unhealthy food** is spoiled, unhygienic, or overloaded with sugar, salt and fat. A healthy plate — grains, vegetables, egg — feeds growth and strength. A pile of sweets and fried snacks loads the body with sugar and fat and gives little back.

## Sub lesson 2 — Spoiled food and keeping food safe

The Grade 3 guide teaches the same line from the other side: healthy food has **no unusual smell, taste or appearance**; contaminated food is food spoiled by **micro-organisms** and other harmful things, and it exposes people to various diseases. Preparing the lesson, pupils in the guide stage a short drama showing how healthy foods and drinks are prepared, stored and eaten.

And food is **preserved** — kept safe for longer — the ways the guide's answers name: keeping it **refrigerated**, **drying** it, and **salting** it.

[Picture 12110 — fresh food beside spoiled food with flies over it]

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the healthy-food rules and the spoiled-food danger signs. Your companion names a food; you judge it — healthy, unhealthy, or spoiled — and give your reason.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort ten foods from your home into HEALTHY and NOT TOO MUCH piles, using the lesson's rules, and defend the hardest one.
2. Smell and look at food before eating it today. If it smells or looks unusual, do not eat it — tell an adult.
3. Ask at home how food is kept safe: dried, salted, or cooled — and bring back one method.
