# Lesson plan — Healthy food and spoiled food

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Healthy food and spoiled food
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — the food side of health (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** telling healthy food from unhealthy and spoiled food — fresh, clean and not overloaded with sugar, salt and fat, against spoiled, unhygienic food
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Keeping our bodies clean — clean hands and clean surroundings; this lesson turns the same cleanliness onto food. This lesson rests on teacher-guide evidence only; its sources say so plainly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what makes food healthy: uncontaminated, fresh, and not overloaded with sugar, salt or fat.
2. Say what makes food unhealthy or spoiled: spoiled, unhygienic, or overloaded with sugar, salt and fat; spoiled by micro-organisms, with an unusual smell, taste or look.
3. Say how food is preserved the books' way: refrigerated, dried, salted.
4. Say the danger: contaminated food exposes people to various diseases.

## What you need
Real foods or pictures of foods to sort; a place to wash hands first.

## If the learner is stuck
- Judges by taste alone ('sweet means good') → the rule is not sweetness: fresh, clean, and not overloaded with sugar, salt or fat.
- Cannot tell spoiled food → use the three danger signs together: unusual smell, unusual taste, unusual look.
