# Chat, tobacco and alcohol: stimulants and their harm

## Do it first
Plants feed us — teff, maize, coffee, greens. But not everything green is food. Some plants are taken as stimulant 'medicines', and when they are misused, their harm outweighs any use. The book names them without softness, and counts their harm in full. This lesson does the same.

## Stimulants, and the counting of their harm
The book names **chat** and **tobacco — the cigarette plant** — with **alcohol** beside them: stimulant plants and drinks, used wrongly as 'medicines', whose **harm outweighs their use when misused**.

The harms, as the book counts them: they **harm mental health**; they **waste working time**; they **lower productivity**; they **encourage crime**; and they **expose users to mental illness and HIV/AIDS**.

The book's activity puts pupils as **guests on a community-health radio programme** — the harms are meant to be said aloud, in public, in the community's own voice. And its closing counsel is a roll-call: the successful people of the community — **pupils, farmers, traders, teachers, doctors, soldiers, police, judges** — are those who **keep away from harmful drugs**.

## Grade 5 — addictive substances and addiction; why people start, what it costs
The Grade 5 chapter opens with two stories: a shopkeeper who cannot function without his 8-o'clock chat, and a wife who cannot start her day without coffee. **Addictive chemicals and dangerous drugs damage brain and body**; repeated use bends the body's normal working until the person is ruled by the substance — that slavery is **addiction (የዕፅ ሱስ)**. Drug kinds sort by effect: **stimulants, narcotics, and those that dull pain/feeling**. Why people start — push factors printed: **the community's cultural habits, easy availability, peer pressure, migration and joblessness**. The two-sided account: positives claimed (stimulation, focus, brief groundless happiness) against the negatives — **heavy money loss, mental disorder, sleeplessness, self-neglect, lost appetite, family break-up, a wasted life** — and the behaviour changes: suspecting everyone, quarrelsomeness, living in a fantasy world, distorted decisions, emotional rashness, lost self-control. The case activity: Getachew the driver, whose hands shake without areke — what do you advise him?

## Grade 6 — Ayele and Seada (story); addiction and the region's drugs; borders and sport
**Ayele and Seada** (plot summary only): close friends in Debre Berhan town, both Grade 6 pupils who support their schooling by selling lottery tickets and face masks into the evening. Following friends who do the same work, they take up cigarettes and other smoked things, and sometimes chew chat. Their homeroom teacher, noticing, is angered — but decides to **rescue the pupils rather than merely punish** them; the class discusses why the teacher counts the behaviour a problem and what she could do. Addiction restated: taken by mouth or injection, repeated use makes an addict — **ruled by the substance, unable to control himself, unable to stop, neglecting himself, his whole social life shaken**; both addictive chemicals and narcotic drugs **alter the body's normal functioning** — pain-relieving medicines such as **aspirin** and **alcohol** drinks are the printed examples of function-altering chemicals. The region's named narcotic drugs: **chat, cigarettes, cocaine, heroin and morphine** — in East Africa best-known are **chat and cigarettes**, the cigarette carrying the stimulant **nicotine**, chat carrying **cathinone**. Two discussion fields close the teaching: **contraband movements across the region's borders** — what should East African countries do together? — and **doped athletes**: the Olympic committee medically tests famous athletes, strips the medals of users and bars them — why is this done, why do athletes use the drugs, and is using them proper?

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the three stimulants, the five harms in the book's counting, and the roll-call of successful people who keep away.

## Do It Yourself
1. Radio guest, the book's activity: with two friends, stage the community-health programme — one hosts, two guest. Topic: the harms of chat, tobacco and alcohol, from the lesson.
2. Time audit: the book counts wasted working time as a harm. Ask a working adult what an hour of work is worth — in grain, in money, in finished tasks. Time is the currency stimulants steal first.
3. Roll-call poster: write the book's closing counsel — successful people keep away — with the roll-call of workers beneath it. Pin it where your family can see it.
