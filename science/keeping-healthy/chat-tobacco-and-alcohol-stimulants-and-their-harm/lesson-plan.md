# Lesson plan — Chat, tobacco and alcohol: stimulants and their harm

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Chat, tobacco and alcohol: stimulants and their harm
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy — the urgent-issues chapter at Grade 4 depth: the stimulant plants and drinks (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** chat, tobacco and alcohol are stimulants whose harm outweighs their use when misused: they harm mental health, waste working time, lower productivity, encourage crime, and expose users to mental illness and HIV/AIDS — and the successful people of the community are those who keep away from harmful drugs
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What disease is, and how it spreads — harm travelled by germs there; here harm rides in things people choose to take.

## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Keeping Healthy — What disease is, and how it spreads. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the stimulants the book names: chat, tobacco (the cigarette plant), and alcohol.
2. List their harms: harm to mental health, wasted working time, lowered productivity, encouraged crime, exposure to mental illness and HIV/AIDS.
3. Say the book's closing counsel: successful people in the community — pupils, farmers, traders, teachers, doctors, soldiers, police, judges — are those who keep away from harmful drugs.

## What you need
Nothing to handle — this lesson is told, never shown. The book's activity is a radio discussion; the classroom version needs only voices.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls them medicines without the warning → the book's quotes matter: stimulant 'medicines' whose harm outweighs their use WHEN MISUSED — the misuse is the lesson.
- Thinks the harms are only sickness → the book counts time, work, crime and exposure to HIV/AIDS too; the harm is social as well as bodily.
