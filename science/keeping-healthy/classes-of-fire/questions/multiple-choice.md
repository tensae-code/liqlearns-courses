# Classes of fire — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** What does this lesson say about classes of fire?
a) Fires are sorted into classes by what is burning — A: solids like wood and paper, B: flammable liquids, C: gases, D: metals, E: electrical fires, F: cooking oils and fats — because the right way to fight a fire depends on its class.
b) It plays no part in this topic at all
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: a) Fires are sorted into classes by what is burning — A: solids like wood and paper, B: flammable liquids, C: gases, D: metals, E: electrical fires, F: cooking oils and fats — because the right way to fight a fire depends on its class.**

**2.** What does this lesson say about class by fuel?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) The classes sort fires by what burns.
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: b) The classes sort fires by what burns.**

**3.** What does this lesson say about why the class matters?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) The class decides the safe response.
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: c) The class decides the safe response.**

**4.** What does this lesson say about name it before you fight it?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) It is found only in non-living things
d) The first step with any fire is to identify what is burning.
