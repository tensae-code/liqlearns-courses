# Classes of fire

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Classes of fire. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Kenya's Grade 8 Integrated Science book, inside its oxygen strand, teaches fire as a classified thing: before you can control a fire you must know which class it belongs to, and the class is named by the fuel.

In your mind's eye: fires are sorted into classes by what is burning — A: solids like wood and paper, B: flammable liquids, C: gases, D: metals, E: electrical fires, F: cooking oils and fats — because the right way to fight a fire depends on its class. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Class by fuel.** The classes sort fires by what burns. Class A fires burn solid materials — wood, paper, cloth, rubbish. Class B fires burn flammable liquids — petrol, paraffin, paint. Class C fires burn gases — cooking gas and other fuel gases. Class D fires burn metals. Class E fires involve live electrical equipment. Class F fires burn cooking oils and fats, as in a deep pan or fryer.

**Why the class matters.** The class decides the safe response. Water cools a Class A fire but spreads a burning liquid and is dangerous on live electricity and on burning oil. Knowing the class is knowing which extinguisher and which method can be used — and which must never be used.

**Name it before you fight it.** The first step with any fire is to identify what is burning. Look at the fuel, name the class, and only then choose how to act — or judge the fire too big, raise the alarm and get out. A small fire of the wrong class fought the wrong way becomes a big fire.
