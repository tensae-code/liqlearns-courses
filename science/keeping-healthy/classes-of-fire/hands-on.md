# Hands-on — Classes of fire

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Walk through a kitchen, a workshop and a classroom (in your mind or in fact) and list what could burn in each. Sort your list into the fire classes A–F. For each item, say why it belongs to its class.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of classes of fire — fires are sorted into classes by what is burning — A: solids like wood and paper, B: flammable liquids, C: gases, D: metals, E: electrical fires, F: cooking oils and fats — because the right way to fight a fire depends on its class. — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
