Today: Classes of fire.

Remember this one thing: fires are sorted into classes by what is burning — A: solids like wood and paper, B: flammable liquids, C: gases, D: metals, E: electrical fires, F: cooking oils and fats — because the right way to fight a fire depends on its class.

The classes sort fires by what burns. Class A fires burn solid materials — wood, paper, cloth, rubbish. Class B fires burn flammable liquids — petrol, paraffin, paint. Class C fires burn gases — cooking gas and other fuel gases. Class D fires burn metals. Class E fires involve live electrical equipment. Class F fires burn cooking oils and fats, as in a deep pan or fryer.

The class decides the safe response. Water cools a Class A fire but spreads a burning liquid and is dangerous on live electricity and on burning oil. Knowing the class is knowing which extinguisher and which method can be used — and which must never be used.

The first step with any fire is to identify what is burning. Look at the fuel, name the class, and only then choose how to act — or judge the fire too big, raise the alarm and get out. A small fire of the wrong class fought the wrong way becomes a big fire.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
