# Lesson plan — Food — where our food comes from

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Keeping Healthy · **Lesson:** Food — where our food comes from
**Place in the curriculum:** Keeping Healthy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** where our food comes from: food is anything eaten or drunk that suits health, and the book's foods sort into four source groups
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Food — what food is and the four food groups; this lesson sorts a whole spread of foods by their source.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Food that suits health** — food is anything eaten or drunk that suits health
2. **Sorting foods by source** — the book's spread of about 33 foods, sorted by pupils into the four source groups

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title in the Science course, in order. Recommended, never forced — a learner who lands here directly can start here.
- Nothing else is required; every term used is taught inside this lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what food is in the Grade 4 book's words: anything eaten or drunk that suits health.
2. Sort a spread of foods into the four source groups.

## What you need
Pictures of foods, or the learner's own list of home foods; paper for the sorting.

## If the learner is stuck
- A food fits two groups → name its main source first; mixed dishes are taken apart into their parts.
- The two definitions seem different → they say the same thing two ways: useful/suits health, and no harm.
