# Food — where our food comes from

## Do it first

Name ten foods you know. Where does each one come from — a plant, an animal, or somewhere else? Hold your answers; the lesson sorts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Food that suits health

Food is anything eaten or drunk that suits health. That is the Grade 4 book's own wording, and it stands beside the Grade 3 wording: what we eat and drink that carries useful things and does no harm.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting foods by source

The book spreads out pictures of about thirty-three foods, and the pupils' work is to sort them into the four source groups — the groups food comes from. Do the same sorting with your own foods: name the food, name its source, and put it in its group. A food you cannot place is a question to bring back, not a guess to hide.

## Say it to your companion

Give the Grade 4 definition of food, then sort six foods of your own into their source groups, saying the source aloud for each.

## Do It Yourself

1. List ten foods from your home and sort each into its source group.
2. Ask at home where three of this week's foods came from before they reached your table.
3. Draw the four source groups as four baskets and fill each with two foods.
