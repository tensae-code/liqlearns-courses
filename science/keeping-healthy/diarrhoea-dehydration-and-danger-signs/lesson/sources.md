| diarrhoea defined (three or more loose or liquid stools a day) in three clinical types: acute watery diarrhoea (hours to days — e.g. cholera), acute bloody diarrhoea (dysentery) and persistent diarrhoea (14 days or more) (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 21 |
| the causes of acute diarrhoeal diseases: water contaminated by sewage, septic tanks or latrines; infections by parasites, bacteria and viruses; inadequate nutrition; and transmission person to person, through badly cooked or insalubriously prepared food, seafood from contaminated waters, and food irrigated with contaminated water or fertilised with chemicals (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 21 |
| diarrhoea's toll: developing-country children under five commonly suffer at least three episodes a year, which steal growth and proper brain development and can kill — WHO (2013) figures of some 760,000 child deaths a year; basic access to drinking water, sanitation and hygiene at home is what prevents cases (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 21 |
| dehydration defined as the loss of the body fluids the body needs to function — the most dangerous factor in diarrhoeal illness, able to kill or leave sequelae; its signs run from dry mouth and eyes, lethargy and sunken eyes to loss of consciousness, and it is graded mild, moderate and severe (the last a potentially mortal emergency) (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 22 |
| rotavirus: the virus causing gastroenteritis and the main common cause of diarrhoea in children from 6 months to 5 years, its spread tied to malnutrition, low family education and lack of drinking water and basic services (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 22 |
| Escherichia coli's double face: mostly a beneficial bacterium that helps produce vitamin K, but its enterohemorrhagic type causes gastroenteritis worldwide and the hemolytic-uremic syndrome, the common cause of acute kidney failure in children under five (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 22 |
