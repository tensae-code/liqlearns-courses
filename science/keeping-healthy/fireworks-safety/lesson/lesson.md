# Fireworks safety

## Do it first
Picture a celebration night: bright bursts in the sky, loud bangs, a crowd watching. Fireworks light the sky — and they burn and explode at the same time. Say one rule you would give a child standing near them. Then read on: Iceland's Grade 3 safety chapter names learning how to handle fireworks as an aim of its own.

## Sub lesson 1 — What fireworks are — fire that bursts in the sky

**Fireworks** are made to burn and burst with light, colour and noise. That is what makes them celebration — and what makes them dangerous: the same fire that paints the sky can burn skin and harm eyes and ears at close range.

## Sub lesson 2 — Handling fireworks safely — the chapter's own aim

Because fireworks are fire, they must be **handled safely**: Iceland's aims sheet puts learning how to handle fireworks inside its "Safe in Our Surroundings" chapter, beside playground dangers and harbour equipment — safety knowledge a child is taught outright, not left to chance on the night.

## The key ideas

- Fireworks burn and burst with light, colour and noise — celebration and danger in the same thing.
- The fire that paints the sky can burn and harm at close range.
- Iceland teaches handling fireworks safely as its own named aim in the safety chapter.
