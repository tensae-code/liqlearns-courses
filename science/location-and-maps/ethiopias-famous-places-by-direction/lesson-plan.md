# Lesson plan — Ethiopia's Famous Places by Direction

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Location and Maps · **Lesson:** Ethiopia's Famous Places by Direction
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** placing Ethiopia's famous places by their direction from Addis Ababa — the heritage sites and the natural sites alike.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the directions and relative-location lessons — a whole country now becomes the map, and the capital its centre.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Famous places from Addis Ababa** — from the capital, pupils place the heritage sites — Aksum obelisks, Fasil Ghebbi, Harar Jugol, Tiya stelae, Abba Jifar palace — and the natural sites by direction: Adwa north, Gambela and the Baro west, Chebera Churchura south, Erta Ale north-east, Karamara east (Grade 5).

2. **Famous places on the map (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 5 guide's famous-places subtopics: locating Lalibela, Aksum, Gondar and Harar on the map, and making different maps to point the places out (teacher guide — evidence).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's own world (home, school, neighbourhood) supplies the rest.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Stand at the centre of the room and call it Addis Ababa. Point north, east, south, west. A partner names a famous place — Aksum, Harar, Gambela. Point the way it lies from the capital. Try it first. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts of the lesson in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD Picture [Picture 13306]: a simple Ethiopia map with Addis Ababa at the centre and the named sites placed by direction — the student studies it and names what it shows in the lesson's own words.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a blank Ethiopia map being filled in step by step with the famous places — the student studies it and names what it shows in the lesson's own words.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing to a companion and answers one question back (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
- The learner cannot do the Do It Yourself → do the first item together, then let them finish the rest alone.
