# The National Grid and Grid References

## Do it first

Draw a squared grid on paper, six squares by six. Hide a cross in one square. Tell a partner which square it is in using only the numbers along the bottom and up the side. Try it first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The National Grid — eastings and northings

The **National Grid** is a square grid laid over the whole country. Its origin — its zero corner — sits where the **equator meets 34°30′ E**. The grid's vertical lines, running up and down, are called **eastings**, because their numbers grow toward the east. The horizontal lines are **northings**, growing toward the north. Every square of the country has its pair of numbers, and a position is given as a **grid reference**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Four figures and six figures

A **4-figure** reference locates a whole square. The book's worked example is **1845**: read the easting first — 18, along the corridor — then the northing — 45, up the stairs — and you have the square. A **6-figure** reference pinpoints a place *inside* the square: the worked example **234457** splits each side further, so the place is fixed not just to its square but to its spot within it. The order never changes: **eastings first, then northings** — along the corridor, then up the stairs.
