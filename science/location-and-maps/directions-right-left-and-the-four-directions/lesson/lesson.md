# Directions — Right, Left and the Four Directions

## Do it first

Stand up. Stretch out both arms. Which hand is your right hand? Turn around slowly. What is in front of you now? What is behind you? Try this before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Right and left, front and back, on your own body

First we learn directions on our own bodies: this is my right hand, this is my left hand; this is in front of me, this is behind my back. Then we use the same words for things around us — the desk is in front of me, the door is behind me.

## Sub lesson 2 — Finding east and west from the sun (teacher guide — evidence)

The four directions are found from the sun. In the morning the sun rises — that side is east. In the evening the sun sets — that side is west. Face the sunrise with your arms spread wide: your right hand points one way along the sun's path and your left hand the other, and in front of you and behind you lie the other two directions.

## Sub lesson 3 — The four main directions, and places beside the road

Facing the sunrise with arms spread, we name all four: east where the sun rises, west where it sets, and north and south to the sides. We use them with real places: the school stands on one side of the road and the police station on the other; one kebele lies across the river from another.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: the directions — right and left, front and back on your own body, and east, west, north and south found from the sun. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Tomorrow morning, stand facing the sunrise. Spread your arms and name east, west, north and south.
2. Draw your classroom. Write which side is east (toward the sunrise).
3. Say where three things in your home are, using right, left, front and back.
