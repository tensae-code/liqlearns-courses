# Lesson plan — Map Scale, Key and Symbols

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Location and Maps · **Lesson:** Map Scale, Key and Symbols
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading a map through the information printed around it — its scale, its north arrow, its key and colours, its symbols, and its year.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on what a map is — a map can only be read when the reader can use the information printed around its edges.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The information around a map** — four things printed around a map make it readable: its scale, a direction (north) arrow, a key/legend (መፍቻ) and the publication year (Grade 6).

2. **Scale — map distance against ground distance** — scale is map distance divided by ground distance; worked example: Bahir Dar to Asmara, 700 km drawn as 10 cm, so 1 cm stands for 70 km; the larger the scale, the more detail (Grade 6).

3. **The key and the colour code** — map colours are a shared language: blue = water (deep blue = deep water), red = major roads, urban areas, airports, military camps, buildings; yellow = urban areas; green = parks, forests, orchards; brown = deserts, historic sites, contour lines; purple = major roads (Grade 6).

4. **Map signs and symbols** — the printed symbol table: perennial vs summer-dry rivers, wells, lakes and dried lakes, seasonal streams, all-season vs summer-only roads, single vs multiple rail lines, airports and heliports, international borders (red) vs internal (black), villages, industrial sites, churches/mosques, factory sites (Grade 6).

5. **Reading a map's date** — a map must be read in its own time: the book's example is a 2011 World Bank map of HIV/AIDS in Africa — its picture belongs to 2011, not to today (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
