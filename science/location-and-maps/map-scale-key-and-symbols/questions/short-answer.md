# Map Scale, Key and Symbols — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Name the four things printed around a map that make it readable.**
**Answer:** Its scale; a direction (north) arrow; a key/legend (መፍቻ); the publication year.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) Work the practice problem: 824 km drawn in 16 cm — what does 1 cm stand for?**
**Answer:** 824 ÷ 16 = 51.5 km, so 1 cm stands for 51.5 km.

**3. (Sub lesson 3) A town is coloured red and yellow on the map. Is that a mistake?**
**Answer:** No — the code uses red for urban areas among its meanings, and yellow for urban areas; both are printed in the book's code for towns.

**4. (Sub lesson 4) Name four things the symbol table gives signs for.**
**Answer:** Any four of: rivers, wells, lakes, streams, roads, rail lines, airports, heliports, borders, villages, industrial sites, churches/mosques, factory sites.

**5. (Sub lesson 5) Give the book's own example of why a map's date matters.**
**Answer:** The 2011 World Bank map of HIV/AIDS in Africa: its picture of the disease belongs to 2011, not to today.
