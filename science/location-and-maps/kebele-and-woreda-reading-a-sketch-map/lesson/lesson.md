# Kebele and Woreda — Reading a Sketch Map

## Do it first

Your village is not alone. Other villages stand near it. Do you know the name of the bigger area that holds them all? Look at a map if your teacher has one, then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Villages make a kebele, kebeles make a woreda

A kebele is a group of villages together. A woreda is a group of kebeles together. So the scale grows: one village, then its kebele, then its woreda — like a family inside a village inside a country.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading a woreda sketch map

A sketch map of a woreda shows its kebeles and its main places. We read it by asking: what borders our kebele on this side? What stands to the east, to the west? We work with a real example — the sketch map of Bahir Dar Zuria woreda and its kebeles.

## Sub lesson 3 — Drawing your own woreda

Now draw: your own woreda as a rough sketch map, your kebele marked inside it, and the things that border it on each side written or drawn at the edges.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: how villages group into a kebele and kebeles into a woreda, and how to read — and draw — a woreda sketch map. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw your woreda's sketch map with your kebele marked.
2. Write what borders your kebele on the east and on the west.
3. Ask at home: how many villages are in our kebele?
