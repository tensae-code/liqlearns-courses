# Relative Location — Where a Place Lies

## Do it first

Tell someone where your school is without using a map. You will name things near it — 'beside the market', 'across from the clinic'. That way of telling is today's lesson. Try it first.

## Sub lesson 1 — What relative location is (teacher guide — evidence)

Relative location (አንጻራዊ መገኛ, anṣarawi megeña) fixes where a place is by comparing it with known places around it: the market is next to the school, behind the church. Anyone who knows those landmarks can find the place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Our zone's relative location

At zone scale we do the same with whole neighbours. Our worked example is South Gondar: its neighbours are named on all eight sides — East Gojjam, Central Gondar, West Gojjam, Lake Tana, North Wollo, Wag Himra and South Wollo among them — using both natural features like the lake and human-made areas. Pupils then draw a rough sketch map of their own zone with its neighbours.

## Sub lesson 3 — Your sub-city, described both ways (teacher guide — evidence)

In the city the same skill applies to a sub-city: pupils describe their own sub-city's location by the sub-cities and landmarks around it, before they meet its fixed, absolute location in the next lesson.

## Sub lesson 4 — Two ways of fixing a place — the Grade 4 definitions

In Grade 4 the two ways are named side by side and defined formally. Relative location fixes a place against its neighbours — the way this lesson began. Absolute location fixes it by latitude and longitude numbers that never change. The Grade 4 teacher guides give the same formal definitions (teacher guide — evidence). Hold both: neighbours for everyday finding, numbers for a fixing that never moves.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: relative location (አንጻራዊ መገኛ) — describing where a place lies by the things around it and by direction, from a market stall to a whole zone. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Describe where your home is using three landmarks.
2. Draw a rough sketch map of your zone (or woreda) with its neighbours on all sides.
3. Describe a friend's house so well that someone else could find it.
