# Where My Home Is

## Do it first

Think of your walk to school. When you leave home, do you walk toward the sunrise or away from it? Is your home near the school or far? Before reading, try to say where your home is.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our home, compared with the school (teacher guide — evidence)

We place our own home by comparing it with places we know: our home lies on this side of the school, or that side. This is how a young child first tells where their home is — by the school they walk to every day.

## Sub lesson 2 — Our home, compared with the woreda (teacher guide — evidence)

A bigger step: our home also stands somewhere inside our woreda, and we can say where — near its middle, or toward one side of it. The neighbouring kebeles around us have names, and naming them helps say where we are.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: saying where your own home is — which side of the school it lies on, and how it stands compared with the woreda. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw your home and your school. Draw the path you walk between them.
2. Write or say: 'My home is ___ of the school' using a direction word.
3. Ask at home: what is the name of the kebele next to ours?
