# Lesson plan — Where My Home Is

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Location and Maps · **Lesson:** Where My Home Is
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** saying where your own home is — which side of the school it lies on, and how it stands compared with the woreda.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the walk between home and school that the child makes every day (teacher-guide evidence only — Grade 1 has no student book).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Our home, compared with the school (teacher guide — evidence)** — we place our own home by comparing it with places we know: our home lies on this side of the school, or that side.
2. **Our home, compared with the woreda (teacher guide — evidence)** — a bigger step: our home also stands somewhere inside our woreda, and we can say where — near its middle, or toward one side of it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title in the Science course, in order. Recommended, never forced — a learner who lands here directly can start here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and use what the part "Our home, compared with the school (teacher guide — evidence)" teaches, in their own words.
2. Say and use what the part "Our home, compared with the woreda (teacher guide — evidence)" teaches, in their own words.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's own world (home, school, neighbourhood) supplies the rest.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Think of your walk to school. When you leave home, do you walk toward the sunrise or away from it? Is your home near the school or far? Before reading, try to say where your home is. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts of the lesson in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 12003]: a picture of a child's home drawn between the school and a neighbouring kebele — the student studies the picture and names what it shows in the lesson's own words.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing to a companion and answers one question back (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
- The learner cannot do the Do It Yourself → do the first item together, then let them finish the rest alone.
