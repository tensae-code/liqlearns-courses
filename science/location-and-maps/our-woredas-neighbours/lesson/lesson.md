# Our Woreda's Neighbours

## Do it first

Just as your home has neighbours, your woreda has neighbours too. Can you name even one woreda that touches yours? Try before you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Woredas have neighbours, like houses do

The woredas around our own are its neighbours (አዋሳኝ, awasañ — bordering). We learn them the same way we learned our household neighbours: by name, and by the side on which each one lies.

[Picture 12006 — a woreda map with its neighbours labelled on four sides]

## Sub lesson 2 — Naming them on every side

Work around your woreda like a clock: which woreda lies to the east? To the west? To the north and the south? When you can name them all, you can describe your woreda's location to anyone.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: the names and positions of the woredas around our own — neighbours at woreda scale. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw your woreda in the middle of a page and its neighbours around it, each on its true side.
2. Write each neighbour's name on your drawing.
3. Ask a parent or teacher to check one neighbour you are unsure of.
