# The Amhara Region — Where It Sits and Its Zones

## Do it first

Here is a region — a whole region, bigger than any zone you have drawn. Can you say where it sits without naming a single town inside it? Try with neighbours first, then with numbers.

## Sub lesson 1 — The region's relative location

Read the region off its map by its neighbours: Eritrea to the north, Sudan to the west, Oromia to the south and south-east, Tigray to the north-east, Benishangul-Gumuz to the south-west, and Afar to the east. Six neighbours, each on its own side — the region's relative location, complete.

[Picture 12201 — a map of the Amhara Region with each neighbouring region and country labelled on its side]

## Sub lesson 2 — The region's zones and their capitals

Inside the region sit its zones — thirteen zones plus the Bahir Dar city administration, fourteen rows in the book's table, each with its capital and its coordinates. One capital is given as the worked example: Bahir Dar, at 11°57′ N, 37°36′ E. A region is not one place; it is a family of zones, each with its own centre.

[Picture 12216 — a map of the region divided into its zones, capitals dotted]

## Sub lesson 3 — Our region's location in the teacher guides (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 4 teacher guides teach the same lesson for their own regions (teacher guide — evidence): the region's relative location — who its neighbours are — and its absolute, astronomical location in latitude and longitude. The Oromia guide names Oromia itself this way. Every region of the country can be fixed both ways, exactly as Amhara is fixed here.

## Sub lesson 4 — Famous and important places of our region (teacher guide — evidence)

The guides finish the map with its famous places (teacher guide — evidence): historical heritage places, natural heritage places, the zones and special woredas, and the region's main institutions — the places a map of home should never leave out. The Oromia guide likewise places Oromia's famous places on its map.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: the Amhara Region — fixed by its six neighbours, fixed by its coordinate box, and known zone by zone with its capitals. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the Amhara Region and label its six neighbours, each on its true side.
2. Write Bahir Dar's coordinates from memory, then check them.
3. Name your own zone and its capital; find both in the region's table.
