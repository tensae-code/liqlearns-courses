# My Neighbours and My First Sketch Map

## Do it first

Think of the houses near your home. Who lives in the house on your right? Who lives on your left? Try to name two neighbours before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Neighbours — the people who live near us (teacher guide — evidence)

The people living in the houses near ours are our neighbours. A good learner can name their neighbours and say on which side of their home each one lives — on the right, on the left, in front, or behind.

## Sub lesson 2 — Drawing a sketch map (teacher guide — evidence)

A sketch map (ንድፍ ካርታ, ndif karta) is a quickly drawn picture-plan of a place. Draw your home first. Then draw your neighbours' houses on the right sides. Then add the school. Now anyone can see where everything is.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: naming the neighbours around your home, saying which side each lives on, and drawing your first sketch map. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a sketch map of your street: home first, neighbours next, school last.
2. Under your map, write one sentence for each neighbour: the name and the side.
3. Show your map to someone at home and explain where everything is.
