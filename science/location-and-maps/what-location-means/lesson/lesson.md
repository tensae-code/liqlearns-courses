# What Location Means

## Do it first

Look around your classroom. Where is the teacher's desk? Where is your bag? Where is the door? Before anyone explains, try to say where three things in the room are.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where things are, inside and around us

Location (መገኛ, megeña) means where a thing is found. We learn it first with the things closest to us: where things are inside the house, in the home compound and in the school compound.

Is the teacher's desk inside the classroom? Asking questions like this — where is the book, where is the bag — is how we find where things are.

## Sub lesson 2 — Places we know, in pictures

Pictures of familiar places teach the word: a house, a church and a mosque — every one of them is somewhere, and we say where by pointing to what is near it. A famous example: Fasil Ghebbi stands in Gondar — its location is told by the town it stands in.

## Sub lesson 3 — Locating the school's rooms (teacher guide — evidence)

At school we also locate the school's own rooms and the places that serve us, and we learn to say where our school, our home and our neighbours are, each one compared with the others.

## Sub lesson 4 — What a map is

A map is the tool that shows location. Where a body or a place sits is described against other bodies and places — and a map draws that 'where' on paper, small enough to hold, so we can see the whole place at once. The Grade 4 teacher guides teach the same idea (teacher guide — evidence): a real place and its map show the same features, matched one to one.

## Sub lesson 5 — Map and globe — two ways to show the Earth

There are two ways to show the whole Earth. A **map** is the Earth — or part of it — reduced and drawn flat, as seen from above; cartographers make maps from field data and aerial images. A **globe** is the Earth's true model, round like the Earth itself. Maps are used to find a place's location, size, direction and distance, and they serve travellers, pilots, ship captains, the military and town planners.

## Sub lesson 6 — What a map is (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 5 teacher guide defines a map with care (teacher guide — evidence). A map is a tool that shows places and events on the Earth's surface, in part or in whole, drawn **from above** on flat paper, **simplified** in content, **reduced** in size, and drawn **to scale** (ልኬት). A map shows a place's location, its direction, its area and its distances, and it shows both natural and man-made things.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: what location (መገኛ) means — every thing and every place is somewhere, and we say where by pointing to what is near it. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name where five things in your classroom are, each time naming something near the thing.
2. At home, say where your house is by naming two things near it.
3. Draw your classroom and mark where the desk, the door and your seat are.
4. Draw the same place as a flat map and say how a globe would show it differently.
