# GPS and Digital Maps

## Do it first

If a phone or a map app is at hand, open it and watch it find where you are. If not, imagine: a dot appears on a map of your town, exactly where you stand. How did it know? Try to say it first.

## Sub lesson 1 — GPS — positioning from satellites

**GPS — the Global Positioning System** — is positioning from satellites. It gives five things: **position, speed, time, altitude and area**. It comes in several forms: **handheld units** carried on foot, **vehicle** units in cars, **survey** units for measuring land, and the **GPS in a phone**. Behind every form stands the same idea: signals from above fix the place below.

## Sub lesson 2 — Google Earth and Google Maps

Two digital tools continue the map lessons. **Google Earth** shows the whole Earth to explore; **Google Maps** gives places, routes and measures for everyday finding. Both are built from satellite images, and both serve the skills of this title: finding a place, measuring a distance, viewing the Earth from above — the sketch map and the printed map, carried onto a screen.

## Sub lesson 3 — GPS and its five uses — the Grade 6 measure

Grade 6 lists GPS with a user's eye. The system gives **place, direction, maps and precise time**; its receivers include **handheld GPS units, smartphones, watches and car units**; it works **in any weather and at any time**; and its users include surveyors, explorers, scientists, pilots, ship captains and miners. Its **five uses** are printed as a list: **find a location, navigate, track movement, make maps, and keep precise time**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Google Maps and Google Earth at region scale

At region scale the two tools divide their work. **Google Maps** is internet-based and mostly used on mobile phones, for **daily routes, traffic and travel time**. **Google Earth** is a program for **exploring the whole Earth**. The chapter's own figures show the Google Earth opening view and Lake Tana on Google Maps, and its activities send pupils to **find Bahir Dar on Google Maps** and to visit a **land-administration office** to report how GPS is used there. The Grade 5 and Grade 6 teacher guides carry the same subtopic — finding places with map-finder applications and Google Earth — at country and region scale (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 5 — Field activity — interviewing the experts

The location chapter closes outside the classroom. Pupils go to **interview the experts**: **land-administration experts**, asked about location and boundaries, and **ICT experts**, asked about GPS and digital maps — then they **report back to the class**. The lesson of the whole title lands here: location is not only read from books; it is a working skill, held by real offices in your own town.
