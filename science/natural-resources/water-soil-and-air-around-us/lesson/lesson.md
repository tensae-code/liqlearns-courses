# Water, soil and air around us

## Do it first
You used three treasures before breakfast: the soil that grew your food, the water in your cup, the air in your chest. Nature gives them free and people ruin them carelessly. This lesson names the three, counts their uses, and teaches their protection.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three resources at woreda scale

The Grade 2 book gathers the woreda's great three. **Water**: where it comes from — the rivers, springs, ponds and wells of the area — and its uses. **Soil**: what soil is used for — growing, building, making. **Air**: breathed by **all living things** — proved by the book's stark little activity: hold the nose shut for five seconds, and the chest itself lectures.

These are **natural resources** — things nature gives, not things people make — and the chapters that follow in Grade 3 take each one deeper: its nature, its pollution, its protection.

[Picture 12169 — people drawing water at a spring, and a farmer holding a handful of soil]

## Sub lesson 2 — Soil — where food begins

**Soil** is a resource **given by nature**, and the Grade 3 book defines it with a farmer's precision: it is the **fertile top layer where roots reach**, found in **black, red and brown** colours. Under it, poorer earth; in it, all growing begins.

The book then chains the world to it: **without soil, no plants — and without plants, no animals**. Every resource lesson afterward stands on this chain: soil is not dirt underfoot; it is the first link of food, and its thin fertile skin is the part to protect.

[Picture 12170 — plant roots reaching into dark topsoil]

## Sub lesson 3 — Water — and keeping it clean

**Water** the Grade 3 book gathers by **sources** — **river, well, spring, rain, pond, lake** — and by **uses**: **drinking, cooking, transport, power**. Then the warning, in the same section: water is **polluted** by **latrine waste, dung near sources, and factory waste** — and pollution is **prevented** by keeping those things from the sources.

The book's concept-web exercise sends pupils out to web water's uses themselves; do it in the hands-on below, and let the pollution list hang beside the web: every use depends on the source staying clean.

[Picture 12171 — a spring with people drawing clean water, beside a polluted stream]

## Sub lesson 4 — Air — the air we breathe

**Air** closes the three. **All living things must breathe air**, and the green world does more with it: **green plants make food using carbon dioxide from the air** — the sunlight chain of the Light lessons, breathing. But air is **being polluted**, and the book's protection list is practical: **cutting carbon emissions, planting trees, cleaning surroundings, and disposing of waste properly**. Trees are air's cleaners — the same trees the plant lessons taught us to keep.

[Picture 12172 — smoke rising over a town beside trees at work cleaning the air]

## Say it to your companion
Guide your companion resource by resource: source, uses, pollution, protection. At each one they must ask 'and if it were gone?' — answer from the book's chains: no soil, no plants; no plants, no animals.

## Do It Yourself
1. Resource walk: visit your water source. Report its state honestly — clean or threatened, and by what on the book's pollution list.
2. Soil handful report: dig topsoil with roots. Colour (black, red, brown), depth of the fertile layer, what grows in it. That layer is the treasure.
3. Air audit of one street: smoke sources counted, trees counted. Write the protection sentence your street most needs.
