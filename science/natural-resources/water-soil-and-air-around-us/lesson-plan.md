# Lesson plan — Water, soil and air around us

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Natural Resources · **Lesson:** Water, soil and air around us
**Place in the curriculum:** Natural Resources — the ninth title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** soil, water and air are natural resources: soil is the fertile top layer where roots reach, in black, red and brown; water comes from river, well, spring, rain, pond and lake and serves drinking, cooking, transport and power; air is breathed by all living things and green plants use its carbon dioxide to make food — and each resource can be polluted, and protected
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Weather and climate (rain and air known) and Plants and Animals (the users of soil, water and air). This lesson gathers the three resources the younger books meet one by one.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The three resources at woreda scale** — the Grade 2 book: where water comes from and its uses; what soil is used for; air breathed by all
2. **Soil — where food begins** — the Grade 3 book's soil: the fertile top layer, its colours, the chain no-soil → no-plants → no-animals
3. **Water — and keeping it clean** — sources and uses; pollution by latrine waste, dung and factory waste; prevention
4. **Air — the air we breathe** — breathing, plants' food-making from carbon dioxide, pollution, and the four protections

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Weather and Seasons — Weather and climate. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Plants — What a plant needs to grow. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell where water comes from and its uses, and say how it is polluted — latrine waste, dung near sources, factory waste — and how pollution is prevented.
2. Say what soil is: the fertile top layer where roots reach, in black, red and brown colours; without soil no plants, and without plants no animals.
3. Say air's two gifts: all living things breathe it, and green plants make food using carbon dioxide from it; and name how air is protected — cutting carbon emissions, planting trees, clean surroundings, proper waste disposal.

## What you need
A spring, well or tap to visit if allowed; a handful of topsoil with roots in it; smoke and trees to observe.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats resources as endless because they are free → the books pair every resource with its pollution and its protection in the same breath; free is not the same as unbreakable.
- Mixes the protections up → soil is protected by cover and terraces, water by keeping waste from sources, air by trees and less smoke; each resource keeps its own rule.
- The Grade 3 chapters (soil, water, air) are the lesson's spine; the Grade 2 chapter gathers the three at woreda scale first — stacked young to old in the part.
