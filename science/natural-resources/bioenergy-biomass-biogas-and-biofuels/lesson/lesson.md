# Bioenergy — biomass, biogas and biofuels

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's subject — bioenergy — biomass, biogas and biofuels. Then read on: this is Honduras's way in — its *Ciencias Naturales* books teach this thing at the places named in Sources, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bioenergy defined

bioenergy defined: energy produced from organic matter (biomass — everything living beings are made of, their products and their wastes), used by direct combustion or by transformation into solid, liquid and gaseous biofuels.

## Sub lesson 2 — Solid biofuels

solid biofuels: firewood (cut wood not used as timber — the main solid biofuel in Honduras), agro-industrial residues (stalks, leaves, seeds and fruit husks left after harvest) and wood chips, made into compacted pellets and briquettes that substitute for firewood.

[Picture — solid biofuels as Honduras's books show it — the student names the part that carries the sub lesson's point]

## Sub lesson 3 — Biodiesel

biodiesel: made from oil plants (sunflower, soy, African palm, peanut, mustard) and from used cooking oils and fats; it is degradable, low in toxicity and produces fewer polluting gases, and powers cars and electric generators.

## The key ideas

- bioenergy defined: energy produced from organic matter (biomass — everything living beings are made of, their products and their wastes), used by direct combustion or by transformation into solid, liquid and gaseous biofuels.
- solid biofuels: firewood (cut wood not used as timber — the main solid biofuel in Honduras), agro-industrial residues (stalks, leaves, seeds and fruit husks left after harvest) and wood chips, made into compacted pellets and briquettes that substitute for firewood.
- biodiesel: made from oil plants (sunflower, soy, African palm, peanut, mustard) and from used cooking oils and fats; it is degradable, low in toxicity and produces fewer polluting gases, and powers cars and electric generators.
