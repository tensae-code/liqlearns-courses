| Lesson part | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| bioenergy defined: energy produced from organic matter (biomass — everything living beings are made of, their products and their wastes), used by direct combustion or by transformation into solid, liquid and gaseous biofuels (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 143 |
| solid biofuels: firewood (cut wood not used as timber — the main solid biofuel in Honduras), agro-industrial residues (stalks, leaves, seeds and fruit husks left after harvest) and wood chips, made into compacted pellets and briquettes that substitute for firewood (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 143 |
| biodiesel: made from oil plants (sunflower, soy, African palm, peanut, mustard) and from used cooking oils and fats; it is degradable, low in toxicity and produces fewer polluting gases, and powers cars and electric generators (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 143 |
| bioethanol: produced by fermenting the sugars in plant remains and then distilling the alcohol; a high-energy fuel sometimes mixed with petrol to reduce toxic emissions (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 143 |
| biogas: when microorganisms decompose organic matter without oxygen they release a gas mixture — mainly methane and carbon dioxide, with hydrogen, ammonia and hydrogen sulphide — captured in a sealed biodigester where wastes are diluted with water to ferment; the gas serves for engines, lighting and cooking, and the digester also yields organic fertiliser (bioabono) (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 144 |
| bioenergy weighed: advantages (sustainable, renewable production; uses waste materials; fewer greenhouse gases than fossil fuels; less contamination from biological waste) against disadvantages (limited use, high production cost, land-use change to grow energy crops, the fuel needs prior transformation) (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 144 |
