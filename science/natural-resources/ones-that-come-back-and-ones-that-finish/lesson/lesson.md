# Ones that come back and ones that finish

## Do it first
Rain falls, and the pond fills again. A tree is cut, and a seedling rises. But burn a litre of petrol, and no rain will ever refill it. Some resources come back. Some are finished forever. The book sorts them — first with a guessing table, then with the rule.

## The book's sorting table, then the rule
The book begins with a **sorting table** — **soil, water, minerals, walia, trees** — and asks: which of these come back, and which finish?

Then the rule:

- **Renewable resources come back after use and are not exhausted** — the book's examples: **water, air, plants**.
- **Non-renewable resources are finished once used** — the book's examples: **petrol, diesel, iron, gold, copper, coal**.

The families explain the care lessons on both sides of this one: the coming-back family must be **kept clean and growing**; the finishing family must be **spent with thought**, because no season refunds it.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion both rules with their examples, then sort the book's starting table — soil, water, minerals, walia, trees — as far as the rule lets you, saying which entries need care-thinking (soil renews slowly; a walia is a living resource whose kind can finish if unprotected).

## Do It Yourself
1. Kitchen sort: list ten resources your home uses this week. Sort them: comes back / finishes. Which family does your home lean on harder?
2. Barrel and pond drawing: draw the picture's two halves yourself — the pond the rain refills, the barrel that only empties.
3. Walia thinking: the walia is a living resource. Explain how a living resource can still be lost for good if its kind vanishes — the sort has a warning inside it.
