# Energy sources around us

## Do it first
When the power cuts, a house discovers what energy was doing: light, radio, the fridge, the mill. Energy is invisible until it stops. This lesson defines it, then maps where Ethiopia's comes from.

## Energy — what it is; Ethiopia's sources
The teacher guides define it in one line (teacher guide — evidence): **energy is the ability to do a work (አንድን ሥራ የመሥራት አቅም)**, and its sources are compared by their location, use and effects. Ethiopia's power comes from **water, sun, wind, fuel and biogas** — used for **light, industry and farming**. The Grade 5 guide's energy subtopic (teacher guide — evidence) pictures the same strip: sun warming, a river turning a mill, food giving strength.

## The named assets
Named assets: hydro dams — the **GERD, Gilgel Gibe I and II, Tana Beles, Fincha, Tekeze**; wind farms — **Ashegoda and Adama**; **solar panels** for household power. Pupils identify the energy source in each of the book's four pictures — the dam's water, the turbine's wind, the panel's sun, the digester's biogas. Energy sources sort, in the guides' competency (teacher guide — evidence), into **renewable and non-renewable** — the family line the earlier resource lessons drew.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the definition first — energy is the ability to do a work — then the five sources, then three named dams and two wind farms from memory.

## Do It Yourself
1. Home energy census: list everything in your home that uses energy and name its source for each — light, cooking, radio, phone charging. Which source feeds the most lines?
2. Picture drill, the book's own: four pictures — dam, turbine, panel, digester. Name each source without the lesson open.
3. Renewable or not? Sort the five sources — water, sun, wind, fuel, biogas — by the guides' line, and defend the hard one (fuel) aloud.
