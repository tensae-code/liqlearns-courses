# Lesson plan — What counts as a natural resource

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Natural Resources · **Lesson:** What counts as a natural resource
**Place in the curriculum:** Natural Resources — the title's definition lesson at Grade 4 depth, before minerals and the living resources (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a natural resource is everything found in nature, on or under the Earth, that serves people directly or indirectly — soil, air, water, plants, animals, minerals and sunlight
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Water, soil and air around us — the resources were met at home scale; now the definition is drawn wide, at region scale, with the guides' regional evidence.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural Resources — Water, soil and air around us. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the definition: everything found in nature, on or under the Earth, that serves people directly or indirectly.
2. List the book's examples: soil, air, water, plants, animals, minerals (stone, sand, limestone, coal, oil, gold), sunlight.
3. Say a resource can serve directly or indirectly — both count.

## What you need
A window or a doorway: the lesson's examples are visible from either.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts only dug and sold things → sunlight and air are resources too: found in nature, serving people, though nobody sells them by the sack.
- Counts things people make — a plough, a house → those are made FROM resources; the resource is what nature provided first.
