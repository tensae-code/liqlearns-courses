# What counts as a natural resource

## Do it first
Stand at the door. The soil under your feet serves you. The water in the jerrycan serves you. The tree, the donkey, the stone of the wall, the sunlight on all of it — servants, every one. Things found in nature that serve people have a name. This lesson gives it, and draws its borders wide.

## Found in nature, serving people
**Natural resources** are **everything found in nature, on or under the Earth, that serves people directly or indirectly**.

The book's list: **soil, air, water, plants, animals, minerals — stone, sand, limestone, coal, oil, gold — and sunlight**.

Two corners of the definition matter. **On or under**: the stone in the wall and the gold deep below both count. **Directly or indirectly**: water serves directly when you drink it; the sunlight serves indirectly — through the plants that feed you — and it counts just the same.

The two regional teacher guides carry the same teaching at region scale: **our region's own resources** — its wild animals and minerals among them — are the national list wearing local names (teacher guide — evidence).

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the definition word-perfect in idea — found in nature, on or under the Earth, serving directly or indirectly — then give six examples.

## Do It Yourself
1. Doorway census: from one doorway, name ten natural resources and say how each serves — directly or indirectly.
2. Under-earth list: name three resources no one can see until they are dug. On-or-under is the definition's reach.
3. Indirect detective: pick sunlight and trace two indirect services — light → plant → food; light → warmth → dried grain.
