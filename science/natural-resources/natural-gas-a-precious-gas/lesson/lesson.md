# Natural gas — a precious gas

## Do it first
Oil is a liquid you can picture in a barrel. But some of what the ground gives for energy is a gas — invisible, with no barrel to fill. How is such a thing found, and how is it brought up? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Greece's Grade 6 book calls this gas precious, and gives its finding a chapter of its own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Searching for natural gas

Natural gas lies hidden underground, like oil — so its story also begins with a **search**. The book names the gas **precious**: a gas worth searching for, worth the work of finding where it lies. First the searching, as with oil; the gas chapter follows the oil chapters, and the pattern is the same — what the ground keeps must first be found.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pumping natural gas

Found is not yet had. The second step is **pumping**: natural gas is pumped up from underground, brought to the surface where it can be gathered and used. Searching then pumping — the chapter's two steps bring the precious gas out of the ground, ready for the next chapter's work, where it serves as an energy source.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What two steps bring natural gas out of the ground, in the book's order?" Answer: Searching for it underground first, then pumping it up — the book calls natural gas a precious gas.
