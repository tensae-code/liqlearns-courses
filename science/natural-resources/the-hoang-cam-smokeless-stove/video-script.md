Today: **The Hoàng Cầm smokeless stove** — Vietnam's way, from its book (Vietnam, Grade 5 — *Khoa học 5*, printed p. 41).

In the Hòa Bình campaign (1951–1952), Vietnam's soldiers cooked in the field — but a cooking fire's smoke rises in one column and shows the enemy exactly where the kitchen is. The Hoàng Cầm stove answers that: a field kitchen built so its **smoke is scattered** as it leaves, spread out until no single plume betrays the cooks below.

The stove stands in Vietnam's Grade 5 *Khoa học 5* inside its fuel-energy lesson, beside the rules for not wasting fuel — it is taught as the country's own heritage of careful fuel use: cook the meal, spend the wood wisely, and leave no sign. A stove from a war campaign, remembered in a children's science book as a lesson in using energy well.
