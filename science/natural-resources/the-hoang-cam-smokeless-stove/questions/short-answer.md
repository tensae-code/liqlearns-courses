# The Hoàng Cầm smokeless stove — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: A kitchen the planes cannot find.
**Answer:** In the Hòa Bình campaign (1951–1952), Vietnam's soldiers cooked in the field — but a cooking fire's smoke rises in one column and shows the enemy exactly where the kitchen is. The Hoàng Cầm stove answers that: a field kitchen built so its **smoke is scattered** as it leaves, spread out until no single plume betrays the cooks below.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Vietnam's fuel-saving heritage.
**Answer:** The stove stands in Vietnam's Grade 5 *Khoa học 5* inside its fuel-energy lesson, beside the rules for not wasting fuel — it is taught as the country's own heritage of careful fuel use: cook the meal, spend the wood wisely, and leave no sign. A stove from a war campaign, remembered in a children's science book as a lesson in using energy well.

**Answer:** A kitchen the planes cannot find → Vietnam's fuel-saving heritage — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
