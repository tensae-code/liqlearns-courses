# Lesson plan — How a rich place becomes bare

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Natural Resources · **Lesson:** How a rich place becomes bare
**Place in the curriculum:** Natural Resources — the memoir lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a printed memoir: a place once rich in native plants, wild animals and birdsong ('the monkeys' house', 1970 E.C.) found forty years later a barren, gullied wasteland — resource loss is the natural content shrinking or vanishing, driven by fast population growth raising demand for farmland, building, firewood and grazing
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the resource lessons before it — the learner knows the wealth; this lesson hears what its loss sounds like, in one man's memory.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural Resources — Plants and wild animals as resources. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Retell the memoir: the rich place of 1970 E.C. — native plants, wild animals, birdsong — and the same place forty years later: barren, gullied, species gone, farmland eroded.
2. Define resource loss in the book's terms: the natural content shrinking or vanishing.
3. Name the driver: fast population growth raising demand for farmland, building land, firewood and grazing.

## What you need
An elder's memory of your own area, if one can be asked; a hillside you can visit twice in imagination.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the memoir as only a story → the book prints it inside the resources chapter as the definition's evidence: loss = natural content shrinking or vanishing, and the driver is named. Story is the vehicle; the concept is the load.
- Blames 'people' in general → the book names the mechanism: population growth raising four demands. Mechanisms can be answered; blame cannot.
