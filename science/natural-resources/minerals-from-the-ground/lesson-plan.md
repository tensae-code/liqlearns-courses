# Lesson plan — Minerals from the ground

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Natural Resources · **Lesson:** Minerals from the ground
**Place in the curriculum:** Natural Resources — the dug and found resources (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** minerals are things dug from the ground or found on it — stone, gravel, sand, salt, iron, and in Grade 3 also coal, fuel/oil and gold; they are non-renewable, made into rings, wire, crosses and coins, and they earn the country income
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Water, soil and air — the surface resources known; this lesson goes under the surface, to the resources that do not grow back.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural Resources — Water, soil and air around us. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the Grade 2 minerals and where they are got: dug from the ground or found on it — stone, gravel, sand, salt, iron.
2. Name products made from minerals: ring, electric wire, silver cross, coins.
3. Add the Grade 3 list: sand, stone, coal, iron, fuel/oil, gold — solid and liquid forms.
4. Say what non-renewable means here: minerals are used once and gone; they earn the country income, so they are spent with thought.

## What you need
Local mineral things to collect or visit: a stone, sand, gravel, salt — the Grade 3 book's own field activity.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats salt and sand as growing back like grass → the Grade 3 lesson's title is the correction: minerals are non-renewable, used once and gone.
- Counts only shiny things (gold) as minerals → the books lead with stone, gravel, sand and salt: the building minerals are the everyday treasure.
- This lesson's next door: Plants and wild animals as resources — the living resources, which CAN grow back if cared for.
