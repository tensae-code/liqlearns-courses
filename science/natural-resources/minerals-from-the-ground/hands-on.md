# Hands-on — Minerals from the ground

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The collection
Build the field collection: stone, gravel, sand, salt, a piece of iron or charcoal. Label each: FOUND WHERE / USED FOR. That two-line label is the lesson in miniature.

## Coin study
Take one coin. It began as ore in the ground. List what had to happen between the ground and your hand — digging, heating, shaping — as far as you know, marking guesses as guesses.

## Once-only ledger
List the mineral things your family used this month. Beside each: will the ground replace it? The ledger teaches non-renewable without a lecture.


## The field-trip questions
The book's Grade 4 method: go to where minerals are worked — a quarry, a mason's yard, a goldsmith — and ask the local people two questions: what is dug or found here, and what is it used for? Write the answers beside the mineral's name. Local knowledge is part of the lesson.
