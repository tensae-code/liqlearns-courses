# Lesson plan — Plants and wild animals as resources

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Natural Resources · **Lesson:** Plants and wild animals as resources
**Place in the curriculum:** Natural Resources — the living resources (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** plants keep the climate steady and serve food, industry and more; wild animals, not tamed and living wild, earn the country income through tourism and create jobs — and the rare ones, like the Walia ibex, can be saved from decline
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Plants and Animals titles — the living world known kind by kind; this lesson values it as a resource, which is a reason to protect it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Plants — Plants give us things and food. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Animals — Tame animals and wild animals. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Complete the book's concept-web of plant uses: climate kept steady, food, industry and more; and do the leaf-collection activity — bring local leaves, paste and label them.
2. Say how wild animals differ from domestic ones, and how they earn: tourism income and jobs.
3. Name the rare/endemic animals pictured — the Walia ibex among them — and say how their decline can be saved.

## What you need
Leaves to collect (fallen ones serve), paste and paper for the collection page; pictures of the Walia ibex.

## If the learner is stuck
- Values wild animals only when dead or tamed → the book's economy is the opposite: alive and wild is exactly what visitors pay to see.
- Calls a plant 'just a plant' → run the web: climate, food, industry, medicine, soil, shelter — six spokes minimum.
- This lesson's next door: How a rich place becomes bare — the memoir of what happens when the living resources are spent carelessly.
