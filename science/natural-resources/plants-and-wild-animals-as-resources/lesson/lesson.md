# Plants and wild animals as resources

## Do it first
It gives no milk. It pulls no plough. It will never be ridden. Yet the Walia ibex earns money for the whole country just by being alive on its mountain. This lesson values the living resources — plants at work everywhere, wild animals that visitors cross the world to see.

## Sub lesson 1 — Plants at work everywhere

The Grade 3 book counts **plants among the natural resources** in full: they **keep the climate steady** and serve **food, industry and more** — the uses the Plants title taught (wood, paper, cloth, food, medicine, soil-feeding) now gathered under one word: resource. Its activity is a collector's: **bring local leaves, paste them, and label them** — a leaf page is a resource inventory. And its concept-web of plant uses is to be **completed by the pupils**: the web below in the hands-on is that exercise, kept.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wild animals and the visitors who come to see them

**Wild animals** are resources of a strange kind: **not tamed, living in the wild**, they earn the country **income through tourism** and **create jobs** — guides, guards, lodges — precisely by staying wild. The book pictures the **rare and endemic** ones — the scene of the **Walia ibex** on its rocky peak is its example — and asks how their decline can be **saved**: protected places, protected lives.

Set this beside the domestic ledger of the Animals title: the cow earns by service, the ibex by existing. Both earnings end the same way if care ends — which is why the resources course keeps arriving at care.

## Say it to your companion
Build the plant-uses web aloud for your companion: they call 'another!' until the web has climate, food, industry and the uses from the Plants title. Then the ibex question: let them argue its worth before you give tourism and jobs.

## Do It Yourself
1. Leaf collection the book's way: collect fallen local leaves, paste, label with the plant's name. A page of ten labelled leaves is a herbarium's first page.
2. Concept-web on paper: PLANTS at the centre; spokes for climate, food, industry, medicine, shelter, soil. Fill every spoke from the earlier lessons.
3. Endemic poster: draw the Walia ibex on its peak with visitors watching, and caption it with the earning sentence: alive, it earns; gone, it earns nothing.
