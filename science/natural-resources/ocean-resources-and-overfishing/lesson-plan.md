# Lesson plan — Ocean resources and overfishing

**Course:** Science · **Title:** natural-resources · **Lesson:** Ocean resources and overfishing
**Place in the curriculum:** Natural Resources — appended to Unit 10 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ocean resources — the sea's produce (Malaysia's seafood and aquaculture) and what overfishing would do to it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Resources of the sea** — Malaysia's Additional Science Form 5 book catalogues the **resources of the sea**: the **seafood of Malaysia — fish, shellfish (molluscs, crustaceans, echinoderms) and edible seaweed** — and the **aquaculture animals: tilapia, prawns, catfish, oysters and mussels**, farmed to add to the catch.
2. **Overfishing — the economics and the issue** — The book then weighs **the economics of ocean produce and its issues: what overfishing would do** — the catch is a living resource, and taking too much breaks the supply the economy stands on.

## Before this lesson
- No required first lesson. Recommended, never forced: the neighbouring lessons that touch the same ground.

## What the learner can do when it ends

2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Overfishing — the economics and the issue.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks ocean resources are only fish → return to sub lesson 1: shellfish, edible seaweed and the aquaculture animals are resources too.
- Thinks more fishing is always better → return to sub lesson 2: overfishing is the issue the book sets against the economics.
