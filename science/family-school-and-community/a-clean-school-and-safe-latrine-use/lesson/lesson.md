# Our School — A Clean School and Safe Latrine Use

## Do it first

Look at your school compound. Who made it clean today — or who left it dirty? Is cleaning one person's job? Decide, then read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cleanliness is everyone's responsibility

The classroom, the walls, the compound and the latrine are everyone's responsibility — not the cleaners' alone. A school kept clean by all its people stays healthy for all its people.

[Picture 12016 — pupils sweeping the school compound and washing hands at a tap]

## Sub lesson 2 — The latrine, used properly — and hands washed after

Use the latrine properly and leave it clean for the next person. And after the latrine, always: wash your hands. Clean hands after the latrine stop sickness travelling from child to child.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: keeping the classroom, walls, compound and latrine clean as everyone's shared work — and washing hands after the latrine. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Pick up litter in your compound today — yours or not.
2. Wash your hands after the latrine every time today; count your washes.
3. Draw a 'wash hands' sign for your school's latrine wall.
