# Knowing myself: what I like and do well

## Do it first
Before you read: answer out loud. What is one thing you like? What is one thing you do not like? What is one thing you can do well, that you are proud of? Keep your three answers in mind. Then read on: this is Suriname's way in — its Grade 6 *Natuuronderwijs* book opens the whole year with this lesson, *Ik ben uniek*, before it teaches anything about the body or about nature.

## Sub lesson 1 — I am unique

Suriname's book starts the year with the self.

Its first lesson is called **Ik ben uniek** — **I am unique**. Unique means one of a kind: there is no other child exactly like you. Your face, your voice, your thoughts, the things you enjoy and the things you can do — the whole mix is yours alone, and it is not the same mix as your friend's, your brother's or your sister's.

That is why the book puts this lesson **first**. The two opening lessons of the book are a social-skills pair — first knowing yourself, then learning to say no — and together they start the year with the **self before the body**: who you are comes before what your eyes and ears do, before food, before plants and animals. Every later lesson is learned by a person, so the book first asks the person to know themselves.

## Sub lesson 2 — My likes, my dislikes, and what I am good at

Knowing yourself is not only a feeling. In Suriname's lesson each child **lists** it, in three parts:

- **What I like** — the things, foods, games and work this child enjoys.
- **What I dislike** — the things this child does not enjoy. Saying what you do not like is part of knowing yourself too; it is not complaining, it is honesty about who you are.
- **What I am proud of being good at** — the skills and strengths this child has, named with pride. Everyone is good at something; the lesson asks the child to say what their something is.

Three lists, all your own. A friend's lists cannot stand in for yours, because the lists describe a different, unique child. When you can read your three lists and say "that is me", you know yourself in the way this lesson teaches — clearly enough to say it out loud, and clearly enough for the year's next step: a child who knows what they like and what they are good at can also learn to say no to what is wrong for them, which is the book's very next lesson.

## The key ideas

- **Unique** means one of a kind — no other child is exactly you.
- Knowing yourself is said in three lists: **what I like**, **what I dislike**, and **what I am proud of being good at**.
- Suriname's Grade 6 book opens the year with the **self before the body** — this lesson and the saying-no lesson are its social-skills opening pair.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — unique first, then the three lists. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why can't I just copy my friend's lists?" Answer from the meaning of unique, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write your three lists: at least three true entries under each head — I like; I do not like; I am good at, and proud of it.
2. Read your lists again tomorrow. Change any entry that is not truly yours, and add one entry you forgot.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — what unique means, and why Suriname's book starts the year with the self.
