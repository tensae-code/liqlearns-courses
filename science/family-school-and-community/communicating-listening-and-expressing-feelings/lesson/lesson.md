# Communicating — listening and expressing feelings

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the last time someone interrupted you while you were speaking. How did it feel, and did they understand what you meant? Now think of a time you knew how someone felt before they said a word — how did you know? Say both answers out loud. Then read on: this is Turkey's way in — its Grade 2 Hayat Bilgisi book teaches communicating at school under the heading İletişim Var, Sorun Yok: there is communication, there is no problem.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening carefully and expressing feelings and thoughts

Good communication at school starts with listening: listen carefully, without interrupting, until the other person has finished. Then express your own feelings and thoughts clearly, in words — say what you feel and what you think, so the other person does not have to guess.

## Sub lesson 2 — Non-verbal messages

Not every message uses words. Faces, gestures and posture also send messages — non-verbal messages. A communicator watches those messages as well as listening to words, and notices the non-verbal messages they themselves are sending.

## The key ideas

- Listen carefully without interrupting, until the other person has finished.
- Express your feelings and thoughts clearly in words, so no one has to guess.
- Faces, gestures and posture send non-verbal messages — watch them, in others and in yourself.
