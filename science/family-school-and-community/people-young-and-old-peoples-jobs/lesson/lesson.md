# Our Community — People Young and Old; People's Jobs

## Do it first

The potter, the teacher, the grandmother, the baby — a community holds them all. How many age groups can you name? How many different jobs? Try both lists before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — The community by age

We group people by age: a child is 0–18 years; a youth is 19–30; an adult is 31–64; an elder is over 64. Every age has its place: children learn, elders carry memory and counsel.

[Picture 12018 — a potter, a weaver and a blacksmith at work]

## Sub lesson 2 — The community by work — trades and their products

People are also grouped by their work: farmers, weavers, tailors, traders, doctors, teachers, police, metalworkers, potters and carpenters. Each trade gives its product: the potter makes the jebena and pots; the blacksmith makes the sickle and the plough. Learn a trade by what it makes.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: grouping the community by age — child, youth, adult, elder — and by work: the trades of the community and the product each trade makes. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort ten people you know into the four age groups.
2. Make a trade–product table for your community with at least six trades.
3. Interview one tradesperson: what do you make, and what do you start from?
