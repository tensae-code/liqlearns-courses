# Lesson plan — Population — How a Country's People Grow

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Family, School and Community · **Lesson:** Population — How a Country's People Grow
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a population grows or shrinks: births minus deaths, plus people moving in minus people moving out — and how each of the three movers works in Ethiopia.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the family lessons — families joined together are a country's population, and this lesson counts them as the books do.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Population growth and its three movers** — a country's population grows by births minus deaths plus in-migration minus out-migration; Ethiopia's growth rate above 2.5%, total estimated to reach 115 million by 2014 E.C. (Grade 5).

2. **Birth in Ethiopia** — birth defined; the common measure is the average birth rate — the average number of children a woman has in her lifetime; rural rates high; reasons births stay high: children counted as wealth, preference for one sex, weak reproductive-health support (Grade 5).

3. **Death and life expectancy** — death varies with age, sex, work and health; the measures: crude death rate, child death rate, average life expectancy (Grade 5).

4. **Migration — the third mover** — migration splits into international and internal; Ethiopia's internal kinds run all four directions: countryside→countryside, countryside→town, town→town, town→countryside; pupils weigh why countryside→town runs high, and migration's gains and losses (Grade 5).

5. **The same three movers in East Africa** — a place's population changes through birth (raises), death (lowers) and migration — internal inside one country, international across a border (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
