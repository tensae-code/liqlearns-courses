# Kinds of homes people live in

## Do it first
Look at the home you live in. What kind of home is it? Now think of another home you know that is different from yours. What makes the two different? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 2 *Estudo do Meio* book, after asking what the family needs, sorts the kinds of housing (Tipos de habitação) people live in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Homes are of different kinds

Not every family lives in the same kind of home. Angola's Grade 2 book teaches **kinds of housing** (Tipos de habitação) as their own thing: homes differ — in what they are built from, in their shape and size, and in where they stand. Naming the kind of a home is the first step: this home is of one kind, that home of another, and both are homes.

## Sub lesson 2 — What makes one kind of home different from another

The differences have reasons. A home is built from the **materials** at hand, in the **place** where the family lives, for the **people** who must shelter in it. That is why the kinds differ from place to place and family to family — and why the lesson sits inside the family run of the book: the home is one of the things a family needs, and it comes in kinds.

## The key ideas

- Homes come in **kinds** — they differ in what they are built from, their shape and size, and where they stand.
- The kind of a home is shaped by the **materials at hand**, the **place**, and the **family** it shelters.
- Every kind is still a home: the lesson sorts kinds, it does not rank homes.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What three things make one kind of home different from another?" Answer from the lesson: What it is built from, where it stands, and the family it is made to shelter.
