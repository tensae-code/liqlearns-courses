# Kinship — how family members are related

## Do it first
Name the people in your family. Now say how each one is related to you — not just their name, but the relation. Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 2 *Estudo do Meio* book first gathers the family and its other members, then teaches the kinship relations (Relações de parentesco) between them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kinship — the relations between family members

**Kinship** is how the members of a family are related to one another. A family is not only a list of people: each person stands in a relation to the others — this one is a parent, that one a grandparent, another a brother or sister. Angola's Grade 2 book teaches these **kinship relations** (Relações de parentesco) as their own thing, right after the lesson that gathers my family and its other members.

## Sub lesson 2 — The names of the relations

Each relation has its name. My mother and father are my **parents**; their parents are my **grandparents**. My parents' brothers and sisters are my **uncles and aunts**, and their children are my **cousins**. My own brothers and sisters share my parents with me. Naming the relation is what kinship teaching does: given any two members of the family, the learner can say how they are related.

## The key ideas

- **Kinship** is how the members of a family are related to one another — every member stands in a named relation to the others.
- Parents, grandparents, brothers and sisters, uncles and aunts, and cousins are **names of relations**.
- Given any two family members, kinship lets you say **how they are related**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How is your cousin related to you, in the words of this lesson?" Answer from the lesson: A cousin is the child of my uncle or aunt — my parent's brother or sister.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw your family as a tree of relations: yourself at the centre, and each person joined to you with the name of the relation written on the line.
2. Pick two people in your family who are not yourself. Say how they are related to each other, not only to you.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
