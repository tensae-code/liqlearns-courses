# Lesson plan — Children's Rights

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Family, School and Community · **Lesson:** Children's Rights
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what every child should have — love, a name, care, food, clothing and education — what happens when those rights are broken, who protects children, and the story of a family whose children were made to work and miss school (plot summary only). The rights song is carried as a theme summary only.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the family lessons — rights are what a family, a school and a community owe every child.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What every child should have — with the rights song** — the rights list and the rights song as theme summary (Grade 2 student pp.106–107).
2. **When rights are broken — and who protects children** — rights violations, child labour exploitation, and the protectors (Grade 2 student pp.108–109).
3. **When children are made to work and miss school — a story (plot summary only)** — the Ato Lebewled story (plot summary only) and the violations list; a child is under 18 (Grade 3 student pp.87–88).

4. **The rights of the child — the 1989 convention** — under the UN convention of 1989 every person under 18 is a child; the printed rights: life, a name and a nationality; to know and be cared for by parents or caregivers; protection from exploitative work; special protection from cruel treatment (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
- The learner cannot do the Do It Yourself → do the first item together, then let them finish the rest alone.
