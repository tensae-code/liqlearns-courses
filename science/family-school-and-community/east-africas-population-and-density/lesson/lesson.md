# East Africa's Population and Density

## Do it first

Two fields, the same size. One holds six farming families, the other holds sixty. Which field is crowded? You already know the idea. Now we will give it a number. Try it first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The region in numbers

The region counted: on the estimates the book prints (UN figures via Worldometer, 2021), East Africa's countries hold **445,638,590 people in total — 5.71% of the whole world's population** — at an average density of **67 people per km²**. By population the region stands **first among Africa's regions**. And the book names its worry: fast population growth in countries whose economic growth is still low is a problem that presses on food, schools, housing and jobs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Population density — working it out

**Density** is the relation between a place's population and the land it lives on: **density = total population ÷ total area**, giving **people per square kilometre**. The book's Table 5.1 works it for every country. Ethiopia: **1,106,000 km² and 117,876,227 people → 106.74 people per km²**. The crowded end: **Mauritius 624.23**, **Rwanda 504.08**, **Burundi 440.30**, **Uganda 195.09**. The spread-out end: **Somalia 25.66**, **Zambia 25.14**. Several rows — Kenya, Madagascar, Zimbabwe among them — are left **blank for pupils to compute** from the formula.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where people crowd, and the price of fast growth

Where do the people crowd? The book's answer is a land-answer: **high places** with **comfortable temperatures, fertile soil, freedom from harmful insects and better rainfall** than the lowlands carry the region's highest populations and densities. The class work maps the high- and low-density countries from the table onto the region — dark on the highlands, pale in the dry lowlands — and debates what fast growth does to **food supply, education services, housing and jobs**, and which methods could slow it.
