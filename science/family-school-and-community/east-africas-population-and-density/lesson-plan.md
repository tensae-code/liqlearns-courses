# Lesson plan — East Africa's Population and Density

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Family, School and Community · **Lesson:** East Africa's Population and Density
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how many people East Africa holds, how population density is worked out from population and area, and why people crowd in some places and spread thin in others.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the population lesson — the three movers counted the people; this lesson spreads them over the land.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The region in numbers** — on the book's printed UN/Worldometer 2021 figures, the region's countries hold 445,638,590 people — 5.71% of the world's population — at an average density of 67 people per km²; the region stands first among Africa's regions by population, and fast growth with low economic growth is named a worrying problem (Grade 6).

2. **Population density — working it out** — density = total population ÷ total area, people per km²; Table 5.1 prints area, 2021 population and density per country — Ethiopia 1,106,000 km², 117,876,227 people, 106.74/km²; Rwanda 504.08; Mauritius 624.23; Uganda 195.09; Burundi 440.30; Zambia 25.14; Somalia 25.66 — with several densities left blank for pupils to compute (Grade 6).

3. **Where people crowd, and the price of fast growth** — high places with comfortable temperatures, fertile soil, freedom from harmful insects and better rainfall carry the highest populations and densities; the class maps high- and low-density countries and debates what fast growth does to food, education, housing and jobs (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
