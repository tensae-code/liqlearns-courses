# Our Community — Places That Help Us

## Do it first

Imagine your area with no health station, no school, no market. Which place would you miss first? Name three places that help your community before you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Important places of the kebele (teacher guide — evidence)

Even the smallest area has its significant places and institutions, named and located along its roads: the school, the health post, the church or mosque, the market. Knowing where they stand is part of knowing your kebele.

[Picture 12017 — a health station, a bank, a police station and a school in one strip]

## Sub lesson 2 — Institutions of the woreda and what each gives

At woreda scale the list grows, and each place gives its own service: the health station treats the sick; the bank keeps money safely; the police station keeps order; the school teaches; the iddir helps families in sorrow and need; the court settles disputes fairly; the church and mosque serve the faithful. Put it as a table: place — service.

## Sub lesson 3 — Main places of the sub-city (teacher guide — evidence)

In the city, the same learning grows again: a sub-city's main places — stadium, market, hospital, bus station — are pinned on its map, each found by the service people go to it for. Its neighbouring sub-cities are named too, with the side each one lies on.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: the places and institutions that serve a community — health station, church and mosque, bank, police station, school, iddir, court — what each one gives, from kebele to sub-city. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make the place–service table for your own area: every important place and what it gives.
2. Pin your area's main places on your sketch map.
3. Visit one institution with an adult and ask what service it gives most in a week.
