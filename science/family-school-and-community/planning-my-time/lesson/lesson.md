# Planning my time

## Do it first
Before you read: think of yesterday. Can you list everything you did, in the order you did it? What did you forget until later, and what took longer than you expected? Say your list out loud, in order. Then read on: this is Turkey's way in — its Grade 2 Hayat Bilgisi book teaches Planlı Olmak, being planned, by listing and ordering the day's work and keeping a daily plan for a week.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listing and ordering the day's work

Being planned starts with seeing the day clearly: name what we do in a day, list the day's work, and put that work in order. A list in order turns a jumbled day into work that can actually be done, one piece after another.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keeping a daily plan for a week and reviewing it

One day's list becomes a plan when it is kept: keep a daily plan for a whole week, then review it — look back over the week, see what was done as planned and what was not, and let that review shape the next plan. Planning is not only making the list; it is checking the list against the real week.

## The key ideas

- Being planned means listing the day's work and putting it in order.
- A daily plan is kept for a week, not made once and forgotten.
- Reviewing the week's plan — what was done and what was not — shapes the next plan.
