# About the Science course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — the first part, the
primary level, is built from Ethiopia's own Environmental Science
books for Grades 1–6, read in full, page by page. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the
people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'.

## Where the course comes from

Science is one world course: the thing itself — the body, light,
plants, weather — is the same everywhere, and each country's way
of teaching it shows inside as examples. The first way stocked is
Ethiopia's: start from the child's own surroundings — their house,
kebele, woreda, family and school — observe, talk, and only then
name the thing. That way is kept as teaching throughout the
primary level.

Book entries are named in full in each lesson's

only — those are counted nowhere, and say so.

## Notes from the books' own front matter

- **What the subject is** (Grade 1 teacher guide, pp. vi–1):
  "science" comes from the Latin *scientia*, knowledge; the
  subject studies how the living and non-living things around us
  interact and how they affect people.
- **The Grade 1 shape** (guide, pp. i–v): five chapters — Location
  of our kebele → Understanding science → The natural environment
  → The social environment → Cross-cutting issues, 238 teaching
  periods. When the real Grade 1 student book is secured, Grade 1
  is built to this exact shape.
- **Why the books look as they do** (student-book introductions,
  Grades 2–3, pp. iv–v): the renewed curriculum aims at
  21st-century skills and learning-to-learn from local knowledge;
  the guides state the way plainly — deliberately picture-led
  ("children understand more easily by seeing and touching than
  by hearing") and learner-centred, with group discussion led by
  a pupil leader and secretary.
- **The widening circles**: the guides grow the child's world in
  scale — Grade 1 the neighbourhood, Grade 2 the woreda, Grade 3
  the sub-city — and the course preserves that growth.
- **Regional examples**: the worked examples in the held student
  books are the Amhara regional edition's own (South Gondar zone;
  Debre Berhan, Dessie, Bahir Dar Zuria). The course keeps the
  examples as teaching and names the region here.
- **A dated passage**: the Grade 2 book's Abbay / Renaissance Dam
  pages (pp. 110–111) were written while the dam was "being
  built… when completed" — the lesson teaches the river and the
  dam as the book presents them; this note carries the dating.

## The books (Ethiopia, Biology — Wave 4, 2026-11-12)

Built from the placements at `liqlearns-courses-work/ethiopia-2026-11-11/biology/`.
Student textbooks Grades 9–12 (MoE with Hawassa University, GEQIP-E,
first published 2023) were read in full and are the pupil-taught core.
Teacher guides are used as evidence only, never counted as pupil-taught,
and are labelled "(teacher guide — evidence)" in every lesson they touch.
**The Biology teacher guides are pre-publication drafts**: their
publication pages carry placeholders (a date of "xxxxx 2022", placeholder
ISBNs and printer lines) — the course records that fact and never cites
a placeholder ISBN or date as fact. Grade 10's student book landed after
the first build pass; its band was built on arrival and the eleven
lessons that had stood on guide evidence alone were upgraded to
pupil-taught, with their acceptance lines corrected.

## The books (Ethiopia, Chemistry and Physics — Wave 4, 2026-11-12)

Built from the placements at `liqlearns-courses-work/ethiopia-2026-11-11/chemistry/`
and `.../physics/`. Student textbooks Grades 9–12 were read in full and
are the pupil-taught core of both titles. Teacher guides are evidence
only, labelled "(teacher guide — evidence)" wherever they are used, and
their pairing warnings are carried in the lessons' sources exactly as
the reads state them. Four Physics topics exist at two depths as
separate lessons (the Grade 10 lesson carries a `-g10` slug): scalars
and vectors, electric fields and field lines, resistors in series and
parallel, and a current makes a magnetic field.
