# Lesson plan — Care for Your Book

**Course:** Science · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Care for Your Book
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how to care for your school book so it stays clean, whole and readable all year.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the child's own book in their own hands — this is the course-opening lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The book-care message** — every book in this course opens with the same message to you, the pupil: this book is yours to learn from, and it will only stay good if you care for it.
2. **Using the book kindly** — use a bookmark to hold your place instead of folding a page corner.

3. **The same message in the Grade 4 book** — the Grade 4 book-care message (printed ii) — the same ten rules, now the older pupil's own responsibility.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title in the Science course, in order. Recommended, never forced — a learner who lands here directly can start here.
- Nothing else is required; every term used is taught inside this lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and use what the part "The book-care message" teaches, in their own words.
2. Say and use what the part "Using the book kindly" teaches, in their own words.
3. Say and use what the part "The same message in the Grade 4 book" teaches, in their own words.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's own world (home, school, neighbourhood) supplies the rest.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Hold your book. Look at its cover, its pages, its corners. Is it clean? Is any page torn or folded? Today you learn how to keep it like new. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts of the lesson in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 12000]: a picture of a pupil covering a book to protect it — the student studies the picture and names what it shows in the lesson's own words.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the book-care message aloud, slowly — the student listens and joins in.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of an older pupil handing a clean, covered book carefully to a younger pupil — the student says what careful handing-over looks like.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing to a companion and answers one question back (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
- The learner cannot do the Do It Yourself → do the first item together, then let them finish the rest alone.
