# Making materials locally — siapo and paper

## Do it first
A siapo cloth was once the bark of a tree. A sheet of paper in your hand was once a plant. Who changed them — and what kind of change was each step? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Samoa's Year 9 way in — its Book 2 stops inside its chemistry units to ask what Samoa itself makes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Materials Samoa makes — siapo and the rest

Samoa makes many of its own materials: **siapo (tapa cloth)** from bark, **coconut cream**, **ice cream**, **bread**, **rope**, **beer** and **kofe powder**. The book's task is a maker's audit: for each local material, trace the steps of its making from the raw thing to the finished one (printed pp. 32–34).

## Sub lesson 2 — Hand-made paper — and naming every change

Paper can be made by hand from plant materials: the plants are pulped, the pulp is spread to **form sheets**, the sheets are **blotted** and **dried**. The lesson inside the making is the naming: at each step, say whether the change is **physical** (the material is still itself, only reshaped) or **chemical** (a new material has formed). Pulping, forming, blotting and drying reshape; fermenting beer and baking bread make new materials. A local craft, read with a chemist's eye (printed pp. 32–34).
