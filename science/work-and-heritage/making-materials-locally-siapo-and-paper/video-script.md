Today: **Making materials locally — siapo and paper** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**Materials Samoa makes — siapo and the rest.** Samoa makes many of its own materials: siapo (tapa cloth) from bark, coconut cream, ice cream, bread, rope, beer and kofe powder. The book's task is a maker's audit: for each local material, trace the steps of its making from the raw thing to the finished one (printed pp. 32–34).

**Hand-made paper — and naming every change.** Paper can be made by hand from plant materials: the plants are pulped, the pulp is spread to form sheets, the sheets are blotted and dried. The lesson inside the making is the naming: at each step, say whether the change is physical (the material is still itself, only reshaped) or chemical (a new material has formed). Pulping, forming, blotting and drying reshape; fermenting beer and baking bread make new materials. A local craft, read with a chemist's eye (printed pp. 32–34).
