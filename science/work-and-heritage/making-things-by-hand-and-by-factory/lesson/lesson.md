# Work and Livelihood — Making Things: By Hand and by Factory

## Do it first

Your dress began as cotton in a field. Your injera's mitad began as clay. Between the field and your home, hands — or machines — changed them. That changing is today's lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What industry is

Industry turns farm and mineral products into other useful things: grain into flour and oil, cotton into cloth, clay into pots. Nothing is eaten or used exactly as nature gave it — industry is the changing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cottage industry — by hand and skill

Cottage industry works by hand and skill: blacksmithing, pottery, leather work and weaving. Its products: the ensira jar, pots, the mitad, and woven cloth — gabi, netela and the habesha kemis dress.

## Sub lesson 3 — Modern industry — by factory

Modern industry works in factories: sugar, paper, goods and textile factories, making clothes, shoes, sheets, blankets, bags, oil and sugar for the whole country at once.

## Sub lesson 4 — Cottage and modern industry — the Grade 4 picture

Grade 4 fills in both kinds. Cottage industry (የጎጆ ኢንዱስትሪ) uses simple hand tools and traditional methods at small scale — weaving, pottery, metalwork, woodwork, leatherwork, jewellery — and it made the tools people have used for thousands of years: the plough, the sickle, the doma spade, household goods and traditional dress. Modern industry uses fuel- or electricity-driven machines and makes much in a short time: light industry works on farm products (food, textile, sugar, paper and drinks factories), and heavy industry uses minerals with complex machines, big capital and trained labour, making production tools themselves. The region's metal factories stand in Bahir Dar, Kombolcha and Debre Berhan.
