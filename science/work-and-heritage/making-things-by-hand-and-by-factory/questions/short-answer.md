# Work and Livelihood — Making Things: By Hand and by Factory — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 2) Name two cottage industries and their products.**
**Answer:** Pottery — ensira, pots, mitad; weaving — gabi, netela, habesha kemis; blacksmithing — tools; leather work (any two with products).

**2. (Sub lesson 3) How do cottage and modern industry differ?**
**Answer:** Cottage industry works by hand and skill; modern industry works in factories at large scale.

**3. (Sub lesson 4) Name the three towns where the region's metal factories stand.**
**Answer:** Bahir Dar, Kombolcha and Debre Berhan.

**4. (Sub lesson 5) Name two printed problems of Ethiopia's modern industry.**
**Answer:** Any two of: light-industry only; capital shortage; weak inter-industry links; backward thinking; missing infrastructure; no clear industrial policy.

**5. (Sub lesson 6) Give one example of each: cottage, light and heavy industry.**
**Answer:** Cottage: e.g. home weaving or pottery. Light: e.g. a garment or food factory making goods people use directly. Heavy: metal or cement industry (Ethiopia/Kenya) or metal industry (Zambia/Zimbabwe).

**6. (Sub lesson 7) Why is East Africa's industry still small, in the book's account?**
**Answer:** Most countries of the region lived through civil war and social-economic crisis, so industry never grew — it stays under 8.9% of their economies.
