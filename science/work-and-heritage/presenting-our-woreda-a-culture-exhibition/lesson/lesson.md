# Presenting Our Woreda — a Culture Exhibition

## Do it first

Imagine the letter arrives: your woreda is invited to the big exhibition in Bahir Dar. What will your stall show the whole region? Decide before you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The exhibition in Bahir Dar

The Region's Culture and Tourism Commission plans an exhibition in Bahir Dar. Pupils imagine presenting their own woreda there: its dress, its jewellery, its traditional way of settling conflicts, and its traditional sports — with a small market beside the stalls, selling what the woreda makes.

[Picture 12203 — a culture exhibition hall with stalls from many woredas]

## Sub lesson 2 — What each woreda brings

Every place brings its own best: Gondar children bring photos of Fasil Ghebbi; North Wollo brings Lalibela posters and jano kuta cloth; Awi brings bamboo goods; Wag Himra brings white honey; Gojjam brings honey and enqutatash eskista; Dessie brings Wollo incense; North Shewa brings ztit and bernos. No two stalls are the same — that is the exhibition's whole point.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: at the Bahir Dar exhibition, every woreda shows its own dress, jewellery, conflict-settling custom and sports — and no two stalls are alike. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Plan your own woreda's stall: list its dress, jewellery, conflict custom and sport.
2. Draw the stall with a small market table beside it.
3. Write the one sentence your stall's sign will carry.
