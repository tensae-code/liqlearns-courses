# Our food heritage — traditional dishes of the Amhara Region

## Do it first

Name a dish your family makes that a visitor from far away would not know. Who taught it? How is it made? This lesson keeps the region's answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — The eaten list and the drinks

The region's traditional foods, as the book lists them: injera with doro or shiro wot, tiresh, dabbo, tukusie, qwanṭa, genfo, qinche, anebabero, qocho, chibto and chechebsa. And its traditional drinks: atmit, beso, absh, ergo, korefe, tella, buqri and tej. This list is heritage: it is what the region hands down.

## Sub lesson 2 — How four dishes are prepared (teacher guide — evidence)

For four dishes the book gathers short preparation methods from zone teachers' interviews — how the dish is made, in outline: **Abzi**, from the Awi zone; **Qatagna**, from Wag Himra; **Chechebsa**, from the Oromo zone; and **Qinche**, made across most Amhara zones. Learn each dish with its zone and its way of preparation as the interviews give it: the dish, where it is made, and the making, step by step in the teacher's own order. The regional teacher guide's teaching on Ethiopia's traditional food preparation stands beside this part as labelled evidence.

## Say it to your companion

Name five dishes and three drinks from the region's lists, then take one of the four prepared dishes and tell its zone and its making, step by step.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eaten list and the drinks list from memory, then check yourself.
2. Ask at home how one listed dish is prepared, and compare it with the book's zone method.
3. Draw a mesob with injera and wots, genfo and chechebsa on it.
