# Keeping goats, sheep and pigs

## Do it first
Before you read: whose goats graze near your home? Are they the local kind, or a kind brought from far away? What sicknesses have you heard named for goats — or for pigs? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Uganda's way in — its Primary Five home-study booklet teaches the keeping of goats, sheep and pigs whole: breeds, diseases, and care.

## Sub lesson 1 — Goats, sheep and pigs — exotic vs local breeds

The booklet keeps three animals together — **goats, sheep and pigs** — and sorts their breeds two ways: **exotic breeds**, brought from outside, against **local breeds**, the kinds kept in the place itself. A keeper names which kind they keep, and why.

## Sub lesson 2 — Diseases — signs and control

Each animal has its **diseases**, and the booklet teaches them whole: the disease, its **signs**, and its **control**. The named diseases are **pneumonia**, **heartwater**, and — for pigs — **African swine fever**. A keeper reads the signs early, because control starts with seeing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Care of the animals

Underneath breeds and diseases stands the daily work: the **care of the animals**. Keeping goats, sheep and pigs is the care given day by day — the booklet closes on the keeper's own work with the animals.

## The key ideas

- Goats, sheep and pigs are sorted by **breeds**: **exotic** (brought from outside) against **local** (the kinds of the place).
- Their diseases are taught with **signs and control** — **pneumonia**, **heartwater**, and **African swine fever** in pigs.
- Keeping the animals stands on daily **care**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — breeds first, then diseases with their signs and control, then care. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Which disease belongs to pigs?" Answer with African swine fever and one thing about its control.

## Do It Yourself
1. Make a two-column chart — exotic breeds | local breeds — and under each, write what the words mean and one reason a keeper might choose that kind.
2. Write the three named diseases with the animal each belongs to, as far as the lesson gives it.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — reading a sick animal's signs early.
