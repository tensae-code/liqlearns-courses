# Work and Livelihood — When Our Work Hurts Nature

## Do it first

A hill that was green in your grandparents' time is bare in yours. Who bared it? Not an enemy — ordinary work did. How? Read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where the pressure comes from

As population grows, demand grows with it: more farmland is cleared, more firewood is cut. Where people lack other energy, wood is the only fuel — so the forest falls, tree by tree, for cooking fires.

[Picture 12029 — an overgrazed, eroding field]

## Sub lesson 2 — Overgrazing and backward land use

Too many animals on too little grass — overgrazing — strips the cover; backward land use does the same. Bare land erodes: the rain takes the soil, and the farm itself is eaten by the work that was meant to feed it. The pressure can be eased: plant trees, graze in turns, use land wisely, and find other energy.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: how economic activity can harm natural resources — growing demand for farmland and firewood, lacking energy alternatives, overgrazing and backward land use stripping forest and eroding soil — and how the pressure can be eased. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the same field twice: covered and cared for; bare and eroding.
2. Count the trees near your home; say whether the number is growing or shrinking.
3. Ask an elder how a nearby hillside looked when they were young.
