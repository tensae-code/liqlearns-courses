# Culture — Many Cultures Side by Side

## Do it first

At one festival, you may hear three languages and see five kinds of dress. Is that a problem — or a richness? Decide, then read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Many cultures, one community

Cultural diversity means many cultures in one community: neighbours who speak different languages, follow different religions, prepare food differently, marry with different ceremonies, and sing and dance their own songs. Diversity is the normal shape of a real community.

[Picture 12023 — a festival where neighbours of different languages and faiths celebrate together]

## Sub lesson 2 — Eating, singing, celebrating — differently

Even the table differs: eating customs vary from culture to culture, and so do the songs at a wedding and the dances at a festival. Living side by side means meeting these differences every day — at the market, at school, at celebrations.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why cultures differ — diffusion and assimilation

Why do neighbouring cultures differ at all? The Grade 5 book gives the causes: **landform, climate, minerals, native plants, beliefs and philosophies** shape each people's way. And cultures living side by side **borrow** from each other. Borrowing single features is **diffusion** (የባህል ስርፀት) — one custom, one food, one tool travels. A society wholly taking another's culture is **assimilation** — the whole way replaced. Values are relative — what one culture honours another may not — yet all cultures share common features too.

## Sub lesson 4 — Culture is always changing

Culture never stands still. Change can be **slow or fast** — and today's change, driven by fast-growing science and technology, is reshaping work, food, dress, housing and the whole way of life, helped by education, media and contact between peoples. But the book adds a warning worth keeping: **not every incoming change is good** — harmful foreign customs can spoil good ones. The dress-and-ornament activity makes the point visible: six outfits, each belonging to its own community — change the outfit and you change what it says.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: cultural diversity — many cultures living in one community: different languages, religions, foods, marriages, songs and eating customs, side by side. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a festival in your community with two cultures celebrating side by side.
2. Learn one greeting in a neighbour culture's language.
3. List the languages you hear at your market.
4. Tell one real example of diffusion from your own community and say why it is not assimilation.
