# Selective breeding of plants and animals

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — selective breeding of plants and animals. Hold it in your mind, say what you already know about it out loud. Then read on: this is Bhutan's way in — Bhutan's Class VIII book teaches the breeding of plants and animals inside its Living Things and their Environment chapter — the same chapter as adaptation and survival: what nature does by survival, the farmer does by choosing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing the parents — the breeder's one tool

**Selective breeding** is improvement by choosing. The farmer or breeder looks at the plants or animals they have, picks the ones with the wanted quality — the plant that yields most, the animal that grows strongest, resists disease, or gives most milk — and breeds from those, generation after generation. The unwanted quality is bred out by never choosing it as a parent; the wanted quality grows stronger in the stock because every generation's parents were chosen for it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Breeding plants and animals — the same choosing at work

In **plants**, the choosing is done with seed and pollen: seed is saved from the best plants, and their offspring are grown on and chosen again — this is how farming peoples, over centuries, turned wild grasses into the grains and vegetables of today. In **animals**, the choosing is done with mates: the strongest, healthiest, best-yielding animals are paired, and their young inherit the chosen qualities in their own mixture. Bhutan's book sets this inside the chapter on adaptation and survival for a reason: adaptation is fitting by survival in the wild; breeding is fitting by choosing on the farm — the heritage of every farming community is written in its seeds and its herds.

## The key ideas

- **Selective breeding** improves plants and animals by choosing which ones become parents, generation after generation.
- In plants the choosing works through saved **seed** and pollen; in animals through chosen **mates**.
- The wanted quality strengthens because every generation's parents were picked for it; the unwanted quality is bred out by never being picked.
- Breeding is fitting by **choosing**, where adaptation is fitting by **survival** — Bhutan's book teaches them in the same chapter.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A farmer's neighbour has stronger goats than anyone nearby. How did selective breeding most likely do it?" Answer from the lesson: By choosing, generation after generation: the strongest, healthiest goats were kept as the parents of the next herd, and the weaker ones were never bred from — so the chosen qualities strengthened in the stock.
