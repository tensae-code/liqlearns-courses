Today: **Selective breeding of plants and animals** — Bhutan's way: improvement by choosing.

A farmer holds two handfuls of seed. From one handful, generation after generation, she plants only the fullest grains — and her harvest slowly changes: bigger grains, stronger plants. That is selective breeding, and it works the same with animals: choose the strongest as parents, never breed from the weak, and the herd itself improves. Nature fits living things by survival; the farmer fits them by choosing. Bhutan teaches the two side by side, in one chapter — because they are one idea with two hands.
