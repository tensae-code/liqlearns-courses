# The Region's Food and Dress

## Do it first

Injera is on the mesob — but the dish beside it tells visitors which community cooked it. And the cloth on a shoulder tells which place the wearer calls home. Read how.

## Sub lesson 1 — Food traditions of the region

How food is made and eaten is tied to culture and place. Injera, doro wot, genfo, dabbo, qolo and nifro are common everywhere in the region — while chechebsa belongs to the Oromo community and abzi to the Awi. And faith rules shape the table too: not every animal's meat is eaten, and not every milk is drunk, by every community.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional dress — and what it is made from

Clothing is one of the marks of a culture. The book pictures the traditional dress of five places — Awi, Gojjam, Gondar, Wollo and Shewa — worn for work, for joy, for mourning, at festivals and for adornment. Pupils sort local dress two ways: by material — cotton, nylon, leather, silk — and by use.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: food and dress are marks of culture — some dishes belong to one community, and each place's dress can be sorted by what it is made from and what it is worn for. Then answer one question they ask you about it.
