# Our Heritage — Lalibela, Gondar and the Simien Mountains

## Do it first

A church cut down into living rock, eight hundred years ago. A castle city. Mountains that touch the sky. All three are yours — handed down. What does 'handed down' demand of you?

## Sub lesson 1 — Heritage and its kinds

Heritage is what passes from generation to generation — the material, spiritual and cultural wealth of past generations. It sorts three ways: natural, cultural and historical. And cultural and historical heritage splits again: tangible heritage you can touch — dress, tools, architecture, alphabet and literature; and intangible heritage you carry inside — joy and mourning ceremonies, music (zefen, qererto, shilela), tales, proverbs, and values like justice, faith, togetherness and freedom. Natural heritage holds parks, lakes, animal shelters and church forests.

## Sub lesson 2 — The region's UNESCO World Heritage table

Three of the region's heritages stand on the UNESCO World Heritage list: the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, in North Wollo, Lasta; the Simien Mountains National Park, in North Gondar — Debarq, Ada Arqay, Beyeda, Janamora; and the Gondar emperors' castles, in Gondar town. A second table fills the value of Lake Tana's islands, the Tis Abbay falls and the Ankober palace.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tourism — and why visitors come

Tourism is visiting and admiring attractions for mental and physical satisfaction; a tourist stays away from home not more than a year. Tourism earns foreign exchange, creates jobs and speeds cultural exchange. And heritage does quieter work too: it builds identity and unity, and gives the new generation a past to build on.

## Sub lesson 4 — Great thinkers and heroes of the region

Citizens whose research, discoveries and deeds serve the country greatly are called great thinkers (እሳቤዎች); heroes are remembered by their work across generations. Four are pictured and named for the region: Emperor Tewodros II and Emperor Menelik II, for statecraft; the world-laureate master artist Afewerk Tekle; and the author Dr Haddis Alemayehu, for art and literature. Pupils research and report on Tewodros's and Menelik's rule and deeds — from books, from people, or from the internet.

## Sub lesson 5 — Our heritages in the teacher guides (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guides carry the same heritage lesson (teacher guide — evidence), and the Oromia guide's syllabus spells the skill: list the heritages found in your zone and surroundings, state their uses, and tell natural heritages from man-made heritages and from intangible heritages (የማይታዩ ቅርሶች).

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: heritage is the wealth handed down — rock churches, castles, mountains, songs and values — and Lalibela, Gondar and the Simien Mountains stand on the world's own list. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort eight heritages you know into natural, cultural and historical — then sort the cultural ones again: tangible or intangible.
2. Draw one UNESCO site of the region and caption it with its place.
3. Research one of the four great figures and report three deeds.
