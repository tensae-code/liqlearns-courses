# Settling Quarrels — and the Warka Tree Mediation

## Do it first

Two neighbours are shouting over a fence. A judge could settle it — slowly, at cost. Or three elders could sit under a tree this week. Which road does your community take, and why?

## Sub lesson 1 — Two ways of settling a quarrel

Conflict comes from differences over resources or over thinking. It is settled two ways: formally, in court — or by the traditional system most people use. The traditional way saves time and money, keeps social bonds and kinship intact, and rests on respect, tolerance, faith and shared purpose — settling disputes once and for all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shimiglina — a mediation under the warka tree (case study, plot summary only)

The case, in summary: siblings Ato Debashu and W/ro Workyantifu fall out over their parents' garden plot. The administration directs them to settle it or go to court. Elder Ato Haile proposes mediation, and a panel — Haile, W/ro Yimenashu and a religious father — is named to sit under the big warka tree between the two houses on Tir 21. Debashu argues the elder brother who carried the parents more deserves the bigger share; Workyantifu answers that she cared for the parents most, and their testament was an equal split. The elders rule an equal split; Debashu must apologise. He lifts a stone onto his shoulder, saying 'I have wronged you, forgive me.' She rises, takes the stone off his shoulder, saying 'I forgive you' — and the two embrace, as peace descends.

## Say it to your companion
