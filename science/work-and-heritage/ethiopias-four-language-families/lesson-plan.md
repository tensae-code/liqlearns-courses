# Lesson plan — Ethiopia's Four Language Families

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** Ethiopia's Four Language Families
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's 70+ languages sorted into their four families — Semitic, Cushitic, Omotic and Nilo-Saharan — and where each family lives.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the culture lessons — language is culture's first element, and here the country's languages are mapped as families.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A museum of peoples** — Ethiopia holds 80+ nations and nationalities and 70+ languages — 'a museum of peoples' — and all its languages fall into four families; all languages are equal (Grade 5).

2. **The four families** — Semitic (Ge'ez, Amharic, Tigrinya, Harari, Guragigna, Argobbigna, Gafat — north and centre); Cushitic (Oromigna, Somaligna, Afarigna, Agewigna — the most widely spread); Omotic (Wolaytigna, Gamogna — the south); Nilo-Saharan (Mezhenger, Gumuz, Mursi, Anyuak, Nuer — Gambela and Benishangul-Gumuz in the west) (Grade 5).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
- The learner cannot do the Do It Yourself → do the first item together, then let them finish the rest alone.
