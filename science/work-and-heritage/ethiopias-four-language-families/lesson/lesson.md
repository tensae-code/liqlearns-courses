# Ethiopia's Four Language Families

## Do it first

How many languages can your class name that are spoken in Ethiopia? List them. Could anyone sort them into families? Try it first.

## Sub lesson 1 — A museum of peoples

Ethiopia holds **more than 80 nations and nationalities** and **more than 70 languages** — the book calls it **a museum of peoples**. And the languages are not a heap: every one of them falls into one of **four families**, sorted by their kinship. One rule stands over the whole sorting, printed plainly: **all languages are equal** — a family is a kinship, never a rank.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four families

The four families, with their example languages and their homes. **Semitic**: Ge'ez, Amharic, Tigrinya, Harari, Guragigna, Argobbigna, Gafat — the **north and centre**. **Cushitic**: Oromigna, Somaligna, Afarigna, Agewigna — the **most widely spread** of the families. **Omotic**: Wolaytigna, Gamogna — the **south**. **Nilo-Saharan**: Mezhenger, Gumuz, Mursi, Anyuak, Nuer — **Gambela and Benishangul-Gumuz in the west**. The lesson's own exercise places eight named languages into their families — the sorting is the skill.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: Ethiopia's 70+ languages sorted into their four families — Semitic, Cushitic, Omotic and Nilo-Saharan — and where each family lives. Then answer one question they ask you about it.
