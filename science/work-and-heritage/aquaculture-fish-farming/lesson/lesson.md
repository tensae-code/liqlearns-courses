# Aquaculture — fish farming

## Do it first
Before you read: farmers rear chickens and cattle on land. Can fish be farmed too — kept, fed and reared on purpose, instead of only caught? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Barbados's way in — its Primary Science Syllabus closes its Agricultural Science strand with aquaculture.

## Sub lesson 1 — Aquaculture — farming fish

**Aquaculture** is **fish farming**: rearing fish on purpose, in a made place, instead of only catching wild fish. Barbados's syllabus (Class 4) asks three things together: what aquaculture is, the **fish reared** this way, and **how fish farms are created** — the farm is built first, then stocked and reared, the way a crop field is prepared before it is planted.

Aquaculture belongs in the farming lessons: it is agriculture done in water, standing beside the poultry and cattle rearing the same syllabus teaches for the land.

## Sub lesson 2 — Advantages and disadvantages

The syllabus does not leave aquaculture as a marvel; it asks for its **advantages and disadvantages** together. Farmed fish are reared close and on purpose — a supply people control, as with reared chickens and cattle. Weighed against that, a fish farm must be created and kept, and its rearing can fail or harm if it is badly done — the account has two sides, as with every farming choice in this title.

The learner's job is the weighing itself: name an advantage, name a disadvantage, and hold both.

## The key ideas

- **Aquaculture** is fish farming — rearing fish on purpose in a made place, agriculture done in water.
- Barbados's syllabus teaches the **fish reared** and **how fish farms are created**, beside poultry and cattle rearing on land.
- Aquaculture has **advantages and disadvantages** — the lesson's job is to weigh both.
