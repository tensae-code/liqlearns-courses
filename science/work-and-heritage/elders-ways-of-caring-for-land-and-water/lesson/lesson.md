# Culture — Our Elders' Ways of Caring for Land and Water

## Do it first

Long before modern farming books, farmers held the soil on steep hills. How? Walk a hillside in your mind and look for their tricks. Then read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Holding the land: terraces, drains and contours

Elders' land care includes: guarding fields from trampling; building terraces down a slope; cutting field drains (ቦይ/ፈሰስ) to guide water; ploughing along the contour, across the slope, so rain runs slowly instead of tearing the soil away.

## Sub lesson 2 — Resting and feeding the soil; guarding water and trees

Fields are rested by fallowing (ማከር) — left unplanted for a time to recover — and fed with manure. Rivers and springs are protected from waste. And trees are planted, because roots hold banks and shade keeps water sweet. The lesson's project: ask local agricultural experts for more methods and what each gives the farmer.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: the traditional ways of caring for natural resources — guarding fields, terracing, field drains, protecting rivers and springs, fallowing, contour ploughing, manuring and planting trees. Then answer one question they ask you about it.
