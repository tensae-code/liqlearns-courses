# Lesson plan — Culture — Our Elders' Ways of Caring for Land and Water

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** Culture — Our Elders' Ways of Caring for Land and Water
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the traditional ways of caring for natural resources — guarding fields, terracing, field drains, protecting rivers and springs, fallowing, contour ploughing, manuring and planting trees.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the culture lessons meet the natural-resources lessons — tradition carries land care.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Holding the land: terraces, drains and contours** — elders' land care includes: guarding fields from trampling; building terraces down a slope; cutting field drains (ቦይ/ፈሰስ) to guide water; ploughing along the contour, across the slope, so rain runs slowly instead of tearing the soil away.
2. **Resting and feeding the soil; guarding water and trees** — fields are rested by fallowing (ማከር) — left unplanted for a time to recover — and fed with manure.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
