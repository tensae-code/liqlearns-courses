# Culture — Harmful Customs We Must Stop — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Which practice does the book list as harmful?**
a) Hospitality  b) Early marriage  c) Elder mediation
**Answer: b) Early marriage** — Early marriage is on the harmful list.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) The book's pictured alternative to early marriage is —**
a) a girl in school with her books  b) a bigger feast  c) nothing
**Answer: a) a girl in school with her books** — Schooling is the pictured alternative.

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Into which column does female circumcision go?**
a) Useful  b) Harmful — must be stopped  c) Neither
**Answer: b) Harmful — must be stopped** — It is listed among harmful practices.

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Smoking and drunkenness harm —**
a) health and work  b) nothing  c) only clothes
**Answer: a) health and work** — They destroy health and work.

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Sorting customs into useful and harmful teaches us to —**
a) keep every old thing blindly  b) keep the good and stop the harmful  c) stop everything old
**Answer: b) keep the good and stop the harmful** — Keep the useful; stop the harmful.

**6. (Sub lesson 3) The Grade 5 harmful list includes —**
a) uvula cutting, gum extraction, early marriage, cutting girls' genitals, marriage by abduction  b) only smoking  c) only feasting
**Answer: a) uvula cutting, gum extraction, early marriage, cutting girls' genitals, marriage by abduction** — That is the printed Grade 5 list.

**7. (Sub lesson 3) Zerfe's story ends with —**
a) her community's support lifting her to a better state  b) her second marriage succeeding  c) her leaving the country
**Answer: a) her community's support lifting her to a better state** — Community support lifts her at the plot's end.

**8. (Sub lesson 4) Cutting girls' genitals is defended as —**
a) a school duty  b) settling a girl, and a religious duty  c) a medical treatment
**Answer: b) settling a girl, and a religious duty** — Those are the printed defences.

**9. (Sub lesson 4) A printed harm of cutting is —**
a) heavy bleeding and damage to the urinary organs  b) stronger legs  c) better eyesight
**Answer: a) heavy bleeding and damage to the urinary organs** — Bleeding and urinary damage are printed harms.

**10. (Sub lesson 5) The uvula is —**
a) a tooth  b) the part hanging between mouth and throat  c) a bone
**Answer: b) the part hanging between mouth and throat** — That is the book's definition.

**11. (Sub lesson 5) The book's harm list for uvula cutting includes —**
a) unclean tools spreading communicable diseases, and bleeding that can kill  b) better singing  c) faster teething
**Answer: a) unclean tools spreading communicable diseases, and bleeding that can kill** — Those are printed harms.

**12. (Sub lesson 6) Babies usually cut their first teeth at —**
a) 6–8 months  b) 3 years  c) birth
**Answer: a) 6–8 months** — 6–8 months, lower front first.

**13. (Sub lesson 6) Teething signs include —**
a) fretfulness, biting hard things, heavy drooling, trouble sleeping  b) fever charts only  c) walking
**Answer: a) fretfulness, biting hard things, heavy drooling, trouble sleeping** — Those are the printed signs.

**14. (Sub lesson 7) Abduction (ጠለፋ) is —**
a) a wedding gift  b) taking a woman or man by force to an unknown or unwanted place  c) a court order
**Answer: b) taking a woman or man by force to an unknown or unwanted place** — That is the printed definition.

**15. (Sub lesson 7) A printed reason for abduction is —**
a) avoiding bride-price and wedding costs  b) school fees  c) harvest work
**Answer: a) avoiding bride-price and wedding costs** — Avoiding bride-price and wedding costs is printed.

**16. (Sub lesson 8) Table 6.1 sets early marriage's —**
a) songs against dances  b) causes against harms  c) prices against wages
**Answer: b) causes against harms** — Causes against harms.

**17. (Sub lesson 8) A printed harm of early marriage is —**
a) fistula  b) strong teeth  c) more schooling
**Answer: a) fistula** — Fistula is a printed harm.

**18. (Sub lesson 9) The three stopping duties include —**
a) awareness again and again, enlisting religious fathers and respected persons, and strong kebele and government laws  b) silence  c) waiting
**Answer: a) awareness again and again, enlisting religious fathers and respected persons, and strong kebele and government laws** — Those are the three printed duties.

**19. (Sub lesson 9) Strong laws against the practices are passed at —**
a) kebele and government level  b) household level only  c) market level
**Answer: a) kebele and government level** — Kebele and government level.

**20. (Sub lesson 10) A harmful traditional practice, by the Grade 6 definition, negatively affects —**
a) only crops  b) children's and women's wellbeing, human rights, and social and economic participation  c) only buildings
**Answer: b) children's and women's wellbeing, human rights, and social and economic participation** — That is the printed definition.

**21. (Sub lesson 10) The Grade 6 list is found —**
a) only in one village  b) in Ethiopia and across East Africa  c) only abroad
**Answer: b) in Ethiopia and across East Africa** — Ethiopia and across East Africa.

**22. (Sub lesson 11) The national strategy's order is —**
a) rescue, prevent, teach  b) teach (awareness-education), prevent, rescue and support  c) prevent only
**Answer: b) teach (awareness-education), prevent, rescue and support** — Teach → prevent → rescue, in that order.

**23. (Sub lesson 11) The field task sends pupils to —**
a) the kebele office or women-and-children's affairs branch  b) the market only  c) another country
**Answer: a) the kebele office or women-and-children's affairs branch** — The kebele-office field task.
