# Culture — Harmful Customs We Must Stop

## Do it first

Not every old custom is a good custom. Can you think of one practice that hurts people — especially children? This lesson names them plainly.

## Sub lesson 1 — The harmful list, named plainly

Practices sort into useful and harmful. The harmful ones — acts the community does not accept, that must be stopped — include: early marriage; feasting beyond one's means; female circumcision; uvula cutting (እንጥል መቁረጥ); foreign dress against the culture; smoking and drunkenness.

[Picture 12025 — a girl staying in school with books]

## Sub lesson 2 — Their harms, and how they are prevented

Each harmful practice carries its harm: early marriage steals schooling and health; feasting beyond one's means ruins a family's living; cutting practices wound the body and can bring lasting damage; smoking and drunkenness destroy health and work. The alternative is pictured too: a girl staying in school with her books. Prevention begins with naming the harm honestly, as this lesson does.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: the traditional practices the community does not accept and that must be stopped — early marriage, feasting beyond one's means, female circumcision, uvula cutting — and their harms, sorted honestly against the useful customs. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a two-column table: useful customs | harmful customs, from both lessons.
2. Draw the alternative picture: a girl in school with books, captioned in your own words.
3. Talk with an elder: which harmful custom is your community leaving behind?
