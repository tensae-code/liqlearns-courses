# How people lived in the past

## Do it first
Before you read: name one thing in your home that your grandparents did without — and one thing they used that you never use. Then read on: this is Iran's way in — its books hold the past and the present side by side, from the fire-lit home to the instant message, and ask what changed and why.

## Sub lesson 1 — Warmth and light from fire

Iran's Grade 1 book opens the past with the home: in old times people used fire to warm and light their homes — no switches, no lamps wired to a grid. The day's work, the cooking and the evening all bent around the fire and the daylight.

## Sub lesson 2 — Messages — the long road to fast and easy

Iran's Grade 6 book tells the communication story: humans have always needed to communicate with each other, and suppose today at 18:30 there is a football match everyone must hear about — in the past that news travelled as fast as a walker, a rider or a letter; now it travels at once. The book sets pupils to talk about the features, uses and changes of communication tools, and the reason for each change.

[Picture — messages — the long road to fast and easy as Iran's book shows it — the student names the part that carries the sub lesson's point]

## Sub lesson 3 — Why life-tools change

Tools change because people need them to do more: faster news, safer light, warmer rooms. Each new tool keeps the old job — warming, lighting, telling — and does it in a new way. Reading the past by its tools shows both what stayed the same (the needs) and what changed (the ways).

[Picture — why life-tools change as Iran's book shows it — the student names the part that carries the sub lesson's point]

## The key ideas

- In old times, fire warmed and lit the home — daily life bent around fire and daylight.
- Humans have always needed to communicate; the tools changed — walker, rider, letter, then fast and easy messages.
- Tools change because people need the same jobs done better: the needs stay, the ways change.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What stayed the same between the past and now, in this lesson?" Answer: the needs — warmth, light, and telling each other things. What changed is the tools that do those jobs.

## Do It Yourself
1. Ask an older person how news of a match or a wedding travelled when they were young, and write the journey step by step.
2. Draw one job — lighting a room — in the past and now, side by side.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home in five sentences: fire first, then the road of messages.
