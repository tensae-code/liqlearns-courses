# Lesson plan — How people lived in the past

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** How people lived in the past
**Place in the curriculum:** Work and Heritage — appended to Unit 13 (Work and Heritage) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how daily life itself was different in the past, and how it changed — Iran's way, from the Grade 1 "from past to future" lesson (in old times people used fire to warm and light their homes) to the Grade 6 "from past to future" lesson on communication: humans have always needed to communicate, and the tools for it changed from age to age until messages became fast and easy
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Warmth and light from fire** — Iran's Grade 1 book opens the past with the home: in old times people used fire to warm and light their homes — no switches, no lamps wired to a grid. The day's work, the cooking and the evening all bent around the fire and the daylight.
2. **Messages — the long road to fast and easy** — Iran's Grade 6 book tells the communication story: humans have always needed to communicate with each other, and suppose today at 18:30 there is a football match everyone must hear about — in the past that news travelled as fast as a walker, a rider or a letter; now it travels at once. The book sets pupils to talk about the features, uses and changes of communication tools, and the reason for each change.
3. **Why life-tools change** — Tools change because people need them to do more: faster news, safer light, warmer rooms. Each new tool keeps the old job — warming, lighting, telling — and does it in a new way. Reading the past by its tools shows both what stayed the same (the needs) and what changed (the ways).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Warmth and light from fire.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Messages — the long road to fast and easy.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: Why life-tools change.
