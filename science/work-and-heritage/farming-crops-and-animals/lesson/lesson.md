# Work and Livelihood — Farming: Crops and Animals

## Do it first

Your breakfast grew somewhere, or was raised somewhere. Who grew it? Most work in our country is one kind of work. What is it? Read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Economy and the backbone

Economy means the daily activities by which people meet their basic needs. And our country's backbone is agriculture: 82% of the people live by it — if farming stops, the country stops.

[Picture 12028 — a farm with cereal fields and cattle, sheep and chickens]

## Sub lesson 2 — Crop production

One half of farming grows crops: potato, wheat, maize, teff, barley, sorghum, finger millet, niger seed, linseed, peas, beans, chickpeas, and fruits and vegetables besides.

## Sub lesson 3 — Animal rearing

The other half keeps animals: horned cattle, equines (horses, donkeys, mules), sheep, goats and chickens — each giving food, work or wealth to the farming family.

## Sub lesson 4 — Agriculture — the backbone, and mixed farming

Grade 4 says it with numbers: about 80% of Ethiopians live by farming. Farming is the main food source, it supplies industry with raw materials, and it earns foreign exchange. And the common pattern in Gojjam, Gondar, Wollo and Shewa is mixed farming (ቅይጥ ግብርና) — crops and animals together on one farm, each half helping the other. The region's three climates — dega, woina dega and qolla — grow different crops, so the backbone bends with the land.

[Picture 12208 — a labelled crop-sample board: cereals, pulses and oilseeds in rows]

## Sub lesson 5 — Kinds of crops and the three growing seasons

Crops sort into families: cereals (teff, barley, wheat…), pulses (faba bean, lentil, chickpea), oilseeds (sesame, linseed, nug, sunflower), cash crops, and vegetables and fruit. They are grown in the meher main season, in the belg rains, and by irrigation. Traditional farming yields little, for want of modern tools and inputs; modern crop farming adds fertiliser, irrigation water, improved seed, pest and weed killers and machines. The class project: collect local crop samples, glue them in labelled rows on stiff paper, class them, and present the board.

## Sub lesson 6 — Animals we keep and what they do for us

Alongside the crops, the region keeps chickens, sheep, goats, oxen and cows, camels, horses, donkeys and bees. Animals give food, transport, farm power and other services — pack oxen carry, a dairy cow gives milk, a beehive gives honey, chickens give eggs and meat.

## Sub lesson 7 — Agriculture — Ethiopia's backbone

Grade 5 gives agriculture its full weight. Agriculture — **crop growing and animal keeping together** — is Ethiopia's greatest economic base: **over 82% of Ethiopians** make their living from it. It divides into the two parts this lesson already knows, now at country scale: **crop production** and **animal husbandry**. When agriculture does well, the country eats and earns; when it fails, everything else feels it.

## Sub lesson 8 — Crops and where they grow

The crop lists are sorted by where they grow. **Cereals**: wheat, barley, **teff**, sorghum, maize. **Pulses**: beans, chickpeas, lentils — mostly highland. **Oil crops**: noug, sunflower, linseed, sesame, castor, groundnut, rapeseed — mostly lowland. Plus **coffee** and other cash products. Highland growers raise teff, barley, wheat and maize; the lowlands raise sorghum and zengada. Most crops are grown once a year, depending on the **kiremt** rains — the book says plainly that the methods are still backward, and yields feel it.

## Sub lesson 9 — Traditional farming and modern farming

Two farmings stand side by side. **Traditional farming**, for home consumption, has three printed features: cereal growing in weyna dega and the highlands; **shifting cultivation** — moving fields as the soil tires; and **mixed farming** — crops and cattle together. **Modern farming** grows industrial inputs — sugar cane, sesame, soya, cotton, coffee, tea, tobacco — where rain or irrigation allows, and produces Ethiopia's export earners: **coffee, oil seeds, flowers, tea leaf and spices**. The class mind-map completes the ways to raise crop productivity, with improved seed as the given example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Animal husbandry — first in Africa

Ethiopia's animals carry world rankings: **1st in Africa and 10th in the world for cattle**. Livestock contributes **30% of GDP** and ranks **2nd among foreign-exchange earners**. Pastoralists move with their herds where water is scarce. The two methods: **traditional** — kept with crop farming or alone, with too little grazing land capping its yield — and **modern** — better feed, health care and improved breeds giving better results, though still not widespread. The book's ways to improve agriculture overall: control erosion, use profitable technology, strengthen land management, use improved extension services.
