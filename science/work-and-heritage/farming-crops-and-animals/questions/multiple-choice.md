# Work and Livelihood — Farming: Crops and Animals — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Agriculture is lived by about what share of the people?**
a) 8%  b) 82%  c) 18%
**Answer: b) 82%** — 82% of the people live by agriculture.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) Which list is all crops?**
a) Teff, barley, peas  b) Cattle, goats, chickens  c) Pots, cloth, sickles
**Answer: a) Teff, barley, peas** — Teff, barley and peas are crops.

**3. (Sub lesson 3) Equines in the book means —**
a) cattle  b) horses, donkeys and mules  c) goats
**Answer: b) horses, donkeys and mules** — Equines: horses, donkeys, mules.

**4. (Sub lesson 1) A farmer who grows wheat and keeps sheep is doing —**
a) only trade  b) agriculture — both halves  c) industry
**Answer: b) agriculture — both halves** — Crops and animals together: agriculture.

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Chickpeas and beans are —**
a) pulses among the crops  b) animals  c) minerals
**Answer: a) pulses among the crops** — They are crop pulses.

**6. (Sub lesson 1) Why is agriculture called the backbone?**
a) It is hard  b) Most people live by it — the country stands on it  c) It is old
**Answer: b) Most people live by it — the country stands on it** — 82% live by it.

**7. (Sub lesson 4) Mixed farming (ቅይጥ ግብርና) means —**
a) only crops  b) crops and animals together  c) only animals
**Answer: b) crops and animals together** — The two halves of farming on one farm.

**8. (Sub lesson 4) About how many Ethiopians live by farming?**
a) about 20%  b) about half  c) about 80%
**Answer: c) about 80%** — Farming is the backbone for most of the country.

**9. (Sub lesson 5) Which family does the chickpea belong to?**
a) cereals  b) pulses  c) oilseeds
**Answer: b) pulses** — Faba bean, lentil and chickpea are pulses.

**10. (Sub lesson 5) The three ways crops get their water are —**
a) meher, belg and irrigation  b) rivers only  c) dew only
**Answer: a) meher, belg and irrigation** — Main season, belg rains, and irrigation.

**11. (Sub lesson 6) A dairy cow gives —**
a) honey  b) milk  c) wool only
**Answer: b) milk** — Dairy cattle are kept for milk.

**12. (Sub lesson 6) Which animal is kept in a hive?**
a) the goat  b) the bee  c) the donkey
**Answer: b) the bee** — Bees are kept for honey.

**13. (Sub lesson 7) Agriculture means —**
a) only crop growing  b) crop growing and animal keeping together  c) only trade
**Answer: b) crop growing and animal keeping together** — The book joins the two: crops and animals are one agriculture.

**14. (Sub lesson 7) What share of Ethiopians make their living from agriculture?**
a) about half  b) over 82%  c) under 10%
**Answer: b) over 82%** — Over 82% — it is the country's greatest economic base.

**15. (Sub lesson 8) Which set is a highland crop set?**
a) sesame and castor  b) teff, barley, wheat, maize  c) only coffee
**Answer: b) teff, barley, wheat, maize** — Those four cereals are the highland growers' set.

**16. (Sub lesson 8) Most Ethiopian crops depend on which rains?**
a) the belg only  b) the kiremt  c) imported water
**Answer: b) the kiremt** — Crops are grown mainly once a year, depending on the kiremt rains.

**17. (Sub lesson 9) Shifting cultivation means —**
a) moving fields as the soil tires  b) shifting crops to market  c) changing seed every week
**Answer: a) moving fields as the soil tires** — The farmer moves to a new field when the old soil tires — a traditional feature.

**18. (Sub lesson 9) Which is an export earner of modern farming?**
a) coffee  b) only home teff  c) firewood
**Answer: a) coffee** — Coffee heads the export list with oil seeds, flowers, tea leaf and spices.

**19. (Sub lesson 10) Ethiopia's rank in Africa for cattle is —**
a) 10th  b) 1st  c) 5th
**Answer: b) 1st** — First in Africa — and 10th in the whole world.

**20. (Sub lesson 10) Modern animal husbandry improves results by —**
a) moving more often  b) better feed, health care and improved breeds  c) keeping more animals on less land
**Answer: b) better feed, health care and improved breeds** — Feed, health care and breeds are the modern method's three tools.
