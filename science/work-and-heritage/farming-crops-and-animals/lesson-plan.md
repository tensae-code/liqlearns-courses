# Lesson plan — Work and Livelihood — Farming: Crops and Animals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** Work and Livelihood — Farming: Crops and Animals
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** agriculture, the country's backbone — what 'economy' means, and farming's two halves: crop production and animal rearing, with the crops and animals named.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the work lessons begin — farming first, because most people's living comes from it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Economy and the backbone** — economy means the daily activities by which people meet their basic needs.
2. **Crop production** — one half of farming grows crops: potato, wheat, maize, teff, barley, sorghum, finger millet, niger seed, linseed, peas, beans, chickpeas, and fruits and vegetables besides.
3. **Animal rearing** — the other half keeps animals: horned cattle, equines (horses, donkeys, mules), sheep, goats and chickens — classified and pictured in the book, each giving food, work or wealth to the farming family.

4. **Agriculture — the backbone, and mixed farming** — agriculture as food source, industry supplier and foreign-exchange earner; mixed farming (ቅይጥ ግብርና) across Gojjam, Gondar, Wollo and Shewa (Grade 4 student p.113).
5. **Kinds of crops and the three growing seasons** — the five crop families, the meher/belg/irrigation seasons, traditional against modern farming, and the crop-sample board project (Grade 4 student pp.113–114).
6. **Animals we keep and what they do for us** — the kept animals and their services — food, transport, farm power (Grade 4 student p.115).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
- The learner cannot do the Do It Yourself → do the first item together, then let them finish the rest alone.
