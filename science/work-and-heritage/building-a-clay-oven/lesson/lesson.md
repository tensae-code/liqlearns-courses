# Building a clay oven

## Do it first
Before metal stoves, bread was baked in ovens built from the earth itself. If all you had was clay, water and your hands, how would you build an oven that holds fire? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 4 book teaches making ovens from clay as a hands-on lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Clay — the oven's material

Mauritania's Grade 4 *Sciences Naturelles* book teaches **making ovens from clay, the Mauritanian way** (printed p. 68). The material is the ground's own: clay — earth that can be shaped when wet and that hardens, holding its shape against the fire it will contain.

That is why clay is the oven's material wherever this craft is kept: it is underfoot, it shapes by hand, and fired or dried hard it stands heat that would crack lesser building.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the oven by hand

The lesson is a making lesson: the clay is prepared, shaped into the oven's body — walls rising around the fire space, an opening for the fire and the bread — and left to harden into the finished oven.

Built by the learners' own hands, the oven teaches the whole craft chain at once: material, shaping, structure and use. It is Mauritania's heritage carried as a school lesson — the traditional oven not described from outside, but made.

## The key ideas

- The traditional oven is made from **clay** — shaped wet, hardened to stand the fire.
- Mauritania's Grade 4 book teaches **making ovens from clay, the Mauritanian way** (printed p. 68) as a hands-on making lesson.
- The making carries the craft chain: **material, shaping, structure, use**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why is clay the right material for this oven?" Answer from the lesson: Because it is underfoot, it can be shaped by hand when wet, and hardened it stands the fire's heat — the ground's own material doing the oven's whole job.
