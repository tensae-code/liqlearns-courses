# Lesson plan — East Africa's Farming

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** East Africa's Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** agriculture at region scale: its biggest-sector place and its puzzle, its four activities, and what each country farms.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the farming lessons for Ethiopia — the same backbone, now weighed across the whole region.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The biggest sector — and its puzzle** — agriculture holds the largest place in East Africa's economy — mixed small-scale farming of livestock, food crops, cash crops and fish — yet averages only 43% of GDP: about 50% in Burundi, Uganda, Ethiopia and Tanzania, up to 46% in Rwanda, under 30% in Eritrea, Kenya and Madagascar; the sector is backward and rain-dependent (Grade 6).

2. **The four farm activities** — Table 5.3: food crops (maize, rice, potato, banana, coffee, vegetables, wheat, sorghum, teff); cash crops (tea leaf, coffee, cotton, oilseeds, coconut, tobacco); livestock husbandry (horned cattle, sheep, goats, poultry, hides and skins work); fishery (rivers, lakes, dams, and sea fishing in the Indian Ocean) (Grade 6).

3. **What each country farms** — Rwanda (banana, sweet potato, cassava, potato, pulses at home; tea and coffee for trade); Kenya (tobacco, coffee, tea, hides and skins; large livestock work); Burundi (coffee and tea; cotton, maize, sweet potato); Ethiopia (foreign trade almost wholly farm products, coffee first — Africa's greatest coffee producer — oilseeds and pulses; first in Africa for livestock); Eritrea (barley, sorghum, wheat, horned cattle) (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
- The learner cannot do the Do It Yourself → do the first item together, then let them finish the rest alone.
