# Rabbit keeping

## Do it first
Before you read: a family near you keeps rabbits. What do they keep them for? Where do the rabbits sleep? And what can go wrong for a kept rabbit? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Uganda's way in — its Primary Four home-study booklet teaches rabbit keeping whole: the animals, their house, and their care.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rabbits — breeds and uses

Rabbit keeping starts with the animals themselves: there are different **breeds** of rabbits, and rabbits are kept for their **uses** — a keeper chooses and keeps rabbits knowing both which breed they are and what they are kept for.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building a hutch

Rabbits are kept in a **hutch** — the keeper **builds** the rabbits' house. Building the hutch is part of the science lesson itself: the home must be made, by the keeper, before the keeping can go well.

## Sub lesson 3 — Care, parasites and diseases of rabbits

Keeping rabbits is daily **care** — and watching for what harms them: **parasites** that live on or in the rabbits, and **diseases** of rabbits. The keeper who knows the parasites and diseases can guard the animals before harm is done.

## The key ideas

- Rabbits come in different **breeds** and are kept for their **uses** — the keeper knows both.
- Rabbits are housed in a **hutch** the keeper builds.
- Keeping rabbits means daily **care**, and guarding against **parasites and diseases**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — breeds and uses first, then the hutch, then care, parasites and diseases. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What does a rabbit keeper actually do?" Answer with the keeping, not by repeating the title.
