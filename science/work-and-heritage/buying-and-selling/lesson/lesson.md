# Work and Livelihood — Buying and Selling

## Do it first

The farmer has grain but no shoes. The town has shoes but no grain. How do both end up with what they need? You already know the answer's name.

## Sub lesson 1 — What trade is

Trade is buying and selling between people. It is necessary because no one makes everything they need: each person has some things in plenty and lacks others, and trade moves goods from those who have them to those who need them.

[Picture 12031 — a farmer selling grain and buying oil and clothes at market]

## Sub lesson 2 — The two-way road of trade

The road runs both ways: farmers sell farm products at market and buy industrial goods — oil, cloth, tools; townspeople and traders supply industrial goods to farmers and to each other. Grain flows to town; goods flow back to the farm.

## Sub lesson 3 — Markets — how countryside and town feed each other

Grade 4 follows the goods. The farmer sells the surplus left after family use — mashilla, teff, barley, maize, wheat — at the local market or to traders at the farm, and buys farm inputs and factory goods back. The town dweller buys farm produce. The region's trade splits into markets: the crop market, the animal-products market, vegetables and fruit, and industrial products. Neither side feeds itself alone; trade is the road their food walks.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bringing natural resources to market — legal and illegal trade (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guides carry trade one step further (teacher guide — evidence): the region's natural resources and their products are brought to market and traded — and that trade has a legal form and an illegal form. Legal trade follows the rules and keeps the resource alive for next year; illegal trade in natural resources takes without right and eats the future. Trade fairly, trade legally.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: trade — buying and selling between people: farmers sell farm products and buy industrial goods, while townspeople and traders supply those goods to everyone. Then answer one question they ask you about it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Keep a one-day trade diary: everything your family bought or sold today.
2. Draw trade's two-way road between a farm and a town, with goods labelled both ways.
3. Role-play a market bargain kindly: seller names a price, buyer answers, both end satisfied.
