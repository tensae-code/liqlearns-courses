# Lesson plan — Work and Livelihood — Buying and Selling

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** Work and Livelihood — Buying and Selling
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** trade — buying and selling between people: farmers sell farm products and buy industrial goods, while townspeople and traders supply those goods to everyone.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the industry lesson — trade is how industry's goods and the farm's goods change hands.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What trade is** — trade is buying and selling between people.
2. **The two-way road of trade** — the road runs both ways: farmers sell farm products at market and buy industrial goods — oil, cloth, tools; townspeople and traders supply industrial goods to farmers and to each other.

3. **Markets — how countryside and town feed each other** — the farmer's surplus, the four market kinds, and the countryside↔town exchange (Grade 4 student pp.121–124).
4. **Bringing natural resources to market — legal and illegal trade (teacher guide — evidence)** — marketing the region's natural resources and their products; legal and illegal natural-resource trade (SNNP TG pp.100–102; Oromia TG pp.65–69, teacher guide — evidence).

5. **Trade — raw goods out, factory goods in** — the region's foreign-trade pattern: farm raw materials and minerals exported, industrial goods imported; single-product dependence (Burundi 86% coffee, Zambia 94% copper); Africa supplies 20% of the world's coffee; flower production is the newer sector (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
