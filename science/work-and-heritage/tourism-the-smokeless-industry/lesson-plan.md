# Lesson plan — Tourism — the Smokeless Industry

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** Tourism — the Smokeless Industry
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** tourism as an industry: what visitors come for in Ethiopia and across East Africa, what tourism earns, and what it builds.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the heritage lessons — heritage is what tourism shows the world; this lesson counts what that showing is worth.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Tourism in Ethiopia** — Ethiopia is rich in tourist assets — plants and animals, historic places, landforms, cultures — yet the sector is undeveloped; attractions in three groups: historic, landform, national parks and sanctuaries (Grade 5).

2. **The smokeless industry** — tourism counted for East Africa: a fast-growing 'smokeless industry' bringing about 17% of the region's foreign exchange and roughly 10% of total yearly income, about 7% of jobs in Tanzania, Uganda and Kenya; it loosens dependence on agriculture, spreads infrastructure and builds friendship (Grade 6).

3. **A tour of the attractions** — country by country: Kenya (Maasai Mara, Maasai culture, Mount Kenya, Amboseli, Lake Nakuru, Mombasa); Burundi (Bujumbura, Lake Tanganyika, Kasumo); Tanzania (Kilimanjaro, Serengeti, Zanzibar Stone Town); Uganda (Rwenzori, great apes, Jinja, Bwindi); Rwanda (Akagera, Nyanza palace museum, Lake Kivu); Mauritius and Seychelles; Ethiopia (man-made heritage, endemic animals, Lucy/Dinknesh) (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- The words are new → go back to the lesson's picture and say what it shows, in home words first, then in the lesson's words.
- The parts blur together → take one part at a time and answer that part's questions before moving on; the gate is per part where sub lessons exist.
