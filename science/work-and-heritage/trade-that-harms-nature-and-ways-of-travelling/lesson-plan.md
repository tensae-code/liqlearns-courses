# Lesson plan — Work and Livelihood — Trade That Harms Nature; Ways of Travelling

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** Work and Livelihood — Trade That Harms Nature; Ways of Travelling
**Place in the curriculum:** the Science course's Ethiopia Environmental Science stock, primary levels (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the trade in firewood, charcoal, timber, sand and stone — and what it costs nature — plus transport: pack animals of old and the modern vehicles that move people and products.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the trade lesson — its shadow side, and the movement that carries all trade.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **When trade strips nature** — selling firewood and charcoal, timber, sand, stone and gravel earns money — and strips forests, disturbs the climate, dries rivers, drives wild animals away and erodes the soil.
2. **Transport — the camel and the truck** — transport moves people and products — without it, no trade.

3. **Transport — the blood vessel of the country** — transport as 'the blood vessel of a country'; traditional kinds and modern land/air/water kinds, compared by speed and load (Grade 4 student pp.125–126; SNNP TG pp.102–104 and Oromia TG pp.69–72, teacher guide — evidence).

4. **Transport — land, rail and air** — the region's transport falls into land, rail and air; Ethiopian Airlines stands among the world's foremost; flow runs centre-to-port only, and roads joining country to country are lacking (Grade 6); trade's bottleneck is transport — congested roads around Lake Victoria (Grade 6).

5. **The Northern Corridor** — 1,700 km from Mombasa through Nairobi and Kampala to Rwanda and Burundi; it carries Uganda's and Rwanda's great cargoes and serves freight to Ethiopia and South Sudan; congestion in Mombasa and Nairobi makes it hard (Grade 6).

6. **The Central Corridor and the roads to come** — 1,300 km from Dar es Salaam to Burundi, Rwanda and Uganda — wide and comfortable, few problems; Rwanda's and Burundi's tea and coffee, and western Tanzania's cotton, leave by it; planned lines: Addis Ababa–Nairobi, Addis Ababa–Juba, Lamu into Ethiopia and South Sudan (Grade 6).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
