# Work and Livelihood — Trade That Harms Nature; Ways of Travelling — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Name three things whose trade harms nature, and one harm.**
**Answer:** Firewood/charcoal, timber, sand, stone, gravel (any three); harm: stripped forests, disturbed climate, dried rivers, animals driven away, eroded soil (any one).

**2. (Sub lesson 2) Name two traditional and two modern ways of travelling.**
**Answer:** Traditional: mule, horse, donkey, camel. Modern: bajaj, train, car, aeroplane.

**3. (Sub lesson 3) Why is transport called the connecting bridge between producers and users?**
**Answer:** Because it moves farm products, factory goods and people from those who make them to those who use them, speeding all development.

**4. (Sub lesson 4) Why does transport decide whether trade grows?**
**Answer:** Goods must move: where roads are few or congested, heavy freight crawls, costs rise and trade between countries is held back (any answer tying movement to trade).

**5. (Sub lesson 5) Trace the Northern Corridor town by town from the sea.**
**Answer:** Mombasa → Nairobi → Kampala → on to Rwanda and Burundi.

**6. (Sub lesson 6) Compare the two corridors in two sentences.**
**Answer:** The Northern Corridor runs 1,700 km from Mombasa through Nairobi and Kampala to Rwanda and Burundi, fighting city congestion. The Central Corridor runs 1,300 km from Dar es Salaam to Burundi, Rwanda and Uganda on a wide, comfortable road.
