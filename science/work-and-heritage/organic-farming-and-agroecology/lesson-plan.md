# Lesson plan — Organic farming and agroecology

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Work and Heritage · **Lesson:** Organic farming and agroecology
**Place in the curriculum:** Work and Heritage — appended to Unit 13 (Work and Heritage) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** organic crops defined: food produced by natural methods without chemical substances, genetic manipulation or the agrochemicals that degenerate soils, letting the soil regenerate (guide evidence)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Organic crops defined** — organic crops defined: food produced by natural methods without chemical substances, genetic manipulation or the agrochemicals that degenerate soils, letting the soil regenerate (guide evidence)
2. **Traditional agriculture (indigenous practices developed over generations, aimed at** — traditional agriculture (indigenous practices developed over generations, aimed at conserving the environment and using resources sustainably) and agroecology (ecological principles applied to food production, weighing productivity, stability, sustainability and equity) (guide evidence)
