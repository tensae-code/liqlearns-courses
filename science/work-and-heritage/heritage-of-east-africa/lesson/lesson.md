# Heritage of East Africa

## Do it first

Hold something old from your home — or remember something old your grandparents showed you. Who made it? Who will hold it after you? Try to answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Heritage — the bridge between generations

**Heritage (ቅርስ)** is the material and spiritual things past generations made, used and handed down. The book's image is a **bridge**: heritage joins yesterday's generation to today's. It is a **people's identity mark**, and the surest historical evidence of how a people lived, grew and built its civilisation. Heritage has two faces: a **world face** — it lets peoples know each other and exchange knowledge — and a **national face** — it is how one people is told from another.

## Sub lesson 2 — Spiritual heritage — what cannot be touched

The first of heritage's two great classes is **spiritual (intangible) heritage** — what cannot be touched. It holds the **songs of grief and joy (ዝማሬ, ሽለላ, ቀረርቶ, እንጉርጉሮ)**, the **many dances**, the **yearly festivals**, **tales, riddles and proverbial sayings** — a whole wealth that a people carries in **memory and performance**, and nowhere else. If the singing stops, this heritage does not sit in a store waiting; it must be performed to live.

## Sub lesson 3 — Movable heritage — what can be carried

Tangible heritage splits in two, and the first half can move. **Movable heritage** can be carried from place to place: **metalwork of gold, silver, copper and bronze** — coins, jewellery and ancient crosses; objects of **stone, clay and glass** with their inscriptions and pictures, and small tools found by excavation; things of **wood, fibre, leather, bone and horn**, and **garments of rank and honour**; **ancient writings** on stone and parchment, **archives and seals**; and the **fossil remains** of ancient animals and plants.

## Sub lesson 4 — Immovable heritage — and natural heritage

**Immovable heritage** stays where it was made: ancient **dwelling caves** with their wall paintings and carvings; **stelae and standing stones** and **houses of worship**; **palaces, ancient buildings, ruined old cities, castles, ancient bridges, battlefields**, and the **tombs of kings and great persons**. And the book adds one more class to the tangible side: **natural heritage** — a country's **rivers, waterfalls, valleys, parks, mountains and great trees**, made by no hands yet handed down all the same.

## Sub lesson 5 — Heritage cards — famous sites of the region

The chapter's heritage cards tour the region. **Kasubi Tombs**, Kampala — built 1882, burial place of four Buganda kings. **Fasil Ghebbi**, Gondar — 17th century, a 70,000 m² compound ringed by a 900 m wall. **Timket** — Ethiopia's festival, UNESCO-listed intangible heritage. **Songo Mnara**, Tanzania — a 15th–16th century Indian Ocean trading post: five mosques, a palace, 33 houses. **Lalibela** — eleven churches cut from single rock, 800+ years ago. **Kilimanjaro** — 5,895 m, which has lost 82% of its ice in a hundred years. **Mombasa's annual festival** each November. **Fort Jesus** — built by the Portuguese in 1593–96. The **Dire Sheikh Hussein mosque** in Bale — a pilgrimage centre 800+ years old. And the **Serengeti** — 14,760 km² of migrating herds. The class task sorts every card: spiritual, movable, or immovable?

## Sub lesson 6 — Protecting heritage — teach, guard, register, repair

Heritage is in danger — from carelessness, growing cities and industries, technological change, and **war** among the worst destroyers. The region's chief threats are listed: **climate change, great infrastructure works (dams and bridges), heritage looting and theft, civil war and instability**. Against them stand **four protections**: **education** — teach the public through every medium that heritage is the people's wealth; **guarding** — protect sites so thieves cannot strip them; **registration (ምዝገባ)** — record each heritage in time, its kind, count, place and condition, so illegal export can be stopped and researchers can find it; and **repair (ጥገና)** — carefully mend what nature, mishandling or age has damaged, keeping it in its own condition.
