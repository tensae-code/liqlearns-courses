# Energy resources — resource depletion and its drivers

## Do it first
Picture a woodlot at the edge of a village. Every day, families take wood. Every year, some trees grow back — but fewer than were taken. Walk the story forward five years, ten years. What does the last chapter look like? This lesson names what is happening to that woodlot. (This whole lesson comes from teachers' material — it is taught here as teacher evidence, and it is labelled that way everywhere it appears.)

## Depletion
**Resource depletion is using a resource faster than it can be replenished, until it is exhausted.** The rate is the whole definition: use slower than return, and the resource endures; use faster, and the store drains toward empty — however large it once was.

## The seven drivers
Teachers name **seven drivers** of depletion: **overpopulation** — over seven billion people and rising, all drawing on the same stores; **consumption habits** — how much each person takes; **industrialisation** — factories drawing resources at machine speed; **climate change** — shifting rain and heat cutting what returns; **pollution** — spoiling stores so they cannot be used; **mismanagement and poor practice** — waste in the taking itself; and **deforestation** — the woodlot's own story, cutting the return off at its root.

## The taking itself pollutes
Depletion is not the only damage: **extraction and processing themselves degrade the environment** — air, land and water pollution and noise, and piles of solid waste — even before the resource runs out. A mine can spoil a valley it has not yet emptied.

## Say it to your companion
Define depletion with its rate condition, name the seven drivers with one local example, and explain the extraction point.

## Do It Yourself
1. A well is drawn down every dry season and refills every rainy season — but each year it refills a little less. Is it depleting? Say why, with the rate condition.
2. Pick two of the seven drivers and trace each one's mechanism on a local resource, step by step.
3. A quarry leaves noise, dust and waste heaps but its stone will last a century. Which two harms from this lesson does it show — and which one does it not yet show?
