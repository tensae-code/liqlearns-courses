# Hands-on — The Gaia hypothesis — the living Earth

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Build a tiny world in a clear sealed jar or bottle: a layer of small stones, soil, a little water and one or two small fast-growing plants (beans or birdseed sprout quickly). Seal it and set it where it gets light but not direct hot sun. Watch it over two weeks: where does the water go? What grows, what fades? Then, for the last three days, warm your little world — set it somewhere warmer or cover it — and watch what the change does to the living and non-living parts together. Write down what kept itself in balance, and what could not.

## Teach it on paper
Draw Daisyworld from memory — a planet with black daisies and white daisies under a brightening sun — label which daisies warm the planet and which cool it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
