# Lesson plan — How living things affect each other

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** How living things affect each other
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ecosystem and Conservation of Natural Resources, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** living things that meet affect each other in six named ways — predation, competition, parasitism, mutualism, commensalism and amensalism — and each way is told by who gains (+), who loses (−) and who is unaffected (0).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Ecosystems — populations, communities and their surroundings (this title, Grade 8): populations are known; now what happens when they meet. The Biology title carries secondary-depth ecology further on; this is the Grade 8 treatment in Our Environment.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Meetings where one side loses** — predation, competition and parasitism — the three interactions with a loser.
2. **Meetings with no loser — and one-sided meetings** — mutualism, commensalism and amensalism.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Ecosystems — populations, communities and their surroundings (Our Environment) — recommended, never forced.
## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six interactions and give the +/−/0 marks for each partner.
2. Classify a local pair of living things into the right interaction.
3. Say why the marks, not the animals' sizes, decide the name.
## What you need
Local pairs to think about: bees and flowers, lice and people, egrets and cattle or elephants, two crops in one field.
