# Lesson plan — The air quality index

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** The air quality index
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — appended to Unit 11 (Our Environment) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how the quality of the air itself is measured and reported as an index people can act on — Iran's way, from its Grade 11 Human and Environment book: the air quality index, set inside the book's air lesson beside the pollution of big cities, and the same lesson's note on a pollution no one can see or smell — the electromagnetic kind
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Reading the air's grade** — Iran's Grade 11 book puts the air quality index at the centre of its air lesson: human breathing and life are affected by air quality, so quality is graded and published as an index — a single scale that gathers the air's pollutants into a grade everyone can read, the way a thermometer gathers warmth into a number. A high index is a warning: the air itself has become a health matter for the whole city.
2. **Pollution you cannot see** — The same lesson reaches a pollution with no smoke and no smell: devices create stronger electromagnetic fields around themselves, and the book names the result electromagnetic pollution. The lesson of the index widens: what harms the air is not only what can be seen — some of it must be measured to be known at all.
