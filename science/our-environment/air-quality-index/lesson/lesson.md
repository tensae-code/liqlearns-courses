# The air quality index

## Do it first
Before you read: on some mornings the air looks clear and on others it hangs grey over the streets — but looks can lie about what is in it. How would a whole city know, on the same morning, whether its air is safe to breathe hard in sport? Then read on: this is Iran's way in — an index that grades the air itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading the air's grade

Iran's Grade 11 book puts the air quality index at the centre of its air lesson: human breathing and life are affected by air quality, so quality is graded and published as an index — a single scale that gathers the air's pollutants into a grade everyone can read, the way a thermometer gathers warmth into a number. A high index is a warning: the air itself has become a health matter for the whole city.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pollution you cannot see

The same lesson reaches a pollution with no smoke and no smell: devices create stronger electromagnetic fields around themselves, and the book names the result electromagnetic pollution. The lesson of the index widens: what harms the air is not only what can be seen — some of it must be measured to be known at all.

## The key ideas

- The air quality index grades the air's quality on one published scale, so a whole city can read it and act.
- Breathing and life are affected by air quality — a high index is a health warning.
- Not all pollution can be seen or smelled: strong electromagnetic fields around devices are named in the same lesson as a pollution of their own kind.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why grade air with an index instead of just looking at it?" Answer: because looks cannot measure what is in the air — the index gathers the pollutants into one published grade that everyone can read the same way.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find today's air quality index for your town (radio, phone or newspaper) and write what its grade tells people to do or avoid.
2. List three things in the air that eyes cannot judge, and say how each is known instead.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
