# Mangroves — protecting coast and sea

## Do it first
Before you read: picture the edge of an island, where the land runs into the sea. The ground is mud, the water is salty, and the tide comes in and goes out. What kind of tree could live standing in that? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Palau's way in — its Ridge to Reef book for Grade 5 teaches the mangrove tree part by part, and then the people and animals whose lives hang on it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The mangrove tree and its parts

A **mangrove** is a tree that lives where the land meets the sea. Its ground is not dry soil: it stands in salt water and soft mud at the coast, in the strip the tide covers and uncovers.

The tree's parts fit that home. Its **roots** spread through the mud and hold the tree standing in ground that would let an ordinary tree fall. The **trunk** rises out of the water and the mud. Above it, the **branches and leaves** stand in the air, where the tree can catch the sun and make its food like any green plant — while its feet stay in the sea.

That is the mangrove's trick, and the lesson keeps it whole: one tree, joined together — roots in salty mud below, green crown in the air above.

## Sub lesson 2 — Food, markets and the animals of the mangroves

The mangrove is not scenery. In Palau, people's food and market life depends on it: the mangrove shelters and feeds the sea creatures around it, and what lives in the mangroves is caught, eaten and sold. A coast with its mangroves standing is a coast that keeps feeding its people.

Named animals make the mangrove their home ground. The **saltwater crocodile** hunts in its waters. The **mangrove crab** lives and feeds among the roots and mud. The **night heron** comes to the mangroves to feed. Residents and visitors together — the mangrove is a living place, not an empty shore.

So the mangrove protects twice over: the tree itself shields the coast where it stands, and the life inside it feeds the village and the market. That is why the lesson's title calls mangroves protecting — coast and sea both.

## The key ideas

- A **mangrove** is a tree that stands in salt water and mud where the land meets the sea; its parts — roots, trunk, branches and leaves — fit it for that home.
- Palauans' **food and market** life depends on the mangroves and what lives in them.
- The mangrove is home ground for the **saltwater crocodile**, the **mangrove crab** and the **night heron**.
- Standing mangroves protect the coast and keep the sea life that feeds people.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the tree and its parts first, then the food, market and animals that depend on it. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why can a mangrove live where an ordinary tree cannot?" Answer from the tree's parts, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw a mangrove and label its parts: roots, trunk, branches, leaves — and draw the water line and the mud.
2. Write the mangrove's two gifts in two lists: what it gives people (food and market life), and which animals it homes (crocodile, crab, night heron).
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the tree's parts, and one animal that depends on it.
