# Natural disasters — and how people face them

## Do it first
What happens to a town when a disaster strikes — and why is that the whole world's problem, not only that town's? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Sudan's way in — its Grade 12 Geography and Environmental Studies book places disasters in its Man-and-the-environment chapter, as a problem of the whole world.

## Sub lesson 1 — Disasters as a world problem

Sudan's Grade 12 book teaches **disasters (الـكـوارث) as a world problem**: a disaster strikes one place, but it belongs in the chapter of problems the whole world shares — beside pollution, the draining of resources and the care of forests and water. No country faces its disasters as a matter for itself alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — How people face them

Because disasters are a world problem, people face them **together**: the place struck bears the harm first, and the facing of it — the help, the rebuilding and the learning for next time — is shared work, the same shared work the chapter asks for with every world environmental problem.
