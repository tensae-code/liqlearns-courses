Today: **Natural and human-caused phenomena** — Nicaragua's way, from its book (Nicaragua, Grade 3 — *Ciencias Naturales, 3er grado*, printed pp. 122–126).

Nicaragua's Grade 3 book teaches changes in the environment from **natural phenomena — drought, volcanic eruptions, earthquakes** ( Managua after a quake is its own example). The Grade 8 book lists the **natural agents** that transform our environment: **atmospheric, biological, hydrological, volcanic, seismic and tectonic** — set against human action, with the flooded street, the factory, the dump and Managua's 1972 earthquake as its pictures.

The Grade 7 book names the mixed kind: **socio-natural events, expressed through nature but driven by natural and human factors together**, and **vulnerability** — a condition that predisposes a place or a people to disaster. Its Grade 7 Módulo gives Law 337's basic definitions in the module's own text: **secondary threat, disaster, mitigation, and the disaster levels** — the words Nicaragua's own disaster-prevention law works with.
