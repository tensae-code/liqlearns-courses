# Lesson plan — Natural and human-caused phenomena

**Course:** Science · **Title:** our-environment · **Lesson:** Natural and human-caused phenomena
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — appended to Unit 11 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how the environment changes — through natural phenomena (drought, volcanic eruptions, earthquakes) and through human (anthropic) action — and the mixed, socio-natural phenomena where the two drive each other, with vulnerability named
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Natural phenomena that change the environment** — Nicaragua's Grade 3 book teaches changes in the environment from **natural phenomena — drought, volcanic eruptions, earthquakes** ( Managua after a quake is its own example). The Grade 8 book lists the **natural agents** that transform our environment: **atmospheric, biological, hydrological, volcanic, seismic and tectonic** — set against human action, with the flooded street, the factory, the dump and Managua's 1972 earthquake as its pictures.
2. **Human action, socio-natural phenomena and vulnerability** — The Grade 7 book names the mixed kind: **socio-natural events, expressed through nature but driven by natural and human factors together**, and **vulnerability** — a condition that predisposes a place or a people to disaster. Its Grade 7 Módulo gives Law 337's basic definitions in the module's own text: **secondary threat, disaster, mitigation, and the disaster levels** — the words Nicaragua's own disaster-prevention law works with.

## Before this lesson
- No required first lesson. Recommended, never forced: the neighbouring our-environment lessons that touch the same ground.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Natural phenomena that change the environment.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Human action, socio-natural phenomena and vulnerability.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: household materials only where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks every disaster is purely natural → return to sub lesson 2: socio-natural events are driven by natural and human factors together, and vulnerability decides how hard they strike.
- Mixes a phenomenon and a disaster up → return to sub lesson 2 and Law 337's definitions: the phenomenon is the event; the disaster is what it becomes for the people in its path.
