# Resources people make — and looking after them

## Do it first
The dam holds back the river for everyone. The school teaches everyone's children. The hospital heals anyone carried in. These are the resources people make — and because they belong to all, they are kept by all. This lesson names them and their keeping.

## Shared things, kept properly

Beside nature's resources stand the ones **people create for use**: the **dam, mosque, church, school, hospital**. The Grade 3 book files them as **human-made resources** — and its teaching about them is one sentence long and civic to the bone: **as shared property, they must be used and kept properly**.

Used properly: the school for learning, the hospital for healing, the house of worship for worship, the dam for the water and power it was built to give. Kept properly: repaired, cleaned, unbroken, un-stolen-from — because damage to a shared thing is damage to everyone, including the one who did it. The child who carves the desk sits at a carved desk.

## Say it to your companion
List your companion the five, then audit your school against the keeping rule together: what is kept properly here, what is not — and one repair each of you can truly do.

## Do It Yourself
1. Shared-property audit: walk the school (or another shared place) and list what is kept well and what is neglected. Propose fixes in writing to the keeper.
2. One act of keeping: clean, repair or guard one shared thing this week — a desk, a tap, a library book — and log it.
3. Dam story: find out (ask!) what a dam near your region does — water held, power made, fields watered — and tell its story as a human-made resource's biography.
