# Lesson plan — Green technology for a sustainable environment

**Course:** Science · **Title:** our-environment · **Lesson:** Green technology for a sustainable environment
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — appended to Unit 11 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** green technology — the technology sectors (energy, waste and wastewater, agriculture and forestry, transportation, industry) organised to keep the environment sustainable, and its place in daily life
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The sectors of green technology** — Malaysia's Form 4 book teaches **Green Technology** through its **sectors — energy, waste and wastewater, agriculture and forestry, transportation, and industry** — and what each sector focuses on. In the **energy sector** it sets the socio-scientific issues openly: rising population needs pressing on the environment and natural resources.
2. **Green technology in daily life** — The chapter closes by bringing **green technology into daily life** — the sectors' work landing in the choices and systems of an ordinary day — with the chapter's summative practice gathering the whole treatment.

## Before this lesson
- No required first lesson. Recommended, never forced: the neighbouring lessons that touch the same ground.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: The sectors of green technology.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Green technology in daily life.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: household materials only where the hands-on says so.

1. **Do it first** — the learner answers the opening questions out loud before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).

3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson in their own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson told in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer and fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks green technology is only solar panels → return to sub lesson 1: its sectors are energy, waste and wastewater, agriculture and forestry, transportation and industry.
- Thinks it belongs only to engineers → return to sub lesson 2: the book closes with green technology in daily life.
