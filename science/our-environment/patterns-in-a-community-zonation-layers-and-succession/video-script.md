Today: **Patterns in a community — zonation, layers and succession** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**Zonation — bands across a shore and up a mountain.** Zonation is the banding of organisms across a place. Around the Aleipata blowholes the bands run: seaweeds, sponges and shellfish nearest the sea; then fewer shellfish; then periwinkles and crabs highest. The same pattern climbs a volcanic island: plant zonation rises from coastal marsh to cloud forest. Each band lives where its conditions — wetting, drying, light, cold — suit it (printed pp. 99–105).

**Stratification — the layers of a forest.** A forest is also layered from top to bottom — stratification. The layers: canopy, sub-canopy, tree-fern, shrub, ground and leaf-litter, and each layer has its own light, wind and shelter, so each holds different living things. To name a forest properly you must say not only what lives there, but at which height (printed pp. 99–105).

**Succession — the lava field grows a community.** Succession is the pattern in time: on the Saleaula lava fields, the first plants settle in the cracks, break the rock up and make the first soil; each stage changes the conditions, until the first plants themselves die out and later kinds take their place. Landslides and old fire sites are the other places to study it. All three patterns are investigated the book's way: rope transects and profile diagrams, with temperature and light measured along the line (printed pp. 99–105).
