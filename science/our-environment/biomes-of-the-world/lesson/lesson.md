# Biomes of the world

## Do it first
Before you read: picture two far-apart regions of the Earth. What covers the ground in each — and how do the covers differ? If you had to sort the world's regions into kinds using only what grows on them, how would you start? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Guatemala's way in — its Grade 9 guide teaches the biome inside Learning Experience 3, "Our environment, a harmonious space".

## Sub lesson 1 — What a biome is

A **biome** is the kind of region known by **the type of vegetation** that covers it.

The guide's definition is short and exact: the biome is **the type of vegetation of a region**. Not the animals first, not the weather first — the plant cover is what names the biome. Where the vegetation type is the same, the biome is the same, even across an ocean; where the vegetation changes, the biome changes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading a region by its vegetation

Because the biome is the vegetation type, you can **read a region by its vegetation**.

Stand anywhere and ask the guide's question: what type of vegetation covers this region? The answer places the region in its biome — and places it among the **biomes of the world**, the world's regions sorted by the plant cover that characterises each. Guatemala's guide teaches this inside an experience called **"Our environment, a harmonious space"**: reading the world's vegetation kinds is part of seeing the environment as one harmonious whole.

## The key ideas

- A **biome** is **the type of vegetation of a region** — Guatemala's guide's own definition.
- Regions with the same vegetation type share a biome, wherever they are; a change of vegetation is a change of biome.
- The biomes of the world are the world's regions sorted by their plant cover.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — what a biome is first, then reading a region by its vegetation. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What names a biome?" Answer with the vegetation, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Describe the vegetation of your own region in three sentences, and say what biome that makes it, by the guide's definition.
2. Name two regions of the world whose vegetation is very different from yours, and say how their biomes differ from yours.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
