# Lesson plan — Waste, flies, germs and trachoma

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Waste, flies, germs and trachoma
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — what badly dumped waste does: the fly's journey, and the latrine that stops it (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** waste dumped anywhere breeds flies, and flies carry germs from waste to people's bodies — one named result is trachoma, an eye disease that untreated leads gradually to blindness; a properly built latrine is the named prevention
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What waste is and its three kinds and What disease is, and how it spreads — the germ road was mapped there; here it lands on one named disease.

- Recommended first: Our Environment — What waste is and its three kinds. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the chain: waste dumped anywhere → flies breed → flies carry germs to people's bodies → disease.
2. Name trachoma: an eye disease carried this way, which untreated leads gradually to blindness.
3. Name the prevention the book gives: human waste kept in a properly built latrine.

## What you need
Eyes open around home: where waste lies open, flies will show the lesson by themselves.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks trachoma comes from dust or age alone → the book's road is waste → flies → germs → eyes; the latrine is named precisely because it cuts that road.
- Thinks blindness comes at once → the book says gradually: untreated, trachoma darkens sight step by step — which is why early prevention matters.
