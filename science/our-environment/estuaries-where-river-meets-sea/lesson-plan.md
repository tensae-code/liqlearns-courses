# Lesson plan — Estuaries — where river meets sea

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Estuaries — where river meets sea
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — appended to Unit 11 (Our Environment) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what an estuary is — the place where a river's fresh water and the sea's salt water mix, where the saltiness changes with the tide and with distance, and where that mixing makes a rich food supply — Micronesia's own way, in its PCEP high-island reader for Grades 6–8
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Fresh water and salt water, mixed** — An estuary is where a river meets the sea: there the river's fresh water and the sea's salt water mix. It is neither river water nor sea water, but a mixture that changes from place to place and hour to hour.
2. **A rich food supply** — That mixing is not a problem for life — it is a gift. The estuary carries a rich food supply, gathered from both the river and the sea.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks estuary water is simply fresh or simply salt → return to sub lesson 1: it is a mixture, and its saltiness changes with tide and distance.
- Thinks the saltiness is fixed all day → return to sub lesson 1: the tide pushes the sea in and lets it fall back, so the saltiness at one spot changes.
