# Estuaries — where river meets sea

## Do it first
Before you read: a river runs down to the sea and they meet. Is the water at the meeting place fresh, or salt — or something else? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Micronesia's way in — its high-island reader teaches the meeting place of its own rivers and sea.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fresh water and salt water, mixed

An **estuary** is where a river meets the sea: there the river's **fresh water and the sea's salt water mix**. It is neither river water nor sea water, but a mixture that changes from place to place and hour to hour.

How salty a spot in the estuary is — its saltiness — **changes with the tide**, as the sea pushes in and falls back, **and with distance**: nearer the sea it is saltier, nearer the river fresher.

## Sub lesson 2 — A rich food supply

That mixing is not a problem for life — it is a gift. The estuary carries a **rich food supply**, gathered from both the river and the sea.

So the estuary is a feeding place: the meeting of the two waters stocks food where many living things come to eat and to grow.

## The key ideas

- An **estuary** is where river meets sea: **fresh and salt water mix** there.
- Its saltiness **changes with the tide and with distance** from the sea.
- The mixing makes a **rich food supply** in the estuary.
