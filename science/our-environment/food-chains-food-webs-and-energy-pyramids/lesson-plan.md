# Lesson plan — Food chains, food webs and energy pyramids

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Food chains, food webs and energy pyramids
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ecosystem and Conservation of Natural Resources, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** living things are linked by eating: producers make food, consumers eat (herbivores plants, carnivores animals, omnivores both) and decomposers break down the dead; chains of eating widen into webs, and energy shrinks up the trophic levels by the 10% rule, drawn as pyramids of numbers, biomass and energy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on How living things affect each other (this title, Grade 8): predation is known; now eating is followed as a chain and measured as energy. The Biology title's Food Chains and Food Webs, Trophic Levels and Ecological Pyramids lessons carry this at secondary depth — cross-referenced in the level line, never copied.

## Sub lessons
1. **Who makes, who eats, who breaks down** — producers, the consumer kinds, and decomposers; chains widen into webs.
2. **Trophic levels and the 10% rule** — energy shrinks by nine-tenths at each level; pyramids of numbers, biomass and energy.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: How living things affect each other (Our Environment) — recommended, never forced.
## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort living things into producers, consumers (herbivore/carnivore/omnivore) and decomposers.
2. Draw a food chain and say how chains join into a web.
3. Use the 10% rule to say how much energy reaches the next trophic level.
## What you need
A field edge, garden or market scene to build a chain from; paper for the pyramid.
