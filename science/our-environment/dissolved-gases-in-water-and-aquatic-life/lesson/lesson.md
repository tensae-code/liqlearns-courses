# Dissolved gases in water and aquatic life

## Do it first
A fish never leaves the water, yet it breathes. Where does the air it breathes come from? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Egypt's Secondary 10 way in — its Integrated Science asks what happens because atmospheric gases dissolve in water (Topic 1-7, printed p. 43).

## Sub lesson 1 — Gases from the air dissolve in water

Water in contact with air takes air's gases into itself: atmospheric gases **dissolve** in water, the way salt dissolves, until the water holds them invisibly. Egypt's Integrated Science builds its water unit step by step — the hydrosphere and where Earth's water is, water's chemistry and solutions, its importance for living things, and organisms' adaptations to the aquatic environment — and then names this step on its own: the gases of the atmosphere are present inside the water, not only above it.

## Sub lesson 2 — What dissolved gases mean for aquatic environments

Because gases dissolve in water, an **aquatic environment** carries its air with it — and what dissolves, and how much, becomes one of the conditions every water-living organism lives under. Topic 1-7 teaches the **effect of atmospheric gases dissolving in water on aquatic environments**: the water's store of dissolved gases is part of the habitat itself, as real a part as its temperature or its light — the topics that stand on either side of this one in Egypt's unit.
