# Lesson plan — Ecosystems — populations, communities and their surroundings

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Ecosystems — populations, communities and their surroundings
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ecosystem and Conservation of Natural Resources, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** living things are organised in widening levels — a habitat is where a kind lives, a population is all of one kind in a place, a community is all the kinds living together, and an ecosystem is the community together with its non-living surroundings — and every ecosystem holds abiotic (non-living) and biotic (living) parts.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the primary Our Environment lessons (resources and care are known) and opens the Grade 8 ecology set at its own depth; the secondary-depth Biology lesson Ecologys Key Words treats the same words further on — this lesson stands in Our Environment, cross-referenced, not copied.

## Sub lessons
1. **The four levels, nested** — habitat, population, community and ecosystem, each one holding the ones before it.
2. **The ecosystem — living plus non-living** — an ecosystem is the community together with its surroundings; its parts are biotic and abiotic.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Resources around us and why they matter (Our Environment) — recommended, never forced.
## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a habitat, a population and a community are, with an example of each.
2. Say what makes the step from a community to an ecosystem.
3. Sort the parts of a school compound into abiotic and biotic.
## What you need
A pond, a garden corner or the school compound to look at, and a notebook.
