# Energy resources — environmental degradation and its effects

## Do it first
Stand before a hillside where the trees have been cut: stumps on the slope, a gully torn by rain, a stream running muddy below. List everything that is worse there now than when the forest stood. Count your list when you finish — see if it sorts itself into families. (This whole lesson comes from teachers' material — teacher evidence, labelled that way everywhere it appears.)

## Degradation
**Environmental degradation is the deterioration of the environment** — its getting worse — **chiefly through resource depletion**. Drain the stores faster than they return, and the world around the stores deteriorates with them.

[Picture 33322 — the degraded hillside]

## The five effect families
**Human health** — acute and chronic illness follow fouled water and air; wildlife loss even changes diets. **Biodiversity loss** — deforestation drives species toward extinction and breaks the water cycle that everything drinks from. **Climate change** — glaciers and ice sheets shrink, ice breaks earlier, species' ranges shift, plants bloom earlier — toward famine, extremes and species loss. **Water and air quality** — water undrinkable, air unfit to breathe. **Natural disasters** — floods and landslides grow **more frequent and more intense** as the environment's capacity to meet needs shrinks.

## One forest, five chains
The hillside tells it as one story: trees cut (depletion) → stream fouled (water quality; then health), habitats gone (biodiversity), rains destabilised (climate), bare ground letting go in the storm (disasters). Degradation is never one harm — it is the web of them, and depletion is its chief thread.

## Say it to your companion
Define degradation, name its chief cause, list the five families with one effect each, and trace the hillside through all five.

## Do It Yourself
1. A town's river runs undrinkable below a new factory's outflow. Which families does this touch already, and which could it reach next?
2. Earlier blooming is listed under climate change. Build its chain to famine, step by step.
3. Which family do floods and landslides belong to — and what makes them worse as degradation deepens?
