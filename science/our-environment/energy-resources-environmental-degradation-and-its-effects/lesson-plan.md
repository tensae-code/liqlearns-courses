# Lesson plan — Energy resources — environmental degradation and its effects

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Energy resources — environmental degradation and its effects
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — the Grade 7 teacher guide's environment strand (§7.9, continued) filed here as teacher evidence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** environmental degradation is the environment's deterioration, chiefly through resource depletion — and its effects fall in five families: human health, biodiversity loss, climate change, water and air quality, and natural disasters grown more frequent and intense (teacher evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Energy resources — resource depletion and its drivers (this title): depletion is defined; now what the draining does to the world around it. THIS LESSON STANDS ON TEACHER-GUIDE EVIDENCE ONLY.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define degradation and name its chief cause: resource depletion.
2. Name the five effect families with one concrete effect in each.
3. Trace one felled forest through all five families in a single chain.

## What you need
A local hillside, stream or roadside where degradation can be observed or honestly imagined.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands (in place or in mind) before a hillside where the trees have been cut — stumps, a gully, a muddy stream — and lists everything that is worse there now than when the forest stood, before degradation is defined (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses degradation and depletion as one word → depletion drains a resource; degradation is the wider deterioration of the environment that follows, chiefly through depletion.
- Names effects without their chain → every effect must hang on its cause: deforestation → broken water cycle → …
