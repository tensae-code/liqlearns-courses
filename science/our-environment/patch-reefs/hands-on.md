# Hands-on — Patch reefs

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Model the lagoon floor
In a tray, lay sand for the lagoon floor, then stand a rough stone or a piece of sponge upright in the middle as a patch reef. Say what the stone gives a small animal that the sand around it cannot, and point to where it is growing toward — the light.

## Count the hiding places
Look at any rough, holey object — a stone, a sponge, a piece of bark — and count its holes and sheltered edges. Say why a reef shape like that shelters more small animals than a smooth ball of the same size, as the patch reef does in the lagoon.
