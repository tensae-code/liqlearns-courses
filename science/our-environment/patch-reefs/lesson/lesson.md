# Patch reefs

## Do it first
Before you read: a lagoon floor is mostly sand and mud. Could solid reef stand in the middle of it — and if it did, who would use it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Micronesia's way in — its low-island reader dives into its own lagoon to the reefs that stand inside it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reef islands on the lagoon floor

A **patch reef** is a **solid reef island on the lagoon floor** — a stand of real reef rising from the lagoon's sediment bottom, separate from the reef ring at the lagoon's edge.

Like every reef, it **grows toward the sunlight** — building upward from the lagoon floor toward the light its builders need.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hiding places for shallow-water animals

A solid reef standing in open lagoon water is full of edges, holes and shelter — **hiding places for shallow-water animals**.

Small lagoon animals live in and around the patch reef, sheltered there in a way the open sand and mud bottom around it cannot offer. The patch reef is an island of reef life inside the lagoon.

## The key ideas

- A **patch reef** is a **solid reef island on the lagoon floor**, separate from the lagoon's edge reef.
- It **grows toward the sunlight**, building up from the lagoon floor.
- It gives **hiding places for shallow-water animals** — shelter the open sediment bottom cannot offer.
