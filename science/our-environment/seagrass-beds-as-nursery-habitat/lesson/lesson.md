# Seagrass beds as nursery habitat

## Do it first
Before you read: grass grows in a sunny field on land. Could a plant like it grow under the sea? What would it need — and where in the sea could it get it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Palau's way in — its Ridge to Reef book for Grade 5 teaches the seagrass beds of its own shallow lagoons.

## Sub lesson 1 — Seagrass — a plant that lives in the sunlit shallows

**Seagrass** is a flowering plant that lives in the sea. It is not a seaweed and not a land plant washed in: it is a plant whose home is under the water.

Its leaves work like the leaves of any green plant — they **photosynthesise**, making food from sunlight. And that one fact fixes where seagrass can live: only where sunlight reaches down through the water. So seagrass beds stay in **sunlit shallow water**, a green meadow under a thin sheet of sea.

The beds grow wider mostly without seeds. Most of Palau's seagrasses **spread by their roots**, creeping outward under the sand, one plant joining to the next until a whole bed covers the shallow floor.

## Sub lesson 2 — Enhalus and its moon-timed pollen

A flowering plant must spread its pollen — the dust that lets it make seed. On land, wind and insects carry it. One of Palau's seagrasses does it in the sea itself.

**Enhalus acoroides** releases its pollen onto the **surface of the sea** — and it keeps time with the moon: the pollen goes out at **full moon and at new moon**, riding on the water, which carries it where it needs to go.

That is the seagrass's own way, and the lesson keeps it as Palau teaches it: a plant of the shallows that spreads by its roots day by day, and by the moon's own calendar when it flowers.

## The key ideas

- **Seagrass** is a flowering plant that lives in the sea; its leaves photosynthesise, so its beds stay in **sunlit shallow water**.
- Most of Palau's seagrasses **spread by their roots**, creeping under the sand.
- **Enhalus acoroides** releases its pollen onto the sea surface at **full moon and new moon**.
- The bed is a nursery habitat — a sheltered meadow of the shallows where young sea life gathers (the bed's animals are stocked in the ecosystems lesson).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the plant of the sunlit shallows first, then Enhalus and its moon-timed pollen. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why can't a seagrass bed grow in deep water?" Answer from the photosynthesis, not by repeating.
