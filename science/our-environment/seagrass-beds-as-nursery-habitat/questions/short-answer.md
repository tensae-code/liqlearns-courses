# Seagrass beds as nursery habitat — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do seagrass beds live in shallow water and not in the deep sea?
**Answer: Because seagrass leaves photosynthesise — they make food from sunlight — and sunlight only reaches the shallow water; the deep sea is too dark for the plant to feed itself.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) How does a seagrass bed grow wider without seeds?
**Answer: Most of Palau's seagrasses spread by their roots, creeping outward under the sand, one plant joining to the next until the bed covers the shallow floor.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Tell the Enhalus pollen story in order.
**Answer: Enhalus acoroides, a Palau seagrass, releases its pollen onto the surface of the sea at full moon and at new moon, and the water carries the pollen where it needs to go.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) How is Enhalus pollination different from a land flower's?
**Answer: A land flower's pollen rides the wind or insects through the air; Enhalus releases its pollen onto the sea surface, timed to the full and new moon, and the water does the carrying.**
