# Hands-on — Seagrass beds as nursery habitat

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Light or no light
Take two matching green plants or leafy cuttings. Put one where sunlight reaches it and stand the other in a dark cupboard or box for some days. Watch both. Say what the light-fed one can do that the dark one cannot — and say why that is why seagrass beds live in shallow water.

## The root-creep check
Find a patch of grass or a creeping plant on land. Lift one edge gently and follow a root or runner under the soil with your fingers: see how one plant becomes many without any seed. Say how Palau's seagrasses spread the same way, under the sand of the shallows.

## Draw the bed by the moon
Draw a seagrass bed in shallow water: sun rays reaching the leaves, roots creeping under the sand. Above the water draw two small moons — one full, one new — and pollen dots riding on the surface between them. Label each drawing with the sub lesson it comes from.
