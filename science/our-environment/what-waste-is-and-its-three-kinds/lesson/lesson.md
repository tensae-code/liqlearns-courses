# What waste is and its three kinds

## Do it first
Cook a meal, eat it, wash up. What is left? Peelings. Bones. Dirty water. Smoke in the air from the fire. Nobody bought any of it, nobody wants it — it is what use leaves behind. It has a name, and exactly three forms. This lesson names both.

## Left over after use — in three states
**Waste is what is left over after things are used** — the book's examples run from **leftover food and products** to **human and animal waste**.

And waste comes in **three states**, matching the states of matter themselves:

1. **Solid waste** — the scraps, peelings and broken things you can pick up.
2. **Liquid waste** — the dirty wash-water and effluent that flows away.
3. **Polluted air** — the smoke and foul air that rises and drifts.

Every disposal lesson before this one handled one state at a time — dry rubbish (solid), dirty water (liquid). The Grade 4 book gathers all three under one name, because a home, a school or a town makes all three every day, and all three must be dealt with.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the definition and the three states, with one fresh example of each from your own home today.

## Do It Yourself
1. Three-state audit: walk your home and yard and list today's waste in three columns — solid, liquid, air. Which column is longest?
2. Leftover line: pick one object — a banana. Follow it: fruit → eaten → peel (solid waste) → washed hands (liquid). One thing, two states.
3. Smoke source: where does your home's polluted air come from? Name the fire or engine. The third state has an address too.
