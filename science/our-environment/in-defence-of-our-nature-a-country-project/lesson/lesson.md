# In defence of our nature — a country project

## Do it first
Before you read: somewhere near where you live there is a place that is protected — a reserve, a park, a guarded stretch of river or forest. Do you know its name? Do you know what rare living thing it guards, and why the place matters? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Bulgaria's way in — its Grade 4 book ends by sending every learner to research the protected territory near their own home.

## Sub lesson 1 — The project — one protected territory near home

Bulgaria's Grade 4 book's Lesson 56 is a **project**: *In Defence of Bulgarian Nature*. Each learner researches **a protected territory near home**.

The research has named objects: the territory's **species** — what lives there; its **rare inhabitants** — the uncommon living things the protection exists for; and **why the place matters** — what would be lost if it were not defended.

## Sub lesson 2 — Knowing a place is the first defence

The project form is the teaching: a protected territory is defended first by being **known** — by the people who live near it being able to name it, name its rare inhabitants, and say why it matters.

Research first, defence second: the learner who has done this project holds the facts a defence is made of — which place, which species, which rare inhabitants, and what is at stake.

## The key ideas

- The project: research **one protected territory near home**.
- Name its **species**, its **rare inhabitants**, and **why it matters**.
- A place is defended first by being **known** — name the place, its rare life, and what is at stake.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What three things must the project find out about the protected territory?" Answer: Its species, its rare inhabitants, and why the place matters.

## Do It Yourself
1. Choose the protected place nearest your home — a reserve, park or guarded stretch of nature.
2. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences, in the lesson's own order.
3. Write the lesson's key ideas from memory, then check them against the lesson.
