# Lesson plan — Beaches — made of coral and shell

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Beaches — made of coral and shell
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — appended to Unit 11 (Our Environment) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a Pacific beach is made of and who lives in it — sand and rock that are broken coral and shells on low islands, dark volcanic sand against white coral-and-shell sand on high islands, and the tiny sand animals and salt-tolerant shore plants that make the beach a habitat — Micronesia's own way, in its PCEP readers for Grades 3–5 and 6–8
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
1. **Sand made of broken coral and shell** — On Micronesia's low islands, beach sand and rock are broken coral and shells — the hard remains of reef animals, broken down grain by grain until they pile up as a beach. A lagoon-facing shore is a sheltered sandy beach of coral fragments.
2. **The beach as a habitat** — A beach is a habitat, not empty ground. Tiny sand animals live in and on the sand, and they are food: they feed crabs and seabirds (on the high islands, crabs and birds).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks sand is just ground-up rock everywhere → return to sub lesson 1: on the low islands the sand is broken coral and shells — reef remains — and the white colour gives it away.
- Thinks nothing lives on a beach → return to sub lesson 2: the sand animals are tiny, but crabs and seabirds feed on them, and shore plants root in the sheltered sand.
