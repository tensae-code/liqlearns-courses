# Beaches — made of coral and shell

## Do it first
Before you read: pick up a handful of beach sand in your mind. What is it actually made of — where did each grain come from? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Micronesia's way in — its island readers teach the beach by what the sand is made of.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sand made of broken coral and shell

On Micronesia's low islands, beach **sand and rock are broken coral and shells** — the hard remains of reef animals, broken down grain by grain until they pile up as a beach. A lagoon-facing shore is a sheltered sandy beach of **coral fragments**.

The high islands show the contrast: their beaches can be **dark volcanic sand**, from the island's own black rock — set against the **white coral-and-shell sand** of the reef islands. The colour of the sand tells you what the beach is made of, and where it came from.

## Sub lesson 2 — The beach as a habitat

A beach is a **habitat**, not empty ground. Tiny **sand animals** live in and on the sand, and they are food: they **feed crabs and seabirds** (on the high islands, crabs and birds).

Where the sheltered lagoon-facing shore keeps the sand still, **salt-tolerant shore plants** take hold — plants that can live with the sea's spray and salt where other plants cannot. The beach feeds and shelters life from its smallest grains up.

## The key ideas

- Low-island beach sand and rock are **broken coral and shells**; a lagoon-facing beach is coral fragments.
- High-island beaches can be **dark volcanic sand**, against the **white coral-and-shell sand** of reef islands.
- Tiny **sand animals** feed crabs and seabirds, and **salt-tolerant shore plants** grow on the sheltered lagoon-facing shore.
