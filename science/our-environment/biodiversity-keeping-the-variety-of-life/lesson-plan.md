# Lesson plan — Biodiversity — keeping the variety of life

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Biodiversity — keeping the variety of life
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ecosystem and Conservation of Natural Resources, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** biodiversity is the variety of life, counted in species; its value is direct (medicine and agriculture — most human drugs began in living organisms) and indirect (nutrient cycling, waste disposal, fresh water), widened by the guide's key into five families of services; it is lost through overexploitation, exotic introduced species, pollution and habitat loss, and protected in-situ (national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves) and ex-situ (botanical gardens, zoos, seed banks).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Forests — what they give and what cutting them costs (this title, Grade 8) and on the whole Grade 8 ecology set before it. The Biology title's Losing Biodiversity and conservation lessons carry the variety of life at secondary depth — cross-referenced in this level line, never copied; the Agriculture title's biodiversity lessons approach it from farming.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **What biodiversity is worth** — direct and indirect value (student book); the guide's five families of services.
2. **How it is lost — and how it is protected** — four losses; in-situ and ex-situ protection.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Forests — what they give and what cutting them costs (Our Environment) — recommended, never forced.
## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define biodiversity and say how it is counted.
2. Give direct and indirect values, and name the guide's five service families.
3. Name the four losses, and sort protection into in-situ and ex-situ with examples.
## What you need
Two places to compare if possible — a varied garden or field edge and a single-crop plot — or the picture in this lesson.
