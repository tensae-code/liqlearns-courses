# Biodiversity — keeping the variety of life

## Do it first

Count the kinds of living things in a wheat field: wheat, a few weeds, some insects — a short list. Now count in a forest edge or a wild garden corner. The list runs and runs. That difference in the length of the list — the variety of life — is what this lesson values, watches being lost, and learns to protect.

## Sub lesson 1 — What biodiversity is worth

**Biodiversity** is the **variety of life** in a place, **counted in species** — the forest edge is rich in it, the wheat field poor (Activity 5.28's contrast).

**Direct value** — what people take and use: **medicine** (most human drugs began in living organisms) and **agriculture** (the crops and animals themselves, and their wild relatives).

**Indirect value** — the work the variety does unseen: **nutrient cycling**, **waste disposal** and keeping **fresh water** fresh.

The guide's unit-end key widens the value into **five families**: economic (raw materials; food, fibre and water; livelihoods of farmers, fishers and timber workers), ecological life support (oxygen, clean air and water, pollination, pest control, climate and disease regulation, wastewater treatment), recreation (birdwatching, hiking, camping, fishing — and tourism), cultural (spiritual and recreational benefits) and scientific (ecological data for understanding the natural world).

## Sub lesson 2 — How it is lost — and how it is protected

Biodiversity is **lost** four ways: **overexploitation** (taking faster than life replaces), **exotic introduced species** (foreign kinds that overwhelm local ones), **pollution**, and **habitat loss** — the greatest, because a kind whose home is gone has nowhere to be protected.

**In-situ** protection keeps living things **in their natural habitat**: national parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves — the home protected, the life inside it with it.

**Ex-situ** protection keeps them **outside** the habitat: botanical gardens, zoos and seed banks — a safeguard and a store, ready to return kinds to the wild where that is possible.

The two ways answer different dangers: protect the home first (in-situ), and keep a living copy safe outside it (ex-situ) against the home's loss.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — what biodiversity is worth first, then how it is lost — and how it is protected. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Show me where that is in the picture." Answer by pointing, not by repeating.
