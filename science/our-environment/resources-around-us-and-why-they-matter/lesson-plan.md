# Lesson plan — Resources around us, and why they matter

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Resources around us, and why they matter
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — the evidence lessons from the city-scale guides (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** natural resources are soil, water, plants, air and wild animals; man-made resources are monuments, bridges, houses of worship, government buildings and squares; soil is formed from tiny rock fragments and decayed plants and animals, and serves plant growth, pottery, farming and construction; wild animals bring tourist income and their parts income when they die; a self-reliant country needs no imports; and vegetation is protected by not cutting without purpose, covering bare land, and planting trees in towns to clean the air
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Natural Resources — the resources valued one by one; this lesson is the sub-city scale view, from the teacher guides, and it rests on guide evidence only; its sources say so plainly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort resources into natural (soil, water, plants, air, wild animals) and man-made (monuments, bridges, houses of worship, government buildings, squares).
2. Say how soil is formed — tiny rock fragments and the decay of plants and animals — and its uses: plant growth, pottery (ሸክላ), farming, construction.
3. Say the value argument: wild animals attract tourists; a country self-reliant in food and clothing needs no imports; resources used carefully serve this generation and the next.
4. Recite the vegetation protections: no cutting without a purpose; cover bare land with grass and trees against flood erosion; plant many trees in towns to clean the air.

## What you need
Your own sub-city, town or village to audit: its natural and made resources, its bare land, its trees.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts buildings as 'natural' because they stand outdoors → the guides' line is origin: made by people = man-made, however grand; given by nature = natural.
- Cuts 'useless' bush → the guides' rule is purpose: any plant, whatever its size, must not be cut without a purpose.
