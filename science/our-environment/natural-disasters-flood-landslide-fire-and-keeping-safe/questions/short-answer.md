# Natural disasters — flood, landslide, fire — and keeping safe — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: flood.
**Answer: When heavy rain swells rivers beyond their banks, water covers land, homes and crops. Embankments — raised banks along the river — hold back ordinary floods, and keeping river channels and drains clear lets water escape instead of spreading.**

**2.** Explain: landslide.
**Answer: On steep, rain-soaked slopes the soil itself slides, burying roads, fields and houses below. Tree roots bind a slope together, so planting trees on hillsides and not cutting them is landslide prevention: a green slope is a held slope.**

**3.** Explain: fire.
**Answer: Fire disasters start small — a stove, a spark, dry grass near a flame — and run when fuel and wind feed them. Keeping fire from dry material, watching every flame, and clearing firebreaks stop most fires before they become disasters. Prevention, in all three, is work done before the disaster arrives.**
