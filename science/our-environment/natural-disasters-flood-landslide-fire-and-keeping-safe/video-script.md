Today: Natural disasters — flood, landslide, fire — and keeping safe.

Remember this one thing: flood, landslide and fire are natural disasters that damage homes, land and lives — and much of their damage can be prevented by how people build, plant and prepare: embankments against floods, trees holding slopes, and care with fire.

When heavy rain swells rivers beyond their banks, water covers land, homes and crops. Embankments — raised banks along the river — hold back ordinary floods, and keeping river channels and drains clear lets water escape instead of spreading.

On steep, rain-soaked slopes the soil itself slides, burying roads, fields and houses below. Tree roots bind a slope together, so planting trees on hillsides and not cutting them is landslide prevention: a green slope is a held slope.

Fire disasters start small — a stove, a spark, dry grass near a flame — and run when fuel and wind feed them. Keeping fire from dry material, watching every flame, and clearing firebreaks stop most fires before they become disasters. Prevention, in all three, is work done before the disaster arrives.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
