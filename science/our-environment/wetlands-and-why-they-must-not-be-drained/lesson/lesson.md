# Wetlands — and why they must not be drained

## Do it first
A wet, boggy field looks empty and useless to a builder who wants dry land. Drain it, and the land is 'useful' — but something that lived there is gone. What could that something be? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Iceland's wetland chapter counts the cost in birds.

## Sub lesson 1 — Draining — the habitat that shrank

**Draining wetlands** badly shrank wader habitat in Iceland: the **water rail is nearly extinct** in Iceland and the **red-necked phalarope rare** — the cost of draining, counted in the birds that lost their wet ground. A wetland drained is a home removed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Restoring — and peat bogs binding carbon

The guide's answer runs the other way: **restoring wetlands** — bringing the water back — and a second reason stands beside the birds: **peat bogs bind carbon dioxide**, holding it in the wet ground. Wet ground kept wet keeps both its birds and its bound carbon.

## The key ideas

- Draining wetlands badly shrank wader habitat in Iceland — the water rail is nearly extinct there, the red-necked phalarope rare.
- Restoring wetlands brings the water — and the habitat — back.
- Peat bogs bind carbon dioxide, a second reason to keep wet ground wet.
