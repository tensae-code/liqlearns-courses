Today: **Biodiversity of Nepal** — Nepal's way, from its Curriculum Development Centre books.

Nepal's own biodiversity: 27th in the world, 10th in Asia, 2nd in South Asia, with 3.2% of the world's plant and 1.1% of its animal variety (the country's own case)

Nepal's endangered plants and animals and why they are declining (guide evidence; lesson first proposed in the Nepal sections above): the pictured Bengal tiger, red panda, snow leopard, one-horned rhino, pangolin, musk deer and great hornbill, and the rare plants panchaunle, sarpagandha, padamchal, satisal, kutki, jatamansi, yarsagumba and lauth salla; decline blamed on climate change, pollution, smuggling and uncontrolled use of living resources.

Nepal's protected list as printed, the country's own way (guide evidence): 26 protected mammals (from pygmy hog, red panda, blackbuck, gaur, wild yak and arna to Bengal tiger, snow leopard, Gangetic dolphin and Himalayan brown bear), 9 protected birds (great hornbill, cheer pheasant, sarus crane, Bengal and lesser floricans, danphe and munal among them) and 3 protected reptiles (gharial, Asiatic rock python, golden monitor lizard); CITES explained with its three appendices — Nepal signed on 12 June 1975, as printed — and the IUCN Red Data Book named.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
