# Flood danger and protection

## Do it first
Before you read: this lesson teaches one thing — flood danger and protection — what flowing water can do, how floods and landslides are prevented where we live, and how to keep safe when flooding happens — as placed from Laos’s Grade 2 and Grade 3 Science and Environment books. What do you already know about it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Laos's way in — its Science and Environment books teach this as its own topic, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — The dangers of flood water

Laos’s Grade 2 book (Ch. 25, printed pp. 103–104) teaches the dangers of **flowing water**: it can wash away houses and flood roads. Moving water is strong — the same flow that does useful work (the water chapters teach its uses) becomes a danger when it rises and runs where people live.

The Grade 3 book (Ch. 1, printed pp. 5–8) widens the picture to **floods and landslides and their impacts**: flood water spreading over the land, and earth sliding where slopes are soaked — two dangers that heavy rain brings together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Preventing floods and landslides where we live

Both books teach prevention as the community’s own work. The Grade 2 book asks how to **prevent and reduce the danger** of flowing water; the Grade 3 book teaches **preventing floods and landslides where we live** — the danger is met before the flood, in the place itself, by the people who live there.

This is protection, not planning paperwork: where the Costa Rica lesson (flood and landslide emergency plans) makes the community’s plan, Laos’s books teach the danger and its prevention directly to the youngest learners, as knowledge they carry home.

## Sub lesson 3 — Keeping safe when flooding happens

The Grade 3 book closes the chapter with **keeping safe when flooding happens**. Prevention cannot hold every flood back, so the last knowledge is conduct during the flood itself — the behaviour that keeps people safe while the water is up.

The lesson’s order is the books’ order: know the danger, prevent what can be prevented where you live, and know how to keep safe when the water comes anyway.
