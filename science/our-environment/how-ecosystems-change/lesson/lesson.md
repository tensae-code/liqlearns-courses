## Sub lesson 1 — Change comes slowly and suddenly

An **ecosystem** does not stay the same for ever. Some changes are slow: a pond fills with silt over years and becomes dry land; trees spread into grassland grain by grain of soil and seed by seed.

Other changes are sudden: a fire, a flood, a drought or a storm can remake an ecosystem in a day. Kuwait's Grade 4 book teaches both speeds side by side, so the learner asks of every change: slow or sudden, and what started it?

## Sub lesson 2 — People change ecosystems too

People change ecosystems by cutting forests, building on land, draining wetlands and polluting water — and also, in the other direction, by planting, protecting and cleaning. When an ecosystem changes, its living things must fit the new home, move, or die out.

The book's lesson ends where the course's caring lessons begin: because change can be chosen as well as suffered, people who understand how ecosystems change can choose the changes that keep living things alive.

## The key ideas

- Ecosystems change slowly (silting, spreading trees) and suddenly (fire, flood, drought, storm).
- People change ecosystems by cutting, building, draining and polluting — and by planting and protecting.
- When an ecosystem changes, living things fit in, move, or die out.
- Understanding change lets people choose changes that keep life alive.
