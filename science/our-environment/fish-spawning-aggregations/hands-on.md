# Hands-on — Fish spawning aggregations

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The scattered-and-gathered test
Take two handfuls of small scraps of paper — one colour for eggs, one for sperm. First, scatter them one by one across a wide floor: see how rarely the two colours land together. Then gather both handfuls and drop them together in one spot: now the colours are side by side everywhere. Say which drop is the spawning aggregation, and why the fish do it that way.

## Count a gathering
Watch any gathering of animals you can see safely — birds settling together, ants at food, fish in a tank or pond. Count roughly how many are together, and say what the gathering is for. Then say how a spawning aggregation is the same shape — many of one kind, one place, one time — with a different job.

## Draw the aggregation
Draw a reef with many fish gathered above it in one crowd. Draw tiny dots streaming from the crowd into the water — eggs from the females, sperm from the males. Label each drawing with the sub lesson it comes from.
