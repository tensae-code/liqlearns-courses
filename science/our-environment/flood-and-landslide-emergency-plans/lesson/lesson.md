# Flood and landslide emergency plans

## Do it first
When heavy rain comes to a community on a river or below a steep slope, the water and the sliding earth do not wait for anyone to improvise. What should a community have ready before the rain starts? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Costa Rica's own preparedness task — its Grade 7 Ciencias closes by having learners make a community emergency plan for floods and landslides.

## Sub lesson 1 — The two dangers — floods and landslides

A **flood** is water rising and spreading over land that is usually dry; a **landslide** is earth and rock sliding down a slope. Heavy rain can bring both, and a community in their path faces them as named, known dangers — not surprises.

Naming the dangers first is the plan's foundation: a community that knows which places flood and which slopes can slide knows what it is planning against.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making the community emergency plan

An **emergency plan** is made **before** the emergency: the community decides in advance what will be done, who does it, and where people go when a flood or a landslide threatens.

Making that plan is the task Costa Rica's book sets its learners — the country's own preparedness way: the plan belongs to the whole community, is made in calm weather, and is ready to be followed when the rain comes. A plan made during the flood is no plan at all; this lesson's one thing is the making of it beforehand.
