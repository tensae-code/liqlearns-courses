# Habitats around the world — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: dry and cold extremes.
**Answer: A desert habitat is hot and short of water; its plants store water and its animals come out at night. Polar habitats are the opposite extreme — ice, long dark winters and bitter cold — where thick fur and fat keep animals alive. Both are homes, but only for the fitted.**

**2.** Explain: forest and wetland.
**Answer: Forests give shade, rain and layers of living space from floor to canopy, so they hold more kinds of life than almost anywhere. Wetlands stand between land and water — flooded ground where reeds grow, fish breed and birds feed — and they clean water as it passes through.**

**3.** Explain: the ocean habitat.
**Answer: The ocean is the largest habitat of all: salty water without edges, lit near the surface and dark below. Its plants and animals live by depth — floating, swimming and bottom-dwelling kinds, each in the layer that suits it.**
