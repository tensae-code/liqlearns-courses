# Habitats around the world

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Habitats around the world. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Bangladesh's Class 4 Elementary Science book, after teaching the habitats near home, widens the map: the same idea — a habitat is a living thing's home — stretched across the whole Earth.

In your mind's eye: the world's great habitats — desert, forest, wetland, ocean and polar lands — each offer a different mix of water, temperature and food, and each is home to plants and animals fitted to exactly that mix. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Dry and cold extremes.** A desert habitat is hot and short of water; its plants store water and its animals come out at night. Polar habitats are the opposite extreme — ice, long dark winters and bitter cold — where thick fur and fat keep animals alive. Both are homes, but only for the fitted.

**Forest and wetland.** Forests give shade, rain and layers of living space from floor to canopy, so they hold more kinds of life than almost anywhere. Wetlands stand between land and water — flooded ground where reeds grow, fish breed and birds feed — and they clean water as it passes through.

**The ocean habitat.** The ocean is the largest habitat of all: salty water without edges, lit near the surface and dark below. Its plants and animals live by depth — floating, swimming and bottom-dwelling kinds, each in the layer that suits it.
