Today: Habitats around the world.

Remember this one thing: the world's great habitats — desert, forest, wetland, ocean and polar lands — each offer a different mix of water, temperature and food, and each is home to plants and animals fitted to exactly that mix.

A desert habitat is hot and short of water; its plants store water and its animals come out at night. Polar habitats are the opposite extreme — ice, long dark winters and bitter cold — where thick fur and fat keep animals alive. Both are homes, but only for the fitted.

Forests give shade, rain and layers of living space from floor to canopy, so they hold more kinds of life than almost anywhere. Wetlands stand between land and water — flooded ground where reeds grow, fish breed and birds feed — and they clean water as it passes through.

The ocean is the largest habitat of all: salty water without edges, lit near the surface and dark below. Its plants and animals live by depth — floating, swimming and bottom-dwelling kinds, each in the layer that suits it.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
