# Measuring biodiversity — the Simpson index

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's title — Measuring biodiversity — the Simpson index. Say out loud anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on: this is Venezuela's way in — its Grade 7 *Alimentando con Ciencias* does not stop at naming biodiversity: it measures a locality's biodiversity with an index, and works the numbers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Biodiversity can be measured

To compare the biodiversity of two places, counting kinds is not enough — a place of many kinds held by few individuals each differs from a place ruled by one kind. The book measures a locality's biodiversity with an **index** — the **Simpson index** — one number that reads the variety and its balance together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the index — the worked example

The index runs toward **1**: **nearer 1 = more diverse**. The book's worked example sets **region A at 0.78** against **region B at 0.54** — region A is the more diverse, because its number stands nearer 1. Two localities, two numbers, one comparison anyone can check.
