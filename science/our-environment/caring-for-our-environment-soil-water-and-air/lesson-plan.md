# Lesson plan — Caring for our environment: soil, water and air

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Caring for our environment: soil, water and air
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — the tenth title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** care, resource by resource: stopping soil erosion (eroded land against protected land), keeping water sources unpolluted, keeping the air clean — with the care methods for each
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Natural Resources — the three resources known and valued; this lesson is their protection, organised as a carer's manual.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Natural Resources — Water, soil and air around us. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Compare eroded land with protected land in the book's pictures, and name how soil erosion is stopped.
2. Name the water sources to keep unpolluted: river, spring, pond, lake — and the care methods for water.
3. Say how the air is kept clean, completing the book's three-resource care.

## What you need
An eroding place and a protected place to compare — or the book's pictures of each; your own water source to inspect.

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives one care act for all three → the book organises care by resource: soil kept by cover and terraces, water kept at the source, air kept by trees and less smoke.
- Sees erosion as only a farm problem → the cared-for field and the gullied field are the book's central pictures; every slope, schoolyard included, is in the lesson.
- The waste lessons are this title's next doors: care applied to what we throw away.
