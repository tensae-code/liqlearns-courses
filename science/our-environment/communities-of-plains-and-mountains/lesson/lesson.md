# Communities of plains and mountains

## Do it first
Before you read: walk in your mind from flat farmland up into high mountains. The plants change, the animals change, even the soil changes. What is the one thing that is changing under all of it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Bulgaria's way in — its Grade 4 book reads the land's communities in two stops, the plains first, then the mountains.

## Sub lesson 1 — Communities of the plains

Bulgaria's Grade 4 book's Lesson 51, *Communities on land*, begins low: the **plains**.

The plains community stands on **fertile soil** and open **meadows**. Its named animals are the **small mammals** of the open ground and the **partridge**, camouflaged against the ground it lives on — protection by matching, in the flat land where there is nowhere to hide but in plain sight.

## Sub lesson 2 — Communities of the mountains

Then the book climbs: the **mountains**.

The mountain community is layered by height: **broadleaf forest** below, **conifers** above it, and **alpine shrubs** higher still. Its named plants and animals are mountain kinds: the **edelweiss** among the high plants, **wild goats** on the slopes, and **eagles** over them. Same country, same lesson — and the community changed completely, because the land rose.

## The key ideas

- **Plains** community: **fertile soil, meadows, small mammals**, and the **camouflaged partridge**.
- **Mountains** community: **broadleaf forest, conifers, alpine shrubs, edelweiss, wild goats, eagles**.
- The land's **height** is what changes the community — flat land and high land hold different life.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why does the community change as the land rises?" Answer: Because the conditions change with height — soil, plants and shelter all differ — so different living things fit the mountains than fit the plains.
