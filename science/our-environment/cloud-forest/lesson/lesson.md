# Cloud forest

## Do it first
Before you read: a cloud sits on a mountaintop and does not leave. What would the forest inside that cloud be like — its soil, its trees, its smallest plants? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Micronesia's way in — its high-island reader climbs its own island to the forest in the cloud.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mountaintops in cloud

A **cloud forest** is the forest of the **mountaintops** — the high ground that stands **in cloud**. The peaks of Micronesia's high islands rise into the cloud layer, so their forests live wrapped in mist and wet air.

That height changes the ground itself: the soil up there is **thin** — there is little depth of earth over the mountain rock.

## Sub lesson 2 — Small trees, and mosses and ferns over everything

In thin soil, in cloud, the trees stay **small** — no valley giants grow on the mountaintop.

What covers everything instead is the small wet-world growth: **mosses and ferns**, spreading over trunks, branches and ground, in a forest that is always damp from the cloud it stands in.

## The key ideas

- A **cloud forest** grows on **mountaintops that stand in cloud**.
- Its soil is **thin**, and its trees are **small**.
- **Mosses and ferns cover everything** in the always-damp cloud forest.
