# River life — from source to mouth

## Do it first
Follow a river in your mind from where it starts high and cold to where it slows and widens. Would the same fish live at both ends? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Serbia's way in — the river, one community changing down the course.

## Sub lesson 1 — One river, one changing community

The **river** is taught as one **community** — but not the same community all the way down. As the river travels from its source to its mouth, its water changes — and the living community changes with it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The upper course — trout in fast cold water

High up, the river runs **fast and cold** — and that water's fish is the **trout**. Fast cold water, trout: the upper course has its own member, fitted to its water.

## Sub lesson 3 — The lower course — catfish and carp in slow water

Low down, the river runs **slow** — and its fish are the **catfish** and the **carp**. Slow lower water, catfish and carp: the lower course is a different home from the upper, inside the same one river.

## The key ideas

- **One river, one changing community** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **The upper course — trout in fast cold water** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **The lower course — catfish and carp in slow water** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Are we at the river's upper course, or its lower course?" Answer: Upper: fast, cold water, and the trout. Lower: slow water, and the catfish and the carp. One river — the water decides who lives where.
