Today: **The ozone layer and its holes** — North Macedonia's way, from its Grade 6 book (printed p. 189).

High in the atmosphere sits a covering of **ozone** that **shields Earth's surface from harmful ultraviolet rays** — the part of sunlight that harms living skin and growing plants. North Macedonia's Grade 6 book teaches it inside its Care for the environment theme: the shield is part of the conditions that make the surface fit for life, and it can be damaged.

The damage came from **freon (CFC) gases** made by people: they **thin** the ozone covering, and the thinning shows most over the **North and South Poles** — the 'holes' are places where the covering has grown thin, **noticed in 1980**. A gas released at the surface ends by opening the shield at the ends of the Earth.

Where the covering thins, more ultraviolet reaches the surface: **skin diseases grow commoner, and plants and animals die**. The book closes with the answer people gave: they now **make far fewer of these gases**, and the covering is **slowly recovering** — proof carried in one page that a harm people caused can be turned back when its cause is cut.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
