# Green cities — designing for a changing climate

## Do it first
Imagine your town in thirty years: hotter summers, heavier storms, scarcer water. What would you build differently — today — so the town still works then? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Egypt's Primary 6 way in — after measuring climate change, its book sets the unit project: building environmentally friendly cities (printed p. 78).

## Sub lesson 1 — A city under a changing climate

Egypt's Primary 6 book reaches this project through its climate unit: weather against climate, the water cycle under heat, measuring climate change, and soil and living things adapting as environments change. A **city** gathers all of those pressures in one place — heat collects between buildings, rain runs off hard surfaces, water and soil are shared by millions. Designing for a changing climate starts from naming what is changing and what the city must still do: shelter people, move water safely, and keep life possible inside it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building an environmentally friendly city — the project

The unit project is to **build an environmentally friendly city**: learners design a city that works with its environment instead of against it. The design joins the unit's science — shade and green ground against heat, ground that lets rain soak in, water saved and soil protected — into one plan, and the plan is judged as a whole city, not as single tricks. Egypt's own way is the order: measure the change first, then design the answer.
