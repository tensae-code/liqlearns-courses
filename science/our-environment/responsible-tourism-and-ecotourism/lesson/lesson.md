# Responsible tourism and ecotourism

## Do it first
Before you read: think of a beautiful place visitors love — a castle, a wetland, a forest. What do the visitors leave behind, good and bad? Then read on: this is Iran's way in — its Grade 11 book ends its whole environment course with the traveller, and asks what kind of guest a place can survive.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tourism — travelling, and what it brings

Iran's book defines tourism plainly: travel and activity done outside one's living place, for at least one day. Our country Iran has many places for touring, and tourism brings real benefits — to the traveller's own life and to the places visited. The book also reaches back: Iran has a long antiquity in tourism, and the Hajj stands in it as a symbol of religious travel.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ecotourism — travel that protects

Against ordinary travel the book sets ecotourism: a responsible journey to natural areas that protects the environment — and it states the test sharply: any tourism in conflict with the environment's sustainability principles is not an instance of ecotourism. How visitors treat an attraction is the whole question; two approaches follow from it, one that uses a place up and one that keeps it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Protected places, and travel that fits

Iran keeps protected areas — set aside by its Environment Protection Organisation since 1353 to preserve natural areas, with wildlife refuges among them — and the lesson closes with success: villages whose tourism fits their culture and customs, and the book's own counsel for the road — take your pack light and your steps slow.

## The key ideas

- Tourism is travel and activity outside one's living place, for at least one day — and it brings benefits to traveller and visited alike.
- Ecotourism is a responsible journey to natural areas that protects the environment; tourism against sustainability principles is not ecotourism, whatever it calls itself.
- Protected areas and wildlife refuges keep sample habitats safe; tourism that fits a place's culture and customs is the kind that lasts.
- The book's counsel for nature journeys: take your pack light and your steps slow.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A tour brings crowds and money to a wetland but tramples its reeds and leaves its litter. Is it ecotourism?" Answer: no — ecotourism must protect the environment; tourism in conflict with sustainability principles is not ecotourism, whatever it earns.

## Do It Yourself
1. Name a place near you that visitors come to, and write one rule that would make their visits protect it.
2. Say, in your own words, the test that separates ecotourism from other tourism.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — end with the pack-light counsel.
