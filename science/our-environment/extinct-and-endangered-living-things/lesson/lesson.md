## Sub lesson 1 — Extinct means gone for ever

A kind of living thing is **extinct** when every last one of it has died and none will ever live again. Extinction is for ever: no care, no money and no science can bring the kind back.

Kuwait's Grade 4 book sets extinction beside its warning form: a kind that still lives but is down to few, and falling, is **endangered** — in danger of extinction. The two words are a before-and-after: endangered is the last chance before extinct.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why kinds disappear — and how they are saved

Kinds disappear when their habitat is destroyed, when they are hunted too much, when pollution poisons them, or when new living things brought by people out-compete or eat them. Every one of those causes has a human hand in it — which means human hands can also stop it.

Endangered kinds are saved by protecting their habitat, stopping the hunting, and breeding them carefully until their numbers climb. The lesson's point is the difference one stage makes: an endangered kind can still be saved; an extinct kind cannot.

## The key ideas

- Extinct: every one of the kind has died; it is gone for ever.
- Endangered: the kind still lives but is few and falling — in danger of extinction.
- Kinds disappear through lost habitat, over-hunting, pollution and introduced living things.
- Endangered kinds can be saved by protecting habitat, stopping hunting and careful breeding.
