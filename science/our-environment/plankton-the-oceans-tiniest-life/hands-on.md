# Hands-on — Plankton — the ocean's tiniest life

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Drift like a plankton
Fill a wide bowl or basin with water. Float a tiny speck — a crumb, a grain, a fleck of leaf — on the surface. Blow gently across the water and watch the speck drift where the surface current takes it. Say what the speck stands for, and why the plankton cannot choose where they go.

## Half the air from the sea
Take ten deep breaths, counting on your fingers. For more than five of those ten breaths, the oxygen came from the sea's phytoplankton, not from trees. Say which job of the phytoplankton you are thanking, and the name of the process that does it — photosynthesis.

## Anchor the web
Draw the ocean food web as a chain with an anchor: phytoplankton at the anchor point, then the small creatures that eat them, then the fish, then the people who eat the fish. Label each drawing with the sub lesson it comes from.
