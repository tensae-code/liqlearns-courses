# Dirty water and what it brings

## Do it first
It smells. Flies walk on it. Mosquitoes are born in it. That grey puddle behind the house is not 'just water' — it is liquid waste, and it works harm in five directions at once. This lesson webs them all.

## The harms web of liquid waste

**Liquid waste** is **urine, wash-water, and effluent from homes and factories** — water that has served and carries what it served away. Left standing or running wild, the Grade 3 book webs its harms: **bad smell**; **respiratory illness — colds and asthma**; **breeding places for flies and mosquitoes** — the very carriers of the disease lessons; **spoiled beauty** of the home and neighbourhood; and **flood-borne pollution of water sources**, when flood water mixes the waste into wells and springs.

The book's exercise is a **concept-web on these harms** — build it in the hands-on, and the web does the teaching: five spokes, one centre, no spoke forgettable.

## Say it to your companion
Draw the concept-web aloud for your companion: LIQUID WASTE in the centre, they must supply each spoke — smell, sickness, breeding, beauty, flood — with the 'how' attached before you accept it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Drainage walk: after rain, map where liquid waste stands around your home or school. Each standing place gets its harms named from the web.
2. The fix list: for each standing place, one fix — a drain line, a soak pit, a filled hollow — proposed in writing to whoever owns the ground.
3. Breeding check: revisit one drained or fixed place after a week. Flies? Mosquitoes? Smell? The web predicts; observation judges.
