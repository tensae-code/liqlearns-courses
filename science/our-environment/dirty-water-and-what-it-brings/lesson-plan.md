# Lesson plan — Dirty water and what it brings

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Dirty water and what it brings
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — waste, second of two (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** liquid waste — urine, wash-water and effluent from homes and factories — brings bad smell, respiratory illness (colds, asthma), breeding places for flies and mosquitoes, spoiled beauty, and flood-borne pollution of water sources
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Dry rubbish in its place — waste's solid half handled; this is the liquid half, and its harms reach back into the disease lessons.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the liquid wastes: urine, wash-water, and effluent from homes and factories.
2. Complete the book's concept-web of harms: bad smell; respiratory illness (colds, asthma); breeding places for flies and mosquitoes; spoiled beauty; flood-borne pollution of water sources.

## What you need
A compound after rain to observe: where does dirty water stand, and what visits it?

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands by a puddle of standing wash-water on a warm day — smell it, watch what lands on it — and lists its harms before the book's web is opened (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls it 'just water' → water that has served is waste: urine, wash-water and effluent carry the harms by the book's web, however clear they look.
- Names harms but not the flood spoke → in flood, standing waste rides the water into sources: the pollution of the Water lesson begins here.
- This lesson ends the Grades 1–3 waste band; proper and improper disposal methods at school and woreda are promised by the Grade 3 guide's contents (§3.4) but those pages are NOT in the truncated file — see the sources honesty note.
