Today: **Ocean currents and upwelling** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**Warm water, cold water and the thermocline.** The ocean is layered by temperature. Sun-warmed water floats at the top; below it the water is cold. Between the warm top and the cold deep lies the thermocline — the layer where the temperature drops quickly as you go down. Samoa's Year 10 Book 3 teaches the ocean environment of the islands in exactly these terms: warm and cold water, and the thermocline between them (printed pp. 27–31).

**The trade winds, the currents and upwelling.** The southeast trade winds blow steadily over Samoa's seas, and moving air drags the surface water with it — that is how currents are driven around Samoa. Where surface water is carried away, deep water rises to take its place: that rising is upwelling. Upwelling matters because the deep water it brings up is cold and rich, feeding the sea life above it. Wind, current, layers and upwelling are one system: the winds move the top, the layers stack by temperature, and the deep answers by rising (printed pp. 27–31).
