# Ocean currents and upwelling

## Do it first
Stand on a Samoan beach and look out. The water at your feet is warm; far below the surface the same ocean is cold. What moves this water, and can the deep cold water ever reach the top? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Samoa's Year 10 way in — its Book 3 opens the ocean environment around the islands themselves.

## Sub lesson 1 — Warm water, cold water and the thermocline

The ocean is layered by temperature. Sun-warmed water floats at the top; below it the water is cold. Between the warm top and the cold deep lies the **thermocline** — the layer where the temperature drops quickly as you go down. Samoa's Year 10 Book 3 teaches the ocean environment of the islands in exactly these terms: warm and cold water, and the thermocline between them (printed pp. 27–31).

## Sub lesson 2 — The trade winds, the currents and upwelling

The **southeast trade winds** blow steadily over Samoa's seas, and moving air drags the surface water with it — that is how **currents** are driven around Samoa. Where surface water is carried away, deep water rises to take its place: that rising is **upwelling**. Upwelling matters because the deep water it brings up is cold and rich, feeding the sea life above it. Wind, current, layers and upwelling are one system: the winds move the top, the layers stack by temperature, and the deep answers by rising (printed pp. 27–31).
