# Video script — Coastal ecosystems — dunes, seagrass and coral banks

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Coastal ecosystems — dunes, seagrass and coral banks** — the Dominican Republic's way: the coast as a joined set of ecosystems.

Where the sea meets the land, wind piles the sand into dunes — low hills of sand along the shore. Dunes are not bare, dead ground: coastal vegetation — the plants that can live in sand, salt and wind — grows on them and holds them together. The dune and its plants are the coast's first ecosystem: a living strip that stands between the open sea and the land behind it. In the shallow water just off the shore grow seagrass meadows — underwater fields of grass-like plants rooted in the sandy bottom. The Grade 5 book calls them the ocean's prairies: like a grassland on land, a meadow is a whole community's home ground, sheltering small sea life among its blades. The meadow is the second ecosystem in the shore-to-sea walk: after the dunes on the sand, the grass under the water. Further out lies the Banco de la Plata — the Silver Bank — a great offshore bank of corals that the book calls a paradise of corals and humpback whales: the whales come to its warm, sheltered waters, and the coral bank is their meeting place. The bank completes the walk: dunes on the shore, seagrass in the shallows, and the coral bank offshore — three coastal ecosystems, each with its own life, joined in one coast. This is the Dominican Republic's own way: its coasts taught from its own named places.
