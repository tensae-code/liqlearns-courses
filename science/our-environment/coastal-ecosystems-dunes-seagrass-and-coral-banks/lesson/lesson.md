# Coastal ecosystems — dunes, seagrass and coral banks

## Do it first
Before you read: Why does the book call seagrass meadows the ocean's prairies? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is the Dominican Republic's way in — its Libro Abierto Ciencias de la Naturaleza books teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dunes and coastal vegetation

Where the sea meets the land, wind piles the sand into **dunes** — low hills of sand along the shore. Dunes are not bare, dead ground: **coastal vegetation** — the plants that can live in sand, salt and wind — grows on them and holds them together.

The dune and its plants are the coast's first ecosystem: a living strip that stands between the open sea and the land behind it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Seagrass meadows — the ocean's prairies

In the shallow water just off the shore grow **seagrass meadows** — underwater fields of grass-like plants rooted in the sandy bottom. The Grade 5 book calls them **the ocean's prairies**: like a grassland on land, a meadow is a whole community's home ground, sheltering small sea life among its blades.

The meadow is the second ecosystem in the shore-to-sea walk: after the dunes on the sand, the grass under the water.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Banco de la Plata — corals and humpback whales

Further out lies the **Banco de la Plata** — the Silver Bank — a great offshore bank of **corals** that the book calls a paradise of corals and **humpback whales**: the whales come to its warm, sheltered waters, and the coral bank is their meeting place.

The bank completes the walk: dunes on the shore, seagrass in the shallows, and the coral bank offshore — three coastal ecosystems, each with its own life, joined in one coast. This is the Dominican Republic's own way: its coasts taught from its own named places.

## The key ideas

- **Dunes** are wind-piled sand hills on the shore, held together by **coastal vegetation**.
- **Seagrass meadows** are underwater grass fields in the shallows — **the ocean's prairies**.
- The **Banco de la Plata** is a great coral bank — a paradise of corals and **humpback whales**.
- The book walks the coast in order: dunes, seagrass meadows, coral banks — three joined ecosystems.
