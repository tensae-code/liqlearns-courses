# Hands-on — Mangroves — four types, and why they matter

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The shore walk (or the shore in your mind)
Go to a mangrove shore you can reach safely with an adult — or work from a shore you know well. Find the water's edge and look at the first trees: prop roots or pencil roots? Move your eye back from the water: can you pick out the change of trees toward the drier ground? Name every type you can find, by roots and place.

## The nursery count
Watch one patch of mangrove roots at the water's edge for five quiet minutes. Count and list every living thing you see moving or feeding there — fish, crabs, birds, anything else. Beside each, write where it was: among roots, on mud, in branches.

## The guard test
At home, build the test in a wide tray or basin: water at one end, a strip of bare sand, then 'land'. Make waves with your hand and watch the sand move. Then stand a row of pencils or sticks (your mangrove roots) in the water's edge and make the same waves. Write what changed — the roots' guarding, proved with your own hands.
