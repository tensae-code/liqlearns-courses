Today: **Desert and polar habitats — hot dry and cold dry** — Saudi Arabia's way, from its book (Saudi Arabia, Grade 2, Part 1 — *Science, Grade 2, Part 1*, printed pp. 96–101).

Unit 2, Ch. 4, Lesson 1 "Hot and Cold Deserts": the hot desert is a dry habitat with very little rain, cold nights and sandy or rocky soil, where desert plants keep water (thick stems, spreading or deep roots, leaves that roll up by day) and its animals need little water and mostly sleep by day and come out at night; the polar region is a cold desert near the North Pole — reindeer, seals, the polar bear and the polar fox have thick white fur that warms them and hides them, and its small plants grow close to the ground in a very short summer
the desert ecosystem pictured whole: the falcon hunts lizards and snakes, the cactus shelters birds, lizards and rats and feeds others, desert shrubs release a substance that stops other plants growing near them, vipers shelter from the sun under rocks, and scorpions leave their burrows at night to hunt — all in a place that reaches 50 °C by day and turns cold at night
