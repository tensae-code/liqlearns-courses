# Video script — Cycles — the water cycle: the pan-and-ice experiment

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). This is the FULL walkthrough script for the pan-and-ice condensation experiment, filmed step by step when videos are made.

---

**You need:** a pan or pot of water, a stove or fire to boil it, a second pan or a metal lid, and ice — or very cold water — to chill it. An adult works the stove. Steam can burn: hands stay clear of the rising steam.

**Step 1 — Boil.** Put the pan of water on the heat. Wait until the water boils and steam rises steadily. Watch the steam: water is leaving the pan as vapour, rising into the air. In the big world, this rising is **evaporation** — the sun does the heating, on seas, lakes and rivers.

**Step 2 — Chill the catcher.** Put the ice in the second pan, or fill it with the coldest water you have. The pan's underside must be cold.

**Step 3 — Hold and wait.** The adult holds the cold pan above the steam, underside down, a hand's height above the boiling pan. Count slowly to thirty. Do not touch the steam.

**Step 4 — Look underneath.** Lift the cold pan away and turn it over. Its underside is covered in **droplets** of water. The vapour rose, met the cold surface, and gathered back into liquid water. That gathering is **condensation** — the same gathering that builds clouds in the cold air high above the ground.

**Step 5 — Let it rain.** Tilt the cold pan a little. The droplets join, grow heavy, and **drip** off its edge. That falling is **precipitation** — rain in miniature. Below the pan is where the "ground" would be: the drips land, soak in, and flow away — back toward the water they came from.

**Step 6 — Say the loop.** Evaporation up. Condensation into droplets. Precipitation down. Flow back. And again — the water in the pan is the sea, the steam is the rising vapour, the cold pan is the cold sky, the drips are the rain. One loop, no starting point. That is the water cycle.
