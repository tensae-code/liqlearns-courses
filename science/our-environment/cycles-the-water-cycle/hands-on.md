# Hands-on — Cycles — the water cycle

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your reading.

## The pan-and-ice experiment (the lesson's own)
With an adult on the stove: boil water in a pan until steam rises steadily. Chill a second pan or metal lid with ice or very cold water and hold it, underside down, above the steam (adult holds; keep hands out of the steam — it burns). After half a minute, turn the cold pan over: droplets cover its underside. Tilt it and watch the droplets join and drip. You have run condensation and precipitation on a stove top.

## The cold-bottle cloud
Take a bottle of cold water from a fridge or a cool store and stand it in warm air. Its outside mists and then beads with water. That water did not leak — it condensed out of the air, the pan experiment with no stove. Wipe it dry and watch it mist again: the air always carries vapour.

## Puddle watching
After rain, chalk a line around a puddle's edge in the morning. Mark it again at midday and at day's end. The puddle shrinks without draining anywhere you can see — evaporation, measured with chalk. Where is that water by evening? In the air above you, on its way to a cloud.
