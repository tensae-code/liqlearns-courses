# Lesson plan — Cycles — the water cycle

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Cycles — the water cycle
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — the eleventh title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Earth, at General Science depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** water travels in an endless loop — evaporation from surface water and transpiration from plants rise, condensation gathers the vapour into droplets and clouds, precipitation returns it as rain, and absorption and flow carry it back to the water bodies — a loop with no starting point, which is why the Earth's water endures.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Earth — four spheres, one system and on Cycles — the carbon cycle next door: water is the second great traveller between the spheres, and this lesson's experiment-first opening mirrors the carbon lesson's shape.

## Sub lessons
1. **Water rises and gathers** — the pan-and-ice experiment first (vapour meeting a cold surface and condensing), then the rising moves named: evaporation from surface water, transpiration from plants, and condensation into droplets.
2. **Water returns and flows back** — precipitation as rain; absorption into the soil and flow back to the water bodies; the loop whole, with no starting point.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Cycles — the carbon cycle. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Do the pan-and-ice experiment and say what the droplets on the cold pan prove.
2. Name the two rising moves and tell them apart.
3. Say what condensation and precipitation each do in the loop.
4. Explain why the Earth's water does not run out, using the loop.

## What you need
A pan or pot of water, heat to boil it (adult), and ice or a cold lid for the experiment.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes evaporation and transpiration → both rise, but from different surfaces: evaporation from open water, transpiration from plants' leaves. Ask "rising from what?"
- Thinks rain is new water being made → the loop: rain is the sea's and the land's own water, returned. Nothing is made; everything travels.
- Cannot see condensation outside the experiment → a cold bottle on a warm day mists outside exactly as the pan did; the cloud is the same gathering, at sky size.
