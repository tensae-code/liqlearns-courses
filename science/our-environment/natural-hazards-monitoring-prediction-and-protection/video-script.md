The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Natural hazards — monitoring, prediction and protection** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Grade 8).

Protection begins with **monitoring**: instruments and observers collect **data** on hazards continuously, so changes are caught as they start, not as they arrive (Grade 8, 8.4.5, printed p. 187). A hazard that is monitored is a hazard whose first signs are on record.

The monitored data feeds **prediction**: from the measurements, scientists work out what is coming, where, and when — and the prediction is issued as a **warning**. The book's worked example is the **tsunami warning system**: sea and seismic data, read together, turned into a warning sent to coasts in the wave's path.

A warning protects only if the community acts on it: **protect communities** is the lesson's third step — people who know the warning signs, know where to go, and go. Monitoring, prediction and protection are one chain; break any link and the data saves no one.
