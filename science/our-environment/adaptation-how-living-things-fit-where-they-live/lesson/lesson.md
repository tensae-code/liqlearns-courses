# Adaptation — how living things fit where they live

## Do it first
Before you read: think of an animal you know from hot, dry ground, and one from water. Could each one live in the other's place? Why not? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Djibouti's way in — its Grade 4 guide teaches adaptation in three lessons: why the same animals are not found everywhere, how animals adapt when conditions change, and how plants adapt to their environment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why the same animals are not found everywhere

Djibouti's Grade 4 guide opens its adaptation run with a question of place: **why are the same animals not found everywhere?**

Because a living thing **fits** its environment. The animal of the water is built and behaves for the water; the animal of the dry land for the dry land. Put each in the other's place and the fit fails. That fit — between a living thing and the conditions of the place it lives — is what the whole run of lessons is about, and its name is **adaptation**.

## Sub lesson 2 — How animals adapt when conditions change

Conditions change — seasons turn, water comes and goes, food grows scarce or plenty. Djibouti's guide's second lesson follows the animal through the change: **how animals adapt when their environment's conditions change**.

The animal's way of living adjusts — what it does, when it does it, how it meets the new conditions. Adaptation is not only a fit to a place; it is a fit that answers change.

## Sub lesson 3 — How plants adapt to their environment

The run closes with the plants: **how plants adapt to the conditions of their environment**. A plant stands where it stands — it cannot move to a better place — so its adaptation shows in how it lives and grows in its conditions: the plant fits itself to its environment from the spot where it grows.

Animals and plants together complete the idea: every living thing fits where it lives, and the fitting — to place, and to change — is adaptation.

## The key ideas

- The same animals are not found everywhere because each living thing **fits** its own environment — that fit is **adaptation**.
- When an environment's conditions change, animals **adapt** — their way of living adjusts to the new conditions.
- Plants adapt too, from where they stand: their way of living and growing fits the conditions of their environment.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why are the same animals not found everywhere?" Answer from the fit: each animal fits its own environment — and then say what happens when conditions change.

## Do It Yourself
1. Pick two animals from two different environments near you. Write for each: its environment, and two ways it fits that place.
2. Think of one change of conditions you know — the rains coming, a dry season, cold nights. Write how one animal and one plant meet that change.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — what adaptation is, with one animal and one plant as your examples.
