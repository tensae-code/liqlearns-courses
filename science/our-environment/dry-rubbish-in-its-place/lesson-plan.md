# Lesson plan — Dry rubbish in its place

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Dry rubbish in its place
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — waste, first of two (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** dry waste — peelings (onion, potato, banana, orange), paper and plastic — goes into a waste container and then to a proper disposal place, or the home and neighbourhood become dirty and people fall sick
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Caring for our surroundings — disposing of waste properly was one of its five ways; this lesson is that way, in detail, for dry waste.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Our Environment — Caring for our surroundings, and reusing what we throw away. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the dry wastes: peelings of onion, potato, banana and orange; paper; plastic.
2. State the rule: into a waste container, then to a proper disposal place.
3. Say the consequence of failure: a dirty home and neighbourhood, and people falling sick; and ask, as the book does, how the school compound's waste is handled and how a health worker chooses a disposal site.

## What you need
A waste container to place or find; the school compound's own waste system to inspect.

## If the learner is stuck
- Drops 'biodegradable' peelings anywhere → the book puts peelings in the container with paper and plastic alike: rotting on the ground is the dirt it forbids.
- Knows the rule but not the local system → the book's enquiry (how does OUR compound handle it?) is part of the lesson, not homework decoration.
