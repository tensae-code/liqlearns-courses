# Lesson plan — Global warming — the greenhouse effect

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Global warming — the greenhouse effect
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ecosystem and Conservation of Natural Resources, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a parked car in the sun shows the greenhouse effect: its windows let light in and hold the heat; greenhouse gases — carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour and CFCs — do the same for the Earth; about three-quarters of human carbon dioxide comes from burning fossil fuels and most of the rest from deforestation, and the warming brings sea-level rise, uneven rainfall, flooding, drought, food and water shortage and extinctions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Air pollution and acid rain (this title, Grade 8) and on Cycles — the carbon cycle (this title): carbon's loop is known; now what happens when extra carbon dioxide thickens the heat-trapping layer. The Biology title's Climate Change and Global Warming lessons carry this at secondary depth — cross-referenced, not copied.

## Sub lessons
1. **The trapped heat — the greenhouse effect** — the parked-car metaphor (Activity 5.23); the five greenhouse gases.
2. **Where the extra gas comes from — and what warming brings** — fossil fuels three-quarters, deforestation most of the rest; the effects list; pupils propose reductions.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Air pollution and acid rain (Our Environment)
- Recommended first: Cycles — the carbon cycle (Our Environment) — recommended, never forced.
## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain the greenhouse effect using the parked-car comparison.
2. Name the five greenhouse gases taught.
3. Give the two sources of human carbon dioxide with their shares, and list warming's effects.
## What you need
A parked car or a glass jar in the sun, and a shaded place, to feel the comparison (never sit in a hot parked car — feel the air at its open door or use the jar).
