# Global warming — the greenhouse effect

## Do it first

A car parked in the sun grows hot inside — far hotter than the air outside. Its windows let the sunlight IN, but the heat cannot easily get OUT. Now scale the car up to a whole planet and give it a layer of gases instead of glass. That is this lesson, and you have already felt its mechanism.

## Sub lesson 1 — The trapped heat — the greenhouse effect

The parked car (Activity 5.23): **windows let light in and hold heat** — the inside warms beyond the outside air.

The Earth has no glass, but it has a gas layer that works the same way. A coating that traps more heat stands for the **greenhouse gases**: **carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour and CFCs**.

These gases are not enemies in themselves — a natural share of them keeps the Earth warm enough for life. **Global warming** is what grows when human activity thickens the trapping layer beyond that share: more heat held, a warmer world. (The book's spelling 'chlorofloro carbons' for chlorofluorocarbons — CFCs — is corrected in this lesson and noted in sources.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Where the extra gas comes from — and what warming brings

**Three-quarters of human carbon dioxide comes from burning fossil fuels** — coal, oil and gas in engines, factories and power stations. **Most of the rest comes largely from deforestation** — cut forests stop taking carbon in, and their burning or rotting sends it out.

The effects are listed plainly: **sea-level rise** as ice melts and water expands; **uneven rainfall**; **flooding** in some places and **drought** in others; **food and water shortage**; and **extinctions** as living things fail to adjust.

The lesson ends as the book ends it: having traced the cause, **propose reductions** — every source named above has an opposite: burn less fossil fuel, cut fewer trees, plant more.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the trapped heat — the greenhouse effect first, then where the extra gas comes from — and what warming brings. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Show me where that is in the picture." Answer by pointing, not by repeating.
