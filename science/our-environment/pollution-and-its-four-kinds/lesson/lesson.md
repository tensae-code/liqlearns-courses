# Pollution and its four kinds

## Do it first
Stand still outside for one minute. Something in the water, something on the ground, something in the air, something too loud — damage has kinds, and kinds can be answered one by one. This lesson names them as the book names them.

## The problems list; pollution's four kinds
Ethiopia's main environmental problems are listed together: **soil erosion, land damage, deforestation, water shortage, biodiversity loss, and pollutions**. And pollution itself comes in **four kinds — water, soil, air and noise** — each with causes to identify locally and solutions to propose. The Grade 5 teacher guide's closing answer key (teacher guide — evidence) names the same pollution triad at its core — water pollution, air pollution and soil pollution — and its Unit 3 competencies preview the same environmental-problems teaching.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the problems list, then the four kinds — water, soil, air, noise — with one cause you have seen yourself for each.

## Do It Yourself
1. The one-minute audit, repeated at home: stand, look, smell, listen. Write one piece of evidence for each of the four kinds — or honestly write 'none found'.
2. Solutions round: for each kind, propose one solution a pupil can actually start (a bin used, a tap fixed, a tree planted, a quiet hour kept). The book asks for proposals — make yours real.
3. Problem or pollution? Sort the big list: erosion, land damage, deforestation, water shortage, biodiversity loss. Which of them is pollution itself, and which are its cousins? (Pollution stands beside them in the list — all six are the problems.)
