# Water environments — fresh and salt

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's one thing — the kinds of water environment — rivers, seas, oceans, lakes and marshes — most of them salty and some fresh, as Iraq's Grade 1–2 pupil's and activity books teach it, with the Euphrates as Iraq's own example. Say what you already know about it out loud. Then read on: this is Iraq's way in — its Ministry of Education *Science* books (the Primary Science Book Series) teach this thing at the places named in this lesson's sources.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rivers and seas — most water is salty, some is fresh

Iraq's Grade 1 book opens the water environment with its two great kinds at once: **rivers and seas**. And it gives the measure of the whole subject: **most water environments are salty, some are fresh** — lakes are its fresh example.

Kinds are then named one by one. The **lake** is a water environment with **closed edges** — water held inside its shores. And Iraq's own example of a river is named: the **Euphrates** river.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sea and ocean water — and the fresh marshes

The Grade 2 book studies the water itself: the **properties of sea and ocean water** — the salt water that fills most of the Earth's water environments.

Against it stand the fresh water environments, and they are **varied**: the book names the **marshes**, fresh waters that are **home to fish and birds**. Fresh is not one kind of place — marsh, lake and river are all fresh, and each is its own environment.

## The key ideas

- Water environments are of kinds — **rivers, seas, oceans, lakes and marshes** among them.
- **Most water environments are salty; some are fresh** (lakes).
- The **lake** is a closed-edge water environment; the **Euphrates** is Iraq's own river example.
- The **marshes** are fresh water environments, home to fish and birds.
