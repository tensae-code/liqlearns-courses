# Environmental change — causes and effects

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Environmental change — causes and effects. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Bangladesh's Class 4 Elementary Science book teaches the environment as something that moves: the surroundings a child knows are not fixed, and the book sorts the movers into nature's own changes and the changes people make.

In your mind's eye: the environment changes from natural causes — floods, droughts, storms and shifting seasons — and from human activity, and every change lands on rainfall, temperature and the habitats living things depend on. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Nature changes the environment.** Natural disasters and slow natural shifts remake a place: a flood drowns fields and homes, a drought dries ponds and crops, a storm tears trees and roofs, and the seasons themselves change what grows and what animals do. These changes come whether people act or not.

**People change the environment.** Human activity changes it too: cutting forests, building towns, farming, burning fuels and throwing waste all alter the land, water and air around them. Some human changes are useful and planned; others damage the very surroundings the changers live in.

**Effects reach everything living.** However it starts, environmental change shows in the same places: rainfall patterns shift, temperatures rise or fall, rivers and soils change, and habitats — the homes of plants and animals — shrink, move or vanish. Living things, people included, must adapt to the new conditions or suffer them.
