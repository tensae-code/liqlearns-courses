Today: Environmental change — causes and effects.

Remember this one thing: the environment changes from natural causes — floods, droughts, storms and shifting seasons — and from human activity, and every change lands on rainfall, temperature and the habitats living things depend on.

Natural disasters and slow natural shifts remake a place: a flood drowns fields and homes, a drought dries ponds and crops, a storm tears trees and roofs, and the seasons themselves change what grows and what animals do. These changes come whether people act or not.

Human activity changes it too: cutting forests, building towns, farming, burning fuels and throwing waste all alter the land, water and air around them. Some human changes are useful and planned; others damage the very surroundings the changers live in.

However it starts, environmental change shows in the same places: rainfall patterns shift, temperatures rise or fall, rivers and soils change, and habitats — the homes of plants and animals — shrink, move or vanish. Living things, people included, must adapt to the new conditions or suffer them.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
