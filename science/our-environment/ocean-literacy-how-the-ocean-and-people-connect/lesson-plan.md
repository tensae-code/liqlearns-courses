# Lesson plan — Ocean literacy — how the ocean and people connect

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Ocean literacy — how the ocean and people connect
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — appended to Unit 11 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ocean literacy — the seven essential principles of how the ocean and people connect, as Canada's Science First Peoples guide teaches them in its Ocean Connections unit: one ocean, the ocean shaping Earth, driving weather and climate, making Earth habitable, holding life's diversity, joined to people's lives, and still largely unexplored
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The one ocean and what it does** — the first principles: there is one big ocean; the ocean shapes the Earth; the ocean drives weather and climate; and the ocean made the Earth habitable — the ocean as the one system the whole planet runs on.
2. **The ocean's diversity, our connection, and the unexplored** — the closing principles: the ocean holds a great diversity of life; the ocean and people are interconnected — each changes the other; and the ocean is still largely unexplored — what we know is the smaller part.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: The one ocean and what it does.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: The ocean's diversity, our connection, and the unexplored.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: no special materials — the hands-on works from the learner's own home and street.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks of the oceans as separate seas with nothing joining them → return to sub lesson 1: the first principle is one big ocean.
- Thinks people only take from the ocean → return to sub lesson 2: the interconnection runs both ways — the ocean changes people, and people change the ocean.
