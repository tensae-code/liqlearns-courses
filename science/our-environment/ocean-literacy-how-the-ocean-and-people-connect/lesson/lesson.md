# Ocean literacy — how the ocean and people connect

## Do it first
Before you read: how many oceans are there? And what does the ocean do for a person who lives far from any shore? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Canada's way in — its Science First Peoples guide closes with Ocean Connections, and teaches the ocean through **seven essential principles of ocean literacy** — the seven things a person who understands the ocean knows.

## Sub lesson 1 — The one ocean and what it does

The first principle overturns the map in most heads: there is **one big ocean** — the named oceans are parts of a single connected water.

The next principles give that one ocean its jobs. The **ocean shapes the Earth** — its coasts, its floor, its very land. The **ocean drives weather and climate** — the water's movement and heat steer the air above it, so the weather far inland still carries the ocean in it. And the **ocean made the Earth habitable**: life began in it, and the air and climate it steadied are what let life live on land at all.

Hold those four together: one water, shaping the planet, driving its weather, and making it fit for life. That is the ocean as a system, not a scenery.

## Sub lesson 2 — The ocean's diversity, our connection, and the unexplored

The closing principles turn from the planet to life, and to us. The ocean holds a **great diversity of life** — more kinds, in more ways of living, than the land holds.

The **ocean and people are interconnected**: the ocean feeds people, carries their goods, gives work and steadies climate — and people reach back: what people put into the water, take from it, and build on its shores changes the ocean. Neither side stands alone.

And the last principle is the honest one: the **ocean is largely unexplored**. For all its size and all it does, most of it has never been seen by human eyes. Ocean literacy ends in humility: the biggest part of our own planet is still unknown country.

## The key ideas

- There is **one big ocean**; the named oceans are its connected parts.
- The ocean **shapes the Earth**, **drives weather and climate**, and **made the Earth habitable**.
- The ocean holds a **great diversity of life**.
- The **ocean and people are interconnected** — each changes the other.
- The ocean is **largely unexplored** — most of it has never been seen.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the one ocean and its jobs first, then diversity, interconnection and the unexplored. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Which principle touches a person who lives far from the sea?" Answer from the lesson, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the seven principles in your own words, one line each, and number them in the guide's order.
2. Beside each principle, write one thing in your own life it touches.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — start with the one ocean.
