# Hands-on — Cycles — the carbon cycle

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your reading.

## Tokens around the loop
Draw a big loop on the ground or on paper with five stops: AIR — PLANT — ANIMAL — GROUND — FOSSIL STORE. Take five stones as carbon tokens. Move one token at a time and say its move aloud: "air to plant: photosynthesis." "Plant to animal: eating." "Animal to air: respiration." "Living thing to ground: death and decay." "Ground to store: burial." "Store to air: burning." Run all five tokens right around.

## Breath on glass
Hold a cool glass or metal lid near your mouth and breathe out slowly. The mist that forms is your breath's water — and travelling invisibly with it is the carbon dioxide of respiration, the loop's first release. Say the sentence: "My breathing is one door of the carbon cycle."

## The charcoal story
Look at a piece of charcoal. It was once part of a living tree that took carbon from the air. If it is burned for cooking, that carbon returns to the air as carbon dioxide. Tell the charcoal's whole story in four sentences: air, tree, charcoal, fire, air.
