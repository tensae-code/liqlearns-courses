# Cycles — the carbon cycle

## Do it first

Breathe out slowly onto a cool glass or a metal lid. It mists. Something real leaves your body in every breath — and one part of what leaves is a gas carrying **carbon**. Carbon does not stay still. It moves between the air, living things, the ground and deep stores, round and round. This lesson walks its whole journey: the **carbon cycle**.

Carbon moves between the Earth's spheres — **atmosphere ↔ biosphere ↔ lithosphere and hydrosphere** — and the loop has no starting point. We begin with the moves that put carbon into the air.

## Sub lesson 1 — Carbon out: release

Two doors open **into** the atmosphere:

- **Respiration.** Every living thing that respires — you included, as your misted glass showed — releases **carbon dioxide** into the air.
- **Burning.** Fires burning wood, charcoal and fuels release carbon dioxide into the air too.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carbon in and around: uptake, eating, return and storage

- **Uptake — photosynthesis.** Green plants take carbon dioxide **out** of the air and build it into their own bodies. This taking-in is **photosynthesis**.
- **Eating.** Animals eat the plants, and the plants' carbon becomes part of the animals.
- **Return — death and decomposition.** When plants and animals die, they decompose — rot down — and their carbon returns to the ground and the air.
- **Storage — fossil fuels.** Some buried carbon does not return quickly. Pressed and stored deep for ages, it becomes **fossil fuels**. When those fuels are **burned**, their carbon is released into the air again — and the loop closes.

## Say it to your companion

Start at burning — a cooking fire. Walk your companion around the loop: where does the fire's carbon go, which plant takes it in, which animal eats the plant, and how does the carbon get back to a fire in the end? Your companion stops you at any point and asks: "Which door is that — into the air, or out of it?"

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five moves in loop order, starting at respiration: respiration → … → back to respiration.
2. A tree grows for fifty years, is cut, and is burned as firewood. Tell its carbon's story in four steps.
3. Explain why the carbon cycle is called a cycle and not a line: where does it "end"? (It does not — every ending is another move's beginning.)
