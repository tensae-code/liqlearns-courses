# Primary and secondary productivity

## Do it first
A field of grass and the herd that eats it are both growing — but their growth is not the same kind of making. Which one makes food from light and air, and which one remakes food already made? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 10 book measures an ecosystem by its productivity, in its ecology chapter after the food chains.

## Sub lesson 1 — Primary productivity — what the producers make

Mauritania's Grade 10 *Sciences Naturelles* book, in its ecology chapter, teaches **primary productivity** (printed p. 113): the making done by the producers — the green plants — which build living matter out of light, air, water and salts. It is called primary because it is first: every other living thing in the ecosystem lives on this making, directly or through a chain of eaters.

Productivity is a measure of making over time — how much living matter the producers build — and it sets the ceiling for everything above it in the food chain.

## Sub lesson 2 — Secondary productivity — what the consumers build

**Secondary productivity** is the making done by the consumers: the animals that eat the producers (or eat other animals) and build their own bodies from that food. It is second-hand making — nothing in it is new to the ecosystem; all of it was primary productivity first, passed up the food chain and rebuilt.

The pair together is the ecosystem's account book: primary productivity is the income, secondary productivity is what the consumers manage to build from that income — always the smaller figure, because eating and living spend most of what was made.

## The key ideas

- **Primary productivity**: the living matter built by the producers (green plants) — first, and the ceiling for all the rest.
- **Secondary productivity**: the living matter built by the consumers from the food they eat — second-hand making, always smaller.
- Mauritania's Grade 10 book teaches the pair in its ecology chapter, after food chains (printed p. 113).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why is secondary productivity always smaller than primary?" Answer from the lesson: Because consumers build only from what they eat of the producers' making, and eating and living spend most of it — what is rebuilt into animal bodies is the smaller remainder of the primary income.
