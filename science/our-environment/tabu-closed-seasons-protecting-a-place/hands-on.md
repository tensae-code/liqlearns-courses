# Hands-on — Tabu — closed seasons protecting a place

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Walk a place that is tired
Walk a garden, shore, reef edge or piece of bush you know that people take from often. Name two signs that the place is tired — plants thinned or cut short, animals or fish few or small, ground bare. Say which of them a closed time would let recover first.

## Put a small tabu at home
With your family's agreement, mark one small part of a garden or one fruit tree tabu for a set time — a ribbon, a stick sign, a stone border — and agree that nobody picks from it while the tabu stands. Write the start day on paper. Look at it each week and note one change: new shoots, flowers, fruit forming, birds or insects visiting.

## Draw the resting place
Draw the same place twice: on the left, the tired place before the tabu; on the right, the place after its closed time — plants grown back, animals returned. Label each drawing with the sub lesson it comes from.
