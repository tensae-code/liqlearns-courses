# Video script — Coral reefs — what they give and why they are being lost

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Coral reefs — what they give and why they are being lost** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**What the reefs give.** Oceans, lagoons and coral reefs stand under the Samoan way of life: fish and shellfish for food, sheltered lagoon water for travel and fishing, and the reef itself breaking the ocean's force before it reaches the shore. The lesson's writing task is the learner's own: the importance of the oceans, lagoons and reefs to the Samoan way of life, argued from what the learner knows (printed pp. 93–94, 98).

**Why the reefs are being lost.** The Global Coral Reef Monitoring Network's 2000 report found 27% of the world's reefs effectively lost. The 1998 El Niño/La Niña bleaching struck reefs worldwide. The human causes the report names: sediment and nutrient pollution, over-exploitation, and sand and rock mining and 'reclamation'. Against that world picture, the report places the Pacific: its reefs are in reasonably good health, protected by traditional management and marine protected areas — Samoa's own way of caring is part of the evidence. The book teaches the report by drawing bar graphs from its regional table (printed pp. 93–94, 98).

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
