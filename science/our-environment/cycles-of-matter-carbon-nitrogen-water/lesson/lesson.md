# Cycles of matter — carbon, nitrogen, water

## Do it first

Breathe in. Most of what you just breathed is nitrogen — about four-fifths of the air — yet your body cannot use one bit of it straight from the air. Plants cannot either. Somewhere between the air and a growing plant, nitrogen must be CHANGED. This lesson follows that change, step by working step.

## Sub lesson 1 — The nitrogen cycle — its working steps

Air is rich in **nitrogen gas**, but living things cannot use it in that form. First it must be **converted** — fixed — into **ammonia and nitrates**, forms plants can take in through their roots and build into protein.

Animals get their nitrogen by eating the plants (or eating animals that did). Then the return road: **decomposers** break the protein in wastes and dead bodies down into **ammonium compounds** in the soil.

The loop closes by **denitrification**: the reduction of nitrate back to inert nitrogen gas, performed by *Pseudomonas* and *Clostridium* species under anaerobic (no-oxygen) conditions, releasing the gas back to the air.

The carbon and oxygen cycles run beside it the same way — the carbon loop you walked (air → plant → animal → ground → air) and oxygen moving between breathing and photosynthesis — and water loops as evaporation, condensation, rain and collection.

## Sub lesson 2 — The contrast — matter cycles, energy flows

Put the cycles beside the food chain and the difference stands out. **Matter cycles**: the same carbon, nitrogen and water atoms are used, returned and used again — the ecosystem wastes almost nothing.

**Energy flows one way**: sunlight enters, passes up the trophic levels shrinking by the 10% rule, and leaves as heat. It is not recycled; the ecosystem needs the sun's new energy every day, but it does NOT need new nitrogen or carbon — it needs the cycles kept working.

That is why breaking a cycle — clearing the decomposers' soil life, for instance — starves a place even under full sunlight.
