# Lesson plan — Cycles of matter — carbon, nitrogen, water

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Cycles of matter — carbon, nitrogen, water
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Ecosystem and Conservation of Natural Resources, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** carbon, oxygen, nitrogen and water cycle round and round between the living and non-living parts of the ecosystem, while energy flows one way and is lost — matter is recycled, energy is not; the nitrogen cycle's working steps (conversion to ammonia and nitrates, decomposers making ammonium, denitrifying bacteria releasing nitrogen gas) carry the lesson.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Cycles — the carbon cycle and Cycles — the water cycle (this title), which walk those two loops leg by leg, and on Food chains, food webs and energy pyramids (this title, Grade 8) for energy's one-way flow; this lesson adds the nitrogen cycle in its working steps and states the contrast the two earlier cycles show. Teacher-guide evidence (item 14) adds the named bacteria, labelled as such in sources.

Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The nitrogen cycle — its working steps** — conversion to ammonia and nitrates; decomposers making ammonium; denitrification closing the loop.
2. **The contrast — matter cycles, energy flows** — materials are used again and again; energy passes one way and dissipates.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Trace nitrogen from the air into plants and animals and back to the air, naming the working steps.
2. Say what decomposers do in the nitrogen cycle, and what denitrifying bacteria do.
3. State the contrast: matter cycles, energy flows one way — and say why it matters.
## What you need
The carbon and water loops already walked; a bean or other legume plant to look at if one is near.
