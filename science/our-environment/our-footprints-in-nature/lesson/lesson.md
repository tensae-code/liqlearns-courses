# Our footprints in nature

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's title — Our footprints in nature. Say out loud anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on: this is Venezuela's way in — its Grade 8 *El Porvenir de la Vida* asks, in its ecology run, what marks human life leaves on nature: our footprints in nature.

## Sub lesson 1 — The marks we leave

Every life leaves **footprints in nature**: the resources it takes, the waste it leaves, the land and water it changes. The reading gathers the human impact into one picture — a footprint — so it can be seen whole, not act by act.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring, then reducing

A footprint can be **measured** — and what can be measured can be **reduced**. The book's order is the lesson's order: first read your impact honestly, then lessen it, choice by choice, in the community that shares the same nature.

## The key ideas

- **The marks we leave** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Measuring, then reducing** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — The marks we leave first, then Measuring, then reducing. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is our footprint in nature, and what is done with it?" Answer: It is the human impact a life leaves on nature — the resources taken, waste left and land and water changed. It is measured, and then reduced.
