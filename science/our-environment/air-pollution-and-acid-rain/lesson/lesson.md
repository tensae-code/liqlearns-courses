# Air pollution and acid rain

## Do it first

Stand downwind of a cooking fire or a busy road for a moment and breathe carefully. The sharpness you feel is the air carrying things that are not air. Some of those things are specks so small they can settle deep in your lungs. Others are gases that will come back down — in rain that is acid. This lesson follows both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Polluted air — and what enters the lungs

Air serves every living thing for breathing, and its uses run through the whole living world — which is why loading it with harmful matter, **air pollution**, reaches everything.

Fumes from factories, vehicles and fires carry **particulates** — tiny solid specks. Breathed in, the smallest lodge in the **lungs** and harm breathing and health over time.

The same fumes carry gases, and two of them start the second half of this lesson: **sulphur dioxide** and **nitrogen oxides**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Acid rain — from fume to damage

**Acid rain** forms when sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides join **water vapour** in the air and fall with rain as a weak acid.

Its harms are named one by one: it **strips nutrients from soil**, so fields feed crops less; it **blocks leaf stomata**, the leaf's breathing pores; it **degrades chlorophyll**, the green matter that catches sunlight, so plants weaken.

In lakes it **harms lake life** — fish and the small living things of the water. And on towns it **eats buildings** — stone and metal surfaces are slowly dissolved and stained.

Follow the chain backwards and the cure's address is clear: the damage lands far away, but it starts at the fume.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — polluted air — and what enters the lungs first, then acid rain — from fume to damage. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Show me where that is in the picture." Answer by pointing, not by repeating.
