| atmospheric contamination defined: the presence of substances or materials in the air that, in determined quantities, imply a risk, damage or grave molestation for living beings; it can reduce visibility and produce disagreeable odours; the increase of carbon dioxide emissions proceeding from fossil fuels and of methane proceeding from livestock accentuates the greenhouse effect, and the nitrogen or sulphur oxides proceeding from the chimneys of industries cause acid rain (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 58 |
| contamination can be natural or anthropogenic (influence of the human being): natural contamination is produced by volcanic eruptions, unprovoked forest fires or the biological activity of living beings; this type of contamination has existed always and planet Earth is capable of self-regulating; in contrast, contamination by human activity is the most damaging and difficult to regulate, and the industrial processes and the burning of fossil fuels are the principal foci of contamination (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 58 |
| acid deposition contributes to the reduction of the pH in the terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems and moreover permits the mobilisation of toxic metals, especially aluminium; this occasions a variety of effects: damage to forests, soils, fish and other living beings, and also to the construction materials and to human health; acid rain reduces visibility (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 73 |
| acid rain is an indicator of the quality of the air; the term acid rain comprises both the precipitation or humid deposition of acid substances dissolved in the rain water, snow and hail, and the dry precipitation, where the aerosols or acid gaseous compounds are deposited as ashes, soot or as gases in the soil, in the leaves of the trees and in the surfaces of the materials; in reality these particles do not have acid character while they are in the atmosphere, but when they enter in contact with the mist, the dew or the surface water, they convert into acids and have effects similar to those of the humid precipitation (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 74 |
| the history the guide adds: the English chemist Robert Angus Smith in 1872 was who gave the name of acid rain to this type of destructive precipitation; but only in the Conference of Stockholm in 1972 was the theme spoken of for the first time, and at the start of the decade of the 70 there were already alarming figures of contamination of rivers and forests of the industrialised countries; in 1983 many industrialised nations recognised the terrible threat and agreed to restrict the contamination by sulphur dioxide — although today it is surpassed by the nitrogen oxides (NOx), proceeding from the escapes of motor vehicles and domestic and industrial sources (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 74 |
| the presence of acid rain is diminished in the measure that the precipitations are greater: that is, the more the quantity of rain, the more the acidity diminishes; depending on the intensity and duration of the rain an atmospheric washing is realised, which ends with the transport of the contaminating elements toward the earth, where their effects are felt; the guide prints the set of reactions by which the sulphur and nitrogen oxides combine with water during the formation of acid rain (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 74 |
| the chalk experiment explained: on placing the chalk in the glass of lemon juice an acid-base reaction is produced; the lemon juice contains citric acid (C6H8O7) and on reacting with the calcium carbonate forms water (H2O), carbon dioxide (CO2) and calcium citrate (Ca3(C6H5O7)2); in the experiment it will be seen that the carbon dioxide is the bubbles that the reaction liberates and the calcium citrate is the white mass that stays over the lemon juice; in the glass of vinegar something very similar happens, and the plant that is watered with acid water will wilt by the effect of the acidity (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 75 |
| more effects: some effects of acid rain are the impossibility of survival of the fauna for lack of food; acid rain penetrates in the earth and affects the roots and turns the leaves yellowish, generating a poisoning of the sap that ends with the death of plants and trees; in human beings there is an increase of respiratory infections and an increase of cancer cases, due to the decrease of defences and a greater propensity to contract contagious diseases (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 76 |
| the final answers: the solutions can be acidic, basic or neutral; the oxides that combine with the water and form acid rain are the nitrogen oxides and the sulphur oxides; the acids present in acid rain are sulphuric acid and nitric acid; the sources that emit the oxides causing acid rain: energy centrals (centrales energéticas), factories and vehicle emissions; and the damage by area: buildings, monuments and rock structures are weakened, corroded and deteriorated; rivers and lakes suffer acidification provoking an disequilibrium in the aquatic ecosystem; in fish, the great concentrations of acid kill the small fish on leaving their eggs; in birds, the eggshells (cascarones) are affected, becoming more fragile and breaking easily, and the small birds that manage to be born can have deformed bones or die; in forests, it affects the trees and weakens them in such a way that they die from the attack of insects and fungi (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 76 |
