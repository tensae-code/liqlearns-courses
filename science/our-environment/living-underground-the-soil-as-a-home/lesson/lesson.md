# Living underground — the soil as a home

## Do it first
Before you read: look at the ground under your feet. Everything you can see lives on top of it. Could anything live inside it — under the surface, in the soil itself? Say what you think, and why. Then read on: this is Moldova's way in — its Grade 3 Științe (Sciences) book teaches the living environments one by one, and one whole lesson of that module is the underground living environment, Mediul de viață subteran.

## Sub lesson 1 — The underground living environment

A **living environment** is the place where a living thing lives — its home in nature. Most homes we name stand in the open: on the land, in the air above it, in the water. The underground is a home too.

The **underground living environment** is the home below the surface: the soil itself. The living things of the underground live their lives in the earth, out of sight under the ground we walk on. Their home is not on the soil — it is in it.

## Sub lesson 2 — One environment among the others

Moldova's Grade 3 book does not leave the underground mixed into the others. Its living-environments module (Modulul 2, Mediile de viață) takes the homes in order: the land-and-air living environment, then the underground living environment, then the water living environment, then the human being's own living environments.

The underground is counted as a home of its own because what lives there lives in the soil — not on the land, not in the air, and not in the water. A home is known by where its living things actually live, and the underground's living things live below the surface.

## The key ideas

- A living environment is the place where a living thing lives — its home in nature.
- The underground living environment is the soil itself as a home, below the surface.
- Moldova's Grade 3 module teaches the environments one by one — land-and-air, underground, water, human — and the underground stands as a home of its own.
