# Lesson plan — Air pollution — what dirties the air

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Air pollution — what dirties the air
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment (CURRICULUM.md Unit 11), beside Pollution and its four kinds and Caring for our environment — soil, water and air: this is the primary-depth lesson on the air kind alone — what dirties it, and what clean air asks of us
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** clean air is invisible and free of bad smells, but smoke, dust, exhaust fumes and bad smells dirty it — this pollution comes mostly from what people burn (wood fires, rubbish, fuel in engines) and from dust raised on dry roads; dirty air hurts lungs, eyes and plants, and it is prevented at the source: burn less, burn cleaner, never burn rubbish in the open, and let trees do their cleaning work
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Pollution and its four kinds is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **What dirties the air — smoke, dust and fumes** — air pollution is anything harmful mixed into the air: smoke from fires and burning rubbish, exhaust fumes from vehicles and generators, dust from dry roads and bare ground, and chemical smells from factories and sprays; the dirtiest air is made by burning — nearly every source is something people light, pour or drive
2. **What dirty air does — and keeping air clean** — dirty air makes breathing harder, stings eyes, worsens coughs and asthma, coats plants so they grow poorly, and dirties homes and water it settles into; air is cleaned by stopping the dirt at its source — burn rubbish never, cook with less smoke, keep engines maintained, plant and protect trees, whose leaves trap dust and refresh the air

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Pollution and its four kinds**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Air pollution is harmful stuff mixed into the air: smoke, exhaust fumes, dust and chemical gases.
2. Say, in their own words: The greatest source is burning — fires, rubbish, charcoal, engines and generators.
3. Say, in their own words: Dirty air hurts lungs and eyes, worsens coughs and asthma, and coats plants and homes.
4. Say, in their own words: Clean air is made at the source: do not burn rubbish, let cooking smoke escape, care for engines.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Your eyes and nose, a walk around your home or school, a white cloth or tissue, and paper and pencil for the pollution hunt.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the grey smear on your cloth fell out of the air. Pollution is whatever that smear is made of, while it is still floating.
- Thinks smoke disappears when it fades → it only spreads thinner. Faded smoke is pollution shared with the whole neighbourhood.
- Thinks air pollution is only factories → the guide's own list starts at home: cooking smoke and burned rubbish dirty more children's air than any factory.
