# Air pollution — what dirties the air

## Do it first
Before you read: take a clean white cloth or tissue and wipe it across a windowsill, a wall near a road, or the leaf of a plant by a path. Look at what came off on the cloth. That grey smear was floating in the air before it settled — you have been breathing the air it fell out of. Now read what air pollution is, and where that smear comes from.

## Sub lesson 1 — What dirties the air — smoke, dust and fumes

**Air pollution** means the air has harmful things mixed into it. The biggest family is **smoke**: from cooking fires, from rubbish burned in the open, from charcoal and from bush fires. Smoke is not just grey air — it carries tiny specks of half-burned material, small enough to float for hours and to enter lungs.

The second family is **fumes** — the gases pouring from vehicle exhausts, generators and factory chimneys, smelling sharp and making eyes water. The third is **dust**: fine soil lifted by wind and traffic from dry, bare ground, coating leaves and sills — the grey smear on your cloth. Different dirt, one lesson: almost all of it comes from what people **burn, drive or leave bare**.

## Sub lesson 2 — What dirty air does — and keeping air clean

Breathing dirty air day after day **hurts the body**: it stings eyes, starts coughs, makes asthma and chest sickness worse, and tires the lungs — children and old people first. It also **coats plant leaves** with dust, dimming the sunlight they feed on, and everything it settles on — roofs, washing, stored water — gets dirty with it.

So air is kept clean **at the source**. Never burn rubbish in the open — bury or bin it instead. Cook so the smoke escapes away from faces, and use dry fuel that smokes less. Engines that are cared for fume less. And **trees are air-cleaners on duty**: their leaves trap dust and their shade cools the ground — planting and protecting them is pollution work, not decoration. The rule is the whole lesson: dirty air is made by people, so people can stop making it.

## The key ideas

- **Air pollution** is harmful stuff mixed into the air: smoke, exhaust fumes, dust and chemical gases.
- The greatest source is **burning** — fires, rubbish, charcoal, engines and generators.
- Dirty air hurts **lungs and eyes**, worsens coughs and asthma, and coats plants and homes.
- Clean air is made **at the source**: do not burn rubbish, let cooking smoke escape, care for engines.
- **Trees clean air** — leaves trap dust; planting and protecting trees is anti-pollution work.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion through the three families of air dirt — smoke, fumes, dust — with one real example of each from your own neighbourhood. If your companion can name one change that cleans air at its source, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do a pollution hunt: list every source of smoke, fumes or dust you can find between home and school, and mark which family each belongs to.
2. Wipe a white cloth on a leaf near a road and on a leaf far from the road. Compare the smears and explain the difference.
3. Teach the picture: tell someone at home why burning rubbish in the yard dirties the whole street's air — and what to do with rubbish instead.
