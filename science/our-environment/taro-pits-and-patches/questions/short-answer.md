# Taro pits and patches — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must a low-island farmer dig a pit instead of planting on the surface?
**Answer: Because the low island's surface is dry sand; the wet ground is underground, so the pit is dug down to reach it — on the lowest islands down to the freshwater lens — where the taro's roots can stay wet.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the freshwater lens?
**Answer: Rain water that has soaked through the island's sand and gathered underground as a layer of fresh water — the wetness the taro pits are dug down to.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the living things the taro patch shelters, besides the taro.
**Answer: Shrimp, crabs and fish live in the pits' water, and young dragonflies and mosquitoes begin their lives there.**
