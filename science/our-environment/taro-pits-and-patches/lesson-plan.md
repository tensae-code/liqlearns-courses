# Lesson plan — Taro pits and patches

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Our Environment · **Lesson:** Taro pits and patches
**Place in the curriculum:** Our Environment — appended to Unit 11 (Our Environment) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a taro patch is — pits dug down to wet ground, on the lowest islands down to the freshwater lens itself, where giant swamp taro grows and a whole habitat of small water animals lives — Micronesia's own way, in its PCEP low-island readers for Grades 3–5 and 6–8
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **A pit dug down to the wet ground** — On a low island there is no river and no wet valley. The wet ground is under the island: rain soaks through the sand and gathers as a freshwater lens — a layer of fresh water resting underground.
2. **The patch as a habitat** — A taro patch is not only a farm. The standing wet ground of the pits is a habitat — a home with food and water where young are raised.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks a taro patch is a field on ordinary soil → return to sub lesson 1: on a low island the wet ground is underground, so the farmer digs down to it — the pit is the field.
- Mixes the freshwater lens up with the sea → return to sub lesson 1: the lens is fresh water gathered from rain under the island, and it is that fresh wetness the taro roots reach.
