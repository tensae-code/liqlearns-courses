# Communities in fresh and salt water

## Do it first
Before you read: a river and the sea are both water, full of life. But the life is not the same life. What is different about the water itself — and how would that change who can live there? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Bulgaria's way in — its Grade 4 book walks the water communities in two stops, fresh first, then salt.

## Sub lesson 1 — Communities in fresh water

Bulgaria's Grade 4 book's Lesson 49, *Communities in the water environment*, begins with **fresh waters** — rivers and lakes.

Its named inhabitants: **water lilies** among the plants; **carp, catfish and pike** among the fish; and **herons** at the water's edge. Plant, fish and bird together make the community — each kind in the place the fresh water gives it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Communities in salt water

Then the book turns to **salt waters** — the sea.

Here the named life is different: **algae living near the sunlit surface**, where the light reaches, and **plankton** — the tiny drifting life of the sea. The salt community is read from its surface down: light at the top, the algae that use it, and the plankton the sea's food relations begin from. Fresh and salt are both water communities — and no two of their named inhabitants lists are the same.

## The key ideas

- **Fresh-water** community in Bulgaria's book: **water lilies, carp, catfish, pike, herons**.
- **Salt-water** community: **algae near the sunlit surface**, and **plankton**.
- The water itself — fresh or salt — decides who can live in the community.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why do the algae live near the surface of the sea?" Answer: Because that is where the sunlight reaches — the book places them in the sunlit surface water.
