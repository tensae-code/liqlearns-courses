# Caring for our surroundings, and reusing what we throw away

## Do it first
A plastic bottle lies by the path. One child sees rubbish. Another sees a plant pot, a funnel, a seedling guard. The difference is a taught eye. This lesson teaches the carer's five community habits — and the reuser's eye that sees treasure in the thrown-away.

## The five care ways, and the reuser's eye

The Grade 2 guide states the matter plainly: our natural resources are being badly damaged for various reasons, so we must protect them. Its **main ways of caring for the environment**: **planting and tending trees; disposing of waste properly; preventing forest fire; taking part in cleanliness campaigns; and creating awareness in the community**.

With it comes the unit's reuse competency: **telling apart materials that can be reused or recycled, and imagining useful things made from discarded materials**.

The Grade 1 guide works the same field at home scale: **our homes and surroundings produce leftover waste every day that must be disposed of carefully** — and its assessment is beautifully practical: pupils **keep their school clean in group work** and **present how waste can be reused (መልሶ መጠቀም)**. Care, in both guides, is a thing done with hands and neighbours, not a thing only said.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion the five care ways, then play the reuser's game: they name a discarded thing, you must imagine its useful second life in ten seconds — or forfeit to their imagination.

## Do It Yourself
1. Bottle-into-pot build: reuse one discarded bottle as a plant pot — the guide's own example — plant in it, and tend what grows.
2. Cleanliness campaign: organise the group clean-up of one shared place, the Grade 1 guide's assessment done for real, and present your reuse ideas to the group.
3. Forest-fire talk: ask an elder how forest fire starts here and how it is prevented. Report under the guide's heading: prevention.
