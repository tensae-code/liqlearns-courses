## Sub lesson 1 — Symbiosis in its three kinds

symbiosis in its three kinds: mutualism, where both gain (the bird that picks insects off the zebra's skin); commensalism, where one gains and the other is unaffected (birds nesting in trees); parasitism, where one gains at the other's expense (skin insects feeding on the zebra)

## Sub lesson 2 — The Friendship of the Insect and the Tree

science writing: "The Friendship of the Insect and the Tree" — the yucca tree cannot seed without the yucca moth, which carries its pollen flower to flower; the moth lays its eggs inside the flower, and its young feed on some of the tree's seeds while the tree gives them food and shelter — neither can live without the other; the passage doubles as the book's model of explanatory writing (main idea supported by facts and details)
