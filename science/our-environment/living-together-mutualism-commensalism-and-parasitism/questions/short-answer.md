# Living together — mutualism, commensalism and parasitism — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Symbiosis in its three kinds.
**Answer:** symbiosis in its three kinds: mutualism, where both gain (the bird that picks insects off the zebra's skin); commensalism, where one gains and the other is unaffected (birds nesting in trees); parasitism, where one gains at the other's expense (skin insects feeding on the zebra).

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: The Friendship of the Insect and the Tree.
**Answer:** science writing: "The Friendship of the Insect and the Tree" — the yucca tree cannot seed without the yucca moth, which carries its pollen flower to flower; the moth lays its eggs inside the flower, and its young feed on some of the tree's seeds while the tree gives them food and shelter — neither can live without the other; the passage doubles as the book's model of explanatory writing (main idea supported by facts and details).

**Answer:** Symbiosis in its three kinds → The Friendship of the Insect and the Tree — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
