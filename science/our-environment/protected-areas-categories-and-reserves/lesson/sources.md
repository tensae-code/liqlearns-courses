| a management plan (plan de manejo) sets a protected area's objectives and actions for protection, conservation, restoration and use, and its zoning orders the space: the core zone (zona núcleo) is absolute conservation, while the buffer zone (zona de amortiguamiento) promotes conservation with sustainable multiple use and local social, economic and cultural development (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 188 |

| Wildlife Refuge (essential protection for defined species, assuring the perpetuity of species, populations and habitats, with controlled use of some resources — Cuero y Salado, Atlántida) and Natural Monument (one or more outstanding natural features of national interest, protected for their unique character — the Taulabé caves) (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 193 |
| Biological Reserve (areas that cannot be altered, holding ecosystems or flora and fauna of scientific value in an unaltered state for research), Anthropological Reserve (areas inhabited by indigenous peoples living in balance with nature — the Pech Montaña El Carbón Anthropological and Forest Reserve, Olancho) and Marine National Park (intertidal and underwater areas protecting water, flora, fauna and historic-cultural resources — Cayos Miskitos Marine National Park, Caribbean) (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 193 |
| how an area gets protected: any interested person may petition; the declaration is made by Executive Agreement through the natural-resources secretariat, proposed by the Forestry Administration (now the ICF) and approved by the National Congress (SINAPH Regulation, articles 33–34), with the petition stating justifications, objectives, location, land tenure, proposed category and the resources to protect (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 194 |
| the Río Plátano Biosphere: the largest forest area in Honduras, with swamps, mangroves, pine forests, savannas and tropical rainforest, home to diverse cultures living in balance with nature, declared a UNESCO World Heritage site and part of the Mesoamerican Biological Corridor (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 197 |
| the Tawahka Asangni Biosphere (Olancho, Gracias a Dios, Colón and El Paraíso, between the Wampú, Patuca and Segovia rivers): part of the country's cultural and archaeological heritage, inhabited by Tawahka communities who protect it through their federation, FITH (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 197 |
| biological corridors: delimited spaces that connect landscapes, ecosystems and habitats so species can move between natural areas instead of being isolated, maintaining biodiversity and ecological and evolutionary processes; the Mesoamerican Biological Corridor runs from southern Mexico through Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua and Costa Rica to Panama (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 198 |
| the Honduran Caribbean Biological Corridor: eight interconnected protected areas between the Guatemalan border and the Río Plátano Biosphere — Punta Izopo, Texiguat, Capiro y Calentura, the Lancetilla Botanical Garden, Cuero y Salado, Jeannette Kawas, Guaimoreto Lagoon and Pico Bonito, across Colón, Atlántida, Yoro and Cortés (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 198 |
| the legal frame: the Honduran Constitution charges the State with conserving the environment through technical, rational exploitation of natural resources, and the Forest, Protected Areas and Wildlife Law (Decree 156-2007, published in La Gaceta on 26 February 2008) sets the principles — legal security of forest property, sustainable management through plans by category, science-based development, conservation of protected areas and their genetic potential and water resources, public participation and co-management, obtaining environmental goods and services, and national priority for natural regeneration, afforestation and reforestation (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 207 |
| the international treaties Honduras has signed, each with its objective: the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, the Kyoto Protocol (sustainable development, cutting greenhouse-gas emissions), the Convention on Biological Diversity (conserve diversity, use it sustainably, share the benefits of genetic resources fairly), the Vienna Convention (protect health and environment from ozone-layer changes), Ramsar (prevent the loss of wetlands), the Inter-American Convention for the Protection and Conservation of Sea Turtles, the Convention to Combat Desertification, and CITES (protect wild species against overexploitation through international trade) (Honduras — HN CN6) | Honduras, *HN CN6*, printed p. 208 |

| a protected area is a space created by different associations and authorities of a country; efforts are articulated to guarantee the conservation of the biodiversity and the maintenance of the ecological processes necessary for its preservation and the development of the human being; the protected areas contribute to the conservation of the natural and cultural patrimony of a country and help reduce the pressures caused by some human activities on these places — in them the environmental impact is much reduced (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 157 |
| the importance of protected areas for the economy: their principal objective is to care for the distinct species of living beings, principally the flora and fauna in danger of extinction, and the ecosystems in general; besides protecting living beings, protected areas also favour the country's economy — a good administration of these areas brings economic benefits to Honduras; according to the report of Rationalisation of the National System of Protected Areas of Honduras, 41% of the tourists arriving in Honduras come for business, 32% for vacations and 26% to visit family and friends, and the majority of tourists coming to spend vacations in Honduras seek non-traditional activities such as diving (buceo), archaeology, raft trips (viajes en balsa) and exploration of nature; nevertheless many business travellers and those visiting family incorporate a walk (paseo) in their visit, and most visitors end up doing more than a walk or excursion during their stay (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 158 |
| protected areas are also excellent places to realise distinct scientific investigations, and in some cases the quality facilities for investigators in the protected areas are in growing demand; per the Society of Ecotourism (1998) as cited, North Americans usually go out to make a walk (caminata), preferably by natural trails where they can observe wildlife, and naturally the national parks play an important role in this field (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN8 Guide) | Honduras, *CN8 Guide*, guide PDF p. 158 |
