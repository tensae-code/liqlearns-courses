# Decomposers — rotting and recycling in nature

## Do it first
A dead leaf falls in a wood. A dead branch lies beside it. A year later both are mostly gone. Where did they go — who took them? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Iceland's forest chapter teaches the takers — the rotting crew of the forest floor.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rotting — dead matter broken down

Dead matter in the forest does not pile up forever: it **rots** — it is broken down, piece by piece, until it becomes part of the soil again. The guide teaches rotting and digestion together in the forest floor: the forest's own recycling, returning dead things to the ground that feeds the living trees.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rotting crew — fungi and many-legged soil animals

The breakers-down are named: **fungi**, and the **many-legged soil animals — millipedes** among them — working through the dead matter of the forest floor. Fungi as a kind of living thing are stocked in `animals/fungi-moulds-mushrooms-and-yeasts`; this lesson's one thing is the rotting work itself, and who does it.

## The key ideas

- Rotting is dead matter being broken down until it becomes part of the soil again — nature's recycling.
- The forest floor's rotting crew: fungi and many-legged soil animals such as millipedes.
- Iceland teaches rotting and digestion together, in the forest floor chapter.

## Say it to your companion
