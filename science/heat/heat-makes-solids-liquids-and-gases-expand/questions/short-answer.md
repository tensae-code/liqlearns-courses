# Heat makes solids, liquids and gases expand — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: solids expand.
**Answer: A heated metal bar grows longer; a tight metal lid loosens in hot water because the metal has expanded more than the glass. The growth is small but real, which is why bridges and rails are built with gaps for hot days.**

**2.** Explain: liquids expand more.
**Answer: Heated water rises in a narrow tube — the working of every thermometer: the liquid expands with heat and climbs, and its height reads the temperature. Liquids expand more than solids for the same heating, and their expansion can be seen and measured.**

**3.** Explain: gases expand most — and states change.
**Answer: Air in a bottle with a balloon over its neck inflates the balloon when warmed: gases expand most of all. Keep heating, and expansion gives way to change of state — ice melts, water boils away as steam — while cooling reverses both: contraction, condensing, freezing.**
