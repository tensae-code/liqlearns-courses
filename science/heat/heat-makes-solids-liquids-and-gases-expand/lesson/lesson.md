# Heat makes solids, liquids and gases expand

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Heat makes solids, liquids and gases expand. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Nepal's Grade 4 My Science, Health and Physical Education book teaches energy as heat through one visible rule: heating makes things grow bigger, in all three states of matter, and enough heating changes the state itself.

In your mind's eye: when a solid, a liquid or a gas is heated, it expands — it takes up more space — and when it cools, it contracts; heat also drives the changes of state that turn ice to water and water to steam. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Solids expand.** A heated metal bar grows longer; a tight metal lid loosens in hot water because the metal has expanded more than the glass. The growth is small but real, which is why bridges and rails are built with gaps for hot days.

**Liquids expand more.** Heated water rises in a narrow tube — the working of every thermometer: the liquid expands with heat and climbs, and its height reads the temperature. Liquids expand more than solids for the same heating, and their expansion can be seen and measured.

**Gases expand most — and states change.** Air in a bottle with a balloon over its neck inflates the balloon when warmed: gases expand most of all. Keep heating, and expansion gives way to change of state — ice melts, water boils away as steam — while cooling reverses both: contraction, condensing, freezing.
