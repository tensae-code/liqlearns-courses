# Heat — radiation and the black/white test

## Do it first

The Sun warms your face across a distance that is — between the Sun and you — almost entirely empty space. No contact, so not conduction. No fluid out there, so not convection. Something still carried the heat. And a second puzzle, closer: two cars in the same sun, one black, one white. Open both doors at noon. One is an oven. The other is merely hot. Same sun. Different surfaces.

## Sub lesson 1 — Radiation — heat with no medium at all

**Radiation transfers heat by electromagnetic waves**: it needs **no contact and no particles**, and works **even through a vacuum** — which is how the Sun's heat crosses empty space, as neither conduction nor convection can. **Every hot object radiates**: hands held near (not on) a red-hot heater warm this way, and food in a microwave warms this way too (Activity 7.21 argues it from the home fire). A room heated by charcoal is keyed as convection and radiation together — the air circulates, and the glow radiates (teacher evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — Black heats faster — the fair test

Surfaces differ at receiving radiation: **absorption** hands the wave's energy to the surface as heat — **black absorbs well and heats fast; white and silver reflect and heat slowly**, which is why a white car's interior stays cooler than a black car's in the same sun. Test it fairly (Activity 7.22): identical water bottles wrapped in black and white cloth, in direct sun, temperatures read **every 30 minutes for two hours** and **line-graphed** — the two lines diverge, black climbing faster. End where the book ends: dress and car-buying advice that you must justify **from your data**, not from the lesson's say-so.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why the Sun's heat cannot be conduction or convection, which colour heats faster and why, and the advice your graph earns (dark or light clothes at noon? which car?).

## Do It Yourself

1. The black/white test (Activity 7.22): run the full two hours, five readings per bottle, one line graph with both lines. Conclude in one sentence from the graph alone.
2. Advice page: from your data, write justified advice for (a) a market trader choosing a shirt colour, (b) a family choosing between a black and a white car. Each advice must cite a reading.
3. Home-fire argument (Activity 7.21): sit near a fire WITHOUT touching anything warm and without air blowing on you (still evening). Explain, in the lesson's terms, how the warmth reached you.
