# Specific heat capacity — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Equal heat, unequal warming.
**Answer:** Inside its Heat and Temperature unit, Eritrea's Grade 8 book teaches **specific heat** (printed p. 125) as its own topic, after heat's sources, heat's transfer and temperature itself. Its starting observation is one any kitchen shows: give the same heat to the same mass of different materials and they do **not** warm by the same amount. A metal pan is too hot to touch minutes before the water in it is even warm — the two took the same fire very differently. Materials differ in how much heat a temperature rise costs them.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Specific heat — the heat one kilogram needs for one degree.
**Answer:** That cost is measured as the material's **specific heat capacity**: the heat needed to raise the temperature of **one kilogram** of the material by **one degree Celsius**. A high specific heat capacity — water is the standing example — means the material warms slowly and cools slowly, soaking up much heat for little rise; a low one means it heats and cools fast. The number explains everyday things: why coastal places swing less in temperature than inland ones, and why water, not sand or iron, carries heat around a house or an engine.

**3.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Equal heat, unequal warming → Specific heat — the heat one kilogram needs for one degree — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
