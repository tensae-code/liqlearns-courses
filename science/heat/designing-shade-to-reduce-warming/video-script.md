# Video script — Designing shade to reduce warming

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Designing shade to reduce warming** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Kindergarten).

The problem is named first: **sunlight warms** the surfaces it falls on — the eating tables, the ground, a dog, a toy left in the sun (Kindergarten, K.1.4, printed p. 39). The warming is the thing to reduce, and it comes from the sunlight itself, so the solution must stand between the sunlight and the area.

The solution is **shade**: something placed so its shadow falls on the area to be kept cool. The book's way is the designer's way — **design** the shade, then **sketch or model** it before building: the drawing or model shows what will shade what, and can be judged and changed on paper first.

A shade design is **tested**: put it in place and compare — the shaded area against the same area in full sun. If the shaded area warms less, the design does its job; if not, the design, not the idea of shade, is what changes. Testing is what turns a drawing into a solution.
