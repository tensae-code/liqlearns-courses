# Expansion and contraction — when things heat and cool

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — expansion and contraction — when things heat and cool. Then read on: this is Seychelles' way in — the National Institute of Pedagogy's Integrated Science unit booklets (Unit 10).

## Sub lesson 1 — Heated things expand

Seychelles' Unit 10 Topic 2 states the rule plainly: metals and liquids expand when heated and contract when cooled. The topic's bar-breaking apparatus proves the force of it — a heated bar lengthens so much that cooling it can snap a strong bar. Expansion is small in amount and enormous in force.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cooled things contract — and the thermometer uses both

The same rule is a tool: a thermometer works because its liquid expands up the tube as it warms and contracts down as it cools. Every gap left in a bridge or a rail, and every loose jar lid freed under hot water, is this topic at work — things are built and handled knowing they will change size with heat.
