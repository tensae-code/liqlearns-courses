# Lesson plan — Hot and cold — temperature

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Heat · **Lesson:** Hot and cold — temperature
**Place in the curriculum:** Heat — appended to Unit 22 (Heat) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** temperature itself, at first depth — that things are not all equally warm, how we tell hotter from colder, and what a change of temperature does — Iran's way, from the Grade 1 cold-and-hot world (the water-bottle figure in the freezer, the ice figure and the snowman) through the Grade 4 forehead touch that cannot judge precisely, to the Grade 5 frozen bottle that shows temperature change splitting rock
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Hotter and colder — not everything is equally warm** — Iran's Grade 1 book opens the cold-and-hot world by asking where it is warmer, and sets a water-bottle figure in a freezer beside an ice figure and a snowman: cold and hot are not two boxes but a scale — a thing can be warmer than one thing and colder than another. Warmth has degrees, and the degrees can be compared.
2. **Feeling is not measuring** — When Sara tells her mother "I am very hot", her mother puts a hand on her forehead — Iran's Grade 4 book uses that moment to show the limit of touch: the sense of touch cannot say precisely which body is hotter than another. Feeling gives a rough answer; a number needs a thermometer (taught in its own lesson).
3. **What a change of temperature does** — Change the temperature and the thing itself changes: ice figures melt as they warm, and water that freezes takes more room — Iran's Grade 5 book fills a plastic bottle with water and freezes it to show the force that, repeated in cracks, splits rocks apart over long years.

- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Hotter and colder — not everything is equally warm.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Feeling is not measuring.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: What a change of temperature does.
