# Heat — convection currents in liquids and gases

## Do it first

Heat water gently in a beaker with a pinch of sawdust in it and watch the specks. They do not sit still, and they do not wander randomly: they ride a loop — up over the heat, across the top, down the cool side, along the bottom, up again. A loop that carries heat with it. Where does the loop get its push?

## Sub lesson 1 — The convection loop

In **fluids (liquids and gases)** the particles move freely. Warmed fluid **expands, grows lighter and rises**, while **cooler, heavier fluid sinks** to take its place — and that circulation, a **convection current**, carries the heat around. Sawdust in a gently heated beaker traces the loop for the eye (Activity 7.19): rising over the flame, sinking at the cool side — drawn and explained as a labelled diagram, up the hot side, across, down the cool side, under, and up again. A room heated by charcoal is keyed in the guide as convection and radiation working together (teacher evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — The two-bottle exchange

Prove the current with the two-bottle test (Activity 7.20): cold coloured water in one bottle, hot water in another, the cold bottle inverted over the hot through a sheet of newspaper, the sheet drawn out — **predict first**: will the coloured water stay put, sink, or exchange? Watch the exchange happen and conclude from what you saw: the warm water rises into the upper bottle and the cold coloured water sinks, the convection current running between the bottles. State the general rule your two activities share: convection needs a fluid, and it runs because warm fluid is lighter than cold.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the loop's four legs (up, across, down, under), why the rising leg rises, and what the two-bottle test showed that the beaker only suggested.

## Do It Yourself

1. Diagram the beaker (Activity 7.19): draw the sawdust loop with arrows and label ‘warmed — expands — lighter — rises’ and ‘cools — heavier — sinks’ on the right legs.
2. Predict-and-watch record (Activity 7.20): write your two-bottle prediction BEFORE the sheet is drawn, then the observation, then the conclusion — three separate lines.
3. Room currents: a charcoal stove or heater warms a room. Sketch where the warm air goes and where the cool air comes from, using the loop. Mark where you would sit to warm fastest.
