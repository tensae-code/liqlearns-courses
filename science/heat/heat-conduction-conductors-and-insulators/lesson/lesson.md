# Heat — conduction, conductors and insulators

## Do it first

Hold a cup of hot tea. The cup warms your hands; the tea cools towards your hands' temperature. Iron trousers with a hot iron; walk barefoot on hot asphalt at noon. In every case something is moving — always the same direction. Before the names arrive, draw the arrow for each case: which way does the warmth go?

## Sub lesson 1 — Heat flows hot to cold — and conduction is contact

**Heat is transfer driven by temperature difference**: matter's particles hold kinetic and potential (thermal) energy, and heat moves **from the hotter to the cooler until temperatures equalise**, by three methods this title takes in turn. **Conduction is transfer through direct contact** — stove to pot, hot coffee to cup and spoon, warm water to cold hands (and cold water drawing heat OUT of warm hands the other way: the flow is always warm to cool, never the reverse). Narrate the three contact cases (Activity 7.16) — hot tea held, ironing trousers, bare feet on hot asphalt — with the flow arrow drawn each time, always hot to cold. The guide carries the same idea through the hands themselves: hands from cold and hot water feel the same mixed water differently, and that transferred energy is what is named heat (teacher evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting materials: conductors and insulators

Two investigations sort the materials:

- **Five cups** — aluminium, steel, plastic, glass, wood/wancha — filled with boiled water and ranked warmest to coldest by touch (Activity 7.17).
- **Wax spots** timed as they melt along **aluminium, steel, copper and iron rods** on one hot plate, graphed as bars (Activity 7.18 — a full fair-test write-up: question, variables, aim, prediction, conclusion).

The verdicts: **metals conduct — copper and aluminium best** — hence cooking pots; **wood and plastic insulate** — hence pot handles, the cloth used to lift a hot pan, geyser blankets and thick clothing. The guide's local keys agree: metal pans are fitted with insulating handles, and cotton gabi clothing insulates against the cold (teacher evidence).

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the one direction heat always flows, what conduction needs (contact), the rod-test order, and why a pot is metal but its handle is not.

## Do It Yourself

1. Flow arrows (Activity 7.16): write the three cases — tea held, ironing, bare feet on asphalt — with an arrow and the words ‘from ___ (hotter) to ___ (cooler)’ completed for each.
2. Cup ranking (Activity 7.17): with an adult pouring, rank the five cups warmest to coldest after two minutes. File each under conductor or insulator and defend the borderline one.
3. Handle audit: list five hot things at home and what insulates the hand from each (handle, cloth, glove, wood). One entry must be a case where the insulation is missing — and what happens.
