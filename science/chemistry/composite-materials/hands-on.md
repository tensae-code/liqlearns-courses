# Hands-on — Composite materials

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Two materials, one job (home materials — paper and string)
Tear a strip of paper on its own and note how little force it took. Now lay lengths of string along a second strip, fold the paper over them so the string is held inside, and pull again. Write down what changed and say it in lesson terms: the paper alone tears under pulling, the string carries the pull — a partnership of materials, as steel bars partner concrete.

## The composites hunt (observation only — home and street)
Find three objects around your home or street that are made of more than one material working together — a reinforced-concrete step or beam, a cable, lenses that darken in the sun. For each, write the parts you can identify and the one job the combination does that no part does alone, as Malaysia's section sets its four examples side by side.

## Single material or composite? (paper exercise)
List six materials or objects from your home. Sort them into two columns — one material doing the job / a combination of materials doing the job together — and defend the column you put each one in, in one line each.
