# Composite materials — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is a composite material, and how is it different from a single material?
**Answer: A composite material is made by combining two or more different materials so the combination does a job neither part does as well alone. Unlike a single material, each part keeps its own identity and strength inside the combination — the strength comes from the partnership.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How do the two partners in reinforced concrete divide the work?
**Answer: Concrete carries the squeezing force (compression) but cracks under pulling; the steel bars set inside it carry the pulling force (tension). Together they carry loads — buildings and bridges — that neither could carry alone.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does Malaysia count fibre optics among composite materials?
**Answer: The working material is not a lump of glass but glass drawn into hair-thin fibres and combined into cables; the fibres guide light along their length, so the combination does a new job — carrying light signals for communication.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What does photochromic glass do, and why is it a combination rather than plain glass?
**Answer: Photochromic glass darkens in bright light and clears again in shade. It is glass combined with light-sensitive material, so the finished material answers the light — a job plain glass never does.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Name Malaysia's four composite-material examples and the job of each.
**Answer: Reinforced concrete (carrying buildings and bridges — compression and tension together); fibre optics (guiding light to carry signals); photochromic glass (darkening in bright light, clearing in shade); superconductors (carrying electric current without resistance).**
