# Lesson plan — Carbocations and carbanions — charged carbon

## Sub lessons
1. **The carbocation — carbon with a positive charge** — Carbon can carry a charge. The **carbonium ion — the carbocation** — is a carbon species carrying a **positive charge**: a carbon that has lost out in the sharing of electrons and stands electron-poor. It is an ion built on carbon itself, not on a metal or a halogen — charged carbon is the lesson's thing, and the carbocation is its positive face.
2. **The carbanion — carbon with a negative charge** — The mirror species is the **carbanion**: a carbon carrying a **negative charge**, electron-rich where the carbocation is electron-poor. The pair belong together — one carbon framework, two opposite charge states — and Iraq's chapter names them side by side as the charged carbon species a learner must recognise before reaction mechanisms can make sense.
3. **The structural formula — what the molecular formula cannot show** — To tell such species — and isomers — apart, the chapter defines the **structural (displayed) formula**: it shows **each atom's partners, the bond types and the valence**, which the molecular formula cannot. The proof is one formula with two substances: **C₂H₆O is both ethyl alcohol and dimethyl ether** — same molecular formula, different structures, different substances. Only the structural formula separates them.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Confuses which ion is which → ask "who holds the charge, and which sign?": the carbonium (carbocation) carries the positive charge; the carbanion carries the negative.
