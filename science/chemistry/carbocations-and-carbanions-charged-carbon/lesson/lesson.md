# Carbocations and carbanions — charged carbon

## Do it first
One formula, C₂H₆O, names two different substances — an alcohol you could meet in a laboratory and an ether. Before reading, say out loud: what must a formula show, beyond the atoms and their counts, to tell the two apart — and can a carbon atom itself carry a charge? Then read on: this is Iraq's Grade 10 (Scientific Track) way in its organic chapter (Ch.4) — charged carbon, and the drawing that sees structure.

## Sub lesson 1 — The carbocation — carbon with a positive charge

Carbon can carry a charge. The **carbonium ion — the carbocation** — is a carbon species carrying a **positive charge**: a carbon that has lost out in the sharing of electrons and stands electron-poor. It is an ion built on carbon itself, not on a metal or a halogen — charged carbon is the lesson's thing, and the carbocation is its positive face.

## Sub lesson 2 — The carbanion — carbon with a negative charge

The mirror species is the **carbanion**: a carbon carrying a **negative charge**, electron-rich where the carbocation is electron-poor. The pair belong together — one carbon framework, two opposite charge states — and Iraq's chapter names them side by side as the charged carbon species a learner must recognise before reaction mechanisms can make sense.

## Sub lesson 3 — The structural formula — what the molecular formula cannot show

To tell such species — and isomers — apart, the chapter defines the **structural (displayed) formula**: it shows **each atom's partners, the bond types and the valence**, which the molecular formula cannot. The proof is one formula with two substances: **C₂H₆O is both ethyl alcohol and dimethyl ether** — same molecular formula, different structures, different substances. Only the structural formula separates them.

## The key ideas

- The **carbocation (carbonium ion)** is carbon carrying a **positive charge**; the **carbanion** is carbon carrying a **negative charge**.
- The **structural (displayed) formula** shows each atom's **partners, bond types and valence** — what the molecular formula cannot show.
- **C₂H₆O** is both **ethyl alcohol** and **dimethyl ether**: one molecular formula, two structures, two substances.

## Do It Yourself
1. A carbon species carries a negative charge. Name it, and name its positive mirror. (A carbanion; its mirror is the carbocation — the carbonium ion, carbon carrying a positive charge.)
2. C₂H₆O is written on a bottle. Why can nobody yet say which substance is inside? (The molecular formula gives only atoms and counts; C₂H₆O is both ethyl alcohol and dimethyl ether. The structural formula, showing partners, bond types and valence, is what separates them.)
3. What three things does a structural (displayed) formula show that a molecular formula does not? (Each atom's partners, the bond types between them, and the valence — the arrangement itself.)
