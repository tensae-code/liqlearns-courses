# Lesson plan — Atoms, elements and chemical symbols

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Atoms, elements and chemical symbols
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a pure substance has one kind of constituent and cannot be split into simpler substances; the atom is the fundamental constituent — the smallest separable constituent of matter; atoms of one kind make an element, and each element carries a symbol written with one capital letter, or a capital followed by a small letter (Mauritania, Grades 8, 9 and 10 — *Sciences Physiques 2AS*, Unit I, printed p. 11; *Sciences Physiques 3AS*, Ch. I, printed p. 9; *Sciences Physiques 4AS*, Ch. II, printed p. 22)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Proton, neutron, electron is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
