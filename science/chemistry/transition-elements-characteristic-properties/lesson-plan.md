# Lesson plan — Transition elements — characteristic properties

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Transition elements — characteristic properties
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bhutan's Class X book (Ch.5 §5.3–5.4): the transition elements as a family defined by its behaviour — variable oxidation states, coloured ions and compounds, catalytic activity, complex formation and magnetic character, all flowing from the partly filled d subshell and the closeness of the (n−1)d and ns energies — closing with group 11, copper, silver and gold, the family's coinage metals, their similarities, reactions and uses
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. What makes an element a transition metal is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The characteristic properties — one family's signature** — Bhutan's §5.3 lists the properties that mark the transition elements off from the main-group metals, and this lesson carries the list as the book gives it.
2. **Why — the partly filled d subshell** — §5.3's explanation is a single structural fact: a transition element's **(n−1)d subshell is partly filled**, and the (n−1)d and ns electrons are **close in energy**, so both sets can be spent in bonding — lose the ns electrons only, or ns and some d electrons too, and the **variable oxidation states** follow.
3. **Group 11 — copper, silver and gold** — The chapter closes (§5.4) with the group-11 trio — **copper, silver and gold**, the coinage metals.
