# Transition elements — characteristic properties — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) List four characteristic properties of the transition elements.
**Answer: Any four of: variable oxidation states; coloured ions and compounds; catalytic activity of the metals and their compounds; formation of complex ions; paramagnetism from unpaired electrons; hard, dense, high-melting metals that form alloys and interstitial compounds readily.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Give one catalyst example from the lesson and the process it serves.
**Answer: Iron catalyses the Haber process (ammonia synthesis); or vanadium(V) oxide catalyses the Contact process (sulfuric acid manufacture). The catalyst is recovered unchanged at the end.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain variable oxidation states from the electron energies.
**Answer: In a transition atom the (n−1)d and ns subshells are close in energy, so a reaction can spend the ns electrons alone or those plus a varying number of d electrons. Different numbers spent mean different oxidation states — iron +2 and +3, manganese +2 up to +7.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why are transition-metal ions coloured when sodium ions are not?
**Answer: The transition ion's d subshell is partly filled, so an electron can be promoted between d levels by absorbing part of the visible spectrum; the colour seen is the remainder. The sodium ion has a complete shell with no d electrons and no visible-light promotion, so its compounds are colourless/white.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) State two similarities of copper, silver and gold and one use of copper that spends a shared property.
**Answer: Similarities: very high electrical and thermal conduction (silver best, copper next), high density and melting points, and low, falling reactivity down the group. Copper wiring spends its conduction — second only to silver and far cheaper.**
