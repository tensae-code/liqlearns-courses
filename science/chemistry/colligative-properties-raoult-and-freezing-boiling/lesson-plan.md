# Lesson plan — Colligative properties — Raoult's law and the freezing and boiling shifts

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Colligative properties — Raoult's law and the freezing and boiling shifts
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the colligative properties as one family — vapour-pressure lowering, boiling-point elevation, freezing-point depression and osmotic pressure — each shift set by the number of dissolved particles and not their kind, with the full maths Bolivia's Soluciones II teaches: Raoult's law p = x(solvent) × P° (and the lowering ΔP = x(solute) × P°), freezing-point depression ΔTf = Kf × m and boiling-point elevation ΔTb = Kb × m, each carried through a fully worked problem
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Raoult's law and ideal solutions, and Molarity, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Colligative properties — counting particles, not kinds** — The four shifts named as one family, bound by the one rule: particle number rules, particle kind does not.
2. **Raoult's law — the vapour pressure falls by the solvent's share** — p = x(solvent) × P°, the lowering as the solute's share, worked end to end and run backwards to count particles.
3. **Freezing down, boiling up — the two temperature shifts** — ΔTf = Kf × m and ΔTb = Kb × m worked on one molality, and the road-salt and antifreeze uses read off the maths.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the family rule: particles counted, kinds ignored — every formula in the lesson is that rule wearing numbers.
- Uses molarity for the temperature shifts → the shifts count particles per kilogram of solvent: molality, m — litres of solution change with temperature, kilograms do not.
- Puts the freezing shift the wrong way → dissolving always pushes the freezing point DOWN and the boiling point UP: the solvent's two doors move apart.
