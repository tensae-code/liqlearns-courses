# Hands-on — Colligative properties — Raoult's law and the freezing and boiling shifts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Two trays in the freezer (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only)
Fill two identical shallow trays with the same amount of tap water. Stir two level tablespoons of table salt into one until dissolved; leave the other pure. Stand both in the freezer side by side and check every 20 minutes, noting the time each first skins with ice and the time each is solid throughout. The salted tray must lag — freezing-point depression working in your own freezer. Write the two times and, in lesson terms, say what the dissolved particles did to the salted water's freezing point.

## The worked maths, by hand (paper exercise)
Repeat the lesson's two worked problems on paper with every step shown: (1) x(solvent) = 0.90, P° = 23.8 mmHg — find p and ΔP. (2) m = 0.50 mol/kg in water — find the freezing point and the boiling point. Then redo both with the count doubled and confirm, in writing, that every shift doubled.

## Road salt read backwards (paper exercise)
A road crew salts ice at −5 °C. Using ΔTf = Kf × m with Kf = 1.86 °C·kg/mol, find the molality the melted surface layer needs to stay liquid at −5 °C. In two lines, say why the crew cares about the count of particles and not the kind.
