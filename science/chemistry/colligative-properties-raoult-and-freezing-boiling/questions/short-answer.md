# Colligative properties — Raoult's law and the freezing and boiling shifts — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the four colligative properties and the one rule that binds them.
**Answer: Vapour-pressure lowering, boiling-point elevation, freezing-point depression and osmotic pressure — bound by one rule: each shift is set by the number of dissolved particles, never by their kind.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A solution has x(solvent) = 0.80 with P° = 47.4 mmHg. Find p and ΔP.
**Answer: p = 0.80 × 47.4 = 37.9 mmHg; the lowering is the solute's share, ΔP = 0.20 × 47.4 = 9.5 mmHg.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How does Raoult's law let a measurement count dissolved particles?
**Answer: The lowering is exactly the solute's mole fraction of the pure pressure, so measuring ΔP reads the solute's share of the particles straight off — the vapour pressure becomes a particle counter.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A 2.0 mol/kg non-electrolyte solution in water: find its freezing and boiling points.
**Answer: ΔTf = 1.86 × 2.0 = 3.72 °C, so it freezes at −3.72 °C; ΔTb = 0.512 × 2.0 = 1.024 °C, so it boils at 101.02 °C (about 101.0 °C).**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does road salt melt ice below 0 °C?
**Answer: The dissolved salt particles depress the water's freezing point below the air temperature, so at that temperature the salted water's liquid state wins and the ice melts into it.**
