# Cuba's nickel and cobalt industry — Moa

## Do it first
Most countries teach "obtaining metals" from somebody else's mines. Before reading, say out loud: which two metals would a Cuban learner expect to meet first, and where in Cuba are they produced? Then read on: this is Cuba's Grade 11 way (§5.5) — the metals chapter told from Moa.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lateritic ores — Cuba among the world's main nickel holders

Cuba's nickel sits in **lateritic ores**, and the Grade 11 book names **Cuba among the world's main nickel holders** — **Canada, Cuba, Russia and New Caledonia** are the holders named. The ore in the ground is the starting fact: Cuba does not import its example; it digs it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Industrial production of Ni and Co compounds at Moa, Holguín

At **Moa, in Holguín**, the mining-metallurgical industry produces **nickel and cobalt compounds** industrially. The Grade 11 programme places this production inside the metals unit's obtaining line (§3.4) as a **research-work topic**, and the student book gives it its own section (§5.5, printed p. 150) — obtaining metals is learned by studying the plant at home.

## Sub lesson 3 — Nickel — the leading export of Cuban mining

**Nickel is the leading export of Cuban mining.** The metals chapter closes its obtaining story with Cuba's own production: the chemistry of obtaining metals, the industry that does it, and the export it becomes are one story, told from the country itself.

## The key ideas

- Cuba's nickel comes from **lateritic ores**; Cuba is named among the **world's main nickel holders** (Canada, Cuba, Russia, New Caledonia).
- **Moa, Holguín** produces **nickel and cobalt compounds** industrially — the programme's research-work topic and the student book's §5.5.
- **Nickel is the leading export of Cuban mining** — Cuba's own way: obtaining metals is taught through the home industry.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Cuba's nickel and cobalt industry to your companion: the ores, the place, the two metals and the export. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Which two metals' compounds are produced industrially at Moa? (Nickel and cobalt compounds.)
2. What kind of ores hold Cuba's nickel, and which four holders does the book name? (Lateritic ores; Canada, Cuba, Russia and New Caledonia.)
3. Why does Cuba teach obtaining metals through Moa instead of a foreign example? (Because the industry is Cuba's own — nickel is the leading export of Cuban mining, so the home production is the worked example, set in the programme as a research-work topic.)
