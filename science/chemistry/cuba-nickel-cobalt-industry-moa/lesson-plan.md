# Lesson plan — Cuba's nickel and cobalt industry — Moa

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Cuba's nickel and cobalt industry — Moa
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how Cuba obtains its metals, taught through its own mining-metallurgical industry — the industrial production of nickel and cobalt compounds at Moa, Holguín, from lateritic ores (Cuba, Grade 11 — Ch. 5 §5.5, printed p. 150; Grade 11 programme, Unit 3 §3.4, printed p. 22)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Extracting metals is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Lateritic ores — Cuba among the world's main nickel holders** — Cuba's nickel sits in lateritic ores, and the Grade 11 book names Cuba among the world's main nickel holders, alongside Canada, Russia and New Caledonia — the ore in the ground is the starting fact of the industry.
2. **Industrial production of Ni and Co compounds at Moa, Holguín** — At Moa in Holguín the mining-metallurgical industry produces nickel and cobalt compounds industrially — the programme sets this production as a research-work topic inside the metals unit, and the student book gives it its own section (§5.5).
3. **Nickel — the leading export of Cuban mining** — Nickel is the leading export of Cuban mining: the metals chapter closes its obtaining story not with a foreign example but with Cuba's own production, tying the chemistry of obtaining metals to the country's economy.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: ore first, then production, then the export it becomes.
- Mixes nickel and cobalt up → ask "which two metals does Moa produce compounds of? ": nickel and cobalt, together, from the same lateritic industry.
