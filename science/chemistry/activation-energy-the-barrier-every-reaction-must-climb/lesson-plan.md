# Lesson plan — Activation energy — the barrier every reaction must climb

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Activation energy — the barrier every reaction must climb
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Activation energy read off a distribution curve, as Yemen's Grade 11 book reads it: at temperature T1 only the black-shaded molecules have enough energy, at T2 the red-shaded crowd joins them, so heating multiplies the activated fraction; the activated complex drawn for H2 + I2 as [H...H...I...I], a fleeting arrangement where the old bonds are half-broken and the new half-formed, and the whole journey plotted as an energy hill — with the homely picture of a rock pushed from a mountainside over the pass before it can roll down to the valley
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Collision theory — what speeds a reaction up is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
