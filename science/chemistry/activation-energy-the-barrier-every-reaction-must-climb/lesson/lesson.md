# Activation energy — the barrier every reaction must climb

## Do it first
Before you read: a mixture of hydrogen and iodine can sit together, mixed and ready, and almost nothing happens — then a little heat is added and the reaction runs. Say out loud: if the molecules are already touching, what is the heat actually buying? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book answers with a curve, a crowd, and a mountain pass.

## Sub lesson 1 — The distribution curve — few are ready

Activation energy is read off a **distribution curve**: the molecules of a mixture do not all carry the same energy — most carry the average, few carry much. At temperature **T₁, only the black-shaded molecules** — the small crowd past the activation-energy line — have **enough energy** to react on collision. The reaction's slowness is a head-count: too few molecules are rich enough.

## Sub lesson 2 — Heating — the red crowd joins

Raise the temperature to **T₂** and the curve shifts: the **red-shaded crowd joins** the activated — the fraction of molecules with at least the activation energy **multiplies**. That is what heat buys: not faster molecules only, but **more qualifying molecules**. The activated few become an activated many, and the rate climbs with their number.

## Sub lesson 3 — The activated complex — and the rock over the pass

At the top of the barrier sits the **activated complex**, drawn for H₂ + I₂ as **[H···H···I···I]** — a fleeting arrangement where the old H–H and I–I bonds are **half-broken** and the new H–I bonds **half-formed**: neither reactants nor products, the pass between them. The book plots the whole journey as an **energy hill** and pictures it homely: a rock that must be pushed from a mountainside (أ) **over the pass (ب)** before it can roll down to the valley (ج) and stay there — the push is the activation energy; the valley is the products; the pass is the complex.

## The key ideas

- On the **distribution curve**, only the molecules past the **activation-energy** line can react — at **T₁ a black-shaded few**; heating to **T₂ adds the red-shaded crowd**, multiplying the activated fraction.
- The **activated complex** — for H₂ + I₂, **[H···H···I···I]** — old bonds **half-broken**, new bonds **half-formed**, at the top of the energy hill.
- The book's picture: the rock must be pushed **over the pass (ب)** before it can roll to the valley (ج) — activation energy is the push.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does a small rise in temperature often give a large rise in rate — answer with the curve, not with 'molecules move faster'. (Because the rate counts the molecules past the activation-energy line: heating shifts the distribution so a much larger fraction qualifies — the red-shaded crowd joins the black — and the number of fruitful collisions multiplies, not merely quickens.)
2. Draw the activated complex of H₂ + I₂ from the lesson and say what is happening to each bond in it. ([H···H···I···I]: the H–H and I–I bonds are half-broken while the two H–I bonds are half-formed — the fleeting arrangement at the top of the barrier, neither reactants nor products.)
3. In the rock picture, name what the mountainside, the pass and the valley stand for. (The mountainside (أ) is the reactants; the pass (ب) is the activated complex at the barrier's top, reached only by the push of activation energy; the valley (ج) is the products, where the rock stays.)
