# Molar volume of gases — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the molar-volume fact with its conditions.
**Answer: One mole of any gas occupies the same 22.4 L at the two standard conditions — 0 °C and 1 atm — as read off Syria's three one-mole cylinders of helium, oxygen and nitrogen.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Work the reverse example from printed p. 45.
**Answer: 44.8 L of hydrogen ÷ 22.4 L per mole = 2 mol — the volume divided by the molar volume gives the amount.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the gas's identity not change the 22.4 L?
**Answer: At the standard conditions a mole of gas particles — the same count of particles, whatever the gas — spreads to the same volume; heaviness changes the mass of the mole, not its gas volume.**
