# Lesson plan — Molar volume of gases

## Sub lessons
1. **One mole of any gas — the same 22.4 L** — Syria shows **three one-mole cylinders — helium, oxygen and nitrogen** — and reads the fact off them: **one mole of any gas occupies the same volume, 22.4 L**, at the **two standard conditions**: **0 °C and 1 atm** (standard temperature and pressure).
2. **Forward — V = n × 22.4 L** — The fact becomes a working rule: **V = n × 22.4 L** — the volume of a gas at the standard conditions is its amount in moles multiplied by the molar volume. Two moles of a gas occupy 44.8 L; half a mole occupies 11.2 L.
3. **Backwards — volume into amount** — The book works the rule **in reverse** on printed p. 45: **44.8 L of hydrogen at the standard conditions is 2 mol** — the volume divided by 22.4 L per mole gives the amount. From there the amount can go on to a mass through n = m/M.

## If the learner is stuck
- Expects heavier gases to take more room → as gases at the standard conditions, a mole of any gas takes 22.4 L — the identity and mass do not enter the volume.
- Uses 22.4 L at any temperature and pressure → 22.4 L belongs to the standard pair, 0 °C and 1 atm; the conditions are part of the number.
- Multiplies when the problem asks backwards → forward is V = n × 22.4; backwards, divide the volume by 22.4 to get the amount — 44.8 L ÷ 22.4 = 2 mol.
