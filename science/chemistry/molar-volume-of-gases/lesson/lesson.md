# Molar volume of gases

## Do it first
Before you read: a mole of helium and a mole of oxygen hold the same number of particles, but their masses are very different. Say out loud: as gases, do you think they take up the same room, or different room? Then read on: Syria's Grade 8 book answers with three cylinders, on printed p. 44.

## Sub lesson 1 — One mole of any gas — the same 22.4 L

Syria shows **three one-mole cylinders — helium, oxygen and nitrogen** — and reads the fact off them: **one mole of any gas occupies the same volume, 22.4 L**, at the **two standard conditions**: **0 °C and 1 atm** (standard temperature and pressure).

The gas's identity does not enter the volume: heavy or light, a mole of gas particles spreads to the same 22.4 L when temperature and pressure are the standard pair. The conditions are part of the number — change them and the volume changes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Forward — V = n × 22.4 L

The fact becomes a working rule: **V = n × 22.4 L** — the volume of a gas at the standard conditions is its amount in moles multiplied by the molar volume. Two moles of a gas occupy 44.8 L; half a mole occupies 11.2 L.

The rule runs on amount, not on mass: turn the mass into moles first (n = m/M), then into volume. Two steps, each with its own rule.

## Sub lesson 3 — Backwards — volume into amount

The book works the rule **in reverse** on printed p. 45: **44.8 L of hydrogen at the standard conditions is 2 mol** — the volume divided by 22.4 L per mole gives the amount. From there the amount can go on to a mass through n = m/M.

Volume → amount → mass, or mass → amount → volume: the mole stands in the middle of every gas calculation, and 22.4 L is the bridge between amount and volume.

## The key ideas

- **One mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L** at the standard conditions, **0 °C and 1 atm** (U1 L7, printed p. 44) — read off one-mole cylinders of He, O₂ and N₂.
- Forward: **V = n × 22.4 L**.
- Reverse (printed p. 45): **44.8 L of hydrogen = 2 mol** — volume divided by 22.4 L gives the amount.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the molar volume of gases to your companion in your own words: the 22.4 L fact and its two conditions, the forward rule, and the reverse working.
