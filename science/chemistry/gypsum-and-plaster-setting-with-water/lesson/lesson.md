# Gypsum and plaster — setting with water

## Do it first
Before you read: a powder that is soft in the bag turns to stone in the bucket — no kiln, no glue, only water. Say out loud: what could water possibly do to a powder to make it hard — is the water a glue, a food, or a building block? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 10 book answers with the plaster on its own craftsmen's hands.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gypsum — the mineral with water inside

Calcium sulfate is met as **gypsum, CaSO₄·2H₂O** — a calcium salt that carries **its own water inside the crystal**, two water molecules built into every unit. That built-in water is the whole story of the lesson: take it away by heating and you get the powder called plaster; give it back and the stone returns.

## Sub lesson 2 — The setting — the book's bench moment

The book's way in is a hand test from Yemen's own craft: the plaster used by **Yemen's traditional craftsmen (صانعي القمريات, makers of the carved window screens)** — powder a little of it, **add water, and it sets quickly into a hard mass, giving out heat**. The setting is a chemical taking-back: the powder re-takes its water and locks again into the hard crystal — and it is **quick**, setting before it is even placed if you dawdle.

## Sub lesson 3 — What the set plaster is for

The same paste does the country's quiet work: it **fills house-ceiling voids** and **casts statues** — poured or pressed while soft, hard when set, holding the shape it was given. Speed is both its gift and its warning: the worker must have the mould, the ceiling and the design ready before the water goes in, because the chemistry will not wait.

## The key ideas

- Gypsum is **CaSO₄·2H₂O** — calcium sulfate with **two waters built into the crystal**.
- Powdered plaster + water **sets quickly into a hard mass, giving out heat** — the crystal taking its water back; it can set **before it is placed** if you dawdle.
- Yemen's **qamariya window-screen craftsmen** work it; the set paste **fills ceiling voids and casts statues**.

## Do It Yourself
1. What is the water's role in plaster's setting — glue, solvent, or building block? Defend your answer from the formula. (Building block: gypsum's formula, CaSO₄·2H₂O, holds water inside the crystal; the plaster powder is that crystal with its water driven off, and setting is the water being taken back into the crystal — the hardness is the crystal rebuilt.)
2. Why does the book warn that plaster sets 'before it is even placed if you dawdle' — what working rule follows? (Because the setting reaction is fast and gives out heat as it runs; the rule is to have mould, surface and design fully ready before mixing — the chemistry will not wait for the worker.)
3. Name the Yemeni craft the book takes its plaster from, and two other uses of the set paste. (The qamariya makers — the traditional craftsmen of the carved window screens. The set paste also fills house-ceiling voids and casts statues.)
