# Soap and detergents — Unit 3 review

## Do it first
A soap molecule has a head that loves water and a tail that loves grease — it is a double agent. Before the review, draw its ambush of a grease droplet in words.

[Picture 21769 — A micelle trapping a grease droplet.]

Soap is made by saponification — hydrolysing fats/oils with alkali (Grade 11's reaction, industrialised here, with local manufacture presented).

Soap cleans because its molecules form micelles: tails inward gripping grease, heads outward in the water, so grease lifts away in the rinse.

Detergents are synthetic cleaners that keep working in hard water where soap forms scum. Unit 3 closes with summary and review.
