# Lesson plan — Green chemistry and the circular economy

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Green chemistry and the circular economy
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Out of the linear economy — repair, reuse, recycle — guided by green chemistry's working principles, against Bulgaria's pollutant census for air, water and soil (Bulgaria, Grade 10 — §62, printed pp. 162–163)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Green chemistry — the principles is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **From linear to circular — and the principles** — **Pollution is defined first**: a pollutant is a substance **foreign to the environment, or present above permitted levels**. The technology scheme runs raw materials + energy → chemical processes → product. The way out of the **linear economy** is the **circular** one — products **repaired, reused in another form, or recycled back into production**, saving both raw materials and energy. **Green chemistry's working principles** are listed: **prevent waste rather than clean it up; design less hazardous syntheses; save energy by avoiding high temperatures and pressures; use catalysts for lower energy and safer reagents; prefer renewable/recyclable raw materials; and restrict or render safe the auxiliary substances such as solvents**.
2. **The pollutant census** — Bulgaria's census tables the enemies across three media. **Air:** aerosols to 500 µm raising dust and fog; NO/NO₂/SO₂/CO driving acid rain, deforestation and toxic photochemical smog; CO₂ and CH₄ the greenhouse pair; N₂O, NO and freons thinning the ozone layer. **Water:** nitrates, phosphates and nutrients feeding microorganisms until dissolved oxygen falls; oil breaking oxygen exchange; synthetic detergents; discarded polymers hampering marine feeding and movement. **Soil:** over-applied nitrogen and phosphorus salts shifting pH and salinising groundwater; herbicides, fungicides and insecticides killing useful organisms; toxic arsenic, antimony, selenium, lead, cadmium and mercury from enrichment, metallurgy and transport.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Lists recycling as the whole answer → return to the principles' order: prevention stands first; recycling is the circle's last bend, not its first.
