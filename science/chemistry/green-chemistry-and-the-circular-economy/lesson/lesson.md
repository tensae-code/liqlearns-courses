# Green chemistry and the circular economy

## Do it first
A factory's old road runs in a straight line: dig, make, use, dump. Before reading, say out loud: where could that line be bent into a circle — and who pays when it is not bent? Bulgaria's Grade 10 environment lesson draws both shapes and lists the principles that bend the line.

## Sub lesson 1 — From linear to circular — and the principles

**Pollution is defined first**: a pollutant is a substance **foreign to the environment, or present above permitted levels**. The technology scheme runs raw materials + energy → chemical processes → product. The way out of the **linear economy** is the **circular** one — products **repaired, reused in another form, or recycled back into production**, saving both raw materials and energy. **Green chemistry's working principles** are listed: **prevent waste rather than clean it up; design less hazardous syntheses; save energy by avoiding high temperatures and pressures; use catalysts for lower energy and safer reagents; prefer renewable/recyclable raw materials; and restrict or render safe the auxiliary substances such as solvents**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The pollutant census

Bulgaria's census tables the enemies across three media. **Air:** aerosols to 500 µm raising dust and fog; NO/NO₂/SO₂/CO driving acid rain, deforestation and toxic photochemical smog; CO₂ and CH₄ the greenhouse pair; N₂O, NO and freons thinning the ozone layer. **Water:** nitrates, phosphates and nutrients feeding microorganisms until dissolved oxygen falls; oil breaking oxygen exchange; synthetic detergents; discarded polymers hampering marine feeding and movement. **Soil:** over-applied nitrogen and phosphorus salts shifting pH and salinising groundwater; herbicides, fungicides and insecticides killing useful organisms; toxic arsenic, antimony, selenium, lead, cadmium and mercury from enrichment, metallurgy and transport.

## The key ideas

- Pollutant: foreign to the environment, or above permitted levels. Circular economy: repair, reuse in another form, recycle into production.
- Green chemistry: prevent waste first; safer syntheses; save energy; catalysts; renewable/recyclable raw materials; safe auxiliaries.
- Census: air (aerosols, acid-rain gases, greenhouse pair, ozone thinners), water (nutrients, oil, detergents, polymers), soil (salts, pesticides, toxic metals).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion: from linear to circular — and the principles; the pollutant census — in that order, in your own words, with the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. A process redesign replaces a solvent with water and stops a waste stream forming at all. Which two principles are at work? (Restricting/rendering safe the auxiliary substances (solvents), and preventing waste rather than cleaning it up.)
2. Why are nitrates named water pollutants when plants need them? (A pollutant is also a normal substance above permitted levels: excess nutrients feed microorganisms until the water's dissolved oxygen falls.)
