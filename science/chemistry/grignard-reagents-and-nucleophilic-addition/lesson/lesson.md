# Grignard reagents — and nucleophilic addition

## Do it first
A carbonyl carbon is short of electrons; it invites attack by anything carrying electrons to give. Say out loud: what would a reagent look like that delivers a whole carbon group, electrons and all, onto that carbon — building a bigger molecule in one step? Then read on: Jordan's Grade 12 book draws exactly that delivery, atom by atom.

## Sub lesson 1 — The reagent — carbon carrying the electrons

A **Grignard reagent** is an organomagnesium halide, written **RMgX**: a carbon group R bonded to magnesium, which is bonded to a halogen X. The bond's electrons sit toward the carbon, so the R group behaves as a **nucleophile** — an electron-pair giver with a carbon to deliver. The book's worked reagent is **ethyl magnesium chloride, CH₃CH₂MgCl**: the ethyl group is the parcel, magnesium chloride the handle.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nucleophilic addition — the drawn mechanism

The book **draws the addition**: the carbonyl's C=O is polarised, oxygen carrying δ− and carbon δ+; the Grignard's carbon group attacks the δ+ carbon, the π bond's electrons move onto the oxygen, and the first product is an alkoxide bound to magnesium — written with its **–OMgX** ending. Addition, not substitution: nothing leaves; the carbon group joins, the double bond opens, and the molecule grows by the delivered group.

## Sub lesson 3 — The worked case — ethyl magnesium chloride with ethanal

The lesson's worked reaction runs the drawing on real names: **ethyl magnesium chloride + ethanal**. The ethyl group adds to ethanal's carbonyl carbon; the oxygen leaves the step as –OMgCl; and the product, on the usual aqueous work-up that the synthesis lessons use, stands as an **alcohol** — ethanal's two carbons grown by the delivered ethyl into a four-carbon alcohol skeleton. One addition has done the organic chemist's favourite trick: a new C–C bond, made to order.

## The key ideas

- Grignard reagent: RMgX — a carbon group on magnesium halide; the carbon end is nucleophilic and deliverable.
- Nucleophilic addition: R attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon, π electrons move to the δ− oxygen, product carries –OMgX; nothing leaves.
- Worked case: ethyl magnesium chloride + ethanal — the ethyl group adds, growing the carbon skeleton toward an alcohol.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the book's worked Grignard reagent and name its parts. (Ethyl magnesium chloride, CH₃CH₂MgCl: the ethyl group is the carbon parcel to deliver; MgCl is the magnesium-halide handle.)
2. In the addition drawing, where do the π bond's electrons go? (Onto the oxygen — it carries δ− and ends the step as –OMgX, while the delivered carbon group bonds to the carbonyl carbon.)
3. Why is this addition, not substitution? (Nothing leaves the molecule: the carbon group joins and the C=O π bond opens — the product contains every atom of both partners, grown by one C–C bond.)
