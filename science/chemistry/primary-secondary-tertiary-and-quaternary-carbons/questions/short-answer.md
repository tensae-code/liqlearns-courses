# Primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary carbons — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** State the four carbon classes with the neighbour count for each.
**Answer: Primary (1°) — joined to one other carbon; secondary (2°) — two; tertiary (3°) — three; quaternary (4°) — four.**

**2.** What exactly is counted when a carbon is classified?
**Answer: Only its carbon neighbours — how many other carbon atoms it is joined to. Hydrogens and other atoms do not enter the count.**

**3.** How are hydrogens classified in this scheme?
**Answer: By the carbon that holds them: hydrogens on a primary carbon are primary hydrogens, on a secondary carbon secondary, on a tertiary carbon tertiary — the book sets this matching as an exercise.**

**4.** Why does the classification come before naming in Iraq's chapter?
**Answer: Because a structure can then be read at sight: each carbon's class follows from one count, and the class names describe the skeleton the naming rules will name.**
