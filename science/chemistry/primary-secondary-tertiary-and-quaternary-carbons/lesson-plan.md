# Lesson plan — Primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary carbons

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary carbons
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a carbon atom classified by how many other carbons it touches — primary (one), secondary (two), tertiary (three), quaternary (four) — with the matching classes of hydrogen (Iraq, Grade 10, Scientific Track — Ch.4, printed p. 93)

## Sub lessons
1. **Count the carbon neighbours** — The classification begins with one count: **how many other carbon atoms is this carbon joined to?** Not hydrogens, not oxygens — carbon neighbours only. Every carbon in a chain or ring takes its class from that single number, and Iraq's chapter sets the classification before the naming rules so structures can be read at sight.
2. **The four classes — 1°, 2°, 3°, 4°** — The count gives four classes. A carbon joined to **one** other carbon is **primary (1°)**. Joined to **two**, it is **secondary (2°)**. Joined to **three**, **tertiary (3°)**. Joined to **four**, **quaternary (4°)** — a carbon holding four carbon neighbours and no hydrogen at all. The four names are the count, said in Latin.
3. **The matching hydrogens** — The hydrogens take the class of the carbon that holds them: hydrogens on a primary carbon are **primary hydrogens**, on a secondary carbon **secondary**, on a tertiary carbon **tertiary** — the book sets the matching hydrogen classes as an exercise, completing the scheme: classify the carbon by its carbon neighbours, and its hydrogens inherit the name.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Counts hydrogens or oxygens instead of carbons → return to the rule: count only the carbon neighbours — the class is named by carbon's carbon company alone.
