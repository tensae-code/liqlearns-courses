# Hands-on — Primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary carbons

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Classifying a chain — the neighbour count
Draw 2-methylpropane's skeleton and butane's skeleton on paper. For every carbon, write its carbon-neighbour count, then its class (1°, 2°, 3° or 4°). Check the pair against each other: the branched skeleton must produce a class the straight one cannot. Then label every hydrogen primary, secondary or tertiary by the carbon that holds it.

## Build a quaternary carbon
With paper atoms, build the smallest carbon skeleton that contains a quaternary carbon (a central carbon joined to four carbons). Count the bonds on the centre carbon out loud and prove it can hold no hydrogen. Then remove one carbon arm and re-classify the centre.
