# Metals in nature — and extraction methods — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does occurrence read reactivity backwards?
**Answer: Reactive metals combined long ago and stay locked in widespread compounds; only metals too unreactive to combine are still found free — so the tighter the lock, the more reactive the metal.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the ladder's three rungs in order and one metal on each.
**Answer: Heating alone (e.g. mercury), carbon reduction (e.g. iron, zinc, lead, copper), electrolysis (e.g. sodium, magnesium, aluminium).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What two steps bracket every extraction, first and last?
**Answer: Concentration of the ore first — separating the metal compound from waste rock — and refining of the freed metal last.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What is Vietnam's own way in this lesson, compared with the course's other extraction lessons?
**Answer: It teaches the general survey first — occurrence and all the methods as one system (Bài 20) — so the single-metal cases (aluminium, copper, iron) become examples of a rule already stated.**
