## Sub lesson 1 — Metals in nature — free or locked

Vietnam's Grade 12 metals chapter opens its extraction lesson (Bài 20) with occurrence. A few metals are found **free (native)** — gold, platinum and sometimes copper and silver — always the **least reactive** metals, the ones that never combined. Every other metal is found **combined**, locked in compounds in the rocks: the metal-bearing rocks worth working are **ores**.

The lock takes set forms: oxides (iron's haematite, aluminium's bauxite), sulfides (zinc blende, copper pyrites), carbonates (limestone's calcium, malachite's copper) and chlorides (common salt's sodium, the sea's magnesium). Occurrence is reactivity read backwards: the more reactive the metal, the tighter and more widespread its compounds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Separating the metal — the general methods

Bài 20 surveys the methods as one system. First the ore is **concentrated** — the metal compound separated from the waste rock. Then the metal is freed, and the freeing method climbs a ladder of force: **heating alone** frees the least reactive (mercury's oxide decomposes); **reduction by carbon or carbon monoxide** frees the middle metals (iron in the blast furnace, zinc, lead, copper); **electrolysis** — the strongest method, priced in electricity — frees the most reactive (sodium, magnesium, aluminium), whose compounds no chemical reducer can break.

Finally the freed metal is **refined** — electrolytic refining makes copper pure enough for wire. One ladder, three rungs, and the reactivity series is the price list that assigns every metal its rung.

## Sub lesson 3 — Vietnam's way — the survey before the cases

This course already holds the single-metal cases — aluminium by electrolysis, copper from chalcopyrite, iron in the blast furnace. Vietnam's book teaches the **general survey first**, one lesson for occurrence and all the methods together, so each case that follows is an example of a rule already stated.

That order is itself the lesson's content: a learner who holds Bài 20's ladder can meet a new metal, place it in the series, and predict — before any detail — whether it sleeps free in a river gravel or waits in an ore for the furnace or the electrolytic cell.

## The key ideas

- Only the **least reactive** metals occur **free**; the rest occur combined — in **ores**: oxides, sulfides, carbonates, chlorides.
- Extraction climbs a ladder: **heat alone** (least reactive) → **carbon reduction** (middle) → **electrolysis** (most reactive).
- The **reactivity series assigns the method** — it is the price list of extraction.
- Concentration comes first and refining last; the single-metal lessons of the course are this survey's cases.
