## Sub lessons
1. **Analysis by weighing — the two forms** — **Gravimetric analysis** measures by mass: turn the sought constituent into something that can be weighed exactly, and let the balance do the counting. Egypt teaches it in **two forms — volatilisation**, where the sought part is driven off and weighed by its loss, and **precipitation**, where it is brought down as a solid and weighed directly.
2. **Volatilisation — the water in barium chloride** — Heat **hydrated barium chloride** and its **water of crystallisation** leaves as vapour. Heat, cool and weigh — then heat again, **to constant mass**, until no more water can leave. The mass lost is the water; the residue is the anhydrous salt; their ratio fixes the formula: **BaCl₂·2H₂O**. Constant mass is the method's honesty rule — you stop when the balance stops changing.
3. **Precipitation — barium weighed as its sulphate** — To find the **barium chloride in a solution**, precipitate its barium completely as **barium sulphate**, collect the BaSO₄ on **ashless filter paper**, **ignite it in a crucible** (the paper burns away to nothing), cool and weigh the pure BaSO₄. From that one mass, the barium — and so the original barium chloride — is calculated exactly.
