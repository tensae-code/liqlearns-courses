# Gravimetric analysis — by volatilisation and precipitation

## Do it first
Before you read: no indicators, no burette, no colour change — just a balance, a crucible and patience, yet the answer comes out to the milligram. Say out loud: what could you possibly weigh to find how much of a substance was dissolved invisibly in a beaker of water? Then read on: Egypt's gravimetry chapter weighs what leaves, and what falls.

## Sub lesson 1 — Analysis by weighing — the two forms

**Gravimetric analysis** measures by mass: turn the sought constituent into something that can be weighed exactly, and let the balance do the counting. Egypt teaches it in **two forms — volatilisation**, where the sought part is driven off and weighed by its loss, and **precipitation**, where it is brought down as a solid and weighed directly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Volatilisation — the water in barium chloride

Heat **hydrated barium chloride** and its **water of crystallisation** leaves as vapour. Heat, cool and weigh — then heat again, **to constant mass**, until no more water can leave. The mass lost is the water; the residue is the anhydrous salt; their ratio fixes the formula: **BaCl₂·2H₂O**. Constant mass is the method's honesty rule — you stop when the balance stops changing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Precipitation — barium weighed as its sulphate

To find the **barium chloride in a solution**, precipitate its barium completely as **barium sulphate**, collect the BaSO₄ on **ashless filter paper**, **ignite it in a crucible** (the paper burns away to nothing), cool and weigh the pure BaSO₄. From that one mass, the barium — and so the original barium chloride — is calculated exactly.

## The key ideas

- **Gravimetric analysis measures by weighing**, in two forms: **volatilisation** (weigh the loss) and **precipitation** (weigh the solid).
- Volatilisation case: heating hydrated BaCl₂ **to constant mass** gives its water of crystallisation — **BaCl₂·2H₂O**.
- Precipitation case: barium weighed as **BaSO₄**, collected on **ashless filter paper** and ignited in a crucible.
