# What environmental chemistry studies

## Do it first
A new discipline names itself in this lesson. Before reading, write its job description in one sentence: which processes, which substances, which three homes?

Environmental chemistry studies chemical processes and pollutants in air, water and soil.

It asks where pollutants come from, how they move and change, and what they do to living systems.

It supplies the science under every pollution lesson that follows in this unit.

## Say it to your companion
Explain What environmental chemistry studies to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
