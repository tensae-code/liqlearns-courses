# Aldehydes vs ketones — telling them apart

## Do it first
Two clear liquids, both C=O. One coats a test tube with a mirror of silver; the other does nothing. Before reading, say which one performs — and what 'easily oxidised' has to do with it. (Teacher guide evidence confirms this is a full standalone teaching section, §6.3.3.)

[Picture 21692 — A silver-mirror tube beside a negative one.]

Both families are polar (higher boiling than alkanes) but cannot hydrogen-bond to each other, and the smaller members dissolve in water. They are prepared by oxidation of alcohols — primary giving aldehydes, secondary ketones.

Aldehydes oxidise easily — on to carboxylic acids — while ketones resist oxidation. This difference is the telling-apart test.

Tollens' reagent gives a silver mirror with aldehydes only; Fehling-type tests likewise answer aldehydes (as presented). Addition and reduction reactions (to alcohols) are shared ground.
