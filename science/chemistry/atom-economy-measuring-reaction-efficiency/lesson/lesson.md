# Atom economy — measuring reaction efficiency

## Do it first
Two factories make the same product. One turns nearly everything it buys into product; the other throws half its raw material away as by-products. Say out loud: which is the better reaction — and could you have known it from the equations alone, before either factory ran? Then read on: Jordan's Grade 11 book closes its stoichiometry lesson with exactly that measure.

## Sub lesson 1 — Efficiency beyond yield

Stoichiometry already has one score for a reaction — percentage yield, what you actually got against what the equation promised. But yield is measured after the fact, and it forgives a wasteful equation: a reaction can yield 100% and still throw away half its atoms, if the equation itself makes an unwanted by-product alongside the wanted one. The book adds the prior question: **how much of what reacts is even capable of ending in the product?**

## Sub lesson 2 — Atom economy — the share that lands in the product

**Atom economy** is that share, expressed as a percentage: the mass of the wanted product the equation can make, against the total mass of all the products (equivalently, of all the reactants), times 100. It is read straight off the balanced equation — no experiment, no laboratory, just the mole masses in the equation. Every atom of reactant that the equation sends into a by-product lowers the score; an equation whose only product is the wanted one scores 100%.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading reactions by their atom economy

The measure sorts reaction types by design. **Addition** reactions, where everything joins into one product, stand at 100% by construction. Reactions that swap partners — substitutions and eliminations — score lower by exactly the mass they discard. That is the lesson's use: atom economy is the efficiency check a chemist runs **on paper first**, choosing between rival routes to the same product before yield, cost or waste are ever measured in glass.

## The key ideas

- Atom economy: (mass of wanted product ÷ total mass of all products) × 100, read off the balanced equation.
- It measures the equation's own efficiency — unlike yield, it is known before any experiment, and a 100%-yield reaction can still score low.
- Addition reactions score 100%; routes that discard by-products score lower by the mass discarded.

## Do It Yourself
1. A reaction's equation makes 60 g of wanted product and 40 g of by-product per run of the equation. Find its atom economy. (60 ÷ (60 + 40) × 100 = 60%.)
2. Why can a reaction have 100% yield and poor atom economy? (Yield compares what you got with what the equation promised; if the equation itself makes a by-product, every atom in that by-product is wasted even when the reaction runs perfectly.)
3. Which reaction type scores 100% by construction, and why? (Addition — all the reactant atoms join into the single wanted product, so nothing is discarded.)
