Today: **Stereoisomerism — enantiomers and diastereomers** — Greece's way, from its own book.

Constitutional isomers differ in their connections. Stereoisomers keep the connections — and differ only in space.

A carbon with four different groups has two arrangements: mirror images that will never fit onto each other. Those are enantiomers — left hand, right hand.

Enantiomers share almost everything, except one thing: each turns plane-polarised light the opposite way.

Stereoisomers that are not mirror images are diastereomers — different melting points, different solubilities, and separable by ordinary means.
