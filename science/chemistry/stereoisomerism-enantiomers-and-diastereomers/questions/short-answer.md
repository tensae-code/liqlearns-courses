# Stereoisomerism — enantiomers and diastereomers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can the two arrangements of a carbon with four different groups not be turned into each other?
**Answer: They are mirror images, and no rotation in space superposes a thing on its mirror image — as with left and right hands, the handedness itself differs.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Two enantiomers have the same melting point. Name the one physical measurement that tells them apart, and say how.
**Answer: The rotation of plane-polarised light: one rotates it by an amount in one direction, the other by the same amount in the opposite direction — optical activity.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A molecule has two handed centres; form A and form B match at one centre and differ at the other. What is their relationship?
**Answer: Diastereomers — stereoisomers that are not mirror images of each other, so they differ in physical properties and can be separated by ordinary methods.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) What does the D/L notation do in this subject?
**Answer: It labels the handed (mirror) forms — a naming strand for which enantiomer is which, the notation biochemistry keeps for its sugars and amino acids (filed in Greece's placement at p. 318).**
