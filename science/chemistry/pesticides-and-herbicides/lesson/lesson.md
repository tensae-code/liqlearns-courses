# Pesticides and herbicides

## Do it first
A sprayed field yields more — and the spray does not read labels about which insects are welcome. Before reading, say what two ideas must travel with every farm chemical: class and care.

[Picture 21756 — Farm-chemical containers with safety marks.]

Pesticides kill pests; herbicides kill weeds — classes of farm chemicals grouped by their target and chemistry, as presented.

They raise agricultural yields by protecting crops.

The section frames their hazards and care: correct dose, protective handling and respect for water and non-target life.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Pesticides and herbicides to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
