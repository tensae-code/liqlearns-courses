# Flame tests — colours in the flame — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Give the flame-test routine in three steps.
**Answer: Clean the wire loop; dip it into the sample; hold it in a hot flame and read the colour the metal gives the flame while the sample is in it.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is a muddy or unexpected colour a reason to repeat the test rather than record a name?
**Answer: The colour is the whole evidence: a contaminated wire or leftover sample mixes colours, so the observation cannot support an inference — clean the wire and test again until the colour is true.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What is the flame test's place in the workbook's qualitative-analysis scheme?
**Answer: It is Experiment 10 in the ion-identification run — the fastest answer to which metal (cation) is present — surrounded by wet-test experiments (metal ions, the metal in three salts, ions in solution, a transition metal) that confirm what the colour claims.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Distinguish the observation and the inference in a completed flame test.
**Answer: The observation is the colour the flame showed; the inference is the metal named from that colour — the workbook's discipline is to record both, separately, in the observation-and-inference table.**
