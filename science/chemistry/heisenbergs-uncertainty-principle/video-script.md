# Video script — Heisenberg's uncertainty principle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

To find an electron you must bounce light off it — and the bounce knocks it off course.

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: the position and the momentum of a particle cannot both be known exactly at the same time.

Measuring one more precisely makes the other less certain — this is built into nature, not a fault of instruments.

Because of it, an electron cannot be assigned a sharp orbit path.

The atom must instead be described by probabilities — where the electron is likely to be.

Now you. Say it back: What two quantities cannot both be known exactly?
Answer: Position and momentum.
