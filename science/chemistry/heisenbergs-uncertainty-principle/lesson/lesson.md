# Heisenberg's uncertainty principle

## Do it first
To find an electron you must bounce light off it — and the bounce knocks it off course. Before reading, say why this is not just bad instruments, but a law of nature.

[Picture 21619 — The sharp orbit fades into a cloud of probability.]

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: the position and the momentum of a particle cannot both be known exactly at the same time.

Measuring one more precisely makes the other less certain — this is built into nature, not a fault of instruments.

Because of it, an electron cannot be assigned a sharp orbit path.

The atom must instead be described by probabilities — where the electron is likely to be. This opens the quantum-mechanical model.
