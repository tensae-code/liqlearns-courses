## Hydrogen bonding and intermolecular forces

Where the student textbook names intermolecular forces only in passing and defers them to Grade 11, the guide's teaching notes and review answers treat them as teachable content here: ionic bonds are stronger than the intermolecular forces in covalent molecules; covalent compounds' melting/boiling and density behaviour is explained through three named intermolecular attractions — dipole–dipole forces, hydrogen bonding and van der Waals forces — with hydrogen-bond examples listed as water, hydrogen fluoride, ammonia, methanol and ethanol, and a review question requiring hydrogen-bond formation to be shown with a diagram.
