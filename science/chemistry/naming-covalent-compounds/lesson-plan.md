# Lesson plan — Naming covalent compounds

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Naming covalent compounds
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** which elements form covalent compounds, writing their formulas from the outer-electron ratio, and naming them with the Greek prefixes mono/di/tri/tetra/penta/hexa and an -ide ending — as Laos's Grade 11 (ມ.6) textbook teaches it in Ch. 7 §8 (printed pp. 44–45), closing table naming CO₂, CO and P₄O₁₀ in English
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. No other lesson is needed first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Which elements form covalent compounds** — Laos's Grade 11 book (§8 of its covalent-bonding chapter) first sorts the pairs that bond covalently: **non-metal with non-metal** (H₂O, CO₂), **metalloid with non-metal** (BF₃), and even **a few metals such as Be and Sn** with non-metals.
2. **Writing the formula from the outer electrons** — The formula is fixed by the atoms' outer electrons: each atom shares until its outer shell is complete, and the ratio of atoms is the ratio that completes both.
3. **Naming with Greek prefixes and -ide** — The name states the counts: the second element takes an **-ide** ending, and **Greek prefixes** announce how many atoms of each element stand in the formula — mono (1), di (2), tri (3), tetra (4), penta (5), hexa (6).
