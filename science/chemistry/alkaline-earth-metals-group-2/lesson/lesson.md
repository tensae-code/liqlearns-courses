## Sub lesson 1 — Two outer electrons — the family character

The **alkaline-earth metals** are **group 2 (IIA)**: **beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium and barium** (radium, radioactive, at the foot). Vietnam's Grade 12 book follows its group IA lesson with Bài 25, Group IIA elements — the two s-block families taught as a pair.

Each atom holds **two outer electrons**, both lost in reactions to give **+2 ions**. Holding two electrons more tightly than group 1 holds its one, the family is harder, denser and less reactive than the alkali metals — the same s-block story at a steadier pitch.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reactions — steadier, and stronger down the group

With water the order shows itself: beryllium and magnesium barely react with cold water (magnesium burns brilliantly in steam and in air, the photographer's flash), while **calcium reacts steadily**, strontium and barium vigorously — reactivity again **increasing down the group** as the outer electrons sit farther out.

The metals burn in oxygen to their oxides and react with acids to salts and hydrogen. Their hydroxides are sparingly soluble alkalis — calcium hydroxide is **limewater**, the test for carbon dioxide — and their carbonates, above all calcium carbonate, are the rocks of half the landscape.

## Sub lesson 3 — The calcium strand — lime, bone and shell

Calcium carries the family's public face: **limestone and marble** (CaCO₃) for cement and building, **quicklime and slaked lime** for farms and mortar, calcium in **bones, teeth and shells**, and magnesium at the centre of every chlorophyll molecule and in hundreds of the body's enzymes.

Flame tests paint the family: calcium brick-red, strontium crimson, barium apple-green — the colours of fireworks. One electron count, two lost electrons, explains the hardness, the flames and the limestone hills alike.

## The key ideas

- Group 2 — **Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba** — holds **two outer electrons**, lost to form **+2 ions**; the family is harder and steadier than group 1.
- Reactivity **increases down the group**: magnesium needs steam to show it, calcium reacts steadily with cold water.
- Calcium hydroxide is **limewater**; calcium carbonate is **limestone** — the family's chemistry is written across buildings, bones and shells.
- Flame colours mark the members: **calcium brick-red, strontium crimson, barium apple-green**.
