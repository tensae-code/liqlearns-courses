# Lesson plan — Alkaline earth metals — group 2

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Alkaline earth metals — group 2
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the alkaline-earth metals as a group lesson, as Vietnam's Grade 12 book teaches them (Ch.7, Bài 25 Group IIA elements): beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium and barium — two outer electrons each, harder and less violent than group 1, forming +2 ions and the lime-and-limestone chemistry the world builds with
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Alkali metals — group 1 is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Two outer electrons — the family character** — The **alkaline-earth metals** are **group 2 (IIA)**: **beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium and barium** (radium, radioactive, at the foot). Vietnam's Grade 12 book follows its group IA lesson with Bài 25, Group IIA elements (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
2. **Reactions — steadier, and stronger down the group** — With water the order shows itself: beryllium and magnesium barely react with cold water (magnesium burns brilliantly in steam and in air, the photographer's flash), while **calcium reacts steadily**, strontium and barium vigorously (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
3. **The calcium strand — lime, bone and shell** — Calcium carries the family's public face: **limestone and marble** (CaCO₃) for cement and building, **quicklime and slaked lime** for farms and mortar, calcium in **bones, teeth and shells**, and magnesium at the centre of every chlorophyll molecule and in hundreds of the body's enzymes. (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Confuses group 2 with group 1 → return to the electron count: two outer electrons, +2 ions, a steadier family — then compare the two water reactions side by side.
