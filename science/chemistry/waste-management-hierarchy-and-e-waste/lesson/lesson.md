# Waste management — the hierarchy and e-waste

## Do it first
Every town's waste could be attacked at five different heights — before it exists, or after it is buried. Before reading, say out loud: which attack must come first, and which must be the last resort? Bulgaria's Grade 10 lesson ranks the five and then faces the stream growing fastest of all.

## Sub lesson 1 — The five ranked activities

Waste management runs in **five ranked activities: 1 prevent, 2 reduce, 3 reuse, 4 recycle, 5 landfill**. Attention falls **first on preventing and reducing (Reduce)**, then reuse and recycling preparation, other utilisation such as energy, and **only lastly disposal**. The ranking is the lesson: every tonne stopped at step 1 never needs steps 2–5 at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — E-waste — and Bulgaria against the EU

The **fastest-growing stream is e-waste** — plastics, **valuable gold and copper**, **harmful mercury**, and the **NF₃ of LCD screens with a greenhouse effect 17,000 times CO₂'s**. **Batteries are collected separately** (a school collection is photographed); **fryer fats find a second life as fuel**. The numbers compare Bulgaria with the EU: waste generated **460 vs 492 kg per person yearly**; recycled **103 vs 132**; landfilled **318 vs 162** — Bulgaria landfills nearly double the EU's share, which is the hierarchy read backwards.

## The key ideas

- Hierarchy: 1 prevent, 2 reduce, 3 reuse, 4 recycle, 5 landfill — disposal is the last resort, prevention the first attention.
- E-waste is the fastest-growing stream: gold and copper (value), mercury (harm), NF₃ at 17,000× CO₂. Batteries collected separately.
- Bulgaria vs EU per person yearly: generated 460 vs 492 kg; recycled 103 vs 132; landfilled 318 vs 162.
