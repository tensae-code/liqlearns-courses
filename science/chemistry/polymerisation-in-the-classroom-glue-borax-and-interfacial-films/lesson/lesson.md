# Polymerisation in the classroom — glue, borax and interfacial films

## Do it first
Before you read: one beaker of school glue, four paper cups, and a spoon of a white powder dissolved in water — and the four cups' contents come out with four different stretches. Say out loud: what could a dissolved powder do *between* a glue's chains to change how far the lump stretches? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 12 teacher guide runs polymerisation as a classroom preparation, and this lesson stands on that guide evidence, labelled as such.

## Sub lesson 1 — The preparation — four cups, rising doses [teacher evidence]

The guide's **Activity 15, preparing a polymer (تحضير البوليمر)**, works with classroom materials: **60 mL of paper glue mixed with 60 mL of water** in a glass beaker, poured into **four paper cups**; a **borax solution — 5 g in 100 mL water** — is added in **increasing doses, 5 mL, 10 mL, 20 mL**, and each cup's contents are **drawn out and stretched**. The variable is one only: how much borax each cup receives.

## Sub lesson 2 — The result — stretch follows cross-linking [teacher evidence]

The result is the lesson: **the stretch length grows as the borax share rises**. The guide's explanation is structural: **borax cross-links the glue polymer** — it ties chain to chain — and **the more cross-linking, the greater the molecular weight and the stronger the polymer**. Four cups, one ladder: dose, cross-links, strength, read off in centimetres of stretch.

## Sub lesson 3 — The family of processes [teacher evidence]

The guide then classifies **the ways polymerisation is run**: **bulk polymerisation (بلمرة الكتلة), solution, suspension (مستحلبات) and emulsion polymerisation** — and names **interfacial polymerisation (البلمرة البينية), the nylon rope trick's method**, as the same family of process: polymers are made where two phases meet, in a stirred tank, in a droplet cloud — or, as in this activity, in a paper cup, by cross-linking a polymer that already exists.

## The key ideas

- [Teacher evidence] Activity 15: **glue + water (60 + 60 mL)**, four cups, **borax solution (5 g/100 mL)** at **5, 10, 20 mL** — **stretch grows with the borax dose**.
- [Teacher evidence] The mechanism is **cross-linking**: borax ties the glue's chains; **more cross-linking → greater molecular weight → stronger polymer**.
- [Teacher evidence] Polymerisation's ways are classified: **bulk, solution, suspension, emulsion** — and **interfacial** polymerisation (the nylon rope trick) is the same family.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does the third cup stretch further than the first — answer with the guide's mechanism, not with 'more borax'. (More borax means more cross-links tying the glue polymer's chains together; the guide's rule is that more cross-linking gives greater molecular weight and a stronger polymer, and the stretch length is that strength measured [teacher evidence].)
2. The activity changes one thing between cups. Name it, and name what is held the same. (Changed: the borax dose — 5, 10, 20 mL (one cup keeps none or the smallest share as the comparison). Held the same: the glue–water mixture each cup receives — 60 mL glue with 60 mL water, divided among the four cups [teacher evidence].)
3. The nylon rope trick is named in this lesson though no nylon is made in the cups. Why does the guide name it here? (Because it classifies the process family: the rope trick's interfacial polymerisation is one of the ways polymerisation is run, and the guide sets the classroom cross-linking inside that whole family — bulk, solution, suspension, emulsion, interfacial [teacher evidence].)
