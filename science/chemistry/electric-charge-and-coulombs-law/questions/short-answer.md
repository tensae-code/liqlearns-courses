# Electric charge and Coulomb's law — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** State one thing Italy's book teaches in this lesson about Coulomb's law.
**Answer: Like charges repel and opposite charges attract; Coulomb's law, F = k·Q1·Q2/d², makes the force directly proportional to the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance, with k ≈ 9.0·10⁹ N·m²/C² in vacuum.**

**2.** State one thing Italy's book teaches in this lesson about elementary charge.
**Answer: Crookes's cathode rays, Thomson's 1897 charge/mass measurement and Millikan's 1910 elementary charge (−1.602·10⁻¹⁹ C) together reveal the electron.**

**3.** State one thing Italy's book teaches in this lesson about conserved.
**Answer: Rubbing creates nothing — charge is conserved: one body loses exactly the electrons the other gains.**
