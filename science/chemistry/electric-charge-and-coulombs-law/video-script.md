Today: **Electric charge and Coulomb's law** — Italy's way, from the book's own treatment. Like charges repel and opposite charges attract; Coulomb's law, F = k·Q1·Q2/d², makes the force directly proportional to the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance, with k ≈ 9.0·10⁹ N·m²/C² in vacuum. Crookes's cathode rays, Thomson's 1897 charge/mass measurement and Millikan's 1910 elementary charge (−1.602·10⁻¹⁹ C) together reveal the electron. Rubbing creates nothing — charge is conserved: one body loses exactly the electrons the other gains.
