# Lesson plan — Electric charge and Coulomb's law

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Electric charge and Coulomb's law
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Like charges repel and opposite charges attract; Coulomb's law, F = k·Q1·Q2/d², makes the force directly proportional to the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance, with k ≈ 9.0·10⁹ N·m²/C² in vacuum.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State in their own words: Like charges repel and opposite charges attract; Coulomb's law, F = k·Q1·Q2/d², makes the force directly proportional to the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance, with k ≈ 9.0·10⁹ N·m²/C² in vacuum.
2. State in their own words: Crookes's cathode rays, Thomson's 1897 charge/mass measurement and Millikan's 1910 elementary charge (−1.602·10⁻¹⁹ C) together reveal the electron.
3. State in their own words: Rubbing creates nothing — charge is conserved: one body loses exactly the electrons the other gains.
