# Salts that turn basic

## Do it first
Sodium ethanoate comes from a strong base and a weak acid. Its solution turns litmus blue. Before reading, accuse the right ion: which one reacts with water, and what does it leave behind?

[Picture 21723 — An acetate ion leaving OH⁻ behind.]

In a salt of a weak acid and a strong base, the anion hydrolyses: it takes a proton from water, leaving OH⁻ behind.

The solution is therefore alkaline. The hydrolysis constant Kh links to the parent acid's Ka (through Kw), and the pH is calculated from it as presented.

The cation of the strong base spectates — it does not hydrolyse.
