# Lesson plan — Adsorption and catalysis

## Sub lessons
1. **Adsorption — gathering at the surface** — Adsorption accumulates a substance at a surface, never through the bulk: adsorbate on adsorbent, exothermic, growing with surface area. Held by van der Waals forces it is physisorption — multilayer, reversible, favoured cold; held by chemical bonds it is chemisorption — one layer, specific, hard to undo. The Freundlich isotherm, x/m = k·p^(1/n), measures the gathering.
2. **Catalysis — promoters, poisons, and the two phases** — A catalyst speeds a reaction by a lower-energy path and is not consumed: homogeneous when it shares the reactants' phase, heterogeneous when it does not — Haber iron, Contact V₂O₅, nickel hydrogenation. Promoters raise its activity (molybdenum for iron), poisons destroy it by occupying the surface (carbon monoxide for iron), and activity and selectivity are its two measures.
3. **Shape-selective catalysis — and the living catalysts** — The zeolites select by shape as well as chemistry: only molecules that fit their molecular-sized pores can enter, react and leave — ZSM-5 dehydrates alcohols inside its pores directly to petrol-range hydrocarbons. Enzyme catalysis stands beside them as life's own selective catalysis, one substrate per enzyme at an optimum temperature and pH — the bridge to the Biomolecules unit's enzymes lesson.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Confuses adsorption with absorption → return to the spelling's meaning: adsorption gathers at the surface only (aDhesion at the face); absorption soaks through the whole bulk, as a sponge takes water.
