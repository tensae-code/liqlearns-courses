# Hands-on — Adsorption and catalysis

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Surface census (paper only)
Adsorption grows with surface area. Rank these by the surface they offer a gas, smallest first: a solid lump of charcoal, the same mass crushed coarse, the same mass powdered, the same mass as activated charcoal. Justify the winner in one sentence using sub lesson 1.

## Kitchen adsorption (kitchen materials only)
With an adult, put a spoonful of powdered charcoal (from a water-filter cartridge or aquarium shop grade, food-safe work only — do not taste anything) into a glass of strongly coloured drink such as cola, stir, wait ten minutes and filter through a coffee filter. Record the colour before and after. Say which partner is adsorbate and which adsorbent, and why the colour left the liquid.

## Catalyst matchmaker (paper only)
Five industrial reactions are listed with a space for their catalyst and one for a promoter or poison where the lesson names one: Haber synthesis of ammonia; Contact-process oxidation of SO₂ (name the older and the modern catalyst); hydrogenation of vegetable oils; alcohols to petrol over ZSM-5; decomposition of KClO₃ in the laboratory preparation of oxygen. Fill the spaces from the lesson text and mark each case homogeneous or heterogeneous.
