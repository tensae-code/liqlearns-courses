Today: **Adsorption and catalysis** — India's way: the Surface Chemistry unit's first two sections — chemistry that happens not in the bulk but on the face where two phases meet.

Lay a gas against a solid and some of it stays on the surface. That gathering is adsorption: the gas is the adsorbate, the solid the adsorbent. Held by weak van der Waals forces it is physisorption — layers deep, easily reversed, gone on warming. Held by chemical bonds it is chemisorption — one layer only, specific, and hard to undo. Charcoal in a gas mask, silica gel against humidity, the catalyst's working face: all adsorption at work. How much gathers follows Freundlich's rule, x/m = k·p^(1/n).
Catalysis is adsorption put to work. A catalyst speeds a reaction and emerges unchanged; a promoter makes the catalyst better, a poison ruins it. Iron carries the Haber process with molybdenum as its promoter and carbon monoxide as its feared poison. And the zeolites add shape to chemistry: their pores admit only molecules of a size, so ZSM-5 turns alcohols into petrol-range hydrocarbons and lets nothing larger form.
