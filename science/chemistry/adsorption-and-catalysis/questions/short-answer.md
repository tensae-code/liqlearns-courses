# Adsorption and catalysis — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does adsorption always release heat?
**Answer: A surface particle holds unbalanced forces; when an adsorbate molecule is held, those forces are satisfied and the system's energy falls, the surplus leaving as heat — adsorption is exothermic.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A gas adsorbs in many layers, leaves the surface completely on gentle warming, and adsorbs on any solid. Name the kind and its holding force.
**Answer: Physisorption — held by weak van der Waals forces: multilayered, non-specific and reversible, exactly as described.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Distinguish a promoter from a poison with the Haber iron as the stage.
**Answer: A promoter (molybdenum) is not itself a catalyst but increases the iron catalyst's activity. A poison (carbon monoxide) occupies the iron's surface and destroys its activity.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) What two measures decide a good heterogeneous catalyst, in NCERT's account?
**Answer: Activity — chemisorbing the reactants strongly enough to hold and activate them, but not so strongly they cannot leave; and selectivity — speeding the wanted reaction among the several the reactants could undergo.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can a zeolite do what a flat catalyst surface cannot?
**Answer: Its catalysis happens inside pores of molecular size. Only molecules that fit can enter and react, and only products that fit can leave, so the pore's shape selects the reaction — shape-selective catalysis.**
