## Democritus

Leucippus and his pupil Democritus (c. 460–370 BC) proposed atomism, in five points: all matter is atoms too small to see and indivisible (atomos = uncuttable); a void of empty space lies between atoms so they can move;

atoms are completely solid; atoms are homogeneous, with no internal structure; atoms differ in size, shape and weight, and change is their rearranging — the "solid sphere" model.

Aristotle's opposition buried the idea for two millennia; the Greeks' drawback is named plainly: they reasoned but never experimented.
