# Aluminium sulfate — from paper to fire foam

## Do it first
Before you read: one salt puts out oil fires with foam, sizes paper, tans leather and cleans drinking water. Say out loud: what would a salt need to be — acid or alkali, calm or hungry — to be hired for all four? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book makes the salt, heats it, dissolves it, and puts it to work.

## Sub lesson 1 — Making it — and what heat does to it

Aluminium sulfate is made from the oxide: **Al₂O₃ + 3H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂O**. It **crystallises with 18 waters** — Al₂(SO₄)₃·18H₂O. Heat it and the waters leave; at **red heat it breaks down altogether to alumina, SO₂ and O₂** — the salt unmaking itself back toward the oxide it came from.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its solution — acidic by hydrolysis

Dissolved, aluminium sulfate's solution is **acidic by hydrolysis**: the aluminium ion, child of a weak base, takes hydroxide from the water and leaves the solution acid — the salt-hydrolysis behaviour the Grade 11 course teaches in its own unit, met here inside a working salt. A salt of a strong acid and a weak base does not give a neutral solution, and this one's work depends on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — At work — foam, paper, leather, water

The uses are practical and named one by one. In **foam fire extinguishers**, aluminium sulfate with **sodium bicarbonate** makes a **stable CO₂ foam** that blankets **oil fires** — water alone spreads burning oil, foam floats and smothers. In industry it serves **paper making, leather tanning, dye fixing and water purification** — one salt, four trades, each spending its acidity and its aluminium.

## The key ideas

- Made by **Al₂O₃ + 3H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂O**; crystallises with **18 waters**; at **red heat → alumina + SO₂ + O₂**.
- Its solution is **acidic by hydrolysis** — the salt of a strong acid and a weak base.
- Uses: **foam fire extinguishers** (with NaHCO₃, a stable CO₂ foam for **oil fires**), **paper making, leather tanning, dye fixing, water purification**.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why is aluminium sulfate solution acidic, though no acid was added? (By hydrolysis: the aluminium ion comes from a weak base, so it reacts with water, taking hydroxide and leaving excess hydrogen ions — the solution of a strong-acid/weak-base salt is acidic.)
2. Why is foam, not water, the answer to an oil fire — and what makes the foam in the extinguisher? (Water spreads burning oil; the foam floats on the oil and smothers the flame. In the extinguisher, aluminium sulfate reacting with sodium bicarbonate generates the CO₂ that the foam is made of.)
3. A sample of the crystals is heated gently, then to red heat. What leaves at each stage? (Gently: the 18 waters of crystallisation leave. At red heat the salt itself breaks down, leaving alumina and giving off SO₂ and O₂.)
