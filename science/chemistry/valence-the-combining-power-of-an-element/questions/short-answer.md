# Valence — the combining power of an element — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How is valency counted in a shared-bond compound? Give the lesson's three examples.
**Answer: It equals the number of shared pairs the atom forms: oxygen 2 in water, nitrogen 3 in ammonia, carbon 4 in methane — read off the models.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How is valency counted in an ionic compound, and why does the counting change?
**Answer: It equals the number of electrons the atom loses or gains, because ionic compounds transfer electrons instead of sharing pairs — the combining power is the same idea, counted the other way.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What follows the countings in the lesson, and what is it for?
**Answer: An element valency table, gathering each element's valency — the tool for writing compound formulas by electrical neutrality, where the partners' valencies must balance.**
