# Degree of dissociation α — strong, weak and medium electrolytes

## Do it first
Two lamps, two acid solutions of the same strength — one lamp blazes, one barely glows. Before reading, say out loud: if both solutions hold the same number of acid molecules, what must differ inside them? Bulgaria's Grade 10 book answers with a single number, α, and a three-band scale of its own.

## Sub lesson 1 — The lamp test and α

At equal **0.1 mol/L**, hydrochloric acid lights the test lamp **bright** and acetic acid **dim** — the free ions differ, so the splitting differs. The **degree of dissociation α = a/A**: the particles that dissociated over all the particles dissolved. α turns the lamp's verdict into a number every electrolyte can be filed by.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bulgaria's three bands

**Strong electrolytes (α > 30%)** dissociate **irreversibly** — nearly all inorganic salts, the alkali and alkaline-earth hydroxides, HCl, H₂SO₄ and HNO₃. **Weak electrolytes (α < 3%)** — acetic acid, phenol, H₂S, HCN — sit in a **molecules ⇌ ions equilibrium**, mostly undissociated. Between them Bulgaria keeps a **medium class (3–30%)** — formic, sulfurous and phosphoric acids — where many two-band courses keep only strong and weak.

## The key ideas

- α = a/A — dissociated particles over dissolved particles; the lamp test at 0.1 mol/L shows it (HCl bright, acetic dim).
- Strong α > 30%: salts, alkali/alkaline-earth hydroxides, HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃ — irreversible. Weak α < 3%: acetic acid, phenol, H₂S, HCN — an equilibrium.
- Medium 3–30% (formic, sulfurous, phosphoric acids) — Bulgaria's third band.

## Do It Yourself
1. Of 1000 dissolved particles, 25 are dissociated. Compute α and file the electrolyte. (α = 25/1000 = 2.5% — a weak electrolyte (below 3%).)
2. Why does 0.1 mol/L HCl light the lamp brightly while 0.1 mol/L acetic acid does not? (HCl is strong — nearly all its particles are free ions to carry the current; acetic acid is weak, so only a small fraction are ions.)
