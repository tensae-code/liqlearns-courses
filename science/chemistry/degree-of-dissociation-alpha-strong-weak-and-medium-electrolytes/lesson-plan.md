# Lesson plan — Degree of dissociation α — strong, weak and medium electrolytes

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Degree of dissociation α — strong, weak and medium electrolytes
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** How far a dissolved substance splits into ions, measured by α = a/A — and Bulgaria's three bands: strong above 30%, medium 3–30%, weak below 3% (Bulgaria, Grade 10 — §22, printed p. 63)

## Sub lessons
1. **The lamp test and α** — At equal **0.1 mol/L**, hydrochloric acid lights the test lamp **bright** and acetic acid **dim** — the free ions differ, so the splitting differs. The **degree of dissociation α = a/A**: the particles that dissociated over all the particles dissolved. α turns the lamp's verdict into a number every electrolyte can be filed by.
2. **Bulgaria's three bands** — **Strong electrolytes (α > 30%)** dissociate **irreversibly** — nearly all inorganic salts, the alkali and alkaline-earth hydroxides, HCl, H₂SO₄ and HNO₃. **Weak electrolytes (α < 3%)** — acetic acid, phenol, H₂S, HCN — sit in a **molecules ⇌ ions equilibrium**, mostly undissociated. Between them Bulgaria keeps a **medium class (3–30%)** — formic, sulfurous and phosphoric acids — where many two-band courses keep only strong and weak.
