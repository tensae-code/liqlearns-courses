# Amides and the peptide bond

## Do it first
Before you read: proteins are chains — and a chain needs links. Say out loud: what single link, repeated hundreds of times, could hold amino acids together into a protein? Then read on: Mauritania's terminal book gives the link a family first — the amides.

## Sub lesson 1 — The amide — a carbonyl carbon carrying nitrogen

The Chimie 7AS book opens its amides chapter (Ch. III, printed p. 55) on structure: an amide is a carboxylic-acid derivative whose carbonyl carbon carries a nitrogen atom. That one arrangement — C(=O)–N — is the family's mark, placing amides in the acid-derivatives family tree beside esters, anhydrides and acyl halides.

## Sub lesson 2 — Classes, naming, preparation

Amides are sorted primary, secondary and tertiary by the nitrogen's substitution. Their names swap the acid's ending: -oïque becomes -amide. Preparation runs from the amines: amine + acid, or the faster doors of the acyl chloride and the anhydride — with two amine molecules needed when the HCl formed must be trapped, one to react and one to catch the acid.

## Sub lesson 3 — The peptide bond — the amide link in proteins

The chapter's named prize: the amide link between amino acids is the peptide bond. Amino acid to amino acid, the same C(=O)–N link repeats, and the chain it builds is a protein. The amide family is thus not a bench curiosity — its link is the one living bodies are sewn together with.

## The key ideas

- An amide: a carboxylic-acid derivative whose carbonyl carbon carries nitrogen — the C(=O)–N mark.
- Classes primary/secondary/tertiary; names swap -oïque for -amide; preparation from amines (acid, acyl chloride or anhydride — two amine molecules when HCl must be trapped).
- The amide link between amino acids is the peptide bond — proteins are chains of it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why are two amine molecules needed when an acyl chloride is the starting door? (One amine molecule forms the amide; the reaction also releases HCl, and a second amine molecule is needed to trap it.)
2. An acid named with the ending -oïque gives an amide by what naming swap? (The -oïque ending is swapped for -amide.)
3. What joins amino acids in a protein, and which family does that link belong to? (The peptide bond — an amide link, C(=O)–N — so proteins are chained by the amide family's bond.)
