# Writing electron configurations

## Do it first
Nineteen electrons must be placed around a potassium nucleus, in order, obeying three rules. Before reading, say what could go wrong if everyone placed them wherever they liked.

[Picture 21624 — Boxes fill by Hund's rule — singly first.]

## Sub lesson 1 — Aufbau — lowest first
The Aufbau principle: electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals first, in the filling order the diagram gives.

[GIF 21625 — boxes filling one by one, then pairing]

## Sub lesson 2 — Pauli — two per orbital, opposite spins
The Pauli exclusion principle: one orbital holds at most two electrons, and they must have opposite spins.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hund — singly before pairing
Hund's rule: in a set of equal-energy orbitals, electrons occupy each singly first, with spins parallel, before pairing up.

## Sub lesson 4 — Configurations, diagrams and the exceptions
With the three rules you can write the ground-state configuration and the orbital (box) diagram of any atom or ion — including the Cr/Cu-type exceptions, where a half-filled or fully filled subshell wins extra stability.
