# Grade 11 chemistry practicals and activities

## Do it first
Every unit of Grade 11 chemistry carries hands-on work. Before reading, name one activity you remember from each unit — this lesson gathers the strand so none of it is lost.

[Picture 21711 — A bench with the units' simple apparatus.]

Unit openings begin with a Start-up Activity in groups — burning wood and rusting iron open the atomic unit's discussion.

Rate is measured practically: collecting gas, timing mass loss and watching colour change (Unit 4's experiments).

The rancidity activity stores potato chips open and sealed and compares smells (Unit 6). Safety runs through all of it: follow the book's own handling for acids and flames, work in a supervised laboratory, and never taste laboratory materials.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Grade 11 chemistry practicals and activities to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
