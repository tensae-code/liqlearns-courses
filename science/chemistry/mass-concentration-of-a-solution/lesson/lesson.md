# Mass concentration of a solution

## Do it first
Before you read: two drinks both taste sweet. Which one actually carries more sugar in each glass — and how could you prove it with numbers instead of taste? Say your method before reading. The Djibouti Grade 8 guide's activity answers it off the drinks' own labels, with one formula. This lesson stands on the teacher guide only; no Djibouti pupil book is held. Note what this lesson is not: the course already has molar and percent concentrations — mass concentration, mass per litre, is its own measure, and the guide's placement is stocked here for exactly that reason.

## Sub lesson 1 — The measure — mass in each litre

**Mass concentration** answers one question about a solution: **how much dissolved substance (solute) is there in each litre of solution?** It is a mass divided by a volume: the mass of solute dissolved, spread over the volume of solution it is dissolved in.

Written as the guide's formula: **Cm = m / V**, where m is the mass of solute (in grams) and V is the volume of solution (in litres), so Cm comes out in **grams per litre, g/L**. Read any mass concentration as a sentence: '118 g/L' means 118 grams of solute in every litre of that solution.

## Sub lesson 2 — The guide's activity — reading drink labels

Ch. 6's Activité 1 works the measure on **real drink labels** — the sugar declared on the drinks pupils buy. The labels give a mass of sugar for a stated volume of drink; dividing mass by volume (converting the volume to litres first) gives each drink's mass concentration of sugar.

The activity's headline comparison, as the guide computes it: **Coca-Cola at 118 g/L** of sugar against **orange juice at 26 g/L**. Same formula, both drinks — the numbers are now comparable, which they were not as raw label figures in different serving sizes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Answering with the number — the sweetest drink

With both concentrations in g/L, the activity's question answers itself: **the sweetest drink is the one with the highest mass concentration of sugar — Coca-Cola, at 118 g/L**, carrying more than four times the sugar per litre of the orange juice at 26 g/L.

That is the whole skill the placement teaches: define the measure (m/V), keep the units honest (grams, litres, g/L), compute from real data, and let the number — not the taste, not the label's serving size — give the answer. The same Cm measure is then the concentration used through the ionic-solutions chapter that this activity opens.

## The key ideas

- **Mass concentration: Cm = m / V** — mass of solute (g) divided by volume of solution (L); unit **g/L**.
- Read it as a sentence: 118 g/L = **118 grams of solute in every litre** of solution.
- Guide's label comparison (Ch. 6, Activité 1): **Coca-Cola 118 g/L vs orange juice 26 g/L** of sugar.
- The **sweetest drink is the one with the highest Cm** — the number decides, not the taste or the serving size on the label.
- Mass concentration is **not** molar or percent concentration — it is its own measure, mass per litre.
- This lesson stands on **teacher-guide evidence only** (Djibouti, Grade 8, Ch. 6 Activité 1, printed p. 52) — counted nowhere.
