# Mass concentration of a solution — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A label gives 13 g of sugar in 0.5 L. Compute the mass concentration and identify which of the guide's drinks it matches.
**Answer: Cm = m/V = 13 g / 0.5 L = 26 g/L — the orange juice figure in the guide's activity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the activity convert label figures to g/L before comparing the drinks?
**Answer: Labels state masses for different serving volumes, so the raw figures are not comparable. Dividing each mass by its volume in litres puts both drinks on the same per-litre measure, and only then does the comparison — and the 'sweetest' verdict — mean anything.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How is mass concentration different from the molar concentration taught elsewhere in this course?
**Answer: Mass concentration counts mass per litre (g of solute per L of solution, Cm = m/V). Molar concentration counts amount of substance per litre (mol/L). Same volume idea, different numerator — grams here, moles there.**
