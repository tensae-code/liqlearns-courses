Today: **Discovering the nucleus — the gold-foil experiment**.

Rutherford, Geiger and Marsden fired α-particles at thin gold foil (1909/1911): most passed straight, a small fraction deflected at large angles, a few rebounded —…

Conclusion: mass and positive charge concentrate in a tiny nucleus (about 10⁻¹³ cm across) with electrons around it — the planetary/nuclear model.

Its two named drawbacks: a circling electron should radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus (the atom should collapse), and it cannot explain line-spectra patterns.

Rutherford predicted a neutral nuclear particle to explain why proton repulsion does not blow the nucleus apart.
