# Lesson plan — The Earth's crust and common minerals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The Earth's crust and common minerals
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what the Earth's crust is made of — its few abundant elements, read from a crustal-abundance table, and its two great mineral families, the carbonates and the silicates — as Sri Lanka's Grade 12 Chemistry Part II teaches it in its descriptive inorganic chapters (Table 6‑6)

## Sub lessons
1. **What the crust is made of — the abundance table** — The Earth's crust is the thin solid skin we live on, and its chemistry is a short list: a handful of elements make up nearly all of it, with oxygen and silicon together accounting for about three-quarters. Sri Lanka's Part II teaches this descriptively, from a printed abundance table (Table 6‑6, crustal abundances).
2. **Carbonates — the carbonate minerals** — One of the two great mineral families of the crust: salts of carbonic acid, built on the carbonate group CO₃²⁻ — limestone's calcite (CaCO₃) first among them, and dolomite, CaCO₃·MgCO₃, carrying calcium and magnesium together — the Group 2 metals' strand of the same descriptive chapters.
3. **Silicates — the silicon-oxygen framework minerals** — The other great family, and the crust's largest: minerals built on silicon-oxygen units, from quartz (SiO₂) to the feldspars and micas to gem minerals such as beryl, Be₃Al₂(SiO₃)₆. Because oxygen and silicon dominate the crust, most rocks are silicates or built from them.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the abundance table: a handful of elements, oxygen and silicon first, build nearly the whole crust.
- Mixes the two families up → look at the building group: carbonate minerals are built on CO₃²⁻; silicates on silicon-oxygen units.
- Treats dolomite as two separate minerals → read its formula, CaCO₃·MgCO₃: one mineral carrying both Group 2 metals.
