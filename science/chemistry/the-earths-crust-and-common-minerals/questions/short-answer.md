# The Earth's crust and common minerals — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the crust's first four elements in order of abundance.
**Answer: Oxygen first (close to half), then silicon (a little over a quarter), then aluminium — the most abundant metal — then iron.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What field test names a carbonate rock on the spot, and what is seen?
**Answer: A drop of dilute acid on the rock fizzes — bubbles of carbon dioxide are given off — which a silicate does not do.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is dolomite named in the Group 2 strand of the same chapters?
**Answer: Its formula, CaCO₃·MgCO₃, carries both Group 2 metals — calcium and magnesium — together in one mineral.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name three silicate minerals from the lesson and the one the lesson calls a gem.
**Answer: Quartz, feldspar and mica are silicates; the gem named is beryl (whose green variety is emerald).**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) In one sentence: why does the abundance table explain the silicates' dominance before any mineral is named?
**Answer: Because oxygen and silicon — the silicates' building elements — are themselves about three-quarters of the crust, so most of the crust's minerals are bound to be built from them.**
