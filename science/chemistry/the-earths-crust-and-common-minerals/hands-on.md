# Hands-on — The Earth's crust and common minerals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Read a stone like an abundance table
Collect three different stones or pieces of gravel (from a path or garden, with permission). For each one, guess its family — carbonate or silicate — from what you can see: glassy and hard like quartz, or pale and rock-like. A teacher can settle one guess with the family's field test: a drop of dilute acid fizzes on a carbonate and does nothing on a silicate. Record which guesses the test proved right.

## Weigh the crust in handfuls
Take 100 counters, beans or bottle caps. Deal them out as the crust's rough make-up: about 46 for oxygen, 28 for silicon, 8 for aluminium, 5 for iron, and the remaining 13 for everything else together. Look at the piles and say, in one sentence, why most minerals are silicates.

## Formula detective
Write the formulas CaCO₃, CaCO₃·MgCO₃, SiO₂ and Be₃Al₂(SiO₃)₆ on four cards. Sort them into the two families, then answer out loud for each card: which building group decided you — CO₃²⁻ or a silicon-oxygen unit?
