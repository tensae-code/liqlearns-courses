# Laboratory preparation of gases — oxygen, carbon dioxide and ammonia — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can oxygen be collected over water while ammonia cannot?
**Answer: Oxygen is only slightly soluble in water, so it bubbles through and gathers above it; ammonia is extremely soluble and would dissolve into the water instead of collecting.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Write the reason, in the book's terms, that calcium chloride does not stop the carbon dioxide preparation.
**Answer: Calcium chloride is soluble — it leaves the carbonate's surface in the solution, so fresh acid keeps reaching fresh carbonate and the gas keeps forming.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why would concentrated sulfuric acid fail as a drying agent for ammonia, quite apart from safety?
**Answer: Ammonia is alkaline and the acid would react with and consume the gas it was meant only to dry — a drying agent must not react with its gas, which is why calcium oxide is the permitted dryer.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Name two of oxygen's uses from the lesson and the property each spends.
**Answer: Any two: hospital breathing support (life's respiration runs on it); oxy-acetylene and oxy-hydrogen torches (things burn far more fiercely in pure oxygen); spacecraft (a carried supply of the reactive gas where no air exists).**
