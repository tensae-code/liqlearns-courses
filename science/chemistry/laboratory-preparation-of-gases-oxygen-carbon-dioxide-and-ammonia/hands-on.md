# Hands-on — Laboratory preparation of gases — oxygen, carbon dioxide and ammonia

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Carbon dioxide in the kitchen (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only)
Drop a spoon of bicarbonate of soda into a glass of vinegar (ethanoic acid) and watch the fizz — a carbonate meeting an acid, the kitchen cousin of the book's marble and hydrochloric acid. Hold a lighted match just above the foam: the flame dies in the heavy gas pooling there. Write the two properties of carbon dioxide your match has just demonstrated, and the book use each one funds.

## Design before apparatus (paper exercise)
For each of the three gases, complete the design row before looking back: reactants | catalyst or heat? | drying agent (and the forbidden ones) | collection method | fullness/identity test. Then check each cell against the lesson and correct in a different colour, writing the deciding property in the margin.

## The coated chip (paper exercise)
Draw a marble chip twice: once mid-reaction with hydrochloric acid, surface clean and fizzing; once after a minute in sulfuric acid, crusted over. Label the crust with its name and formula class, and write the sentence that explains why one acid works and the other stops.
