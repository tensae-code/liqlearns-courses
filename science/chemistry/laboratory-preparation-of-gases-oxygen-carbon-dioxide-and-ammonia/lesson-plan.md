# Lesson plan — Laboratory preparation of gases — oxygen, carbon dioxide and ammonia

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Laboratory preparation of gases — oxygen, carbon dioxide and ammonia
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how the three school gases are prepared in the laboratory, each preparation designed from the gas's own properties — oxygen from hydrogen peroxide on a manganese(IV) oxide catalyst; carbon dioxide from calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid (never sulfuric, whose insoluble calcium sulfate coats the carbonate and stops the reaction); ammonia from calcium hydroxide and ammonium chloride, dried only with calcium oxide and collected by downward displacement, its fullness tested with moist red litmus — with drying agents and collection methods reasoned from solubility, reactivity and density, and the uses of oxygen and carbon dioxide tied back to their properties
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Describing a gas — p, V, T, n, is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Designing a preparation from the gas's properties — oxygen first** — Preparation designed from solubility, reactivity with drying agents and density; oxygen from hydrogen peroxide with a manganese(IV) oxide catalyst; uses of oxygen tied to properties.
2. **Carbon dioxide — and the acid you must not use** — Carbon dioxide from calcium carbonate + hydrochloric acid (never sulfuric — insoluble calcium sulfate coats the carbonate and stops it); uses: fire extinguishers, dry ice at −78.5 °C, fizzy drinks.
3. **Ammonia — the gas that must stay dry** — Ammonia from calcium hydroxide + ammonium chloride, dried only with calcium oxide, collected by downward displacement, fullness tested with moist red litmus.

## If the learner is stuck
- Chooses a drying agent by name alone → the agent must not react with the gas: ammonia is alkaline, so acidic dryers consume it; calcium oxide is the one the book allows.
- Reaches for sulfuric acid for carbon dioxide → its product calcium sulfate is insoluble and coats the carbonate, sealing the reaction off — hydrochloric acid's soluble chloride keeps the surface clean.
- Collects every gas over water → collection follows solubility and density: ammonia dissolves eagerly and is lighter than air, so it is collected dry, by downward displacement.
