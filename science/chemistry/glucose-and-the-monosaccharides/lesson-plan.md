# Lesson plan — Glucose and the monosaccharides

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Glucose and the monosaccharides
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Glucose's structure deduced test by test — a polyhydroxyaldehyde (aldose) — and the monosaccharide family around it (Bulgaria, Grade 9 — §40, printed pp. 115–117; Grade 10 — §44, printed p. 113)

## Sub lessons
1. **Deducing glucose — Bulgaria's way** — The deduction runs in order: heating shows **C, H, O** and gives **C₆H₁₂O₆**; **neutral litmus excludes –COOH**; **Cu(OH)₂ dissolving to the dark-blue solution proves a polyhydric alcohol, and five –OH groups are counted**; the **silver mirror proves an aldehyde group**. Verdict: glucose is a **polyhydroxyaldehyde — an aldose** — drawn **open-chain with the aldehyde carbon numbered 1**, and **in equilibrium with its cyclic form** (oxygen bridge C1–C5). As an aldehyde it **hydrogenates to sorbitol** and **oxidises to gluconic acid**.
2. **The family — and the cellular fuel** — **Fructose** is the isomeric **ketose** (ketone group at C2) of fruits and honey; **galactose and mannose** stand beside them, **ribose and deoxyribose** in the nucleic acids. Bulgaria's Grade 10 summary adds the classification — carbohydrates split **mono-/di-/polysaccharides** from **photosynthesis (6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂)**, with glucose oxidised even by weak oxidisers like ammoniacal Ag₂O while fructose carries its ketone group. Glucose is the **'cellular fuel'**: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy; blood holds **80–120 mg per 100 cm³**, raised in diabetes, and 5% solutions are infused.
