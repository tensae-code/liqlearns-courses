# Glucose and the monosaccharides

## Do it first
A white powder, sweet, from honey and blood — but what is it inside? Before reading, say out loud: how could bench tests alone prove a molecule holds five alcohol groups AND an aldehyde group? Bulgaria's books deduce glucose exactly that way, test by test.

## Sub lesson 1 — Deducing glucose — Bulgaria's way

The deduction runs in order: heating shows **C, H, O** and gives **C₆H₁₂O₆**; **neutral litmus excludes –COOH**; **Cu(OH)₂ dissolving to the dark-blue solution proves a polyhydric alcohol, and five –OH groups are counted**; the **silver mirror proves an aldehyde group**. Verdict: glucose is a **polyhydroxyaldehyde — an aldose** — drawn **open-chain with the aldehyde carbon numbered 1**, and **in equilibrium with its cyclic form** (oxygen bridge C1–C5). As an aldehyde it **hydrogenates to sorbitol** and **oxidises to gluconic acid**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The family — and the cellular fuel

**Fructose** is the isomeric **ketose** (ketone group at C2) of fruits and honey; **galactose and mannose** stand beside them, **ribose and deoxyribose** in the nucleic acids. Bulgaria's Grade 10 summary adds the classification — carbohydrates split **mono-/di-/polysaccharides** from **photosynthesis (6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂)**, with glucose oxidised even by weak oxidisers like ammoniacal Ag₂O while fructose carries its ketone group. Glucose is the **'cellular fuel'**: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy; blood holds **80–120 mg per 100 cm³**, raised in diabetes, and 5% solutions are infused.

## The key ideas

- Deduction: C₆H₁₂O₆; no –COOH (neutral litmus); five –OH (Cu(OH)₂ dark-blue); one aldehyde group (silver mirror) → an aldose, open-chain ⇌ cyclic.
- Fructose is the ketose isomer; ribose/deoxyribose sit in nucleic acids. Glucose hydrogenates to sorbitol, oxidises to gluconic acid.
- Glucose is the cellular fuel: burned with O₂ for energy; blood sugar 80–120 mg/100 cm³.
