# Hands-on — How the electron was discovered

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only reading. Safety first: laboratory work belongs in a supervised school laboratory, following the book's own safety teaching exactly. At home, do only the observe, describe, draw and discuss tasks — never mix, heat or taste chemicals at home. Activities from the book that need a laboratory are written here as observe/describe tasks, and say so.

## Observe and describe
Look again at the lesson's opening task: Imagine a sealed glass tube with almost all the air pumped out, wired to a high voltage. Do it (or, where it names laboratory materials, picture it in full detail and describe it — an observe/describe task standing in for the laboratory activity). Write three sentences: what you see, what changes, and what stays the same.

## Draw it yourself
On paper, draw the lesson's central picture from memory — The ray bends to the positive plate — it is negative. — and label every part. Compare with the lesson and fix what your drawing got wrong.

## Sort and explain
Write the lesson's key terms on separate slips of paper. Sort the slips into 'I can explain this' and 'not yet'. For every 'not yet' slip, find its place in the lesson, then explain it aloud to a companion until it moves piles.

## If it is hard
Work with the lesson open beside you and only close it for the final explain-aloud. Smaller steps are fine: one key idea per sitting, in the lesson's own order.
