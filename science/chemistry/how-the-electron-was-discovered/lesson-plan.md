# Lesson plan — How the electron was discovered

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** How the electron was discovered
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Atomic Structure and Periodic Properties of the Elements (Grade 11 Chemistry).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In a cathode-ray tube, a ray travels from the negative cathode across the tube.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 11 Chemistry lessons before it in Unit 1 — Atomic Structure and Periodic Properties of the Elements — and on the learner's earlier chemistry. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Grade 11 Chemistry lessons, especially the lessons of Unit 1 before this one. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Answer, in their own words: toward which plate does a cathode ray bend?
2. Answer, in their own words: what particle did Thomson identify from cathode rays?
3. Answer, in their own words: what did Millikan's oil-drop experiment measure?
4. Answer, in their own words: what charge does the electron carry?

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Any practical work named in this lesson belongs in a supervised school laboratory, following the book's own safety teaching — see hands-on.md.
