## Sub lessons
1. **Infrared spectroscopy — the bonds confess** — **All organic compounds absorb in the infrared**, and each kind of **bond vibration absorbs a characteristic wavelength**: the C=O stretch drinks infrared at its own frequency, the O–H stretch at another, C–H at another.
2. **Nuclear magnetic resonance — counting hydrogen environments** — **Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR)** is a **structure-determining technique**: hydrogen nuclei in different surroundings — a CH₃ beside oxygen against a CH₃ beside a carbonyl — respond at slightly different positions in the spectrum.
3. **Ultra-violet spectroscopy — the conjugated clue** — **Ultra-violet spectroscopy** records a substance's absorption in the ultraviolet.
4. **Mass spectroscopy — weighing the molecule** — **Mass spectroscopy** (the unit's own spelling) answers the plainest question: **what does the molecule weigh?** The sample's molecules are ionised and broken into fragments; the instrument sorts ions by mass and records the pattern.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes IR with NMR → IR reads bonds and groups from vibrations; NMR reads hydrogen environments. Expects UV to map the whole structure → UV is the slimmest witness: conjugation and concentration, not the full map.
