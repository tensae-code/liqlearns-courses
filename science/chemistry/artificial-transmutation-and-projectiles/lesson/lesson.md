# Artificial transmutation and projectiles

## Do it first
Before you read: natural decay happens on its own schedule — but a laboratory can change one element into another on demand, today, before lunch. Say out loud: what would you throw at a nucleus to change it, and what could go wrong with a charged missile? Then read on: Egypt's chapter teaches induced transmutation, projectile by projectile.

## Sub lesson 1 — Induced radioactivity — decay on demand

**Artificial (induced) radioactivity** is made, not found: a stable **target nucleus is bombarded with a projectile**, absorbs it or is struck by it, and emerges as a **new, often radioactive, nucleus**. The change is a transmutation — one element becomes another — and it obeys the same A/Z bookkeeping as natural decay.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four projectiles — and the favourite

Egypt lists the missiles: **alpha particles, protons, neutrons and deuterons**. Three of the four carry charge, and a charged projectile is repelled by the target's positive nucleus before it can strike. The **neutron is preferred, being uncharged** — nothing pushes it away, so it reaches the nucleus even at modest speeds.

## Sub lesson 3 — The worked strike — aluminium to sodium

The book's worked example: **aluminium-27 + neutron → sodium-24 + alpha**. Check it by the bookkeeping: A, 27 + 1 = 24 + 4; Z, 13 + 0 = 11 + 2. Both balances hold — a light, stable metal has been transmuted into a radioactive isotope of another element, made to order.

## The key ideas

- **Artificial transmutation**: bombard a target nucleus with a projectile to make a new, often radioactive, nucleus.
- Projectiles: **alpha, proton, neutron, deuteron** — the **neutron preferred, being uncharged**, so the nucleus cannot repel it.
- Worked: **²⁷Al + n → ²⁴Na + ⁴He** — A and Z both balance.
