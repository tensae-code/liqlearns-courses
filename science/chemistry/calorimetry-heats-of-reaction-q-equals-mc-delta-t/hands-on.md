# Hands-on — Calorimetry — heats of reaction, q = mcΔT

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The cold cup, measured (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only)
Weigh 100 g of tap water into a polystyrene or well-wrapped cup and record its temperature. Stir in one level tablespoon (about 10 g, roughly 0.19 mol) of ammonium chloride — a fertiliser salt; wash hands after, and do this only with a teacher or parent present — until dissolved, and record the lowest temperature reached. Use q = mcΔT to calculate the heat absorbed, then the heat per mole, and compare your value with the book's +25.6 kJ mol⁻¹. List which of the three standard assumptions your kitchen calorimeter broke worst.

## Thermometric titration on paper (paper exercise)
A student adds alkali to acid in 5 cm³ portions and records: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 cm³ → 20.0, 21.8, 23.6, 25.4, 27.2, 26.6, 26.0 °C. Plot or tabulate the two lines (rising, then falling) and read off the peak temperature and the portion at which it came. Explain in one sentence why the peak marks the neutralisation endpoint.

## Sign drill (paper exercise)
For each change write exothermic or endothermic and the sign of ΔH before checking the lesson: (a) strong acid + strong alkali mixing warm; (b) ammonium chloride dissolving cold; (c) the mixture in (a) cooling back to room temperature afterwards — careful: is (c) a reaction heat at all? Write one line defending your answer.
