# Calorimetry — heats of reaction, q = mcΔT — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the three standard assumptions of the simple-calorimeter calculation.
**Answer: The solution's specific heat capacity is taken as water's; its density is taken as water's (so cm³ read as grams); and no heat is counted as lost to the cup or the air.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) 100 g of a neutralisation mixture rises 6.7 °C with 0.050 mol reacting. Find the heat of neutralisation.
**Answer: q = 100 × 4.2 × 6.7 = 2,814 J for 0.050 mol; per mole 2,814 ÷ 0.050 ≈ 56,300 J, and the heat is given out, so ΔH ≈ −56.3 kJ mol⁻¹.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why would a weak acid give a different heat of neutralisation?
**Answer: Part of the measured heat is spent pulling the weak acid's molecules apart into ions (or returned by it), so the total is no longer the bare OH⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O value that every fully-ionised strong pair shares.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) 0.10 mol of ammonium chloride dissolves in 100 g of water. Predict the temperature change.
**Answer: Heat absorbed = 0.10 × 25.6 kJ = 2.56 kJ = 2,560 J; ΔT = 2,560 ÷ (100 × 4.2) ≈ 6.1 °C, and the temperature falls by about 6.1 °C.**
