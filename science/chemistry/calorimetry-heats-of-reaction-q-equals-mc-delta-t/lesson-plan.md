# Lesson plan — Calorimetry — heats of reaction, q = mcΔT

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Calorimetry — heats of reaction, q = mcΔT
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how heats of reaction are measured and calculated — the energy change q = m × c × ΔT in a simple calorimeter under its three standard assumptions, the heat of neutralisation (about −56.3 kJ mol⁻¹ for every strong acid with a strong alkali, because the ionic equation underneath is always OH⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O), and the heat of solution of ammonium chloride (+25.6 kJ mol⁻¹, endothermic) explained by bond breaking set against solvation
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Exothermic and endothermic — enthalpy change, is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **The simple calorimeter and q = mcΔT — putting a number on the warmth** — The simple calorimeter; q = m × c × ΔT; the three standard assumptions.
2. **Heat of neutralisation — one reaction underneath them all** — Heat of neutralisation ≈ −56.3 kJ mol⁻¹ for every strong acid + strong alkali, because the ionic equation is always OH⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O; measured by experiment and by thermometric titration.
3. **Heat of solution — ammonium chloride and the cold cup** — Heat of solution of ammonium chloride (+25.6 kJ mol⁻¹, endothermic), explained by bond breaking against solvation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the sign → temperature up means the reaction gave heat out: exothermic, ΔH negative; temperature down means heat taken in: endothermic, ΔH positive.
- Uses the mass of one solution only → m in q = mcΔT is the whole mixture whose temperature changed — acid plus alkali together.
- Expects every neutralisation to differ → strong acid + strong alkali always runs the one ionic reaction OH⁻ + H⁺ → H₂O, so the heat per mole of water formed is the same.
