# Amines — structure, naming and basic character

## Do it first
Aniline built an industry: from this one amine came the dyes that coloured the modern world. Say out loud: what must a molecule carry to belong to the amine family — and why would such a family behave like a base, kin to ammonia? Then read on: Jordan's Grade 11 book tours the amines inside its functional-groups unit.

## Sub lesson 1 — The family — nitrogen at the centre, aniline at the door

An **amine** carries nitrogen bonded to carbon: the **–NH₂ amino group** itself, or that group with its hydrogens replaced by carbon groups, one or two or three. The book enters the family through **aniline, C₆H₅NH₂** — the amine of **dye manufacture**, the working member whose industry made the family matter. Structure first: find the nitrogen, count what it carries, and you have read the amine.

## Sub lesson 2 — Naming — the IUPAC way, worked

The book names amines by the **IUPAC** route: the carbon chain is named and the amine group closes the name — the worked example builds **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂** as **butylamine**, a four-carbon chain carrying –NH₂. Aniline stands beside the systematic names as the family's retained working name — the book keeps both: the system for new names, the famous one where industry fixed it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Properties — boiling points and basic character

The book **tabulates the boiling points of amines against alkanes and alcohols** of similar size: amines boil above their alkane cousins — the N–H bonds hydrogen-bond, though more weakly than alcohols' O–H — and below the matching alcohols. And the family keeps ammonia's signature behaviour: amines are **bases** — the nitrogen's lone pair takes a proton, as ammonia's does, so amines neutralise acids and form salts with them. Structure, name, table, character: the family's whole portrait in one lesson.

## The key ideas

- An amine carries nitrogen bonded to carbon — the –NH₂ group or its substituted forms; aniline (C₆H₅NH₂), of dye manufacture, is the book's entry.
- IUPAC naming worked: CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ is butylamine.
- Boiling points sit between alkanes and alcohols (N–H hydrogen bonding, weaker than O–H); amines are bases — the nitrogen lone pair takes a proton.
