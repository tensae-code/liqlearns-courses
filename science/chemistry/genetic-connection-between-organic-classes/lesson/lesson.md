# The genetic connection between organic classes

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's thing is, in one sentence — then read on and check it against Armenia's way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — The genetic idea, carried over

In Grade 8 the classes of **inorganic** compounds were joined in **genetic chains** — each class reached from the one before. Armenia's Grade 9 **teacher guide** carries the same idea into **organic chemistry** (printed p. 124, contents): the organic classes, too, are joined by the transformations that turn one into another. This lesson stands on that guide evidence, labelled as such.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hydrocarbons → alcohols — the named link

The guide names the link it teaches: **the genetic link between hydrocarbons and alcohols**. The alcohols are not an island: they stand on a road that starts from the hydrocarbons, and the road is a real transformation — one class is **obtained from** the other.

## Sub lesson 3 — One family from another

A genetic link means the classes form **one connected system**: to know a class is also to know where it comes from and what it can become. The guide's organic chain does for hydrocarbons and alcohols what the Grade 8 chains did for oxides, acids, bases and salts — it teaches the **connection** as content, not only the classes one by one.

## The key ideas

- The **genetic-connection** idea is carried from inorganic into **organic** chemistry in the Grade 9 teacher guide.
- The named link: **hydrocarbons → alcohols** — one class obtained from the other.
- The chain is **parallel to the inorganic chains of Grade 8** — connection taught as content.
