# Hands-on — Water purification — filtering and chlorination

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Settle and filter (observation only — muddy water, cloth, sand and gravel)
Stir a spoon of soil into a jar of water. Watch it settle for five quiet minutes and sketch what you see. Then pour the top water slowly through a funnel lined with clean cloth, and — with a teacher — through a layered bottle filter (gravel, clean sand, cloth). Compare the three waters side by side against the light. Say exactly what each step removed — and name, from sub lesson 2, what none of your steps could remove. Do not drink any of it.

## The clear-but-unsafe argument (paper only)
A classmate says: 'My filtered water is clear, so it is safe.' Write the reply the lesson gives: which enemies of drinking water a filter cannot stop, and which step of the chain answers each one. Finish with the chain in order and one job under each step.

## Reading a waterworks (observation / drawing)
From the lesson's chain, draw the purification of a town's water as three labelled boxes — settle, filter, chlorinate — with one arrow between each pair labelled with what the water gains at that step. Under the drawing, add the home version of the same chain in one line.
