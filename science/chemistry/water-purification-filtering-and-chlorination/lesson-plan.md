# Lesson plan — Water purification — filtering and chlorination

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Water purification — filtering and chlorination
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the purification of drinking and household/industrial water as one chain — settling and filtering to take out the suspended matter, chlorinating to kill the germs the filter lets through, and chemical recognition as the way water is told by test rather than by look; Mongolia's Grade 9 book states exactly this as the goal of its §6.1 Water section, in a country whose scarce-water position the same section states (about 97% of Earth's water salt, about 3% fresh)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Separating mixtures — filtration, evaporation and distillation, and Nature's cycles — water, carbon, nitrogen, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why water must be purified — the water there is** — Three enemies, not one: suspended matter that can be seen, dissolved matter that cannot, and germs — invisible and dangerous — so purification is always two jobs, removing and killing.
2. **Filtering — taking out what is suspended** — Settle first, then filter through gravel, sand and cloth or paper: the suspended solid stays as residue, the filtrate runs clear — and the honest limit: filters stop particles, not dissolved matter, not reliably germs.
3. **Chlorinating — and recognising water** — A measured dose of chlorine kills the germs filtering let through and guards the water after; and water itself is recognised by chemical test, not trusted by its look — the section's stated closing goal.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the two jobs: take out what is suspended, kill what is alive — every step in the chain is one of those two jobs, in the order that works.
- Thinks clear water is safe water → germs and dissolved matter are both invisible; clarity proves only the first job was done.
- Mixes filtering up with chlorinating → filtering is a barrier (particles stopped), chlorinating is a disinfectant (germs killed); the chain needs both because each fails where the other works.
