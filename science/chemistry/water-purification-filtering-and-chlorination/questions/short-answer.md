# Water purification — filtering and chlorination — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name drinking water's three enemies and say which can be seen.
**Answer: Suspended matter (can be seen), dissolved matter (cannot be seen) and germs (cannot be seen — and the dangerous share).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the water settled before it is filtered?
**Answer: Settling lets the heaviest suspended matter sink and be left behind, so the filter receives clearer water, works on finer matter, and clogs more slowly.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) State the honest limit of filtering, in one sentence.
**Answer: A filter stops particles — it does not stop dissolved matter, and it does not reliably stop germs, which pass through with the water.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is a trace of chlorine left in the purified water on purpose?
**Answer: The remaining chlorine keeps guarding the water after purification, killing germs that reach it on its way to storage and the tap.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) How does the lesson say water should be recognised, and why not by its look?
**Answer: By chemical test — because look cannot be trusted: dangerous water can look clear, and a clear liquid may not be water at all; only a test tells.**
