## Sub lessons
1. **Half-life — read off the decay curve** — Plot how much of a radioactive substance remains against time and the curve falls steeply, then ever more slowly — but it crosses one mark with perfect regularity: the **half-life is the time for half of what remains to decay**. After one half-life a half is left; after another, half of that — a quarter; the halving never speeds up, slows down or reaches quite to zero.
2. **Counting steps — D = t ÷ t½** — Egypt turns the definition into arithmetic: the **number of decay steps D = total time ÷ half-life**. Each step halves what remains, so after D steps the fraction left is **(½)^D**. The book's worked example runs the method on **manganese-56**: divide the elapsed time by its half-life, then halve that many times.
3. **The table — from ages to milliseconds** — The chapter's table of half-lives spans the whole craft: **uranium-238 at 4.51×10⁹ years** — old enough to date the Earth — down to **polonium-214 at fractions of a millisecond**. The half-life is a property of the isotope, fixed by its nucleus: chemistry, temperature and pressure do not move it.
