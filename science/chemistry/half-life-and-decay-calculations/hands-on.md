# Hands-on — Half-life and decay calculations

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Halving chain
Take 64 counters as your sample. Remove half, then half of what is left, four times, writing the count after each step: 64, 32, 16, 8, 4. Under the counts write the fractions 1, ½, ¼, ⅛, 1⁄16 — the decay curve in counters.

## Use the table's ends
Write the two ends of Egypt's table — uranium-238 and polonium-214 — and one job each end is fit for, argued from its half-life alone (dating rocks against a flash of radiation that vanishes at once).
