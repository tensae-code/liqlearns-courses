# Group 15 — the nitrogen family survey

## Do it first
Before you read: this family holds the gas that is four-fifths of the air and a metal used in fire sprinklers. Say out loud: what could the air's most reluctant gas and a soft metal share that puts them in one family? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book surveys the family the way it surveys every main group — members, molecules, hydrides, oxides.

## Sub lesson 1 — The members — and how they exist

The **nitrogen family (VA, group 15)** — **N, P, As, Sb, Bi**, configuration **ns²np³** — exists in grouped molecules: **N₂, P₄, As₄ and Sb₄**, while **bismuth stands apart as a crystalline solid metal**. Five outer electrons, but the family's faces run from the air's inert gas to a true metal — the survey's first lesson is how far one configuration can stretch.

## Sub lesson 2 — Allotropy in the family — nitrogen the exception

Several members show **two allotropic forms** — one lighter and transparent, growing more stable down the group — while **nitrogen has none**: N₂ is N₂, the one form, which is part of its famous reluctance. Allotropy, met in carbon's lesson as one element in several solids, is here a family trait with one named exception.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hydrides and oxides — the family's two series

The **hydrides run NH₃ and PH₃** — ammonia and phosphine — while **bismuth's hydride falls apart to the element**, too unstable to keep. The **oxides come in +3 and +5 forms** across the family, and their **acidity fades from nitrogen down to bismuth** — the head's oxides are acid-formers, the foot's are a metal's. Two series, one direction: down the group, the nonmetal fades and the metal grows.

## The key ideas

- Group 15 (VA): **N, P, As, Sb, Bi — ns²np³**; molecules **N₂, P₄, As₄, Sb₄**, bismuth a crystalline metal.
- **Allotropy**: several members show **two forms** (the lighter, transparent one growing more stable downward); **nitrogen has none**.
- Hydrides **NH₃, PH₃** (bismuth's falls apart); oxides in **+3 and +5** forms, their **acidity fading** from nitrogen to bismuth.
