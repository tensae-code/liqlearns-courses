## Sub lesson 1 — Three temperatures that describe a fuel

Vietnam's advanced Grade 10 book gives fire a topic of its own — *chemistry in fire and explosion prevention* — and Bài 6 names the three temperatures every fuel carries. The **flash point** is the lowest temperature at which a fuel's vapour mixes with air to flash when a flame or spark is brought near: below it, too little vapour rises to catch.

The **flame temperature** is the temperature the burning fuel's flame itself reaches — how hot the fire burns once going. The **auto-ignition temperature** is the temperature at which the fuel catches **by itself**, with no spark or flame at all: hot enough, and ignition needs no invitation. Bài 6 defines the three and compares them — three different questions (when will vapour flash? how hot is the flame? when does it self-start?) that everyday speech collapses into one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading a fuel by its numbers

The three numbers sort fuels by danger. A **low flash point** (petrol's is far below room temperature) means ignitable vapour hangs over the liquid on an ordinary day — the storage and handling rules tighten accordingly. A high auto-ignition temperature means a hot surface alone will not start the fuel; a low one makes hot machinery itself an ignition source.

Flame temperature prices the fire's power to spread: the hotter the flame, the faster it heats neighbouring fuel past its own flash and ignition points. Fire chemistry is this chain — vapour, ignition, heat feeding heat — read off three measured temperatures.

## Sub lesson 3 — Prevention built on the flash-point science

Bài 8 builds **prevention** on Bài 6's science. Keep fuels **below their flash point** and sealed, so vapour never gathers; keep sparks, flames and hot surfaces away from anywhere vapour can gather — remembering auto-ignition, which needs no spark, only heat; and ventilate, so vapour is carried away faster than it can collect.

When fire starts anyway, the same science gives the three ways to kill it, one for each leg of the fire's needs: **cool** it below its ignition temperature (water on wood), **smother** it by cutting off oxygen (a blanket, foam, CO₂), or **remove the fuel**. Prevention and firefighting are one lesson: the temperatures tell you where the chain can be broken.

## The key ideas

- **Flash point**: the lowest temperature at which a fuel's vapour will flash with an ignition source near. **Auto-ignition temperature**: where it catches with no source at all. **Flame temperature**: how hot its flame burns.
- A **low flash point** makes a fuel dangerous at ordinary temperatures — ignitable vapour is already present.
- Prevention is the science applied: keep fuels cool, sealed and ventilated; shut out sparks **and** hot surfaces (auto-ignition needs no spark).
- A fire dies by **cooling, smothering or fuel removal** — one break for each thing the chain needs.
