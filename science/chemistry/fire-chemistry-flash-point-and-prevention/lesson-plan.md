# Lesson plan — Fire chemistry — flash point and prevention

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Fire chemistry — flash point and prevention
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the chemistry of fire safety as Vietnam's Grade 10 study-topics book teaches it (Topic 2, Bài 6 and Bài 8): the three temperatures that describe a fuel — flash point, flame temperature and auto-ignition temperature — defined and compared, and prevention built directly on that science
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Alkanes at work — fuels is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Three temperatures that describe a fuel** — Vietnam's advanced Grade 10 book gives fire a topic of its own (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
2. **Reading a fuel by its numbers** — The three numbers sort fuels by danger. A **low flash point** (petrol's is far below room temperature) means ignitable vapour hangs over the liquid on an ordinary day (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
3. **Prevention built on the flash-point science** — Bài 8 builds **prevention** on Bài 6's science. Keep fuels **below their flash point** and sealed, so vapour never gathers; keep sparks, flames and hot surfaces away from anywhere vapour can gather (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Merges flash point and auto-ignition → return to the definitions: flash point still needs a spark brought near; auto-ignition is the fuel catching alone, from heat only.
