# Fire chemistry — flash point and prevention — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Distinguish flash point from auto-ignition temperature in one sentence each.
**Answer: Flash point: the lowest temperature at which the vapour will flash when an ignition source is brought near. Auto-ignition temperature: the temperature at which the fuel ignites by itself, with no source at all.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a hot exhaust pipe make 'no naked flames' an incomplete safety rule?
**Answer: Because of auto-ignition: a surface hot enough starts the fuel with no flame or spark, so prevention must also keep fuels away from hot surfaces, not only from flames.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What does flame temperature tell a firefighter that flash point does not?
**Answer: Flame temperature prices how hot the fire itself burns — how fast it will heat neighbouring fuel past its flash and ignition points and so how fast the fire spreads.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the three ways to kill a fire and the need each one removes.
**Answer: Cooling (removes heat, below ignition temperature), smothering (removes oxygen), and removing the fuel (removes what burns).**
