# Kevlar and high-performance polymers — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Kevlar is…
a) A metal alloy  b) A synthetic aramid polymer spun into a fibre  c) A natural cotton fibre  d) A ceramic
**Answer: b) A synthetic aramid polymer spun into a fibre**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A high-performance polymer is chosen because…
a) It is the cheapest polymer  b) It is the easiest to colour  c) It keeps its strength where ordinary materials fail — under blows, loads and heat  d) It dissolves in water
**Answer: c) It keeps its strength where ordinary materials fail — under blows, loads and heat**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Kevlar's chains make the fibre strong because they are…
a) Tangled at random  b) Straight, regular and aligned side by side, tied to their neighbours by hydrogen bonds  c) Very short  d) Filled with air
**Answer: b) Straight, regular and aligned side by side, tied to their neighbours by hydrogen bonds**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) When a Kevlar fibre is pulled, the force is…
a) Carried by one chain alone  b) Carried by the water inside it  c) Lost as heat at once  d) Shared across the whole packed bundle of chains
**Answer: d) Shared across the whole packed bundle of chains**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Iran pictures Kevlar (Fig. 18) inside its Grade 11 chapter on…
a) Food  b) Clothing  c) Acids  d) Electricity
**Answer: b) Clothing**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) Which list names pictured Kevlar applications?
a) Window glass, soap, fertiliser  b) Bullet-resistant vests and helmets, protective gloves, reinforcement in tyres, cables and ropes  c) Paint, paper, candles  d) Bottles, bags, food wrap
**Answer: b) Bullet-resistant vests and helmets, protective gloves, reinforcement in tyres, cables and ropes**
