# Kevlar and high-performance polymers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is Kevlar, and what makes it a high-performance polymer rather than an ordinary one?
**Answer: Kevlar is a synthetic aramid polymer spun into a fibre. It is high-performance because it is chosen to keep its strength where ordinary materials fail — under sudden blows, heavy loads and heat — not for comfort or low cost.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How do Kevlar's chains share a pulling force?
**Answer: The chains are straight, regular and aligned side by side, tied to their neighbours by hydrogen bonds all along their length, so a pull is spread across the whole packed bundle instead of snapping one chain at a time.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why can woven Kevlar cloth stop a bullet when a single thread cannot?
**Answer: The weave spreads the bullet's energy across many layers and thousands of bonded chains at once; the stopping power is the bundle's shared strength, which no single thread has on its own.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name three applications Iran pictures for Kevlar and the property each one needs most.
**Answer: Any three of: bullet-resistant vests and helmets (strength under a sudden blow, kept light); protective gloves and clothing (strength that stands heat and cutting); reinforcement in tyres (strength under repeated load); cables and ropes (strength kept light, without snapping).**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does Iran teach Kevlar inside a chapter on clothing?
**Answer: Clothing is the chapter's frame — "Clothing, an unending need" — and Kevlar is its extreme case: a fibre judged by the work it can do, pictured in Fig. 18 with its applications, showing how far a clothing fibre can be pushed.**
