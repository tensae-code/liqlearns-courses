## Sub lessons
1. **A polymer made to perform** — Kevlar is a synthetic polymer fibre in the aramid family: long chains built to be drawn into fibres, standing at the top end of what polymers can do — the high-performance end of the polymer family the clothing chapter has been teaching.
2. **Why the fibre is so strong** — The chains are straight, regular and packed side by side, held to their neighbours by hydrogen bonds between the chains, so a pulling force is shared across the whole bundle instead of snapping one chain at a time; the fibre is famously strong for its weight and stands heat that melts ordinary synthetics.
3. **Kevlar at work — the applications Iran pictures** — Fig. 18's pictured applications: bullet-resistant vests and helmets, protective gloves and clothing, reinforcement in tyres, cables and ropes, and parts where a light material must not fail.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the one element: Kevlar is still a polymer — chains of monomers — but its chains are built and packed to perform, and performance is what makes it a high-performance polymer.
- Mixes Kevlar up with ordinary clothing fibres → ordinary fibres are chosen for comfort and cost; Kevlar is chosen where failure is dangerous — protection, reinforcement, heat — which is why Iran pictures it inside the clothing chapter as the chapter's extreme case.
- Thinks a single chain does the stopping → no single chain stops anything: the strength is shared, chain to chain, by the hydrogen bonds between the packed chains.
