# Hands-on — Kevlar and high-performance polymers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## One thread against a bundle (home materials — string and thread)
Take a single sewing thread and pull it until it snaps; note how little force it took. Now twist twenty threads together into one cord and pull again with the same grip. Write down what changed and say it in lesson terms: the force is now shared across the whole bundle, as it is across Kevlar's packed chains — the bundle, not the single chain, does the work.

## The applications hunt (observation only — home and street)
Find three objects around your home or street whose labels or makers name a high-performance fibre or a protective job — protective gloves, a reinforced tyre's sidewall markings, safety clothing. For each, write the job it does and the one property — light, strong, heat-standing — the job cannot do without, as Iran's Fig. 18 sets fibre and applications side by side.

## Ordinary or high-performance? (paper exercise)
List six fibres or fibre uses from your own wardrobe and home. Sort them into two columns — chosen for comfort and cost / chosen because failure is dangerous — and defend the column you put each one in, in one line each.
