# Radioactive decay — emissions and nuclear equations

## Do it first
Before you read: a uranium nucleus quietly becomes a thorium nucleus in the dark of a rock — no heat, no reagent, no hand touching it. Say out loud: what bookkeeping could prove the change is possible — what must add up before and after? Then read on: Egypt's chapter balances nuclear changes the way other chapters balance equations.

## Sub lesson 1 — The bookkeeping — A and Z must balance

Every nuclear equation is kept with two numbers: the **mass number A** (nucleons) and the **atomic number Z** (protons). In any emission, **the A values sum equally on both sides, and the Z values sum equally on both sides** — that double balance is how a proposed decay is checked, and how an unknown product is found.

## Sub lesson 2 — Alpha and beta-minus — the nucleus steps down

In **alpha emission** the nucleus throws out a helium nucleus: **²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + ⁴He** — A falls by 4, Z by 2. In **negatron (beta-minus) emission** a neutron inside the nucleus decays to a proton and the electron is emitted: **¹⁴C → ¹⁴N** — A unchanged, Z rises by 1, and the element steps one place up the table.

## Sub lesson 3 — Positron and gamma — the other two exits

In **positron emission** a proton turns to a neutron and a positive electron leaves: **²²Na → ²²Ne** — A unchanged, Z falls by 1. **Gamma emission** carries away energy alone, changing neither A nor Z, and so **accompanies the other emissions** — Egypt's case is **⁶⁰Co**, whose decay finishes with gamma rays from the excited product nucleus.

## The key ideas

- Nuclear equations balance **twice**: total **A** and total **Z** are the same on both sides.
- **Alpha**: A −4, Z −2 (²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th). **Beta-minus**: a neutron decays, A same, Z +1 (¹⁴C → ¹⁴N).
- **Positron**: A same, Z −1 (²²Na → ²²Ne). **Gamma**: energy only, A and Z unchanged, accompanying the others (⁶⁰Co).
