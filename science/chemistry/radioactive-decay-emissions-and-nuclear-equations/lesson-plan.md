## Sub lessons
1. **The bookkeeping — A and Z must balance** — Every nuclear equation is kept with two numbers: the **mass number A** (nucleons) and the **atomic number Z** (protons). In any emission, **the A values sum equally on both sides, and the Z values sum equally on both sides** — that double balance is how a proposed decay is checked, and how an unknown product is found.
2. **Alpha and beta-minus — the nucleus steps down** — In **alpha emission** the nucleus throws out a helium nucleus: **²³⁸U → ²³⁴Th + ⁴He** — A falls by 4, Z by 2. In **negatron (beta-minus) emission** a neutron inside the nucleus decays to a proton and the electron is emitted: **¹⁴C → ¹⁴N** — A unchanged, Z rises by 1, and the element steps one place up the table.
3. **Positron and gamma — the other two exits** — In **positron emission** a proton turns to a neutron and a positive electron leaves: **²²Na → ²²Ne** — A unchanged, Z falls by 1. **Gamma emission** carries away energy alone, changing neither A nor Z, and so **accompanies the other emissions** — Egypt's case is **⁶⁰Co**, whose decay finishes with gamma rays from the excited product nucleus.
