# Lesson plan — Alpha, beta and gamma radiation compared

## Sub lessons
1. **Alpha — the helium nucleus** — **Alpha radiation is a stream of helium nuclei** — two protons and two neutrons, charge **+2**. Being heavy and doubly charged, it is **bent slightly** as it crosses electric and magnetic fields: the field grips it, but its mass resists the turn.
2. **Beta — the fast electron** — **Beta radiation is fast electrons**, charge **−1**. Far lighter than alpha, the same field **bends beta strongly** — and the opposite way, since its charge is negative. Egypt's table holds the contrast still: same field, slight bend against strong bend, and the reasons are charge and mass together.
3. **Gamma — the uncharged photon** — **Gamma radiation is photons** — pure electromagnetic energy, **uncharged**, and so **unaffected by fields**: it passes straight through the field that bends both its partners. The chapter frames the table by distinguishing **stable isotopes from radioactive ones** — only the radioactive nuclei pour out these three radiations at all.
