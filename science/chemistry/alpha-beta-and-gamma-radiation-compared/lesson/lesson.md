# Alpha, beta and gamma radiation compared

## Do it first
Before you read: one invisible ray bends a little in a magnet's field, one bends a lot, and one does not bend at all. Say out loud: what must be different about the three rays for the same field to treat them so differently? Then read on: Egypt's Grade 11 nuclear chapter sets the three radiations side by side in one drilled table.

## Sub lesson 1 — Alpha — the helium nucleus

**Alpha radiation is a stream of helium nuclei** — two protons and two neutrons, charge **+2**. Being heavy and doubly charged, it is **bent slightly** as it crosses electric and magnetic fields: the field grips it, but its mass resists the turn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Beta — the fast electron

**Beta radiation is fast electrons**, charge **−1**. Far lighter than alpha, the same field **bends beta strongly** — and the opposite way, since its charge is negative. Egypt's table holds the contrast still: same field, slight bend against strong bend, and the reasons are charge and mass together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gamma — the uncharged photon

**Gamma radiation is photons** — pure electromagnetic energy, **uncharged**, and so **unaffected by fields**: it passes straight through the field that bends both its partners. The chapter frames the table by distinguishing **stable isotopes from radioactive ones** — only the radioactive nuclei pour out these three radiations at all.

## The key ideas

- **Alpha = helium nucleus, +2**, bent **slightly** in fields. **Beta = fast electron, −1**, bent **strongly**.
- **Gamma = photon, uncharged** — **unaffected** by electric and magnetic fields.
- Only **radioactive** isotopes emit these radiations; **stable** isotopes do not.
