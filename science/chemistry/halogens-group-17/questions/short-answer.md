# Halogens — group 17 — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do free halogens travel as diatomic molecules such as Cl₂?
**Answer: Each atom is one electron short of an octet; by sharing a pair in a two-atom molecule, both atoms complete their shells — so the free element is found as X₂ pairs.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student says iodine should displace chlorine from sodium chloride because iodine is heavier. Correct the claim.
**Answer: Displacement runs by reactivity, not mass: reactivity falls down the group, so chlorine — the stronger halogen — displaces iodine's family members, never the reverse; iodine frees nothing above it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the four hydrogen halides and say what they become in water.
**Answer: HF, HCl, HBr and HI; in water they form the hydrohalic acids (hydrofluoric, hydrochloric, hydrobromic and hydriodic acids).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Give two uses of chlorine and the family property both spend.
**Answer: Disinfecting drinking water/pools and making bleach/disinfectants — both spend the halogens' strong pull on electrons, i.e. their powerful reacting (oxidising) character, tamed to kill microbes and break down stains.**
