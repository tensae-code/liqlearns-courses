# Water and life — the properties of water

## Do it first
Before you read: name three places you met water today before breakfast — in a glass, in the air, in your own body. Then say out loud: why do you think a chemistry book would begin with water, of all substances — before atoms, before reactions? Then read on: Greece's Grade 8 book opens its main journey exactly there, with Unit 2.1, Το νερό στη ζωή μας — water in our life.

## Sub lesson 1 — Water's place in life and nature

Greece's Grade 8 book opens its main journey — Unit 2, "from water to the atom" — with water itself (Unit 2.1, Το νερό στη ζωή μας). Water covers most of the Earth's surface; it fills the seas, rivers and lakes, falls as rain and rises again as vapour. Every living thing carries it: the human body is mostly water, plants draw it from the soil, and no known life manages without it. That is why Greece starts here — before mixtures, before atoms, the substance life runs on.

## Sub lesson 2 — The unusual properties

For so small a molecule, water behaves unusually. It stays **liquid** across the whole temperature range life uses on Earth, and it must be heated a long way — to 100 °C at ordinary pressure — before it boils, where similar small molecules are gases long before. It also dissolves more substances than almost any other liquid, which is why the very next section of Greece's book (Unit 2.2) makes it the solvent of chemistry.

Strangest of all: water **expands as it freezes**. Ice is less dense than liquid water, so ice floats; a lake freezes from the top down, the floating ice insulates the water below, and life survives the winter underneath. If ice sank, lakes would freeze solid from the bottom up.

## Sub lesson 3 — The water cycle — the entry point

The Sun drives the **water cycle**: water **evaporates** from seas, lakes and land, **condenses** into clouds, falls as **precipitation** — rain and snow — and **collects** again in seas, lakes and groundwater, round and round with nothing lost. The water you drink is older than the hills.

Greece's book places the cycle here on purpose, as the **entry point to all the later chemistry**: the rain that falls is a mixture-maker, the sea is a solution, separating mixtures and finally splitting water into its elements are the chapters that follow. Water first — then, in Greece's own phrase, "from water to the atom".

## The key ideas

- Water is **in life and all around it** — most of the Earth's surface, every living body; Greece's book starts its main journey with it.
- Water's properties are **unusual** for so small a molecule: liquid across life's temperatures, slow to boil, a solvent for more substances than almost any other liquid.
- **Ice is less dense than liquid water** — water expands as it freezes, ice floats, and lakes freeze from the top down.
- The **water cycle** — evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection — moves the same water round forever, and is Greece's entry point to mixtures, solutions and the atom.
