# Hands-on — Biodegradable and non-biodegradable polymers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The two-family sort (paper exercise, with observation)
List ten polymer objects from your home and street — a wooden spoon, a plastic bag, a nylon rope, a cotton shirt, a PVC pipe, a food container and four more. Sort them into Fiji's two families where the lesson's named members let you, writing beside each the polymer if you know it (starch, cellulose, polythene, nylon, PVC, polypropene, Teflon, polyester) and the ending it can have. Mark honestly any object you cannot place — an unsorted object is a question, not a guess.

## The dropped-object diary (observation only)
Choose one spot outdoors that you pass daily — a verge, a beach path, a drain. For one week, note (do not move) one dropped biodegradable object and one dropped plastic object there. Write what changes in each across the week and what does not, and finish with the lesson's sentence in your own words: which of the two is on its way back into the cycles, and which is being carried by the world.

## Cost the ending (paper exercise)
For a plastic bag and a paper (cellulose) bag used once and dropped, write the three problems from Fiji's list — pollution and harm to land and marine life, toxic emissions on exposure and burning, recycling cost — and mark for each problem whether it lands on the plastic bag, the paper bag, both or neither, with one line of reason each.
