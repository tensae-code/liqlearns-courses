# Lesson plan — Biodegradable and non-biodegradable polymers

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Biodegradable and non-biodegradable polymers
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** polymers sorted by what the living world can do with them when they are thrown away — biodegradable polymers, broken down by micro-organisms and enzymes (starch, cellulose, polyesters), against non-biodegradable ones that persist (polythene, nylon, PVC, polypropene, Teflon), and the problems of the persisting kind, met the way Fiji's Year 13 book closes its polymer strand (Fiji, Grade 13 — *Chemistry for Year 13*, Strand 5.1, printed pp. 313–314)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Polymers — chains of monomers is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Biodegradable polymers — the living world can take them back** — A **biodegradable polymer** is one that **micro-organisms and their enzymes can break down** — eaten, in effect, by the living world and returned to it as simple substances.
2. **Non-biodegradable polymers — the ones that persist** — A **non-biodegradable polymer** is one the living world **cannot break down**: no micro-organism's enzymes make a meal of it, so it **persists** — staying in the ground, the water and the landscape essentially as it was thrown away.
3. **The problems of the non-biodegradable kind** — Fiji's book closes the strand by counting the cost of persistence, and this lesson carries its list exactly.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks biodegradable means weak or useless → starch, cellulose and the polyesters do full service in use; biodegradable describes the ending, not the quality.
- Thinks non-biodegradable plastics are bad materials → in use, persistence is their virtue (a pipe must not rot); the problem is after use, when the same persistence has nowhere to go.
- Recites the problems loosely → carry Fiji's list exactly: pollution and harm to land and marine life; toxic emissions on exposure and burning; recycling costs.
