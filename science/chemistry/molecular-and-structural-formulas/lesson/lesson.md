# Molecular and structural formulas

## Do it first
Before you read: C₂H₄Cl₂ is a complete molecular formula — and it names two different substances. Say out loud: what information does the formula fail to carry, and how would you write a formula that carries it? Then read on: Egypt's organic chapter separates the counting formula from the drawing formula.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two formulas — count against arrangement

A **molecular formula counts** the atoms: C₂H₄ tells you two carbons and four hydrogens, nothing about their arrangement. A **structural formula draws** the arrangement — which atom is bonded to which, and by what bond. The same count can hide different arrangements, which is why organic chemistry needs the second kind: structure is where the chemistry lives.

## Sub lesson 2 — Valency — the bond count of each element

Structural drawing is ruled by **valency — the count of covalent bonds an atom forms**. Egypt's table: **carbon tetravalent (4), oxygen divalent (2), nitrogen trivalent (3), hydrogen monovalent (1)**. A drawn structure is legal only when every atom carries exactly its valency of bonds — the rule that checks every drawing.

## Sub lesson 3 — The drawn set — Egypt's four

The book draws structural formulas for **C₂H₄Cl₂, C₂H₄, C₂H₂ and CH₃OH** — a chlorinated ethane, the alkene, the alkyne and the alcohol. Work them against the valency table: carbon always with four bonds, the double bond in C₂H₄ and the triple in C₂H₂ counting in full, until the drawings check themselves.

## The key ideas

- **Molecular formula counts atoms; structural formula draws their arrangement** — the count alone can hide different substances.
- **Valency = the count of covalent bonds**: C 4, O 2, N 3, H 1 — every drawn structure must satisfy it.
- Egypt draws **C₂H₄Cl₂, C₂H₄, C₂H₂ and CH₃OH** in full structural form.
