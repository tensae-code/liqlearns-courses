# Lesson plan — Molecular and structural formulas

## Sub lessons
1. **Two formulas — count against arrangement** — A **molecular formula counts** the atoms: C₂H₄ tells you two carbons and four hydrogens, nothing about their arrangement. A **structural formula draws** the arrangement — which atom is bonded to which, and by what bond. The same count can hide different arrangements, which is why organic chemistry needs the second kind: structure is where the chemistry lives.
2. **Valency — the bond count of each element** — Structural drawing is ruled by **valency — the count of covalent bonds an atom forms**. Egypt's table: **carbon tetravalent (4), oxygen divalent (2), nitrogen trivalent (3), hydrogen monovalent (1)**. A drawn structure is legal only when every atom carries exactly its valency of bonds — the rule that checks every drawing.
3. **The drawn set — Egypt's four** — The book draws structural formulas for **C₂H₄Cl₂, C₂H₄, C₂H₂ and CH₃OH** — a chlorinated ethane, the alkene, the alkyne and the alcohol. Work them against the valency table: carbon always with four bonds, the double bond in C₂H₄ and the triple in C₂H₂ counting in full, until the drawings check themselves.
