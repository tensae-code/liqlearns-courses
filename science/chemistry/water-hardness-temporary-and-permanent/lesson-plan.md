# Lesson plan — Water hardness — temporary and permanent

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Water hardness — temporary and permanent
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** hard water and its two kinds — water made hard by dissolved calcium and magnesium ions, which waste soap as scum; temporary hardness from calcium and magnesium hydrogencarbonates, formed as rainwater's dissolved carbon dioxide eats into limestone (CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂, the reaction that carves limestone caves); and permanent hardness from calcium and magnesium sulfates, with soil leaching named as the same solvent power at work
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. The water lesson — Hydrogen bonding and intermolecular forces — is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Hard water — the ions that waste soap** — Hard water from dissolved Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ wasting soap as scum (equation given).
2. **Temporary hardness — the limestone transaction** — Temporary hardness from calcium/magnesium hydrogencarbonates, formed as rainwater CO₂ dissolves limestone (CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂ — the reaction that carves limestone caves).
3. **Permanent hardness — the sulfates stay** — Permanent hardness from calcium/magnesium sulfates; soil leaching as the same solvent power.

## If the learner is stuck
- Blames the soap → the soap is the victim: it is the Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ already in the water that precipitate it as scum before any lather can form.
- Calls all hardness one thing → the book's line is the anion: hydrogencarbonates make it temporary (boiling breaks them down), sulfates make it permanent (boiling changes nothing).
- Learns the cave reaction as a curiosity → it is the hardness reaction itself: the same equation that hollows the cave loads the water with calcium hydrogencarbonate.
