# Water hardness — temporary and permanent

## Do it first
Before you read: in one village the kettle furs up white and soap will not lather; in the next, soap foams freely and kettles stay clean — yet both waters look crystal clear. Say out loud: what invisible passenger makes the difference? Then read on: this is Dominica's CSEC unit C21.1 — **water hardness**, the dissolved calcium and magnesium that tax every wash, in its two kinds, **temporary and permanent**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hard water — the ions that waste soap

**Hard water** is water carrying dissolved **Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions** — invisible, tasteless at these levels, and expensive: soap added to hard water is spent first on those ions, precipitating as a grey **scum** (the book gives the equation: the soap's stearate ions meet Ca²⁺ and fall out as solid calcium stearate) instead of forming a lather. Only when every hardness ion has been precipitated can any lather stand — the wash pays a soap tax before it begins. The ions arrive honestly: rainwater is a weak acid and a tireless solvent, and the rocks it crosses pay it in calcium and magnesium. Which rock, and which partner ion came with them, decides the kind of hardness — the subject of the next two sub lessons.

## Sub lesson 2 — Temporary hardness — the limestone transaction

**Temporary hardness** is hardness from dissolved **calcium and magnesium hydrogencarbonates**. Its forming reaction is the book's limestone equation: rainwater carrying dissolved carbon dioxide meets limestone and dissolves it — **CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂**. Read forwards, it is how rain loads itself with calcium hydrogencarbonate and the water runs hard out of limestone country; read at cave scale over ages, it is the very reaction that **carves limestone caves**, hollowing the rock a molecule at a time.

It is called temporary because heat undoes it: boiling decomposes the hydrogencarbonate back to insoluble carbonate, which precipitates out — the white fur in the kettle is the hardness leaving the water, which is why the kettle both proves the hardness and cures it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Permanent hardness — the sulfates stay

**Permanent hardness** is hardness from dissolved **calcium and magnesium sulfates**. No cave equation forms it and — the point of its name — **boiling does not remove it**: the sulfates survive the kettle unchanged, the water stays hard, and only chemical softening (the next lesson's washing soda, ion exchange and the rest) can take those ions out.

Dominica's book closes the unit by widening the lens: **soil leaching is the same solvent power at work** — rain dissolving and carrying ions out of soil exactly as it dissolves them out of limestone. One power, three sightings: hard washing water, hollowed caves, and leached fields.

## The key ideas

- **Hard water** carries dissolved **Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺**, which precipitate soap as **scum** before any lather can form — the wash pays a soap tax first.
- **Temporary hardness** comes from calcium/magnesium **hydrogencarbonates**, formed by CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂ — the reaction that also carves limestone caves; boiling removes it, leaving kettle fur.
- **Permanent hardness** comes from calcium/magnesium **sulfates**; boiling leaves it untouched, and only chemical softening removes it.
- **Soil leaching** is the same rainwater solvent power, carrying ions out of soil as out of rock.
