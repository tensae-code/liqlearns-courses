# Water hardness — temporary and permanent — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does lather appear in hard water only after extra soap has been added?
**Answer: The first soap is consumed precipitating the Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ as scum; only once those ions are all precipitated is any soap left free to form a lather.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) State the limestone equation and its two readings.
**Answer: CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ → Ca(HCO₃)₂ — read one way it is rainwater loading itself with calcium hydrogencarbonate (temporary hardness formed); read at geological pace it is limestone being dissolved away (caves carved).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A sample stays hard after long boiling. What do you conclude, and what is the dissolved salt family responsible?
**Answer: Its hardness is permanent — carried by calcium/magnesium sulfates, which boiling cannot decompose or precipitate, so chemical softening is required.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is kettle fur evidence *for* temporary hardness rather than against the water?
**Answer: The fur is the hydrogencarbonate hardness precipitated out by boiling — proof the temporary kind was there, and proof it has now left the water.**
