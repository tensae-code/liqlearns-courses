# Hands-on — Water hardness — temporary and permanent

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The soap-tax test (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only)
Fill two jam jars: one with distilled or rain water, one with tap water. Add the same five drops of liquid soap to each, cap, and shake both ten times. Photograph or sketch the lather in each and any scum ring. Then boil a cup of the tap water, let it cool, and repeat its test: if the lather improves, your tap water's hardness had a temporary part — write the conclusion in the lesson's terms, naming the partner ion you have evidence for.

## Fur evidence (field exercise)
Inspect a kettle or pot that boils water daily. Describe any white deposit: where it sits thickest, how it feels, and what it is in the lesson's chemistry. Under your description, write the equation of its formation run in reverse — the boiling that undid the hydrogencarbonate.

## Two wells (paper exercise)
Village A's water crosses limestone; village B's crosses gypsum (calcium sulfate) beds. For each village predict: hardness kind, scum on washing day, kettle fur or none, and whether boiling the drinking water softens it. One row per village, one column per prediction.
