## Polar and nonpolar molecules

Equal sharing (same electronegativity, e.g. H₂ and all homonuclear diatomics) gives a nonpolar bond; unequal sharing (HF, pulled toward fluorine) gives a polar bond with partial charges, the polarity growing with the electronegativity difference and shown with a polarity arrow (the book's working threshold for a polar bond is a difference around 0.5).

Whole-molecule polarity then depends on geometry: ethane is nonpolar overall; CO₂'s two polar C–O bonds cancel in its linear shape, and CCl₄'s polar bonds cancel symmetrically, so both molecules are nonpolar, while heteronuclear molecules with uncanceled pull (water, HCl among them) are polar.
