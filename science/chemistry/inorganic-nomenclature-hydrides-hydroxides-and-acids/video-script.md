Today: **Inorganic nomenclature — hydrides, hydroxides and acids** — Bolivia's naming strand: names built, not memorised.

A metal holding hydrogen is a hydride — hidruro de sodio, and calcium holds two, because its charge says so. Ammonia is a parent: swap its hydrogens and its derivatives grow. A metal holding the –OH group is a hydroxide — hidróxido de calcio, Ca(OH)₂, the brackets holding the group together. And one dry oxide can drink one water, two waters or three — and become three acids: meta, pyro, ortho. The name counts the water for you.
