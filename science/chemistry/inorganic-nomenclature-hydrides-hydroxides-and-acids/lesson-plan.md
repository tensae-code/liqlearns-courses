# Lesson plan — Inorganic nomenclature — hydrides, hydroxides and acids

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Inorganic nomenclature — hydrides, hydroxides and acids
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the systematic Spanish nomenclature strand for the inorganic families the binary-compounds lessons stop short of — metallic hydrides (hidruro de + metal, the metal's charge fixing the H⁻ count) and the derivatives of ammonia (named from NH₃ as parent), hydroxides (hidróxido de + metal, brackets for repeated OH groups, Roman numerals for variable charge), and the polyhydrated oxoacids, where one anhydride drinking different amounts of water gives the meta (least water), pyro (between) and ortho (most water) acids — every name a construction from which the formula can be rebuilt
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Naming ionic compounds is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Metallic hydrides and the derivatives of ammonia** — Hydrogen as H⁻ beside a metal, named hidruro de + metal with the count fixed by charge; ammonia as the parent of a derivative family built by replacement.
2. **Hydroxides — the –OH family and its names** — The OH⁻ group joined to a metal; hidróxido de + metal, brackets for Ca(OH)₂, Roman numerals where the metal's charge varies.
3. **The polyhydrated oxoacids — meta, pyro and ortho** — One anhydride, three acids: P₂O₅ drinking one, two or three waters gives the meta, pyro and ortho phosphoric acids, the prefix counting the water.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the one rule: every name in this strand is a construction — parent, partner, charge and count — so the formula can always be rebuilt from the name.
- Writes CaOH₂ → the OH group travels as one ion, so two of them stand in brackets: Ca(OH)₂.
- Mixes meta and ortho up → count the water: meta drank the least, ortho the most, pyro stands between with two central atoms.
