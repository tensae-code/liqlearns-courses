# Hands-on — Inorganic nomenclature — hydrides, hydroxides and acids

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Name ↔ formula, both ways (paper exercise)
Write six cards: NaH, CaH₂, NaOH, Ca(OH)₂, Fe(OH)₃, H₃PO₄. On the back of each, write its systematic Spanish name from the strand. Shuffle the cards and run the road both ways: formula side up, say the name; name side up, write the formula. Note any card that fails and say which construction rule it broke — charge count, brackets, or the Roman numeral.

## The water ladder (paper exercise)
Draw P₂O₅ at the left of a page and three arrows leaving it, labelled +1 H₂O, +2 H₂O, +3 H₂O. At each arrow's end write the acid it makes, with its full name. Under the page, in three lines, say how the pyro acid's two phosphorus atoms connect it to the ortho acid (two ortho molecules, one water gone).

## The label hunt (observation only — home cleaning cupboard)
Read the ingredient labels of cleaning products in your own home. Many list sodium hydroxide (hidróxido de sodio) or a phosphoric acid. Write down each name you find, its formula as the lesson builds it, and the family it belongs to. Observation only — read the labels; do not open, mix or use the products for this exercise.
