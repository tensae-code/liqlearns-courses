# Lesson plan — Salt hydrolysis — why salt solutions are not always neutral

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Salt hydrolysis — why salt solutions are not always neutral
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Salt hydrolysis built on the general scheme, as Yemen's Grade 11 book builds it (and its practical workbook checks on the pH meter): the salt dissociates, water ionises slightly, the ion from the weak partner reacts with water — and the pH verdict follows the parentage: both strong (NaCl) neutral at pH 7; weak acid + strong base (CH3COONa) alkaline, the acetate ion hydrolysing, with pH = 7 + 1/2(pKa + log Cs); strong acid + weak base (NH4Cl) acidic, with its own working formula — the five salt solutions of workbook Activity 14 measured with a pH meter and universal indicator paper
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Salts that turn acidic is recommended first, never forced.
