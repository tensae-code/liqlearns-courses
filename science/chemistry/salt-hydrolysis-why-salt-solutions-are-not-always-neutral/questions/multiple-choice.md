# Salt hydrolysis — why salt solutions are not always neutral — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) In the hydrolysis scheme, the ion that reacts with water is the one from the…
a) Weak partner  b) Strong partner  c) Larger parent  d) Salt's cation always
**Answer: a) Weak partner**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Sodium chloride solution has pH 7 because…
a) Na⁺ hydrolyses fully  b) Neither ion reacts with water  c) Cl⁻ takes protons  d) Water cannot ionise
**Answer: b) Neither ion reacts with water**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Sodium acetate solution is alkaline because the acetate ion…
a) Dissociates into OH⁻ directly  b) Reacts with the glass  c) Hydrolyses, leaving OH⁻ behind  d) Removes H⁺ from the air
**Answer: c) Hydrolyses, leaving OH⁻ behind**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Ammonium chloride solution is…
a) Alkaline  b) Neutral  c) Unpredictable  d) Acidic — NH₄⁺ hydrolyses to give H⁺
**Answer: d) Acidic — NH₄⁺ hydrolyses to give H⁺**

**5.** (Sub lesson 2) Activity 14 measures the five solutions with…
a) A pH meter and universal indicator paper  b) Litmus only  c) A thermometer  d) A balance
**Answer: a) A pH meter and universal indicator paper**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) The pH formula printed for the weak-acid/strong-base case is…
a) pH = −log Cs  b) pH = 7 + ½(pKa + log Cs)  c) pH = 14 − pKa  d) pH = ½ log Cs
**Answer: b) pH = 7 + ½(pKa + log Cs)**
