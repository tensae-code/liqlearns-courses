# Salt hydrolysis — why salt solutions are not always neutral — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Write the general hydrolysis scheme in its three steps.
**Answer: The salt dissociates into its ions; water ionises slightly; the ion from the weak partner reacts with water, unbalancing [H⁺] and [OH⁻] and so setting the pH.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give the hydrolysis equation for each of the book's two acting ions.
**Answer: Acetate: CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻ (alkaline result). Ammonium: NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄OH + H⁺ (acidic result).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Describe Activity 14's bench exactly: the salts' amount, the water, the two instruments.
**Answer: Half a teaspoon of each salt in 100 mL of pure distilled water; the pH of the five solutions (sodium chloride, sodium acetate, ammonium chloride, ammonium carbonate and one more of the set) measured with a pH meter and universal indicator paper, and the five values compared.**
