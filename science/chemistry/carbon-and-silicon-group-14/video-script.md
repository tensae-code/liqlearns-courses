Today: **Carbon and silicon — the group-14 pair** — Belarus's way: the group-14 pair in one section — the jewel, the pencil and the chip.

Start with the group. Carbon and silicon stand one above the other in group 14, and both carry four electrons in the outer shell. Four bonds apiece — that is the family likeness, and it shows even in their formulas: CO₂ and SiO₂, CH₄ and SiH₄.
Carbon then shows what one element can do. Lock the atoms in a rigid network and you have diamond, the hardest thing nature makes. Lay them in sliding layers and you have graphite, soft enough to write with, carrying current along its sheets. Same atoms — the architecture decides.
Silicon is the Earth's own element — second in the crust after oxygen, never found free — and its gift is in-between conduction: a semiconductor. The pencil, the jewel and the phone chip are two elements' work.
