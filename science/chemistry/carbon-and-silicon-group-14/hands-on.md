# Hands-on — Carbon and silicon — the group-14 pair

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The pencil test (kitchen materials only)
Take a pencil and a battery circuit with a small bulb (or a school conductivity tester). First draw a thick graphite line on paper and test whether the line itself conducts; then test a wooden splint the same size. Record both results and say, from sub lesson 2, which structure the conducting line has and why the wood fails.

## Build the two architectures
With modelling balls and sticks (or drawn on paper), build eight carbon atoms two ways: first as a diamond fragment — every ball joined to four neighbours in a rigid block; then as graphite — two flat layers of joined balls resting loosely on each other. Push each model gently and record which one slides and which one resists. Say what real property each behaviour matches.

## Stone hunt
Walk your schoolyard or street and list five things made mostly of silicon's compounds — sand, glass, concrete, brick, pottery. Beside each, write the compound family it belongs to (oxide or silicate) as far as the lesson lets you judge, and mark the one that began as the semiconductor's raw material.
