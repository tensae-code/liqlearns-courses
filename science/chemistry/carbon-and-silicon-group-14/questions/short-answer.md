# Carbon and silicon — the group-14 pair — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What does "tetravalent" mean for carbon and silicon, and what kind of chemistry does it make possible?
**Answer: Each atom forms four bonds, sharing its four outer electrons — which makes endless networks possible: carbon's chains and rings, silicon's –Si–O– frameworks in rock and sand.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon. Give the structural reason one is the hardest substance known and the other writes on paper.
**Answer: Diamond locks every atom into a rigid three-dimensional network, so nothing can slide or dent; graphite bonds its atoms in flat layers held only weakly to each other, so the layers slide off onto the paper.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Name carbon's three families of simple substances met in the lesson.
**Answer: Diamond, graphite, and the amorphous carbons — charcoal, soot and coke.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is silicon never found free in nature, and how is the element obtained?
**Answer: It is bound into its oxide and the silicate rocks everywhere; the element is won by reducing its oxide.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What is a semiconductor, and why did that one property matter so much?
**Answer: A material that conducts poorly when pure but in a controlled way when traces of other elements are worked into its crystal — the controllable conduction on which chips and solar cells, and so the electronics industry, stand.**
