# Brønsted–Lowry — protons handed over

## Do it first
Ammonia turns litmus blue yet owns no OH⁻. Before reading, watch it meet water and steal something. What it steals explains everything.

[Picture 21713 — A proton passing, both pairs labelled.]

Brønsted–Lowry: an acid is a proton (H⁺) donor; a base is a proton acceptor. No water solvent is required by the definition itself.

Every acid, having donated its proton, becomes its conjugate base; every base, having accepted one, becomes its conjugate acid — conjugate pairs differ by exactly one proton.

Water ionises itself slightly: one water molecule donates a proton to another, giving H₃O⁺ and OH⁻. Ammonia does the same with water, accepting a proton to become NH₄⁺ and leaving OH⁻ — hence its basicity.

Amphiprotic substances (water, HCO₃⁻ and kin) can donate or accept a proton, acting as acid or base as the partner demands.
