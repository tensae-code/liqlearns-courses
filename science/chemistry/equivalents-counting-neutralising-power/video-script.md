# Video script — Equivalents — counting neutralising power

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

One mole of sulfuric acid neutralises twice what one mole of hydrochloric acid does.

The equivalent mass of an acid is its molar mass divided by the number of replaceable H⁺ it gives; for a base, by the OH⁻ it gives (or H⁺ it accepts).

Equivalents count neutralising power: equal equivalents of acid and base exactly neutralise each other.

Normality-style accounting multiplies molarity by the number of equivalents per mole.

Now you. Say it back: What do equivalents count?
Answer: Neutralising power.
