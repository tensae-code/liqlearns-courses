# Equivalents — counting neutralising power

## Do it first
One mole of sulfuric acid neutralises twice what one mole of hydrochloric acid does. Before reading, say what unit counts neutralising power fairly, mole by mole.

The equivalent mass of an acid is its molar mass divided by the number of replaceable H⁺ it gives; for a base, by the OH⁻ it gives (or H⁺ it accepts).

Equivalents count neutralising power: equal equivalents of acid and base exactly neutralise each other.

Normality-style accounting multiplies molarity by the number of equivalents per mole.
