# Hands-on — Raoult's law and ideal solutions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Work Raoult's law on paper
The pure solvent's vapour pressure is 32 kPa. Calculate the vapour pressure over solutions where the solvent's mole fraction is 1.00, 0.95, 0.80 and 0.50. Plot your four points (mole fraction across, vapour pressure up) and draw the straight line through them — that line is Raoult's law itself. Under the graph, write in one sentence what the dissolved solute did to the vapour pressure and why the mole fraction, not the mass, is the share that counts.

## Sort the deviations
Write these three mixture reports on cards: (A) vapour pressure above the Raoult line, beaker cools on mixing; (B) vapour pressure below the Raoult line, beaker warms on mixing; (C) vapour pressure exactly on the Raoult line at every strength, no temperature change. Sort the cards into positive deviation, negative deviation and ideal, saying out loud for each card the state of the attractions between the unlike molecules.

## Trace a distillation on the diagram
Draw a boiling-point/composition diagram with a minimum in the middle (the azeotrope). Mark a starting liquid on the left of the minimum. Draw the staircase of boil-and-condense steps and say where it ends; then mark a starting liquid on the right and do the same. Finish by writing, in your own words, the one composition the distillation can never cross and why.
