Today: **Raoult's law and ideal solutions** — Uganda's way: the rule that prices a solution's vapour.

Here is the rule. The vapour pressure over a solution is the pure liquid's vapour pressure times the liquid's share of the moles — its mole fraction. Nine-tenths solvent, nine-tenths of the vapour pressure. That is Raoult's law, and each liquid in a mixture of two volatile liquids follows it with its own share.
A solution that follows the law at every strength is ideal — the unlike molecules attract each other just as strongly as their own kind, so mixing makes no heat and no change of volume. When the unlike attraction is weaker, the vapour pressure climbs above the law's line — a positive deviation, and mixing takes in heat. When it is stronger, the pressure falls below the line — a negative deviation, and mixing gives heat out.
And distillation lives here: the vapour is always richer in the more volatile part, so boiling and condensing again and again climbs toward it — until a deviating mixture reaches its azeotrope, the mixture whose vapour is exactly itself, where the climb stops.
