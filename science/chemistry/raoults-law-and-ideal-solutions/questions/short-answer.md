# Raoult's law and ideal solutions — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State Raoult's law in symbols and say what each symbol means.
**Answer:** p = x(solvent) × P°: p is the vapour pressure over the solution, x(solvent) is the solvent's mole fraction (its share of all the moles present), and P° is the pure solvent's vapour pressure.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What three signs together mark a solution as ideal?
**Answer:** It obeys Raoult's law at every concentration, mixing produces no heat change, and the total volume is exactly the sum of the volumes mixed — because the attractions between unlike molecules match the attractions in the pure liquids.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A mixture gives out heat when it is mixed and its vapour pressure falls below the Raoult line. Name the deviation and explain it.
**Answer:** A negative deviation. The attractions between the unlike molecules are stronger than those in the pure liquids, so molecules are held back from escaping (lower vapour pressure) and energy is released as the stronger attractions form (heat given out).

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can fractional distillation separate an ideal mixture completely?
**Answer:** Because at every boiling the vapour is richer in the more volatile component than the liquid it left; condensing and re-boiling repeats the enrichment step by step until the pure volatile liquid (and finally the pure less volatile one) is reached.

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) A positively deviating mixture is distilled. What limits the separation?
**Answer:** Its minimum-boiling azeotrope: distillation can reach the azeotrope and one pure liquid, but cannot cross the azeotrope's composition, because there the vapour has the same composition as the liquid and boiling changes nothing.
