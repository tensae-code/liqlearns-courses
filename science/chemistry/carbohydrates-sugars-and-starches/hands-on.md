# Hands-on — Carbohydrates — sugars and starches

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort the family by unit count
Write these names on six cards: glucose, sucrose, lactose, starch, cellulose, fructose. Sort the cards into three piles — single sugars, double sugars, and long chains (polysaccharides) — saying out loud for each card how many single-sugar units it holds. Then check your piles against sub lessons 1 and 2.

## Build a joining on paper
Draw two glucose units side by side as two hexagons. Draw the water molecule that leaves when they join — one oxygen and two hydrogens — and draw the joined pair with the join boxed. Under it, write the name of the reverse change that splits the pair apart again, and where in daily life that splitting happens.

## Keep a one-day carbohydrate diary (kitchen materials only)
For one day, list every food you eat that is mainly carbohydrate — grains, tubers, fruits, sweets, drinks. Beside each, write which family member does the work (a single sugar, a double sugar, or starch) and mark the free sugars with a star. At the day's end, count your stars and say, from sub lesson 3, one change that would cut the harm without cutting the staple foods.
