# Carbohydrates — sugars and starches — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Where does the name "carbohydrate" come from?
**Answer: From the family's make-up — carbon together with hydrogen and oxygen in roughly water's proportion (two hydrogens to one oxygen), as if the carbon carried water: carbon + hydrate.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) What does the D in D-glucose tell you?
**Answer: It tells the sugar's handedness in the D/L sorting — the –OH at the reference carbon stands on the D side; nearly all natural sugars, glucose included, are D.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What is lost when two single sugars join, and what is the joining's product class for two, a few, and hundreds of units?
**Answer: A molecule of water is lost at the join. Two units give a disaccharide, a few give an oligosaccharide, and hundreds or thousands give a polysaccharide.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Starch and cellulose are both glucose chains, yet one feeds us and the other passes through as fibre. Why?
**Answer: Their glucose units are joined differently: the starch join is one human digestion can hydrolyse, while the cellulose join makes straight tough fibres no human enzyme can split.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) State the chief benefit of carbohydrates to living beings and the chief harm named in the lesson.
**Answer: Benefit: they are the body's first fuel and the world's staple energy foods — digestion hydrolyses them to glucose, whose respiration releases energy. Harm: excess free sugar brings tooth decay and weight gain with its following sicknesses, type 2 diabetes among them.**
