# Lesson plan — Carbohydrates — sugars and starches

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Carbohydrates — sugars and starches
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the carbohydrates (glúcidos) as one family — built of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, sorted by how many single-sugar units they join: monosaccharides (one unit, with the D/L notation that sorts the sugars), disaccharides and oligosaccharides (a few units joined, losing water at each join), and polysaccharides (long chains — starch, glycogen and cellulose) — and their essential role in living beings, their daily use, and their benefits and harms; Nicaragua's Grade 10 book teaches them inside its oxygenated-and-nitrogenated-compounds unit (Unidad VI)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Oxygen functional groups — the map is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **The single sugars** — A carbohydrate is a compound of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, the hydrogen and oxygen roughly in water's 2-to-1 count, which is where the name (carbon + hydrate) comes from. The single sugars, monosaccharides, cannot be split into smaller sugars: glucose, fructose and galactose, all C₆H₁₂O₆, are the chief ones. Sugars are sorted D or L by the handedness of their structure — by which side the –OH sits on at the reference carbon — and nearly all natural sugars are D.
2. **Sugars joined — doubles, chains and stores** — Join two single sugars, losing a molecule of water, and the double sugar is a disaccharide: sucrose (table sugar), lactose (milk sugar) and maltose. Join a few and the family is the oligosaccharides; join hundreds or thousands and it is a polysaccharide: starch, the plant's store; glycogen, the animal's store; and cellulose, the plant's building fibre — all three built of glucose units, differing in how the units are joined. Splitting the joins back — hydrolysis — gives the single sugars again.
3. **Carbohydrates in living beings — use, benefits and harms** — Carbohydrates are the body's first fuel: digestion hydrolyses them to glucose, and respiration releases glucose's energy. In daily use they are the staple foods — grains, tubers, fruits — and table sugar sweetens food and preserves it. The harms come with excess and with free sugars in particular: tooth decay, and the weight gain and sickness that follow a diet overloaded with sugar.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes starch up with table sugar → count the units: a sugar is one unit or a few joined; a starch is hundreds of glucose units in one long chain.
