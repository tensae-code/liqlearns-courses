Today: **Carbohydrates — sugars and starches** — Nicaragua's way: the family taught by counting its building units.

Start with one single sugar — glucose. Six carbons, and hydrogen and oxygen riding along in water's own count. Glucose, fructose and galactose are all single sugars with the same formula and different shapes — and the sugars of living things are almost all of the D handedness.
Now join them. Two singles join, a drop of water leaves, and you have a double sugar — the sucrose in your table sugar bowl. Join hundreds of glucose units and you have a polysaccharide: starch, the store a plant keeps in its seeds and roots; glycogen, the quick store in your own muscles; cellulose, the tough fibre that builds the plant itself.
And the point of it all: your body unjoins these chains back to glucose and burns the glucose for energy. That is the benefit. The harm comes when free sugar floods in faster than the body needs it — rotten teeth and a heavy body are its signatures.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
