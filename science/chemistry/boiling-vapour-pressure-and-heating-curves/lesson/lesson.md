# Boiling, vapour pressure and heating curves

## Do it first
Heat ice steadily and its temperature climbs — then stops dead at melting, though heat still flows in. It stops again at boiling. Before reading, say where the heat goes during the stops.

[Picture 21664 — A heating curve with flat plateaus.]

Vapour pressure is the pressure of a liquid's vapour above it; it rises with temperature. Boiling happens when vapour pressure equals the outside pressure; the normal boiling point is measured at 1 atm.

The molar heat of vaporisation is the energy to turn one mole of liquid into gas; the molar heat of fusion does the same for melting.

On a heating curve, sloping parts show temperature rising; the flat plateaus are phase changes, where added heat breaks particle arrangements instead of raising temperature. Calculations use the heats across each plateau.
