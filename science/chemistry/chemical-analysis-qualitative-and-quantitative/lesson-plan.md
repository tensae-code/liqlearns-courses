# Lesson plan — Chemical analysis — qualitative and quantitative

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Chemical analysis — qualitative and quantitative

## Sub lessons
1. **What analysis is for** — **Chemical analysis** serves **medicine, agriculture, industry and the environment** — Egypt's examples run from **blood sugar** in the clinic to **soil** on the farm and **air pollutants** over the city. In every field the craft is the same pair of questions asked of a sample: what is in it, and how much.
2. **The two kinds — what, then how much** — **Qualitative analysis identifies the constituents** — what the sample contains. **Quantitative analysis measures how much** of each constituent is present. And the order is fixed: **qualitative always before quantitative** — there is nothing sensible to measure until you know what you are measuring.
3. **The salt case — anion and cation** — For **inorganic salts**, qualitative analysis has a definite shape: identify the **acidic radical (the anion)** and the **basic radical (the cation)**. Those two identifications name the salt, and they set up everything the chapter does next — the anion groups, the cation groups, and then the measuring arts of titration and gravimetry.
