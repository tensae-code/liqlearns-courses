# Fizzy drinks and the limewater test

## Do it first
Before you read: open a bottle of fizzy drink in your mind. Bubbles rush up, the bottle hisses. What is that gas? Where was it before the bottle was opened? And does a gas weigh anything? Answer all three before reading. The Djibouti Grade 6 chapter 'Boissons gazeuses' answers them as an investigation, starting from the bottles its pupils actually drink — Sprite and Kalimane. This lesson stands on the teacher guide only; no Djibouti pupil book is held.

## Sub lesson 1 — A fizzy drink is a mixture — collecting its gas

A fizzy drink is a **mixture of a liquid and a gas**: the drink itself, with a gas **dissolved** in it under pressure in the sealed bottle. Open the bottle, the pressure drops, and the gas escapes as bubbles — the drink 'degasses'.

To study the gas, the class **collects it by displacement of water**: the gas is led into an inverted, water-filled vessel standing in water; as the gas bubbles in, it pushes the water down and takes its place, so a measurable volume of gas is gathered without mixing with air. Collecting over water is the standard first method for a gas, and this chapter is where Djibouti's pupils meet it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Naming the gas — the limewater test for CO₂

Which gas is it? The test is **limewater** — clear lime water (itself prepared by **filtration**, the method of the previous chapter). Bubble the collected gas through limewater and the limewater **turns cloudy (milky)**. That cloudiness is the recognition test for **carbon dioxide, CO₂** — so the gas in the fizzy drink is named: carbon dioxide, dissolved in the drink in the bottle and escaping when it is opened.

Note the pattern Djibouti is teaching across the two chapters: a recognition test for water (copper sulfate), a recognition test for carbon dioxide (limewater) — a substance is identified by the change it produces in a test reagent, not by guessing.

## Sub lesson 3 — A gas has mass — and cold holds the gas in

The chapter's second proof is on the balance. **Weigh the sealed bottle; let the gas escape completely; weigh again.** The opened, degassed bottle weighs **less** — the missing mass is the mass of the escaped gas. A gas is matter: it takes up volume (it filled the collection vessel) and it **has mass** (the balance loses exactly its mass).

And temperature matters: **a cold drink holds more of its gas** — warm the drink and more gas escapes, faster. That is why a warm bottle fizzes over and a cold one pours quietly, and why the amount of gas collected depends on the drink's temperature — a variable the class must control to compare fairly.

## The key ideas

- A fizzy drink is a **liquid + gas mixture**; the gas is **dissolved** under pressure and escapes on opening.
- The gas is **collected by displacement of water**.
- **Limewater turning cloudy identifies the gas as carbon dioxide (CO₂)** — the limewater test; the limewater itself is prepared by filtration.
- **A gas has mass:** the bottle weighs less after degassing by exactly the escaped gas's mass.
- **Cold drinks hold more of their gas**; warmth drives more gas out, faster.
