# Lesson plan — Fizzy drinks and the limewater test

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Fizzy drinks and the limewater test
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what the Djibouti Grade 6 guide proves about a fizzy drink (Sprite, Kalimane): it is a liquid+gas mixture whose dissolved gas can be collected by displacement of water, is identified as carbon dioxide because it clouds limewater, and has mass — shown by weighing the bottle before and after degassing, with cold drinks holding more of their gas (teacher-guide evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Separating mixtures — filtration and settling is recommended first, never forced.
**Evidence standing:** this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Djibouti) — its sources name the guide and count it nowhere (acceptance 0).

Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **A fizzy drink is a mixture — collecting its gas** — A fizzy drink is a **mixture of a liquid and a gas**: the drink itself, with a gas **dissolved** in it under pressure in the sealed bottle.
2. **Naming the gas — the limewater test for CO₂** — Which gas is it? The test is **limewater** — clear lime water (itself prepared by **filtration**, the method of the previous chapter).
3. **A gas has mass — and cold holds the gas in** — The chapter's second proof is on the balance.
