# Hands-on — Periodicity — patterns that predict

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Observe and describe (the book's laboratory work, adapted)
The practical work behind this lesson is laboratory work. Do NOT do it at home with real chemicals, heat or glassware. Instead: study the lesson picture — the calendar grid with blanks being filled from the pattern, beside an element grid — and write the full observation a pupil in the laboratory would make: what is set up, what changes, what stays the same, and which safety rule protects each step. The laboratory safety rules from this course (goggles, apron, tied hair, tidy bench, acid into water, never point a heated tube at anyone, report every injury) are part of the answer.

## Paper model
Build the lesson's idea as a paper or card model — draw it, cut it out, label every part, and use the model to teach the lesson to a partner. A model that cannot be explained is not finished.

## Spot it in daily life
Find one place where this lesson's idea shows up in daily life — a market, a workshop, a kitchen, a farm — and describe what you observed in three sentences, without touching or tasting any chemical.
