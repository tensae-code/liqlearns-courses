# Sulfur dioxide — bleacher and preservative — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** What is sulfur dioxide, and what is the sign that a bleaching was done by it?
**Answer: Sulfur dioxide, SO₂, is the gas made when sulfur burns. The sign of its bleaching is that it does not last: most bleached materials regain their colours on exposure to air.**

**2.** How does burning sulfur sterilise a space?
**Answer: The sulfur burns inside the closed space and fills it with sulfur dioxide; the gas itself sterilises the space, killing what would spoil or infect.**

**3.** Name two faces of SO₂ as a guard against spoiling.
**Answer: It sterilises spaces when sulfur is burned inside them, and it preserves foods — in both, the gas is hostile to the microbes that rot things.**

**4.** Why is SO₂ both a tool and a hazard?
**Answer: Dose and place decide: used in a closed space or on food it sterilises and preserves, but poured into the air from coal burning and industry it is a serious health hazard; sulfur itself also ignites spontaneously near 400 °C.**
