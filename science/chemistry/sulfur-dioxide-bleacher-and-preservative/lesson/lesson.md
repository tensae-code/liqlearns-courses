# Sulfur dioxide — bleacher and preservative

## Do it first
A strip of coloured cloth comes out of one treatment white — and a week later, left in the open air, its colour has come back. Before reading, say out loud: what kind of bleaching must that have been, and what does the colour's return prove about it? Then read on: this is Iraq's Grade 9 way in its sulfur chapter (Ch.8) — sulfur dioxide, SO₂, the gas of burning sulfur, at its three jobs.

## Sub lesson 1 — A bleaching gas — whose work air undoes

**Sulfur dioxide, SO₂**, bleaches: materials bleached by it are whitened — and the book's test of the thing is what happens next: **most bleached materials regain their colours on exposure to air**. SO₂'s bleaching is therefore temporary in a way that sets it apart: leave the bleached material in the air and the colour creeps back. A bleach whose work the air itself undoes — that is sulfur dioxide's signature.

## Sub lesson 2 — Burning sulfur — steriliser and food preservative

The same gas does guard duty. **Burning sulfur inside a space sterilises it** — the SO₂ from the burning fills the closed space and kills what would spoil or infect. And the gas **preserves foods**: sulfur dioxide is used to keep food from spoiling, the fumigating and preserving faces of one property — SO₂ is hostile to the microbes that rot things.

## Sub lesson 3 — The hazard side — a gas the air should not carry

The lesson closes on the danger. **Sulfur ignites spontaneously near 400 °C**, and the SO₂ poured into the air **from coal burning and industry is a serious health hazard**. The gas that sterilises a closed store-room and preserves food is, loose in the air people breathe, a pollutant that harms health — dose and place decide whether SO₂ is a tool or a danger.

## The key ideas

- **Sulfur dioxide (SO₂)** bleaches — and **most bleached materials regain their colours on exposure to air**: its bleaching is undone by air.
- **Burning sulfur inside a space sterilises it**, and SO₂ **preserves foods**.
- **Sulfur ignites spontaneously near 400 °C**; SO₂ from **coal burning and industry** is a **serious health hazard**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain sulfur dioxide to your companion: why its bleaching does not last, how burning sulfur sterilises a space and preserves food, and why the same gas in city air is a hazard. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. A dyed straw hat bleached with SO₂ slowly turns coloured again on the line. What does the return of the colour prove? (That the bleaching was SO₂'s temporary kind — most materials bleached by sulfur dioxide regain their colours on exposure to air.)
2. Why is sulfur burned inside a store-room that must be sterilised, rather than the room being sprayed with the gas from a cylinder — and what is actually doing the sterilising? (The burning itself makes the gas in place: sulfur burning in the closed space fills it with SO₂, and the sulfur dioxide is what sterilises the space.)
3. A town downwind of coal fires has constant SO₂ in its air. Tool or hazard — and what decides? (Hazard. Dose and place decide: measured into a closed space or food, SO₂ sterilises and preserves; poured into the air from coal burning and industry, it is a serious health hazard.)
