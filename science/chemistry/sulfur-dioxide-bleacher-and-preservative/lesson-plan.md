# Lesson plan — Sulfur dioxide — bleacher and preservative

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Sulfur dioxide — bleacher and preservative
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sulfur dioxide, SO₂ — the gas sulfur makes when it burns — at work as a bleacher whose work air undoes, as the steriliser and preservative of burning sulfur, and as a serious health hazard when coal and industry pour it into the air (Iraq, Grade 9 — Ch.8, printed p. 128)

## Sub lessons
1. **A bleaching gas — whose work air undoes** — **Sulfur dioxide, SO₂**, bleaches: materials bleached by it are whitened — and the book's test of the thing is what happens next: **most bleached materials regain their colours on exposure to air**. SO₂'s bleaching is therefore temporary in a way that sets it apart: leave the bleached material in the air and the colour creeps back. A bleach whose work the air itself undoes — that is sulfur dioxide's signature.
2. **Burning sulfur — steriliser and food preservative** — The same gas does guard duty. **Burning sulfur inside a space sterilises it** — the SO₂ from the burning fills the closed space and kills what would spoil or infect. And the gas **preserves foods**: sulfur dioxide is used to keep food from spoiling, the fumigating and preserving faces of one property — SO₂ is hostile to the microbes that rot things.
3. **The hazard side — a gas the air should not carry** — The lesson closes on the danger. **Sulfur ignites spontaneously near 400 °C**, and the SO₂ poured into the air **from coal burning and industry is a serious health hazard**. The gas that sterilises a closed store-room and preserves food is, loose in the air people breathe, a pollutant that harms health — dose and place decide whether SO₂ is a tool or a danger.
