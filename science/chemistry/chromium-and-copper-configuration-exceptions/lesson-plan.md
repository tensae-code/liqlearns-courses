# Lesson plan — Chromium and copper — configuration exceptions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Chromium and copper — configuration exceptions
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the anomalous configurations of chromium and copper in Kuwait's Grade 10 student book (Unit 1, Lesson 5-1, printed p. 62): one electron moves from 4s to 3d so the d sublevel becomes half-full in chromium — the named exception to the filling rules

## Sub lessons
1. **What the filling rules predict** — By the configuration rules of the earlier lessons — Aufbau order, Hund's rule, Pauli's principle — chromium (Z = 24) should fill 4s before 3d and stop at **[Ar] 4s² 3d⁴**: two electrons in 4s, four in 3d. That is the prediction the rules make, and it is the prediction the book sets up — so that the exception can be seen against it.
2. **What chromium actually does — the half-full d** — Chromium does not keep the predicted arrangement. **One electron moves from 4s to 3d**, and the real configuration is **[Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵**. The book's own account of the move is the landing place: with five electrons the **d sublevel becomes half-full** — one electron in each of its five orbitals — and that half-full arrangement is the stable one the atom settles into. The move costs one 4s electron and buys the half-filled d sublevel. This is why the book teaches chromium as a **named case** — "What happened in chromium" — inside its applications of the configuration rules (printed p. 62), not as a footnote.
3. **Copper — the full-d twin case** — Copper (Z = 29) is the exception's twin, the second atom this lesson is named for. The rules predict [Ar] 4s² 3d⁹; the atom again moves **one electron from 4s to 3d**, giving **[Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰** — this time the d sublevel becomes **completely full**. Half-full in chromium, full in copper: the same one-electron move, landing on the two specially stable d counts, 5 and 10. Every other atom in the row keeps the predicted order — these two are the exceptions a learner must simply know, with the half-full/full reason the book gives for chromium carrying both.
