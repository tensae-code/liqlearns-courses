# Shapes of s, p and d orbitals

## Do it first
An orbital is not a path — it is a region where the electron is likely to be found. Before reading, picture a ball, then a dumbbell. Those are your first two orbital shapes.

[Picture 21622 — s is a sphere; p dumbbells point three ways.]

An s orbital is a sphere around the nucleus.

A p orbital is a dumbbell with two lobes, and there are three of them, pointing along the x, y and z axes.

d orbitals have more complex shapes, and f orbitals more complex still.

Each shape is a probability region — the electron is found inside it most of the time, not on a drawn track.
