# Basic, applied and technological research — the penicillin chain

## Do it first
Before you read: a mould on a spoiled dish became a medicine made by the ton. Say out loud: how many different kinds of work stand between a strange observation and a factory — and are they all 'research'? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 10 book sorts research into three kinds and walks all three down one chain — penicillin.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three kinds of research

Yemen's book links chemistry, technology (التقنية) and society through **three kinds of research**. **Basic research** finds out how the world is, for knowledge's sake. **Applied research** takes a finding and aims it at a use. **Technological research** turns the use into a working, repeatable production — the machines, the process, the scale.

## Sub lesson 2 — One chain — penicillin three times

The book traces the three kinds through **one worked chain**. Basic research **discovers penicillin and works out its structure**. Applied research turns that discovery into an **antibiotic industry** — a medicine that can be made on purpose. Technology **mass-produces it** — the same medicine, in factory quantities, reaching everyone who needs it. One discovery, three kinds of work, each standing on the one before.

## Sub lesson 3 — Society in the loop — gains, harms, feedback

The chain does not run one way. Positive applications the book names include **local drug manufacture, pesticides, fertilisers and optical fibres**; the negative side is named too — the **atomic bomb** — and **society feeds back on science**, judging, funding and restraining what research does. Chemistry, technology and society are one loop, not a line.

## The key ideas

- Research comes in **three kinds: basic** (find out), **applied** (aim at a use), **technological** (produce at scale).
- The **penicillin chain** shows all three: discovery and structure → an antibiotic industry → mass production.
- Society is **inside the loop**: gains (drugs, pesticides, fertilisers, optical fibres), harms (the atomic bomb), and society's feedback on science.

## Do It Yourself
1. A team measures exactly how a new mould kills bacteria, with no product in mind. Which kind of research is that, and what are the other two kinds? (Basic research. Applied research would aim the finding at a use (a medicine); technological research would turn the use into mass production.)
2. Why does the book teach the three kinds through one chain instead of three separate examples? (So the joins show: each kind stands on the one before — no mass-produced penicillin without the industry, no industry without the discovery and its structure.)
3. Name the negative application the book sets against the positive ones, and the closing idea it supports. (The atomic bomb. It supports the book's close: society feeds back on science — the loop includes judgement and restraint, not only production.)
