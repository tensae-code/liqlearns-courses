# Lesson plan — How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 8 way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 8 way
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Syria's Grade 8 chemistry course-shape taught as content in its own right — the shared Syrian design as the book's Chemistry unit (Unit 1, Structural chemistry) runs it: objectives and key words per lesson, “I try and I conclude” experiments, worked applications, enrichment boxes, a self-test per lesson, unit questions and a closing project (Syria, Grade 8 — *الفيزياء والكيمياء للصف الثامن الأساسي (Physics & Chemistry, Grade 8 Basic)*, printed p. 3)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Objectives and key words open every lesson** — Every lesson of the Chemistry unit opens with its **printed objectives** and **key words**: the objectives state what the learner will be able to do, and the key words name the lesson's vocabulary before the teaching begins.
2. **“I try and I conclude”, worked applications and enrichment** — The body of each lesson is built around **“I try and I conclude” bench experiments** with an explicit **conclusion box**: try first, watch, then conclude — the finding stated separately so it can be checked against the trying.
3. **A self-test per lesson, unit questions, a closing project** — Each lesson closes its own loop with a **self-test**, so the learner checks the lesson before leaving it. The unit then gathers everything into **unit questions**, and the Chemistry unit ends with a **closing project** — in this book, the iron-rust project — where the unit's chemistry is used on a real question and reported.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit and a teacher present — never home chemicals; kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the self-test and unit questions are the same check → the self-test checks ONE lesson as you leave it; the unit questions check the WHOLE unit together.
- Treats enrichment boxes as the main line → enrichment stretches the idea sideways; the main line runs objectives → try and conclude → applications → self-test.
- Skips the objectives → the objectives are the lesson's contract — read them first and the rest of the lesson has a target.
