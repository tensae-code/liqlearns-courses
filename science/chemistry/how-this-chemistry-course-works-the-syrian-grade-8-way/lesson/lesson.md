# How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 8 way

## Do it first
Before you read: the Grade 8 book carries Physics and Chemistry in one volume, and its Chemistry unit runs in a fixed shape. Say out loud: what parts do you expect to find inside one well-built lesson? Then read on: this lesson names the Syrian Grade 8 shape part by part, as printed p. 3 sets it running.

## Sub lesson 1 — Objectives and key words open every lesson

Every lesson of the Chemistry unit opens with its **printed objectives** and **key words**: the objectives state what the learner will be able to do, and the key words name the lesson's vocabulary before the teaching begins.

In the Grade 8 unit this opening heads seven lessons of structural chemistry — from the atom and the element through bonds, formulas, reactions and the two laws, to chemical calculation. The shape does not change as the content climbs; the learner always knows where a lesson starts.

## Sub lesson 2 — “I try and I conclude”, worked applications and enrichment

The body of each lesson is built around **“I try and I conclude” bench experiments** with an explicit **conclusion box**: try first, watch, then conclude — the finding stated separately so it can be checked against the trying.

**Worked applications** then carry the concluded idea into solved examples, and **enrichment boxes** stretch it sideways — a striking fact, a danger, a piece of history — without breaking the lesson's line.

## Sub lesson 3 — A self-test per lesson, unit questions, a closing project

Each lesson closes its own loop with a **self-test**, so the learner checks the lesson before leaving it. The unit then gathers everything into **unit questions**, and the Chemistry unit ends with a **closing project** — in this book, the iron-rust project — where the unit's chemistry is used on a real question and reported.

Self-test, unit questions, project: three different checks — on the lesson, on the unit, and on the learner's own hands.

## The key ideas

- The Syrian Grade 8 Chemistry unit runs the shared design: **objectives and key words** open every lesson (printed p. 3).
- **“I try and I conclude”** bench experiments with an explicit conclusion box form the body, with **worked applications** and **enrichment boxes**.
- A **self-test per lesson**, **unit questions** and a **closing project** (here, iron rust) close the unit.
