# Lesson plan — Pure substances — simple and compound

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Pure substances — simple and compound
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** pure substances sorted by their building: a corps pur simple is built from atoms of one kind (H₂, O₂), a corps pur composé from several kinds (H₂O, CO₂, C₄H₁₀, NaCl); the molecule is an association of atoms, and the chemical formula is the writing that shows the nature and number of its atoms (Mauritania, Grades 8 and 9 — *Sciences Physiques 2AS*, Unit I, printed p. 11; *Sciences Physiques 3AS*, Ch. I, printed p. 9)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Atoms, elements and chemical symbols is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit and a teacher present — never home chemicals; kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.
