# Pure substances — simple and compound

## Do it first
Before you read: oxygen gas and water are both pure substances — nothing mixed into either. Say out loud: what difference in their building could still divide pure substances into two kinds? Then read on: Mauritania's collège books divide them by the kinds of atom inside.

## Sub lesson 1 — Simple — atoms of one kind

The 2AS book's sorting (Unit I §II, printed p. 11): a pure simple substance — corps pur simple — is built from atoms of one kind only. Its examples are H₂ and O₂: two atoms in each molecule, but both of the same kind, so the substance is simple. The count of atoms does not decide; the count of kinds does.

## Sub lesson 2 — Compound — several kinds joined

A pure compound substance — corps pur composé — is built from several kinds of atom: H₂O, CO₂, C₄H₁₀, NaCl in the 2AS list; the 3AS book's reactions chapter re-sorts the molecules as simple (O₂, H₂, N₂) against complex (CO₂, H₂O, C₄H₁₀, Fe₂O₃ as printed, glucose) at its door (Ch. I, printed p. 9). Joined kinds make a new substance with its own properties — not a mixture of its elements sitting side by side.

## Sub lesson 3 — Molecules and formulas — the writing of the building

Under both kinds sits the molecule: an association of atoms. The chemical formula is the writing that shows the building — the nature of the atoms (their symbols) and the number of each (their subscripts). Read a formula and you can sort the substance at sight: one symbol kind — simple; several — compound.

## The key ideas

- Corps pur simple: atoms of one kind (H₂, O₂). Corps pur composé: several kinds (H₂O, CO₂, C₄H₁₀, NaCl).
- The sorting counts kinds of atom, not numbers of atoms.
- A molecule is an association of atoms; its formula shows the nature and number of its atoms.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the lesson to your companion in your own words, working through its sub lessons in order (simple — atoms of one kind; compound — several kinds joined; molecules and formulas — the writing of the building), using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort NaCl, O₂, CO₂ and H₂ into simple and compound, with the rule. (Simple: O₂, H₂ (one kind of atom each). Compound: NaCl, CO₂ (several kinds joined). The rule counts kinds, not atoms.)
2. O₂ and O₃ both hold only oxygen, in twos and threes. Are both simple? (Yes — both are built from atoms of one kind; the number of atoms in the molecule does not change the sorting.)
3. What two facts does a chemical formula show? (The nature of the molecule's atoms (their symbols) and the number of each (their subscripts).)
