# Nuclear accidents and radiation safety — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A radioactive source is a working tool while it stays…
a) Glowing brightly  b) Sealed, shielded, registered and in trained hands  c) In a scrap yard  d) Passed from hand to hand
**Answer: b) Sealed, shielded, registered and in trained hands**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) What makes a lost radioactive source so dangerous to its finders?
a) It is very heavy  b) It explodes on touch  c) Radiation cannot be seen, smelled or felt as it does its damage  d) It smells strongly of chemicals
**Answer: c) Radiation cannot be seen, smelled or felt as it does its damage**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) The Goiânia accident of 1987 began with…
a) A nuclear power reactor failing  b) A discarded radiotherapy capsule of caesium-137 handled by people who did not know the danger  c) A bomb test  d) A hospital treatment going wrong on a patient
**Answer: b) A discarded radiotherapy capsule of caesium-137 handled by people who did not know the danger**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) How many people died in the Goiânia accident?
a) None  b) One  c) Four  d) Four hundred
**Answer: c) Four**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) The three physical levers of radiation protection are…
a) Water, soap and sunlight  b) Shielding, distance and short time near the source  c) Gloves, boots and a hat  d) Speed, strength and size
**Answer: b) Shielding, distance and short time near the source**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) A radioactive source that is finished with must be…
a) Thrown away with ordinary rubbish  b) Buried in a garden  c) Given to a scrap dealer  d) Disposed of as radioactive waste under control, by the responsible authority
**Answer: d) Disposed of as radioactive waste under control, by the responsible authority**
