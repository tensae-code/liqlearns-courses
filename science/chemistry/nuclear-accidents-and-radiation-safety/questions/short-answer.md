# Nuclear accidents and radiation safety — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) When does a radioactive source stop being a tool and start being an accident?
**Answer: When control is lost — the source leaves its shielding, its capsule is broken open, and people who have no way to know what they are handling touch it and carry it among others.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Tell the Goiânia case in two sentences.
**Answer: In 1987, in Goiânia, Brazil, a discarded radiotherapy capsule containing caesium-137 was handled and opened by people who did not know the danger. Four people died, and Brazil teaches the case in its chemistry book as the national case for radiation safety and waste control.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must every source be registered and accounted for at every handover?
**Answer: So that no source can quietly leave the system — a registered, accounted source cannot become anonymous scrap; the moment a handover is unrecorded is the moment a Goiânia can begin.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) You find an unknown metal capsule bearing the radiation trefoil. What do you do?
**Answer: Do not handle it. Keep away and keep others away, and call the authorities — distance and expert control are the protection; curiosity is the danger.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can the same caesium-137 source heal in a hospital and kill in a street?
**Answer: In the hospital it is sealed, shielded, aimed and timed by trained hands, so its radiation falls only where it is wanted; in the street none of those controls exist, and the same radiation falls on whoever is near, for as long as they stay.**
