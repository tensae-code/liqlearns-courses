# Hands-on — Nuclear accidents and radiation safety

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes. Nothing in this hands-on touches any real radioactive or unknown material — it works with signs, distances and paper.

## Read the trefoil (observation only — signs and labels)
Find or draw the radiation warning sign, the trefoil. Say what it marks, where you would expect to meet it (a hospital radiotherapy room, a source container, a waste store), and what you would do — and not do — if you met it on an unlabelled object in a disused building.

## Distance does the work (tape measure and a torch)
In a darkened room, shine a torch at a wall from 1 metre, then from 2 metres, then 3. Sketch how the lit patch grows and dims. Say, in the lesson's terms, why distance protects a person near a source in the same kind of way — and why shielding and short time are still needed beside it.

## One source, guarded on paper (paper exercise)
Write the life of one radiotherapy source as a chain of custody: arrival at the hospital, storage, use, and the day it is finished. At every step name the control in force (sealed, shielded, registered, signed over, disposed of under control). End by marking the exact step where Goiânia's chain broke — and what that break cost.
