Today: **Nuclear accidents and radiation safety** — Brazil's way: the country's own accident, taught so it never happens again.

A radioactive source is a tool while it is sealed, shielded and guarded. In a hospital it treats cancer. But radiation cannot be seen or felt — so if the source is thrown away and strangers pick it up, they are harmed without any warning.
That is what happened in Goiânia, in Brazil, in 1987. A discarded capsule of caesium-137 was handled by people who did not know the danger. Four people died.
So the rules are strict: keep the source sealed and shielded, keep your distance, keep the time short, register every source — and when a source is finished, it is disposed of under control, never thrown away.
