# Lesson plan — Nuclear accidents and radiation safety

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Nuclear accidents and radiation safety
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a nuclear accident of the lost-source kind as a failure of control — a radioactive source (caesium-137 in a sealed radiotherapy capsule) is a working tool while it stays sealed, shielded, registered and in trained hands, and becomes a killer when it is discarded without control into the hands of people who cannot know the danger; Brazil teaches it through its own 1987 Goiânia accident — a discarded caesium-137 radiotherapy capsule handled by people who did not know the danger, 4 deaths — as the national case for radiation safety and waste control
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Radioactivity and Rutherford's nucleus is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
1. **A radioactive source out of control** — A sealed, shielded, registered source in trained hands is a tool (radiotherapy); the accident begins when control is lost — shielding gone, capsule broken, handlers who cannot know what they hold, radiation giving no sight, smell or feeling to warn them.
2. **Goiânia, 1987 — Brazil's own case** — The discarded caesium-137 radiotherapy capsule, handled by people who did not know the danger; 4 deaths; the clean-up that followed — the national case, told straight.
3. **Radiation safety and waste control** — The controls that answer the accident: shielding, distance and short time; sealing, the warning sign, registration and accounted handovers; and finished sources disposed of as radioactive waste under control, never as ordinary rubbish.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: A radioactive source out of control.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Goiânia, 1987 — Brazil's own case.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: Radiation safety and waste control.
4. Tell the Goiânia case straight (what the object was, how control was lost, the 4 deaths), name the three physical levers of protection and the human controls, and state what must happen to a finished source.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the one contrast: same capsule, same substance — a tool inside its controls, a killer outside them. The accident is the missing controls, not the atom.
- Thinks Goiânia was a reactor or bomb accident → it was a medical source, a radiotherapy capsule, thrown away without control; no reactor, no explosion — the spread happened in people's hands and homes.
- Mixes this lesson up with Radioactivity and Rutherford's nucleus → that lesson teaches what radiation is and does; this lesson teaches what happens when a source escapes human control, and the controls that keep it in.
