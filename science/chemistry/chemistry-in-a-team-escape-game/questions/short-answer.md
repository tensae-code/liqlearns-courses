# Chemistry in a team escape game — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can no team finish the JUMISSA! game alone?
**Answer: The final puzzle needs the jigsaw pieces earned by all four teams in phase 1. Each team holds only its own pieces, so the central puzzle in phase 2 can only be solved when the teams combine what they earned — the bonding is built into the rules.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name three of the game's general (non-chemistry) puzzles and say what that tells you about the book.
**Answer: Any three of: a Caesar cipher, flags, matchsticks, a health alphabet, a football World Cup text, arithmetic. It tells you the book is game instructions for group bonding, not a chemistry textbook — exactly one case in it carries chemistry.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What does the chemistry case hold, and how are the six key elements picked out?
**Answer: The case holds an article about elements together with a periodic table. The six elements most common in the human body — oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, calcium and phosphorus — are marked in the article with a UV pen, so they appear under ultraviolet light.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A team reads calcium's atomic number as 12 and its lock will not open. What went wrong, and what should they do?
**Answer: They misread the table — calcium's atomic number is 20 (12 is magnesium's). One wrong lookup makes a wrong code, so they should re-find calcium's cell by its symbol Ca and read the number again; the lock itself is the check.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) In this game the periodic table is used differently from a chart memorised for a test. Explain the difference.
**Answer: The table lies inside the case as a working reference tool: the team reaches for it in the middle of a task to look up the numbers the formula needs. The skill taught is the lookup itself — find the element's cell, read its atomic number — which is how the table is used in real chemistry work.**
