# Hands-on — Phenol — properties, preparation and uses

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The acidity ladder (paper only)
Write these five on cards: ethanol, water, phenol, carbonic acid, ethanoic acid. Order the cards from neutral to strongest acid, using only the evidence in sub lessons 1 and 2 (who reacts with NaOH, who fizzes with carbonates). Read your ladder aloud and defend each step with its test.

## Test detective
Three tubes hold benzene, phenol solution and ethanol. Bromine water is added to each in the school laboratory demonstration (teacher present — phenol is corrosive and is handled by the teacher). Record the observation for each tube and say which single tube the bromine water identifies outright, and why the other two need a second test.

## Carbolic history at home (kitchen materials only)
With an adult, look through the home's cleaning and care cupboard for any product whose label names phenol or a phenolic disinfectant/antiseptic. Record the product, its job, and one label warning. Say, from sub lesson 2, which property of phenol both the job and the warning come from.
