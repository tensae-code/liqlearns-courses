# Lesson plan — Phenol — properties, preparation and uses

## Sub lessons
1. **An –OH on the ring — and not an alcohol** — Phenol is a hydroxyl group joined directly to a benzene ring, C₆H₅OH — and that one structural fact separates it from the alcohols: the ring draws on the –OH, weakens its O–H bond, and makes phenol weakly acidic where ethanol is neutral. Belarus gives phenol its own strand (§27–28) at the close of its alcohols part, exactly because it is the alcohols' ring-bearing relative that behaves differently.
2. **Properties of phenol — acid enough to test** — Phenol is a colourless crystalline solid, slightly soluble in cold water, poisonous and corrosive to skin. Its acidity is real but weak: it reacts with sodium and with sodium hydroxide (where ethanol does not), yet does not fizz with carbonates as the carboxylic acids do. Its ring is activated by the –OH: bromine water is decolourised at once, giving a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol — phenol's recognition test — and iron(III) chloride gives a violet colour.
3. **Obtaining phenol and putting it to work** — Phenol was first won from coal tar, and is now made industrially from benzene (the cumene route: benzene → cumene → phenol with acetone as co-product). Its uses build the modern world of plastics and care: phenol–formaldehyde resins (Bakelite) and other plastics, disinfectants and antiseptics (the carbolic soap of the old hospitals), dyes, and the derivatives — picric acid among them — that §28 surveys.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Calls phenol a carboxylic acid → stand the formulas side by side: phenol has no –COOH; its acidity is the ring-weakened –OH, and the carbonate test it fails is the boundary.
