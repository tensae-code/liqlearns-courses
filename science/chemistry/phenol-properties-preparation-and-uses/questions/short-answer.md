# Phenol — properties, preparation and uses — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is phenol weakly acidic when ethanol, with the same –OH group, is neutral?
**Answer: In phenol the –OH is joined directly to the benzene ring, whose π cloud draws electron density from the oxygen and weakens the O–H bond, so the hydrogen is given up far more readily than an alcohol's.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give two reactions that prove phenol's acidity and one carbonate test result that limits it.
**Answer: It reacts with sodium metal (hydrogen evolved) and with sodium hydroxide solution to give sodium phenoxide and water; but it gives no fizz with carbonates or hydrogencarbonates, showing it is weaker than carbonic acid.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does phenol decolourise bromine water instantly while benzene needs a catalyst?
**Answer: The –OH group activates phenol's ring to electrophilic substitution, feeding electron density into the ring, so three bromines enter at once with no catalyst — benzene's unactivated ring reacts only slowly, with a catalyst.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Outline the cumene process for phenol and say what makes it economical.
**Answer: Benzene is converted to cumene, the cumene is oxidised by air, and the oxidised product is split into phenol and acetone — two saleable products from one process, which is the economy.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Name three uses of phenol or its derivatives from §28's survey.
**Answer: Any three of: phenol–formaldehyde resins such as Bakelite and other plastics; antiseptics and disinfectants (carbolic soap and its gentler successors); dyes and pharmaceuticals; picric acid.**
