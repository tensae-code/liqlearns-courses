# Lesson plan — Enzymes — biological catalysts

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Enzymes — biological catalysts
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the enzymes as NCERT's Class XII Biomolecules unit teaches them (§14.3): globular proteins that catalyse the reactions of living bodies, each with an active site that holds its one substrate, working only near an optimum temperature (around 310 K) and pH (around 7), ruined outside them — named by the substrate and reaction they serve (maltase, the oxidoreductases) and met at work in digestion and fermentation (invertase, zymase, maltase, pepsin, trypsin)

## Sub lessons
1. **What an enzyme is — a protein with an active site** — Enzymes are globular proteins that catalyse biochemical reactions, lowering activation energy inside living bodies. Each enzyme's folded surface carries an active site shaped to hold its substrate; enzymes are named from the substrate and the reaction — by the –ase rule (maltase on maltose) and by reaction class (oxidoreductases moving oxidation–reduction, transferases moving groups).
2. **How it works — the complex, and the optimum conditions** — The substrate settles into the active site and an enzyme–substrate complex forms; the bonds rearrange at the site, an enzyme–product complex follows, the products leave and the enzyme stands ready again, unchanged. The working is fenced by conditions: maximum activity at an optimum temperature (near 310 K, body temperature) and an optimum pH (near 7 for most), with activity collapsing outside them as the protein denatures.
3. **Enzymes at work — digestion and fermentation** — NCERT's roll of workers: invertase splits sucrose into glucose and fructose; maltase takes maltose to glucose; zymase ferments glucose to ethanol and carbon dioxide (the brewer's enzyme); urease hydrolyses urea, specific to it alone; pepsin and trypsin cut proteins in digestion; the lipases and amylases stand beside them — each reaction of the body with its named catalyst, the specificity of sub lesson 1 made visible.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Calls an enzyme a living thing → return to the chemistry: an enzyme is a protein molecule, made by a living body but not itself alive; it catalyses in a test tube exactly as in the cell, while its conditions hold.
