Today: **Enzymes — biological catalysts** — India's way: the enzymes section of the Class XII Biomolecules unit — life's own catalysts, met three sections into the molecules of life.

Every reaction in a living body runs at blood temperature, in water, at near-neutral pH — conditions under which ordinary chemistry crawls. What makes it run is the enzymes: globular proteins, each folded around an active site shaped for one substrate. The substrate settles into the site, the enzyme–substrate complex forms, the reaction happens at the site, and the products leave — the enzyme itself unchanged, ready again.
This is catalysis with manners. Maltase takes maltose and nothing else; invertase splits sucrose; zymase ferments glucose to ethanol in the yeast vat; pepsin and trypsin cut proteins in the gut. But the manners are strict: heat the enzyme past its optimum, or move the pH away from its narrow band, and the folded shape — the denaturation of the proteins lesson — collapses, and the catalysis stops.
