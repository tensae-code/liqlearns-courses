# Video script — Faraday's first law — mass from charge

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Run a cell twice as long, deposit twice the metal.

Faraday's first law: the mass of a substance deposited or discharged at an electrode is proportional to the quantity of electricity passed: m ∝ Q.

Charge Q = current × time = I × t, measured in coulombs.

One faraday (F ≈ 96,500 C) is the charge of one mole of electrons, and calculations convert Q to moles of electrons to moles of product.

Now you. Say it back: State Faraday's first law.
Answer: Mass deposited ∝ charge passed (m ∝ Q).
