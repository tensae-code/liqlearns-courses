# Element symbols and chemical formulas — first steps — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does potassium have the symbol K?
**Answer: Its symbol comes from its Latin root, *Kalium* — the oldest-known elements keep symbols from their Latin names, as Syria's table shows.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Read S₈ in words, and say what it tells you.
**Answer: One molecule of sulfur holding eight sulfur atoms joined together — the subscript 8 counts the atoms of the symbol before it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How are ammonia, sulphur dioxide, methane and ethanol used in this lesson?
**Answer: As the first compound formulas to be read atom-by-atom — each formula read as a count of atoms, kind by kind, which is exactly what the Unit 4 questions (printed p. 164) test.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Where does the book's enrichment take the ozone writing?
**Answer: Into the upper atmosphere — ozone, O₃, met as enrichment after the subscript writings.**
