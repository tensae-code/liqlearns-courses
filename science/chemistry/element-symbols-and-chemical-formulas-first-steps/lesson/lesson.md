# Element symbols and chemical formulas — first steps

## Do it first
Before you read: chemists write water as H₂O and oxygen gas as O₂. Say out loud: what do you think the small 2 is doing in each one? Then read on: Syria's Grade 7 book teaches this written language as its own first step, on printed p. 146.

## Sub lesson 1 — Writing element molecules with subscripts

Some elements travel **alone**: helium is written **He** — one atom, no subscript. Others travel as **molecules of the same atom joined together**, and the small number written low at the right — the **subscript** — counts the atoms in one molecule: **O₂** is two oxygen atoms, **O₃** (ozone) is three, **Cl₂** is two chlorine atoms, and **S₈** is a ring of eight sulfur atoms.

The subscript belongs to the symbol just before it and counts only that atom. No subscript means one. Reading a formula always means reading each symbol together with its subscript.

## Sub lesson 2 — Element symbols and their Latin roots

Every element has a **symbol** — one or two letters, the first a capital. Many symbols come straight from the English name (H, O, C), but the oldest-known elements keep symbols from their **Latin roots**, and Syria's table gives them side by side: **Na** from *Natrium* (sodium), **K** from *Kalium* (potassium), **Fe** from *Ferrum* (iron), **Au** from *Aurum* (gold), **Hg** from *Hydrargyrum* (mercury) and **Pb** from *Plumbum* (lead).

That is why the symbol sometimes looks unrelated to the name you know: the symbol remembers the older name. The book's enrichment follows one of these writings into the sky — **ozone, O₃, in the upper atmosphere**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading compound formulas atom-by-atom

A **compound's formula** lists every kind of atom in one molecule, each with its subscript: read it atom-by-atom and it tells you the molecule's whole make-up. Syria's first set: **ammonia**, **sulphur dioxide**, **methane** and **ethanol** — each formula read aloud as a count of atoms, kind by kind.

This reading is a skill the book tests directly: the Unit 4 questions (printed p. 164) ask exactly for this symbol-and-formula reading, so the step is learned by doing it, formula after formula.

## The key ideas

- Element molecules are written with **subscripts** counting the atoms: He alone; **O₂, O₃, Cl₂, S₈** joined.
- Symbols may come from **Latin roots**: Na *Natrium*, K *Kalium*, Fe *Ferrum*, Au *Aurum*, Hg *Hydrargyrum*, Pb *Plumbum*.
- A compound's **formula is read atom-by-atom** — ammonia, sulphur dioxide, methane and ethanol are Syria's first set (U4 L2, printed p. 146; tested at printed p. 164).
