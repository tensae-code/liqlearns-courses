# Types of organic reactions — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the five families of organic reactions by result, with the bookkeeping mark of each.
**Answer: Addition — two molecules become one across a multiple bond; substitution — one atom or group swapped for another; elimination — a small molecule removed, a multiple bond formed; rearrangement — the skeleton reorders into an isomer; oxidation/reduction — oxygen gained or hydrogen lost, or the reverse, read on carbon.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How do homolytic and heterolytic fission differ in what each fragment carries away?
**Answer: Homolytic breaking is even — each fragment keeps one electron of the pair and leaves as a radical; heterolytic breaking is uneven — one fragment takes both electrons (an anion) and the other leaves electron-poor (a cation).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Distinguish nucleophile from electrophile by what each does with an electron pair.
**Answer: A nucleophile donates an electron pair, seeking electron-poor (positive) centres; an electrophile accepts an electron pair, seeking electron-rich centres such as a double bond's π cloud.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Give the full two-axis classification of (a) bromoethane + aqueous hydroxide → ethanol and (b) bromoethane + hot concentrated alcoholic alkali → ethene.
**Answer: (a) Nucleophilic substitution — substitution by result, run by a nucleophile. (b) Elimination — the small molecule HBr is removed and the double bond of ethene forms; the conditions, not the reactant, chose the family.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is this classification not the same lesson as Four types of chemical reaction?
**Answer: That lesson is the inorganic scheme — combination, decomposition and displacement bookkeeping for inorganic reactions; §59's families (addition, substitution, elimination, rearrangement, redox, sorted again by mechanism) are built for carbon's molecules — a different system for a different territory.**
