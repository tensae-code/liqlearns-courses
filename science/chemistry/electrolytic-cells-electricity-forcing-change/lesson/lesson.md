# Electrolytic cells — electricity forcing change

## Do it first
Some decompositions will never happen alone — pass a current and they are forced. Before reading, say which electrode the positive ions swim to, and what sign that electrode carries in an electrolytic cell.

[Picture 21732 — A cell with battery, electrodes, ions.]

Electrolysis is the decomposition of an electrolyte by an electric current, in an electrolytic cell driven by an external power source.

The cathode is the negative electrode: cations (positive ions) move to it and are reduced there. The anode is positive: anions move to it and are oxidised there.

Ion movement through the melt or solution and electron flow through the wires complete the circuit.
