# Lesson plan — Practical: measuring mass, volume and density

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Practical: measuring mass, volume and density
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — Unit 2 — Measurements and Scientific Methods (Grade 9 band, CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A complete 2–3 hour laboratory investigation is printed in full: mass measurement technique (zeroing, cleaning the pan, repeated weighing of a beaker on up to three balances);
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier Chemistry lessons of the Grade 9 band — matter, measurement and the atom come before bonding, reactions and carbon; a learner can still land here directly and follow it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Measuring mass with a balance** — part 1 of this lesson, in order.
2. **Measuring volume — pipet, cylinder and beaker** — part 2 of this lesson, in order.
3. **Density two ways — displacement and dimensions** — part 3 of this lesson, in order.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Unit 2 — Measurements and Scientific Methods. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, what this lesson teaches: practical: measuring mass, volume and density.
2. Explain the lesson's main example: pipet technique in step-by-step detail (TD pipets, suction device never the mouth, rinsing, filling above the line, meniscus on the line, draining with the tip…
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit (goggles, gloves, apron) and a teacher present — never home chemicals.
