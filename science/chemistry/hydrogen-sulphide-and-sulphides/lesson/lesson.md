# Hydrogen sulphide and sulphides

## Do it first
Hydrogen chloride is a gas, but dissolved in water it takes a second name — hydrochloric acid. Before reading, say out loud: if hydrogen sulphide, H₂S, does the same thing, what should its solution be called, and what family of salts should that solution make? Then read on: this is Mozambique's way in its PESD distance module (Módulo 4, 1º ciclo, Lição 9) — one substance, two names, and the salts that complete the sulphur family's story.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hydrogen sulphide — the gas

**Hydrogen sulphide, H₂S** (in the book's Portuguese: *sulfureto de hidrogénio*), is the compound of hydrogen and sulphur met as a **gas**. It belongs to sulphur's own lesson sequence in the module — after sulphur itself, its oxides and sulphuric acid — as the sulphur compound built on hydrogen instead of oxygen. Named as a gas, it is a sulphide of hydrogen: the name tells the two elements and nothing about water, because there is no water in it yet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hydrosulphuric acid — the gas in water

Dissolve hydrogen sulphide in water and the same H₂S is present as an **acid solution** — and, exactly as with hydrogen chloride and hydrochloric acid earlier in the same module, the dissolved form carries its own name: **hydrosulphuric acid** (in the book's Portuguese: *ácido sulfídrico*). The naming rule is the lesson's spine: **the gas keeps the sulphide name; the aqueous solution takes the acid name.** One substance, two states, two names — calling the solution "hydrogen sulphide" or the gas "hydrosulphuric acid" mixes the states up.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sulphides — sulphur's main salts

An acid is known by its salts, and hydrosulphuric acid's salts are the **sulphides** — the book's "main salts of sulphur" (*principais sais de enxofre*). A sulphide is built the way every salt in this course is built: a **metal takes the place of the acid's hydrogen**, and what remains of the acid is the **sulphide ion, S²⁻** — H₂S with both hydrogens given up. So sodium gives sodium sulphide, Na₂S, and calcium gives calcium sulphide, CaS — the formulas written by the same valency exchange the course teaches for every salt family. The sulphides close the lesson's triangle: gas, acid, salts — one sulphur compound in its three forms.

## The key ideas

- **H₂S as a gas** is **hydrogen sulphide** — the compound of hydrogen and sulphur.
- **H₂S dissolved in water** is **hydrosulphuric acid** — the same substance, the acid name belonging to the solution.
- The salts of hydrosulphuric acid are the **sulphides**, built on the **sulphide ion, S²⁻** — a metal in hydrogen's place.
- The gas/acid name pair repeats the module's own hydrogen chloride / hydrochloric acid pair.

## Say it to your companion
Explain hydrogen sulphide and the sulphides to your companion: the gas and its name, the solution and its different name, and how the sulphide salts are built from the acid. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the two names of H₂S — as a gas and in water — and say which name belongs to which state. (Gas: hydrogen sulphide. In water: hydrosulphuric acid.)
2. Write the formula of the sulphide ion, and say where its charge comes from. (S²⁻ — H₂S with both of its hydrogens given up, leaving the sulphur carrying the two negative charges.)
3. Build the formulas of sodium sulphide and calcium sulphide by valency exchange. (Na₂S — two Na⁺ for one S²⁻; CaS — one Ca²⁺ for one S²⁻.)
