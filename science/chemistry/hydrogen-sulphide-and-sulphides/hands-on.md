# Hands-on — Hydrogen sulphide and sulphides

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes. (Paper work only — hydrogen sulphide is a poisonous gas and is never made or handled in this lesson.)

## Two names — the card sort
Write six cards: hydrogen chloride, hydrochloric acid, hydrogen sulphide, hydrosulphuric acid, carbon dioxide, carbonic acid. Sort them into two piles — GAS names and SOLUTION names — then for each pair say the rule out loud: the gas keeps its own name; dissolved in water, it takes the acid name. Mix the cards and sort them again against the clock.

## Build the sulphides
Using valency exchange (the course's salt-building method), write the formulas of the sulphides of sodium, calcium, zinc and iron(II) from the sulphide ion S²⁻. For each one, say which hydrogen of H₂S the metal has replaced — one hydrogen's worth, or both. Check each formula by counting charges: the positives and the negatives must balance exactly.
