# Ostwald's dilution law and weak-electrolyte calculations — short answer

Answer in your own words, from the lesson.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain the dilution paradox with the law.
**Answer:** α = √(Ka/C): when C falls, Ka/C grows, so α grows. Dilution lowers the amount of acid per litre but raises the fraction of it that is ionised — both from the same square-root law.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) State the two ion-concentration formulas and when each is used.
**Answer:** [H₃O⁺] = √(Ca·Ka) for a weak acid of concentration Ca and constant Ka; [OH⁻] = √(Cb·Kb) for a weak base of concentration Cb and constant Kb — ammonia the book's base case.
