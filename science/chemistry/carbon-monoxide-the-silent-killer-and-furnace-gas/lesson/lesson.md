# Carbon monoxide — the silent killer and furnace gas

## Do it first
Before you read: the same gas that kills families in winter is hired by industry to make iron. Say out loud: what must be true of a gas for it to kill without being seen, smelled or tasted — and what different property makes it valuable in a furnace? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book teaches carbon monoxide as both, in one lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where it comes from — burning starved of oxygen

Carbon monoxide forms when **carbon burns in limited oxygen**: **2C + O₂ → 2CO**. Where oxygen is plentiful the carbon burns fully to CO₂; starve the burning — a charcoal brazier in a closed room, an engine, a furnace's depths — and the half-burned gas is the product. It is **colourless and odourless**: nothing about it warns the senses.

## Sub lesson 2 — The silent killer — haemoglobin hijacked

Its killing is a hijack: breathed in, carbon monoxide **rides the haemoglobin in place of oxygen**, occupying the blood's oxygen seats and holding them, so the body's tissues starve of oxygen while the victim breathes. The book names the human cost plainly: the **yearly winter deaths when families burn charcoal for warmth in closed rooms in cold regions** — the brazier that warms the room fills it with the gas.

## Sub lesson 3 — The furnace gas — and carbon's radioactive tracer

Industry hires the same gas for the opposite purpose: in the **blast furnace, carbon monoxide is the reducing gas** that strips iron oxide of its oxygen — **Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂**. The lesson closes with carbon's other face: natural carbon holds a trace of **radioactive ¹⁴C**, and that trace is a clock — used to **date fossils and old trees** by the radioactivity they have left.

## The key ideas

- Forms when carbon burns in **limited oxygen: 2C + O₂ → 2CO**; **colourless and odourless** — no warning to the senses.
- It kills by **riding haemoglobin in place of oxygen**; the book names the **winter charcoal-brazier deaths in closed rooms**.
- In the **blast furnace it is the reducing gas** (Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂); **¹⁴C** in natural carbon **dates fossils and old trees**.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why is carbon monoxide called a silent killer — which two properties make it silent, and what makes it a killer? (Silent because it is colourless and odourless, so nothing warns the senses. A killer because it takes oxygen's place on haemoglobin, so the blood stops delivering oxygen to the tissues.)
2. A family lights a charcoal brazier in a closed room on a cold night. Trace the whole chain from the lesson. (Charcoal burning in the closed room's limited oxygen makes CO (2C + O₂ → 2CO); the gas builds up unseen and unsmelled; breathed in, it rides the haemoglobin in place of oxygen, and the sleepers starve of oxygen — the winter deaths the book names.)
3. Write the blast-furnace equation for CO's work and say which substance is reduced. (Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. The iron(III) oxide is reduced to iron; the CO takes its oxygen and is oxidised to CO₂.)
