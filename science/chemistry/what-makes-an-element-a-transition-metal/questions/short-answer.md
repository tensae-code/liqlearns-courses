# What makes an element a transition metal — short answer

Answer in your own words, from the lesson.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Apply the definition: does mercury qualify? Show the working.
**Answer:** No. Mercury is d¹⁰ in its atom and its common ion is also d¹⁰ — complete in both witnesses the definition allows, so it fails the test, like zinc and cadmium.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the definition say 'in the atom OR in one of its ions'?
**Answer:** Because some qualifiers, like copper, have a complete d sublevel in the atom but an incomplete one in their ions; the behaviour follows the ions they actually form, so the ion clause is needed to catch them.
