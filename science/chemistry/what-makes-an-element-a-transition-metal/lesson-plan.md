## Sub lessons
1. **The definition — an incomplete d (or f) sublevel** — Egypt examines on this definition: a **transition element has an incomplete d sublevel (or f sublevel) in its atom or in one of its ions**. Either witness counts — the free atom, or any ion it actually forms. The chemistry of the family — variable oxidation states, colour, catalysis — flows from that incompleteness, so the definition is set where the behaviour starts.
2. **Who counts, who does not — coinage against zinc** — The **coinage metals Cu, Ag and Au count**: their atoms may fill the d sublevel, but their **ions are d⁹ or d⁸** — incomplete — so the definition catches them by the ion clause. **Zn, Cd and Hg do not count**: they are **d¹⁰ in the atom and in their +2 ion alike** — complete everywhere, so no witness qualifies them, whatever block they sit in.
3. **The anomalies — chromium and copper's 4s¹** — The same stability story explains the configurations Egypt flags: **Cr and Cu carry 4s¹ instead of 4s²**, because moving one electron makes the d sublevel **half-filled (Cr, 3d⁵) or completely filled (Cu, 3d¹⁰)** — and half-filled and filled sublevels are especially stable. The exception is the definition's own logic: the d sublevel's completeness is what the atom is arranging.
