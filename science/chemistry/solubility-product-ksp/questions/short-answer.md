# The solubility product — Ksp — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the solid itself left out of the Ksp expression?
**Answer: Because the solid's own amount does not change its 'concentration' — a pure solid's activity is constant; the equilibrium is written only in the dissolved ions whose concentrations actually vary.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student mixes two solutions, computes Q < Ksp, and waits for a precipitate. Correct the expectation.
**Answer: No precipitate forms: the solution is unsaturated — it could dissolve still more of the salt; precipitation begins only when Q exceeds Ksp.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Two salts have Ksp values 10⁻¹⁰ (1 : 1) and 10⁻¹¹ (AB₂). Why is 'the second is less soluble' not yet a safe conclusion?
**Answer: Ksp converts to solubility by different formulas for different ion patterns (s = √Ksp against Ksp = 4s³); the solubilities must be calculated and compared like with like before ranking.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Write Ksp for a salt AB₃, naming the ion concentrations.
**Answer: Ksp = [A³⁺][B⁻]³ — the cation concentration to the first power, the anion concentration cubed, following the coefficients in the dissolving equation.**
