# Lesson plan — The solubility product — Ksp

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The solubility product — Ksp
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the solubility product Ksp — the equilibrium constant of a sparingly soluble salt dissolving, ion-product Q against Ksp to predict precipitation, molar solubility calculated from Ksp (s = √Ksp for a 1 : 1 salt, Ksp = 4s³ for AB₂), and the common ion driving solubility down — as Greece's Grade 12 book teaches it (Ch.5.7)

## Sub lessons
1. **A sparingly soluble salt — in equilibrium** — A salt that barely dissolves still dissolves a little: in its saturated solution the solid sits in dynamic equilibrium with its ions, and the product of the ion concentrations (each raised to its coefficient) is the constant Ksp — Greece's Grade 12 chapter (Ch.5.7) gives this equilibrium its own section after the common-ion effect.
2. **Predicting precipitation — Q against Ksp** — Mix two solutions and the question is whether a precipitate forms: compute the ion product Q from the concentrations actually present — if Q > Ksp the salt precipitates until Q falls to Ksp; if Q < Ksp nothing forms, the solution is not even saturated.
3. **Solubility from Ksp — and the common ion again** — Ksp also runs backwards into solubility itself: for a 1 : 1 salt the molar solubility s satisfies Ksp = s², so s = √Ksp; for an AB₂ salt Ksp = 4s³. And a common ion already in the solution drives the solubility down — the common-ion effect, now with numbers.
