Today: **The solubility product — Ksp** — Greece's way, from its own book.

An "insoluble" salt still dissolves a little — and in its saturated solution, dissolving and re-forming balance. That balance has a constant: Ksp.

Ksp is the product of the ion concentrations, each raised to its coefficient. The solid itself is left out.

Mixing solutions? Compute Q from what is actually there. Q above Ksp — a precipitate falls. Below — nothing.

And Ksp runs backwards: from it you can calculate the solubility itself — and watch a common ion push that solubility down.
