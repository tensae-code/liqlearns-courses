# Water of crystallisation — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does blue copper(II) sulfate turn white on heating, and how is the blue restored?
**Answer: The blue belongs to the hydrated crystal, CuSO₄·5H₂O. Heating drives off the water of crystallisation, leaving white anhydrous CuSO₄; adding water rebuilds the hydrate and the blue returns.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student heats a hydrate once, weighs it, and reports the water content. What is the fault?
**Answer: One heating may leave water behind, so the loss reads low. The honest method heats to constant mass — repeating until the balance stops changing — so all the water of crystallisation is counted.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A 2.495 g sample of a hydrate leaves 1.595 g anhydrous. Give the percentage water.
**Answer: Water lost = 2.495 − 1.595 = 0.900 g. Percentage = 0.900 ÷ 2.495 × 100 ≈ 36.1% — the CuSO₄·5H₂O value.**
