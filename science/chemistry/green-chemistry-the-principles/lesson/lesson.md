# Green chemistry — the principles

## Do it first
Chemistry can design its own conscience. Before reading, draft three rules you would give a factory that wants to stop harming — then meet the book's twelve.

[Picture 21785 — A principles wheel/poster.]

Green chemistry designs products and processes that reduce or eliminate hazardous substances. Its principles, in the book's presentation, include: prevention of waste rather than clean-up;

atom economy — design reactions so most reactant atoms end in the product (the guide works this definition with examples: mass of desired product over total mass of products, as a percentage); safer solvents (the guide's dry-cleaning case uses liquid CO₂ in place of toxic organic solvents);

energy efficiency, use of catalysis, and designing products to degrade safely after use. The remaining principles complete the twelve-principle framework in the section.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Green chemistry — the principles to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
