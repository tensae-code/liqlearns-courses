# Collision theory — what speeds a reaction up — Unit 4 review

## Do it first
Powdered chalk vanishes in acid in seconds; the same mass as one lump takes minutes. Same chemicals, same temperature. Before reading, build the particle story that explains it — collision theory is exactly that story.

[Picture 21669 — Collision diagrams for each factor.]

Collision theory: reactions happen when particles collide with enough energy (at least the activation energy) and in the right orientation — an effective collision.

Higher concentration (or pressure for gases) puts more particles in the space, so collisions come more often. Higher temperature raises both collision frequency and, more importantly, the fraction of collisions with energy above the activation energy.

Greater surface area exposes more particles to collision. A catalyst speeds the reaction by providing a path of lower activation energy — it is not used up.

Unit 4 closes: every factor works by raising the rate of effective collisions.
