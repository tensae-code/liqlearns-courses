## Natural gas, crude oil and petroleum

The syllabus table for the natural-sources section (numbered 6.4 there — see the pairing verdict) states content more precisely than the student text at three points: (i) natural gas is composed mainly of methane with smaller amounts of ethane and traces of carbon dioxide and nitrogen, and is **always over 90% methane**, varying by source; (ii) the terms are to be distinguished explicitly — **crude oil** is the raw material obtained from the ground, **petroleum** is the products after refining; crude oil itself is a complex mixture of alkanes of little use as it leaves the ground;

(iii) as fractional-distillation temperature rises, successive fractions become **more coloured, more viscous and less inflammable**, with the fraction–use list given in syllabus form (refinery gases for heating/bottling; petroleum ether as solvent; gasoline as petrol fuel;

kerosene as jet fuel; diesel oil; lubricating oil;

paraffin wax for candles and polishes; residue/bitumen for roof sealing and road surfaces).
