# Carbonic acid and carbonates

## Do it first
Before you read: carbon dioxide dissolves in rain as it falls, and that faintly acid rain has been carving limestone caves for ages. Say out loud: what acid do you think carbon dioxide forms in water, and what family of salts does that acid make? Then read on: this is South Sudan's way in Secondary 2, Unit 4 §4.5 — carbonates, taught after the oxides of carbon.

## Sub lesson 1 — Carbonic acid — carbon dioxide in water

When carbon dioxide dissolves in water, part of it reacts with the water to form **carbonic acid, H₂CO₃**: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃. It is a **weak acid** — only a little of the dissolved gas becomes acid — but it is real: it is the acid in fizzy drinks, the acid of rain, and the acid that attacks limestone, dissolving it slowly as soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate. Carbonic acid is never bottled pure; it exists in its solutions, and it is dibasic — it can give up two hydrogen ions, which is why it forms two families of salts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carbonates and hydrogencarbonates — the two families

Losing one hydrogen, carbonic acid gives the **hydrogencarbonates** (HCO₃⁻); losing both, the **carbonates** (CO₃²⁻). The carbonates are the working family: calcium carbonate (limestone, chalk, marble), sodium carbonate (washing soda), and the carbonates met across the course. The hydrogencarbonates dissolve where many carbonates do not — sodium hydrogencarbonate (baking soda) and the dissolved calcium hydrogencarbonate of hard water are the everyday pair.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reactions of carbonates

Carbonates answer to a small set of reactions, and §4.5 teaches them as a set. **With acids**: every carbonate fizzes with a dilute acid, giving a salt, water and carbon dioxide — the effervescence that is the carbonate test, the gas confirmed by limewater. **With heat**: most carbonates decompose to the oxide and carbon dioxide, the reactive metals' carbonates resisting. **In water with carbon dioxide**: an insoluble carbonate like calcium carbonate dissolves as more CO₂ is passed, forming the soluble hydrogencarbonate — the reaction that carves caves and makes temporary hardness, and it reverses on heating, laying the carbonate back down as scale and stalactites.

## The key ideas

- Carbon dioxide in water forms **carbonic acid, H₂CO₃** — a weak, dibasic acid known only in solution.
- Its two salt families: **hydrogencarbonates** (HCO₃⁻) and **carbonates** (CO₃²⁻).
- Carbonates **fizz with dilute acids**, giving salt + water + CO₂ — the carbonate test.
- An insoluble carbonate dissolves in water with excess CO₂ as the soluble **hydrogencarbonate** — caves and temporary hardness — and heating reverses it.
