## Sub lessons
1. **Carbonic acid — carbon dioxide in water** — When carbon dioxide dissolves in water, part of it reacts with the water to form **carbonic acid, H₂CO₃**: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃.
2. **Carbonates and hydrogencarbonates — the two families** — Losing one hydrogen, carbonic acid gives the **hydrogencarbonates** (HCO₃⁻); losing both, the **carbonates** (CO₃²⁻).
3. **Reactions of carbonates** — Carbonates answer to a small set of reactions, and §4.5 teaches them as a set.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes the carbonate and hydrogencarbonate ions → count the hydrogens and the charge: CO₃²⁻ against HCO₃⁻. Thinks carbonic acid can be bottled → it exists only in solution; bottled, it falls back to water and carbon dioxide.
