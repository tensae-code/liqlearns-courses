# Carbonic acid and carbonates — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How does carbonic acid form, and where is it met outside the laboratory?
**Answer: Carbon dioxide dissolves in water and part reacts to form carbonic acid, CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃. It is met in fizzy drinks, in rain, and in the water that dissolves limestone.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What is the difference between a carbonate and a hydrogencarbonate?
**Answer: A carbonate carries the ion CO₃²⁻ — carbonic acid with both hydrogens given up; a hydrogencarbonate carries HCO₃⁻ — one hydrogen given up, one kept.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Describe the test that proves a powder is a carbonate.
**Answer: Add a dilute acid: the powder fizzes (effervesces), and the gas given off turns limewater milky — carbon dioxide — proving the powder was a carbonate.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does boiling temporary hard water lay down scale?
**Answer: The dissolved calcium hydrogencarbonate decomposes on heating back to insoluble calcium carbonate — the scale — with water and carbon dioxide; the cave reaction reversed.**
