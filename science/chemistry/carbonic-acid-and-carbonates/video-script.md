Today: **Carbonic acid and carbonates** — South Sudan's way: the acid rain makes, and the salts it leaves behind.
Carbon dioxide dissolves in water and forms a weak acid — carbonic acid, H₂CO₃. It makes two families of salts: hydrogencarbonates, with one hydrogen given up, and carbonates, with both given up — limestone, chalk and washing soda among them. Every carbonate fizzes with a dilute acid, giving off carbon dioxide: that fizz is the carbonate test. And carbonic acid in rain dissolves limestone as a soluble hydrogencarbonate — carving caves — until heating lays the carbonate down again as scale.
