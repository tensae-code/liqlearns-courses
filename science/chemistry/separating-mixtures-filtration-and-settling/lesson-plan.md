# Lesson plan — Separating mixtures — filtration and settling

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Separating mixtures — filtration and settling
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how the Djibouti Grade 6 guide gets a heterogeneous mixture's visible parts back apart — filtration through a filter, and décantation (letting the mixture rest, then pouring the liquid off) — carried up to the stages of a wastewater-treatment station (teacher-guide evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Mixtures — homogeneous and heterogeneous is recommended first, never forced.
**Evidence standing:** this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Djibouti) — its sources name the guide and count it nowhere (acceptance 0).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Filtration — the filter holds the solid back** — **Filtration** separates a mixture by pouring it through a **filter** — paper or cloth with holes too small for the solid grains to pass.
2. **Décantation — let it rest, then pour** — **Décantation** (settling and decanting) uses patience instead of a filter.
3. **The same methods at city size — the treatment station** — The chapter carries the pair out of the classroom to **Djibouti's wastewater-treatment station** — the place where the city's used water is cleaned before it goes back to the environment.
