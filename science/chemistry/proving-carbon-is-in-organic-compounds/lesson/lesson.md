# Proving carbon is in organic compounds

## Do it first
Before you read: toast left too long turns black; sugar heated in a spoon turns black too. Say out loud: what is the black stuff, and why would two such different foods leave the same black end? Then read on: Syria's Grade 9 book opens organic chemistry with exactly this proof, on printed p. 148.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two heatings, one black end — bread and sugar

The book heats two unlike organic substances: **a piece of bread burned** and **a spoonful of sugar heated**. Different foods, different tastes, different make-up in every way but one — **both end as black carbon**.

Charring is the visible proof: drive everything else out of an organic substance with heat, and what stays behind, black and unmistakable, is its carbon. The blackening of toast is this experiment happening by accident.

## Sub lesson 2 — The element common to all organic substances

From the two heatings the book draws its conclusion (printed p. 149): **the element common to all organic substances is carbon**. Bread has it, sugar has it — and the claim reaches to every organic substance: no carbon, not organic.

This is what makes the proof a founding one: it does not describe one compound; it fixes the **membership rule of a whole branch of chemistry** — a substance belongs to organic chemistry by its carbon.

## Sub lesson 3 — Organic chemistry — the branch that studies carbon's compounds

The lesson closes by naming the branch: **organic chemistry is the branch that studies carbon's compounds**. The name is historical — these substances first came from living things — but the definition the book teaches is the carbon rule proved by the heatings.

Everything in the unit that follows — hydrocarbons, alkanes, the unsaturated families — is carbon's compounds, met one family at a time under the rule this proof sets.

## The key ideas

- **Bread burned and sugar heated both end as black carbon** (U5 L1, printed p. 148).
- Conclusion (printed p. 149): **the element common to all organic substances is carbon**.
- **Organic chemistry is the branch that studies carbon's compounds.**
