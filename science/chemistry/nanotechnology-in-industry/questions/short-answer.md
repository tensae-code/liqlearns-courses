# Nanotechnology in industry — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How big is a nanometre, and what is a nanoparticle?
**Answer: A nanometre is a billionth of a metre (10⁻⁹ m) — thousands of times thinner than a hair. A nanoparticle is a particle only a few nanometres across: a cluster of atoms or molecules at that scale.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why can the same substance behave differently at the nanoscale?
**Answer: Divided to nanometre size, its surface area compared with its volume becomes enormous — nearly every atom sits at a surface, ready to react, stick, absorb or catalyse. Reactivity and other properties can therefore change, though the substance itself is the same.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What does industry actually buy when it buys nanotechnology, in one sentence?
**Answer: Surface: more reaction, absorption or catalysis per gram, coatings that work in films a few atoms thick, and particles small enough to carry active ingredients where bulk material cannot go.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name three industries from Malaysia's applications section and the nano job in each.
**Answer: Any three of: electronics (nanoscale parts make devices smaller and faster); medicine and cosmetics (nanoparticles carry and release active ingredients; nano sunscreens screen skin); materials and coatings (nano-films add strength, protection and water-shedding); catalysis and water treatment (the huge nanoparticle surface does the work).**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can a technology defined by size appear in every industry at once?
**Answer: Because it is not tied to one substance or one product: any industry that uses materials can use them at nanometre scale, so the same size-regime shows up in electronics, medicine, coatings and treatment processes alike — which is why Malaysia closes its consumer chapter with it.**
