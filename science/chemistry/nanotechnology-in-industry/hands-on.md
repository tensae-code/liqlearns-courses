# Hands-on — Nanotechnology in industry

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Smaller pieces, faster work (home materials — sugar lumps or cubes, sugar powder, two glasses of water)
Drop a sugar lump into one glass of room-temperature water and the same mass of powdered sugar into the other, without stirring either. Time how long each takes to disappear and write down the difference. Say it in lesson terms: dividing the same substance into smaller pieces exposes far more surface to the water — the classroom-scale shadow of why nanoparticles, with their enormous surface for their volume, react and dissolve so differently. (Observation only — do not drink the experiment water.)

## The nano hunt (observation only — home and shops)
Find three products or labels that name nano, nanoparticles or a nano-coating — sunscreen, cosmetics, electronics, treated fabrics. For each, write the job claimed and whether the nanoscale is doing surface work, delivery work or size work, as Malaysia's applications section sorts industry uses.

## Which scale does the job need? (paper exercise)
List six jobs: dissolving a drink powder fast, coating a phone screen thinly, carrying a medicine ingredient to one place, filtering water, making a tiny electronic part, sweetening tea slowly. Sort them into two columns — ordinary scale serves / the nanoscale is the point — and defend each placing in one line.
