# Lesson plan — Nanotechnology in industry

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Nanotechnology in industry
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** nanotechnology — substances and devices built at the nanometre scale, where a material's huge surface for its volume changes what it can do — met the way Malaysia's Form 5 book meets it: as an applications section of its consumer and industrial chemistry chapter

## Sub lessons
1. **What 'nano' means — the scale** — **Nanotechnology** works at the **nanometre** scale: one nanometre is a billionth of a metre (10⁻⁹ m), and nanoparticles are particles only a few nanometres across — clusters of atoms or molecules, far too small for any ordinary microscope of the classroom, thousands of times thinner than a hair.
2. **Why small changes what a substance can do** — Divide a lump into nanoparticles and its **surface area compared with its volume** becomes enormous: nearly every atom is at a surface, ready to react, stick, absorb or catalyse.
3. **Nanotechnology in industry — where Malaysia points** — Malaysia's section is titled an **application** section, and its point is the spread: nanotechnology at work **across industry** — in electronics, where nanoscale parts make devices smaller and faster; in medicine and cosmetics, where nanoparticles carry and release active ingredients and screen skin from the sun; in materials and coatings, where nano-films add strength, protection and water-shedding to ordinary surfaces; and in processes such as catalysis and water treatment, where the huge surface of nanoparticles does the work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the one element: nanotechnology is chemistry at a new size — at the nanometre scale the surface-for-volume becomes enormous, and that changes what the same substance can do.
- Thinks nanotechnology is a new substance → there is no nano element: the substances are ordinary ones, divided or built at nanometre size; the novelty is the scale and the properties the scale brings.
- Cannot feel the scale → anchor it: a nanometre is a billionth of a metre; a hair is tens of thousands of nanometres thick — a nanoparticle is to a hair roughly what a marble is to a stadium.
