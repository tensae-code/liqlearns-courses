Industry wants equilibrium far to the product side AND reached fast — but the conditions for one fight the other.

Le Chatelier's principle is applied to choose industrial conditions: pressure, temperature and recycling of unreacted gases.

For ammonia-type syntheses (the Haber-type case), a high pressure favours product, while the temperature is a compromise — high enough for a workable rate, low enough not to destroy the yield — with a catalyst to speed the approach.

Unit 5 closes with its summary and review: equilibrium is managed, never merely observed, in industry.

Now you. Say it back: Why is industrial temperature a compromise?
Answer: Rate needs heat; yield needs cool.
