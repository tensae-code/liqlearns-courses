# Carbon dioxide — properties, preparation and tests

## Do it first
Before you read: blow gently across the top of a candle flame and it flickers; the gas in your breath is part of why. Say out loud: what do you already know about carbon dioxide — where it comes from, where it goes, and how anyone could prove a jar of invisible gas is full of it? Then read on: Greece's Grade 8 book gives this one gas a section of its own, Unit 3.3.

## Sub lesson 1 — The properties of carbon dioxide

Carbon dioxide is a **colourless, odourless** gas with two properties that run its whole chemistry. First, it is **denser than air**: it sinks, can be poured downward from one vessel into another, and pools in cellars and wells. Second, it **supports neither combustion nor ordinary respiration** — a flame lowered into it goes out, which is both its danger and its use.

It is **slightly soluble in water** — under pressure, enough dissolves to make fizzy drinks — and its solution is faintly acidic. Greece's Grade 8 book gives the gas its own section (Unit 3.3), straight after oxygen, so the two gases of burning and breathing stand side by side.

## Sub lesson 2 — Preparing it in the laboratory

In the laboratory, carbon dioxide is made the easy way: **marble chips — calcium carbonate — with dilute hydrochloric acid**. The mixture fizzes steadily, at room temperature, with no heating:

**CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂**

Because the gas is **denser than air**, it is collected by **downward delivery** — upward displacement of air — the jar filling from the bottom as the heavier gas pushes the air out; collecting over water would lose some gas to dissolving. Greece's Grade 9 laboratory guide runs this exact preparation as its **9th laboratory exercise**, so the Greek pupil meets the gas first in the book and then in the hand.

## Sub lesson 3 — The limewater test — and the gas at work

Carbon dioxide has a signature test no other common gas shares: bubbled through clear **limewater**, it turns the limewater **milky** — a white cloud of calcium carbonate forming in the liquid. That milkiness is the proof of the gas, in the book's section and in the laboratory guide's practical alike.

Its uses spend its properties one by one: **fire extinguishers** and fire blankets of gas, because it smothers; **fizzy drinks**, because it dissolves under pressure; **dry ice**, its solid, because it sublimes away cold and clean. And over the whole planet the gas keeps a balance: **photosynthesis** draws it out of the air into living matter, **respiration and burning** return it — the balance Greece's section closes on, with the **greenhouse effect** as what happens when the returns outgrow the drawing-down.

## The key ideas

- Carbon dioxide is **colourless and odourless**, **denser than air**, **slightly soluble** in water, and supports **neither combustion nor respiration**.
- Laboratory preparation: **marble chips + dilute hydrochloric acid** — CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ — collected by **downward delivery**.
- The test: carbon dioxide turns **limewater milky**.
- Uses spend the properties — **extinguishers, fizzy drinks, dry ice** — and in nature **photosynthesis** draws the gas down while **respiration and burning** return it; the imbalance is the **greenhouse** link.
