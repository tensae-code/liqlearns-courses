# The rate law — and reaction mechanism — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can the orders not simply be read off the balanced equation?
**Answer: Because the equation shows only the overall change, not the steps by which it happens; the order in each reactant is a fact about the mechanism's slow step, and only initial-rates experiments reveal it.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) State the two tests a proposed mechanism must pass.
**Answer: Its elementary steps must sum to the overall equation, and its rate-determining step must predict the experimental rate law.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A reaction's law is rate = k[X]². Give its order in X and its overall order.
**Answer: Second order in X, and second order overall — the overall order is the sum of the powers, here just the one power.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) An intermediate appears in a mechanism but not in the overall equation. How can that be?
**Answer: It is produced in one step and consumed in a later one, so when the steps are added it cancels — it exists during the reaction but is neither a reactant nor a product overall.**
