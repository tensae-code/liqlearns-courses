# Lesson plan — The rate law — and reaction mechanism

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The rate law — and reaction mechanism
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the rate law (rate = k[A]^m[B]^n) with orders found from initial-rates experiments rather than from coefficients, the overall order and the temperature-carrying constant k, and reaction mechanism — elementary steps, intermediates and the rate-determining step tested against the experimental law — as Greece's Grade 12 book teaches them (Ch.3.3, with the worked experiment of §3.4)

## Sub lessons
1. **The rate law — found from experiment** — The rate law links rate to concentrations: rate = k[A]^m[B]^n, where the powers m and n — the orders — are found from initial-rates experiments (change one concentration at a time and watch the rate), never simply read off the equation's coefficients.
2. **Order, and the constant k** — The overall order is the sum of the powers. The constant k gathers everything else — temperature above all — into one number for given conditions: raise the temperature and k itself grows, which is the rate law's way of carrying the temperature effect.
3. **Mechanism — the steps behind the law** — Most reactions do not happen in one collision: a mechanism is the proposed sequence of elementary steps, whose slowest — the rate-determining step — sets the rate law. A mechanism is accepted only if its steps sum to the overall equation and its slow step predicts the experimental law.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Reads the orders off the balanced equation → return to the initial-rates table: the orders are what the data does, never what the coefficients say.
