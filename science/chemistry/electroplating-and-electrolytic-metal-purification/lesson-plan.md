# Lesson plan — Electroplating and electrolytic metal purification

## Sub lessons
1. **Electroplating — the object as cathode** — To plate an object, make it the **cathode**: Egypt's case is a **silver-plated jug** — the jug as cathode, a **silver anode**, and **silver nitrate as the electrolyte**. Silver ions from the solution plate onto the jug as metal, while the anode dissolves to replace them, so the bath's concentration holds steady. The same art lays **chromium on car parts and taps** — beauty and rust-proofing in one skin.
2. **Copper purification — the impure anode dissolves** — For purification the arrangement turns around: the **impure copper is the anode** and dissolves; **pure copper plates onto the cathode at 99.95% purity**. The impurities split by their nature — the trick the whole method stands on.
3. **Where the impurities go — solution and anode mud** — **Iron and zinc impurities stay in solution** — they dissolve but do not plate out under the cell's conditions. **Silver and gold**, too unreactive to dissolve, **fall as anode mud** beneath the anode — and are **recovered** in their own right. Nothing is wasted: the cathode takes the pure copper, the solution keeps the base metals, and the mud pays for the process in precious metal.
