# Electroplating and electrolytic metal purification

## Do it first
Before you read: a cheap metal jug comes out of a bath wearing a skin of real silver, and a lump of dirty copper goes into another bath and comes out 99.95% pure. Say out loud: what single machine does both jobs — and which electrode does the object have to be? Then read on: Egypt's electrochemistry chapter puts electrolysis to work twice.

## Sub lesson 1 — Electroplating — the object as cathode

To plate an object, make it the **cathode**: Egypt's case is a **silver-plated jug** — the jug as cathode, a **silver anode**, and **silver nitrate as the electrolyte**. Silver ions from the solution plate onto the jug as metal, while the anode dissolves to replace them, so the bath's concentration holds steady. The same art lays **chromium on car parts and taps** — beauty and rust-proofing in one skin.

## Sub lesson 2 — Copper purification — the impure anode dissolves

For purification the arrangement turns around: the **impure copper is the anode** and dissolves; **pure copper plates onto the cathode at 99.95% purity**. The impurities split by their nature — the trick the whole method stands on.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where the impurities go — solution and anode mud

**Iron and zinc impurities stay in solution** — they dissolve but do not plate out under the cell's conditions. **Silver and gold**, too unreactive to dissolve, **fall as anode mud** beneath the anode — and are **recovered** in their own right. Nothing is wasted: the cathode takes the pure copper, the solution keeps the base metals, and the mud pays for the process in precious metal.

## The key ideas

- **Electroplating**: object as **cathode**, coating metal as **anode**, its salt as electrolyte — the silver jug (AgNO₃ bath); chromium on car parts and taps.
- **Copper purification**: impure copper anode dissolves; **99.95% pure copper** plates the cathode.
- Impurities split: **Fe/Zn stay in solution**; **Ag/Au fall as anode mud** and are recovered.
