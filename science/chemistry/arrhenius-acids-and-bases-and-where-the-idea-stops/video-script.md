# Video script — Arrhenius acids and bases — and where the idea stops

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Lemon juice, vinegar and drain cleaner open this unit in the book's start-up activity.

Arrhenius: an acid is a substance that gives H⁺ ions in aqueous solution; a base gives OH⁻ ions in aqueous solution.

The idea's limits: it works only in water; it cannot explain basic substances that contain no OH⁻, like ammonia.

And a free H⁺ does not exist alone in water — it rides on a water molecule as H₃O⁺.

Now you. Say it back: What does an Arrhenius acid give in water?
Answer: H⁺ ions.
