# Thermodynamics — spontaneity, entropy and free energy — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A learner says a spontaneous reaction must be a fast one. Correct them, with an example.
**Answer: Spontaneous means the process runs one way by itself under the given conditions; it says nothing about speed. Rusting is spontaneous and takes years.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A gas expands into an evacuated flask. What happens to its entropy, and why?
**Answer: Its entropy increases, because the particles spread over a larger volume and can take far more arrangements — matter is more dispersed.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Water freezing lowers the entropy of the water. How can freezing still be spontaneous below 0 °C?
**Answer: The second law prices the universe, not the system alone: freezing releases heat that disperses into the cold surroundings, raising their entropy by more than the water's entropy falls, so ΔS_univ > 0.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the four sign cases of ΔH and ΔS and which are decided by temperature.
**Answer: ΔH < 0 with ΔS > 0 is always spontaneous; ΔH > 0 with ΔS < 0 never is. When the signs agree the other way — ΔH < 0, ΔS < 0 (low T only) or ΔH > 0, ΔS > 0 (high T only) — temperature decides.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What two standard-condition quantities does ΔG° = −RT ln K connect, and what does a large negative ΔG° promise?
**Answer: The standard Gibbs free-energy change and the equilibrium constant K. A large negative ΔG° means a large K — the equilibrium lies far toward the products.**
