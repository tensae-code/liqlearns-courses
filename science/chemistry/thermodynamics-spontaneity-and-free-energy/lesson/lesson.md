# Thermodynamics — spontaneity, entropy and free energy

## Sub lesson 1 — Spontaneous and nonspontaneous — which way a process goes by itself

A **spontaneous process** is one that, under the conditions given, runs in a particular direction **by itself**, without needing a continuous input of work from outside. Ice melting above 0 °C is spontaneous. Rusting of iron in air is spontaneous. Heat flowing from a hot body to a cold one is spontaneous. The **reverse** of each — water freezing on a warm plate, rust turning back to bright iron by itself, heat climbing from cold to hot — is **nonspontaneous** under those same conditions: it can only happen if work is done on the system, the way a refrigerator's motor works to move heat from its cold inside to the warm room.

Two cautions belong with the word. First, **spontaneous does not mean fast**: rusting is spontaneous and takes years; a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen sits unchanged for years even though its reaction to water is spontaneous — kinetics sets the speed, thermodynamics only settles the direction. Second, **spontaneity depends on the conditions**, above all temperature: water freezing is spontaneous below 0 °C and nonspontaneous above it. The same process flips its answer when the conditions change.

## Sub lesson 2 — Entropy — dispersal of matter and energy

What pushes processes one way? **Entropy**, written **S**. Entropy measures **dispersal** — how spread out a system's matter and energy are, counted as the number of arrangements (called **microstates**) its particles and their energy can take. The more ways the same matter and energy can be arranged, the higher the entropy.

Entropy grows wherever dispersal grows. A gas expanding into a vacuum spreads its particles over more arrangements, so ΔS is positive. A solid dissolving spreads its particles through a solvent. Melting frees a crystal's fixed particles into a liquid's looser ones; boiling frees them far more, so entropy rises solid < liquid < gas, and rises with temperature. Heat itself disperses too: when a warm body cools, its energy spreads into the surroundings.

The **second law of thermodynamics** prices every process with the universe as the account: **ΔS_univ = ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr, and for every spontaneous process ΔS_univ > 0**. A process may lower the entropy of the system itself — water freezing tidies its molecules into a crystal — but only when the heat it releases raises the surroundings' entropy by more. Nothing spontaneous ever lowers the entropy of the universe.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gibbs free energy — one number that decides

Checking two entropies — the system's and the surroundings' — for every process is slow work. **Gibbs free energy**, **G**, gathers the whole test into one number for the system alone:

**ΔG = ΔH − TΔS**

where ΔH is the enthalpy change, ΔS the system's entropy change, and T the temperature in kelvin. The sign of ΔG is the verdict at that temperature: **ΔG < 0 — the forward process is spontaneous; ΔG > 0 — the forward process is nonspontaneous (its reverse is spontaneous); ΔG = 0 — the system is at equilibrium**, with no net drive either way.

Read the four sign cases, because they are the lesson in one table. **ΔH < 0 and ΔS > 0** (exothermic, dispersal growing): ΔG is always negative — spontaneous at every temperature. **ΔH > 0 and ΔS < 0** (endothermic, dispersal shrinking): ΔG is always positive — never spontaneous forward. **ΔH < 0 and ΔS < 0**: spontaneous only while T is low enough that TΔS stays smaller than ΔH — this is water freezing, which is why cold decides it. **ΔH > 0 and ΔS > 0**: spontaneous only when T is high enough for TΔS to outweigh ΔH — this is ice melting, which is why warmth decides it. Temperature is the referee exactly when enthalpy and entropy pull opposite ways.

One link closes the chapter: under standard conditions the same ΔG° fixes the equilibrium constant, **ΔG° = −RT ln K** — a large negative ΔG° means a large K, products strongly favoured. Thermodynamics, equilibrium and spontaneity are one account kept in three columns.

## The key ideas

- A **spontaneous** process runs one way by itself under the given conditions; its reverse is **nonspontaneous** there and needs work done on the system. Spontaneous says nothing about **speed**.
- **Entropy (S)** measures the **dispersal** of matter and energy — the number of arrangements (microstates) available; it rises from solid to liquid to gas and with temperature.
- The **second law**: every spontaneous process increases the entropy of the **universe** — ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr > 0.
- **Gibbs free energy: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.** Negative ΔG means spontaneous forward; positive means nonspontaneous forward; zero means equilibrium.
- When ΔH and ΔS pull opposite ways, **temperature decides**; when they agree, the verdict holds at every temperature. ΔG° = −RT ln K ties free energy to equilibrium.
