# Hands-on — Thermodynamics — spontaneity, entropy and free energy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Watch dispersal happen
Half-fill a clear glass with cold water and add one drop of food colouring without stirring. Watch for five minutes, noting the time: the colour spreads by itself until the whole glass is evenly tinted — matter dispersing, entropy rising, with nobody stirring. Say why the reverse — the colour gathering itself back into one drop — never happens in the unstirred glass.

## Run the sign table on paper
Write the four sign cases for ΔH and ΔS on four cards. For each card, work ΔG = ΔH − TΔS twice — once with a small T, once with a large T — using simple made-up numbers, and write the verdict (always spontaneous / never / low-T only / high-T only) on the back. Then match each card to a real process from the lesson: rusting, melting, freezing, a gas expanding.

## Name the referee
Ice melts spontaneously above 0 °C and water freezes spontaneously below it, though it is the same pair of processes. In two sentences, say what flips the verdict at 0 °C, naming ΔH, ΔS and T for one of the two directions.
