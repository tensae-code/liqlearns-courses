## Sub lessons
1. **Spontaneous and nonspontaneous — which way a process goes by itself** — A spontaneous process runs in one direction by itself, under the conditions given, without a continuous push of work from outside — ice melting above 0 °C, iron rusting, heat flowing from hot to cold. Its reverse is nonspontaneous under the same conditions and can only be driven by doing work on the system. Spontaneous says nothing about speed: rusting is spontaneous and slow.
2. **Entropy — dispersal of matter and energy** — Entropy, S, measures how widely a system's matter and energy are dispersed — how many arrangements (microstates) the particles and their energy can take. Dispersal grows, entropy grows: a gas spreading through a room, a solid dissolving, a warm body cooling into its surroundings. The second law: in every spontaneous process the entropy of the universe increases (ΔS_univ = ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr > 0). Standard entropies grow with temperature and from solid to liquid to gas.
3. **Gibbs free energy — one number that decides** — Gibbs free energy gathers enthalpy, entropy and temperature into one test for the system alone: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. ΔG < 0: spontaneous in the forward direction. ΔG > 0: nonspontaneous forward (spontaneous in reverse). ΔG = 0: at equilibrium. The sign cases: exothermic with entropy increasing is always spontaneous; endothermic with entropy decreasing never is; when ΔH and ΔS share a sign, temperature decides. Under standard conditions ΔG° also fixes the equilibrium constant, ΔG° = −RT ln K.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Calls every endothermic process nonspontaneous → work the ΔG sign table case by case: an entropy increase can pay for an enthalpy increase when TΔS is large enough.
