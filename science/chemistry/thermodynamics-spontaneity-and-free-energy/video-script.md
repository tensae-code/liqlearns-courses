Today: **Thermodynamics — spontaneity, entropy and free energy** — the United States' way: OpenStax gives thermodynamics a chapter of its own.

Some changes run by themselves. Ice melts on a warm plate. A gas spreads through a room. Heat runs from hot to cold, never the other way alone. Those are spontaneous — and spontaneous does not mean fast. Rust is spontaneous, and rust is slow.
Why do they run that way? Entropy. Entropy counts how spread out matter and energy are. Spreading raises entropy, and every spontaneous change raises the entropy of the whole universe — the system plus everything around it.
And here is the one number that decides: delta G equals delta H minus T delta S. Negative delta G — the change runs forward by itself. Positive — it will not. Zero — the system sits at equilibrium. When heat and entropy pull opposite ways, temperature is the referee: cold lets water freeze, warmth makes ice melt.
