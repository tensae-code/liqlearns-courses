## Why atoms bond

Only valence electrons take part in bonding. Most atoms are unstable alone; a chemical bond is the attractive force holding atoms together in molecules/compounds, and Lewis's octet rule explains the drive: atoms form compounds so as to gain eight valence electrons — a noble-gas configuration (hydrogen and helium need only two;

noble gases, already octet-complete, are inert). Three routes satisfy the rule: lose valence electrons, gain them, or share them — metals typically lose (sodium 2,8,1 → 2,8), nonmetals gain or share (oxygen 2,6 → 2,8).
