# Le Chatelier's principle

## Do it first
Squeeze an equilibrium — add reactant, crush the volume, heat it — and it shifts so as to fight your change. Before reading, say what a catalyst does to the position. (Trick question: be careful.)

[Picture 21679 — A balance tipping, then re-levelling.]

Le Chatelier's principle: when a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts in the direction that partly counteracts the change.

Adding a reactant shifts the equilibrium toward products; removing one shifts it back. Increasing pressure (smaller volume) shifts it toward the side with fewer gas moles; temperature shifts favour the endothermic direction when heated (treated as adding a 'reactant' on that side).

A catalyst does NOT shift the position of equilibrium — it speeds both directions equally, so equilibrium is reached sooner at the same position.
