## How this Chemistry course works

The Grade 9 course runs: Unit 1 Chemistry and its Importance, Unit 2 Measurements and Scientific Methods, Unit 3 Structure of the Atom, Unit 4 Periodic Classification of Elements, Unit 5 Chemical Bonding. Every unit opens with printed Unit Outcomes and a Start-up Activity rooted in local life (what are air, water, cloth and food made of; indigenous market measures;

a Nehassie 2013 E.C. calendar used to teach periodicity; Ethiopian cultural-music octaves for Newlands' law), then numbered sections each fronted by group Activities and closed by Exercises, worked Examples with named Ethiopian pupils (e.g.

Chaltu identifying an unknown liquid by density), full Experiments with materials/procedure/observation questions, Key Terms, a Unit Summary, and a Review Exercise in three tiers — Basic, Intermediary and Challenge level. Ethiopian content is structural, not decorative: chemical industries are listed factory by factory (Ziway Caustic Soda, Abay/Abijata Soda Ash, Repi Soap, Adami Tulu Pesticide, cement, sugar and tyre plants), the GERD illustrates construction chemistry, and traditional market measurement opens the measurement unit. This start-from-home, group-discuss, experiment, three-level-review pattern is Ethiopia's own way of teaching chemistry at this grade and must be preserved in the build.
