# Diazonium salts — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Write the diazotisation equation for aniline and state the temperature range and why it is imposed.
**Answer: C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O, run at 273–278 K because the diazonium salt is stable only in the cold; warmer, it decomposes (in water, to phenol and nitrogen).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the reagent that replaces the diazo group by each of: Br, CN, H.
**Answer: Br — CuBr/HBr (Sandmeyer); CN — CuCN/KCN (Sandmeyer); H — hypophosphorous acid, H₃PO₂ (or ethanol), reducing the salt back to the hydrocarbon.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why are diazonium salts called important in the synthesis of aromatic compounds?
**Answer: Because the diazo group is a universal doorway: installed from the easily made amine, it can be swapped for groups — CN, I, F among them — that cannot be put onto a benzene ring by direct substitution, and it can also be kept for coupling into azo dyes.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What structural feature makes the coupling products dyes, and which two conditions-pairs give the orange and the yellow?
**Answer: The –N=N– (azo) link joining two aromatic rings is a chromophore that absorbs visible light. Phenol in mild alkali couples to orange p-hydroxyazobenzene; aniline in mild acid couples to yellow p-aminoazobenzene.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must a diazonium salt never be stored dry?
**Answer: Dry diazonium salts are unstable and can decompose explosively on shock or warming; the salt is therefore prepared in cold aqueous solution and used immediately, in solution.**
