# Diazonium salts

## Do it first
Before you read: aniline is one benzene ring carrying one –NH₂ group. From that single handle, chemists reach chlorobenzene, bromobenzene, benzonitrile, iodobenzene, phenol and a shelf of dyes — most of which cannot be made by attacking benzene directly. Say out loud: what sort of temporary group could sit on the ring, leave cleanly as a gas, and let a procession of other groups take its place? Then read on: India's NCERT Class XII book gives the amines unit's second half (§§13.7–13.10) to exactly that group — the **diazonium salts**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diazotisation — made in the cold

An **arenediazonium salt** carries the group **–N₂⁺** on an aromatic ring. It is made by **diazotisation**: a **primary aromatic amine** — aniline is the type case — is treated with **nitrous acid** (made in the flask from **NaNO₂ and dilute HCl**) at **273–278 K**:

**C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O** (at 273–278 K)

The product, **benzenediazonium chloride**, is a colourless crystalline salt when isolated, but it is **used in solution, freshly made, in the cold**: it is stable only at low temperature, decomposes in warm water (to phenol and nitrogen), and is explosive when dry and shocked. The ring is what saves it at all — the positive charge is steadied by resonance with the ring's π system, so **aryl** diazonium salts live long enough to use, while **alkyl** diazonium salts, with no ring to steady them, lose nitrogen immediately and give the alcohol. Diazotisation is therefore the aromatic amines' own reaction — the door out of the amines unit and into everything after.

## Sub lesson 2 — Swapping the diazo group — the replacement family

The diazo group's genius is its leaving: it departs as **nitrogen gas**, the most contented of leaving groups, and another group takes the seat. NCERT's replacement family:

- **Chlorine and bromine** — the **Sandmeyer reactions**: warm the salt with **CuCl/HCl** for chlorobenzene, **CuBr/HBr** for bromobenzene. Using copper powder and the acid instead is the **Gattermann** variant.
- **Cyanide** — **CuCN/KCN** gives **benzonitrile** — the doorway to carboxylic acids and amides by hydrolysis, reaching products direct substitution cannot.
- **Iodine** — simply warming with **KI** gives iodobenzene (no catalyst needed; iodine will not enter benzene directly at all).
- **Fluorine** — the salt is precipitated as its **fluoroborate** (HBF₄) and the dry salt heated: the **Balz–Schiemann reaction** gives fluorobenzene.
- **Hydrogen** — **hypophosphorous acid** (H₃PO₂) or ethanol reduces the salt back to the plain hydrocarbon, benzene — the way an amino group, used to direct other substitutions, is finally erased.
- **Hydroxyl** — warming the salt **in water** gives **phenol**, nitrogen evolving.
- **Nitro** — heating with copper powder in sodium nitrite solution replaces the group with **NO₂**.

Read the list as the unit intends: groups that direct substitution **cannot put on a ring** — CN, I, F among them — enter it through the diazonium doorway. That is the salts' **importance in the synthesis of aromatic compounds**, §13.10's verdict.

## Sub lesson 3 — Coupling — the azo dyes

Keep the diazo group instead of swapping it, and the salt does its second trick. The diazonium ion is a weak **electrophile**, and on strongly **activated rings** it substitutes: this is **coupling**. With **phenol** in **mildly alkaline** solution it couples at the **para** position (ortho if para is blocked), joining the rings through an **–N=N–** bridge to give **p-hydroxyazobenzene** — an **orange dye**. With **aniline** in **mildly acidic** solution it gives **p-aminoazobenzene** — a **yellow dye**.

The –N=N– (azo) link between two aromatic systems is a chromophore — it drinks visible light — so the products are deeply coloured, and the same reaction, run across a library of diazonium salts and coupling partners, built the **azo dyes**: the largest class of synthetic dyes, colouring textiles, leathers and foods' packaging. NCERT closes the amines unit here deliberately: the amine that began the unit as a base ends it as the ancestor of both the substituted benzenes and the dyes — through a salt that exists only in a cold flask, for an hour, on its way somewhere else.

## The key ideas

- **Diazotisation**: aniline + NaNO₂/HCl at **273–278 K** → **benzenediazonium chloride**. Aryl diazonium salts are resonance-steadied and usable cold; alkyl ones decompose at once. The salts are used fresh, in solution, never stored dry.
- The –N₂⁺ group leaves as **N₂** and is replaced: **Cl/Br/CN** by **Sandmeyer** (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN; Gattermann with Cu powder), **I** by KI, **F** by the fluoroborate route (Balz–Schiemann), **H** by H₃PO₂, **OH** by warm water (phenol), **NO₂** with Cu/NaNO₂.
- **Coupling**: with phenol (mild alkali) → **p-hydroxyazobenzene** (orange); with aniline (mild acid) → **p-aminoazobenzene** (yellow). The **–N=N– azo link** is the chromophore of the azo dyes.

## Say it to your companion
Explain diazonium salts to your companion in your own words: write the diazotisation with its temperature, say why the aryl salt lives and the alkyl salt does not, walk the replacement family naming each reagent, and finish with coupling — the two partners, the two conditions, and the link that makes the colour.

## Do It Yourself
1. Plan the synthesis of benzonitrile from aniline, naming both steps and conditions. (Diazotise: NaNO₂/dilute HCl at 273–278 K; then Sandmeyer cyanation: warm with CuCN/KCN — the –N₂⁺ leaves as N₂ and –CN takes its place.)
2. A chemist needs iodobenzene. Why does she go through aniline rather than treating benzene with iodine? (Iodine does not substitute benzene directly under workable conditions; the diazonium route — aniline → diazonium salt → warm with KI — puts iodine on the ring cleanly.)
3. The diazotisation flask is left in warm water while the chemist answers the door. What is found on return, and what gas filled the flask's headspace? (The salt has decomposed in the warm water to phenol; the gas is nitrogen, evolved as the diazo group left.)
