Today: **Diazonium salts** — India's way: the second half of the Class XII amines unit — the cold-made salts that turn one aromatic amine into a shelf of substituted benzenes, and into dyes.

Dissolve aniline in cold dilute acid, add sodium nitrite, keep everything at 273 to 278 K, and the amino group becomes a diazonium group: benzenediazonium chloride, Ar–N₂⁺ Cl⁻. The salt lives only in that cold solution — warm it and nitrogen bubbles away. Alkyl amines cannot even do this; their diazonium salts fall apart at once, which is why the aromatic ring's steadiness matters.
What the cold salt offers is a doorway. Stir it with cuprous chloride and the diazo group leaves as nitrogen, chlorine taking its place — Sandmeyer's reaction. Bromide, cyanide likewise; iodide needs only KI; fluoride comes through the fluoroborate; water gives phenol; a reducing agent gives back plain benzene. And let the salt meet phenol in mild alkali and it couples, ring to ring through –N=N–, into an orange azo dye. One amine in, a whole shelf of benzenes — and the dye industry — out.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
