# Lesson plan — Diazonium salts

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Diazonium salts
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the diazonium salts as NCERT's Class XII amines unit teaches them (§§13.7–13.10): arenediazonium salts made in the cold by diazotisation of a primary aromatic amine with nitrous acid at 273–278 K, stable only cold and only for the aryl family — and their unmatched value as a synthetic doorway, the –N₂⁺ group replaceable by Cl, Br, CN, I, F, H, OH and NO₂ (Sandmeyer at the head), and coupling with phenols and anilines to give the azo dyes

## Sub lessons
1. **Diazotisation — made in the cold** — A primary aromatic amine (aniline) treated with nitrous acid — NaNO₂ and dilute HCl, generated in situ — at 273–278 K gives the arenediazonium salt, benzenediazonium chloride, the –NH₂ converted to –N₂⁺. The aryl salt is steadied by the ring and usable in cold solution; alkyl diazonium salts, lacking that steadiness, decompose at once to the alcohol and nitrogen.
2. **Swapping the diazo group — the replacement family** — The –N₂⁺ group leaves as N₂ and a procession of groups takes its seat: Cl, Br and CN by the Sandmeyer reactions (CuCl/HCl, CuBr/HBr, CuCN/KCN; copper powder gives the Gattermann variant); I by warming with KI; F through the diazonium fluoroborate (Balz–Schiemann); H by hypophosphorous acid or ethanol; OH by warming in water, giving phenol; NO₂ by copper in nitrite solution.
3. **Coupling — the azo dyes** — With its diazo group kept, the salt couples: in mild alkali with phenol it joins at the para position through an –N=N– link to give p-hydroxyazobenzene, an orange dye; in mild acid with aniline it gives p-aminoazobenzene, yellow. Coupling — electrophilic substitution by the diazonium ion on an activated ring — built the azo dyes, the largest family of synthetic colourants, and is the unit's closing proof of the salts' importance in synthesis.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Treats the diazonium salt as a storable product → return to its standing rule: it is made in solution, in the cold (273–278 K), and used at once; warmed or dried, it loses nitrogen and is gone (or, dry, dangerous).
