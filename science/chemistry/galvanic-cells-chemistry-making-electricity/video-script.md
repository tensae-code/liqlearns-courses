Separate the zinc from the copper ions so the electrons cannot jump directly — force them through a wire — and chemistry lights a bulb.

A galvanic (voltaic) cell turns a spontaneous redox reaction into electric current by separating oxidant and reductant into two half-cells joined by a wire and a salt bridge.

The Daniell cell: zinc anode (oxidation, negative), copper cathode (reduction, positive), Zn | Zn²⁺ || Cu²⁺ | Cu in cell notation.

Electrons flow through the wire from anode to cathode; the salt bridge completes the circuit by letting ions move, keeping both solutions neutral.

Now you. Say it back: In the Daniell cell, which metal is the anode?
Answer: Zinc.
