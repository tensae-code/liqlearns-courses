# Nuclear binding energy and mass defect — short answer

Answer in your own words, from the lesson.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Run the helium calculation in words.
**Answer:** Helium's mass defect is 0.0305 u; multiplying by the 931 MeV-per-u shortcut gives 0.0305 × 931 = 28.3955 MeV of binding energy.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why divide by the number of nucleons before comparing two nuclei?
**Answer:** Bigger nuclei bind more in total simply by having more nucleons; binding energy per nucleon puts all nuclei on the same footing, so the higher value honestly means the more stable nucleus.
