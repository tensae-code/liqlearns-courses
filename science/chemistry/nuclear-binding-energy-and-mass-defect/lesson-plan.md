## Sub lessons
1. **Mass defect — the missing mass** — A nucleus weighs **less than its separate nucleons added together**. The difference is the **mass defect**. Egypt works it for helium: the defect is **0.0305 u** — mass that is not lost, but spent: it left as energy when the nucleus formed, and it is exactly the energy that would be needed to pull the nucleus apart again.
2. **E = mc² — and the 931 shortcut** — The conversion is Einstein's: **E = mc²**, giving joules when the mass is in kilograms. For atomic masses the book's shortcut rules the calculations: **1 u of mass defect corresponds to 931 MeV** of energy. Helium's 0.0305 u × 931 gives its **28.3955 MeV** binding energy — the worked number Egypt drills.
3. **Binding energy per nucleon — the stability measure** — Total binding energy grows with size, so the fair comparison is **binding energy per nucleon**: divide by the mass number. The higher it stands, the **more stable the nucleus**. Egypt works the same calculation again for **oxygen-16**, so the method — defect, ×931, ÷ nucleons — is the lesson, not any one answer.
