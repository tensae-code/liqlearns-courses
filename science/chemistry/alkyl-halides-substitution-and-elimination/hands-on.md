# Hands-on — Alkyl halides — substitution and elimination

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort the two routes on paper
Draw three molecules on cards: an alkyl halide R–CH₂–CH₂X, an alcohol R–CH₂–CH₂OH, and an alkene R–CH=CH₂. For each pair, say which route joins them — substitution (the halogen swapped for –OH) or elimination (HX removed, a double bond formed) — and point to the exact atoms that changed. No chemicals are used: this is model work on paper only.

## Build it with a model kit
If your school has a molecular model kit, build chloroethane, CH₃–CH₂Cl. First rebuild it as ethanol, CH₃–CH₂OH, changing only the chlorine for an –OH group: name that route. Then rebuild the first model as ethene, CH₂=CH₂, removing the chlorine and one hydrogen and joining the carbons with a double bond: name that route. A teacher checks each model before you move on.

## Spot the opening lesson
Sudan's Grade 12 book opens its organic unit with alkyl halides, before alcohols and ethers. In two sentences, say why that order makes sense for the two routes in this lesson: the substitution route is itself a way of making the alcohols the next lesson studies.
