Today: **Alkyl halides — substitution and elimination** — Sudan's way: the Grade 12 organic unit opens here.

Take an alkane and swap one hydrogen for a halogen — chlorine, bromine, iodine. Now you have an alkyl halide, R–X, and that one halogen is where everything happens.
Route one is substitution: the halogen leaves, and another group takes its place. With alkali, out goes the halogen, in comes –OH, and your alkyl halide becomes an alcohol.
Route two is elimination: the halogen leaves with a hydrogen from the next carbon — HX goes out — and the two carbons join with a double bond. Your alkyl halide becomes an alkene.
One starting molecule, two roads: swap the halogen, or lose HX and make a double bond.
