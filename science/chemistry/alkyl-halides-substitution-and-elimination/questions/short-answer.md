# Alkyl halides — substitution and elimination — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What does the X stand for in R–X, and which four elements can it be?
**Answer: X stands for a halogen atom: fluorine, chlorine, bromine or iodine. R is the alkyl group it is attached to.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the C–X bond the reactive place in an alkyl halide?
**Answer: The bond is polar — the halogen pulls the shared electrons toward itself, leaving the carbon with a small positive charge — so reactions attack and change the molecule at that bond, while the rest of the chain stays an ordinary alkane chain.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Write the change when bromoethane reacts with aqueous alkali, and name the reaction route.
**Answer: CH₃–CH₂Br becomes CH₃–CH₂OH (ethanol), the bromine leaving as a bromide ion. It is a substitution: –OH has taken the halogen's place on the same carbon.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A learner heats an alkyl halide and gets an alkene. Which route ran, and what two things in the product prove it?
**Answer: Elimination ran. The proof: HX has been removed from the starting molecule, and the product carries a C=C double bond between the two carbons that lost the halogen and the hydrogen.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Sudan's book opens its organic unit with alkyl halides. Give one reason that order makes sense.
**Answer: Alkyl halides are a doorway family: their substitution route with alkali makes alcohols, the family the next lesson (Alcohols and Ethers) studies, so starting here lets the unit build each new family by reaction from the one before.**
