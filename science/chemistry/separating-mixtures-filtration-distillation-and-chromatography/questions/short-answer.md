# Separating mixtures — filtration, distillation and chromatography — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can a dissolved solid never be recovered by filtration?
**Answer: Its particles are dispersed among the solvent's own particles and pass through the paper with the liquid — filtration only holds back particles far larger than the paper's pores, so a dissolved solid must be recovered by evaporation or crystallisation.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student needs pure water from seawater, and separately needs the salt. Name the method for each job.
**Answer: Distillation for the water (boil, condense in the Liebig condenser, collect the distillate); evaporation for the salt (drive all the water off and the salt remains).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Describe how a separating funnel parts petrol floating on water, naming the moment the tap must close.
**Answer: Let the layers settle, open the tap and run the denser lower layer (water) out first, closing the tap the moment the boundary between the layers reaches it, so the petrol stays in the funnel.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What two things about a spot's climb make chromatography able to tell two black inks apart?
**Answer: Each component is carried at its own speed, so the inks spread into different sets of spots at different heights — the pattern, not the original colour, distinguishes them.**
