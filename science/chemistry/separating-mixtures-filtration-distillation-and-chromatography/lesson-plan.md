# Lesson plan — Separating mixtures — filtration, distillation and chromatography

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Separating mixtures — filtration, distillation and chromatography
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a mixture is taken apart — the method is chosen from the components' physical properties: filtration for the insoluble solid (residue on the paper, filtrate through it), evaporation or crystallisation for a dissolved solid, simple distillation with a Liebig condenser and fractional distillation for liquids (the rum and petroleum industries' own method), a separating funnel for immiscible liquids, and paper chromatography for the tiniest coloured mixtures, forensics included
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Mixtures — what chemistry is, substance, matter and transformation, is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Choosing the method — and filtration** — The rule that chooses every method (a physical property that differs), and filtration: residue and filtrate.
2. **Evaporation, crystallisation and distillation — recovering what dissolved** — Evaporation against crystallisation for a dissolved solid; simple distillation with a Liebig condenser; fractional distillation for liquid mixtures, with the rum and petroleum industries named.
3. **The separating funnel and paper chromatography — liquids that will not mix, colours that will not stay** — The separating funnel for immiscible liquids; paper chromatography and its forensic uses.

## If the learner is stuck
- Picks a method by habit → return to the rule: name the components, name the property that differs, and the method follows.
- Confuses evaporation with crystallisation → evaporation drives off all the solvent for a solid that survives dry heat; crystallisation stops at the point of crystallisation and lets crystals grow, for solids heat would damage.
- Confuses simple with fractional distillation → one vaporisation separates a liquid from a dissolved solid; a fractionating column's many vaporisations separate liquids from each other.
