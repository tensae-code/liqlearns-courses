# Hands-on — Separating mixtures — filtration, distillation and chromatography

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Muddy water, two ways (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only)
Stir a spoon of garden soil into a jar of water. Let half of it settle undisturbed for an hour and pour the clearer top layer off gently (settling and pouring). Pour the other half through a coffee filter or clean cloth into a second jar. Compare the two jars against the light: which method cleared more, what stayed behind in each case, and what are the residue and the filtrate called in the filtered jar?

## Ink on the climb (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only)
Rule a pencil line near the bottom of a strip of coffee-filter paper. Spot black felt-tip ink from two different pens on the line, labelled in pencil. Stand the strip in a cup with water just below the line and watch the climb for fifteen minutes. Sketch each pen's spot pattern. If the two patterns differ, write the forensic sentence: how would this test a questioned signature?

## Method match (paper exercise)
Six mixtures are listed in the lesson's Do It Yourself and above. For two more of your own choosing from your kitchen, write the method you would use and the physical property doing the work — one line each, in the lesson's rule form.
