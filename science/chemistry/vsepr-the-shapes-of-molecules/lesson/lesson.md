# VSEPR — the shapes of molecules

## Do it first
Methane is a perfect tetrahedron; water, with the same four electron pairs around its centre, is bent. Before reading, say what invisible thing squeezes water's bonds.

[Picture 21640 — Five shapes, fixed by repulsion.]

## Sub lesson 1 — Repulsion decides the shape
VSEPR theory: electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible — the arrangement fixes the molecule's shape.

[GIF 21641 — a lone pair squeezing bond angles]

## Sub lesson 2 — The classic shapes and angles
The classic shapes: linear (180°), trigonal planar (120°), tetrahedral (109.5°), trigonal bipyramidal and octahedral (90°).

## Sub lesson 3 — Lone pairs squeeze
Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs, so they squeeze bond angles — water's pairs give it a bent shape, ammonia a pyramidal one.

## Sub lesson 4 — Shape and the polar molecule
Shape plus bond polarity decides whether the molecule itself is polar.
