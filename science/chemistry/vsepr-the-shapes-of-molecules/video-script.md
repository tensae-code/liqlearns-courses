Methane is a perfect tetrahedron; water, with the same four electron pairs around its centre, is bent.

VSEPR theory: electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible — the arrangement fixes the molecule's shape.

The classic shapes: linear (180°), trigonal planar (120°), tetrahedral (109.

Lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs, so they squeeze bond angles — water's pairs give it a bent shape, ammonia a pyramidal one.

Shape plus bond polarity decides whether the molecule itself is polar.

Now you. Say it back: What does VSEPR stand on?
Answer: Electron pairs repel and spread apart.
