## Sub lessons
1. **Zinc the metal** — Zinc (**Z 30, electron arrangement 2 8 18 2, density 7.133**) was known in alloys since antiquity but isolated only in the **mid-18th century**. A **good reducer forming Zn²⁺**, it **skins over in air with a basic carbonate layer that blocks oxygen** — the reason galvanised roofing protects iron. Like aluminium it reacts with **acids and with alkali bases** (the hydrogen proven by the burning-splint pop), and **Zn + HCl is the laboratory's standard hydrogen preparation**.
2. **The compounds at work** — **ZnO is white and amphoteric** — zinc white paint, pharmaceuticals, sunscreens and skin creams; **Zn(OH)₂ is likewise amphoteric** and decomposes to ZnO on heating. Concentrated **ZnCl₂ solutions dissolve starch, cellulose and silk** — the textile industry's trick and the making of **parchment paper**, whose dissolved surface seals the sheet translucent and waterproof, softened with glycerol. **ZnSO₄ impregnates timber against rot**; **ZnS with BaSO₄ makes the white paint lithopone**. The body holds **about 2 g of Zn²⁺** with a **~15 mg daily need** — deficiency stunts growth, excess poisons ('foundry fever') — and a sidebar warns that cadmium, zinc's group-mate, displaces it from enzymes and poisons up the food chain.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks the carbonate skin is rust-like damage → compare: iron's rust flakes and lets oxygen through; zinc's skin seals and blocks — the skin is the protection, not the failure.
