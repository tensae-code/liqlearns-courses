## Properties of covalent compounds

Covalent compounds are typically gases, liquids or low-melting soft solids of discrete molecules held by weak intermolecular forces (named as van der Waals forces, with their full treatment explicitly deferred to Grade 11): low melting/boiling points, low density, slow reactions (bonds must be broken), generally non-conducting (no ions), and soluble by the like-dissolves-like rule (nonpolar in nonpolar solvents; few polar covalent compounds dissolve in polar solvents such as methanol/ethanol). Three exceptions are taught by name: diamond's very high melting point (about 4027 °C), graphite conducting electricity, and HCl conducting in aqueous solution because it ionises.

The unit review forces the full ionic-vs-covalent comparison: state, melting point, density, conductivity and solubility, each explained from bond type.
