# Writing a laboratory report

## Do it first
Before you read: look at the picture for this lesson — a model report page beside a correctly drawn best-fit graph. Say out loud what you see, what you think is happening, and one question it raises. Do not try any chemical step yourself: in this course, laboratory work is observed and described first, and the safety rules come before any practical work.

## Writing a laboratory report

The prelab report (done before the experiment: read the introduction and procedure, answer prelab questions, note sample calculations) and the laboratory report itself (neat final draft in ink or typed, first drafted in the notebook; correct significant figures and units throughout — wrong sig figs "amount to lying about the precision"; complete-sentence answers;

labelled sample computations; stapled, named, dated pages) are specified part by part. Graph rules close the section: numbered labelled axes, a title naming the ordinate quantity first, data points as circles/crosses never dots, explained best-fit lines (a smooth best curve is not joining the dots), and scales that fill the page.


## Say it to your companion
