# Lesson plan — Hydrogen — preparation, properties and uses

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Hydrogen — preparation, properties and uses
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** hydrogen the element whole, as Belarus' Grade 10 book teaches it in one full section (Part VII §36): the first and lightest element, found on Earth only combined; its laboratory preparation from a metal and a dilute acid and its pop test; and its properties and uses — burning to water only, reducing heated metal oxides, and serving as balloon lift, fuel, and factory feedstock

## Sub lessons
1. **The lightest element — everywhere and nowhere free** — Hydrogen is the first element and the lightest of all, the most abundant element in the universe, yet almost never found free on Earth: it is locked inside water, acids, and the compounds of living things, and its chemistry is the chemistry of getting it out and of what it does once free.
2. **Preparing hydrogen — and proving it is hydrogen** — In the laboratory hydrogen is prepared by a metal on a dilute acid — zinc with dilute hydrochloric or sulfuric acid — collected over water or by downward delivery, and proved by the test that belongs to it alone: a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube gives the sharp pop of hydrogen burning in air.
3. **Properties and uses — the reducing gas that makes water** — Hydrogen burns in oxygen to give water and nothing else, and passed over heated metal oxides it takes their oxygen away — it is a reducing agent (CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O). Its uses follow its properties: a fuel whose product is water, a feedstock for making ammonia and margarine (hydrogenation), and the lift in weather balloons.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Confuses hydrogen's test with oxygen's → stand the two splint tests side by side: hydrogen's lighted splint pops; oxygen's glowing splint relights. The two tests are opposites — one gas burns, the other makes burning fiercer.
