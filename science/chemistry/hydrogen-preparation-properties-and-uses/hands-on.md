# Hands-on — Hydrogen — preparation, properties and uses

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The pop test (school laboratory only — teacher present)
In the school laboratory, with the teacher present and the safety kit on: generate hydrogen from zinc and dilute hydrochloric acid in a test tube, collect a tube of the gas over water, and apply the lighted-splint test. Record the sound, then repeat with the gas left standing open for a minute and record how the sound changes. Say, from sub lesson 2, what each sound proves.

## Water from a flame (teacher demonstration — watch and record)
Watch the teacher burn a slow stream of pure hydrogen in air with a cold dry glass held above the flame. Note what appears on the glass, and write the equation of what you saw. Say why this experiment earned hydrogen its name.

## Property-to-use matching
Write hydrogen's four properties on cards — lightest gas, burns to water only, reduces metal oxides, joins nitrogen and oils. Write its four uses on separate cards — weather balloon, rocket fuel, ammonia manufacture, hardening oils to fats. Match each use to the property it rides on, saying the match out loud for each pair.
