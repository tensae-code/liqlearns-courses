# Hydrogen — preparation, properties and uses — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is free hydrogen almost never found on Earth, though it is the universe's commonest element?
**Answer: It is so light that it escapes the Earth, and so ready to combine that what remains is locked inside compounds — water above all, acids, and the substances of living things.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Write the equation for preparing hydrogen from zinc and dilute sulfuric acid, and say how the gas is collected and why.
**Answer: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑. It is collected over water (or by downward delivery) because it is far lighter than air and only very slightly soluble in water.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must hydrogen from a generator be purity-tested before a large amount is lit?
**Answer: Because hydrogen mixed with air is explosive; the pop test quieting to a calm flame shows the air has been flushed out and the gas is pure enough to burn safely.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A stream of hydrogen turns black copper(II) oxide pink and water appears on the tube. Explain both observations with one equation.
**Answer: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O — hydrogen reduces the oxide to pink copper metal, taking its oxygen to form the water that condenses.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Name three uses of hydrogen and the property each one uses.
**Answer: Weather balloons — its extreme lightness. Fuel (welding, rockets) — it burns hot and gives only water. Ammonia manufacture and oil hydrogenation — its readiness to join other elements.**
