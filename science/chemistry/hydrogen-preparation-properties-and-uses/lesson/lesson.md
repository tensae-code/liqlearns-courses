# Hydrogen — preparation, properties and uses

## Do it first
Before you read: hydrogen is the commonest element in the whole universe — the Sun is mostly made of it — yet nobody has ever picked up a lump of it on Earth. Say out loud: where is Earth's hydrogen hiding, and what do you think you would have to do to a substance that holds it to set the gas free? Then read on: Belarus gives hydrogen a full section of its own (§36) in the nonmetals part of its Grade 10 book — the element prepared, proved, and put to work — and that section is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The lightest element — everywhere and nowhere free

**Hydrogen**, H, is the **first element** of the periodic system and the **lightest** of all — its atoms are the simplest there are, one proton and one electron. It is the most abundant element in the universe: stars are built largely of it, and the Sun burns it.

On Earth the picture reverses. Free hydrogen gas, H₂, is almost never met — it is so light it escapes, and so ready to combine that it is found **locked inside compounds**: in **water** above all, in every **acid**, and in the substances of living things. So hydrogen's story is in two acts: first it must be **set free** from a compound, and then what the free gas does is the chemistry that matters.

The gas itself: **colourless, odourless, the least dense substance known** — about fourteen times lighter than air. That lightness rules how it is collected and half of what it is used for.

## Sub lesson 2 — Preparing hydrogen — and proving it is hydrogen

The laboratory preparation is the reaction of a **metal with a dilute acid**. Zinc and dilute hydrochloric acid is the standard pair:

**Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑**

(Dilute sulfuric acid serves as well: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑.) The gas is collected **over water**, or by **downward delivery** — sent down into an inverted vessel, because it is far lighter than air and will not mix its way out quickly.

Hydrogen has a test of its own, and no other common gas passes it: bring a **lighted splint** to the mouth of a tube of the gas and it burns with a **sharp pop** — the small explosion of hydrogen meeting air. Pure hydrogen in a steady stream burns quietly with a pale flame; the pop is the sound of the mixture, and it is the proof the gas is hydrogen.

One safety fact rides with the preparation: hydrogen mixed with air is **explosive**, which is why the gas from a generator is tested for purity — the pop quieting to a calm flame — before any large amount is lit.

## Sub lesson 3 — Properties and uses — the reducing gas that makes water

Hydrogen's great reaction is with oxygen — the union its name records (*hydrogen* = water-maker):

**2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**

It **burns** in oxygen or air to give water and a great deal of heat, and water is the **only** product — the fact behind every plan to use hydrogen as a clean fuel.

Passed over a **heated metal oxide**, hydrogen takes the oxide's oxygen for itself — it **reduces** the oxide to the metal:

**CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O**

Black copper(II) oxide turns to pink copper metal, and water condenses on the cool part of the tube: hydrogen the **reducing agent**, itself oxidised to water. Between burning and reducing, most of hydrogen's chemistry is covered — it is an element that wants oxygen, and takes it from air or from compounds alike.

The **uses** follow the properties one by one. Its lightness fills **weather balloons**. Its burning — heat with water as the only product — makes it a **fuel**, in welding flames and rocket engines. Its eagerness to join other elements makes it a factory feedstock: it is the H₂ of **ammonia manufacture** (the Haber process) and of **hydrogenation**, the hardening of liquid oils into solid fats. Belarus teaches the element whole — prepared, proved, properties and uses in one section — and this lesson keeps that shape.

## The key ideas

- **Hydrogen** is the first and lightest element, the most abundant in the universe, but on Earth it is found **combined** — in water, acids and living matter — almost never free.
- In the laboratory it is prepared by a **metal on a dilute acid** (Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂↑), collected over water, and proved by the **pop test** with a lighted splint.
- It **burns** in oxygen to give **water only** (2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O) — the clean-fuel fact.
- It **reduces** heated metal oxides (CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O) — hydrogen the reducing agent.
- Its uses read off its properties: balloons (lightness), fuel (burning), ammonia manufacture and oil hydrogenation (joining other elements).
