Today: **Temperature scales — kelvin, Celsius, Fahrenheit**.

Temperature measures hotness/coldness;

heat is energy that flows spontaneously from hotter to colder, never the reverse.

The Celsius and Kelvin degree are the same size, offset by 273.15 (K = °C + 273.15), so a temperature *change* is numerically equal on both scales;

the Fahrenheit degree is smaller (180 °F spans the same interval as 100 °C between water's freezing and boiling points) and its zero is shifted, giving the two…

Worked example converts 100 °F to about 38 °C.

Now you. Say it back in your own words. That is the lesson.
