## Temperature scales

Temperature measures hotness/coldness; heat is energy that flows spontaneously from hotter to colder, never the reverse. The Celsius and Kelvin degree are the same size, offset by 273.15 (K = °C + 273.15), so a temperature *change* is numerically equal on both scales;

the Fahrenheit degree is smaller (180 °F spans the same interval as 100 °C between water's freezing and boiling points) and its zero is shifted, giving the two conversion formulas with the −32 adjustment. Worked example converts 100 °F to about 38 °C.
