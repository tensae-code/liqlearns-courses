# Hazards of polymers and plastics — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is a finished polymer chemically quiet, in one or two sentences?
**Answer: Its molecules are very long chains: they do not dissolve, evaporate or react their way into living tissue. The reactivity of the small starting molecules is spent in joining them; the chain that results is large and unreactive.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) What is residual monomer, and how does it reach people?
**Answer: It is the unreacted small monomer left trapped in the plastic when polymerisation stops short of completion. Being small and mobile, it migrates out of the material into food, drink and air that the plastic touches.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain plasticizer leaching from the bonding, step by step.
**Answer: The plasticizer is kneaded between the chains but never chemically bonded to them; only lodging holds it. Over time it diffuses to the surface and into whatever touches the plastic — food, water, soil — while the plastic itself grows stiffer.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the hazard burning plastic waste creates and the alternative the chapter teaches.
**Answer: Burning releases the plastic's small molecules — residual monomer, additives and breakdown products — into the air as fumes. The chapter's alternative is collection with burial or recycling, and keeping plastic waste away from fields, rivers and grazing ground.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Give two prevention measures and the hazard each one answers.
**Answer: Never heat food in plastic not made for it — answers monomer and plasticizer migration, which heat speeds up. Never burn plastic waste and keep it from fields and water — answers the released-molecule hazard of burning and the ingestion/entanglement hazard to animals.**
