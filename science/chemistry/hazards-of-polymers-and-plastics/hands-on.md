# Hands-on — Hazards of polymers and plastics

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Hazard hunt (paper only)
List five plastic objects at home or school. For each, write which of the lesson's three small-molecule hazards could apply — residual monomer, molecules released on heating, plasticizer leaching — and the fact about the object that decides it (does it touch food? is it heated? is it soft and bendable?).

## The burning question (paper only)
A neighbour says burning plastic rubbish is clean because 'it all disappears'. Write the reply the lesson gives: what does not disappear, where it goes, and what should be done with the plastic instead.

## Prevention pairing (paper only)
Copy the chapter's four hazards in a column — residual monomer, released molecules on heating/burning, plasticizer leaching, harm to animals — and beside each write the prevention measure from sub lesson 3 that answers it.
