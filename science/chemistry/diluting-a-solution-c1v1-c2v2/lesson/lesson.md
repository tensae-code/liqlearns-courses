# Diluting a solution — C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

## Do it first
Before you read: you stir a spoonful of sweet tea into a full glass of water. The glass tastes much weaker — but where did the sugar go? Say out loud: was any sugar lost, or only spread out? Then read on: Syria's Grade 9 book builds the dilution law on exactly that taste, on printed p. 99.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dilution discovered by tasting

Syria begins with the tongue: **a spoonful of sugar solution trebled in volume tastes weaker, while the dissolved sugar is unchanged**. Adding solvent does not remove solute — it **spreads the same amount of solute through more volume**, and the concentration falls exactly because the same amount now fills more room.

That is the whole of dilution in one taste: **solvent added, solute conserved**. Every dilution calculation is this taste, written in symbols.

## Sub lesson 2 — The law — C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

Because the amount of solute does not change, the product **concentration × volume is the same before and after**: **C₁V₁ = C₂V₂**. The book works the law on a **sodium hydroxide solution**: know any three of the four quantities — starting concentration and volume, final concentration and volume — and the fourth follows.

Treble the volume and the concentration falls to a third; halve the volume's growth and the fall halves with it. The law is the conservation of solute, and nothing else.

## Sub lesson 3 — The safety rule — acid into water, never the reverse

The safety enrichment (printed p. 100) fixes the bench rule that guards every dilution of an acid: **acid is always added to water, never the reverse**. Water added to concentrated acid can boil violently at the first drop and spit acid; acid added slowly to a body of water spreads its heat safely through the water.

The rule is part of the law's lesson, not an aside: dilution is something the learner will actually do, and this is how it is done without harm.

## The key ideas

- **Dilution adds solvent; the dissolved solute is unchanged** — trebled volume, weaker taste, same sugar (U4 L1, printed p. 99).
- Solute conserved gives the law **C₁V₁ = C₂V₂**, worked on a sodium hydroxide solution.
- Safety (printed p. 100): **acid is always added to water, never the reverse**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain diluting a solution to your companion in your own words: the tasting discovery, why C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ holds, and the acid-and-water safety rule.

## Do It Yourself
1. A solution's volume is trebled by adding water. What happens to its concentration, and to its amount of solute?
2. A solution has C₁ = 2 mol/L and V₁ = 50 mL. It is diluted to V₂ = 200 mL. Find C₂ from C₁V₁ = C₂V₂.
3. Say the safety rule for diluting an acid, and which way round it goes.
