# Lesson plan — Diluting a solution — C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

## Sub lessons
1. **Dilution discovered by tasting** — Syria begins with the tongue: **a spoonful of sugar solution trebled in volume tastes weaker, while the dissolved sugar is unchanged**. Adding solvent does not remove solute — it **spreads the same amount of solute through more volume**, and the concentration falls exactly because the same amount now fills more room.
2. **The law — C₁V₁ = C₂V₂** — Because the amount of solute does not change, the product **concentration × volume is the same before and after**: **C₁V₁ = C₂V₂**. The book works the law on a **sodium hydroxide solution**: know any three of the four quantities — starting concentration and volume, final concentration and volume — and the fourth follows.
3. **The safety rule — acid into water, never the reverse** — The safety enrichment (printed p. 100) fixes the bench rule that guards every dilution of an acid: **acid is always added to water, never the reverse**. Water added to concentrated acid can boil violently at the first drop and spit acid; acid added slowly to a body of water spreads its heat safely through the water.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks diluting destroys solute → the sugar-taste test shows the solute unchanged — only spread through more volume; the concentration falls, the amount does not.
- Applies C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ when solute was added too → the law rests on solute conserved; if solute was added or removed, the two products are not equal.
- Reverses the acid rule → acid INTO water, always — the water body carries the heat away; water into acid can boil and spit at the first drop.
