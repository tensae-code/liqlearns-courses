# Vitamins and hormones — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the solubility sorting predict how regularly a vitamin must be eaten?
**Answer: Water-soluble vitamins (B group, C) are scarcely stored and the excess leaves in urine, so they must be supplied regularly. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are stored in the liver and fatty tissues, so the body's reserve covers gaps in supply — and can be overfilled.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Match the diseases to their vitamins: beri-beri, pernicious anaemia, night blindness, increased clotting time.
**Answer: Beri-beri — vitamin B₁ (thiamine); pernicious anaemia — vitamin B₁₂; night blindness — vitamin A; increased blood-clotting time — vitamin K.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A diet consists almost wholly of polished rice. Which deficiency threatens, and what part of the rice was the protection?
**Answer: Beri-beri, from lack of vitamin B₁ (thiamine). The thiamine stands in the rice polishings/husk fraction that polishing removes; the polished grain that remains is nearly pure starch.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Insulin and glucagon are called antagonistic. Why?
**Answer: Both act on the same balance — blood glucose — in opposite directions from the same gland, the pancreas: insulin lowers blood glucose by promoting uptake and storage; glucagon raises it by releasing glucose from liver stores. Together they hold the level between them.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What element does thyroxine carry, and what follows when the diet lacks it?
**Answer: Iodine. Without iodine the thyroid cannot make enough thyroxine, the metabolic rate falls (hypothyroidism), and the gland enlarges in compensation — goitre.**
