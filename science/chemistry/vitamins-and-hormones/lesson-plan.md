# Lesson plan — Vitamins and hormones

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Vitamins and hormones
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two regulator families of NCERT's Class XII Biomolecules unit (§§14.4 and 14.6): the vitamins — organic substances the diet must supply in small amounts, sorted by solubility into the water-soluble B group and C (taken daily, scarcely stored) and the fat-soluble A, D, E and K (stored in liver and fat), each with its deficiency disease (scurvy, beri-beri, night blindness, rickets) — and the hormones, the endocrine glands' chemical messengers carried by the blood: insulin and glucagon ruling glucose, thyroxine the metabolic rate, adrenaline the emergency response, and the steroid sex and adrenocortical hormones
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Enzymes — biological catalysts is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Vitamins — the diet's small necessities, in two families** — A vitamin is an organic compound the diet must supply in small amounts because the body cannot synthesise it (or not enough); absence, not presence, defines it, through the deficiency diseases. Solubility sorts them: water-soluble — the B group and C — are scarcely stored, excreted in urine, and must be supplied regularly; fat-soluble — A, D, E, K — are stored in liver and fatty tissues, buffering supply but risking excess.
2. **The deficiency table — what each vitamin guards** — NCERT's table at work: A guards the eye (deficiency: night blindness, xerophthalmia — sources: fish liver oils, carrots, butter); B₁, thiamine (beri-beri — yeast, cereals, rice polishings); B₂, riboflavin (cheilosis — milk, eggs, leafy vegetables); B₆, pyridoxine (convulsions); B₁₂ (pernicious anaemia — meat, fish, egg, curd); C, ascorbic acid (scurvy — citrus fruits, amla, green leafy vegetables); D (rickets and osteomalacia — sunlight on skin, fish, fortified milk); E (fragility of red blood cells, muscular weakness — vegetable oils, nuts); K (bleeding, increased clotting time — leafy vegetables, gut bacteria); H, biotin (dermatitis, hair loss).
3. **Hormones — messengers in the blood** — Hormones are secreted by the ductless endocrine glands straight into the blood, which carries them to distant target organs whose rates they set. NCERT's cast by chemistry: the steroids — the sex hormones (androgens, estrogens) from the gonads and the adrenocortical hormones ruling metabolism and salt–water balance; the peptides — insulin (lowers blood glucose; its failure is diabetes) and glucagon (raises it) from the pancreas; and the amino-acid-derived — thyroxine from the thyroid, iodine-carrying, setting the metabolic rate, and adrenaline from the adrenal medulla, the emergency hormone of the racing heart.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Treats vitamins as food/energy → return to the definition: vitamins are regulators needed in small amounts; they yield no energy themselves, and their absence, not their calories, is what the deficiency diseases measure.
