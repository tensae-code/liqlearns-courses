# Vitamins and hormones

## Do it first
Before you read: sailors on long voyages, fed for months on biscuit and salt meat, sickened with bleeding gums and wounds that would not heal — and recovered within days of eating citrus fruit, though the fruit carried almost no energy at all. And inside your own body, a meal's sugar is cleared from the blood within the hour by a signal you never feel. Say out loud: what sort of substance could health depend on in amounts too small to burn — and what carries a command from one organ to another without a nerve? Then read on: India's NCERT Class XII book closes its Biomolecules unit with the two regulator families — **vitamins** (§14.4) and **hormones** (§14.6).

## Sub lesson 1 — Vitamins — the diet's small necessities, in two families

A **vitamin** is an **organic compound that the diet must supply in small amounts** because the body cannot make it (or cannot make enough), and whose absence causes a specific **deficiency disease**. Vitamins are not food in the energy sense — they are regulators and protectors, and NCERT sorts them by the one property that decides their handling in the body: **solubility**.

- **Water-soluble vitamins** — the **B group** and **vitamin C**. They dissolve in water, are **scarcely stored**, and the excess passes out in urine: they must be **supplied regularly** in the diet, and poisoning by excess is rare.
- **Fat-soluble vitamins** — **A, D, E and K**. They dissolve in fats and oils and are **stored in the liver and fatty tissues**: the store buffers an irregular supply — and can be overfilled, so excess of these (A and D above all) can itself do harm.

That single sorting — water against fat — predicts storage, dosing rhythm and risk for the whole table of the next sub lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — The deficiency table — what each vitamin guards

NCERT's table names each vitamin by its **sources** and the **disease of its absence**:

- **Vitamin A** (fish liver oils, carrots, papaya, butter) — deficiency: **night blindness** and **xerophthalmia**, the hardening of the eye's surface.
- **Vitamin B₁ — thiamine** (yeast, cereals, rice polishings, nuts) — **beri-beri**, the nerve and weakness disease of polished-rice diets.
- **Vitamin B₂ — riboflavin** (milk, eggs, leafy vegetables) — **cheilosis**, the cracking of lips and mouth corners.
- **Vitamin B₆ — pyridoxine** (cereals, gram, yeast, egg yolk) — **convulsions**.
- **Vitamin B₁₂** (meat, fish, egg, curd) — **pernicious anaemia**, the blood's failure.
- **Vitamin C — ascorbic acid** (citrus fruits, amla, green leafy vegetables) — **scurvy**: bleeding gums, wounds refusing to heal — the sailors' disease of the Do-it-first.
- **Vitamin D** (sunlight acting on the skin, fish, fortified milk) — **rickets** in children and **osteomalacia** in adults: bones too soft to bear weight straight.
- **Vitamin E** (vegetable oils, nuts, leafy vegetables) — increased **fragility of red blood cells** and muscular weakness.
- **Vitamin K** (leafy vegetables; also made by gut bacteria) — **increased blood-clotting time**: bleeding that will not stop.
- **Vitamin H — biotin** (yeast, liver, kidney) — **dermatitis** and loss of hair.

Read the table as NCERT intends: the disease is the diagnostic — each missing regulator fails one system, and the named sources are the repair.

## Sub lesson 3 — Hormones — messengers in the blood

Where vitamins arrive from the plate, **hormones are made at home**: they are **secreted by the endocrine (ductless) glands directly into the blood**, which carries them to **distant target organs**, where they regulate rates and balances. NCERT sorts its cast by chemical family:

- **Steroid hormones** — built on the steroid frame: the **sex hormones** — **androgens** from the testes and **estrogens** from the ovaries, ruling sexual development and function — and the **adrenocortical hormones** from the adrenal cortex, ruling carbohydrate metabolism (the glucocorticoids) and the body's **salt and water balance** (the mineralocorticoids).
- **Peptide hormones** — chains of amino acids: **insulin** and **glucagon**, both from the **pancreas**, the two hands on the blood-glucose balance — insulin **lowers** blood glucose (promoting its uptake and storage; its deficiency is **diabetes mellitus**), glucagon **raises** it (releasing glucose from the liver's stores).
- **Amino-acid-derived hormones** — **thyroxine**, from the **thyroid gland**, carries **iodine** and sets the body's **metabolic rate** (too little: the slowness of hypothyroidism; the gland itself swells as goitre when iodine is lacking); and **adrenaline (epinephrine)**, from the **adrenal medulla** the **emergency hormone** — heart rate and blood pressure up, glucose released, the body readied for fight or flight in seconds.

Vitamins and hormones are one idea from two directions: tiny amounts of the right molecule, without which whole systems drift — one family supplied by the diet, the other manufactured by the glands. With them §14.6 closes the Biomolecules unit, and the molecules of life stand complete: carbohydrates, proteins, enzymes, vitamins, nucleic acids, hormones.

## The key ideas

- A **vitamin** is an organic compound needed in **small amounts** from the diet; its absence gives a named **deficiency disease**.
- Sorting by solubility: **water-soluble (B group, C)** — scarcely stored, excreted, needed regularly; **fat-soluble (A, D, E, K)** — stored in liver and fat, with excess risk.
- The table's anchors: **A** — night blindness; **B₁** — beri-beri; **B₁₂** — pernicious anaemia; **C** — scurvy; **D** — rickets/osteomalacia; **K** — clotting failure.
- **Hormones** are the endocrine glands' messengers, blood-carried to distant targets: **steroids** (sex hormones, adrenocortical), **peptides** (**insulin** lowers, **glucagon** raises blood glucose), amino-acid-derived (**thyroxine** — metabolic rate; **adrenaline** — emergency).

## Do It Yourself
1. A child in a sunless winter on a milk-poor diet develops bowed legs. Name the vitamin, its deficiency disease, and two sources that answer it. (Vitamin D; rickets. Sources: sunlight on the skin and fish or fortified milk in the diet.)
2. Why must vitamin C be eaten regularly while vitamin A need not be eaten daily? (C is water-soluble: scarcely stored and excreted in urine, so the body holds no reserve. A is fat-soluble and stored in the liver and fatty tissues, buffering gaps in supply.)
3. After a large meal, which hormone acts, from which gland, and in which direction on blood glucose? (Insulin, from the pancreas; it lowers blood glucose by promoting its uptake into cells and storage as glycogen.)
