Today: **Vitamins and hormones** — India's way: the two closing regulator sections of the Class XII Biomolecules unit — the diet's small necessities, and the body's own messengers.

A vitamin is an organic substance the body cannot make, or cannot make enough of, that the diet must supply in small amounts — and small is not unimportant: without vitamin C the gums bleed in scurvy, without B₁ the nerves fail in beri-beri, without A the eye fails at dusk, without D the child's bones bend in rickets. They sort by solubility: B and C dissolve in water, pass out in urine and must come daily; A, D, E and K dissolve in fat and are stored in the liver — a store that protects, and that can also be overfilled.
Hormones are the body's own version of the same idea: chemical messengers made in endocrine glands and carried by the blood to distant targets. Insulin from the pancreas lowers blood glucose, glucagon raises it; thyroxine from the thyroid sets the metabolic rate; adrenaline from the adrenal medulla answers emergencies — heart faster, pressure up, fuel released. Regulators from the plate, regulators from the glands: one lesson, two couriers.
