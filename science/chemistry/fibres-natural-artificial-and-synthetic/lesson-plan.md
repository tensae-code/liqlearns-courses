# Lesson plan — Fibres — natural, artificial and synthetic

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Fibres — natural, artificial and synthetic
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Long-chain polymers you can wear: nature's (cellulose and protein), the artificials rebuilt from cellulose, and the true synthetics — and how the bench tells them apart (Bulgaria, Grade 10 — §§58–59, printed pp. 149–152)

## Sub lessons
1. **The three families** — **Natural fibres** are plant (**cotton in the seed, flax and hemp in the stem — cellulose**) and animal (**wool, and silk from the silkworm — proteins**): cotton hygroscopic, wool elastic and heat-insulating, silk very strong. The **chemical fibres** begin at the end of the 19th century: **artificial viscose** (cellulose worked with NaOH and CS₂; the cheapest, shown at Paris in 1889 rivalling silk's lustre) and **acetate silk** (a cellulose ester, the artificials' best mechanics). Then the **true synthetics** — **polyester PET, polyamide nylon-6,6, polyacrylonitrile** — rot-proof and moth-proof but electrostatic and non-hygroscopic, so modern textiles **blend them with natural fibres**. A sidebar marvels at spider silk; textile waste is recycled into insulation boards, filter media, coarse ceramics and activated carbon.
2. **Recognising fibres — the burning table** — The laboratory lesson identifies fibres as forensic bench work: **burning behaviour** sorts them toward cellulose, protein or synthetic — **cotton burns fast smelling of burnt paper; wool slowly, smelling of horn, leaving a crushable black bead**. A **complete identification table** files each fibre by elementary unit, burning, destruction products and behaviour toward conc. HNO₃, conc. H₂SO₄ and 1 M NaOH — cotton/flax, wool/silk, viscose, acetate, polyamide, polyester and PAN. An unknown fibre is named by its burning; wool's weakness to alkali is shown by dissolving detergent solutions; viscose strips stain dark blue in KMnO₄; and the protein inside wool is to be proven.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Files viscose as synthetic → ask for the starting polymer: if the chain began as cellulose, the fibre is artificial however chemical the working was.
