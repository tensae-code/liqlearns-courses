Today: **Diffusion** — Mongolia's way: a section of its own, in Grade 7 and again in Grade 9.

Drop ink into still water. Do not stir. Wait — the whole glass turns the colour of the drop. Particles move. Always. Where they are crowded, they wander out to where they are scarce, until they are spread evenly. That spreading is diffusion, and nobody pushed it: the particles' own motion did it.
Gases do it fast — a smell crosses a room. Liquids do it slowly — the ink takes its time, but it never needs your spoon.
And the spreading can be measured: light particles wander faster than heavy ones. At the same temperature, the lighter gas outruns the heavier one. Diffusion is motion you can see — and a balance for particles you cannot.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
