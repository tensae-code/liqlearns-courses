# Lesson plan — Diffusion — the spreading of particles

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Diffusion — the spreading of particles
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** diffusion as its own teaching unit — particles in gases and liquids spreading themselves from crowded to scarce until evenly spread, needing no stirring because it is the particles' own motion; proved in gases and in liquids, and measured: the rate of diffusion depends on molecular mass, lighter particles spreading faster (the qualitative Graham-type relation); Mongolia teaches diffusion as its own section — §1.2 in Grade 7 and §1.2 in Grade 9
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. The kinetic theory of matter, and Phase changes — melting, evaporation, boiling and condensation, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **What diffusion is — particles spreading themselves** — Particles wander constantly; from a crowded region they drift to a scarce one until evenly spread — no stirring, no push, only their own motion.
2. **Diffusion in gases and in liquids — the proofs** — Mongolia Grade 9's pair: a smell crossing a room proves it in gases (fast), ink colouring still water proves it in liquids (slow, unstirred, but sure).
3. **The rate of diffusion — lighter particles spread faster** — At the same temperature the rate depends on molecular mass: lighter particles diffuse faster, heavier ones more slowly — the qualitative Graham-type relation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the crowded-and-scarce picture: particles wander, wandering evens any uneven spread, and that evening-out is diffusion.
- Thinks stirring causes diffusion → the proofs are done in still air and still water on purpose: remove every stir and the spreading still happens.
- Mixes diffusion up with dissolving → diffusion is the spreading itself, in gases as much as in liquids; it happens wherever particles are free to move, solvent or none.
