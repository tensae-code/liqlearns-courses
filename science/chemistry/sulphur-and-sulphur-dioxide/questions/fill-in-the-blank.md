# Sulphur and sulphur dioxide — fill in the blank

Fill in the missing word.

**1.** _____ brittle; reacts with Fe, Cu, H₂, O₂) and SO₂: very soluble (Experiment 5-1), an acidic oxide forming unstable sulfurous acid — the book's first reversible reaction, SO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂SO₃; SO₂ is both oxidising (with H₂S) and reducing (to SO₃), and bleaches by forming an unstable colourless compound (fuchsin Experiment 5-2), so its bleaching fades — unlike chlorine's.
**Answer: Sulfur (yellow,**

**2.** _____ sulfur thickens then thins: S₈ crown rings (structure pictured) melt at 113 °C into a runny liquid; above ~160 °C the rings snap open and polymerise into long chains — viscosity peaks near 200 °C — then the chains break down again towards the 445 °C boiling point; the vapour itself is an equilibrium mixture shifting 3S₈ ⇌ 4S₆ ⇌ 6S₄ ⇌ 12S₂, and by 2,000 °C it is mostly single atoms.
**Answer: Why molten**

**3.** _____ at the molecular level: sulfurous acid ADDS across the chromophore of fuchsin (structure of the red dye and its colourless adduct both printed) — a combination, not an oxidation — and the adduct falls apart on heating, which is exactly why the colour returns; the guide directs the comparison with chlorine/HClO and H₂O₂ bleaching, which destroy the chromophore permanently.
**Answer: SO₂ bleaching**
