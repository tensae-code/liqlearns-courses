# Metals in living systems — and heavy-metal poisoning — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A classmate says micro-minerals matter less than macro-minerals. Correct them in two lines.
**Answer: Macro and micro sort by the daily dose (over or under 100 mg a day), not by importance — a micro-mineral needed in traces can be every bit as essential as a macro-mineral.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What do the heavy metals share that the essential metals do not?
**Answer: They have no job in the body at all, and they harm at small doses — the essentials each do named work (magnesium in chlorophyll, iron in haemoglobin) and harm only out of balance.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a swordfish carry more mercury than the small fish it eats?
**Answer: Mercury persists and is barely excreted, so each step up the food chain concentrates the metal from many prey into one hunter; a long-lived top hunter accumulates the most.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the four waste items the lesson sends to collection and recycling, with the metal each carries.
**Answer: Lead-acid batteries (lead), mercury thermometers (mercury), fluorescent bulbs (mercury), and nickel-cadmium batteries (cadmium).**
