# Hands-on — Metals in living systems — and heavy-metal poisoning

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The essentials on labels (field exercise)
Collect three food or drink labels (or photograph them) that declare minerals — a cereal box, a mineral water, a salt packet. List each declared metal, sort it macro or micro by the book's 100 mg-a-day line where the label's daily amounts allow, and match iron and magnesium to their named molecules from the lesson.

## Concentration by counters (paper exercise)
Model the food chain with counters: 100 counters of 'mercury' spread through 100 'algae'. Ten small fish eat ten algae each — count each fish's load. Two big fish eat five small fish each — count again. One hunter eats both big fish. Write the three loads in a row and the lesson's sentence they prove, then name the four real fish the book warns about.

## The battery decision (paper exercise)
A household throws away, in one year: one car battery, two fluorescent tubes, a fever thermometer and a handful of nickel-cadmium cells. For each item write: the heavy metal it carries, where it ends up if binned with ordinary refuse, and the collection or recycling route the lesson asks for instead.
