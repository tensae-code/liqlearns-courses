# Lesson plan — Metals in living systems — and heavy-metal poisoning

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Metals in living systems — and heavy-metal poisoning
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** metal ions as life essentials and as poisons — magnesium at the centre of chlorophyll and iron in haemoglobin, with calcium, zinc, sodium and potassium; the line between macro-minerals (over 100 mg a day) and micro-minerals; then the heavy metals lead, mercury, cadmium and arsenic with their sources and toxic effects (Minamata disease, the mad hatter), their persistence and build-up through food chains into large fish, and the disposal and recycling problem of lead-acid batteries, mercury thermometers and fluorescent bulbs, and nickel-cadmium batteries
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Why metals conduct — properties of metals, is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
1. **The metals life runs on — magnesium, iron and the rest** — Magnesium in chlorophyll, iron in haemoglobin, calcium, zinc, sodium and potassium; macro-minerals (>100 mg/day) against micro-minerals.
2. **The heavy metals — lead, mercury, cadmium and arsenic** — Lead, mercury (Minamata disease; 'mad as a hatter'), cadmium and arsenic — sources and toxic effects tabulated.
3. **Build-up through food chains — and the disposal problem** — Persistence and build-up through food chains into shark, marlin, tuna and swordfish; disposal/recycling of lead-acid batteries, mercury thermometers and fluorescent bulbs, and nickel-cadmium batteries.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats 'metal in the body' as one idea → the lesson's line is dose and identity: the named essentials do named jobs; the heavy four have no job and only accumulate.
- Confuses macro with important → macro/micro counts the daily amount (over or under 100 mg), not the importance — a micro-mineral can be essential.
- Thinks poisoning ends with the source → the heavy metals persist and concentrate up the food chain, which is why the largest fish carry the most.
