# Ionic lattice structures

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's thing is, in one sentence — then read on and check it against Laos's way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — A lattice, not molecules

An ionic compound is not built of separate molecules. Laos's Grade 11 book (Ch. 10 §4) teaches its structure as a **regular three-dimensional lattice of alternating ions** — positive, negative, positive, negative, in every direction, as far as the crystal runs. That is why an ionic compound has **no molecular formula**: only its **empirical formula** — the simplest ratio, NaCl, KCl, MgO — is written, because there is no single NaCl molecule to name.

## Sub lesson 2 — The NaCl type — six neighbours

How many neighbours of the opposite kind touch each ion is set by the **ions' sizes**. In the **NaCl type** (the rock-salt structure) each ion touches **6** of the other kind — one above, one below, and four around. The book lists its family: NaCl itself, NaF, NaBr, KCl, MgO, CaO and others.

## Sub lesson 3 — The CsCl type — eight neighbours

When the ions' sizes suit a tighter surround, the structure is the **CsCl type**: each ion touches **8** of the other kind. The book's examples: CsCl itself, CsBr, CsI and NH₄Cl. Two lattice types, two neighbour counts — 6 and 8 — and the sizes of the ions decide which structure a compound takes.

## The key ideas

- An ionic compound is a **regular 3D lattice of alternating ions** — there are **no separate molecules**.
- Only the **empirical formula** is written (NaCl, KCl, MgO).
- **NaCl type**: each ion touches **6** of the other kind (NaF, NaBr, KCl, MgO, CaO…).
- **CsCl type**: each ion touches **8** of the other kind (CsBr, CsI, NH₄Cl); the ions' **sizes** set the type.
