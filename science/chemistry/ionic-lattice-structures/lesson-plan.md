# Lesson plan — Ionic lattice structures

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Ionic lattice structures
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the structure of ionic compounds as a regular three-dimensional lattice of alternating ions — the NaCl type, each ion touching 6 of the other kind, and the CsCl type, each touching 8, the count set by the ions' sizes, with no separate molecules, only an empirical formula — as Laos's Grade 11 (ມ.6) textbook teaches it in Ch. 10 §4 (printed p. 55)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. No other lesson is needed first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **A lattice, not molecules** — An ionic compound is not built of separate molecules.
2. **The NaCl type — six neighbours** — How many neighbours of the opposite kind touch each ion is set by the **ions' sizes**.
3. **The CsCl type — eight neighbours** — When the ions' sizes suit a tighter surround, the structure is the **CsCl type**: each ion touches **8** of the other kind.
