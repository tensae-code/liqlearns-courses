# Classifying substances — the inorganic and organic family trees

## Do it first
Before reading, say out loud: where would you file table salt, and where would you file sugar? Both are white crystals from the kitchen — but chemistry files them in different kingdoms. Bulgaria's Grade 10 book opens its year with the two family trees that decide, and this lesson teaches those trees.

## Sub lesson 1 — The inorganic tree

**Inorganic substances** split first into **simple substances** — the metals and the nonmetals — and **compounds**. The compounds branch into five families the whole course keeps meeting: **hydrogen compounds, oxides, hydroxides, acids and salts**. If a substance is inorganic, it has a place on this tree: one element alone, or a compound in one of the five families.

## Sub lesson 2 — The organic tree

**Organic compounds** grow on the second tree. Its first branch is the **hydrocarbons** — alkanes, alkenes, alkynes and arenes. The **oxygen-containing** branch splits by its working group: hydroxyl compounds (alcohols and phenols), carbonyl compounds (aldehydes and ketones), esters, and carboxylic acids. The **nitrogen-containing** branch holds the amines and the amino acids. The two trees together are the map the whole Grade 10 year hangs on — every later lesson is a walk to one branch of one tree.

## The key ideas

- Inorganic: simple substances (metals, nonmetals) and compounds (hydrogen compounds, oxides, hydroxides, acids, salts).
- Organic: hydrocarbons; oxygen-containing (alcohols/phenols, aldehydes/ketones, esters, carboxylic acids); nitrogen-containing (amines, amino acids).
- Classification comes first: name the tree, then the branch, then the family.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion: the inorganic tree; the organic tree — in that order, in your own words, with the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. File NaCl and CH₄ on the two trees. (NaCl is inorganic — a salt, in the compounds branch. CH₄ is organic — a hydrocarbon (an alkane).)
2. Which tree holds ethanol, C₂H₅OH, and on which branch? (The organic tree, oxygen-containing branch — a hydroxyl compound (an alcohol).)
