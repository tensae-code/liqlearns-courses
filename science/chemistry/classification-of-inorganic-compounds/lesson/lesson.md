# Classification of inorganic compounds

## Sub lesson 1 — Four main classes

Inorganic compounds sort into **four main classes**: **oxides, bases, acids and salts**. Armenia's Grade 8 book opens its classification theme by setting the four out as **one scheme** (§3/1, printed p. 59), before any class is studied on its own — the map comes first, the journeys after.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cover scheme — how the classes connect

The book's **cover itself diagrams the scheme**: metal / nonmetal / basic oxide / acidic oxide / base / acid / salt. The classes are not four separate boxes — the metals lead to the basic oxides and bases, the nonmetals to the acidic oxides and acids, and the roads meet in the **salts**.

## Sub lesson 3 — One class at a time, after the map

Only after the scheme is set out does the theme study each class in turn — oxides, then bases, then acids, then salts, each with its naming and its behaviour. A learner meeting a new inorganic compound can first **place it in its class** on the scheme, and the class already predicts much of its behaviour.

## The key ideas

- The four main classes of inorganic compounds: **oxides, bases, acids, salts**.
- Armenia's scheme, printed on the cover: **metal → basic oxide → base** and **nonmetal → acidic oxide → acid**, the roads meeting in the **salts**.
- **Place first, study after**: the class of a compound already predicts much of its behaviour.
