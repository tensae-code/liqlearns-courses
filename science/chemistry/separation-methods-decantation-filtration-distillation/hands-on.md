# Hands-on — Separation methods — decantation, filtration, distillation and the rest

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Settle, pour, filter (kitchen materials only)
Stir a spoonful of soil into a jar of water. Leave it at rest and watch the solid settle (decantation getting ready). Pour the clearer liquid off gently into a second jar, leaving the sediment behind. Then pour that liquid through a coffee filter or a clean cloth into a third jar (filtration). Compare the three jars: what did each step take out, and what is still in the last jar?

## The magnet test (dry mixture)
Mix iron filings (or small iron nails/paper clips cut small — teacher's set) with dry sand on a tray. Wrap a magnet in a thin cloth or paper and pass it slowly through the mixture. The iron lifts out on the magnet; peel it off over a separate dish. Weigh or count before and after: did the magnet take anything that was not iron?

## Chromatography on paper (water-based pens only)
Draw a thick dot of black water-based ink near the bottom of a strip of filter or blotting paper. Stand the strip in a shallow dish of water so the water touches the paper just below the dot. Watch the water climb and the dot spread into bands. Mark each band's colour and how far it travelled — that is chromatography showing the ink's components.
