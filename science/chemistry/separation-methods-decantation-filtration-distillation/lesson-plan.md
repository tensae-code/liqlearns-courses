# Lesson plan — Separation methods — decantation, filtration, distillation and the rest

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Separation methods — decantation, filtration, distillation and the rest
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the seven procedures North Macedonia's Grade 7 book teaches as one full practical strand for separating the components of a mixture — decantation, filtration, sublimation, magnetic separation, distillation, crystallisation and chromatography — and the matching rule behind them: each method works because of a property that parts the components (settling and grain size; magnetism; subliming; boiling point; crystallising out; travelling speed through paper) (North Macedonia, Grade 7 — *Хемија за VII одделение во основно образование (Chemistry, Grade 7, primary)*, 1.4 Mixtures, printed p. 33)

## Sub lessons
1. **Decantation and filtration — the insoluble solid comes out** — Decantation: rest the mixture, the heavier solid settles, pour the liquid off gently — no apparatus, but never complete. Filtration finishes the job through a filter: filtrate through, residue held. Working order: settle first, then filter.
2. **Magnetic separation and sublimation — one special property does the work** — A magnet draws the one magnetic component (iron filings) out of a dry mixture untouched by anything else; a subliming solid turns straight to vapour on gentle heating and collects pure on a cool surface, leaving a non-subliming solid behind.
3. **Distillation, crystallisation and chromatography — for what is dissolved or mixed** — Distillation boils and condenses to recover a liquid and to part liquids by boiling point; crystallisation recovers the dissolved solid as crystals as the solvent leaves; chromatography separates dissolved components by the different speeds they travel through paper, showing a mixture's components as bands.

4. Name all seven procedures and match each to the kind of mixture it separates.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Tries to filter a dissolved solid out → no filter can hold what has dissolved; that mixture belongs to sub lesson 3 — distil the liquid off or crystallise the solid out.
