# Hands-on — Metalloids

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort the border
Write three headings on paper — Metal, Metalloid, Non-metal. From the lesson, place boron and silicon under Metalloid and say, for each, one metal-like and one non-metal-like fact the lesson gives. Then say the rule out loud: the side a metalloid shows depends on the conditions.

## Weigh the claim
Hold a steel spoon in one hand and a plastic ruler in the other. The book says boron fibres in plastic beat the spoon's material on strength and the ruler's family on lightness together — stronger than steel, lighter than aluminium. Say why that pair of wins is exactly what an aircraft body needs.

## Find silicon at home
Find one window (glass from silicon melted with CaO) and one electronic device (silicon the semiconductor). Point at each and say silicon's job in it, in one sentence each.
