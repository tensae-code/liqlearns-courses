# Photosynthesis — light reactions, Calvin cycle and hot-climate plants

## Do it first
Before reading, say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is: photosynthesis — light reactions, calvin cycle and hot-climate plants. Then read on — this is Italy's way of teaching it, as the Italy placements describe the book's own treatment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Modulo E, Capitolo 13 — Italy's book (Grade 13)

Photosynthesis in full: chloroplast structure against the mitochondrion (endosymbiotic theory), water splitting, the two photosystems and the light reactions (ATP, NADPH, O₂ by chemiosmotic photophosphorylation on the thylakoid membrane), the Calvin cycle with Rubisco and its energy balance, photorespiration, and the C3/C4/CAM adaptations to hot climates; photosynthesis set against respiration as opposite reactions and against the greenhouse effect.

## The key ideas

- The light reactions — two photosystems, water splitting, ATP, NADPH and O₂ by chemiosmotic photophosphorylation on the thylakoid membrane — feed the Calvin cycle, with Rubisco at its centre.
- Photorespiration is set against the Calvin cycle's energy balance.
- C3, C4 and CAM are the adaptations to hot climates; photosynthesis and respiration are set out as opposite reactions.
