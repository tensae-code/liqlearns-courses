# Hands-on — Ions — atoms that gain or lose electrons

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Charge accounting
Make two ledger cards, one for sodium and one for chlorine. Each card has three rows — positive charges, electrons, net charge — and two columns: atom, ion. Fill sodium's card from the lesson (11 / 11 / 0, then 11 / 10 / +1). For chlorine's card, fill the atom column as 'equal, net 0' and the ion column from the gain of one electron (net −1), and write Cl⁻ at the top. Check both cards against the sign rule.

## Sign-rule drill
On paper, work six short cases your teacher or partner invents ('an atom loses 2 electrons', 'an atom gains 1 electron', and so on). For each: state the ion's sign, the size of its charge, and whether the element changed. The last answer is always no — say why, each time, until it is automatic.
