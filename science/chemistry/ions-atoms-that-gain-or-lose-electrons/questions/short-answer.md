# Ions — atoms that gain or lose electrons — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Work sodium's case in full: counts before, the change, counts after, and the ion's written form.
**Answer: Before: 11 positive charges, 11 electrons, net charge 0. Change: the atom loses one electron. After: 11 positive charges, 10 electrons, net +1 — written Na⁺, a positive ion.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does losing a negatively charged electron make the particle positive, rather than negative?
**Answer: The positive charges stay the same (the nucleus is untouched) while one negative charge leaves. The positives now outnumber the negatives by one, so the particle's overall charge is positive by one.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A pupil says 'Na⁺ is a different element from sodium because it is charged.' Correct the pupil.
**Answer: Charge comes from the electron balance, not from the nucleus. Na⁺ still has sodium's nucleus (11 positive charges), so it is still sodium — a sodium ion, not a new element.**
