# Lesson plan — Domestic water treatment — and softening

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Domestic water treatment — and softening
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how water is made fit for the home — the large-scale treatment in order (flocculation with alum and sedimentation, filtration through gravel, sand and charcoal beds, chlorination at 5 ppm or with monochloramine), home treatment by fibre and carbon filters, chlorination and boiling, and the five softenings that take hardness out: boiling (temporary hardness only, leaving limescale), washing soda precipitating CaCO₃ from both kinds, ion exchange on a zeolite resin Na₂Z with brine regeneration, distillation (and the solar still), and reverse osmosis, the desalination workhorse for sea and brackish water
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Water hardness — temporary and permanent, its companion lesson, is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The town works — flocculate, filter, chlorinate** — Large-scale treatment in order: flocculation with alum and sedimentation, filtration through gravel/sand/charcoal beds, chlorination (5 ppm, or monochloramine).
2. **Treatment at home — filters, chlorine, boiling** — Home treatment by fibre and carbon filters, chlorination and boiling.
3. **The five softenings — taking the hardness out** — The five softenings: boiling (temporary only, limescale), washing soda for both kinds, ion exchange on Na₂Z with brine regeneration, distillation and the solar still, reverse osmosis for desalination.

## If the learner is stuck
- Shuffles the town treatment's order → the order is the logic: clump and settle the solids out first, filter what remains, and only then chlorinate water clear enough for the chlorine to work.
- Expects boiling to soften everything → boiling cures temporary hardness only and pays for it in limescale; permanent hardness needs the chemical softenings.
- Reads the five softenings as rivals → each answers a different situation: a kettle, a laundry, a plumbed house system, a laboratory still, a coastline's desalination.
