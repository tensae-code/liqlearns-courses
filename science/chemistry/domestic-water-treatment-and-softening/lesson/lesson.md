# Domestic water treatment — and softening

## Do it first
Before you read: river water arrives brown with grit, alive with microbes, and hard with dissolved rock — yet the town tap runs clear, safe and, in soft districts, kind to soap. Say out loud: what stations does that water pass through, and in what order? Then read on: this is Dominica's CSEC unit C21.2 — **the treatment of water for domestic purposes**, from the town works to the kitchen filter, and the five ways of softening it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The town works — flocculate, filter, chlorinate

Large-scale treatment runs in a fixed order, and the order is the logic. First, **flocculation with alum and sedimentation**: alum gathers the fine suspended matter into clumps heavy enough to sink, and the water rests in sedimentation tanks while they settle out. Second, **filtration through gravel, sand and charcoal beds**, which strain out what settling missed, the charcoal also taking tastes, smells and colours with it. Last, **chlorination** — chlorine at **5 ppm**, or **monochloramine** — kills the microbes in water now clear enough for the disinfectant to reach them. Reverse the order and each step fails the next: chlorine cannot disinfect mud, and filters choke on unsettled water.

## Sub lesson 2 — Treatment at home — filters, chlorine, boiling

The same three ideas shrink to the kitchen. **Fibre filters** strain out suspended solids as the town's beds did; **carbon filters** adsorb tastes, odours and some dissolved chemicals as the charcoal layer did. **Chlorination** at home scale disinfects stored water, and **boiling** — the oldest home treatment — kills microbes outright, needing no chemical and no apparatus beyond the pot. Dominica's book sets the home methods honestly beside the town's: a filter clarifies but does not disinfect, boiling disinfects but does not clarify, and the household that knows which job each tool does can combine them as the water demands.

## Sub lesson 3 — The five softenings — taking the hardness out

Softening is its own treatment, and the book gives **five** ways. (1) **Boiling** — cures **temporary hardness only**, precipitating the hydrogencarbonates and leaving the **limescale** behind in the vessel. (2) **Washing soda** — sodium carbonate precipitates CaCO₃ from **both kinds** of hardness (one ionic equation serves both), the laundry's classic answer. (3) **Ion exchange** — water passes over a **zeolite resin, Na₂Z**, which trades its sodium for the water's calcium and magnesium; when the resin is spent, **brine regenerates** it, loading it with sodium again. (4) **Distillation** — evaporate and condense, leaving every dissolved ion behind; the **solar still** runs the same cycle on sunlight for a household's drinking water. (5) **Reverse osmosis** — water forced through a membrane that passes water and holds back ions: the **desalination workhorse** that makes sea and brackish water drinkable where no river runs soft.

## The key ideas

- Town treatment, in order: **flocculation with alum + sedimentation**, then **filtration** through gravel/sand/charcoal, then **chlorination (5 ppm or monochloramine)** — each step makes the next one possible.
- Home treatment repeats the ideas small: **fibre filters** strain, **carbon filters** adsorb tastes and odours, **chlorination and boiling** disinfect — clarify and disinfect are different jobs.
- Five softenings: **boiling** (temporary hardness only, at the price of limescale); **washing soda** (precipitates CaCO₃ from both kinds); **ion exchange** on **Na₂Z**, regenerated with brine; **distillation** (and the solar still); **reverse osmosis**.
- **Reverse osmosis** is the desalination workhorse — the softening that answers seawater and brackish water.
