# Domestic water treatment — and softening — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must chlorination come last in the town works?
**Answer: Chlorine can only disinfect water that flocculation, sedimentation and filtration have already cleared — in muddy water it is wasted on the suspended matter and cannot reliably reach the microbes.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A filtered jug of river water looks crystal clear. Why does the lesson still refuse to call it treated for drinking?
**Answer: Filtering clarifies but does not disinfect — the microbes pass or remain; home treatment still needs chlorination or boiling to make the water safe.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How does the zeolite resin Na₂Z soften water, and how is it regenerated?
**Answer: The resin trades its sodium ions for the water's Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺, holding the hardness ions and releasing sodium (which does not harden); when spent, brine floods it with sodium again and carries the hardness ions away.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Which softening answers seawater, and by what means?
**Answer: Reverse osmosis — pressure forces the water through a membrane that passes water molecules and holds back the dissolved ions, desalinating sea and brackish water.**
