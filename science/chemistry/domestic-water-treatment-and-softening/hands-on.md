# Hands-on — Domestic water treatment — and softening

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## A filter bed in a bottle (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only)
Cut the bottom off a large plastic bottle and invert it as a funnel. Layer, from the neck up: a wad of clean cloth, fine sand (washed), coarse sand, then gravel — the town bed in miniature. Pour muddy water through slowly and collect it. Compare before and after against the light, then write the honest sentence the lesson demands: what has this water gained, and what treatment has it *not* had?

## Washing-soda witness (kitchen practical — home-safe materials only; teacher or parent present)
Take two jars of the hardest tap water you have. Stir half a teaspoon of washing soda into one and let both stand an hour. Decant and run the lesson's soap test on each (five drops of liquid soap, ten shakes): record the lather difference and name, in the lesson's terms, what precipitated out of the treated jar and which hardness kinds washing soda answers.

## The order defended (paper exercise)
Write the town treatment as a three-box flow line. Under each box, one line: what enters, what leaves, and what breaks if this box were moved to the end. Finish with the five softenings as a menu: beside each, the hardness kind it cures and one place it belongs.
