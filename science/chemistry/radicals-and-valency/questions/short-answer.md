# Radicals and valency — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What does a radical keep whole as it moves from one compound to another?
**Answer: Its group of atoms and its charge: the radical appears in every compound as the same group with the same formula and charge, behaving as one ion.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the six radicals of sub lesson 1 with their charges.
**Answer: Ammonium NH₄⁺ (+1), hydroxide OH⁻ (−1), nitrate NO₃⁻ (−1), carbonate CO₃²⁻ (−2), sulfate SO₄²⁻ (−2), phosphate PO₄³⁻ (−3).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How is a radical's valency found from its formula?
**Answer: The valency equals the size of the radical's charge: carbonate CO₃²⁻ has valency 2, phosphate PO₄³⁻ valency 3.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does sodium carbonate have the formula Na₂CO₃?
**Answer: Sodium's valency is 1 and the carbonate radical's is 2; the valencies must balance, so two sodium atoms join one carbonate radical: Na₂CO₃.**
