# Open, closed and isolated chemical systems

## Do it first
A kettle boils with its lid off; a sealed flask warms on a bench; a perfect thermos sits untouched. Before reading, say out loud: which of these can swap stuff with the room, which can swap only warmth, and which can swap nothing? Bulgaria's Grade 10 book frames every reaction this way — as a system against its surroundings.

## Sub lesson 1 — System and surroundings

Chemistry studies a **chemical system** — the substances taking part — set against its **surroundings**, everything else. The border between them decides what can pass. A system is also **homogeneous** when it is one phase throughout, or **heterogeneous** when phases meet at **phase boundaries** — two layers, a solid in a liquid, a gas over a liquid.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three kinds of system

An **open system** exchanges **both matter and energy** with its surroundings — methane burning in air is an open, homogeneous system. A **closed system** exchanges **energy only** — water being electrolysed in a sealed apparatus is Bulgaria's closed example. An **isolated system** exchanges **neither matter nor energy** — and the book states plainly that isolated systems **do not exist in reality**; they are the limiting idea the other two are measured against. Zinc dissolving in acid is the book's heterogeneous open system: two phases, and both gas and heat escape.

## The key ideas

- System vs surroundings: the border decides what can pass — matter, energy, both, or neither.
- Open = matter + energy; closed = energy only; isolated = neither (an ideal — none exist in reality).
- Homogeneous = one phase; heterogeneous = phases with boundaries. Bulgaria's examples: burning methane (open, homogeneous), electrolysis of water (closed), zinc in acid (open, heterogeneous).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion: system and surroundings; the three kinds of system — in that order, in your own words, with the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. A reaction runs in an open beaker and hydrogen escapes while the beaker warms. What kind of system is it? (Open — matter (the gas) and energy (the heat) both cross into the surroundings.)
2. Why can no real apparatus be a perfectly isolated system? (Some energy always leaks through real walls; isolated systems are an ideal limit, not a buildable thing.)
