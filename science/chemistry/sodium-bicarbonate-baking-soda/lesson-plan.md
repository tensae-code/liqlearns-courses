# Lesson plan — Sodium bicarbonate — baking soda

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Sodium bicarbonate — baking soda
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sodium hydrogencarbonate as Yemen's Grade 10 book teaches it: prepared in the laboratory by passing CO2 into cold caustic soda solution and met industrially as the Solvay intermediate, breaking down above 100 °C to sodium carbonate, CO2 and water, fizzing CO2 with acids — and at work in baking powder, leather tanning, fire extinguishers and medicine against stomach acidity
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Sodium carbonate — the Solvay process is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Making it — the laboratory way and the Solvay way** — In the laboratory, sodium hydrogencarbonate (sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO₃) is prepared by passing carbon dioxide into cold caustic soda solution. Industrially it is already familiar:…
2. **Its two giving-up reactions** — Heat it above 100 °C and it breaks down: 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O — the bicarbonate gives up its carbon dioxide and settles as the carbonate. Meet an acid and it fizzes CO₂ at …
3. **At work — the gas does the jobs** — Every use spends that gas. In baking powder, the released CO₂ raises the dough — bubbles of gas are the lift. In fire extinguishers the gas smothers the flame. In medicine against …
