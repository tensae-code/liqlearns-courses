| Unit 6 activities [teacher evidence] (Yemen, Grade 12) | Book 1 (Grade 12) [teacher evidence] — Unit 6 activities [teacher evidence] — printed pp. 61–64 — Five bench tests written only in this guide: Activity 8 distinguishes glucose from fructose with Benedict's or Fehling's solution in a water bath for five minutes — glucose throws a red precipitate quickly (it carries an aldehyde group) while fructose's appears slowly (a ketone group); Activity 10 distinguishes maltose from sucrose the same way — maltose reduces the reagent because one aldehyde group stays free in its molecule, sucrose does not react because its glucose aldehyde and fructose ketone groups are both consumed in the bond between the two units; Activity 9 tests urine for sugar with Benedict's solution across four samples and reads the result off a printed scale (no reaction — healthy; greenish tinge/precipitate at 0.1–0.5% — traces; yellow-orange at 1% — moderate diabetes; red-brown at 2% — severe), with urine's daily composition noted (about 40 g of organic matter and 20 g of inorganic salts); Activity 11 hydrolyses oils and fats in an alkaline ethanolic medium — 1 mL oil, 2 mL ethanol, 4 mL sodium hydroxide, five minutes' heating — then pours the tube into saturated brine, on which the fatty-acid soap floats (repeated with butter in place of oil), concluding with the ester chemistry (RCOOH + C2H5OH → RCOOC2H5 + H2O; RCOOH + NaOH → RCOONa + H2O); Activity 12 detects the three food classes themselves — carbohydrates: sugar + phenolphthalein turns pink, and concentrated sulfuric acid on the wall chars it black, the black being carbon; proteins: egg white + concentrated nitric acid gives the white coagulum, and adding ammonium or sodium hydroxide turns it orange (the xanthoproteic reaction); fats: cholesterol + concentrated sulfuric acid loses a water molecule from its first ring and gives the red double-bonded product cholesterylene. |
