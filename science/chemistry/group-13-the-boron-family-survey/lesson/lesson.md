# Group 13 — the boron family survey

## Do it first
Before you read: one family of the table begins with an element that is not a metal at all, and contains a metal that melts in your hand. Say out loud: what could hold such a family together — and what single number, counted on the atom, would you check first? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book opens the family the way it opens every family — with its data table.

## Sub lesson 1 — The family read off its table

The **third main group (IIIA, group 13)** — **B, Al, Ga, In, Tl** — is read off its data table: configurations **ns²np¹**, first ionisation energies, radii in picometres, melting points and electronegativities. Three outer electrons is the family's signature: one plan, five elements, and every property in the table to be read as that plan playing out at five sizes.

## Sub lesson 2 — The family's character — where it differs from IA and IIA

All are **metals except boron, the metalloid** at the head. Against the earlier families the book sets two contrasts: their **hydroxides will not dissolve in water**, unlike groups IA and IIA; and the oxides split the family — **boron's oxide is acidic, while alumina is amphoteric**. Down the group the **basic character grows steadily** — the head of the family leans acidic, its foot is a base-forming metal.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gallium in the hand — the table made visible

The discussion opens on a photograph: **gallium, melting in a hand**. A metal whose melting point sits below body temperature is the data table's most startling row made visible — the family's numbers are not abstractions, and the survey's habit is the book's habit throughout the main groups: table first, character from the table, then the exemplar element (aluminium) in depth.

## The key ideas

- Group 13 (IIIA): **B, Al, Ga, In, Tl — ns²np¹**; all metals **except boron the metalloid**.
- Their **hydroxides will not dissolve in water** (unlike IA/IIA); **boron's oxide is acidic, alumina amphoteric**; **basic character grows down the group**.
- **Gallium melts in a hand** — the data table's melting-point row made visible; the family is read **off its table** before its exemplar is studied.

## Do It Yourself
1. What single fact about the atom puts these five elements in one family? (Each holds three outer electrons in the pattern ns²np¹ — the shared outer configuration is the family bond, read straight off the book's data table.)
2. How does group 13 differ from groups IA and IIA in its hydroxides, and in its head element? (Its hydroxides will not dissolve in water, where IA/IIA hydroxides do; and its head, boron, is a metalloid, not a metal — with an acidic oxide, against amphoteric alumina below it.)
3. What happens to basic character down the group, and which element's photograph opens the discussion? (Basic character grows steadily down the group. Gallium — photographed melting in a hand — opens it.)
