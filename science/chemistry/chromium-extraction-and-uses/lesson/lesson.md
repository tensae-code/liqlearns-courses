# Chromium — extraction and uses

## Do it first
Before you read: the mirror-bright trim on a tap, and the steel of a sink that never rusts, both shine with the same metal. Say out loud: which metal gives stainless steel its shine and its refusal to rust, and how reactive do you expect such a metal to be to extract? Then read on: this is South Sudan's way in Secondary 4, Unit 4 §4.6 — chromium, the last of the unit's six metals.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chromium's ore — chromite

Chromium is won from **chromite, FeCr₂O₄** — iron chromium oxide, the one great ore of the metal. Chromium is a transition metal: hard, bright, and strongly resistant to corrosion because its surface seals itself with a thin, tough oxide layer. Its reactivity puts it beyond easy carbon reduction for the pure metal — Unit 4's rule again: the extraction must match the metal's grip on its compounds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Extraction — by way of the chromates, then reduction

The industrial road runs in stages. Chromite is roasted with sodium carbonate in air to form soluble **sodium chromate**, which is converted to **sodium dichromate**; from the dichromate, **chromium(III) oxide, Cr₂O₃**, is obtained. The oxide is then **reduced** — classically by **aluminium** in the thermite-style reduction (Cr₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Cr + Al₂O₃), aluminium's greater grip on oxygen doing the work carbon does for zinc and iron. Most chromium, though, is never isolated pure: iron and chromite are reduced together to **ferrochrome**, the chromium-iron alloy that feeds steel-making directly.

## Sub lesson 3 — Uses of chromium

Chromium's uses all spend its shine and its armour. **Stainless steel**: iron alloyed with chromium (and often nickel) — the surface oxide layer makes it rust-proof, for sinks, cutlery, tanks and surgical steel. **Chrome plating**: a thin bright chromium coat on steel and plastics — taps, trim and tools, hard and shining. **Pigments and protection**: chromium compounds colour paints (chrome yellow) and tan leather. The metal that resists corrosion is spent wherever corrosion must not win.

## The key ideas

- Ore: **chromite, FeCr₂O₄**.
- Road out: roast to sodium chromate → dichromate → **Cr₂O₃**, then **reduce with aluminium**; most chromium travels as **ferrochrome**.
- Chromium seals itself with a surface oxide layer — the root of its corrosion resistance.
- Chief uses: **stainless steel**, **chrome plating**, pigments.
