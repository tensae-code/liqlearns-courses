# Lesson plan — Molecules and chemical formulas

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Molecules and chemical formulas
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading a molecule from its chemical formula — H₂O, CO₂ and CH₄ counted atom by atom from molecular models, in the Djibouti Grade 8 guide — and sorting bodies into simple bodies (one kind of atom: H₂, N₂) and compound bodies (several kinds of atom) (teacher-guide evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Dalton's atomic theory is recommended first, never forced.
**Evidence standing:** this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Djibouti) — its sources name the guide and count it nowhere (acceptance 0).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **From models to formulas — what a molecule is** — A **molecule** is a small group of atoms joined together — the guide works with **molecular models**, spheres joined in the shapes of real molecules, each sphere one atom, its colour and symbol telling which element it is (the sphere model of the atom is set up in the same chapter, printed p.
2. **Counting the guide's three formulas** — **H₂O (water):** H with subscript 2, O with no subscript — **2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom**.
3. **Simple bodies and compound bodies** — The chapter then sorts bodies by what their molecules are made of.

## Before this lesson
