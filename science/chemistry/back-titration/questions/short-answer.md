# Back titration — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name two reasons a direct titration can fail.
**Answer: The substance may be insoluble (nothing clean to titrate against from the burette), or its reaction may be too slow to give a sharp end point — volatile or unstable substances fail the same way.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) State the back-titration method in two stages.
**Answer: Add a known, measured excess of a standard reagent and let the substance react with it completely; then titrate the leftover reagent with a second standard solution.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must the first reagent be in excess?
**Answer: If it were not in excess, some of the substance would never react and the measurement would read low; the excess guarantees the substance is fully consumed, and the leftover is what carries the information.**
