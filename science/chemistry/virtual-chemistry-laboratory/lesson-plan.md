# Lesson plan — Virtual chemistry laboratory

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Virtual chemistry laboratory
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** experiments done in a virtual laboratory, as Vietnam's Grade 10 study-topics book teaches them as a named practical (Topic 3, Bài 10): carrying out chemistry experiments on a computer — choosing reagents, running the change, observing and recording — where the bench, the chemicals or the hazard would otherwise keep the experiment out of reach
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Laboratory safety rules is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Experiments without a bench** — Vietnam's advanced Grade 10 book closes with a practicals-and-technology topic, and Bài 10 is a named practical of a new kind: **virtual chemistry experiments** (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
2. **Working a virtual experiment well** — A simulation rewards the same discipline as glassware: state what you are testing, change **one condition at a time**, observe and record exactly what the screen shows, and repeat before you conclude. Bài 10's practical is worked this way (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
3. **What the virtual bench cannot give** — Hands learn what eyes cannot: the weight of a flask, the control of a tap, the judgement of a colour change in real light, and the safety habits that only real chemicals teach. Vietnam's book places the virtual practical **inside** a practicals topic, alongside real bench work (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Laboratory safety rules**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Experiments without a bench.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Working a virtual experiment well.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: What the virtual bench cannot give.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit and a teacher present — never home chemicals; kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner answers the opening questions out loud before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's sub lessons, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the lesson's central object or set-up, drawn from the first sub lesson — the student names its parts before reading on.

3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson in their own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson told in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer and fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Treats the simulation as a film → return to sub lesson 1: the learner chooses reagents and conditions and the outcome answers those choices — it is an experiment, run by the learner.
