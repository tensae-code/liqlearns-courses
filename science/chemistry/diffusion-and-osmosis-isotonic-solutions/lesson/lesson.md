# Diffusion and osmosis — isotonic solutions

## Do it first
A drop of colour in still water slowly colours the whole glass, untouched. And a dried apricot in water swells back toward juicy. Before reading, say out loud: what is moving in each case — the colour, the water, or both? Bulgaria's Grade 10 solutions lesson separates the two movements and names the balance between them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diffusion, and osmosis through a membrane

**Diffusion** is particle movement **from higher to lower concentration until uniform** — Bulgaria's layered coloured solution left to mix; it runs **faster warm and stirred**. **Osmosis** is diffusion with a gatekeeper: a **semipermeable membrane passes only the solvent**, so the net flow runs **toward the concentrated side** and raises its level. The height of the stopping column measures the **osmotic pressure Π**, which depends on **temperature and particle count — not on the solute's nature**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Isotonic solutions and the body

A solution matching the body's own osmotic pressure is **isotonic**: **0.9% saline is isotonic with blood**. In a **hypotonic** (weaker) solution red blood cells **swell and burst**; in a **hypertonic** (stronger) one they **shrivel** — Bulgaria draws the cells one by one. The same osmosis swells dried fruits in water and lets plant roots draw soil water.

## The key ideas

- Diffusion: particles move high → low concentration until uniform; faster warm and stirred.
- Osmosis: only solvent crosses the semipermeable membrane, net flow toward the concentrated side; Π depends on temperature and particle count, not the solute's kind.
- Isotonic = same osmotic pressure as the reference (0.9% saline for blood). Hypotonic bursts red cells; hypertonic shrivels them.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does a dried fruit swell in water? (Osmosis: water (the solvent) crosses the fruit's skins toward the concentrated sugars inside, swelling the fruit.)
2. A red blood cell is placed in distilled water. Predict and explain. (It swells and bursts — distilled water is strongly hypotonic, so water flows into the cell toward the concentrated interior.)
