# Lesson plan — Iron and its three oxides

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Iron and its three oxides
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Iron and its three oxides in Egypt's Grade 12 student book (Chapter One, printed pp. 20–24): Iron's own chemistry: +2/+3 states only of importance, reactions with air, steam, chlorine, sulphur and acids (dilute acids give Fe(II) salts; concentrated nitric acid passivates), then FeO, Fe₂O₃ and Fe₃O₄ one by one — preparation (oxalate decomposition, hydroxide ignition, partial reduction), and Fe₃O₄ proven a mixed oxide because hot concentrated acid gives both Fe(II) and Fe(III) salts.

## Sub lessons
1. **The metal — two states that matter** — Iron's chemistry runs on **two oxidation states of importance, +2 and +3**. Egypt works the metal's reactions in order: with **air**, with **steam**, with **chlorine**, with **sulphur**, and with **acids** — where the acid decides the state: **dilute acids give Fe(II) salts**, while **concentrated nitric acid passivates** the metal, sealing it under a protective film so the reaction stops.
2. **The three oxides, one by one** — Then the oxides in turn: **FeO** (iron(II)), **Fe₂O₃** (iron(III)) and **Fe₃O₄** — each with its **preparation**: **oxalate decomposition, hydroxide ignition and partial reduction** are the book's named routes among them. Learned one by one, the three are three different substances with three different iron states, not shades of 'rust'.
3. **Fe₃O₄ — the mixed oxide, proven** — The chapter's proof: **Fe₃O₄ is a mixed oxide** — FeO and Fe₂O₃ in one lattice. The evidence is chemical: treated with **hot concentrated acid, it gives both Fe(II) and Fe(III) salts** — the two states come out of one powder, so both were in it. Egypt's closing move is the test itself: when a formula hides two states, let a reaction separate them and count what comes out.

## If the learner is stuck
- Fe₃O₄ assigned a fractional oxidation state as if real → it is the average of +2 and +3 held together; the acid test separates them. / Passivation read as no chemistry → a film forms and stops further attack; the stillness is a product, not an absence.
