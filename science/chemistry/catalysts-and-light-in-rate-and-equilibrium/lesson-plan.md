# Lesson plan — Catalysts and light — in rate and equilibrium

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Catalysts and light — in rate and equilibrium

## Sub lessons
1. **Catalysts — both directions equally** — A catalyst speeds a reaction by lowering its activation energy — but at equilibrium there are two reactions, forward and back, sharing the same catalyst. **The catalyst speeds both directions equally**, so the mixture **reaches equilibrium faster, but the equilibrium position does not move**: the same final mixture, sooner. That is the chapter's precise claim, and it is why a catalyst is never a way to get more product from an equilibrium.
2. **Enzymes and converters — the named applications** — Egypt names the working cases: **enzymes are biological catalysts** — the living world's own accelerators, each fitted to its reaction — and the **catalytic converter** is the car's application, speeding the cleaning of exhaust gases on a catalyst surface as they pass.
3. **Light — the rate factor with two proofs** — **Light** itself is a rate factor: some reactions run only, or far faster, when lit. The book's two proofs are **photosynthesis** — plants' chemistry driven by light — and the **silver bromide photographic film**, where light drives **Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag**, laying down the image in metallic silver where the light fell.
