# Valence bond theory and hybridisation

## Do it first
Carbon's ground state has only two unpaired electrons, yet carbon forms four equal bonds in methane. Before reading, say what the atom must do to its orbitals before bonding.

[Picture 21649 — Three hybrid sets: sp³, sp², sp.]

Valence bond theory: a covalent bond forms where atomic orbitals overlap, the shared pair sitting in the overlap.

A sigma (σ) bond is end-to-end (axial) overlap; a pi (π) bond is sideways overlap. The sigma bond is stronger — its overlap concentrates electron density between the nuclei (teacher guide evidence).

Hybridisation mixes an atom's orbitals before bonding: sp³ hybrids (tetrahedral, as in methane), sp² (trigonal planar, as in ethene) and sp (linear, as in ethyne). Promotion lifts an electron so enough orbitals stand ready to hybridise.

AlCl₃ is sp² while AlCl₄⁻ differs — hybridisation follows the actual bonded arrangement (teacher guide evidence).
