# Lesson plan — Variables in a fair test

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Variables in a fair test
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The three kinds of variable** — Every investigation is built from variables — the things that could change.
2. **Changing one thing at a time — the dissolving investigation** — The workbook's Experiment 1 hands the learner an unknown solid "A" and a question: what affects how fast it dissolves in water? The learner first **hypothesises** which variables could matter — particle size, temperature, stirring among them — then chooses ONE to test, holds all the others fixed, and times how long complete dissolution takes.
3. **Measuring, timing and presenting the result** — The measurement is the time for complete dissolution, taken the same way on every run; the workbook's sheet scaffolds the whole record — apparatus, materials, results table, and a **graph** of the investigated variable against time.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Confuses independent with dependent → anchor on the dissolving investigation: you CHOOSE the particle size (independent); you WATCH the dissolving time (dependent). Thinks controlling means ignoring → a controlled variable is actively held the same on every run, and the guide's list (temperature, stirring, amounts, identities, even brand/impurity and pH) shows how far that care goes.
