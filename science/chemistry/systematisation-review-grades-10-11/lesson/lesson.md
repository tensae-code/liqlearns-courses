# Systematisation — review of Grades 10 and 11

## Do it first
How should a two-year course end — with one more new topic, or with something else? Before reading, say out loud what Cuba's Grade 11 course does with its last twenty hours. Then read on: this lesson teaches Cuba's closing pattern itself, from its programme and its teachers' guide (teacher evidence).

## Sub lesson 1 — A closing unit, not new content — 20 hours of structured revision

The Grade 11 programme closes with a whole unit — **Unit 4, Sistematización de los contenidos de Química de décimo y onceno grados** — of **20 hours** whose content is the **structured revision and systematisation of two years of chemistry**. Nothing new is introduced; everything already taught is re-organised so it holds together as one system.

## Sub lesson 2 — What gets systematised — the two years in one frame

The unit's frame is named outright: **periodic law and the modern 18-column table**, the **main inorganic substance classes and their genetic relation**, **reaction classification**, **energy and rate**, **equilibrium constants and shifts**, and **quantitative problems on samples, reactions and solutions**. Two years, one frame.

## Sub lesson 3 — The guide's systematisation at work — predicting and classifying

The methodological guide works the same unit in detail (printed pp. 88–104): **periodic-property variation** with **predicting table position from electron distribution**, the principal substance classes revisited (water's chemical structure from Grade 10 among them), **reaction classification by oxidation-number change** with oxidising and reducing agents identified, and **equilibrium constants for homogeneous and heterogeneous, molecular and ionic systems**, closing with the unit's problem classes on substance samples.

## The key ideas

- Cuba closes Grade 11 with a **20-hour systematisation unit** — structured revision of Grades 10 and 11, not new content.
- The frame: **periodic law and the 18-column table, substance classes and their genetic relation, reaction classification, energy and rate, equilibrium** — plus quantitative problems.
- The guide's working moves: **predict position from electron distribution, classify reactions by oxidation-number change, and carry equilibrium constants across every system kind**.
