# Catalysis and enzymes

## Do it first
Before you read: some reactions crawl for years, then a pinch of something — itself unchanged at the end — makes them run. Say out loud: is that pinch a reactant, a product, or something else entirely? Then read on: Mauritania's terminal book names the something else and fences what it is not.

## Sub lesson 1 — The catalyst — present, working, absent from the bilan

The Chimie 7AS kinetics part closes (Ch. VI, printed p. 95) with catalysis: a catalyst speeds both formation and disappearance in a reaction while appearing in no bilan equation — it is not consumed into the overall change — and it acts in tiny quantity. Small amount, no place in the bilan, speed transformed: those are the catalyst's three marks.

## Sub lesson 2 — Homogeneous and heterogeneous — same phase or not

Catalysis is sorted by phase: homogeneous, where catalyst and reaction share one phase, against heterogeneous, where they do not — the catalyst a separate phase, classically a solid surface under gases or liquids. The sorting question is one: is the catalyst in the same phase as the reacting mixture, or apart from it?

## Sub lesson 3 — Enzymes — and what a catalyst is not

The book's biological case: enzymes are catalysts made by living bodies — protein catalysts, each fitted to its reaction. And the chapter fences the idea with a denial: light is explicitly ruled out as a catalyst. Light can drive a reaction as a kinetic factor, but it is not a catalyst — the catalyst's marks (present in the mixture, unchanged, absent from the bilan) are the test, and light fails it.

## The key ideas

- A catalyst speeds formation and disappearance, appears in no bilan equation, and acts in tiny quantity.
- Homogeneous catalysis: catalyst and mixture in one phase; heterogeneous: in different phases.
- Enzymes are biological protein catalysts; light is NOT a catalyst, however much it speeds a reaction.

## Do It Yourself
1. A solid speeds a gas reaction and is recovered unchanged. Which catalysis is this, and why? (Heterogeneous — the catalyst is in a different phase (solid) from the reacting mixture (gas), and it is recovered unchanged, as a catalyst must be.)
2. Why does the catalyst appear in no bilan equation? (It is not consumed by the overall reaction — it takes part and is restored, so the overall (bilan) equation, which counts only net change, has no place for it.)
3. A pupil lists light among the catalysts of a photochemical reaction. Correct them with the book's fence. (The 7AS book rules light out as a catalyst: light is a kinetic factor that can drive a reaction, but it is not a substance present in the mixture, unchanged and absent from the bilan — the catalyst's marks.)
