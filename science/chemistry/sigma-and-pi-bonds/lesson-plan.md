## Sub lessons
1. **Sigma — the head-on bond** — A **sigma (σ) bond** forms by **head-on overlap** of orbitals along the line between the two nuclei. The overlap is direct and deep, the electron density sits between the nuclei, and the bond is strong. **Every single bond is a sigma bond** — it is the first bond made between any two bonded atoms.
2. **Pi — the sideways bond** — A **pi (π) bond** forms by **sideways overlap of pure p orbitals** — the lobes meet above and below the line between the nuclei, not on it. The overlap is shallower, so **the pi bond is the weaker, easier-broken partner**. A pi bond can only exist where a sigma bond already holds the atoms at the right distance.
3. **Counting bonds in multiples** — The rule Egypt drills: **a single bond is one sigma; a double bond is one sigma + one pi; a triple bond is one sigma + two pi**. That is why double and triple bonds are stronger than single ones overall, yet react at the pi part — the weak pi bond breaks while the sigma frame survives, which is the whole basis of addition reactions.
