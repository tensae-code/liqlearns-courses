# Acid–base neutralisation and equivalence

## Do it first
Before you read: pour a base slowly into an acid and the acidity fades — but at one exact volume it is exactly gone, neither acid nor base in excess. Say out loud: how would you know that volume without tasting, and how would you compute it before you pour? Then read on: Mauritania's 5AS book doses to that point and names it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Neutralisation — acid against base

The Chimie 5AS book closes its acids-and-bases chapter with the neutralisation acido-basique (Ch. 5 §V, printed p. 90): an acid dosed against a base — hydrochloric acid against sodium hydroxide is the book's working pair. As base is added, the acid is consumed by the reaction; the mixture moves toward neutrality point by point, and past the end-point the added base is simply in excess.

## Sub lesson 2 — The equivalence point — the exact meeting

One volume is special: the equivalence point, where the acid and base stand in exactly the proportions of the reaction equation — neither in excess, the mixture neutral. Everything in a dosing is organised around finding that point: in this chapter by the colour change of the work, the point where Ca·Va = Cb·Vb holds.

## Sub lesson 3 — The equivalence calculation — Ca·Va = Cb·Vb

At equivalence the amounts match the equation, and for the book's acid–base pair that match is written Ca·Va = Cb·Vb: concentration of acid × its volume = concentration of base × its volume. Know any three and the fourth follows — the volume of base a stated acid will need, or the unknown concentration once its equivalence volume is measured. That is the calculation the whole dosing serves.

## The key ideas

- Neutralisation: an acid dosed against a base is consumed toward neutrality; past equivalence the added partner is in excess.
- Equivalence: the exact meeting point — acid and base in the reaction's proportions, the mixture neutral.
- At equivalence, Ca·Va = Cb·Vb — three knowns give the fourth.

## Do It Yourself
1. 25.0 mL of 0.10 mol/L NaOH exactly neutralises 20.0 mL of an HCl solution. Find the acid's concentration. (At equivalence Ca·Va = Cb·Vb: Ca = (0.10 × 25.0)/20.0 = 0.125 mol/L.)
2. What volume of 0.20 mol/L HCl neutralises 30.0 mL of 0.10 mol/L NaOH? (Va = Cb·Vb/Ca = (0.10 × 30.0)/0.20 = 15.0 mL.)
3. A pupil keeps adding base well past the colour change and still calls it equivalence. Correct them. (Equivalence is the one exact volume where acid and base match the equation's proportions; past it the base is in excess and the mixture is basic, not equivalent.)
