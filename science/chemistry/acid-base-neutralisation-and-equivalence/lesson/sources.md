| Capitolo 17, §9–10 (Italy, Grade 9–10) — placements slug recorded verbatim: chemistry/neutralisation-titration-and-salt-hydrolysis (stocked here, the existing lesson covering this thing) | Book 3 (Grade 9–10), Capitolo 17, §9–10 — printed pp. 400–402 — Neutralisation is the reaction between an acid and a basic solution in stoichiometrically equivalent quantities — when the H+ ions of the acid equal the OH− ions of the base the two solutions cancel each other's properties, producing water, heat and a salt that stays dissolved (HNO3 + NaOH written ionically reduces, spectators cancelled, to H+(aq) + OH−(aq) → H2O(l) + calore — the same result whatever the acid and base, as the jellyfish-sting note illustrates: the skin's acidic toxins are neutralised with sodium hydrogen carbonate); titration (titolazione acido-base) turns it into quantitative analysis: the unknown solution (titolo) receives progressive small additions of the known one (titolante) until an indicator signals the equivalence point (punto equivalente), where the moles of acid equal the moles of base — the strong-acid/strong-base titration photographed in four frames (acid excess, near the point, the bromothymol-blue turn, base excess) — worked: 100 mL of HCl needed 30 mL of 0.10 M NaOH, so n(OH−) = n(H+) = 3.0·10⁻³ mol and the acid was 0.030 M; salts then surprise: NH4Cl solution is acidic, KCl neutral, KNO2 basic (the three tubes photographed with indicator) — hydrolysis (idrolisi) is the reaction between the dissolved salt's ions and water molecules: ions from strong acids and bases are neutral (Tabella 17.7 — anions Cl−, Br−, I−, NO3−, ClO4−; cations Na+, K+, Li+, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Ba²⁺ — they behave as spectators), anions of weak acids act as bases (NO2− + H2O ⇌ HNO2 + OH−, raising the pH; likewise CH3COO−, CO3²⁻, SO3²⁻), the ammonium ion acts as an acid (NH4+ + H2O ⇌ NH3 + H3O+, lowering it), and metal cations such as Zn²⁺ and Fe³⁺, which hold no H+, instead bind water's OH− (Fe³⁺(aq) + H2O(l) ⇌ FeOH²⁺(aq) + H+(aq)) — the smaller and more highly charged the metal ion (Al³⁺), the stronger this acidity, reaching pH 1–2, comparable to hydrochloric acid. |
