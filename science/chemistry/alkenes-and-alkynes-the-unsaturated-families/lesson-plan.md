# Lesson plan — Alkenes and alkynes — the unsaturated families

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Alkenes and alkynes — the unsaturated families
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the unsaturated hydrocarbon families introduced the Syrian way: the alkenes (one double bond, CₙH₂ₙ, -ene names) and the alkynes (one triple bond, CₙH₂ₙ₋₂, -yne names), each read off its models and met in its uses (Syria, Grade 9 — *الفيزياء والكيمياء للصف التاسع الأساسي (Physics & Chemistry, Grade 9 Basic)*, U5 L4, printed pp. 163–165)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

## Sub lessons
1. **The alkenes — one double bond** — Syria builds **models of ethene and propene** and reads the family off them (printed p. 163): the **alkenes (olefins)** carry **one double bond** in each molecule, follow the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ**, and take names ending **-ene**. The double bond is what makes them **unsaturated** — not every carbon bond is single.
2. **The alkynes — one triple bond** — Two pages on (printed p. 165), models of **ethyne and propyne** give the second family: the **alkynes (acetylenes)** carry **one triple bond**, follow the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ₋₂**, and take names ending **-yne**.
3. **Ethyne at work — the welding flame** — The book closes the family with its working member: **ethyne's complete combustion releases 1255 kJ per mole** — the heat behind the **oxy-acetylene welding flame that melts most industrial metals**.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes -ene and -yne up → -ene goes with the double bond (alkenes, CₙH₂ₙ); -yne with the triple (alkynes, CₙH₂ₙ₋₂) — the ending announces the bond.
- Thinks 'unsaturated' describes the liquid → it describes the BONDS: not all carbon bonds are single — a double or triple bond stands in the molecule.
- Counts ethyne as an alkene → ethyne carries a TRIPLE bond — it heads the alkynes, however small the molecule.
