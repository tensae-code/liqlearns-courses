# Alkenes and alkynes — the unsaturated families — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Contrast the two families on bond, formula and ending.
**Answer: Alkenes: one double bond, CₙH₂ₙ, -ene names. Alkynes: one triple bond, CₙH₂ₙ₋₂, -yne names — each read off the families' models, ethene and propene for the alkenes, ethyne and propyne for the alkynes.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What three uses does the book give ethene?
**Answer: Plastics, polyester fibres, and speeding the ripening of fruit in closed stores.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the triple-bond family hold two fewer hydrogens than the double-bond family at the same n?
**Answer: Each extra shared pair between the two carbons takes the place of two carbon–hydrogen bonds: single CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, double CₙH₂ₙ, triple CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ — two hydrogens lost per extra pair.**
