# Alkenes and alkynes — the unsaturated families

## Do it first
Before you read: alkanes carry single bonds only. Say out loud: what changes in a hydrocarbon family when one bond in the molecule is double — or triple? Then read on: Syria's Grade 9 book builds the two unsaturated families from models, on printed pp. 163–165.

## Sub lesson 1 — The alkenes — one double bond

Syria builds **models of ethene and propene** and reads the family off them (printed p. 163): the **alkenes (olefins)** carry **one double bond** in each molecule, follow the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ**, and take names ending **-ene**. The double bond is what makes them **unsaturated** — not every carbon bond is single.

The family's first member is put to work at once: **ethene goes to plastics and polyester fibres, and speeds the ripening of fruit in closed stores** — one gas, met in a factory, a wardrobe and a fruit store.

## Sub lesson 2 — The alkynes — one triple bond

Two pages on (printed p. 165), models of **ethyne and propyne** give the second family: the **alkynes (acetylenes)** carry **one triple bond**, follow the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ₋₂**, and take names ending **-yne**.

Set the three families side by side and the pattern speaks: single bonds only — CₙH₂ₙ₊₂; one double — CₙH₂ₙ; one triple — CₙH₂ₙ₋₂. Each extra shared pair between the carbons costs the molecule two hydrogens.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ethyne at work — the welding flame

The book closes the family with its working member: **ethyne's complete combustion releases 1255 kJ per mole** — the heat behind the **oxy-acetylene welding flame that melts most industrial metals**.

The triple bond is stored work: the same bond kind that defines the family on paper is what makes its first member a welder's tool in the hand.

## The key ideas

- **Alkenes (olefins)**: one **double bond**, general formula **CₙH₂ₙ**, **-ene** names (printed p. 163); ethene → plastics, polyester fibres, fruit ripening.
- **Alkynes (acetylenes)**: one **triple bond**, general formula **CₙH₂ₙ₋₂**, **-yne** names (printed p. 165).
- **Ethyne's complete combustion releases 1255 kJ/mol** — the oxy-acetylene welding flame.
