# Electrochemistry review — including fuel cells

## Do it first
Hydrogen and oxygen, kept apart by a membrane, make electricity and pure water — no flame. Before the review, say which cell family that belongs to, and what its ΔG° sign must be.

[Picture 21747 — A hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell.]

Unit 2 consolidates: electrolytic cells consume electricity to force change; galvanic cells produce it from spontaneous change; potentials rank the push; Faraday's laws do the bookkeeping.

The review's fuel-cell questions run a hydrogen–oxygen cell through ΔG° and E° — a galvanic cell fed continuously with fuel.

This lesson is the unit's summary, checklist and review exercise, worked.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Electrochemistry review — including fuel cells to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.
