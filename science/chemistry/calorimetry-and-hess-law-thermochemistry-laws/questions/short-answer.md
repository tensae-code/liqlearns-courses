# Calorimetry and Hess's law — the laws of thermochemistry — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the two assumptions Greece's calorimetry treatment states aloud.
**Answer: That no heat is lost — the insulation is treated as perfect — and that the solution behaves as water, taking water's specific heat capacity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is Hess's law sometimes the only way to a ΔH?
**Answer: Some reactions are too slow, too violent or too tangled to run in a calorimeter; Hess's law lets their ΔH be built from a cycle of reactions that can be measured.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Forming water from its elements has ΔH = −286 kJ mol⁻¹. State the ΔH for splitting water back into its elements, and the law you used.
**Answer: +286 kJ mol⁻¹ — by the Lavoisier–Laplace law, reversing the reaction reverses the sign of its heat.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) In a Hess cycle, a step is used backwards and another is doubled. What must be done to their ΔH values?
**Answer: The reversed step's ΔH flips sign; the doubled step's ΔH doubles — the bookkeeping must follow the equation exactly, or the cycle's sum is worthless.**
