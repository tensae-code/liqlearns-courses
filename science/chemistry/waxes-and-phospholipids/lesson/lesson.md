# Waxes and phospholipids

## Do it first
Before you read: a leaf sheds rain, a bee's comb sheds honey's water, and every living cell is wrapped in a skin only two molecules thick. Say out loud: what kind of substance could waterproof a leaf and also build a cell's wrapping? Then read on: Kuwait's Grade 12 book closes its lipids lesson with the two answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Waxes — the waterproof esters

**Waxes** are **esters of long-chain fatty acids with long-chain monohydric alcohols** (§5–6, printed p. 85) — where a fat joins its fatty acids to glycerol, a wax joins one fatty acid to one long-chain alcohol. The book's example is **beeswax, built from myricyl alcohol and palmitic acid**. Their work in nature is waterproofing: wax **coats plant leaves and animal fur against water**, a water-shedding skin of ester.

## Sub lesson 2 — Phospholipids — the membrane builders

**Phospholipids** are **glycerol esters carrying two fatty acids and a phosphoric acid**. That one change from the triglyceride pattern — a phosphate in place of the third fatty acid — gives the molecule its life's work: phospholipids **form the membranes of living cells**, the wrapping every cell is built inside.

## Sub lesson 3 — Head and tail — why a membrane can exist

Kuwait's enrichment note gives the phospholipid its two ends: a **polar head — water-loving** — the phosphate end, and a **non-polar tail — water-fearing** — the fatty-acid chains. A molecule with a water-loving head and water-fearing tails is exactly what a membrane needs: set in water, the heads face the water and the tails hide from it, and the molecules line up into the sheet that wraps the cell.

## The key ideas

- **Waxes**: esters of **long-chain fatty acids with long-chain monohydric alcohols** — beeswax from **myricyl alcohol and palmitic acid** — coating **leaves and fur against water**.
- **Phospholipids**: glycerol esters with **two fatty acids and a phosphoric acid**, forming the **membranes of living cells**.
- A phospholipid carries a **polar, water-loving head** and a **non-polar, water-fearing tail** — the two ends that let it line up into a membrane.
