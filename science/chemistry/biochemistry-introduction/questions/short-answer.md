# Biochemistry — an introduction — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How does biochemistry differ from biology itself?
**Answer: Biology studies living things as wholes — their kinds, parts and lives; biochemistry studies the substances and chemical reactions inside them.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the four big families of living matter and give each one's chief job.
**Answer: Carbohydrates — fuels and short-term stores; proteins — builders and workers; lipids (fats and oils) — long-term stores and membranes; nucleic acids — the stored instructions for building proteins.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Which family do enzymes belong to, and which family holds the instructions for making them?
**Answer: Enzymes are proteins; the instructions for making them are held in the nucleic acids (DNA).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why would the cell's reactions be too slow for life without enzymes?
**Answer: In a flask most of them run far too slowly at body conditions; each enzyme speeds its own particular step, again and again without being used up, so the ordered chains can keep life going.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Give one breaking-down chain and one building-up chain from the lesson.
**Answer: Breaking down: food molecules — starch first in digestion — dismantled step by step to glucose and on to release energy. Building up: small molecules joined into big ones, such as amino acids into proteins or glucose into starch.**
