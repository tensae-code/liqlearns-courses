# Lesson plan — Biochemistry — an introduction

## Sub lessons
1. **What biochemistry is** — Biochemistry is the chemistry of living things: the study of the substances living bodies are made of and of the chemical changes that keep them alive — building, storing, releasing energy and repairing. It is where the organic chemistry of the Grade 10 course arrives at life itself, which is why Cambodia's book closes its organic chapter — and the whole Grade 10 course — with it.
2. **The big molecules of life** — Living matter is built chiefly from four families of large carbon compounds: carbohydrates (the fuels and stores), proteins (the builders and workers, themselves built from amino acids), lipids — fats and oils (the long-term stores and membranes), and nucleic acids (the stored instructions). Each family is a chemistry already met in this course, now seen at work inside a living body.
3. **Chemistry that keeps life going** — Inside every cell, chemical reactions run in ordered chains: food molecules are broken down step by step to release energy, and small molecules are built up into the big ones the body needs. Enzymes — the body's own protein catalysts — make each step fast enough and specific enough for life. Biochemistry is the map of those chains.

- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
