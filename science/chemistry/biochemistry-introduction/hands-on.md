# Hands-on — Biochemistry — an introduction

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort the families
Write these names on eight cards: glucose, starch, an enzyme, body fat, DNA, muscle protein, table sugar, cell-membrane lipid. Sort the cards into the four big families of sub lesson 2 — carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids — saying out loud for each card what job it does in a living body. Then check your piles against sub lesson 2.

## Trace one mouthful
On paper, draw the path of one mouthful of rice: the starch it carries, digestion breaking it down step by step, the glucose it gives, and the energy released when the body uses that glucose. Label each step with the family it belongs to and mark where enzymes do the work.

## Two columns — building up and breaking down (kitchen observation only)
List three changes in daily life where living chemistry breaks something down (food digesting, milk souring, fruit ripening) and three where it builds something up (a plant growing, a cut healing, dough rising with yeast). Beside each, write which big family does the main work.
