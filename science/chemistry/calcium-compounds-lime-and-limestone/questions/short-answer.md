# Calcium compounds — lime and limestone — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is limestone made from, and what is its formula?
**Answer: Limestone is calcium carbonate, CaCO₃, a rock laid down from the shells and skeletons of ancient sea creatures.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Write the two equations that join limestone to quicklime and quicklime to slaked lime, and say what each step takes in or drives off.
**Answer: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ — strong heat drives carbon dioxide off limestone, leaving quicklime. CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ — quicklime takes in water, giving out heat, to form slaked lime.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How would you tell quicklime from slaked lime with a drop of water?
**Answer: Quicklime reacts fiercely with water — hissing, steaming and giving out much heat as it slakes; slaked lime is already the product of that change and only mixes quietly.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a farmer spread slaked lime on a field?
**Answer: Slaked lime is an alkali; spread on an acidic soil it neutralises the acid — sweetening the field — and frees the soil's nutrients for the crop.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) In what sense does set mortar close the family's circle?
**Answer: Mortar's slaked lime slowly takes carbon dioxide back from the air and becomes calcium carbonate — limestone's own compound — again: rock to lime and back to rock.**
