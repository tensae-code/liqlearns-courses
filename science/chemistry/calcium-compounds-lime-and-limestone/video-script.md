Today: **Calcium compounds — lime and limestone** — Cambodia's way: one rock, heated and wetted, becomes an industry.

Start with limestone — calcium carbonate, a white rock made of shells from an ancient sea. Put a drop of dilute acid on it and it fizzes: that fizz is carbon dioxide, and it is the rock's signature.
Now heat limestone hard. It breaks apart: the carbon dioxide drives off and what is left is quicklime, calcium oxide — a fierce, thirsty solid. Drop water on quicklime and it hisses and steams as it slakes into slaked lime, calcium hydroxide.
And slaked lime goes everywhere: spread on sour fields to sweeten the soil, mixed into mortar that hardens as it slowly takes carbon dioxide back from the air — and becomes limestone again. One family, one circle: rock, lime, and back to rock.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
