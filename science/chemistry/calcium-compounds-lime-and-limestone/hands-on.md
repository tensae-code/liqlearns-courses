# Hands-on — Calcium compounds — lime and limestone

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Run the lime cycle on paper
Draw four boxes in a circle: limestone CaCO₃ → quicklime CaO → slaked lime Ca(OH)₂ → mortar set hard (CaCO₃ again). On each arrow write what is added or driven off — strong heat drives off CO₂; water is taken in with heat given out; slow taking-in of CO₂ from the air sets the mortar. Then run the circle backwards in words, saying what each step would need.

## Match the compound to the job
Write these jobs on cards: sweetening an acidic field, making mortar, whitewashing a wall, feeding a cement kiln, treating drinking water, flux in the blast furnace. Beside each card write which member of the family does it — limestone, quicklime or slaked lime — and check your matching against sub lesson 3.

## Fizz test (teacher-led, school laboratory only)
With a teacher present and safety kit worn: place a small limestone chip in dilute acid and watch for the fizz of carbon dioxide; pass the gas toward limewater and watch it turn milky. Write what each observation proves, from sub lessons 1 and 2. Never home chemicals — this practical is laboratory-only.
