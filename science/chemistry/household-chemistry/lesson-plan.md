# Lesson plan — Household chemistry

**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)

## Sub lessons
1. **Soaps — the oldest household chemical** — **Soap** is the home's oldest cleaning chemical, and Fiji's strand starts the household family with it.
2. **Detergents — made for the wash** — **Detergents** are the manufactured cleaning chemicals of the home: built, rather than rendered from fats, to do the washing jobs soap does — and to keep doing them where soap struggles, as in water carrying dissolved minerals (hard water), where soap loses its cleaning power and leaves scum behind.

Fiji's strand names soaps and detergents side by side because the home uses both and they are not the same thing: soap is the traditional fat-and-alkali product; a detergent is a designed cleaning agent, sold for a set job — laundry, dishes — with its strength and its directions printed on the packet.
3. **Bleaches and cleaners — the strong end of the cupboard** — **Bleaches** are the strong end of household chemistry: chemicals that attack colour itself, whitening cloth and killing germs on surfaces — which is why a bleach is also the household chemical that demands the most respect.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks soap and detergent are two names for one thing → soap is the traditional fat-and-alkali cleaner; a detergent is a manufactured cleaning agent designed for a set job, still working where soap struggles, as in hard water.
- Thinks bleach is just a stronger soap → bleach does a different job: it attacks colour itself and kills germs; soaps and detergents lift grease and dirt away.
- Forgets the safety rule → it is part of the strand's chemistry: never mix household cleaners, above all anything containing bleach — the mixture can release poisonous gases.
