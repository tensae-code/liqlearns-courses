# Household chemistry — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name Fiji's household family and say where the book teaches it.
**Answer: Soaps, detergents, bleaches and cleaners — taught as Strand 5.3 Household Chemistry, the closing strand of the Year 12 book's Consumer Chemistry theme, at printed p. 177.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) How does soap clean, in one or two sentences?
**Answer: Soap works at the boundary between grease and water, surrounding the grease so rinse water can lift it away — water alone cannot carry grease off.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How does a detergent differ from a soap?
**Answer: A detergent is a manufactured cleaning agent designed for a set job; soap is the traditional fat-and-alkali product. Detergents keep cleaning where soap struggles, as in hard water.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is bleach called the strong end of the cupboard?
**Answer: Because it attacks colour itself — whitening cloth and killing germs on surfaces — a stronger, more dangerous action than lifting dirt, so it demands the most care.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) State the strand's safety rule and the reason behind it.
**Answer: Use household cleaners one at a time, exactly as the label directs, and never mix them — mixing cleaners, above all with bleach, can release poisonous gases.**
