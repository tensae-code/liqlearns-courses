# Hands-on — Priority of organic functional groups

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Rank the groups (paper exercise)
Write the families you have met in this course on cards — carboxylic acids, esters, amides, acyl chlorides, aldehydes, ketones, alcohols, amines, alkenes, alkyl halides and the rest your summary table holds. Pin the two poles first, exactly as Fiji's book fixes them: carboxylic acids at the top, alkyl halides at the bottom. Arrange the rest between them as your course's table gives them, and say the rule the arrangement encodes.

## Suffix or prefix? (paper exercise)
For each pair, write which group takes the suffix and which becomes a prefix, with the reason in one line: (1) a carboxylic acid group with a halogen; (2) a halogen with any other family group present; (3) a carboxylic acid group with an alcohol group. Use only the two poles and the rule — highest takes the suffix, the rest are prefixes.

## Run the method (paper exercise)
Take a molecule described in words: a chain carrying a carboxylic acid group at one end and a chlorine atom on a middle carbon. Run Fiji's five steps in writing — list the groups, rank them, assign the suffix, assign the prefix, assemble the name's shape (acid ending, chloro- as prefix) — and say what the finished name tells a reader about the molecule.
