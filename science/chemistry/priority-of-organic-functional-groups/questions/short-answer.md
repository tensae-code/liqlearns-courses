# Priority of organic functional groups — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What are the two halves of the priority naming rule?
**Answer: The highest-priority group present takes the suffix and decides the name's family ending; every other group present is named as a prefix in front of the name.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the order be fixed and the same for everyone?
**Answer: So a name is unambiguous: two chemists naming the same molecule by the same order arrive at the same name, and either can rebuild the molecule from it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A molecule contains a carboxylic acid group and a chlorine atom. How is it named, in outline?
**Answer: The carboxylic acid group, being highest, takes the suffix — the name ends as an acid; the chlorine, from the lowest family, is named as a prefix (chloro-) in front.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What two things does Fiji's summary table give for every family?
**Answer: Each family's general formula — by which the group is recognised in a structure — and the ending it takes when it wins the suffix.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) List the steps of the worked-example method in order.
**Answer: List every functional group present; rank them by the priority order; give the highest group's ending as the suffix; name each remaining group as a prefix; assemble the name.**
