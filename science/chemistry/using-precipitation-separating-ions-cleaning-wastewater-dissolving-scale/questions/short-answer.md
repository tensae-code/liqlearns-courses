# Using precipitation — separating ions, cleaning wastewater, dissolving scale — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** State one thing China's book teaches in this lesson — point 1 of the key ideas.
**Answer: Crude salt still holds SO₄²⁻, Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ after filtering: learners plan and then perform the precipitation sequence (excess BaCl₂, then NaOH, then Na₂CO₃ — which also removes the added Ba²⁺), filter, neutralise with HCl and evaporate, debating reagent order and why each excess must itself be removed.**

**2.** State one thing China's book teaches in this lesson — point 2 of the key ideas.
**Answer: Precipitation as a tool: add a reagent to precipitate Fe³⁺ from industrial waste by pH control, or Na₂S to drop Cu²⁺/Hg²⁺ as sulfides (chemical-precipitation wastewater flow diagram); precipitation dissolved again by removing an ion (CaCO₃ scale in acid — and boiler-scale chemistry), with the design rule: choose the reagent that leaves the least residual ion and adds nothing worse.**
