# Using precipitation — separating ions, cleaning wastewater, dissolving scale

## Do it first
Before reading, say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is: using precipitation separating ions cleaning wastewater dissolving scale. Then read on — this is China's way of teaching it, as the China placements describe the book's own treatment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 5 §1 discussion + Experiment Activity 4 — China's book (China, Senior High (Compulsory Volume 2))

Crude salt still holds SO₄²⁻, Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ after filtering: learners plan and then perform the precipitation sequence (excess BaCl₂, then NaOH, then Na₂CO₃ — which also removes the added Ba²⁺), filter, neutralise with HCl and evaporate, debating reagent order and why each excess must itself be removed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 3 §4 — China's book (China, Senior High (Selective Compulsory 1 — Principles of Chemical Reactions))

Precipitation as a tool: add a reagent to precipitate Fe³⁺ from industrial waste by pH control, or Na₂S to drop Cu²⁺/Hg²⁺ as sulfides (chemical-precipitation wastewater flow diagram); precipitation dissolved again by removing an ion (CaCO₃ scale in acid — and boiler-scale chemistry), with the design rule: choose the reagent that leaves the least residual ion and adds nothing worse.
