# Hands-on — Separating mixtures — filtration, evaporation and distillation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Settle, pour, filter (kitchen materials)
Stir a spoon of clean sand into a glass of water. Leave the glass at rest for five minutes and look: what settled, and what does the liquid above look like? Pour the liquid gently into a second glass, trying to leave every grain behind — note how the last of the liquid resists. Then fold a coffee filter or a clean cloth over a third glass, pour the second glass through it, and compare: what is on the filter (name it: residue), what is in the glass (name it: filtrate), and which step — décantation or filtration — did the finer work?

## Recover the salt, then the water — on paper
A solution of salt in water stands in front of you. Plan A recovers the salt; Plan B recovers the water as well. For each plan, write the steps in order, the state changes involved, and what is left behind at the end. End by naming the apparatus step that turns evaporation into distillation, and what it adds.

## Sort the mixtures (paper only)
Four mixtures: (1) iron filings and sand; (2) salt dissolved in water; (3) mud in water; (4) two liquids that boil far apart. For each, write the method from this lesson you would start with — or say honestly that this chapter's family is not the first method for it, and why — then write what pure body or bodies each successful separation hands back.
