# Lesson plan — Separating mixtures — filtration, evaporation and distillation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Separating mixtures — filtration, evaporation and distillation
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** separating a mixture's constituents by the whole family of physical methods — décantation and filtration for a visible solid, evaporation for a dissolved solid, distillation to recover the liquid and to part liquid from liquid — and where the family leads: each constituent recovered on its own is a pure body (corps pur) with definite properties of its own; Morocco's Grade 7 *Al Moufid* guide teaches it as Chapitre 12, Séparation des constituants d'un mélange – Corps pur, closing its mixtures sequence (mixtures and dissolution are its Chapters 10–11)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Mixtures — homogeneous and heterogeneous, and Separating mixtures — filtration and settling, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Filtration and décantation — the visible solid comes back** — For a heterogeneous mixture: décantation first (rest, settle, pour off gently), then filtration (the liquid passes as filtrate, the solid stays as residue). Both work because the solid phase is visible and its grains can sink or be held back.
2. **Evaporation and distillation — what dissolved comes back** — A dissolved body passes any filter, so the methods change the liquid's state instead: evaporation drives the liquid off as vapour and leaves the dissolved solid; distillation boils the mixture, condenses the vapour and collects the liquid apart — recovering the liquid too, and parting liquids whose boiling points differ.
3. **The pure body — where separation leads** — Each recovered constituent is a corps pur: one body with definite properties of its own. A mixture holds bodies in proportions that can vary, each keeping its properties — which is why physical methods alone can always take it apart again.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Tries to filter a dissolved body out → a filter holds back grains it can see the size of; a dissolved body has spread as invisible particles and passes through — it needs a state change (evaporation or distillation), not a barrier.
