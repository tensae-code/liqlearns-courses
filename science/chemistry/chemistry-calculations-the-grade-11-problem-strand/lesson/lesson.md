# Chemistry calculations — the Grade 11 problem strand

## Do it first
Four kinds of problem run through Grade 11 like a spine. Before working them, state the method once, in four steps — this lesson's whole content.

The method, for every calculation in the course: write what is Given, state what is Wanted, choose the Formula, then substitute with Units and check the answer's size.

Photon and atomic-mass problems: E = hν and the abundance-weighted average (Units 1).

Gas problems: the laws gathered in PV = nRT (Unit 3). Rate problems from graph tangents and rate laws (Unit 4); equilibrium problems through Kc, Q and ICE tables (Unit 5). Worked example → exercise pairs are the book's drill pattern throughout.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Chemistry calculations — the Grade 11 problem strand to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
