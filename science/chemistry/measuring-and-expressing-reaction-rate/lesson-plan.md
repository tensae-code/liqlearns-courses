# Lesson plan — Measuring and expressing reaction rate

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Measuring and expressing reaction rate
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Chemical Kinetics (Grade 11 Chemistry).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The rate of a reaction is the change in amount or concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 11 Chemistry lessons before it in Unit 4 — Chemical Kinetics — and on the learner's earlier chemistry. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
1. **Rate defined and measured** — The rate of a reaction is the change in amount or concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.
2. **Average and instantaneous rate** — Rate can be followed by gas volume collected, mass loss, or colour change, as the reaction allows.
3. **Rate graphs and tangents** — The average rate covers a time interval; the instantaneous rate is the slope of the tangent to the concentration–time curve at one moment.
4. **Rate law, order and the constant k** — Rate expressions summarise measurement: the rate law links rate to concentrations raised to powers (orders), with k the rate constant.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Answer, in their own words: define reaction rate.
2. Answer, in their own words: what gives the instantaneous rate on a graph?
3. Answer, in their own words: name one way to follow a rate.
4. Answer, in their own words: what is k in the rate law?
