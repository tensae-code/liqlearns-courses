# Measuring and expressing reaction rate

## Do it first
Marble chips fizz in acid — wildly at first, then slower, then not at all. Before reading, say how you would turn that fizz into a number, and why the number keeps changing.

[Picture 21667 — A concentration–time curve with its tangent.]

## Sub lesson 1 — Rate defined and measured
The rate of a reaction is the change in amount or concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.

[GIF 21668 — bubbles forming fast, then slowing]

## Sub lesson 2 — Average and instantaneous rate
Rate can be followed by gas volume collected, mass loss, or colour change, as the reaction allows.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rate graphs and tangents
The average rate covers a time interval; the instantaneous rate is the slope of the tangent to the concentration–time curve at one moment.

## Sub lesson 4 — Rate law, order and the constant k
Rate expressions summarise measurement: the rate law links rate to concentrations raised to powers (orders), with k the rate constant. Worked calculations run through the section.
