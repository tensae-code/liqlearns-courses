# Lesson plan — The named heats — solution, neutralisation, combustion, formation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The named heats — solution, neutralisation, combustion, formation
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A family of standard heats defined and exemplified, as Yemen's Grade 12 book defines them: heat of solution (NH4NO3 dissolving at +25.7 kJ/mol, the cold-pack effect, against NaOH dissolving strongly exothermic), heat of vaporisation read off water's heating curve (+44 kJ/mol), heat of neutralisation constant at -57.7 kJ/mol for any strong acid with any strong base because the only real reaction is H+ + OH- -> H2O, standard heat of combustion measured in the bomb calorimeter, and standard heat of formation with its stability rule — the more negative, the more stable the compound
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Calorimetry — heats of reaction, q = mcΔT is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
