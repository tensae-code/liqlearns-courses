# Lesson plan — Electrodialysis and desalination

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Electrodialysis and desalination
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how salt is separated from seawater by electricity through ion-exchange membranes — Na⁺ and Cl⁻ pulled apart through membranes that each let only one kind of ion pass — as Laos's Grade 12 (ມ.7) textbook teaches it in Ch. 24, Advances in electrochemistry, §3 Electrodialysis (Fig. 24.4)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. No other lesson is needed first.

## Sub lessons
1. **Salt water — the separation problem** — Seawater holds dissolved salt — Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions spread evenly through the water.
2. **Ion-exchange membranes — gates for one kind of ion** — Electrodialysis separates the ions through **ion-exchange membranes** — sheets that let only one kind of ion through.
3. **Electrodialysis at work — the ions migrate apart** — With the membranes stacked between electrodes and a current applied, the ions migrate: Na⁺ moves toward the negative electrode through the cation membranes, Cl⁻ toward the positive electrode through the anion membranes — the migration pictured in the book's Fig.
