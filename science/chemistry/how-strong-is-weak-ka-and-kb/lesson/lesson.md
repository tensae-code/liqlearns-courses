# How strong is 'weak'? Ka and Kb

## Do it first
Hydrochloric acid splits completely in water; ethanoic acid splits about one molecule in a hundred. Before reading, say what constant measures the split — and why 'weak' never means 'dilute'.

[Picture 21717 — Strong acid fully split; weak barely.]

Strong acids and bases ionise completely; weak ones ionise only partly, reaching an equilibrium.

Ka measures a weak acid's ionisation; Kb a weak base's. Their negative logs are pKa and pKb — bigger Ka (smaller pKa) means a stronger weak acid.

Percentage ionisation tells what fraction has split. Polyprotic acids ionise in steps, each step with its own, smaller Ka. Equilibrium calculations with Ka/Kb give the pH of weak solutions.
