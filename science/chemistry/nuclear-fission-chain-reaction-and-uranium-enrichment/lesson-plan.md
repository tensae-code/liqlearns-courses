## Sub lessons
1. **Fission — the split** — **Nuclear fission** is the splitting of a heavy nucleus. A **slow neutron** striking **uranium-235** breaks it into **two lighter nuclei plus more neutrons and huge energy** — the energy of the mass defect of the split, released all at once. Uranium-238 does not fission this way; the fissile isotope is the rare one.
2. **The chain reaction — neutrons begetting neutrons** — Each fission releases neutrons, and those neutrons can strike further U-235 nuclei, whose fissions release still more. That is the **self-sustaining chain reaction**: one split lights the next generation of splits, and the reaction keeps itself going — governed in a reactor, ungoverned in a weapon.
3. **Enrichment — raising the rare share** — Natural uranium is **99.3% U-238 and only 0.7% U-235** — too dilute for the chain to hold in a power reactor. **Enrichment** raises the U-235 share: **power reactors run at 3–5%**, while **weapons exceed 90%**. Egypt names its national example, the **El Dabaa plant**, and fixes the scale: **1 g of U-235 ≈ burning 3 tons of coal**.
