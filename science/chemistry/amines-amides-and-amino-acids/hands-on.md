# Hands-on — Amines, amides and amino acids

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort the family by structure
Draw these six structures on six cards (or write their formulas): CH₃NH₂, (CH₃)₂NH, CH₃CONH₂, NH₂CH₂COOH, CH₃COOH, CH₃COOCH₃. Sort the cards into amines, amides, amino acids — and the two that belong to neither nitrogen family — saying out loud for each card which group decided you: –NH₂ on carbon, C=O joined to N, both groups in one molecule, or no nitrogen at all.

## Build the joining reaction on paper
Write glycine's formula twice, side by side. Circle the –COOH of the first and the –NH₂ of the second, draw the water molecule that leaves when they join, and draw the joined molecule with its amide (peptide) link boxed. Say why a chain of hundreds of these joinings is a protein and not a new kind of substance each time.

## Trace a preparation backwards
Pick one member of each family — an amine, an amide and an amino acid. For each, write where it comes from: the starting material named in the lesson and the change that makes it (reduction, reaction with ammonia, or hydrolysis of a protein). Then swap papers with a companion and check each other's routes against the lesson text.
