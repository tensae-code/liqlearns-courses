Today: **Amines, amides and amino acids** — Nigeria's way: the three nitrogen families taught together, as one family of derivatives.

Start with ammonia — one nitrogen, three hydrogens. Swap a hydrogen for a carbon group and you have an amine. Swap two, or three, and it is still an amine. That nitrogen keeps a spare pair of electrons, so amines act as bases — and the smell of old fish is an amine at work.
Now join that nitrogen to a carbonyl — a carbon double-bonded to oxygen — and you have an amide. Ethanoic acid plus ammonia, take away water, and ethanamide is left. Boil an amide back with acid or alkali and you undo it.
Last, put both groups in one molecule: an amine group at one place, an acid group at another. That is an amino acid. Join amino acids end to end with amide links and you build a protein — the giant molecule your own body is built from.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
