| Unit 1 (Yemen, Grade 10) | Book 1 (Grade 10) — Unit 1 — printed pp. 11–16 — A long honour roll of the Arab and Muslim founders, pictured on the unit's opening page: Jabir ibn Hayyan (721–815), called the true founder of chemistry for his experimental method, his substance classification in "The Book of the Secret of Secrets" (metals, volatile "spirits" such as sulfur and mercury, stones, vitriols, boraxes, salts), his first statement of fixed proportions — quoted from Berthelot as wrongly credited to English scholars ten centuries later — plus caustic soda, "gold water", nitric and hydrochloric acids, and sulfuric acid "oil of vitriol"; al-Razi (~220 works), Ibn Sina of the Canon, al-Jildaki's fixed-proportions statement, al-Hamdani's book on gold and silver, and al-Zahrawi's rule of chemical combination; the word chemistry itself is traced to an Arabic verb meaning "to hide/conceal" and to "alchemy". |
