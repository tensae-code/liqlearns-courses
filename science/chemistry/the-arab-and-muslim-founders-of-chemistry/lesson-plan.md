# Lesson plan — The Arab and Muslim founders of chemistry

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The Arab and Muslim founders of chemistry
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Yemen's Grade 10 history honour roll: Jabir ibn Hayyan, called the true founder of chemistry for his experimental method and his classification of substances, his first statement of fixed proportions, and the scholars who followed him — al-Razi, Ibn Sina, al-Jildaki, al-Hamdani and al-Zahrawi — with the word chemistry itself traced to an Arabic verb meaning to hide/conceal
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. What chemistry is — substance, matter and transformation is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Jabir ibn Hayyan — the true founder, and his method.

4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit and a teacher present — never home chemicals; kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.
