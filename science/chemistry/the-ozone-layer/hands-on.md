# Hands-on — The ozone layer

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Build the two oxygens
With three small balls or bottle caps for atoms and sticks or straws for bonds, build O₂ and O₃ side by side. Say out loud what is the same (the element, oxygen) and what is different (the number of atoms in the molecule) — then write the two formulas from your models without looking at the lesson.

## Trace one chlorine atom
Draw the stratosphere as a wide band on paper, with ten O₃ molecules drawn inside it. Draw one chlorine atom arriving from a broken-open CFC molecule below. Move it from ozone molecule to ozone molecule, crossing one out each time and writing the oxygen molecule left behind, until you have crossed out five. Say why the chlorine is still there at the end, ready to cross out more — and why that is what "catalytic" means.

## A protection plan for a strong-sun day
Pick a real day of outdoor work or sport in your place. Write the hours when the Sun is strongest, and for those hours write one protection for the skin, one for the eyes and one place of shade you will actually use. Swap plans with a companion and check each plan against sub lesson 3.
