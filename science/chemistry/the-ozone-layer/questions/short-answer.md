# The ozone layer — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How is ozone formed in the stratosphere?
**Answer: Ultraviolet radiation splits an oxygen molecule, O₂, into two single oxygen atoms, and each single atom joins a whole O₂ molecule to make ozone, O₃.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) How do ozone and the oxygen we breathe differ, and how are they the same?
**Answer: Both are the single element oxygen; they differ in the molecule — breathing oxygen is O₂, two atoms, and ozone is O₃, three atoms.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do CFCs survive the climb to the stratosphere instead of being destroyed at the ground?
**Answer: They are unreactive and harmless in the lower air — nothing down there breaks them — and they last for years, so they rise unchanged until ultraviolet radiation in the stratosphere breaks them open and frees their chlorine.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two harms of increased UV-B, one to people and one to the wider living world.
**Answer: To people: worse sunburn, raised skin-cancer rates and cataracts. To the wider living world: harm to crops and plant growth, and harm to the tiny floating living things at the base of the sea's food web (either one).**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What is the remediation for a thinning ozone layer, and why does it work only slowly?
**Answer: Stop making and using the chlorine-carrying gases, as the Montreal Protocol phases out CFCs. It works slowly because the gases already released last for years and the layer rebuilds only gradually by its own sunlight chemistry — over decades.**
