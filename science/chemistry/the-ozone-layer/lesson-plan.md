# Lesson plan — The ozone layer

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The ozone layer
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the ozone layer — a stratospheric shield of three-atom oxygen, O₃, that absorbs the Sun's ultraviolet radiation — how it forms, how human-made chemicals thin it, and what its thinning does to people and the environment, with the remediation and protection that answer it; Nicaragua's Grade 10 book teaches it as its own section inside its modern-atomic-theory unit (Unidad I)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Electromagnetic radiation and the spectrum is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **A shield made of three-atom oxygen** — Ozone is oxygen with three atoms in the molecule, O₃, against ordinary oxygen's two, O₂. High in the stratosphere, ultraviolet light splits O₂ and the freed atoms join other O₂ molecules to make ozone; the ozone layer so formed absorbs most of the Sun's ultraviolet — above all the harmful UV-B — before it reaches the ground.
2. **How the shield is thinned** — The layer is thinned when long-lived human-made gases — the chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) once used in refrigerators, sprays and foams — rise to the stratosphere and are broken open by ultraviolet light, freeing chlorine. One chlorine atom destroys ozone molecules again and again, catalytically, so a little CFC thins a lot of ozone; the thinning is worst over the poles, the Antarctic ozone hole being the famous case.
3. **Consequences, remediation and protection** — More UV-B reaching the ground harms people (sunburn, skin cancers, eye cataracts), crops and the small living things of the sea. The remediation is to stop making and using the thinning gases — the world's countries agreed exactly that in the Montreal Protocol — and the personal protection is the ordinary radiation protection: shade, covering clothing and sunscreen at the strong-sun hours.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes ozone up with ordinary oxygen → count the atoms: ozone is O₃, the oxygen we breathe is O₂ — same element, different molecule, different job.
