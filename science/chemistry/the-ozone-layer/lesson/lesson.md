# The ozone layer

## Do it first
Before you read: sunlight feels warm and helps you see, yet a whole day of it can burn your skin red. Something between the Sun and your skin must be stopping the worst of it. Say out loud: what do you think that something is made of — and why would it matter if it grew thinner? Then read on: this is Nicaragua's way in Unidad I — the ozone layer given its own section inside atomic theory itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — A shield made of three-atom oxygen

**Ozone** is a form of the element **oxygen**. The oxygen you breathe is a molecule of **two** oxygen atoms, **O₂**. Ozone is a molecule of **three** oxygen atoms, **O₃**. Same element, different molecule — and a very different job.

Ozone lives high in the air, in the layer called the **stratosphere**, roughly 15 to 35 kilometres above the ground, spread thin through it as the **ozone layer**. It is made there by sunlight itself. The Sun's **ultraviolet** radiation is strong enough to split an oxygen molecule: O₂ breaks into two single oxygen atoms, and each single atom joins a whole O₂ molecule to make O₃ — ozone.

The layer earns its keep by the same radiation that made it. Ozone **absorbs ultraviolet radiation** — above all the harmful part called **UV-B** — and turns it to heat, so that only a small share of the Sun's UV-B ever reaches the ground. Life below lives under that shield: without it, the ultraviolet reaching the surface would be far stronger than skin, eyes, crops and sea life can bear.

## Sub lesson 2 — How the shield is thinned

The shield is being **thinned** — depleted — by gases people put into the air. The chief culprits are the **chlorofluorocarbons**, **CFCs**, compounds of carbon, chlorine and fluorine once used widely as the cooling gas in refrigerators and air conditioners, as spray-can propellants and in making foams. CFCs are harmless down here — that is why they were liked — and they last for years, so they slowly rise, unchanged, into the stratosphere.

Up there the story turns. Ultraviolet radiation, which CFCs never meet at the ground, **breaks a CFC molecule open and frees a chlorine atom**. The freed chlorine attacks ozone: it takes one atom from an O₃ molecule, leaving ordinary O₂ behind — and then, in the following steps, the chlorine atom itself is **set free again**. So one chlorine atom is a **catalyst** of destruction: it can destroy ozone molecules **again and again**, thousands of them, before it is removed. A little CFC therefore thins a great deal of ozone.

The thinning is not even. It is worst where the stratosphere is coldest — over **Antarctica**, where each spring a deep thinning, the **ozone hole**, opens — but the layer has thinned measurably over the whole Earth.

## Sub lesson 3 — Consequences, remediation and protection

What gets through a thinned shield is more **UV-B**, and UV-B harms living things. On people it brings worse **sunburn**, raises the rate of **skin cancers**, and damages eyes — **cataracts** that cloud sight. On land it harms **crops** and slows plant growth. In the sea it harms the tiny floating living things at the base of the ocean food web. Nicaragua's book closes the section the way it closes every unit: with the benefits and harms of the chemistry weighed for people and for **Madre Tierra**, Mother Earth.

The **remediation** — the repair — is to stop freeing the chlorine. The world's countries agreed in the **Montreal Protocol** to phase out CFCs and their relatives, and the substitutes that replaced them in fridges and sprays do not carry chlorine up to the layer. Stopped at the source, the layer is left to heal itself by the same sunlight chemistry that built it — slowly, over decades.

And until it heals, there is **protection from radiation** for each person: seek **shade** at the strong-sun hours in the middle of the day, cover skin with **clothing** and a hat, use **sunscreen** on what stays bare, and protect the eyes. The shield above and the habits below work on the same fact: what UV-B never reaches cannot harm.

## The key ideas

- **Ozone is O₃** — three oxygen atoms in a molecule — against ordinary oxygen's **O₂**; its layer sits in the **stratosphere**.
- Ozone forms when **ultraviolet** light splits O₂ and the freed atoms join other O₂ molecules; the layer **absorbs UV-B**, shielding the surface.
- **CFCs** carry **chlorine** to the stratosphere; freed chlorine destroys ozone **catalytically** — one atom again and again — thinning the layer, worst over **Antarctica**.
- More UV-B means more **sunburn, skin cancers and cataracts**, and harm to **crops** and sea life.
- **Remediation**: phase out the thinning gases (the **Montreal Protocol**). **Protection**: shade, covering clothing, sunscreen at the strong-sun hours.
