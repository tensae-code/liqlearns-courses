Today: **The ozone layer** — Nicaragua's way: the layer gets its own section, inside atomic theory itself.

The oxygen you breathe has two atoms in each molecule. High above the ground there is a cousin of it with three atoms — ozone. Sunlight up there is strong enough to split oxygen molecules, and the loose atoms join other oxygen molecules and make ozone. Spread through the high air, that ozone works like a shield: it soaks up the Sun's burning ultraviolet rays, the UV-B rays, before they can reach us.
But some gases people made — the CFCs from old fridges and sprays — climb up there too. The sunlight breaks them open and frees chlorine, and one chlorine atom can destroy ozone molecule after ozone molecule. The shield grows thin, worst of all over the cold South Pole.
A thin shield lets more burning rays through — more sunburn and skin sickness, harm to eyes, to crops, to the tiny life of the sea. So the world agreed to stop the thinning gases, and each of us does the small part: shade, a hat, and care at the strong-sun hours.
