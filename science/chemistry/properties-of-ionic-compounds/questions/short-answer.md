# Properties of ionic compounds — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. ** In your own words: what does this lesson teach about properties of ionic compounds? Use the lesson's own example.
**Answer: brittle — a strike shifts layers, like charges meet and repel, and the crystal shatters (pictured before/after);**

**2. ** In your own words: what does this lesson teach about properties of ionic compounds? Use the lesson's own example.
**Answer: soluble in polar solvents ("like dissolves like"), generally insoluble in nonpolar ones;**

**3. ** In your own words: what does this lesson teach about properties of ionic compounds? Use the lesson's own example.
**Answer: non-conducting as solids but conducting when molten or dissolved, when ions are free to move to the electrodes (pictured).**

**4. ** How many ordinary covalent bonds and how many coordinate bonds are there in the ammonium ion, NH₄⁺? And in the hydronium ion, H₃O⁺?
**Answer: Ammonium has three ordinary covalent bonds plus one coordinate bond — nitrogen donates its lone pair to the fourth H⁺. Hydronium has two ordinary covalent bonds plus one coordinate bond — oxygen donates a lone pair to the third H⁺.**

**5. ** In your own words: why does close packing of ions explain the density of an ionic compound, while a covalent substance is less dense?
**Answer: Many ions are packed per unit volume in the ionic lattice, so more matter fills the same space; covalent substances are held between molecules only by weaker attractions and sit less tightly packed.**
