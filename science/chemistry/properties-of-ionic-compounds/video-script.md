Today: **Properties of ionic compounds**.

Properties are explained from the strong cation–anion attraction in a regular crystal lattice (NaCl structure pictured): hard crystalline solids of ions, not molecules;

high melting/boiling points (much energy to break the lattice);

brittle — a strike shifts layers, like charges meet and repel, and the crystal shatters (pictured before/after);

soluble in polar solvents ("like dissolves like"), generally insoluble in nonpolar ones;

non-conducting as solids but conducting when molten or dissolved, when ions are free to move to the electrodes (pictured).

Now you. Say it back in your own words. That is the lesson.


## Grade 11 addition
Ionic compounds have high melting points: the lattice attractions are strong in every direction and take much energy to break.

They are brittle: a blow shifts the lattice so like charges align, and repulsion splits the crystal along the plane.

Solid ionic compounds do not conduct — the ions are locked.
