## Properties of ionic compounds

Properties are explained from the strong cation–anion attraction in a regular crystal lattice (NaCl structure pictured): hard crystalline solids of ions, not molecules; high melting/boiling points (much energy to break the lattice); brittle — a strike shifts layers, like charges meet and repel, and the crystal shatters (pictured before/after);

soluble in polar solvents ("like dissolves like"), generally insoluble in nonpolar ones; non-conducting as solids but conducting when molten or dissolved, when ions are free to move to the electrodes (pictured). Experiment 5.1 tests exactly this: a salt solution lights a bulb in a circuit while distilled water and solid salt do not.

## Grade 11 addition — Properties of ionic compounds (Chemistry Band B)

This lesson also carries the Grade 11 teaching of this topic, added by the Chemistry Band B build (Ethiopia Wave 4). Work it after the part above.

[Picture 21636 — A blow shifts the lattice — like charges repel.]

Ionic compounds have high melting points: the lattice attractions are strong in every direction and take much energy to break.

They are brittle: a blow shifts the lattice so like charges align, and repulsion splits the crystal along the plane.

Solid ionic compounds do not conduct — the ions are locked. Molten or dissolved, the ions move freely and carry charge, so they conduct.
