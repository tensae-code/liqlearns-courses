# Common salt — extraction and uses

## Do it first
Before you read: the salt on your table began as rock in a hill or as sea water in a shallow pan. Say out loud: what has to happen to a sea to turn it into salt — and why would rock salt need almost nothing done to it at all? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 10 book starts, as Yemenis meet it, with Yemen's own deposits.

## Sub lesson 1 — Salt in Yemen — rock first

The book places sodium chloride in Yemen first. **Rock salt from al-Salif in al-Hudaydah governorate**, and the deposits of **Shabwa and Marib**, are already **high-purity** — the salt is won by mining and needs only **crushing and packing**. The second source is the sea itself: **solar evaporation of sea water at places such as Aden** leaves salt behind — but sea salt carries **CaCl₂ and MgCl₂ impurities** with it, which the rock salt does not.

## Sub lesson 2 — The substance — an ionic crystal

Even solid, salt is an **ionic crystal of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions** held in a lattice — which writes its properties: it **melts at 804 °C** and **boils at 1413 °C**, the high figures of a lattice that must be pulled apart; and **pure salt does not absorb moisture** — the dampness of table salt comes from its impurities, the same CaCl₂ and MgCl₂ the sea leaves in it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Salt at work

Its uses run from the kitchen outward: **food and its preservation**; **freezing mixtures** (salt with ice drives the temperature below 0 °C); and above all as a **feedstock** — the starting material from which **sodium hydroxide, soda (sodium carbonate) and hydrochloric acid** are made. The salt lessons that follow in the book — chlor-alkali and Solvay — both begin at this crystal.

## The key ideas

- Yemen's rock salt — **al-Salif (al-Hudaydah), Shabwa, Marib** — is high-purity: **crushing and packing** only; sea salt from **solar evaporation (e.g. Aden)** carries **CaCl₂ and MgCl₂** impurities.
- Salt is an **ionic crystal (Na⁺, Cl⁻)**: melts **804 °C**, boils **1413 °C**; **pure salt does not absorb moisture**.
- Uses: **food and preservation, freezing mixtures**, and the **feedstock for NaOH, soda and hydrochloric acid**.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does Aden's sea salt cake and dampen in storage while al-Salif rock salt stays dry? (The sea salt carries CaCl₂ and MgCl₂ impurities, which absorb moisture from the air; pure NaCl does not absorb moisture, and the high-purity rock salt is nearly free of them.)
2. What do salt's melting and boiling points (804 °C, 1413 °C) tell you about what solid salt is? (That it is an ionic crystal — a strong lattice of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions; pulling a lattice apart takes far more heat than separating molecules, hence the very high figures.)
3. Name the three industrial chemicals the book makes from salt. (Sodium hydroxide (caustic soda), soda (sodium carbonate) and hydrochloric acid.)
