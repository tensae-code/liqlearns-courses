# Hands-on — Amino acids, proteins and denaturation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The egg-white change (observation only — teacher handles the heat)
With a teacher, a raw egg white is observed in a clear heat-proof dish: its look, its flow, its transparency. The teacher warms it gently. Sketch and describe it at each stage as it turns white and sets. Say what unfolded, what tangled, which bonds broke and which stayed whole — then leave it to cool completely and record whether it ever runs again.

## The home protein census (observation only — kitchen labels and foods)
List the protein foods in your own home, as Ecuador's book sets the student to do: eggs, milk, beans, meat, and what else you find. For each, note where its protein sits on the table (cooked, raw, dried) and one cooking change it goes through. Say which of those changes are denaturation and what proves it.

## Two repairs judged (paper exercise)
Take two repair jobs Ecuador's closing sections name — one from orthopaedics and one from ophthalmology. For each, argue in four lines: what a natural biopolymer offers the body, what a silicone implant offers instead, and which you would choose and why. End by naming the property — the body's acceptance of the material — that both arguments turn on.
