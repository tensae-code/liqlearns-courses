# Lesson plan — Amino acids, proteins and denaturation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Amino acids, proteins and denaturation
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** proteins as chains of amino acids — every amino acid built on one plan (an amino group, –NH₂, and a carboxyl group, –COOH, on one carbon, differing only in its side chain, the R group), the units joined by peptide links into long chains whose order (sequence) sets how the chain folds into its working shape, and denaturation as the undoing of that shape by heat, acid or alkali without breaking the chain itself; Ecuador teaches the strand inside its closing everyday-compounds sections — protein content of home foods, haemoglobin set beside chlorophyll, natural biopolymers as amino-acid chains, and biomaterials in orthopaedics, cardiovascular and ophthalmology work, with silicone implants weighed
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Amines, amides and amino acids, and Carbohydrates — sugars and starches, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Amino acids — one plan, different side chains** — Every amino acid carries –NH₂ and –COOH on the same central carbon; what differs from one amino acid to the next is only the side chain, the R group — and that difference is what makes each unit distinct.
2. **Proteins — chains folded into working shapes** — Amino acids join by peptide links (water lost at each join) into chains; the chain's sequence sets its fold, and the fold sets the job — Ecuador's pair: haemoglobin in blood beside chlorophyll in the leaf, and the protein content of the foods in the student's own home.
3. **Denaturation — the shape undone, and proteins put to work** — Heat, acid or alkali break the weak bonds holding the fold, so the protein loses its shape and its job while its peptide chain stays whole — the white of an egg setting is the home proof; the same strand closes on natural biopolymers and biomaterials in medicine, silicone implants weighed alongside.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the chain picture: small units on one plan, joined link by link; change the order and you change the fold; undo the fold and the protein stops working, though the chain is still there.
- Thinks denaturation breaks the protein into amino acids → the peptide links survive denaturation; only the weak bonds holding the fold give way. Splitting the chain back to amino acids is hydrolysis, a different, stronger undoing.
- Mixes this lesson up with Amines, amides and amino acids → that lesson sorts the nitrogen families by their groups; this lesson follows one family member — the amino acid — all the way up to the working protein and back down by denaturation.
