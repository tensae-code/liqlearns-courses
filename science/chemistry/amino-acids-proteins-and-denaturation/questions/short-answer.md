# Amino acids, proteins and denaturation — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the amino-acid plan and say what the R group decides.
**Answer: Every amino acid carries an amino group (–NH₂) and a carboxyl group (–COOH) on one central carbon; the R group is the only part that changes, and it decides which amino acid the unit is.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the sequence of a protein decide its job?
**Answer: The order of amino acids along the chain sets how the chain folds on itself, and the folded shape is what lets the protein do its job — a different order folds differently and works differently, or not at all.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What does Ecuador's haemoglobin–chlorophyll pairing show?
**Answer: Two folded working molecules from two kingdoms, each holding a metal at its working centre — iron in haemoglobin, magnesium in chlorophyll — each useless unfolded: shape is what makes a big molecule a working one.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A denatured protein still has its amino acids in their original order. Explain how that can be.
**Answer: Denaturation breaks only the weak bonds holding the fold; the peptide links joining the chain are not broken, so the chain — and its order — survives while the shape and the work are lost.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why are natural biopolymers valued as biomaterials in medicine?
**Answer: They are built of the body's own kind of chemistry — amino-acid chains among them — so the body accepts them in repairs such as orthopaedics, cardiovascular work and ophthalmology, where a stronger material the body rejects would fail.**
