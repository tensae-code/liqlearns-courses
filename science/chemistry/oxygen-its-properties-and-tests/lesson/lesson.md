# Oxygen — its properties and tests

## Do it first

## The idea
Kenya's Grade 8 Integrated Science book prepares oxygen in the laboratory and then studies the gas itself: how it looks and behaves, how its density decides how it is collected, and the test that proves a gas is oxygen.

## The key facts
**What oxygen is like.** Oxygen is a colourless gas with no smell and no taste. It is only slightly soluble in water — which is why fish can breathe the small amount dissolved in rivers and lakes — and it has no effect on litmus: it is a neutral gas, neither acid nor alkali.

**Denser than air — and what that decides.** Oxygen is slightly denser (heavier) than air. Because of that, it can be collected by downward delivery into a gas jar, or over water since so little of it dissolves. The collection method follows from the properties: a gas's density and solubility tell you how to catch it.

**The glowing-splint test.** Oxygen supports combustion far more strongly than air does. A splint that has stopped flaming but still glows, pushed into a jar of oxygen, bursts back into flame — it relights. No other common laboratory gas does this, so relighting a glowing splint is the test for oxygen.

**Oxygen in day-to-day life.** Oxygen keeps people and animals alive in breathing, makes fires and fuels burn, rusts iron, and is given pure to patients and divers and welders. Life and burning alike run on it — about a fifth of the air is oxygen, and that fifth does the work.
