# Oxygen — its properties and tests — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: what oxygen is like.
**Answer: Oxygen is a colourless gas with no smell and no taste. It is only slightly soluble in water — which is why fish can breathe the small amount dissolved in rivers and lakes — and it has no effect on litmus: it is a neutral gas, neither acid nor alkali.**

**2.** Explain: denser than air — and what that decides.
**Answer: Oxygen is slightly denser (heavier) than air. Because of that, it can be collected by downward delivery into a gas jar, or over water since so little of it dissolves. The collection method follows from the properties: a gas's density and solubility tell you how to catch it.**

**3.** Explain: the glowing-splint test.
**Answer: Oxygen supports combustion far more strongly than air does. A splint that has stopped flaming but still glows, pushed into a jar of oxygen, bursts back into flame — it relights. No other common laboratory gas does this, so relighting a glowing splint is the test for oxygen.**
