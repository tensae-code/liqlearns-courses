# Lesson plan — Proteins of the future — food footprints as chemistry

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Proteins of the future — food footprints as chemistry
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Producing 1 kg of beef takes 16,000 L of water against 2,000 L for 1 kg of soy, while soy delivers 0.34 kg of protein per kg against beef's 0.17 kg.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sezione F closing spreads, "Pensa sostenibile: Dove sono le proteine del futuro" and "Calore e acidità: gli effetti sulla vita" — Italy's book (Grade 9–10)**

## Before this lesson
- No forced prerequisite. Recommended first: the neighbouring Chemistry lessons on the same family of ideas. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State in their own words: Producing 1 kg of beef takes 16,000 L of water against 2,000 L for 1 kg of soy, while soy delivers 0.34 kg of protein per kg against beef's 0.17 kg.
2. State in their own words: More than 1900 insect species are considered edible worldwide, and farmed insects emit fewer greenhouse gases and less ammonia than other farm animals.
3. State in their own words: Humanity has been in ecological overshoot since the 1970s — today consuming the resources of 1.7 planets a year, on the Global Footprint Network data the book prints.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit and a teacher present — never home chemicals.
