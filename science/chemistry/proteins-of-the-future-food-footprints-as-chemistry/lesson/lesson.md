# Proteins of the future — food footprints as chemistry

## Do it first
Before reading, say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is: proteins of the future — food footprints as chemistry. Then read on — this is Italy's way of teaching it, as the Italy placements describe the book's own treatment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sezione F closing spreads, "Pensa sostenibile: Dove sono le proteine del futuro" and "Calore e acidità: gli effetti sulla vita" — Italy's book (Grade 9–10)

The section's sustainability thread ties the biomolecule chapters to food choices: humanity has been in ecological overshoot (overshoot ecologico) since the 1970s — consuming more resources than Earth produces (today 1.7 planets a year; Earth Overshoot Day, which in 2000 fell at the end of September, was 29 July in 2021, data from the Global Footprint Network) — and the protein comparison quantifies it: producing 1 kg of beef takes 16,000 L of water against 2,000 L for 1 kg of soy, while soy delivers 0.34 kg of protein per kg against beef's 0.17 kg (the two infographics); insect-based foods are presented as an alternative class — insects can be cooked in many ways (fried, boiled, caramelised), flours are obtained from them for bread, pasta, crackers and biscuits, farmed insects emit fewer greenhouse gases and less ammonia than other farm animals and reproduce fast and resist disease, more than 1900 insect species are considered edible worldwide, and in 2021 the European Commission approved the first insect species for human consumption, the larva of Tenebrio molitor (the mealworm); the paired Sezione E closing spread (printed p. 437) applies the acid–base chapters to coral reefs: the barrier reef hosts at least 4000 fish species and 800 hard-coral species and absorbs 97% of wave energy — when CO2 dissolves in the oceans it reacts with water to form carbonic acid, reducing the carbonate available for shells and skeletons, and average surface ocean pH, 8.2 before the industrial revolution, has since fallen by 0.11 pH units (the quiz questions posed to the reader).

## The key ideas

- Producing 1 kg of beef takes 16,000 L of water against 2,000 L for 1 kg of soy, while soy delivers 0.34 kg of protein per kg against beef's 0.17 kg.
- More than 1900 insect species are considered edible worldwide, and farmed insects emit fewer greenhouse gases and less ammonia than other farm animals.
- Humanity has been in ecological overshoot since the 1970s — today consuming the resources of 1.7 planets a year, on the Global Footprint Network data the book prints.
