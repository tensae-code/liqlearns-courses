# The gas laws and the ideal gas equation

## Do it first
Squeeze a sealed syringe and the push-back grows in an exact pattern. Warm a balloon and it swells in another exact pattern. Before reading, say how many separate laws you think hide in gas behaviour — the answer is in this lesson's sub lessons.

[Picture 21660 — The laws as graphs, gathered in PV = nRT.]

## Sub lesson 1 — Boyle, Charles, Gay-Lussac, Avogadro
Boyle's law: at constant temperature, pressure × volume is constant — squeeze the gas and its pressure rises in inverse proportion. Charles's law: at constant pressure, volume grows in direct proportion to Kelvin temperature. Gay-Lussac's law links pressure directly to Kelvin temperature at constant volume, and Avogadro's law links volume directly to moles at fixed T and P.

[GIF 21661 — a piston compressing gas at constant T]

## Sub lesson 2 — The combined and ideal gas laws
The combined gas law merges the first three; the ideal gas law gathers everything into one equation: PV = nRT, with R the gas constant.

## Sub lesson 3 — Dalton's partial pressures
Dalton's law: the total pressure of a gas mixture is the sum of the partial pressures of its gases.

## Sub lesson 4 — Graham's law — diffusion and effusion
Graham's law: lighter gases diffuse and effuse faster — rates compare inversely with the square roots of their molar masses. Every law comes with graphs and worked calculations.
