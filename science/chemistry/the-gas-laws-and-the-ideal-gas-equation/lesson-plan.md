# Lesson plan — The gas laws and the ideal gas equation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The gas laws and the ideal gas equation
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Physical States of Matter (Grade 11 Chemistry).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Boyle's law: at constant temperature, pressure × volume is constant — squeeze the gas and its pressure rises in inverse proportion.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 11 Chemistry lessons before it in Unit 3 — Physical States of Matter — and on the learner's earlier chemistry. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
1. **Boyle, Charles, Gay-Lussac, Avogadro** — Boyle's law: at constant temperature, pressure × volume is constant — squeeze the gas and its pressure rises in inverse proportion.
2. **The combined and ideal gas laws** — The combined gas law merges the first three; the ideal gas law gathers everything into one equation: PV = nRT, with R the gas constant.
3. **Dalton's partial pressures** — Dalton's law: the total pressure of a gas mixture is the sum of the partial pressures of its gases.
4. **Graham's law — diffusion and effusion** — Graham's law: lighter gases diffuse and effuse faster — rates compare inversely with the square roots of their molar masses.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Answer, in their own words: write the ideal gas equation.
2. Answer, in their own words: state Boyle's law.
3. Answer, in their own words: what does Dalton's law sum?
4. Answer, in their own words: which gas diffuses faster, light or heavy?
