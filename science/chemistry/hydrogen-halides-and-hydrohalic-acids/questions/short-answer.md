# Hydrogen halides and hydrohalic acids — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do the hydrogen halides fume in moist air?
**Answer: Each gas grabs water vapour from the air and forms a mist of tiny acid droplets — the fumes are the acid forming in mid-air.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give the bond reason why HI is a stronger acid than HF.
**Answer: Down the family the H–X bond lengthens and weakens, so the proton leaves more easily; HI's long, weak bond gives its proton up most readily, and HF's short, strong one holds on.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A label reads "hydrogen chloride" and another "hydrochloric acid". What is the difference?
**Answer: Hydrogen chloride is the gas itself; hydrochloric acid is that gas dissolved in water — the solution the laboratory uses as the acid.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name HF's three exceptions to the family pattern.
**Answer: It is a low-boiling liquid where the others are gases, it is a weaker acid than its place suggests, and it attacks glass.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What single feature of HF explains all three exceptions?
**Answer: Hydrogen bonding between its molecules — fluorine's smallness and strong pull make HF travel in hydrogen-bonded chains and clusters, holding its molecules and its proton back.**
