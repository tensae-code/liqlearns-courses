# Hands-on — Hydrogen halides and hydrohalic acids

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The family line-up
Write HF, HCl, HBr and HI on four cards. Lay them in family order. Under each card put two slips: the name of its acid in water, and its place in the acid-strength order (weakest → strongest). Then turn one card over and say everything on its slips from memory before you check.

## Spot the exception
Write these four statements on slips: "boils far higher than its place promises", "attacks glass", "the strongest acid of the four", "a gas that fumes in moist air". Sort the slips: the ones true of HF alone, the one true of HI, the one true of the whole family. Read the lesson again and fix any slip you sorted wrong.

## Names apart (paper only — no chemicals)
Two bottles in a story: one holds hydrogen chloride, one holds hydrochloric acid. Write one sentence for each saying what is inside — a gas of HCl molecules, or HCl dissolved in water — and one thing each would do if the bottle were opened (fume in moist air; behave as a bench acid). Compare with a companion and correct each other from the lesson text.
