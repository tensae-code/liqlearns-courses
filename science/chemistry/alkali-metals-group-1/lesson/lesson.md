# Alkali metals — group 1

## Sub lesson 1 — One outer electron — the family character

The **alkali metals** are **group 1 (IA)**: **lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium and caesium** (francium, at the foot, is vanishingly rare and radioactive). Vietnam's Grade 12 book teaches the group as its own lesson — Bài 24, Group IA elements — after the metals chapters and before group IIA.

Each atom holds **one outer electron** over a noble-gas core, and holds it loosely: the family are the table's most eager electron-losers, forming **+1 ions** in every compound. They are soft enough to cut with a knife, light (lithium, sodium and potassium float), and tarnish in air within moments — so they are stored under oil, away from air and water.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reactions — violence grows down the group

With **water** every member gives the same pattern — a hydroxide solution (an **alkali**, the family's name) and **hydrogen**: lithium fizzes steadily, sodium melts into a darting ball, potassium's hydrogen catches fire with a lilac flame, and rubidium and caesium react explosively. Down the group the outer electron sits farther from the nucleus and leaves more easily, so the reaction grows fiercer.

The same eagerness drives the rest of the family's chemistry: they burn in oxygen to oxides, combine directly with the halogens to salts such as NaCl, and must be won from their compounds by **electrolysis** — no chemical reducing agent is strong enough to free them cheaply.

## Sub lesson 3 — Compounds and uses

Sodium's compounds run daily life: **sodium chloride** (common salt), **sodium hydroxide** (caustic soda, for soap and paper), **sodium carbonate** (washing soda, glass-making) and **sodium hydrogencarbonate** (baking soda). Potassium's salts are plant food — the K of NPK fertilisers — and lithium's light batteries power phones and electric cars.

The group's lesson closes where it began: one loose electron explains the softness, the floating, the flames, the alkalis and the industries — a family read off a single electron count.

## The key ideas

- Group 1 — **Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs** — holds **one outer electron**, lost eagerly to form **+1 ions**; the metals are soft, light, and stored under oil.
- With water they give an **alkaline hydroxide + hydrogen**; the reaction grows from lithium's fizz to potassium's flame and beyond — reactivity **increases down the group**.
- The family is won by **electrolysis** of its compounds; no ordinary reducing agent frees it.
- Sodium compounds (salt, caustic soda, washing soda, baking soda), potassium fertilisers and lithium batteries are the group's working faces.
