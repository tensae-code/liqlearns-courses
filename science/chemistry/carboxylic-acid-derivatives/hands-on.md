# Hands-on — Carboxylic acid derivatives

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The family tree (paper exercise)
Draw the carboxyl group of a carboxylic acid in the centre of a page. Draw three arrows out of it labelled with the three swaps from the lesson — amino group in place of –OH; a second acid minus water; chlorine in place of –OH. At the end of each arrow write the family's name, its ending or naming rule, and its making, all from the lesson text.

## Name that derivative (paper exercise)
Write six cards: ethanoic acid, its ammonium salt heated, ethanoic anhydride, ethanoyl chloride, an amide of ethanoic acid, and the derivative used for aspirin. Sort the cards under the three family headings — amides, acid anhydrides, acyl chlorides — and write the correct name on each card, using the –amide and –oyl chloride rules.

## Which derivative for the job? (paper exercise)
For each job, name the family member the lesson points to and say why in one line: (1) the vitamin example met in food; (2) the chemical used for aspirin; (3) the most reactive member, prepared with PCl₅ or PCl₃ and separated by fractional distillation.
