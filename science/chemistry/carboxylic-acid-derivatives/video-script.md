Today: **Carboxylic acid derivatives** — one parent, three swaps, in Fiji's Year 13 book.

Take a carboxylic acid and replace the –OH of its carboxyl group. Swap in nitrogen and you get an amide — made by heating the ammonium salt, and met in food as nicotinamide. Take water away from two acids and you get an anhydride — ethanoic anhydride, used for aspirin. Swap in chlorine and you get an acyl chloride, an –oyl chloride, made with PCl₅ or PCl₃ and separated by fractional distillation.

Learn the parent group, and the whole family follows.
