# Carboxylic acid derivatives — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the amide functional change, and how are amides named?
**Answer: The –OH of the carboxyl group is replaced by an amino group; the resulting amide is named from its parent acid with the ending –amide.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name Fiji's food example of an amide and say how amides are made.
**Answer: Nicotinamide, met as a vitamin in food. Amides are made by heating the ammonium salt of the carboxylic acid, which loses water.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How is an acid anhydride formed, in lesson terms?
**Answer: By removing water from two carboxylic acid molecules, joining what remains through an oxygen — an acid with its water taken away.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Give the full making of an acyl chloride as the book sets it out.
**Answer: The carboxylic acid is treated with PCl₅ or PCl₃ to give the acyl chloride (named as an –oyl chloride), and the product is separated by fractional distillation.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What single idea makes amides, anhydrides and acyl chlorides one family?
**Answer: Each is made from a carboxylic acid by replacing the –OH of the same carboxyl group — with an amino group, with a second acid minus water, or with chlorine.**
