# Lesson plan — Carboxylic acid derivatives

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Carboxylic acid derivatives

## Sub lessons
1. **Amides — the –OH swapped for nitrogen** — A **carboxylic acid derivative** is made by **replacing the –OH of the carboxyl group** with something else — and Fiji's study (Strand 4.2, printed pp.
2. **Acid anhydrides — two acids, one water removed** — The **acid anhydrides** are the family's second member: an anhydride is formed by **removing water from two carboxylic acid molecules**, joining what is left through an oxygen — the name says it: an acid with its water taken away.
3. **Acyl chlorides — the –oyl chlorides, the reactive end** — The **acyl chlorides** complete Fiji's family: replace the –OH with a **chlorine** and the acid becomes an acyl chloride — **named as –oyl chlorides**, the parent acid's name ending in –oyl chloride, as ethanoic acid gives ethanoyl chloride.

Their making is a preparation to learn exactly as the book gives it: an acyl chloride is **prepared from the carboxylic acid with PCl₅ or PCl₃**, and the product is **separated by fractional distillation**.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the three members up → anchor each to its swap: nitrogen in → amide; water out of two acids → anhydride; chlorine in → acyl chloride.
- Loses the naming rules → amides end in –amide; acyl chlorides are named as –oyl chlorides; anhydrides carry the parent acid's name as its anhydride.
- Thinks derivatives are unrelated families → every member is the same carboxyl group with its –OH replaced; the parent is what makes them one family.
