# Isotopes and average atomic mass

## Do it first
Two atoms are both hydrogen — one proton each. Yet one weighs twice as much as the other. Before reading, say where the extra mass must be hiding.

[Picture 29260 — Three hydrogens — same Z, different neutrons.]

Isotopes are atoms of the same element — the same number of protons Z — with different numbers of neutrons.

Isotope notation writes the mass number (protons + neutrons) up high and Z down low, beside the element symbol.

The atomic mass in the periodic table is an average, weighted by how common each isotope is.

Average atomic mass = the sum over isotopes of (fractional abundance × isotope mass). Worked examples follow this one rule.
