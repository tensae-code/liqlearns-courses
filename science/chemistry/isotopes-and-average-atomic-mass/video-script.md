Two atoms are both hydrogen — one proton each.

Isotopes are atoms of the same element — the same number of protons Z — with different numbers of neutrons.

Isotope notation writes the mass number (protons + neutrons) up high and Z down low, beside the element symbol.

The atomic mass in the periodic table is an average, weighted by how common each isotope is.

Average atomic mass = the sum over isotopes of (fractional abundance × isotope mass).

Now you. Say it back: What differs between isotopes of one element?
Answer: The number of neutrons.
