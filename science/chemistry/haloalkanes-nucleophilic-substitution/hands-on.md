# Hands-on — Haloalkanes — nucleophilic substitution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Hands first (safe, no chemicals)
Hold your two hands palm-down and try to place one exactly over the other, thumbs matching — they will not superimpose. Now hold one hand to a mirror: the image is the other hand. Write, in the lesson's words, what your hands just modelled — chirality, enantiomers, superimposability — and name the four 'different groups' a hand is using (thumb, fingers' side, palm, back) as its four different attachments.

## Mechanism sort (paper only)
Four substitutions are described: (a) rate doubles when either reactant doubles, product inverted; (b) rate unchanged when the nucleophile doubles, product racemic; (c) a primary haloalkane reacting fast with a strong nucleophile; (d) a tertiary haloalkane reacting in a polar solvent even with a weak nucleophile. Assign each its mechanism and give the evidence in each case.

## Fingerprint reading (paper only)
A single-enantiomer haloalkane is converted to an alcohol three times: trial 1 gives the inverted alcohol only; trial 2 gives a 50:50 mixture of both alcohol enantiomers; trial 3 gives mostly inverted with some racemic. Write the mechanism verdict for trials 1 and 2, and a one-line account of trial 3 (both roads running at once).
