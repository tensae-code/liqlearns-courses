# Haloalkanes — nucleophilic substitution — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the compound CH₃–CHBr–CH₂–CH₃ by the chapter's rules and class it.
**Answer: 2-bromobutane: the bromine takes position 2 on a four-carbon (butane) chain. It is a secondary haloalkane, because the carbon carrying the bromine is bonded to two other carbons.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Give the chapter's three preparations of haloalkanes.
**Answer: From alkanes, by free-radical substitution of a hydrogen by halogen; from alkenes, by addition of a hydrogen halide HX across the double bond; and from alcohols, by replacing the –OH group with a halogen.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Write the general substitution of a haloalkane by hydroxide and name the product family.
**Answer: R–X + OH⁻ → R–OH + X⁻: the hydroxide nucleophile takes the halogen's place on the carbon, and the haloalkane becomes an alcohol. (With CN⁻ the product is a nitrile; with NH₃, an amine.)**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Contrast the first step of S_N1 and S_N2.
**Answer: S_N2 has no separate first step: bond-making and bond-breaking happen together as the nucleophile attacks from the back. S_N1's first step is the C–X bond breaking alone — slowly — to give a carbocation and the halide ion, before any nucleophile arrives.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Define a chiral centre and optical activity, each in a sentence.
**Answer: A chiral centre is an atom — in this chapter, a carbon — bonded to four different groups, making its molecule non-superimposable on its mirror image. Optical activity is that molecule's rotation of the plane of plane-polarised light, one enantiomer turning it each way.**
