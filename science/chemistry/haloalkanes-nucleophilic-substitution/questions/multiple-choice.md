# Haloalkanes — nucleophilic substitution — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Haloalkanes are classed primary, secondary or tertiary by…
a) The halogen's position in the table  b) The carbon the halogen rides on — how many other carbons it carries  c) The chain length  d) Their boiling points
**Answer: b) The carbon the halogen rides on — how many other carbons it carries**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) In the C–X bond, the carbon carries δ+ because…
a) Carbon is a metal  b) The halogen pulls the shared electrons toward itself  c) The bond is ionic  d) Hydrogen pushes electrons
**Answer: b) The halogen pulls the shared electrons toward itself**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) In S_N2, the nucleophile attacks…
a) From the same side as the halogen  b) From the back, opposite the leaving halogen — inverting the centre  c) Only after the halogen leaves  d) At the halogen itself
**Answer: b) From the back, opposite the leaving halogen — inverting the centre**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) The rate of an S_N1 reaction depends on…
a) Both reactants  b) The haloalkane alone — its slow first step is the bond breaking  c) The nucleophile alone  d) The temperature only
**Answer: b) The haloalkane alone — its slow first step is the bond breaking**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Enantiomers are…
a) Structural isomers  b) Non-superimposable mirror images of a chiral molecule  c) Atoms of the same element  d) Charged molecules
**Answer: b) Non-superimposable mirror images of a chiral molecule**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) A racemic mixture shows no optical rotation because…
a) It contains no chiral molecules  b) Its equal enantiomers rotate light oppositely and the turns cancel  c) It is too dilute  d) Light cannot pass through it
**Answer: b) Its equal enantiomers rotate light oppositely and the turns cancel**
