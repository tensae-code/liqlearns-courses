# Lesson plan — Haloalkanes — nucleophilic substitution

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Haloalkanes — nucleophilic substitution
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bhutan's Class XI book (Ch.12, the whole chapter): the haloalkanes as a family — named from the halogen on the chain, built around the one polar bond C–X, prepared and characterised — whose chemistry is nucleophilic substitution, the halogen swapped by an electron-pair donor, taught with its two mechanisms and their stereochemistry, and closing where substitution's three-dimensional stage is set: chirality, enantiomers and optical activity

## Sub lessons
1. **The family and its one polar bond** — A **haloalkane** is an alkane carrying one or more halogen atoms — general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X for the monohalogen family — named as the book's nomenclature runs it: position number, halogen prefix, parent chain (2-bromobutane), and classed **primary, secondary or tertiary** by the carbon the halogen rides on.
2. **Nucleophilic substitution — the swap, two ways** — The δ+ carbon of C–X invites a **nucleophile** — an electron-pair donor such as OH⁻, CN⁻ or NH₃ — and the family's characteristic reaction is the swap: **nucleophilic substitution**, R–X + Nu⁻ → R–Nu + X⁻, by which haloalkanes become alcohols, nitriles and amines.
3. **Chirality, enantiomers and optical activity** — The chapter closes in three dimensions.
