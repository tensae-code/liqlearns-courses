# Lesson plan — Crystal structures and unit cells

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Crystal structures and unit cells
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how the particles of a solid are packed — sphere packing, the unit cell as the crystal's smallest repeating box, and counting the atoms a cell owns by the sharing rule, worked on the body-centred cubic cell — as Sri Lanka's Grade 12 Chemistry Part II teaches the solid state in Ch. 9 (figs. 9.23–9.24)

## Sub lessons
1. **Packing spheres — how atoms fill space** — A crystal of a solid element is atoms packed like oranges in a crate. Sri Lanka's Part II Ch. 9 teaches the solid state through sphere packing (figs. 9.23–9.24): layers of spheres, each sphere resting in the hollows of the layer below, with different stackings giving different structures.
2. **The unit cell — the smallest repeating box** — The whole crystal is built by repeating one small box — the unit cell — in every direction. Whatever atom count the box owns, the crystal owns in proportion; so the chemistry of a crystal is read off its unit cell.
3. **Counting atoms in a cell — the bcc count** — An atom on a corner is shared between the 8 cells that meet there, so it counts 1/8 to each; an atom at the body centre belongs to its cell alone. The book's own count for body-centred cubic: 8 corners × 1/8 = 1, plus the centre atom = 2 atoms per unit cell. The same sharing rule counts the simple cubic cell (1) and the face-centred cubic cell (4).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the crate of oranges: a crystal is packing first, and the unit cell is the packing's repeating box.
- Counts a corner atom as a whole atom → ask how many cells meet at one corner of a stack of boxes: eight — so each owns 1/8.
- Memorises 1, 2, 4 without the rule → recount all three cells from the sharing rule alone: corners 1/8, faces 1/2, centre 1.
