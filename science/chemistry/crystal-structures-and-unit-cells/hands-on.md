# Hands-on — Crystal structures and unit cells

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Pack the crate
Take 20–30 round fruits, balls or bottle caps of one size. Build one flat layer in rows, then a second layer resting in the hollows of the first. Say out loud what "repeating" means in your stack: point at the smallest piece of the pattern that, stamped out, rebuilds the whole thing.

## Build a unit cell from a cardboard box
Take a small cube-shaped box (or fold one from card). Put a coin or marble at each corner — held in a drawing or with sticky tack — and one in the centre. Count with the sharing rule, out loud: corners 8 × 1/8 = 1, centre 1, total 2. You have built the bcc cell. Now remove the centre coin and recount: simple cubic, 1.

## Count a face-centred cell on paper
Draw a square (one face of a cube). Mark the 4 corners and the face centre. This face owns 4 × 1/8 of its corners from this face's view — now scale up carefully with the lesson's rule for the whole cube (6 faces, each centre shared by 2 cells) and reach 4 atoms per cell. Swap counts with a companion and audit each other's fractions.
