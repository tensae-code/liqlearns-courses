# Crystal structures and unit cells — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What makes a crystal structure a "structure" rather than a heap?
**Answer: Its particles are packed in a pattern that repeats — the same arrangement again and again through the whole solid, each layer resting in the hollows of the layer below.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do chemists count atoms per unit cell rather than per crystal?
**Answer: The unit cell is the crystal's repeating piece, so its atom count fixes the proportions for the whole crystal, however large the crystal grows.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State the sharing rule for corner, face-centre and body-centre atoms.
**Answer: A corner atom counts 1/8 (shared by 8 cells), a face-centre atom counts 1/2 (shared by 2 cells), and a body-centre atom counts 1 (wholly inside its own cell).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Count the atoms in a simple cubic cell, showing your working.
**Answer: Corners only: 8 × 1/8 = 1 atom per unit cell.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) A drawing of an fcc cell shows 14 atoms. Why does the cell own only 4?
**Answer: The 8 corner atoms are each shared among 8 cells (8 × 1/8 = 1) and the 6 face atoms are each shared between 2 cells (6 × 1/2 = 3); 1 + 3 = 4 atoms actually belong to the cell.**
