# Sorting the elements — blocks and kinds

## Do it first
The table mixes shiny bendable metals, gases that form no compounds easily, and odd borderline cases. Before reading, name one metal, one non-metal and one element you are unsure about.

[Picture 21627 — The table coloured by block.]

Elements sort into metals (left and centre), non-metals (right) and metalloids along the border between them.

Representative elements, transition elements and inner-transition elements are the table's great families.

By which subshell is filling, the table divides into s, p, d and f blocks — the block follows straight from the electron configuration.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Sorting the elements — blocks and kinds to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
