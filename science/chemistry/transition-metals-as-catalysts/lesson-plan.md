# Lesson plan — Transition metals as catalysts

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Transition metals as catalysts
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)

## Sub lessons
1. **Adsorption — the surface does the work** — Egypt explains catalytic activity by **surface adsorption**: the transition metal's **4s and 3d electrons bond reactant molecules onto the metal's surface**. Held there, the reactants are **concentrated** — crowded together far beyond their gas or solution density — and their **bonds are weakened** by the grip of the surface.
2. **Lower activation energy — the profile** — Reactants crowded and weakened need less of a push to react: the **activation energy falls**, which the book shows on an **energy-profile diagram** — the catalysed path crossing a lower hill than the uncatalysed one. The catalyst emerges unchanged because its surface bonding is a temporary grip, not a consumption: adsorb, react, release, repeat.
3. **The four working cases** — Egypt's cases: **nickel in the hydrogenation of oils**, **iron in Haber–Bosch** ammonia synthesis, **V₂O₅ in the contact process** for sulphuric acid, and **MnO₂ decomposing hydrogen peroxide** — the black powder of the opening question, fizzing oxygen out of the liquid and surviving to do it again.

## If the learner is stuck
- Catalyst imagined to work by being consumed gradually → it works by surface grip and is released unchanged each cycle. / Activation energy confused with the reaction's total energy change → the catalyst lowers the hill, not the start or end levels.
