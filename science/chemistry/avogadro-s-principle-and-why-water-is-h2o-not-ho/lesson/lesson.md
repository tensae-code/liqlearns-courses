# Avogadro's principle — and why water is H2O, not HO

## Do it first
Before reading, say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is: avogadro's principle — and why water is h2o, not ho. Then read on — this is Italy's way of teaching it, as the Italy placements describe the book's own treatment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Capitolo 5, §1 — Italy's book (Grade 9–10)

Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes (1808): gases reacting at the same temperature and pressure combine in volume ratios of small whole numbers (hydrogen + oxygen → water always 2:1); the hydrogen chloride experiment breaks Dalton's picture — one volume of hydrogen + one volume of chlorine give TWO volumes of HCl, which single atoms cannot explain (the wrong and right box diagrams are drawn side by side) — and Avogadro's principle resolves it: equal volumes of different gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain the same number of molecules, because hydrogen and oxygen are diatomic molecules — so water is H2O, not the HO Dalton supposed; the noble gases are the monatomic exception; Avogadro's ideas waited half a century until Cannizzaro (1826–1910) persuaded the scientific community in 1860 to distinguish atom from molecule; a molecule is defined as a grouping of two or more atoms, equal or different, with characteristic chemical properties.

## Sub lesson 2 — Capitolo 3 — Italy's book (Grade 9)

Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes (H₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1), the contradiction it posed for Dalton (1 volume H + 1 volume Cl should give 1 volume HCl; experiment gives 2), Avogadro's resolution — equal volumes of gas hold equal numbers of molecules, and the elementary gases are diatomic — and the fifty-year wait (Karlsruhe 1860, Cannizzaro) before the atom/molecule distinction was accepted; the elementary molecules inventoried (diatomic seven, P₄, S₈, the noble gases monatomic).

## The key ideas

- Equal volumes of different gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain the same number of molecules — this is Avogadro's principle.
- Hydrogen and oxygen are diatomic molecules, so water is H2O, not the HO Dalton supposed.
- Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes: gases reacting at the same temperature and pressure combine in volume ratios of small whole numbers — hydrogen and oxygen react 2:1 to make water.
