# Food additives — what each kind does and its limit — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is a food additive, and why does the label not simply print its full name?
**Answer: A food additive is a substance added to food on purpose to do a job — colour, keeping, texture or taste. The label names it by its E-number: one short code for one named substance, the same on every label that uses it.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) By what are the kinds sorted in the book's survey, and which kind does the survey put first?
**Answer: The kinds are sorted by the job each does in the food; the colourings — the additives whose job is the food's colour — come first in the survey.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Which additives does the book class as toxic, as it presents them?
**Answer: Sulfur dioxide and the sulfites, phosphates, and others named in the survey are classed as toxic in the book's presentation.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) E330 and E211 are which substances, and how does the book class them?
**Answer: E330 is citric acid and E211 is sodium benzoate; the book names both among the additives it classes as carcinogenic.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What is the book's advice about synthetically additivated products, and what is it advice against — one meal, or a pattern?
**Answer: The book advises avoiding the long-term consumption of synthetically additivated products — it is advice against the pattern of eating them daily over years, the long-term habit, not against a single meal.**
