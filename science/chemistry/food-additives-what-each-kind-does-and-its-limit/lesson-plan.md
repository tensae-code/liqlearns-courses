# Lesson plan — Food additives — what each kind does and its limit

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Food additives — what each kind does and its limit
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** food additives as Moldova's Grade 12 book surveys them — substances added to food on purpose, each named on the label by its E-number, sorted by the job each kind does (colourings first), sorted again by the harm the book presents (sulfur dioxide and sulfites and phosphates classed toxic; citric acid E330 and sodium benzoate E211 among those classed carcinogenic), and closed with the book's own advice to avoid long-term consumption of synthetically additivated products (Moldova, Grade 12, Unit 9 §9.1 Aditivii alimentari, printed p. 190)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Chemistry in farming and food is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
1. **Added on purpose, named by a number** — A food additive is a substance added to food on purpose to do a job — colour, keeping, texture, taste — and the label names it by its E-number, one number for one named substance.
2. **The kinds, by the job they do** — The book's survey sorts the kinds by their job: colourings first, then the preservative and other working kinds a packaged food carries; an additive is known by its number and judged first by its kind.
3. **The book's safety sorting — and its limit** — The book classes sulfur dioxide and the sulfites and phosphates as toxic, and citric acid (E330) and sodium benzoate (E211) among those classed carcinogenic, as it presents them — and advises avoiding the long-term consumption of synthetically additivated products.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Chemistry in farming and food**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Added on purpose, named by a number.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: The kinds, by the job they do.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: The book's safety sorting — and its limit.
4. Read a real food label and say what each E-number's kind does in that food.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and the labels of packaged foods to read. No practical with chemicals — this lesson's hands-on is label reading only.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes the two sortings up → the first sorting is by the job the additive does; the second is the book's harm sorting. Job first, harm second.
