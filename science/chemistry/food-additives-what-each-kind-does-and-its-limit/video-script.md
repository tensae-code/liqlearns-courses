Today: **Food additives — what each kind does and its limit** — Moldova's way: the label's little codes, read properly.

Look at a food label. Between the flour and the sugar you find codes — E330, E211. Those are food additives: substances put into the food on purpose, to do a job — give it colour, keep it from spoiling, hold it together. Each one has its own number, and the number is its name on every label.
Moldova's book sorts them twice. First by the job: the colourings that give the shade, the preservatives that slow the spoiling. Then by harm, as the book presents them: sulfur dioxide and the phosphates among the toxic ones; citric acid, E330, and sodium benzoate, E211, among those it classes as carcinogenic. And the book ends with plain advice: do not eat synthetically additivated food day after day, year after year.
