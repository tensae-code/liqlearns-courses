# Models of the atom — from Dalton to Bohr — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does chemistry need models of the atom at all?
**Answer: The atom is too small to inspect directly, so science builds a working picture of it and tests the picture against evidence — the model is what can be checked, improved and replaced.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) The Sri Lanka book opens with wave and particle model figures. What does having two different pictures of the same thing teach about models?
**Answer: A model is not the thing itself — different pictures can each be useful where they work, and a model is kept only while it explains and predicts.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) State the fault that ended Thomson's model and the experiment behind the model that replaced it.
**Answer: Thomson's uniform positive sphere could not explain why a few alpha particles bounced sharply off gold foil; Rutherford's scattering experiment replaced it with the nuclear model.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What everyday fact does Bohr's fixed-levels rule protect?
**Answer: That atoms are stable and do not collapse — circling electrons in Rutherford's picture should radiate, spiral in and crash, and matter would not hold together.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) In one sentence, what is the pattern that links all four models?
**Answer: Each model keeps what the one before explained and repairs the exact point where evidence defeated it — models change because evidence changes.**
