# Hands-on — Models of the atom — from Dalton to Bohr

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Judge a model like a scientist
Take any everyday model you know — a map of your town, a toy car, a weather forecast symbol. For each of the two tests in the lesson (does it explain? does it predict?), give it a mark out of five and say why. Then say what evidence would make you throw that model away and draw a better one.

## Draw the sequence, defend the sequence
Draw Dalton's, Thomson's, Rutherford's and Bohr's atoms on four cards, biggest change marked in a second colour on each card after Dalton's. Shuffle the cards, then lay them back in order — out loud, each time naming the evidence that forced the next card. A companion checks your order against the lesson text.

## Break a model on purpose
Write one sentence each model cannot handle: a fact Dalton's solid ball cannot explain, a fact Thomson's sphere cannot explain, a fact Rutherford's circling electrons cannot survive. Finish each sentence with the repair the next model made. Compare your three sentences with a companion's.
