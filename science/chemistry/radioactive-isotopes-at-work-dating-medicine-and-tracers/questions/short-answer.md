# Radioactive isotopes at work — dating, medicine and tracers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can carbon-14 not date a stone tool directly?
**Answer: The clock runs on carbon exchanged with the air by living things; stone was never alive and carries no living-level carbon-14 to decay from — only once-living remains (wood, bone, cloth, charcoal) can be dated.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A sample holds one half of the living carbon-14 level. Give its age and the reasoning in one line.
**Answer: One half remaining is exactly one half-life, so the remains are about 5,700 years old.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) State the difference between radiotherapy and a tracer study in one sentence each.
**Answer: Radiotherapy aims the radiation itself at diseased tissue to destroy it (cobalt-60 beams, implanted seeds, iodine-131 in the thyroid). A tracer study gives a small amount of an isotope such as technetium-99 and only follows its radiation with detectors, to picture the organ at work.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is plutonium-238 fit to power a pacemaker?
**Answer: It decays so steadily over so long a time that a small sealed source keeps delivering energy for years — exactly the unchanging, long-lived supply a heartbeat device needs.**
