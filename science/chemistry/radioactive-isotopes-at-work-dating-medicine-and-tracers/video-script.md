Today: **Radioactive isotopes at work** — Dominica's CSEC applications strand: the decay does the work.

An unstable atom falls apart on a schedule — and the schedule is a tool. Carbon-14 halves every 5,700 years once a living thing dies, so its remains carry their age in the fraction left, readable back some 60,000 years. In the hospital the same decay is aimed — cobalt-60 beams and tiny implanted seeds against cancer — or merely followed, tracer atoms picturing an organ at work. In the field a Geiger counter tracks an element climbing a living plant, and in one chest a plutonium-238 battery has been quietly keeping time with a heart for years.
