# Hands-on — Radioactive isotopes at work — dating, medicine and tracers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Halving by hand (paper exercise)
Take 64 counters (bottle caps, beans, coins). Each round, remove half of what remains and record the round and the count: 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Label each round one half-life of 5,700 years and read off the table: after how many years do 8 counters remain? After how many is less than one counter left — and why does that set carbon-14 dating's limit near 60,000 years?

## Weapon or signal? (paper exercise)
List the lesson's medical isotopes in two columns — radiotherapy and tracers. Under each, write the one property named in the lesson that fits it to its job (aimed beam, gland uptake, implanted seed, detector-followed signal). Then add the pacemaker battery to a third column and name its fitting property.

## The tracer path (paper exercise)
Draw a young plant — roots, stem, leaves. Mark with arrows the path a tagged fertiliser element could take, and write at each part what a Geiger counter held there would report if the element had arrived, and what it would report if it had not.
