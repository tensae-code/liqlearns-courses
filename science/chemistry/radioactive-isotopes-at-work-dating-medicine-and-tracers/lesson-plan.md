# Lesson plan — Radioactive isotopes at work — dating, medicine and tracers

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Radioactive isotopes at work — dating, medicine and tracers
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what radioactive isotopes are put to work doing — carbon-14 dating of once-living remains up to about 60,000 years on its 5,700-year half-life, radiotherapy against cancer (cobalt-60 beams and the implanted seeds of iridium-192, palladium-103 and iodine-125, iodine-131 for the thyroid), medical tracers such as technetium-99, tracers followed through plants with a Geiger counter, and plutonium-238 batteries keeping pacemakers running
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Isotopes, and Nuclear fission and fusion, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Carbon-14 dating — a clock that starts at death** — Carbon-14 dating: the clock starts at death, half-life 5,700 years, reach about 60,000 years.
2. **Radiotherapy and tracers — medicine's two uses** — Radiotherapy (cobalt-60 beams; iodine-131 for thyroid; iridium-192, palladium-103 and iodine-125 seeds) and medical tracers (technetium-99, iodine-131).
3. **Tracers in the field — and a battery in a heartbeat** — Plant-uptake tracers read with a Geiger counter; plutonium-238 pacemaker batteries.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the dating clock up → the living thing keeps its carbon-14 topped up; the clock starts at death, and each 5,700 years halves what remains.
- Confuses radiotherapy with tracers → radiotherapy's radiation is the treatment, aimed to destroy; a tracer's radiation is a signal, followed to reveal.
- Thinks a tracer must be stable to be useful → it is the decay that makes it visible: no radiation, nothing to follow.
