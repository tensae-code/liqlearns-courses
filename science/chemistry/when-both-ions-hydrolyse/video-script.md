# Video script — When both ions hydrolyse

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Take a salt of a weak acid AND a weak base.

When both ions hydrolyse, both reactions run: the anion pulls toward basic, the cation toward acidic.

The outcome is decided by comparing the parent acid's Ka with the parent base's Kb: the larger constant's side wins.

Ka > Kb gives an acidic solution; Kb > Ka basic; near-equal constants give a near-neutral result.

Now you. Say it back: What decides the pH when both ions hydrolyse?
Answer: Comparing Ka and Kb.
