# Heterocyclic rings — with nitrogen, oxygen or sulfur — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** What makes a compound heterocyclic?
**Answer: Its ring holds other atoms besides carbon — nitrogen, oxygen or sulfur sitting in the ring itself as members, not attached outside it.**

**2.** Compare pyridine and pyrrole: ring size, guest atom, and where each is found.
**Answer: Both carry nitrogen. Pyridine is six-membered and is part of nicotine; pyrrole is five-membered and is a building unit of haemoglobin and chlorophyll.**

**3.** What are furan and thiophene, and what benzene-like property do they share?
**Answer: Furan is the five-membered oxygen ring (bp 31 °C, structure proved by Baeyer in 1877) and thiophene the five-membered sulfur ring; both are resonance hybrids.**

**4.** Why does the chapter close the aromatic story with heterocyclic rings?
**Answer: Because life and medicine run on them: penicillin and many antibiotics carry heterocyclic systems, and the nitrogen rings sit inside nicotine, haemoglobin and chlorophyll.**
