# Heterocyclic rings — with nitrogen, oxygen or sulfur

## Do it first
Benzene's ring is all carbon. Before reading, say out loud: what should a ring be called when one of its own members is not carbon at all — a nitrogen, an oxygen, a sulfur sitting in the ring? Then read on: this is Iraq's Grade 11 (Scientific Track) way in its aromatic chapter (Ch.8) — the heterocyclic rings, the ring systems inside nicotine, blood and the antibiotics.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rings with a guest atom

A **heterocyclic compound** is a ring that holds **other atoms besides carbon** — nitrogen, oxygen or sulfur sitting in the ring itself. The guest atom makes the family: these are not benzene rings with something attached, but rings built with the foreign atom as a member. Iraq's aromatic chapter closes with them because life and medicine run on them: **penicillin and many antibiotics carry heterocyclic systems**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The nitrogen rings — pyridine and pyrrole

**Pyridine** is a **six-membered nitrogen ring** — and it is **part of nicotine**. **Pyrrole** is a **five-membered nitrogen ring** — and it is a **building unit of haemoglobin and chlorophyll**, the two great pigment systems of blood and leaf. One guest atom, nitrogen, in two ring sizes, reaching from a plant poison to the molecules that carry oxygen and catch sunlight.

## Sub lesson 3 — The oxygen and sulfur rings — furan and thiophene

**Furan** is the five-membered **oxygen** ring (bp 31 °C; its structure was proved by Baeyer in 1877), and **thiophene** the five-membered **sulfur** ring. Both close the lesson with a benzene echo: furan and thiophene are **themselves resonance hybrids** — the guest atom does not end the ring's shared-electron character.

## The key ideas

- A **heterocyclic** ring holds **atoms besides carbon** — nitrogen, oxygen or sulfur as ring members.
- **Pyridine**: six-membered nitrogen ring, part of **nicotine**. **Pyrrole**: five-membered nitrogen ring, a building unit of **haemoglobin and chlorophyll**.
- **Furan** (oxygen ring, bp 31 °C, structure proved by Baeyer in 1877) and **thiophene** (sulfur ring) are **resonance hybrids**; **penicillin and many antibiotics** carry heterocyclic systems.

## Do It Yourself
1. Is pyridine a benzene ring with a nitrogen attached? Say what it actually is. (No — pyridine is a six-membered ring with nitrogen as a member of the ring itself, which is what makes it heterocyclic.)
2. Name the heterocyclic building unit shared by haemoglobin and chlorophyll, and its guest atom and ring size. (Pyrrole — a five-membered ring with nitrogen as the guest atom.)
3. Furan boils at 31 °C. Name its guest atom, and the sulfur ring named beside it. (Oxygen; the sulfur ring is thiophene — both are resonance hybrids.)
