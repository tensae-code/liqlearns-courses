# Lesson plan — Alkali metals in technology

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Alkali metals in technology
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Alkali metals in technology in Egypt's Grade 11 student book (Chapter Two, STS section, printed pp. 38–40): One use per element as Egypt teaches it: lithium in phone/EV batteries and light alloys (Falcon 9 tanks), sodium in vapour street lamps and as liquid coolant in the sodium nuclear reactor, potassium into fertilisers and potassium superoxide in closed breathing apparatus, rubidium bromide in scratch-resistant glass and night-vision kit, caesium in atomic clocks that keep GPS satellite timing.

## Sub lessons
1. **Lithium and sodium — batteries, lamps and a reactor** — **Lithium** works in **phone and electric-vehicle batteries** and in **light alloys** — the book's example is the **Falcon 9's fuel tanks**, where lightness is everything. **Sodium** glows in **vapour street lamps** and runs liquid through the **sodium nuclear reactor as its coolant** — a metal that melts low and carries heat well.
2. **Potassium and rubidium — fields, breathing and glass** — **Potassium** goes into **fertilisers**, feeding crops, and as **potassium superoxide** into **closed breathing apparatus**, where it releases oxygen for the wearer. **Rubidium bromide** hardens **scratch-resistant glass** and serves in **night-vision equipment** — the rare metal found where its exact properties fit.
3. **Caesium — the timekeeper** — **Caesium** keeps time: **atomic clocks** built on caesium's steady vibrations are the clocks that **keep GPS satellite timing** — every position a phone reports rests on caesium counting the seconds. One element, one job, in Egypt's pattern: the family's uses are as distinct as its members.
