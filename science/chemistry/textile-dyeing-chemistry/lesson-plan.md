# Lesson plan — Textile dyeing chemistry

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Textile dyeing chemistry
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fabric dyeing taught as a chemical industry in its own right — how colour is put into cloth so it stays — as Laos's Grade 12 (ມ.7) textbook teaches it in Ch. 32, the Dyeing industry chapter of its Chemical industry part
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. No other lesson is needed first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Dyeing — colour that must stay in the cloth** — Colouring cloth is a chemical job, not only a soaking job.
2. **How dyeing works — dye, fibre and fixing** — For colour to stay, the dye molecules must bind to the fibre — held by the attractions between dye and fibre, and in some dyeings fixed with the help of additional chemicals.
3. **The dyeing industry — Laos's industry-chapter view** — The chapter's place in the book is its own lesson: in Laos's course, dyeing closes the industry survey (mineral, cement, ceramics, glass, dyeing, perfume, fertiliser industries) — the chemistry of daily manufacture, where coloured cloth is a product an industry plans, controls and sells.
