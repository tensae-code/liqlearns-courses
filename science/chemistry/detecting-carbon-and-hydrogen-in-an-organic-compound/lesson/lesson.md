# Detecting carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound

## Do it first
Before you read: carbon and hydrogen are invisible inside a compound — you cannot see either in a spoonful of sugar. Say out loud: what could you change them into that you *can* see, and how would you catch it? Then read on: Egypt's organic chapter runs the standard practical — burn the compound against copper(II) oxide and interrogate what comes off.

## Sub lesson 1 — The set-up — heating with copper(II) oxide

The substance is **heated with copper(II) oxide**, which supplies the oxygen for a complete burning whatever the compound brought. The compound's carbon is oxidised to **carbon dioxide** and its hydrogen to **water** — the two invisible elements converted into two testable gases and vapours, led off through the apparatus in turn.

## Sub lesson 2 — The water test — white to blue

The vapour meets **white anhydrous copper sulphate**: if water is present, it turns **blue**. That colour change is the hydrogen verdict — the water could only have come from the compound's hydrogen, so **hydrogen is proven present**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The limewater test — milky

The gas is then bubbled through **lime water**: if carbon dioxide is present, the lime water turns **milky**. That clouding is the carbon verdict — the CO₂ could only have come from the compound's carbon, so **carbon is proven present**. Two observations, two elements: this pair of tests is the standard proof that opens organic analysis.

## The key ideas

- Heat the compound with **copper(II) oxide**: C → CO₂, H → H₂O.
- **Water turns white anhydrous copper sulphate blue** — hydrogen proven.
- **CO₂ turns lime water milky** — carbon proven.
