# Lesson plan — Detecting carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Detecting carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Detecting carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound in Egypt's Grade 12 student book (Chapter Five, printed pp. 122–123 (chapter pagination)): The standard practical: heat the substance with copper(II) oxide; water turns white anhydrous copper sulphate blue, and CO₂ turns lime water milky — the two observations that prove C and H.

## Sub lessons
1. **The set-up — heating with copper(II) oxide** — The substance is **heated with copper(II) oxide**, which supplies the oxygen for a complete burning whatever the compound brought. The compound's carbon is oxidised to **carbon dioxide** and its hydrogen to **water** — the two invisible elements converted into two testable gases and vapours, led off through the apparatus in turn.
2. **The water test — white to blue** — The vapour meets **white anhydrous copper sulphate**: if water is present, it turns **blue**. That colour change is the hydrogen verdict — the water could only have come from the compound's hydrogen, so **hydrogen is proven present**.
3. **The limewater test — milky** — The gas is then bubbled through **lime water**: if carbon dioxide is present, the lime water turns **milky**. That clouding is the carbon verdict — the CO₂ could only have come from the compound's carbon, so **carbon is proven present**. Two observations, two elements: this pair of tests is the standard proof that opens organic analysis.
