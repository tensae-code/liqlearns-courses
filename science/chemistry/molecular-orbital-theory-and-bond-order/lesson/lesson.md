# Molecular orbital theory and bond order

## Do it first
Valence bond theory says oxygen's bonds leave all electrons paired — yet liquid oxygen is attracted to a magnet, which needs unpaired electrons. Before reading, say what a better theory must allow.

[Picture 21651 — The MO diagram for O₂.]

Molecular orbital (MO) theory spreads electrons over the whole molecule in molecular orbitals, not between two atoms only.

Overlapping atomic orbitals give a lower-energy bonding MO and a higher-energy antibonding MO.

Bond order = ½(bonding electrons − antibonding electrons). Zero bond order means no stable molecule.

Filling MO diagrams predicts stability and magnetism — oxygen's diagram holds unpaired electrons, matching O₂'s paramagnetism.
