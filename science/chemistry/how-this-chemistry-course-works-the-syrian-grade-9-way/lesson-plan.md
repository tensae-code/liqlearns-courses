# Lesson plan — How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 9 way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 9 way
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Syria's Grade 9 chemistry course-shape taught as content in its own right — the shared Syrian design as it runs across the book's three Chemistry units (inorganic, organic and nuclear chemistry): objectives and key words, “I try and I conclude” experiments, worked applications, enrichment and safety boxes, a self-test per lesson, unit questions and a closing chemistry project (Syria, Grade 9 — *الفيزياء والكيمياء للصف التاسع الأساسي (Physics & Chemistry, Grade 9 Basic)*, Chemistry units opening, printed p. 95)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **One design across three Chemistry units** — From printed p. 95 the book's Chemistry half opens: **Unit 4 Inorganic chemistry, Unit 5 Organic chemistry, Unit 6 Nuclear chemistry**. Different matter in each — solutions, acids, bases and salts; carbon's compounds; the nucleus and its radiations — but **one shared design** carries all three.
2. **Objectives, key words, “I try and I conclude”** — Every lesson opens with **objectives and key words**, and its body is built on **“I try and I conclude” experiments with an explicit conclusion box** — try, watch, conclude — followed by **worked applications** that carry each concluded idea into solved use.
3. **A self-test per lesson, unit questions, a closing project** — Each lesson carries its own **self-test**; each unit gathers itself into **unit questions**; and the Chemistry course closes with a **chemistry project** — in this book the oil-refining project — where the learning is planned, carried out and reported as one piece of work.

Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit and a teacher present — never home chemicals; kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks each unit is built differently → the matter differs — inorganic, organic, nuclear — the design is one and shared; that sameness is the point.
- Reads safety boxes as enrichment → enrichment stretches an idea; safety boxes guard the bench work itself and are read BEFORE the experiment.
- Treats the project as extra → the project is the course's closing check — planned, carried out and reported as one piece of work.
