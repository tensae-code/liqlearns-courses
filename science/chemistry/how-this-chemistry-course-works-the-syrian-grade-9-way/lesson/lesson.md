# How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 9 way

## Do it first
Before you read: the Grade 9 book's Chemistry half runs to three units — inorganic, organic and nuclear chemistry. Say out loud: what would you want every lesson in all three to do the same way, so you always know how to enter it? Then read on: this lesson names that shared design, set running at printed p. 95.

## Sub lesson 1 — One design across three Chemistry units

From printed p. 95 the book's Chemistry half opens: **Unit 4 Inorganic chemistry, Unit 5 Organic chemistry, Unit 6 Nuclear chemistry**. Different matter in each — solutions, acids, bases and salts; carbon's compounds; the nucleus and its radiations — but **one shared design** carries all three.

That sameness is deliberate: the learner who learns the shape in Unit 4 spends no shape-learning in Units 5 and 6; all the effort goes on the new chemistry itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Objectives, key words, “I try and I conclude”

Every lesson opens with **objectives and key words**, and its body is built on **“I try and I conclude” experiments with an explicit conclusion box** — try, watch, conclude — followed by **worked applications** that carry each concluded idea into solved use.

Around that spine sit the boxes: **enrichment boxes** stretching the idea, and — new weight at this grade — **safety boxes**, because Grade 9's bench work handles acids, bases and flames that punish carelessness. The safety box is read before the experiment, not after an accident.

## Sub lesson 3 — A self-test per lesson, unit questions, a closing project

Each lesson carries its own **self-test**; each unit gathers itself into **unit questions**; and the Chemistry course closes with a **chemistry project** — in this book the oil-refining project — where the learning is planned, carried out and reported as one piece of work.

Lesson check, unit check, project: the same three-step closing the Syrian design uses at Grade 8, now carrying three units of heavier chemistry.

## The key ideas

- The Grade 9 Chemistry units (printed pp. 95–180) run **one shared design** across inorganic, organic and nuclear chemistry.
- Every lesson: **objectives and key words**, **“I try and I conclude”** experiments with a conclusion box, **worked applications**, **enrichment and safety boxes**.
- A **self-test per lesson**, **unit questions**, and a **closing chemistry project** (oil refining) close the course.
