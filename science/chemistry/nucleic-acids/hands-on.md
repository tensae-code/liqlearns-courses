# Hands-on — Nucleic acids

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Build the blocks (paper only)
Draw the three parts of a nucleotide and assemble two of them on paper: one DNA nucleotide (name its sugar and the four bases available to it) and one RNA nucleotide (name its sugar, and the base that replaces thymine). Label base, sugar and phosphate on each drawing.

## Complement writing (paper only)
A DNA strand segment reads A–T–G–C–C–A–T–G. Write the complementary strand beneath it, pair by pair, and mark beside each pair the number of hydrogen bonds (2 or 3). Then total the bonds for the segment.

## Job sorting (paper only)
Six jobs are listed: storing the hereditary plan in the nucleus; copying that plan before a cell divides; carrying one gene's instructions to the ribosome; forming part of the ribosome itself; fetching amino acids to the growing protein chain; being the plan's working copy in the cytoplasm. Assign each to DNA, or to mRNA, rRNA or tRNA.
