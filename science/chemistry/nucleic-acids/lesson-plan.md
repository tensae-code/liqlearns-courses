# Lesson plan — Nucleic acids

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Nucleic acids
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the nucleic acids as NCERT's Class XII Biomolecules unit teaches them (§14.5): DNA and RNA, polymers of nucleotides — each nucleotide a nitrogen base (the purines A and G; the pyrimidines C, T and U) on a pentose sugar (β-D-ribose in RNA, β-D-2-deoxyribose in DNA) with a phosphate, the units joined by phosphodiester links — Watson and Crick's double helix with its complementary pairing A=T and G≡C, and the family's work: storing the hereditary information, copying it in replication, and directing the making of proteins

## Sub lessons
1. **Building blocks — bases, sugars, nucleotides** — A nucleotide is three parts joined: a nitrogen base — the purines adenine (A) and guanine (G), the pyrimidines cytosine (C), thymine (T) and uracil (U) — a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group. Base + sugar alone is a nucleoside. The sugar decides the family: β-D-ribose gives RNA, β-D-2-deoxyribose gives DNA; thymine lives in DNA only, uracil in RNA only. Nucleotides join by phosphodiester linkages (3′→5′) into the polynucleotide chain — the molecule's primary structure.
2. **The double helix — pairing rules and the two strands** — DNA is two polynucleotide strands wound in Watson and Crick's double helix, held by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases: A pairs with T (two bonds), G with C (three) — so the strands are complementary, not identical, and the base ratios obey A/T = 1 and G/C = 1 (Chargaff). RNA stays single-stranded. The pairing rule is the whole secret: each strand of DNA specifies the other exactly.
3. **What nucleic acids do — storing, copying, directing** — DNA is the chemical basis of heredity: it stores the instructions for every protein in its base sequence, and at cell division it replicates — the strands part, each building its complement, two identical molecules from one. RNA does the directing: messenger RNA carries a gene's sequence to the ribosome, ribosomal RNA forms the ribosome's core, transfer RNA fetches the amino acids — together running the protein synthesis that the proteins lesson's molecules are made by.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes thymine with uracil → return to the one-letter rule: T lives in DNA only, U lives in RNA only; A, G and C live in both. The sugar tells the same story — deoxyribose for DNA, ribose for RNA.
