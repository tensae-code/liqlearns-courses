# Nucleic acids — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Distinguish a nucleoside from a nucleotide.
**Answer: A nucleoside is a nitrogen base joined to a pentose sugar only. A nucleotide is a nucleoside carrying a phosphate group — the full building unit of the nucleic acids.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the purine and pyrimidine bases, marking which pyrimidine belongs to which nucleic acid.
**Answer: Purines: adenine (A) and guanine (G), in both DNA and RNA. Pyrimidines: cytosine (C, in both), thymine (T, DNA only) and uracil (U, RNA only).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A DNA strand segment reads G–C–A–T. Write its complement and give the total hydrogen bonds across the four rungs.
**Answer: The complement reads C–G–T–A. Bonds: G≡C 3, C≡G 3, A=T 2, T=A 2 — 10 hydrogen bonds in all.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does the double helix make exact replication possible?
**Answer: The strands are complementary, so when they part, each serves as the template for building its partner anew by the pairing rules; the two daughter molecules are therefore identical to the parent — the copying instructions are built into the structure itself.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Assign the three RNAs their jobs in protein synthesis.
**Answer: mRNA carries one gene's base sequence from DNA to the ribosome; rRNA forms part of the ribosome, the assembly machine; tRNA fetches the amino acids and places them in the order the mRNA message dictates.**
