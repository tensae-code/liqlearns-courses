Today: **Nucleic acids** — India's way: the nucleic-acids section of the Class XII Biomolecules unit — the molecules that store the instructions the proteins are built from.

Take a nitrogen base — adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine or uracil. Join it to a five-carbon sugar — ribose, or its deoxy cousin — and you hold a nucleoside. Add phosphate and it becomes a nucleotide, a building block with a handle. Link nucleotide to nucleotide, phosphate to sugar, phosphate to sugar, and the chain that grows is a nucleic acid.
DNA runs two such chains together, wound as Watson and Crick's double helix, and the rungs obey one law: adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, guanine with cytosine through three — so each strand carries the other's copy written in complement. That is how heredity is stored, how replication copies it, and how RNA — single-stranded, uracil for thymine — carries the instructions out to build the proteins of the earlier lessons.
