# Hands-on — Purification of organic compounds

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Method detective (paper only)
Five mixtures are named: crude naphthalene mixed with sand; o-nitrophenol mixed with p-nitrophenol; glycerol that decomposes at its atmospheric boiling point; an impure liquid whose impurity boils 8 K above it; a reaction mixture holding an organic compound dissolved in water with inorganic salts. For each, name the purification method from the lesson, and give the one property of the compound or impurity that makes that method the right one.

## Kitchen crystallisation (kitchen materials only)
With an adult, dissolve as much sugar as will go into a small cup of hot water, let the solution cool undisturbed overnight, and look for crystals on the surface and sides. Record what formed and where. Say which step of sub lesson 1's crystallisation you have just run, and what you would do next in a laboratory to collect and dry the pure solid.

## Chromatography on paper (kitchen materials only)
With an adult, put a small dot of black felt-tip ink near the bottom of a strip of coffee-filter paper, and stand the strip in a little water so the water rises through the dot. Record the coloured bands that separate and their order. Say, from sub lesson 3, which kind of chromatography this is and what the stationary and mobile phases are.
