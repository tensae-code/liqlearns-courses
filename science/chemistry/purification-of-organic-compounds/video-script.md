Today: **Purification of organic compounds** — India's way: the purification section of the Class XI organic-principles unit — before any organic compound can be trusted, it must be made pure, and NCERT lines the methods up in one section.

A compound made in the laboratory arrives mixed — with starting materials, side products, solvent. Purification is the sorting, and the sort is chosen, not guessed. A solid mixed with an insoluble impurity is filtered; a solid whose impurities dissolve differently is crystallised out of a hot solvent and collected clean on cooling; a solid like camphor or naphthalene, which passes straight to vapour, is sublimed away from what cannot follow it.
Liquids are distilled — simply, or fractionally when boiling points stand close, under reduced pressure when the liquid would decompose at its boiling point, and with steam when it is steam-volatile and will not mix with water. Two liquids that refuse to mix are separated by differential extraction, shaking with a solvent that takes one partner and not the other. And finest of all, chromatography lets the components travel at different speeds over a stationary phase — in a column, on a thin layer, on paper — each substance showing as its own spot or band, and one spot alone the sign of purity.
