# Purification of organic compounds — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the two solubility properties a good crystallisation solvent must have.
**Answer: It must dissolve the compound only sparingly when cold but freely when hot, so that most of the compound separates as crystals on cooling while soluble impurities remain dissolved in the mother liquor.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) When is fractional distillation chosen over simple distillation, and what does the fractionating column do?
**Answer: When the liquids' boiling points stand close together. In the column the vapour condenses and re-vaporises many times, so the lower-boiling component enriches toward the top and is drawn off first.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does differential extraction work better with several small portions of solvent than with one portion of the same total volume?
**Answer: Each fresh portion removes a further share of what the previous portions left behind, so the compound left in the original solvent falls portion by portion; several small extractions recover more than a single large one.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Distinguish adsorption chromatography from partition chromatography by the holding mechanism.
**Answer: In adsorption chromatography the components are adsorbed to different extents on a solid stationary phase (alumina or silica gel); in partition chromatography they partition in different ratios between a stationary liquid phase and the moving solvent, as on paper.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 1) An impure solid's impurity is insoluble in the solvent that dissolves the compound. Which method removes it, and at what stage?
**Answer: Filtration — the impure solid is dissolved, the insoluble impurity is caught on the filter paper, and the compound is recovered from the filtrate (by crystallisation or evaporation).**
