# Soil — composition, properties and pH — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the five shares a handful of soil holds.
**Answer: Mineral particles (rock broken down, sand to clay), organic matter (humus), water, air in the spaces between particles, and living things.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a clay soil stay wet long after a sandy soil has dried?
**Answer: Clay's particles are the finest and pack close, leaving only narrow spaces; water is held between them and drains slowly, while sand's coarse grains leave wide spaces that let water run through.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A soil's property is described in the lesson as 'the composition felt from outside'. Say what that means, with one example.
**Answer: The property is caused by the composition: e.g. a soil drains fast (property) because its particles are coarse sand (composition) — read one and you learn the other.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Describe how soil pH is measured, step by step.
**Answer: Mix a soil sample with water, let the mixture settle, and test the liquid with an indicator or a pH meter; the reading places the soil on the pH scale as acidic, neutral or alkaline.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can two fields side by side need different treatment for the same crop?
**Answer: Their soils can differ in composition and in pH; since pH steers which nutrients plants can take up, the same crop can feed well in one field and starve in the other until the chemistry is corrected.**
