Today: **Soil** — Mongolia's way: the ground itself, as the closing chemistry of Grade 7.

A handful of soil is not one thing. It is a mixture: grains of broken rock, from sand down to the finest clay; dark humus, the remains of living things; water and air hiding between the grains; and life itself, all through it. Shake it in water and watch it settle — layer by layer, the handful confesses what it is made of.
And the mixture decides how the soil behaves. Coarse grains drain fast and dry fast. Fine clay holds its water. Humus feeds it and turns it dark.
Last, the measurement: mix soil with water and test it. The pH tells you how acidic the soil's water is — and that number steers what a plant can eat. Soil is chemistry you can hold.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
