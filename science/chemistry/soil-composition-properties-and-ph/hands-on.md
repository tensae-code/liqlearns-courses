# Hands-on — Soil — composition, properties and pH

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The jar test (observation only — soil, water, a clear jar)
Half-fill a clear jar with soil, top it up with water, shake it well (teacher present), and set it down undisturbed. Sketch it after one minute, after ten minutes, and the next day. Label the layers from the bottom up and say which component of sub lesson 1 each layer shows — including anything floating. Wash hands after handling soil.

## Two soils, one watering (observation only)
Put the same amount of two different soils (a sandy one, a clay-rich one) into two funnels lined with cloth over cups. Pour the same measured amount of water into each at the same moment. Time what comes through and measure how much. Say which composition each result belongs to, and which soil a farmer must water more often — and why, from sub lesson 2.

## Reading a soil's pH (teacher-led — indicator work)
With a teacher, mix a soil sample with distilled water as the lesson describes, let it settle, and test the clear liquid above with the indicator provided. Record the colour and the pH it gives. Say, in one sentence, what the number tells a farmer that looking at the soil never could.
