# Lesson plan — Soil — composition, properties and pH

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Soil — composition, properties and pH
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** soil as a chemistry topic in its own right — what soil is composed of (mineral particles from sand to clay, humus, water, air and living things), how its properties follow from that composition (particle size setting drainage and water-holding, humus feeding and darkening it), and its pH — the acidity of soil water, measured by testing a soil-and-water mixture, steering which nutrients plants can take up; Mongolia's Grade 7 book gives soil a full closing section — §4.2, Soil composition and properties — in its Chemistry and life chapter
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. What chemistry is — substance, matter and transformation, and The pH scale, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **What soil is made of — the composition** — Soil is a mixture: mineral particles in sizes from sand down to clay, dark humus from dead living things, water and air between the particles, and living things throughout — shown by the layers it settles into in water.
2. **Soil's properties — what the composition does** — Particle size sets drainage and water-holding (sand drains, clay holds), humus feeds and darkens the soil — every property is the composition felt from outside.
3. **Soil pH — measuring the soil's acidity** — Soil water is a solution with a pH; testing a soil-and-water mixture reads it, and the reading steers which nutrients plants can take up — the section's own practical skill.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the handful: soil is a mixture, and everything after — properties, pH — is that mixture being read three ways.
- Thinks soil is one substance → the jar test: one handful settles into different layers, which one substance could never do.
- Mixes pH up with fertility itself → pH is one measurement of the soil, steering nutrient uptake; it is read from the soil-and-water mixture, not guessed from the soil's look.
