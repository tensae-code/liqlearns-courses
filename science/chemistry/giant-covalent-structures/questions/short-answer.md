# Giant covalent structures — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is a giant covalent structure, in one sentence?
**Answer: It is a substance in which every atom is joined to its neighbours by covalent bonds running through the whole material, so one crystal is effectively one enormous molecule with no separate small molecules in it.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the three examples the book teaches and say what joins the atoms in each.
**Answer: Diamond (carbon atoms bonded in a 3-D network), silica — sand (silicon and oxygen atoms in a giant network), and graphite-style carbon networks; in all three the atoms are joined by covalent bonds throughout.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is diamond hard while solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) is soft and low-melting, though both are covalent?
**Answer: Diamond's covalent bonds run through the whole crystal, so changing it means breaking strong bonds; dry ice is separate CO₂ molecules held by weak forces between molecules, which give way easily — the bonds inside each molecule are never touched.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A substance is very hard and does not melt below 1500 °C. Which structural class does that point to, and why?
**Answer: A giant covalent structure — only a continuous network of strong covalent bonds demands that much energy to break down; ionic lattices are the other high-melting class, and the bonding evidence (covalent network examples like diamond and silica) is what this lesson reads.**
