# Isotopes and the mass spectrometer

## Do it first
Before you read: oxygen's atomic mass is 15.9994, not 16 — a fraction, not a whole number. Say out loud: what could make an element's mass refuse to be whole — and what machine could prove your answer by sorting atoms one from another? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 12 book earns isotopes from that fraction, with the machine that separated them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two chemistries — the eight-row contrast

The unit opens by contrasting **chemical and nuclear reactions in an eight-row table**: nuclei change or not; protons/neutrons against electrons; energy magnitudes; absorption signs; equations balancing mass number and atomic number; rates unaffected by temperature, pressure and catalysts. The contrast is the unit's fence: everything nuclear that follows stands on the nuclear side of it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fractional masses — and Aston's machine

Isotopes are then **earned from the fractional atomic masses** — oxygen **15.9994**, boron **10.811**: if every atom of an element weighed the same whole number, no such fractions could stand. **Aston's 1919 mass spectrometer (مطياف الكتلة)** — its helium beam photograph reproduced, **Bainbridge's 1933 refinement** noted — **separated the atoms themselves**: isotopes are defined on its evidence — **¹H, ²H, ³H; ²³⁴U, ²³⁵U, ²³⁸U** — same element, different masses, sorted by the machine.

## Sub lesson 3 — The weighted mean — and the two kinds of isotope

The **weighted-mean formula** for relative atomic mass is worked for **neon, giving 20.197** — the fraction explained as the abundance-weighted average of the separated masses. Isotopes are then sorted into **stable and radioactive** kinds, and the set is widened by hand: **artificial radioisotopes are made by bombardment** — **²⁷Al + slow neutron → ²⁸Al + γ** — new isotopes manufactured, not only found.

## The key ideas

- Chemical vs nuclear reactions are fenced by an **eight-row table** (nuclei vs electrons; mass/atomic-number balancing; rates untouched by temperature, pressure, catalysts).
- **Aston's mass spectrometer (1919; Bainbridge 1933)** separated atoms by mass — the evidence behind **isotopes** (¹H, ²H, ³H; uranium's three) and behind fractional masses like oxygen's **15.9994**.
- Relative atomic mass is the **weighted mean** (neon worked at **20.197**); isotopes are **stable or radioactive**, and **artificial radioisotopes** are made by **bombardment** (²⁷Al + n → ²⁸Al + γ).

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does oxygen's mass of 15.9994 already argue for isotopes before any machine is mentioned? (If all oxygen atoms had one whole-number mass, the element's atomic mass would be that whole number; a fraction like 15.9994 can only be an average over atoms of different masses — isotopes — mixed in fixed proportions.)
2. Work the neon result's shape: what two quantities per isotope does the weighted mean need? (Each isotope's mass and its fractional abundance; the relative atomic mass is the sum of (abundance × mass) over the isotopes — worked in the book for neon as 20.197.)
3. How is ²⁸Al's isotope different in origin from uranium's three? (²⁸Al is artificial — made by bombarding ²⁷Al with a slow neutron (²⁷Al + n → ²⁸Al + γ); uranium's ²³⁴U, ²³⁵U and ²³⁸U are the natural isotopes the spectrometer separated.)
