# Lesson plan — Isotopes and the mass spectrometer

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Isotopes and the mass spectrometer
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Nuclear chemistry opened through isotopes and the machine that separated them, as Yemen's Grade 12 book opens it: chemical and nuclear reactions contrasted in an eight-row table, isotopes earned from the fractional atomic masses (oxygen 15.9994, boron 10.811) through Aston's 1919 mass spectrometer and Bainbridge's 1933 refinement, the weighted-mean formula for relative atomic mass worked for neon (20.197), and isotopes sorted into stable and radioactive kinds, with artificial radioisotopes made by bombardment
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Isotopes is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
