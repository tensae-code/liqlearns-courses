# Hands-on — Isotopes and the mass spectrometer

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Fraction detective (paper only)
Take the two fractions the lesson gives — oxygen 15.9994, boron 10.811. For each, write what mixture of whole-number isotope masses could average to it (keep it qualitative: which isotope must dominate?). Then write the one sentence that turns the fraction into evidence.

## Fence the unit (paper only)
Draw the eight-row fence as a two-column poster, Chemical | Nuclear, filling the rows the lesson names (what changes, which particles, energies, what balances, what affects the rate). The poster is the unit's gate — every later nuclear lesson stands on the right-hand side.
