# Mixtures — homogeneous and heterogeneous — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Sort water+salt and water+tea, giving the phase count and the reason for each.
**Answer: Water+salt: homogeneous, one phase — the salt dissolves in the water and the mixture looks uniform. Water+tea: heterogeneous, two phases — the tea does not dissolve and stays visible as its own phase.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What decides whether a mixture with water is homogeneous or heterogeneous, underneath the look?
**Answer: Whether the added body dissolves in water. Dissolved bodies (sugar, salt) spread evenly and show one phase; undissolved bodies (tea, sand) stay as a second, visible phase.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A pupil stirs sand in water for a long time and says 'now it must be homogeneous.' Answer the pupil.
**Answer: Stirring does not make sand dissolve. The sand stays visible as its own phase however long it is stirred, so the mixture stays heterogeneous — the sorting follows dissolving, not stirring time.**
