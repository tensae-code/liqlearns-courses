# Phase rule and phase diagrams — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Define degrees of freedom in the phase rule's sense.
**Answer: They are the intensive variables — temperature, pressure, composition — that can be changed independently, within limits, without causing a phase to appear or disappear from the system at equilibrium.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Ice, liquid water and vapour stand together. Count phases and components, and justify each count.
**Answer: Phases: 3 — three physically distinct, uniform, separable parts. Components: 1 — the single constituent H₂O expresses the composition of all three phases, so the smallest writing set has one member.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) State the phase rule and explain the 2 and the minus P.
**Answer: F = C − P + 2. The 2 counts the outside variables temperature and pressure. Each additional phase in equilibrium imposes one more condition on the system, spending one degree of freedom — hence −P: the richer the phase crowd, the less freedom remains.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) When is the reduced form F = C − P + 1 used, and why?
**Answer: When pressure is fixed in advance — the usual open-laboratory case at atmospheric pressure. One of the two outside handles is already spent, so only temperature remains in the count: the +2 becomes +1.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) A one-component system stands inside a single-phase region. Find F and say what the answer looks like on the diagram.
**Answer: F = 1 − 1 + 2 = 2: temperature and pressure can both be chosen independently. On the diagram that freedom is a two-dimensional area — the single-phase region is an area precisely because two variables are free inside it.**
