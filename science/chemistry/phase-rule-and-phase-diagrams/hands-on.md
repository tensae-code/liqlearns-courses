# Hands-on — Phase rule and phase diagrams

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Count the kitchen system (paper only)
A lidded pan holds boiling water and steam, with salt dissolved in the water. Count the phases and the components by the lesson's definitions — carefully: the dissolved salt is not a phase — then compute F and say what can still be chosen.

## Grammar of a diagram (paper only)
Sketch a one-component phase diagram from memory (area, boundary lines, triple point). Label each feature with its P and its F, computed from the rule — the sketch is correct when every label's arithmetic checks.

## Spend a freedom (paper only)
Start from liquid water alone (F = 2). Write what happens to F, step by step, as the system is brought onto the melting line and then to the triple point, naming at each step which freedom was spent and on what.
