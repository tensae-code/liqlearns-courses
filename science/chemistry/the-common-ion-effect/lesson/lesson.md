# The common ion effect

## Do it first
Ethanoic acid barely ionises. Dissolve its own salt — full of ethanoate ions — into it, and it ionises even less. Before reading, explain the squeeze with Le Chatelier's name for it.

[Picture 21718 — An equilibrium pushed back by common ions.]

The common ion effect: adding an ion that a weak electrolyte already produces suppresses its ionisation.

Le Chatelier explains it: the added common ion pushes the ionisation equilibrium back toward the un-ionised form.

It is the working principle underneath buffers, which are built next.
