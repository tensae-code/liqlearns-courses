# Lesson plan — Imperfections and electrical properties of solids

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Imperfections and electrical properties of solids
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the close of NCERT's Class XII solid-state unit (§§1.9–1.11): real crystals are imperfect — point defects that keep or break stoichiometry (Schottky and Frenkel, F-centres, doping) — and a solid's electrical life follows its electrons: conductors, insulators and the n-type and p-type semiconductors that doping creates, with the magnetic kinds (diamagnetic, paramagnetic, ferromagnetic and their kin) beside them

## Sub lessons
1. **Point defects — Schottky and Frenkel** — Stoichiometric point defects keep the crystal's formula and balance. In a Schottky defect equal numbers of cations and anions are missing altogether, so density falls (NaCl, KCl). In a Frenkel defect no ion leaves — a smaller cation is displaced into an interstitial gap, making vacancy and interstitial at once, and density is unchanged (AgCl, ZnS). The density is the test between them.
2. **Broken stoichiometry and impurity defects — F-centres and doping** — Non-stoichiometric defects break the formula's ratio: metal excess, whose trapped electrons in anion vacancies are F-centres and colour the crystal (NaCl yellow in sodium vapour, KCl violet), and metal deficiency, paid for by a higher-charged cation in metals of variable valency (FeO). Impurity defects stand foreign ions in the lattice — by accident, or deliberately, as doping.
3. **Conductors, semiconductors and magnets** — The band gap sorts the electrical kinds: conductors, whose bands overlap; insulators, whose gap no electron can jump; semiconductors, whose small gap heat and light can cross — intrinsic in pure silicon, and extrinsic when doping makes n-type (group-15 dopant, free electrons) or p-type (group-13 dopant, holes). The magnetic kinds close the unit: diamagnetic, paramagnetic, ferromagnetic, antiferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes Schottky with Frenkel → return to the density test: Schottky removes ions from the crystal (density falls); Frenkel only moves an ion into a hole inside the crystal (density unchanged).
