# Imperfections and electrical properties of solids — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Give the density test that separates Schottky from Frenkel defects, with one example of each.
**Answer: In a Schottky defect ions are missing from the crystal altogether, so density decreases (NaCl); in a Frenkel defect an ion is only displaced into an interstitial site inside the same crystal, so density is unchanged (AgCl).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What is an F-centre, and how is one made in KCl?
**Answer: An F-centre is an electron trapped in an anion vacancy, and it gives the crystal colour. Heating KCl in potassium vapour puts extra K⁺ on surface sites with chloride vacancies inside; electrons trapped in those vacancies make the crystal violet.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Build a p-type semiconductor from silicon: name the dopant's group and the carrier.
**Answer: Dope silicon with a group-13 element (B, Al, Ga or In). The dopant has one electron fewer than silicon, leaving electron holes in the lattice, and the holes move as the positive charge carriers — p-type.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a metal's conductivity fall with temperature while a semiconductor's rises?
**Answer: In a metal the carriers are already free; heating makes the lattice vibrate and obstruct them, so conductivity falls. In a semiconductor heating lifts more electrons across the small band gap, creating more carriers, so conductivity rises.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Distinguish ferromagnetic from ferrimagnetic solids by their domains.
**Answer: In a ferromagnetic solid the magnetic domains align in the same direction, giving strong permanent magnetism. In a ferrimagnetic solid the domains point in opposite directions but are unequal in number, so they do not cancel and a net magnetic moment remains (Fe₃O₄).**
