Today: **Imperfections and electrical properties of solids** — India's way: the closing sections of the Class XII Solid State unit — after the perfect lattices come the real crystals, with their missing and misplaced ions, and what those faults do to conduction and magnetism.

No real crystal is perfect. In a Schottky defect, equal numbers of cations and anions are simply missing — the crystal keeps its balance but loses density, as in sodium chloride. In a Frenkel defect nothing leaves: a small cation slips into a gap between the ions, so the crystal crowds in one place, empties in another, and keeps its density, as in silver chloride. Colour can come from a fault too: heat sodium chloride in sodium vapour and trapped electrons in empty anion sites — F-centres — turn it yellow.
Add a stranger deliberately and the fault becomes a tool. Silicon doped with phosphorus gains free electrons — an n-type semiconductor; silicon doped with boron gains holes — a p-type. Between the conductor that always flows and the insulator that never does, the doped semiconductor carries the modern world, and the magnetic kinds — dia-, para- and ferromagnetic — close the unit.
