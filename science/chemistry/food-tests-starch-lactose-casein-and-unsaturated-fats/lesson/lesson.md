# Food tests — starch, lactose, casein and unsaturated fats

## Do it first
Two foods from the fridge — a tub of margarine and a bottle of milk — hold starch, sugars, protein and unsaturated fat, all invisible. Before reading, say out loud: which single reagent could betray each one? Bulgaria's Grade 10 laboratory lesson runs the four betrayals in one sitting.

## Sub lesson 1 — Margarine — starch and unsaturation

Margarine is tested twice: **for starch — iodine in the aqueous layer** (the blue-violet colour convicts it), and **for unsaturated fatty-acid residues — KMnO₄ decolourisation**, the double bonds drinking the permanganate's colour away, exactly the unsaturation test of the hydrocarbon lessons turned onto a food.

## Sub lesson 2 — Milk — casein out, lactose proven

Fresh milk is taken apart: **casein is precipitated by dilute acetic acid** as the protein fraction; in the clear solution **lactose is proven with Cu(OH)₂**, so its functional groups can be deduced from the result; **proteins are confirmed by the biuret reaction**; and the **curdling of milk by ethanol and by CuSO₄ solution** is observed and explained — the protein's structure destroyed two more ways.

## The key ideas

- Starch: iodine → blue-violet (in margarine's aqueous layer). Unsaturated residues: KMnO₄ decolourised.
- Casein: precipitated from milk by dilute acetic acid. Lactose: proven in the clear solution with Cu(OH)₂. Protein: biuret reaction.
- Ethanol and CuSO₄ curdle milk — protein structure destroyed (denaturation at the bench).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion: margarine — starch and unsaturation; milk — casein out, lactose proven — in that order, in your own words, with the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. The iodine test on margarine's aqueous layer stays yellow-brown. Verdict? (No starch detected — the blue-violet colour is the conviction; its absence acquits.)
2. Why is casein removed before lactose is tested? (To separate the protein fraction first, so the clear solution's Cu(OH)₂ result belongs to the sugar alone — the analysis proceeds fraction by fraction.)
