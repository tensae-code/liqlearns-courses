Today: **Thermochemistry in daily life** — Malaysia's heat chapter, applied.

Some chemical changes give heat out; some take heat in. That is the whole shop. A cold pack takes heat in, so it can cool a sprained ankle without ever seeing a fridge. A hot pack gives heat out, straight into cold hands. A self-heating can hides its own little stove beside the food, and cooking fuel is the same idea grown old and ordinary: a burn chosen because it gives its heat out, steadily, where you cook.

Do not ask only what a reaction is. Ask which way its heat goes — somebody has built a product on the answer.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
