# Thermochemistry in daily life — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What question does Malaysia's closing section of the heat chapter ask, and how do its products answer it?
**Answer: It asks which heat direction a daily job needs. Products answer by packaging a change of that direction: endothermic changes where heat must be taken away, exothermic changes where heat must be supplied.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain how a cold pack cools a sprained ankle without ever being in a fridge.
**Answer: When activated, the substances inside mix and react or dissolve endothermically, absorbing heat from whatever the pack touches. Pressed to the ankle, it draws heat out of the ankle, so both pack and ankle turn cold — the cold is made on the spot.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How is a hot pack the mirror image of a cold pack?
**Answer: A hot pack packages an exothermic change instead of an endothermic one: activated the same way, it gives heat out for as long as the change runs, warming cold hands or a sore muscle instead of cooling them.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does a self-heating can work, and which earlier product is it the big cousin of?
**Answer: The can keeps an exothermic reaction in a separate compartment from the food; on activation the reaction runs and its heat passes into the food, heating the meal with no stove. It is the hot pack's idea promoted to a kitchen.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does Malaysia set cooking fuel beside the packs and cans?
**Answer: Because they are one idea at three scales: a chemical change chosen for the heat it gives out or takes in, doing daily work. Cooking fuel is chosen for its steady exothermic combustion, just as the packs and cans are chosen for their packaged heat changes.**
