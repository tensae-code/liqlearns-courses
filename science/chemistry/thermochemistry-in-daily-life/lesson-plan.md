# Lesson plan — Thermochemistry in daily life

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Thermochemistry in daily life
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** thermochemistry at work in daily life — exothermic changes giving heat out and endothermic changes taking heat in, put to work in products: cold packs, hot packs, self-heating cans and cooking fuel — met the way Malaysia's Form 5 book meets them: as the closing section of its heat chapter, applying the chapter's science to daily life
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Exothermic and endothermic — enthalpy change is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **The two directions, at the point of use** — Every product in this lesson is one of the chapter's two heat directions put to work.
2. **Cold packs and hot packs** — A **cold pack** is an endothermic change in a bag.
3. **Self-heating cans and cooking fuel** — A **self-heating can** carries its stove inside itself.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the one element: every product here is a heat direction in a package — endothermic takes heat in (cold pack), exothermic gives heat out (hot pack, self-heating can, cooking fuel).
- Thinks the cold pack stores cold → nothing cold is stored: the pack's change is endothermic, so the cold is made on the spot as the change absorbs heat from whatever the pack touches.
- Mixes up the two directions → anchor on the surroundings: exothermic = the surroundings warm up (heat exits the change); endothermic = the surroundings cool down (heat enters the change).
