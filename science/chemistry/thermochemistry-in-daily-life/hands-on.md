# Hands-on — Thermochemistry in daily life

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Warm water, cold water (home materials — two cups, water, a spoon of sugar or salt)
Fill two cups with the same amount of room-temperature water and feel both. Stir a spoon of sugar into one cup and feel both again after a minute; note any change in how the water feels and write down which cup changed and which way. Say it in lesson terms: dissolving is a change that can take in or give out a little heat — the packs in the lesson run changes chosen to do this strongly, on purpose. (Observation only — do not taste or drink the experiment water.)

## The applications hunt (observation only — home and street)
Find three products around your home, a pharmacy or a sports shop whose job is heating or cooling without a plug or a fridge — an instant cold pack, a hand warmer, a self-heating drink, camping fuel. For each, write the job it does and the heat direction — in or out — the job needs, as Malaysia's section sorts its applications.

## Which direction does the job need? (paper exercise)
List six daily jobs: cooling a sprain, warming cold hands, heating soup on a journey, cooking rice, cooling a fever, keeping a delivery meal hot. Sort them into two columns — needs heat taken in / needs heat given out — and defend each placing in one line.
