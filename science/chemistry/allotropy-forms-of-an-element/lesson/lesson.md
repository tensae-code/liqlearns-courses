# Allotropy — forms of an element

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's thing is, in one sentence — then read on and check it against Armenia's way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — One element, several forms

**Allotropy** is one element existing in **more than one form** — the same atoms, arranged differently, giving substances with different properties. Armenia's Grade 7 **teacher guide** deepens the idea deliberately as an extension of Theme 2.8 (guide PDF p. 53) — this lesson stands on that guide evidence, labelled as such.

## Sub lesson 2 — Molecular against non-molecular structure

The guide's way of deepening is a structural sorting: substances of **molecular structure** against substances of **non-molecular structure**. Which side of that sorting a form stands on follows from how its atoms are held — in separate molecules, or in a structure with no separate molecules in it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ozone among the examples

**Ozone** is named in the guide **among the examples** of allotropy: oxygen's atoms also form ozone, a different substance from the oxygen of the air, though both are built from the one element. The example carries the lesson's point — same element, different form, different substance.

## The key ideas

- **Allotropy**: one element, **more than one form** — same atoms, different arrangement, different properties.
- The guide deepens it by the **molecular against non-molecular** structure sorting.
- **Ozone** is the guide's named example among the forms of oxygen.
