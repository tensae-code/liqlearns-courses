# Lesson plan — Formic acid — the acid that pretends to be an aldehyde

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Formic acid — the acid that pretends to be an aldehyde
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Carboxylic acids surveyed as a family (fatty, aromatic — benzoic acid; dicarboxylic — oxalic acid as analytical reductant), and formic acid's double life: its structure holds both —COOH and an aldehyde-like H—C=O, so it is acidic AND gives a silver mirror, ending as CO₂ + H₂O; a research task designs the experiment chain that orders acetic acid > carbonic acid > phenol by strength (with saturated NaHCO₃ as the CO₂ scrubber in the apparatus).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
