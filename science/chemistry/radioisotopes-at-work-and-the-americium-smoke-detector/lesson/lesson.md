# Radioisotopes at work — and the americium smoke detector

## Do it first
Before you read: the small plastic box on a ceiling holds a radioactive source — deliberately, in millions of homes. Say out loud: what could a radioactive source possibly do for fire safety? Then read on: Egypt's STS section opens the smoke detector, then walks the isotope uses list.

## Sub lesson 1 — The smoke detector — americium at work

Inside the detector, **americium-241** emits **alpha particles that ionise the air** in a small chamber; the ions carry a tiny steady **current** between two plates. When **smoke enters, it stops the current** — the ion flow is broken — and the drop **triggers the alarm**. The radioactivity is not a hazard bolted on; it is the sensing element itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Medicine and food — imaging and gamma

Egypt's uses list runs the working isotopes: **technetium-99 and thallium-201 in medical imaging**, lighting up organs for the camera; **cobalt-60's gamma rays in food irradiation** — killing the microbes that spoil food — and in **wheat breeding**, where irradiation raises new varieties.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tracers and dating — following atoms

A **phosphorus tracer** in a plant is followed with a **Geiger counter** to watch the element travel. **Oxygen-18** settled a famous question: labelled water proved **photosynthesis's oxygen comes from the water**, not the CO₂. And **carbon-14 dating** closes the list at home — applied to a **pharaonic mummy**, reading the age of ancient Egypt from its own remains.

## The key ideas

- **Americium-241** alpha ionises the detector's air; the ions carry a current — **smoke stops the current and triggers the alarm**.
- **Tc-99 and Tl-201** image the body's organs; **Co-60** gamma irradiates food and breeds new wheat.
- **P tracers** are followed by Geiger counter; **O-18** proved photosynthesis oxygen comes from water; **C-14** dated a pharaonic mummy.
