# Lesson plan — Paints — water-based and oil-based, and how they dry

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Paints — water-based and oil-based, and how they dry
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Paints divided by their solvent, as Yemen's Grade 12 book divides them: water-based emulsion paints of styrene-butadiene, polyvinyl acetate and acrylic polymers, coloured with pigments such as titanium dioxide for white and the lead compounds banned from 1977 after child poisonings, with barium sulfate and carbonate as extenders — against oil-based paints built on polymerised oils with turpentine as the classic solvent; and the two drying mechanisms contrasted step by step: the water paint simply loses its water by evaporation, while the oil paint first loses its solvent, then the oil itself dries chemically as oxygen stitches the unsaturated chains into a hard cross-linked film
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Polymers — chains of monomers is recommended first, never forced.
