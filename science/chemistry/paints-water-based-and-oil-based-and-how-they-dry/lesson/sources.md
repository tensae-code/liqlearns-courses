| Unit 8 (Yemen, Grade 12) | Book 1 (Grade 12) — Unit 8 — printed pp. 162–164 — Paints divide by their solvent: water-based emulsion paints — sticky polymer emulsions of styrene and butadiene, polyvinyl acetate, and acrylics polymerised from acrylonitrile (strong adhesion, rust resistance), coloured with pigments such as titanium dioxide for white and the lead compounds (PbCO3, Pb(OH)2, PbSO4) that once coloured most paints until many countries banned them from 1977 after child poisonings and deaths, with barium sulfate and carbonate as extenders — and oil-based paints, all built on polymerised oils (linseed, castor, coconut) with turpentine as the classic solvent; the drying mechanisms are contrasted step by step: a water paint simply loses its water by evaporation and the film sticks, while an oil paint first loses its solvent, then the oil itself dries chemically — an oxygen atom from the air takes the place of a hydrogen on neighbouring unsaturated chains, stitching the chains together into a hard cross-linked film, the equation drawn. |
