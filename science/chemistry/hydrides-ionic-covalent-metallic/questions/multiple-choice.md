# Hydrides — ionic, covalent and metallic — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A hydride is…
a) Hydrogen on its own  b) A compound of hydrogen with another element  c) Any compound containing oxygen  d) A kind of acid only
**Answer: b) A compound of hydrogen with another element**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Fiji's book sorts the hydride family by…
a) Colour  b) Bonding type — ionic, covalent and metallic  c) Alphabetical order  d) Price
**Answer: b) Bonding type — ionic, covalent and metallic**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) In an ionic hydride, hydrogen sits in the solid as…
a) H⁺  b) A shared pair only  c) The hydride ion, H⁻  d) A free gas
**Answer: c) The hydride ion, H⁻**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Water, ammonia and methane are examples of…
a) Ionic hydrides  b) Covalent hydrides  c) Metallic hydrides  d) No hydrides at all
**Answer: b) Covalent hydrides**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) In a metallic hydride, hydrogen is held…
a) As separate water molecules  b) In the gaps inside a metal's lattice  c) Only on the surface as paint  d) As H⁻ in a salt crystal
**Answer: b) In the gaps inside a metal's lattice**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) The general trend across hydrides is that…
a) All hydrides behave the same  b) The kind follows the partner element, and the properties follow the bonding  c) Hydrogen decides everything alone  d) Trends run backwards from properties to partners
**Answer: b) The kind follows the partner element, and the properties follow the bonding**
