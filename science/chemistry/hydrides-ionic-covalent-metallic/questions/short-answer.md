# Hydrides — ionic, covalent and metallic — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Define a hydride and name its three kinds.
**Answer: A hydride is a compound of hydrogen with another element. Its three kinds, sorted by bonding, are ionic, covalent and metallic hydrides.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does Fiji's book study hydrides as one family if they behave so differently?
**Answer: Because the comparison is the lesson: the same hydrogen takes different characters with different partners, and sorting the family by bonding shows why each kind behaves as it does.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How does hydrogen sit in an ionic hydride, and what happens when it meets water?
**Answer: Hydrogen sits as the hydride ion, H⁻, in a salt-like crystal with an active metal. With water it reacts fiercely, giving the metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) How does hydrogen sit in a covalent hydride? Name two examples.
**Answer: Hydrogen shares electrons with a non-metal, forming separate molecules — as in water, ammonia and methane.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What is a metallic hydride, and why is it studied as hydrogen storage?
**Answer: A metallic hydride holds hydrogen atoms in the gaps of a metal's lattice; the metal keeps its character and can take hydrogen in and give it back, so hydrogen is stored inside a solid.**
