Today: **Hydrides** — hydrogen's whole family, in Fiji's Year 13 book.

A hydride is hydrogen joined to another element — and the partner decides everything. With an active metal, hydrogen becomes an ion, H⁻, in a salt-like solid that gives hydrogen back to water. With a non-metal, hydrogen shares electrons and makes molecules — water, ammonia, methane. Inside a metal's lattice, hydrogen hides in the gaps, stored in a solid.

One family, three behaviours — and the bonding explains them all.
