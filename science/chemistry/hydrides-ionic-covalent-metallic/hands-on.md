# Hands-on — Hydrides — ionic, covalent and metallic

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort the family (paper exercise)
Write these hydrides on cards: a salt-like hydride of an active metal, water, ammonia, methane, and a hydrogen-bearing transition metal. Sort the cards into three piles — ionic, covalent, metallic — and under each card write how hydrogen sits in it: as H⁻, in a shared pair, or in the lattice gaps. Defend each pile in one line.

## The water test, on paper (paper exercise — no chemicals)
Two hydrides meet water on paper: the ionic one and the covalent one, water itself. Write what happens in each case and why, in lesson terms — one reacts and releases hydrogen gas; the other simply mixes as molecules. Say which observation would tell the two kinds apart in a laboratory, and why that test must be a teacher's demonstration, never a home experiment.

## Walk the table (paper exercise)
Draw a rough periodic-table outline — active metals on the left, transition metals in the middle, non-metals on the right. Write IONIC, METALLIC and COVALENT over the three regions for their hydrides, and underneath write the trend in one sentence: the kind follows the partner, and the properties follow the bonding.
