# Phlogiston — the theory that burning died of

## Do it first
Before you read: something burns and a grey ash-like powder is left. Say out loud: did the burning thing lose something, or gain something — and how could a balance settle the argument? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 10 book teaches the theory that ruled chemistry for most of the 1700s, and then shows the weighing that killed it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The theory — burning lets phlogiston out

**Stahl's phlogiston theory** (from 1701) explained burning with an invisible substance: everything that burns holds **phlogiston**, and burning **releases** it. A metal that is heated in air turns to a powdery **calx** — in the theory's language, the metal has **lost its phlogiston**: calx = metal minus phlogiston.

## Sub lesson 2 — Charcoal the giver — how metals came back

The theory also explained how a calx becomes a metal again. **Charcoal is rich in phlogiston**; heat a calx with charcoal and the charcoal **gives phlogiston back** to the calx, restoring the metal. Plant charcoal "rich in phlogiston" turning calx back to metal was the theory's everyday proof — smelting worked, and the theory could say why.

## Sub lesson 3 — The weighing that killed it

The theory failed on the balance. **Lavoisier proved that sulfur and phosphorus gain weight when they burn.** If burning released phlogiston, the burned substance should weigh less, not more — the gain could only be saved by giving phlogiston **negative weight**, and a substance with negative weight is no substance at all. The theory died of a weighing: burning is not a loss of phlogiston but a **joining with something from the air** — oxygen.

## The key ideas

- **Phlogiston theory** (Stahl, 1701): burning **releases phlogiston**; a metal's **calx is the metal minus phlogiston**.
- **Charcoal, rich in phlogiston**, restores a calx to its metal by giving phlogiston back — the theory's account of smelting.
- The theory **fails on Lavoisier's weighing**: sulfur and phosphorus **gain** weight on burning, so phlogiston would need **negative weight**.

## Do It Yourself
1. A metal is heated in air and its calx weighs more than the metal did. State the phlogiston problem in one sentence. (If burning only let phlogiston out, the calx should weigh less; a heavier calx forces phlogiston to have negative weight, which destroys the theory.)
2. Why did smelting with charcoal seem to prove phlogiston? (Because the theory could explain it neatly: charcoal, rich in phlogiston, gives phlogiston back to the calx and the metal returns — a working process matched the story, until the weighings were done carefully.)
3. What replaced phlogiston's account of burning? (Burning as combination with oxygen from the air — the burned substance gains the oxygen's weight, which is why sulfur and phosphorus weigh more after burning.)
