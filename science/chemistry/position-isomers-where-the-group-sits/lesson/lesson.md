# Position isomers — where the group sits

## Do it first
Before you read: two bottles hold compounds with exactly the same molecular formula, yet they are different substances. Say out loud: if nothing about which atoms are present has changed, what else could possibly differ between them? Then read on: Kuwait's Grade 11 book gives one answer its own name — the place where the group sits.

## Sub lesson 1 — Isomers — the ground this lesson stands on

Isomers are molecules with the **same molecular formula but different structures**. The atoms are the same in kind and number; the arrangement differs, and a different arrangement makes a different compound, with its own properties. Position isomers are one named way that different arrangement happens.

## Sub lesson 2 — The group's place changes — the formula does not

Kuwait's book defines the kind in one move (§2.4, printed p. 98): **position isomers arise when the functional group's place on the main carbon chain changes**, the compounds keeping the same molecular formula. The chain is the same, the group is the same — only the carbon it sits on differs. Move the group from one carbon of the chain to the next and you have a second compound: same formula, different structure, a position isomer of the first.

## Sub lesson 3 — Telling them apart — the position number

Because the group's place is the whole difference, the **name must carry that place**: the position of the functional group is numbered along the main chain, and the number is part of the compound's name. Two compounds whose names differ only in that position number — the group at carbon 1 against the group at carbon 2, say — are position isomers: one formula, two places, two substances.

## The key ideas

- **Isomers**: same molecular formula, different structures — different compounds.
- **Position isomers**: the functional group's **place on the main carbon chain** changes while the molecular formula stays the same (Kuwait, Unit 5, §2.4, printed p. 98).
- The group's place is told in the **name by its position number** — names differing only in that number name position isomers.
