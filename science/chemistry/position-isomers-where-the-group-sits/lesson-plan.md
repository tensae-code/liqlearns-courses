## Sub lessons
1. **Isomers — the ground this lesson stands on** — Isomers are molecules with the **same molecular formula but different structures**. The atoms are the same in kind and number; the arrangement differs, and a different arrangement makes a different compound, with its own properties. Position isomers are one named way that different arrangement happens.
2. **The group's place changes — the formula does not** — Kuwait's book defines the kind in one move (§2.4, printed p. 98): **position isomers arise when the functional group's place on the main carbon chain changes**, the compounds keeping the same molecular formula. The chain is the same, the group is the same — only the carbon it sits on differs. Move the group from one carbon of the chain to the next and you have a second compound: same formula, different structure, a position isomer of the first.
3. **Telling them apart — the position number** — Because the group's place is the whole difference, the **name must carry that place**: the position of the functional group is numbered along the main chain, and the number is part of the compound's name. Two compounds whose names differ only in that position number — the group at carbon 1 against the group at carbon 2, say — are position isomers: one formula, two places, two substances.

## If the learner is stuck
- Position isomers confused with different formulas → check the formula first: if the formulas differ, they are not isomers of any kind. / Position confused with branching → branching changes the chain's shape; position isomerism keeps the same main chain and moves only the functional group's place on it.
