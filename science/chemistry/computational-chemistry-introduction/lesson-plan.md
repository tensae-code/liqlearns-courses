# Lesson plan — Computational chemistry — an introduction

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Computational chemistry — an introduction
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** school-level computational chemistry as Vietnam's Grade 10 study-topics book teaches it (Topic 3, Bài 11): using a computer to calculate a molecule's structural parameters (bond lengths, bond angles, shape) and energy parameters, and reading those numbers as chemistry
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. VSEPR — the shapes of molecules is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Computing a molecule's structure** — The last practical of Vietnam's advanced Grade 10 book (Bài 11) turns the computer from simulator to calculator: **computing structural and energy parameters** of molecules. A molecule is given to the program as atoms and connections; the program works out the arrangement the molecule settles into (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
2. **Computing a molecule's energy** — The same calculation returns **energy parameters**: how much energy the molecule holds in that arrangement, and how the energy changes when the molecule bends, stretches or reacts. Comparing energies compares stabilities (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
3. **Why chemists compute** — Computation reaches where measurement cannot go alone: molecules too unstable to isolate, reactions too fast to watch, and designs not yet made (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Reads computed numbers as measurements → return to sub lesson 2: the number comes from a model of the molecule; agreement with measurement is what earns trust.
