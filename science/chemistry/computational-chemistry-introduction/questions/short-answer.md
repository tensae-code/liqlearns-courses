# Computational chemistry — an introduction — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How does a computation find a molecule's shape?
**Answer: It prices candidate arrangements and keeps the steadiest — the geometry of lowest energy — reporting its bond lengths, angles and shape; this is VSEPR's prediction made numerical.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Two computed shapes have energies −500 and −498. Which occurs, and by what rule?
**Answer: The −500 shape: of the arrangements a molecule can take, nature keeps the one of lowest energy.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must computed results answer to measurement?
**Answer: The computation runs on a simplified model of the molecule; only agreement with measured bond lengths and energies shows the model is faithful enough to trust.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name one thing computation can study that a bench cannot easily reach.
**Answer: Any one: a molecule too unstable to isolate, a reaction too fast to watch, or a material/drug not yet synthesised.**
