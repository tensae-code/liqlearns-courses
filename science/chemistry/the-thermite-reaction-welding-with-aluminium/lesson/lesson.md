# The thermite reaction — welding with aluminium

## Do it first
Before you read: two railway rails must be joined in the open countryside, with no power line and no furnace. Say out loud: where could the heat to melt steel possibly come from, if it has to travel in a bag? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book answers with a powder that carries its own furnace — thermite.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why aluminium can steal oxygen

Aluminium's chemistry is ruled by its **hunger for oxygen**: it binds oxygen more strongly than many metals bind it in their oxides. So aluminium works as a **reducing agent for winning metals** — it takes the oxygen for itself and leaves the other metal free: **3MnO₂ + 4Al → 3Mn + 2Al₂O₃**. The reaction pays out its stolen oxygen as heat — a great deal of heat.

## Sub lesson 2 — Thermite — the mixture and the lighting

**Thermite** is that hunger packaged: a mixture of **aluminium powder and iron(III) oxide**, placed in a **crucible**. It will not light from a match — it is lit with a **magnesium ribbon**, whose fierce burning supplies the starting heat. Once lit, the reaction needs nothing from outside: the aluminium takes the oxide's oxygen and the iron is left molten.

## Sub lesson 3 — Welding at 3500 °C

The equation is the lesson's centre: **Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃** — and the temperature reached is about **3500 °C**, far above iron's melting point. The iron is born liquid and runs into the joint between **rail ends**, freezing there as a weld: a furnace that travels in a bag, lit by a ribbon, joining rails where no power line reaches.

## The key ideas

- Aluminium's **hunger for oxygen** makes it a **reducing agent** that wins metals from their oxides (3MnO₂ + 4Al → 3Mn + 2Al₂O₃).
- **Thermite** = aluminium powder + iron(III) oxide, lit by a **magnesium ribbon**, in a crucible.
- **Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃** at about **3500 °C** — the iron is born molten and **welds rail joints**.

## Do It Yourself
1. In the thermite equation, which substance is oxidised, which is reduced, and what plays each part? (Aluminium is oxidised (it takes oxygen, becoming Al₂O₃) and is the reducing agent; iron(III) oxide is reduced (it loses oxygen, becoming Fe) and is the oxidising agent.)
2. Why is a magnesium ribbon needed if the reaction gives out so much heat? (The mixture needs a very hot start before its own heat can sustain it; the magnesium ribbon's fierce burning supplies that starting heat — after ignition the reaction runs on its own heat.)
3. Why is thermite fit for welding rails in open countryside where a furnace is not? (It carries its furnace with it: powder and crucible travel in a bag, and the reaction itself reaches about 3500 °C, melting the iron it makes exactly where the joint is.)
