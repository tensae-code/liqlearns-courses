# Recognising substances by their properties

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's thing is, in one sentence — then read on and check it against Armenia's way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Properties are the fingerprints

A substance is known by what can be **observed** about it — how it looks, what it does when it is heated, mixed or tested. Armenia's Grade 7 book makes this a lesson of its own (§1.4, printed p. 22): before a substance is named, its **properties** are watched and recorded, and the name follows from the evidence of the properties, not the other way round.

## Sub lesson 2 — The group experiment — identify the unknown

The lesson is **group experimental work**: a group is given substances and must **identify** each one from its observed properties. Each test adds one fact; the facts together single out one substance. A property that two substances share cannot separate them — the group looks for the property where they **differ**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The indicator clue — litmus

Among the observed properties the book uses **indicator behaviour with litmus**: a substance that turns litmus one way and a substance that turns it the other have told you something about themselves without being named. The colour change is a property like any other in this lesson — observed first, used as a clue, and only then read as an identification.

## The key ideas

- A substance is identified from its **observed properties** — the name follows the evidence.
- **Group experimental work** is Armenia's way in: test, record, compare, then name.
- **Litmus behaviour** is one of the observed properties — an indicator clue to a substance's identity.
