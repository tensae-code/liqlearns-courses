# Lesson plan — Recognising substances by their properties

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Recognising substances by their properties
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** recognising a substance by its observed properties — group experimental work identifying substances, including indicator behaviour with litmus, as Armenia's Grade 7 textbook teaches it in Theme 1, §1.4 (printed p. 22)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. No other lesson is needed first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Properties are the fingerprints** — A substance is known by what can be **observed** about it — how it looks, what it does when it is heated, mixed or tested.
2. **The group experiment — identify the unknown** — The lesson is **group experimental work**: a group is given substances and must **identify** each one from its observed properties.
3. **The indicator clue — litmus** — Among the observed properties the book uses **indicator behaviour with litmus**: a substance that turns litmus one way and a substance that turns it the other have told you something about themselves without being named.
