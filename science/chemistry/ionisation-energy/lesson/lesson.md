## Ionisation energy

Ionisation energy is the energy an isolated gaseous atom in its ground state must absorb to discharge an electron, forming a cation (kJ/mol). Successive ionisations cost ever more (the ion is increasingly positive). Trends: IE rises across a period and falls down a group (larger radius and more shielding = more weakly held electron), with the named exceptions — Group IIA above IIIA and Group VA above VIA, explained by filled and half-filled configurations;

caesium is the lowest, fluorine the highest (helium and neon excepted). Worked comparisons use shielding arguments (O vs S; second IE of Li vs Be).
