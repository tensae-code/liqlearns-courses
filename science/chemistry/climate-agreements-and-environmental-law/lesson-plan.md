# Lesson plan — Climate agreements and environmental law

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Climate agreements and environmental law
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The world's line of agreements — Rio, Kyoto, Paris, Katowice — and the citizen's right and duty at home in Bulgaria's constitution (Bulgaria, Grade 10 — §64, printed p. 168; §66, printed p. 171)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Air pollution is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The agreement line** — **Rio 1992** — the UN Framework Convention: the developed industrial countries carry the **greatest responsibility**. **Kyoto 1997** — the first **binding targets** and the **carbon-credit quota system (one credit = one tonne of CO₂)**, in force from **2005**. **Paris 2015** — hold the global rise **under 2 °C**, every country filing **its own action plan**. **Katowice 2018** — under the motto **'From black to green'**.
2. **The law at home — and the closing project** — At home, the **Bulgarian constitution's Article 55** gives citizens the **right to a healthy environment and the duty to protect it**; the **Environmental Protection Act** sets the framework; national data show industry using only **26.7% of produced energy but 83.62% of consumed water**, with greenhouse emissions **down about 41% over 1990–2015**. The closing project lesson turns law into conduct: **waste archaeology** (accumulations 200,000 years old; Athens' first municipal dump in 400 BC; Rome's collection service in 200 BC), four household situations audited, an **ecological code** to complete and actually apply (know the laws, buy environment-safe goods, collect waste separately, hand hazardous waste in apart), a traffic-light poster for younger pupils, research into Bulgaria's geothermal waters, and a structured debate — 'The environment in which I live and I'.
