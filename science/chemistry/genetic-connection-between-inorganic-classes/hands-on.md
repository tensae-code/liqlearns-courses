# Hands-on — The genetic connection between the classes of inorganic substances

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Lay the two chains on the table
Write these formulas on eight cards: Ca, CaO, Ca(OH)₂, CaCl₂, S, SO₂, H₂SO₃, Na₂SO₃. Lay them out as two chains of four, saying out loud the class of each card as you place it. Then shuffle one chain and rebuild it from memory, naming the reaction on every arrow.

## Draw the map (paper only)
Draw the full system map: the metal chain across the top, the nonmetal chain across the bottom, and the meeting at the salt in the middle. Add zinc oxide in a box straddling both chains, with two arrows out of it — one to its reaction with an acid, one to its reaction with an alkali. Under the map, write one sentence saying what the straddling box proves.

## Chain detective
You are given three starting substances: Mg, CO₂ and Al(OH)₃. For each, write the chain it belongs on (metal, nonmetal, or the amphoteric crossing) and the next stop it can reach, with the equation. Check your answers against sub lessons 2 and 3.
