# The genetic connection between the classes of inorganic substances

## Do it first
Before you read: a piece of calcium can, step by step, end up inside a lump of calcium chloride salt — and a lump of sulfur can end up inside a salt too, by a completely different road. Say out loud: what stops on the road do you think each one passes through — and where do the two roads meet? Then read on: Belarus teaches the classes of inorganic substances this way on purpose — before the classes are drilled one by one, §3 joins them into one connected system, and that joining is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — One family, not four boxes

Inorganic substances are sorted into classes — **oxides, acids, bases and salts** are the four great classes of the system. Taught as lists, they are four boxes to memorise. Belarus' Grade 10 book refuses the boxes: its §3 is titled the **interrelation between the classes**, and the interrelation has a name — the **genetic connection** (*genetic* as in genesis, origin: each member is generated from the one before).

The claim is simple and checkable: **substances of different classes can be transformed into one another in chains**, because the element at the chain's heart travels through every stop. The calcium in a piece of calcium metal is the same calcium in its oxide, its hydroxide and its salts. The chain is the element's biography, written in reactions.

## Sub lesson 2 — The two great chains — metal and nonmetal

Take a **typical metal** — calcium. Its chain runs:

**Ca → CaO → Ca(OH)₂ → CaCl₂**

- Metal to **basic oxide**: the metal burns in oxygen — 2Ca + O₂ → 2CaO.
- Basic oxide to **base**: the oxide reacts with water — CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂.
- Base to **salt**: the base is neutralised by an acid — Ca(OH)₂ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + 2H₂O.

Now take a **typical nonmetal** — sulfur. Its chain is the mirror image:

**S → SO₂ → H₂SO₃ → Na₂SO₃**

- Nonmetal to **acidic oxide**: the nonmetal burns in oxygen — S + O₂ → SO₂.
- Acidic oxide to **acid**: the oxide reacts with water — SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃.
- Acid to **salt**: the acid is neutralised by a base — H₂SO₃ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₃ + 2H₂O.

Read the two chains against each other and the pattern shows itself: **metal → basic oxide → base → salt** on one side, **nonmetal → acidic oxide → acid → salt** on the other — and the chains **meet at the salt**, because salts are exactly where acids and bases end up when they react together. One element, one road; the class of each stop is set by what the element is travelling with.

## Sub lesson 3 — Walking a chain — and the amphoteric crossing

A chain is only as good as its arrows, and every arrow is a reaction type already met: **combination with oxygen** (burning), **combination with water**, and **neutralisation**. Given any stop, the next stop is found by asking what that class reacts with — an oxide meets water or an acid or an alkali; a base meets an acid; an acid meets a base or a metal.

The crossing that proves the system is one: **amphoteric** oxides and hydroxides — zinc oxide, aluminium hydroxide and their kin — stand on both roads at once. ZnO reacts with hydrochloric acid as a basic oxide (ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O) and with sodium hydroxide as an acidic one (ZnO + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O). A substance that walks both chains is the living proof that the two chains are one connected system.

That is why Belarus places this lesson where it does — at the head of the classes system, before the year drills each class in turn. Learn the map first, and every later section is a stop on a road the learner already knows.

## The key ideas

- The **genetic connection** is the system of transformations linking the classes of inorganic substances — oxides, acids, bases and salts — into one family, each member generated from the one before.
- A typical metal walks **metal → basic oxide → base → salt** (calcium: Ca → CaO → Ca(OH)₂ → CaCl₂); a typical nonmetal walks **nonmetal → acidic oxide → acid → salt** (sulfur: S → SO₂ → H₂SO₃ → Na₂SO₃).
- The chains **meet at the salt**, formed where an acid and a base neutralise each other.
- Every arrow is a known reaction: burning in oxygen, reaction with water, neutralisation.
- **Amphoteric** oxides and hydroxides react with both acids and alkalis — the crossing that proves the two chains are one system.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the system to your companion in your own words: walk calcium down the metal chain and sulfur down the nonmetal chain, naming the class at every stop and the reaction on every arrow, and finish with the amphoteric crossing — what zinc oxide does that a purely basic oxide cannot.
