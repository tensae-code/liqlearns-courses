# Chemical elements in the human body

## Do it first
Before reading, say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is: chemical elements in the human body. Then read on — this is Kazakhstan's way of teaching it, as the Kazakhstan placements describe the books' own treatment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 5.1 — Kazakhstan's Grade 7 book (Grade 7, NIS (Kazakh))

Elements of life: body composition by mass (O 65%, C 18.5%, H 9.5%, N 3.2%), and macro-, micro- and ultra-microelements (Ca, F, I, Na, K, Fe) with their jobs and deficiency signs. (Kazakhstan, Grade 7 — Grade 7, NIS (Kazakh), Ch. 5.1, printed p. 44).

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. VIII, §18 — Kazakhstan's Grade 7 book (Grade 7, Ospanova (Uzbek))

Elements in the human body: a distribution figure (O, C, H, N, Ca, P…), macroelements (>0.005%) versus microelements, and a table of Ca, P, K, Cl, S, Na giving food sources, jobs in the body and deficiency signs; proteins, fats and carbohydrates as C/H/O(/N) compounds with calorie shares. (Kazakhstan, Grade 7 — Grade 7, Ospanova (Uzbek), Ch. VIII, §18, printed pp. 100–104).

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 11 §11.1–11.2 Chemical elements in living cells; macro- and microelements in the human organism — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9, NIS trial)

The element inventory of a person and what each group does. (Kazakhstan, Grade 9 — Grade 9, NIS trial, Ch. 11 §11.1–11.2 Chemical elements in living cells; macro- and microelements in the human organism, printed p. 164).

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. 11 §11.3 Balanced nutrition. The diet — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9, NIS trial)

Nutrition read chemically: what a balanced ration must supply. (Kazakhstan, Grade 9 — Grade 9, NIS trial, Ch. 11 §11.3 Balanced nutrition. The diet, printed p. 168).

## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. IX §46 Chemical composition of the human organism. Macro- and microelements — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9 Pt 2, Ospanova)

Carbon chains as the frame of proteins (molecules up to a billion links); iron in haemoglobin — 2–6 g in blood, about 12 mg needed daily, deficiency causing anaemia (same proposed slug as Book 21). (Kazakhstan, Grade 9 — Grade 9 Pt 2, Ospanova, Ch. IX §46 Chemical composition of the human organism. Macro- and microelements, printed p. 70).

## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. IX §47 Determination of some elements in food products — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9 Pt 2, Ospanova)

Iron in food detected with potassium thiocyanate turning blood-red; laboratories find calcium in bones and carbon in foods. (Kazakhstan, Grade 9 — Grade 9 Pt 2, Ospanova, Ch. IX §47 Determination of some elements in food products, printed p. 75).

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. 8.2 + Practice work №4 — Kazakhstan's Grade 7 book (Grade 7, Zhumagulov et al.)

The "big 7" elements making ~99% of body mass (O 65%, C 18.5%, H 9.5%, N 3%, Ca 1.5%, P 1%, K 0.3%) with each one's job, and food tests for nutrients. (Kazakhstan, Grade 7 — Grade 7, Zhumagulov et al., Ch. 8.2 + Practice work №4, printed pp. 132–135).

## Sub lesson 8 — Ch. 10 §10.1 The chemical composition of the human body — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9, ed. Baikenov (English))

## Sub lesson 9 — Ch. 10 §10.2 Important nutrition in your food — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9, ed. Baikenov (English))

Nutrients read as chemical supply. (Kazakhstan, Grade 9 — Grade 9, ed. Baikenov (English), Ch. 10 §10.2 Important nutrition in your food, PDF p. 314).

## Sub lesson 10 — Ch. 9 §40 Chemical composition of the human organism. Macro- and microelements and their significance — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9, Usmanova et al.)

Macroelements at tenths of a percent and above, microelements below 0.01% (glossary definitions) (same proposed slug as Books 21/27/31). (Kazakhstan, Grade 9 — Grade 9, Usmanova et al., Ch. 9 §40 Chemical composition of the human organism. Macro- and microelements and their significance, printed p. 197).

## Sub lesson 11 — Ch. 9 §41 Determination of some chemical elements of the organism — Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book (Grade 9, Usmanova et al.)

## The key ideas

- The chemical elements a person is made of: the macroelements that make up almost all body mass (oxygen 65%, carbon 18.5%, hydrogen 9.5%, nitrogen about 3%, then calcium, phosphorus, potassium and the rest) and the microelements needed in traces — what each group does in the body, how elements are detected in food and in the organism, and what a balanced diet must supply.
