# Corrosion — iron as its own cell

## Do it first
A drop of water on iron is a tiny battery: iron dissolves at one spot of the drop and rust blooms at another. Before reading, say which two things from the air and sky the cell needs.

[Picture 21745 — A droplet on iron, zones marked.]

Rusting is electrochemical: at anodic regions iron is oxidised, Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻; at cathodic regions oxygen and water take the electrons, O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻.

Fe²⁺ and OH⁻ meet and oxidise further to hydrated iron(III) oxide — rust, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O as presented.

Corrosion needs both oxygen and water; prevention routes (barriers, galvanising, sacrificial protection) are introduced here from the cell picture.
