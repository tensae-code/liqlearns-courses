# Video script — Corrosion — iron as its own cell

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A drop of water on iron is a tiny battery: iron dissolves at one spot of the drop and rust blooms at another.

Rusting is electrochemical: at anodic regions iron is oxidised, Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻; at cathodic regions oxygen and water take the electrons, O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻.

Fe²⁺ and OH⁻ meet and oxidise further to hydrated iron(III) oxide — rust, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O as presented.

Corrosion needs both oxygen and water; prevention routes (barriers, galvanising, sacrificial protection) are introduced here from the cell picture.

Now you. Say it back: What two things does rusting need?
Answer: Oxygen and water.
