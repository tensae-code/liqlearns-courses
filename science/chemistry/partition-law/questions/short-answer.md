# The partition law — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the partition law and name its constant.
**Answer:** At a fixed temperature, a solute distributing itself between two immiscible solvents settles so that the ratio of its concentrations in the two layers is constant — that constant is the partition (distribution) coefficient, K = C(1)/C(2).

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does doubling the total solute not change K?
**Answer:** Because both layers' concentrations rise together in the same proportion — the law fixes the ratio, not either concentration on its own.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A solute favours an organic layer with K = 9 toward that layer. What does the 9 tell a chemist planning an extraction?
**Answer:** At equilibrium the solute's concentration in the organic layer is nine times its concentration in the other layer, so most of it crosses in one shaking — the solvent is a strong extracting choice.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two uses of the partition law given in this lesson.
**Answer:** Solvent extraction (recovering a solute by shaking it into a preferred immiscible solvent) and analysis by distribution — including determining the formula of a complex ion from how partition shifts as ligand is added.

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What condition on the solute's state must hold for the simple ratio to be trusted?
**Answer:** The solute must be in the same molecular state in both solvents (and the solution dilute, at a fixed temperature); if it associates or reacts in one layer, the counted species has changed and the simple ratio breaks.
