# Hands-on — The partition law

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Check the ratio on paper
A solute is shaken with two immiscible solvents and settles with these concentrations: trial 1 — 0.30 mol/L in A and 0.10 mol/L in B; trial 2 — 0.12 mol/L in A and 0.04 mol/L in B; trial 3 — 0.45 mol/L in A and 0.15 mol/L in B. Calculate K = C(A)/C(B) for each trial and write, in one sentence, what the three answers together prove about the partition law. Then predict the concentration in A when the concentration in B is 0.07 mol/L.

## Plan the better extraction (paper only)
You must extract a solute from 100 cm³ of water using 100 cm³ of an immiscible extracting solvent. Plan A uses it all at once; Plan B uses four portions of 25 cm³. Without any apparatus, write the two plans step by step, and under each write what the partition law says about the fraction left behind after each shaking — then state which plan you would hand to a laboratory and the law-based reason.

## Follow the complex-ion determination in words
Draw three boxes labelled "free metal ion", "ligand added a little", "ligand added more". Under each, sketch two layers and shade the top layer darker or lighter to show the partition shifting as more of the metal is bound into the complex. Under the drawing, write two sentences: what the shifting partition is measuring, and how the measurements settle the complex ion's formula.
