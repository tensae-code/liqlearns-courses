Today: **The partition law** — Uganda's way: one solute, two layers, one fixed ratio.

Shake a solute with two liquids that refuse to mix. It splits between the layers, and the split obeys a law: at a fixed temperature, the ratio of its concentrations in the two layers never changes. That ratio is the partition coefficient — double the solute and both layers rise together, because it is the ratio that is fixed.
That one ratio runs solvent extraction: shake with the solvent the solute prefers, separate the layers, and the solute has moved house. And split your extracting solvent into small portions and shake several times — each shake takes its fixed share, so portions beat one big pour with the same total volume.
And the law can even name things: watch how the partition shifts as a ligand is added to a metal ion, and the shift tells you how many ligands bind — the formula of a complex ion, read off a ratio.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
