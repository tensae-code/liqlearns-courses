# Lesson plan — The partition law

## Sub lessons
1. **The distribution law — a fixed ratio between two solvents** — When a solute is shaken with two liquids that do not mix, it distributes itself between them, and at a fixed temperature the ratio of its concentrations in the two layers is constant: K = C(1)/C(2). The constant is the partition (distribution) coefficient. The law holds for dilute solutions where the solute is in the same molecular state in both solvents.
2. **Solvent extraction — using the coefficient** — The law is the working rule of solvent extraction: a solute is pulled out of one liquid by shaking it with a second, immiscible liquid in which it is much more soluble. Because the ratio is fixed at each shaking, several extractions with small portions of the extracting solvent remove more solute than one extraction with the whole volume at once.
3. **Applications — including finding a complex ion's formula** — Beyond extraction, the distribution method is an analytical tool: by measuring how a substance partitions — for example how a metal ion divides between water and an organic solvent as a ligand is added — the proportions in which the partners combine can be worked out, and so the formula of a complex ion can be found from partition measurements alone.
