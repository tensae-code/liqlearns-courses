# Sorting polymers — Unit 4 review

## Do it first
A plastic bowl softens in hot water and can be remelted; a pan handle never will. Before the review, sort polymers four ways — the lesson's whole job.

[Picture 21774 — Three chain structures in three panels.]

By origin: natural polymers (rubber, cellulose, proteins) versus synthetic ones (plastics, nylon).

By structure: linear chains, branched chains and cross-linked networks — cross-linking makes rigid, infusible materials.

By response to heat: thermoplastics soften on heating and can be remoulded; thermosetting polymers set permanently and cannot. By use: fibres, elastomers and plastics. Unit 4 closes with summary and review.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Sorting polymers — Unit 4 review to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
