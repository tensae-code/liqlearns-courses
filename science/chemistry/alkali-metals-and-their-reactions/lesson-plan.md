# Lesson plan — Alkali metals and their reactions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Alkali metals and their reactions
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Alkali metals and their reactions in Egypt's Grade 11 student book (Chapter Two, printed pp. 35–37): The full alkali reaction set in Egypt's order: with oxygen (normal oxides, peroxides, superoxides, and their reactions with water, acids and CO₂), with hydrogen (ionic hydrides), with halogens, with other nonmetals (Li₃N from lithium and nitrogen), with water (why sodium fires are not put out with water), with acids, and the thermal decomposition of their carbonates and nitrates (gunpowder vs Chile saltpetre note).

## Sub lessons
1. **With oxygen and hydrogen — three kinds of oxide** — With **oxygen** the alkali metals give three grades of oxide: **normal oxides, peroxides and superoxides** — and Egypt follows those oxides on into their own reactions with water, acids and CO₂. With **hydrogen** they give **ionic hydrides**, salts in which hydrogen itself is the negative ion — the family's eagerness to lose its one electron, running in both directions.
2. **With nonmetals and water — the violent list** — With **halogens** they form salts directly, and with other nonmetals too — **Li₃N from lithium and nitrogen** is the book's named case. With **water** the reaction is the family's fame: hydrogen is released and the metal's hydroxide formed, so fiercely for sodium that the hydrogen can catch fire — which is why **sodium fires are never put out with water**: the water feeds the reaction instead of smothering it.
3. **With acids — and what heat does to their salts** — With **acids** the metals react violently to the salt and hydrogen. The chapter closes with heat: the **thermal decomposition of their carbonates and nitrates**, the nitrate breakdown standing behind the book's closing note — **gunpowder against Chile saltpetre**, two nitrate stories told side by side.
