# Alkali metals and their reactions

## Do it first
Before you read: sodium catches fire on water, yet its compounds sit quietly in every kitchen. Say out loud: what does this family react with — everything, or a definite list? Then read on: Egypt's Grade 11 book works through the alkali metals' full reaction set, partner by partner.

## Sub lesson 1 — With oxygen and hydrogen — three kinds of oxide

With **oxygen** the alkali metals give three grades of oxide: **normal oxides, peroxides and superoxides** — and Egypt follows those oxides on into their own reactions with water, acids and CO₂. With **hydrogen** they give **ionic hydrides**, salts in which hydrogen itself is the negative ion — the family's eagerness to lose its one electron, running in both directions.

## Sub lesson 2 — With nonmetals and water — the violent list

With **halogens** they form salts directly, and with other nonmetals too — **Li₃N from lithium and nitrogen** is the book's named case. With **water** the reaction is the family's fame: hydrogen is released and the metal's hydroxide formed, so fiercely for sodium that the hydrogen can catch fire — which is why **sodium fires are never put out with water**: the water feeds the reaction instead of smothering it.

## Sub lesson 3 — With acids — and what heat does to their salts

With **acids** the metals react violently to the salt and hydrogen. The chapter closes with heat: the **thermal decomposition of their carbonates and nitrates**, the nitrate breakdown standing behind the book's closing note — **gunpowder against Chile saltpetre**, two nitrate stories told side by side.

## The key ideas

- With oxygen the alkali metals give **normal oxides, peroxides and superoxides**; with hydrogen, **ionic hydrides**.
- With water they release hydrogen and form the hydroxide — **never fight a sodium fire with water**.
- Their **carbonates and nitrates decompose on heating** — the nitrate story behind gunpowder and Chile saltpetre.
