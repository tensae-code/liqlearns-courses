# Paper and pulp

## Do it first
Wood becomes a white sheet you can write on. Before reading, say what must be done to wood's fibres before a sheet can form at all.

[Picture 21765 — Pulp becoming a sheet on rollers.]

Pulping breaks wood (or other fibre) down to a fibre pulp — mechanically or chemically, as presented.

The pulp is washed, bleached and beaten, then spread as a watery web on rollers/screens.

Pressing and drying the web gives the paper sheet — Ethiopian production is presented in the section.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Paper and pulp to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
