# Protecting metals — coatings and sacrificial zinc

## Do it first
A scratched painted gate rusts at the scratch — but a scratched galvanised gate does not. Before reading, say out loud: what is the zinc doing at the scratch that paint cannot do? Bulgaria's Grade 10 protection lesson sorts every defence into three families, and that scratch is its sharpest question.

## Sub lesson 1 — Barriers and alloys

**Barrier coatings** wall the metal off: **nickel and chromium plating**, and **paints, lacquers and enamels** — which must **adhere tightly, stay elastic and resist temperature swings**, or the wall cracks and the attack walks in underneath. The third family removes the weakness itself: **corrosion-resistant alloys such as stainless steel** — dearer, but saving both the iron and the resources to remake it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Electrochemical protection — the sacrificial metal

**Electrochemical protection** attaches a **more active metal** — as a coating or as plates — which **oxidises sacrificially while the iron rests as cathode**. Zinc on iron is the working case. Bulgaria sets the traps as questions: **no copper bolts in iron structures** (the pair would make iron the anode of its own cell), and **nickel-plated iron rusts at a scratch, because nickel stands after iron in the activity row** — at the scratch the exposed iron, not the coating, is the one that oxidises.

## The key ideas

- Three families: barrier coatings (plating; paints/lacquers/enamels — adherent, elastic, temperature-proof), electrochemical protection, corrosion-resistant alloys.
- Sacrificial protection: the more active metal oxidises; the iron rests as cathode. Zinc protects even at a scratch; nickel (after iron in the row) cannot.
- Trap pairs: copper bolts in iron — never; the activity row decides who is sacrificed.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion: barriers and alloys; electrochemical protection — the sacrificial metal — in that order, in your own words, with the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. A nickel-plated iron part is scratched through to the iron. Predict, with the reason. (The iron rusts at the scratch: nickel stands after iron in the activity row, so in the pair the iron is the more active metal and oxidises — the coating cannot sacrifice itself for it.)
2. Why must a paint film stay elastic? (A cracked film lets air and water under the barrier, and corrosion then spreads beneath the coating — the wall only works while it is whole and clinging.)
