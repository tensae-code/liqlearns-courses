# Lesson plan — Protecting metals — coatings and sacrificial zinc

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Protecting metals — coatings and sacrificial zinc
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Protection in three families: barrier coatings, electrochemical protection with a more active metal, and corrosion-resistant alloys (Bulgaria, Grade 10 — §53, printed p. 133)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Corrosion — iron as its own cell is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Barriers and alloys** — **Barrier coatings** wall the metal off: **nickel and chromium plating**, and **paints, lacquers and enamels** — which must **adhere tightly, stay elastic and resist temperature swings**, or the wall cracks and the attack walks in underneath. The third family removes the weakness itself: **corrosion-resistant alloys such as stainless steel** — dearer, but saving both the iron and the resources to remake it.
2. **Electrochemical protection — the sacrificial metal** — **Electrochemical protection** attaches a **more active metal** — as a coating or as plates — which **oxidises sacrificially while the iron rests as cathode**. Zinc on iron is the working case. Bulgaria sets the traps as questions: **no copper bolts in iron structures** (the pair would make iron the anode of its own cell), and **nickel-plated iron rusts at a scratch, because nickel stands after iron in the activity row** — at the scratch the exposed iron, not the coating, is the one that oxidises.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Judges coatings by looks → judge by the row: find both metals in the activity series; the earlier one is always the sacrifice.
