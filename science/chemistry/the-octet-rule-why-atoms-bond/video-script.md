# Video script — The octet rule — why atoms bond

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A lone sodium atom is restless and violently reactive; sodium locked in salt is calm.

Atoms bond to become more stable — the drive behind all bonding.

The octet rule: atoms tend to arrange their electrons so the outer shell holds eight electrons, like a noble gas.

Three bond types achieve it in different ways: ionic (transfer), covalent (sharing) and metallic (pooling) — previewed here, built one by one in the lessons ahead.

Now you. Say it back: What do atoms tend to achieve in their outer shell?
Answer: Eight electrons — an octet.
