# The octet rule — why atoms bond

## Do it first
A lone sodium atom is restless and violently reactive; sodium locked in salt is calm. Neon, with a full outer shell, reacts with nothing at all. Before reading, say what atoms seem to 'want'.

[Picture 21630 — Completing the outer shell of eight.]

Atoms bond to become more stable — the drive behind all bonding.

The octet rule: atoms tend to arrange their electrons so the outer shell holds eight electrons, like a noble gas.

Three bond types achieve it in different ways: ionic (transfer), covalent (sharing) and metallic (pooling) — previewed here, built one by one in the lessons ahead.
