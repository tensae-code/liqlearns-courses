## Sub lesson 1 — Shrimp shells — a waste with a polymer in it

Vietnam's Grade 11 advanced book runs a topic of organic-chemistry practicals drawn from the country's own coast and kitchens, and Bài 6 prepares a named product from a waste material: **shrimp shells**. The shells are stiffened by **chitin** — after cellulose, the most abundant natural polymer on Earth — a long chain built of glucosamine-like units joined as the carbohydrates are joined.

Chitin is the shell's skeleton: tough, insoluble and normally thrown away by the tonne wherever shrimp are processed. The practical's first idea is the chemist's eye for waste — a polymer this abundant is a raw material waiting for a reaction.

## Sub lesson 2 — Breaking chitin down — glucosamine hydrochloride

The preparation **hydrolyses** the chitin chain: heated with concentrated hydrochloric acid, the links between units are split by water — the same hydrolysis that splits starch to glucose and proteins to amino acids — and the units are freed as **glucosamine**, an amino sugar (a sugar carrying an –NH₂ group).

In the acid the glucosamine is taken as its salt, **glucosamine hydrochloride** — the stable, crystalline, soluble form in which the product is purified and kept. Bài 6 works the preparation as a practical: shell to chitin, chitin to glucosamine hydrochloride, each step a named operation.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why this product

Glucosamine is the body's own brick for **cartilage** — the joint tissue built of amino-sugar chains — and glucosamine hydrochloride is sold worldwide as a joint-health supplement and used as a starting material for chitosan and other shell-derived products.

The lesson Vietnam draws is the topic's own: an organic practical that starts in a fish-market waste bin and ends at a pharmacy shelf — separation, hydrolysis and salt formation practised on the country's real material.

## The key ideas

- Shrimp shells are stiffened by **chitin**, a natural polymer of glucosamine-like units — a waste produced by the tonne.
- **Acid hydrolysis** splits chitin's links (as starch splits to glucose) and frees **glucosamine**, an amino sugar.
- The product is taken as **glucosamine hydrochloride** — the stable, soluble salt form.
- Glucosamine is cartilage's own building unit; the preparation turns shell waste into a pharmacy product.
