# Chitin and glucosamine — from shrimp shells — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) Set out the preparation's chain in order, naming each stage's chemistry.
**Answer: Shrimp shells supply chitin; hydrolysis with concentrated hydrochloric acid splits the chain's links and frees glucosamine; in the acid it is taken and purified as its hydrochloride salt.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) In what way is chitin → glucosamine the same reaction story as starch → glucose?
**Answer: Both are hydrolyses of a natural polymer: water, helped by acid, splits the links between units and frees the single units the chain was built from.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What extra group does glucosamine carry that glucose does not, and what family name does it earn?
**Answer: An amino group, –NH₂; it makes glucosamine an amino sugar (and, in strong acid, the group is why it is held as a salt).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does Vietnam's book file this preparation under practicals drawn from real life?
**Answer: It starts from a real waste stream — shells discarded where shrimp are processed — and ends at a saleable pharmacy product, practising separation, hydrolysis and salt formation on the country's own material.**
