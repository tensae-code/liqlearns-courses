# Lesson plan — Chitin and glucosamine — from shrimp shells

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Chitin and glucosamine — from shrimp shells
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a named preparation from Vietnam's Grade 11 study-topics book (Topic 2, Bài 6): preparing glucosamine hydrochloride from shrimp shells — the shells' chitin, a natural polymer built of glucosamine units, broken down by acid to glucosamine, taken as its hydrochloride salt
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Carbohydrates — sugars and starches is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Shrimp shells — a waste with a polymer in it** — Vietnam's Grade 11 advanced book runs a topic of organic-chemistry practicals drawn from the country's own coast and kitchens, and Bài 6 prepares a named product from a waste material: **shrimp shells**. The shells are stiffened by **chitin** (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
2. **Breaking chitin down — glucosamine hydrochloride** — The preparation **hydrolyses** the chitin chain: heated with concentrated hydrochloric acid, the links between units are split by water (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
3. **Why this product** — Glucosamine is the body's own brick for **cartilage** (see lesson/lesson.md for the full part).
