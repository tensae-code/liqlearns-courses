# Electrolysis case studies

## Do it first
Brine in, three useful products out. Before reading, predict the products at each electrode for brine and for copper sulfate — first with inert electrodes, then with a copper anode, and notice what changes.

[Picture 21735 — Brine electrolysis, three products.]

Electrolysis of brine (concentrated sodium chloride solution): hydrogen at the cathode, chlorine at the anode, and sodium hydroxide left in solution — three products, one cell.

Copper sulfate with inert electrodes: copper at the cathode, oxygen at the anode, and the blue colour fades as copper leaves.

With a copper anode instead, the anode itself dissolves and replaces the copper deposited — the basis of refining, run as a case here. Dilute acid-type solutions are worked as further cases with their electrode equations.

Explain Electrolysis case studies to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
