## The key ideas

- An atom becomes an ion when its electrons stop balancing its protons: an atom that loses electrons becomes a positive ion — a cation — and one that gains electrons becomes a negative ion — an anion; the charge is q = p − e⁻, and an ion's properties differ from the atom it came from.
- Atoms become cations when protons outnumber electrons and anions in the reverse case; charge q = p − e⁻. (Kazakhstan, Grade 8 — Grade 8, bilingual ed. Kaliev, Ch. 1.2 Formation of ions, PDF p. 20).
- An atom that loses electrons becomes a positive ion, one that gains becomes negative (same proposed slug as Book 17). (Kazakhstan, Grade 8 — Grade 8, Express Publishing, Module 1, printed p. 16).
- How atoms become ions (same proposed slug as Books 17–18). (Kazakhstan, Grade 8 — Grade 8, Ospanova (Russian), Ch. I §4 Formation of ions, printed p. 14).
- What ions are and how their properties differ from atoms (same proposed slug as Books 16–18). (Kazakhstan, Grade 9 — Grade 9 Pt 1, Ospanova, Ch. I §5 Main points of the TED. Properties of ions, printed p. 19).
