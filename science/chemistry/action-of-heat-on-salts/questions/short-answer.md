# Action of heat on salts — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is thermal decomposition, in one sentence?
**Answer: Thermal decomposition is the breaking down of a compound into simpler substances by heat alone — the pattern many salts follow when heated strongly.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give the products when lead nitrate is heated, and the sign by which one gas is recognised.
**Answer: Lead nitrate gives lead oxide (the residue), brown nitrogen dioxide — recognised by its brown fumes — and oxygen; the salt crackles (decrepitates) as it decomposes.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Which common carbonates resist decomposition by ordinary laboratory heating, and why?
**Answer: Sodium carbonate and potassium carbonate — the carbonates of the most reactive metals — resist ordinary heating; the more reactive the metal, the more stable its carbonate is to heat.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A heated salt relights a glowing splint and leaves a residue that is yellow hot and white cold. What ran in the tube?
**Answer: Oxygen was given off and the residue's colours are zinc oxide's — the pattern of a salt decomposing to zinc oxide, e.g. zinc carbonate giving zinc oxide and carbon dioxide (the oxygen sign fits the nitrate pattern to the same oxide).**
