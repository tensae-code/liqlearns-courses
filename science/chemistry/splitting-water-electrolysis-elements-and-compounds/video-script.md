Today: **Splitting water — electrolysis, elements and compounds** — Greece's way, from its own book.

Pass electricity through water and the water splits: hydrogen at one side, oxygen at the other — and always twice as much hydrogen.

Hydrogen pops at a flame. Oxygen relights a glowing splint. Two gases, two tests, one split.

What can be split is a compound — built. What no chemistry can split is an element. Water is built; hydrogen and oxygen are not.

And a pure substance signs its name with fixed numbers: the same boiling point, the same density, every time.
