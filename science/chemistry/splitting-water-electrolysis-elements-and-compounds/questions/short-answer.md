# Splitting water — electrolysis, elements and compounds — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the 2 : 1 volume ratio in water's electrolysis not a coincidence?
**Answer: It matches water's own build: each water unit carries two hydrogen parts to one oxygen part (H₂O), so splitting many units always frees hydrogen at twice oxygen's volume.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student calls oxygen a compound because it is made of atoms joined in pairs, O₂. Correct the claim in the lesson's terms.
**Answer: Joining is not the test — splitting is: no chemical means breaks oxygen into anything simpler than oxygen, so oxygen is an element; its atoms travelling in pairs does not make it a compound.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Salt water boils above 100 °C, and more salt raises it further. What does that wandering constant tell you?
**Answer: That salt water is a mixture, not a pure substance: a pure substance's constants are fixed, while a mixture's constants move with its proportions.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Name both electrode gases from water's electrolysis and the test for each.
**Answer: Hydrogen — burns with a pop at a lit splint; oxygen — relights a glowing splint.**
