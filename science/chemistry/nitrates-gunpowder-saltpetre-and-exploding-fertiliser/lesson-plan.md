# Lesson plan — Nitrates — gunpowder, saltpetre and exploding fertiliser

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Nitrates — gunpowder, saltpetre and exploding fertiliser
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The nitrates as Yemen's Grade 11 book teaches them: sodium nitrate (Chile saltpetre), potassium nitrate — named in Arabic gunpowder salt because it fed the old gunpowder — and silver nitrate; all nitrates dissolving in water, and heat sorting them three ways (alkali-metal nitrates to nitrite and oxygen, silver nitrate all the way to the metal, ammonium nitrate to nitrous oxide and water, with the boxed warning that strongly heated NH4NO3 can detonate) — with the practical workbook's two nitrate tests, hot concentrated sulfuric acid and the brown ring
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Nitrogen fertilisers is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. 1. **The family and its names** — The nitrates are met by their working names: sodium nitrate — Chile saltpetre; potassium nitrate — named in Arabic ملح البارود, "gunpowder salt", because it fed the old gunpowder; …
