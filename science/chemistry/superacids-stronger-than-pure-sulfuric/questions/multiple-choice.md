# Superacids — stronger than pure sulfuric acid — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A superacid is defined as an acid stronger than…
a) Hydrochloric acid  b) Pure (100%) sulfuric acid  c) Nitric acid  d) Any acid found in nature
**Answer: b) Pure (100%) sulfuric acid**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Superacid strength is measured with…
a) The pH scale  b) A thermometer  c) The Hammett acidity function, H₀  d) A balance
**Answer: c) The Hammett acidity function, H₀**

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) Pure sulfuric acid stands at about H₀ = −12. A superacid at H₀ = −22 is…
a) Weaker  b) The same strength  c) Ten times stronger  d) Ten steps — each ten times — stronger
**Answer: d) Ten steps — each ten times — stronger**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Magic acid is a mixture of fluorosulfuric acid with…
a) Antimony pentafluoride  b) Sodium hydroxide  c) Hydrochloric acid  d) Copper sulfate
**Answer: a) Antimony pentafluoride**

**5.** (Sub lesson 2) Magic acid is famous in research because it can hold steady, long enough to study, fragments called…
a) Crystals  b) Carbocations  c) Alloys  d) Colloids
**Answer: b) Carbocations**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) Superacids are never used in a school laboratory because they are…
a) Too weak to show anything  b) Too expensive to buy in shops  c) Violently corrosive and dangerous — specialist laboratories only  d) Not real acids
**Answer: c) Violently corrosive and dangerous — specialist laboratories only**
