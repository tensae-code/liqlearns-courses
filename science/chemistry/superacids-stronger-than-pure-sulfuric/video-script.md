Today: **Superacids — stronger than pure sulfuric acid** — North Macedonia's way: an enrichment box at the end of the acids chapter, one step past the strongest acid on the school bench.

The strongest acid in ordinary work is pure sulfuric acid. Chemists drew the line there and asked: what if an acid gives its protons away even more eagerly than that? The answer has a name — a superacid. The pH scale cannot measure them, so chemists use a deeper scale, H-zero: sulfuric acid sits near minus twelve, and the strongest superacids pass minus twenty-five.
One famous mixture, magic acid, could dissolve candle wax and hold fleeting carbon fragments steady long enough to study them — work that won a Nobel Prize. Industry uses superacids as powerful catalysts. And they are so violently corrosive that they never enter a school laboratory. The lesson is knowing they exist — and where the line is drawn.
