# Lesson plan — Superacids — stronger than pure sulfuric acid

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Superacids — stronger than pure sulfuric acid
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** superacids — acids stronger than pure (100%) sulfuric acid, the line that defines them, measured on the Hammett acidity function (H₀) because pH cannot reach them; the famous examples (fluorosulfuric acid, magic acid, fluoroantimonic acid) and their work as catalysts and as tools that stabilise carbocations; and why such violently corrosive substances belong only in specialist laboratories — taught as an enrichment topic beyond ordinary strong acids (North Macedonia, Grade 8 — *Хемија за VIII одделение во основно образование (Chemistry, Grade 8, primary)*, 2.3, Superacids, printed p. 100)

## Sub lessons
1. **What makes an acid a superacid** — The definition is a comparison: stronger than pure (100%) sulfuric acid. Beyond that line pH fails, so acidity is measured by the Hammett acidity function H₀ — pure sulfuric acid ≈ −12, the strongest superacids ≈ −25 and beyond, each step ten times stronger.
2. **The famous superacids** — Fluorosulfuric acid, the strongest single pure superacid in ordinary accounts; magic acid (fluorosulfuric acid + antimony pentafluoride), named in George Olah's laboratory and able to hold carbocations steady for study; fluoroantimonic acid at the top of the scale.
3. **What superacids are for — and why they stay out of the school lab** — Catalysts for petroleum refining, pharmaceuticals and plastics, and research tools that reveal reaction intermediates; violently corrosive and water-reactive, so specialist laboratories only — which is why the book teaches them as enrichment, not bench work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks "superacid" just means "very concentrated" → return to the definition: a dilute superacid is still a superacid, and no concentration of sulfuric acid crosses its own line.
