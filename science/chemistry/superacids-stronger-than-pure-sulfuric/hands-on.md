# Hands-on — Superacids — stronger than pure sulfuric acid

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes. Paper work only — superacids themselves are never handled by learners, in any amount, anywhere.

## Build the acidity ladder
On a long strip of paper, draw the H₀ ladder from 0 down to −25. Place these cards on it: a dilute classroom acid (near the top, on the pH side — mark it "pH country"), pure sulfuric acid at ≈ −12 (draw the superacid line across the ladder here), fluorosulfuric acid below the line, magic acid lower still, fluoroantimonic acid at the bottom. Label every card above the line "ordinary acids" and every card below it "superacids".

## Ten-times steps
Each step down the H₀ ladder is ten times stronger proton-giving. Starting at pure sulfuric acid (−12), count the steps to −22 and work out, in words and then in figures, how many times stronger that superacid is. Check your figure against sub lesson 1.

## Spot the mistake (correction drill)
A worksheet claims: (1) "A superacid is sulfuric acid with extra water removed past 100%." (2) "Superacids can be measured on the ordinary pH scale like any acid." (3) "Magic acid is safe to use in a school lab because it is famous." Correct each claim in one sentence, using the lesson's own wording.
