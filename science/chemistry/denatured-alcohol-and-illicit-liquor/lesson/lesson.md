# Denatured alcohol and illicit liquor

## Do it first
Before you read: the same clear liquid fills a laboratory burner, a varnish tin and a fatal bottle in a village poisoning — ethanol in the first two, and something riding with it in the third. Say out loud: how can the state let industrial spirit be sold cheaply without it being drunk, and what makes the illegal bottle kill? Then read on: Bhutan's Class X book (Ch.9) teaches denaturing and spurious liquor as one public-health lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Denatured alcohol — spirit made undrinkable on purpose

Ethanol is wanted for two different lives: as a drink, heavily taxed, and as an industrial solvent and fuel, which should be cheap. Bhutan's §9.4 teaches the state's answer — **denatured alcohol**, sold as **methylated spirit**: ethanol deliberately poisoned with **methanol** (with foul-tasting additions and a violet dye to mark it), so that it is unmistakably unfit to drink and can be sold **duty-free** for laboratories, burners, varnishes and industry. The denaturing is not damage; it is a legal-chemical device — the same ethanol, made undrinkable on purpose, with the colour as its warning label. The lesson carries the book's care exactly: denatured spirit is a poison precisely because the device works — drinking it means drinking the methanol in it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Spurious and illicit liquor — the deadly imitation, and its identification

Against the marked, legal poison stands the unmarked, illegal one. **Illicit liquor** is spirit distilled or sold outside the law; **spurious liquor** is worse — drink stretched or faked with cheap **methanol**, an alcohol the body turns into formic acid, which attacks the optic nerve and the blood: small doses blind, larger doses kill, and Bhutan's §9.6 teaches the identification the chapter insists on — liquor of unknown origin, sold cheap and unlabelled, is suspect by those facts alone; the chemical sorting of ethanol from methanol (their different oxidation behaviour, the tests of the alcohol chapter) is the laboratory's answer, and refusing the unlabelled bottle is the drinker's. The section's point is public health, not curiosity: every mass poisoning by spurious liquor is this chemistry, at scale.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ethanol's impacts — environment, economy, society and health

The chapter closes (§9.6) by counting what ethanol costs and earns beyond the bottle, in the book's own four columns. **Environment**: distilleries' waste and the fuel-ethanol balance. **Economy**: revenue and livelihoods against healthcare costs and lost work. **Society**: family breakdown, accidents and violence that follow heavy drinking. **Health**: the liver, the brain and dependence — alcohol is a drug whose dose makes the damage. Bhutan's book then hands the argument to its closing case study — Activity 9.7, **"The Myth behind Alcohol Happiness" by Dr. Chencho Dorji** (printed pp. 234–236), working the same economy, society and health impacts through questions — and this lesson closes where the chapter does: the 'happiness' is the myth the four columns answer.

## The key ideas

- **Denatured alcohol (methylated spirit)** is ethanol made undrinkable on purpose — methanol added, foul taste, violet dye — so industrial spirit can be sold duty-free.
- Denatured spirit is a **poison**: the methanol that denatures it is what harms the drinker.
- **Spurious liquor** is faked drink stretched with methanol; the body turns methanol into formic acid — small doses **blind**, larger doses **kill**.
- Identification begins with origin: **unlabelled, cheap, unknown-source liquor is suspect**; chemical tests sort methanol from ethanol in the laboratory.
- Bhutan's chapter reckons ethanol's impacts in four columns — **environment, economy, society, health** — and closes with Dr. Chencho Dorji's case study, "The Myth behind Alcohol Happiness".

## Say it to your companion
Explain denatured alcohol and illicit liquor to your companion in your own words: say what is added to ethanol to denature it and why, why spurious liquor blinds and kills, how suspect liquor is identified, and the chapter's four columns for ethanol's impacts — ending with the case study's title and what it calls a myth.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does denatured alcohol carry a violet dye — what job does the colour do that the methanol cannot? (The methanol makes the spirit poisonous but is invisible; the dye is the warning label — it marks the liquid as industrial, not drink, at sight, before anyone tastes or drinks it.)
2. Two clear alcohols, ethanol and methanol, sit in unlabelled bottles after a seizure. Why does the identification matter medically, not just legally? (Because the body treats them differently: ethanol is metabolised to substances it can clear, while methanol becomes formic acid, which destroys the optic nerve and acidifies the blood — the same-looking drink is a poison or not depending on which alcohol it is.)
3. A seller argues liquor taxes are pure gain for the economy. Answer with the chapter's own reckoning. (The book counts four columns, not one: against revenue stand healthcare costs and lost work in the economy column, family and accident costs in the society column, and liver, brain and dependence damage in the health column — the case study's point is that the 'gain', like the 'happiness', shrinks when all four columns are read.)
