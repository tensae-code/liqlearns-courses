# Lesson plan — Denatured alcohol and illicit liquor

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Denatured alcohol and illicit liquor
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bhutan's Class X book (Ch.9 §9.4/§9.6): ethanol deliberately made undrinkable — denatured alcohol (methylated spirit), poisoned and dyed so industrial spirit can be sold free of drink duty — against its dark twin, spurious and illicit liquor, methanol-laced and deadly, and how it is identified; closing with the chapter's own reckoning of ethanol's impacts on environment, economy, society and health, carried into the book's closing case study (Activity 9.7, "The Myth behind Alcohol Happiness" by Dr. Chencho Dorji, printed pp. 234–236)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Kinds of alcohols is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Denatured alcohol — spirit made undrinkable on purpose** — Ethanol is wanted for two different lives: as a drink, heavily taxed, and as an industrial solvent and fuel, which should be cheap.
2. **Spurious and illicit liquor — the deadly imitation, and its identification** — Against the marked, legal poison stands the unmarked, illegal one.
3. **Ethanol's impacts — environment, economy, society and health** — The chapter closes (§9.6) by counting what ethanol costs and earns beyond the bottle, in the book's own four columns.
