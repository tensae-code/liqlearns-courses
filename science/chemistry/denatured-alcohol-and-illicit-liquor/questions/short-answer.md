# Denatured alcohol and illicit liquor — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is methylated spirit, and what are its three denaturing marks?
**Answer: Methylated spirit is denatured alcohol — industrial ethanol made undrinkable. Its marks: methanol poisoned into it, foul-tasting additions, and a violet dye that labels it industrial at sight.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is drinking denatured spirit dangerous even though it is mostly ethanol?
**Answer: Because the denaturing methanol is drunk with it. Methanol is metabolised to formic acid, which damages the optic nerve and the blood — the poison is the point of the product, and it does not separate in the glass.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Distinguish illicit liquor from spurious liquor.
**Answer: Illicit liquor is any spirit made or sold outside the law. Spurious liquor is the deadly subset: drink faked or stretched with cheap methanol, so that drinking it poisons — blinding at small doses, killing at larger ones.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the chapter teach a person to treat an unlabelled bottle of cheap liquor?
**Answer: As suspect by its facts alone — unknown origin, no label, low price are the identification marks of the illicit and spurious trade. The safe act is refusal; sorting methanol from ethanol belongs to the laboratory's tests.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Name one impact of ethanol in each of the chapter's four columns.
**Answer: Environment: distillery waste and the fuel balance. Economy: revenue and livelihoods against healthcare costs and lost work. Society: family breakdown, accidents and violence following heavy drinking. Health: liver and brain damage and dependence.**
