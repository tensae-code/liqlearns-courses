# Zinc — extraction and uses

Say out loud: which metal coats steel to protect it, and where do you think it comes from in the ground? Then read on: this is South Sudan's way in Secondary 4, Unit 4 §4.5 — zinc, its ore and its extraction, in the ores-and-extraction unit.

## Sub lesson 1 — Zinc's ore — zinc blende

Zinc is not found free in nature; it is won from its ores. The chief ore is **zinc blende (sphalerite), zinc sulfide, ZnS**, with **calamine, zinc carbonate, ZnCO₃**, as the second ore. Zinc sits in the middle of the reactivity series — below carbon — and that position decides its extraction, as the whole of Unit 4 teaches: a metal below carbon can be won by reduction with carbon, a metal above it cannot. The ore is first concentrated, then sent to the two-step extraction.

## Sub lesson 2 — Extraction — roast, then reduce

The sulfide ore is first **roasted** — heated strongly in air — converting the blende to **zinc oxide**: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂. (The carbonate ore is calcined instead, ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂ — either road reaches the oxide.) Then the oxide is **reduced with carbon** in a furnace: ZnO + C → Zn + CO. Zinc's own trick is its low boiling point: at furnace temperature the metal comes off as a **vapour** and is **condensed** to liquid zinc away from the furnace — extraction by reduction and distillation in one. (The purer modern route dissolves the oxide in sulfuric acid and wins the metal by electrolysis of the solution — electrowinning.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Uses of zinc

Zinc works mostly as a protector and a partner. **Galvanising**: steel coated in zinc resists rust — zinc corrodes first, sacrificially, guarding the iron beneath even through a scratch. **Brass**: zinc alloyed with copper makes the yellow metal of handles, instruments and fittings. **Dry cells**: zinc cases serve as the negative electrode of everyday batteries. And **zinc oxide** goes to paints, rubber and ointments. Protection first — that is zinc's place in the unit's survey of the six metals.

## The key ideas

- Chief ore: **zinc blende, ZnS** (sphalerite); second ore calamine, ZnCO₃.
- Extraction: **roast** the blende to zinc oxide, then **reduce with carbon**; the zinc distils off as vapour and condenses.
- Zinc's reactivity (below carbon) is what allows carbon reduction.
- Chief uses: **galvanising** steel, **brass**, dry-cell cases, zinc oxide.
