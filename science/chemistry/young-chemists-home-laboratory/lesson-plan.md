# Lesson plan — The young chemist's home laboratory

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The young chemist's home laboratory
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the young chemist's home laboratory — a named programme of safe home experiments in Armenia's Grade 8 teacher guide (printed p. 31): hydrogen peroxide decomposition with foam, natural-gas burner observation, and starch and other kitchen reagents (teacher evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. No other lesson is needed first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **A named programme, not odd jobs** — Armenia's Grade 8 **teacher guide** gives home chemistry a name and a place of its own: **the young chemist's home laboratory** (printed p.
2. **Foam from hydrogen peroxide** — The programme's named experiments include the **decomposition of hydrogen peroxide, H₂O₂, with foam** — a safe home version of a decomposition reaction, where the gas given off is caught in foam and seen.
3. **The burner and the kitchen shelf** — The programme also names **natural-gas burner observation** — watching the home burner as chemistry, with the programme's safety frame around it — and **starch and other kitchen reagents**: the substances already in the kitchen, used as the home laboratory's reagent shelf.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: the school laboratory, its safety kit and a teacher present — never home chemicals; kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Home experiments treated as free improvisation → the programme is the point: named experiments inside a safety frame. / The foam read as 'soap magic' → the foam catches the gas the decomposition gives off; the reaction is the chemistry, the foam only shows it.
