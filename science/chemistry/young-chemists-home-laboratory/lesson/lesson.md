# The young chemist's home laboratory

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's thing is, in one sentence — then read on and check it against Armenia's way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — A named programme, not odd jobs

Armenia's Grade 8 **teacher guide** gives home chemistry a name and a place of its own: **the young chemist's home laboratory** (printed p. 31). It is a **programme** — a planned set of safe home experiments — not a pile of tricks: the home becomes a second laboratory, run under the programme's safety frame. This lesson stands on that guide evidence, labelled as such.

## Sub lesson 2 — Foam from hydrogen peroxide

The programme's named experiments include the **decomposition of hydrogen peroxide, H₂O₂, with foam** — a safe home version of a decomposition reaction, where the gas given off is caught in foam and seen. The reaction studied at school is met again at the kitchen table, in the programme's safe form.

## Sub lesson 3 — The burner and the kitchen shelf

The programme also names **natural-gas burner observation** — watching the home burner as chemistry, with the programme's safety frame around it — and **starch and other kitchen reagents**: the substances already in the kitchen, used as the home laboratory's reagent shelf. Nothing is bought and nothing unsafe is improvised; the programme chooses from what a home already holds.

## The key ideas

- The **young chemist's home laboratory** is a **named programme** of safe home experiments in Armenia's Grade 8 teacher guide.
- Named experiments: **H₂O₂ decomposition with foam**, **natural-gas burner observation**, **starch and other kitchen reagents**.
- The home laboratory works **inside a safety frame** — chosen experiments, kitchen substances, no improvisation.
