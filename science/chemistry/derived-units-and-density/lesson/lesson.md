## Derived units and density

Derived units are built by substituting base units into defining equations (area m², speed m/s, force = newton, pressure = pascal, energy = joule, in a table). Volume is length cubed; the laboratory works in litres and millilitres (1 L = 1 dm³, 1 mL = 1 cm³).

Density is mass per unit volume, d = m/V, a characteristic property: a worked example has Chaltu measure an unknown solvent (30.5 g, 35.1 mL, d ≈ 0.869 g/mL) and identify it as toluene from a density table; density also exposes adulterated gold, since likely admixtures are less dense.
