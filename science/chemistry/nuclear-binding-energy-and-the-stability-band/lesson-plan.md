# Lesson plan — Nuclear binding energy and the stability band

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Nuclear binding energy and the stability band
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Binding energy and nuclear stability as Yemen's Grade 12 book builds them: the three conservation rules for nuclear equations, helium-4's mass defect (4.00260 u against 4.032979 u of separate particles — 0.030379 u, the binding energy 7.75 MeV per nucleon), the binding-energy-per-nucleon curve peaking near iron so uranium-235 gains stability by fission while light nuclei gain it by fusion, and the neutron-to-proton stability band — n = p up to atomic number 20, n > p beyond, everything past 83 unstable — with the magic numbers 2, 8, 20, 50, 82, 126 and even-even nuclei picked out as specially stable
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Nuclear binding energy and mass defect is recommended first, never forced.
