| Unit 4 (Yemen, Grade 12) | Book 1 (Grade 12) — Unit 4 — printed pp. 73–78 — Nuclear equations obey three conservation rules (mass numbers balance, atomic numbers balance, mass and energy together conserve); binding energy is computed for helium-4 through Einstein's relation: the nucleus weighs 4.00260 u against 4.032979 u of separate particles — the mass defect 0.030379 u is the binding energy, 4.54×10⁻¹² J, or 7.75 MeV per nucleon; the average binding energy per nucleon is plotted against mass number — rising steeply, peaking near iron (elements of mass 28–138 are the most stable), then sloping down, so uranium-235 at 7.6 MeV/nucleon can gain stability by fission while light nuclei gain it by fusion; and the neutron-to-proton ratio is graphed as the stability band: n = p up to atomic number 20, n > p beyond, everything past 83 unstable — with the magic numbers (2, 8, 20, 50, 82, 126 nucleons) and even–even nuclei picked out as specially stable. |
