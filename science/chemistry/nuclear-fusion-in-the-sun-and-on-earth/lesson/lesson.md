# Nuclear fusion in the sun and on Earth

## Do it first
Before you read: the sun has burned for billions of years without coal, oxygen or any fire you could light. Say out loud: if nothing is burning, where does the light come from? Then read on: Egypt's chapter runs fission backwards — instead of splitting the heavy, it joins the light.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fusion — joining the light nuclei

**Nuclear fusion** is the reverse of fission: **light nuclei join into a heavier nucleus**, and the mass defect of the joining is released as energy. In reactors the worked reaction is **deuterium + tritium → helium-4 + neutron** — two hydrogen isotopes fusing into helium, with a neutron carrying off part of the energy.

## Sub lesson 2 — The sun's own steps

The sun fuses hydrogen by its own steps, which Egypt writes as equations: **helium-3 pairs** meeting, and **a proton + deuterium** joining — step by step building helium out of hydrogen in the sun's core. The sunlight on your skin is the mass defect of those joinings, travelled across space.

## Sub lesson 3 — Nuclear vs chemical — the closing table

The chapter closes with a contrast table. **Chemical reactions** shuffle **outer electrons**, conserve mass, and run at ordinary conditions with modest energies. **Nuclear reactions** rearrange **nucleons**, convert mass to energy by E = mc², demand **extreme conditions**, and release **vastly larger energies**. Two chemistries of change, kept apart by what actually moves.

## The key ideas

- **Fusion joins light nuclei**: in reactors, **deuterium + tritium → helium-4 + neutron**.
- The **sun** fuses hydrogen in steps — helium-3 pairs, proton + deuterium — building helium.
- Contrast: chemical reactions move **outer electrons** and conserve mass; nuclear reactions move **nucleons**, convert mass to energy, and need extreme conditions.
