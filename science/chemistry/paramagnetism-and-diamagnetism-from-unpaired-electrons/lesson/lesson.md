# Paramagnetism and diamagnetism — from unpaired electrons

## Do it first
Before you read: a magnet can sort compounds the eye cannot tell apart — some are pulled in, some are faintly pushed away. Say out loud: what inside the atoms could a magnet possibly be reading? Then read on: Egypt's book reads magnetism straight off the d-orbital box diagram — no unpaired electrons, no pull.

## Sub lesson 1 — Boxes first — count the unpaired

Start where Egypt starts: the **d-orbital box diagram** — five boxes, electrons placed by Hund's rule, singly first. The whole lesson is one count: **how many electrons stand unpaired**. Everything magnetic follows from that number, so the diagram is drawn before any claim is made.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unpaired — paramagnetic, and stronger with more

A substance with **unpaired electrons is paramagnetic** — drawn into a magnetic field — and its **magnetic moment grows with the unpaired count**: more unpaired electrons, stronger pull. The unpaired electron is itself a tiny magnet, and the counts add.

## Sub lesson 3 — All paired — diamagnetic, and the worked ordering

A substance with **all electrons paired is diamagnetic** — not drawn in; if anything, faintly repelled. Egypt works the ordering across **Zn, Ni²⁺ and Fe²⁺ compounds**: zinc's d¹⁰ has no unpaired electrons (diamagnetic), Ni²⁺ (d⁸) has two, and Fe²⁺ (d⁶) has four — so the pull orders itself Fe²⁺ > Ni²⁺ > Zn, read straight off the boxes.

## The key ideas

- Draw the **d-orbital box diagram** first — the lesson is the **unpaired count**.
- **Unpaired electrons → paramagnetic**, moment growing with the count.
- **All paired → diamagnetic**. Worked order: Fe²⁺ (4 unpaired) > Ni²⁺ (2) > Zn (0, diamagnetic).
