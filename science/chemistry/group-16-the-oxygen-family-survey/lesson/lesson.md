# Group 16 — the oxygen family survey

## Do it first
Before you read: the most familiar substance on Earth — water — is this family's odd one out. Say out loud: what is different about the family's head that its hydride holds together with hydrogen bonds while its cousins' hydrides do not? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book surveys the oxygen family anomaly first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The family — and its anomaly first

The **oxygen family (VIA, group 16)** — **O, S, Se, Te, Po, and element 116**, configuration **ns²np⁴** — is surveyed with its anomaly first: **oxygen's high electronegativity** lets it form **ionic compounds with some metals**, while the rest of the family cannot; and **hydrogen bonding exists in H₂O but in none of the other hydrides**. The head of this family does not behave like its body — water's strangeness is the family's first fact.

## Sub lesson 2 — The family's reactions

The survey runs the partners in order: with **metals** (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; Mg + S → MgS), with **oxygen itself** (SeO₂, SO₃), with **halogens** (SCl₂) and with **hydrogen** (H₂O, H₂S). Two outer electrons short of the octet, the family takes what it needs from each partner — the reactions are one hunger read four ways.

## Sub lesson 3 — The family at work

Uses follow the elements. **Sulfur**: for **sulfuric acid, rubber vulcanisation and dyes**. **Selenium**, whose conductivity rises with light: **photocells, camera meters and photocopiers**. **Tellurium**: alloys and rust-proof coatings. And radioactive **polonium**, **named by Marie Curie for her homeland, Poland** — the survey's closing name carrying a discoverer's patriotism inside the table.

## The key ideas

- Group 16 (VIA): **O, S, Se, Te, Po, element 116 — ns²np⁴**; anomaly first: oxygen's **high electronegativity** (ionic compounds with some metals) and **hydrogen bonding in H₂O only** among the hydrides.
- Reactions with **metals, oxygen, halogens, hydrogen** — 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, Mg + S → MgS, SeO₂, SO₃, SCl₂, H₂O, H₂S.
- Uses: sulfur → **sulfuric acid, vulcanisation, dyes**; selenium (light-sensitive conduction) → **photocells, meters, photocopiers**; tellurium → alloys, coatings; **polonium named by Curie for Poland**.
