# Lesson plan — Titrations and their curves — Unit 1 review

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Titrations and their curves — Unit 1 review
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Acid–Base Equilibria (Grade 12 Chemistry).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A titration adds a solution of known concentration from a burette to a measured volume of the other, to find its concentration by neutralisation calculation.

## Sub lessons
1. **Titration procedure and calculations** — A titration adds a solution of known concentration from a burette to a measured volume of the other, to find its concentration by neutralisation calculation.
2. **Equivalence point versus end point** — The equivalence point is where equivalent amounts have exactly reacted; the end point is where the indicator changes — the two must be matched by choosing the indicator for the curve.
3. **The four curves and Unit 1 review** — Titration curves plot pH against volume added: strong acid–strong base jumps widely through pH 7; weak acid–strong base equivalence sits above 7; strong acid–weak base below 7; weak–weak has no sharp jump and no good indicator.
