Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: A titration adds a solution of known concentration from a burette to a measured volume of the other, to find its concentration by neutralisation calculation — stated in the learner's own words and used on one fresh example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Equivalence point versus end point
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The equivalence point is where equivalent amounts have exactly reacted; the end point is where the indicator changes — the two must be matched by choosing the indicator for the curve — stated in the learner's own words and used on one fresh example.

## Sub lesson 3 — The four curves and Unit 1 review
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Titration curves plot pH against volume added: strong acid–strong base jumps widely through pH 7; weak acid–strong base equivalence sits above 7; strong acid–weak base below 7; weak–weak has no sharp jump and no good indicator — stated in the learner's own words and used on one fresh example.
