# Titrations and their curves — Unit 1 review

## Do it first
You add alkali drop by drop to an acid, watching pH. For most of the burette the pH creeps — then, within one drop, it leaps. Before the review, say what that leap's middle is called, and why the indicator must change exactly there.

## Sub lesson 1 — Titration procedure and calculations
A titration adds a solution of known concentration from a burette to a measured volume of the other, to find its concentration by neutralisation calculation.

[GIF 21729 — the pH jump as drops fall]

## Sub lesson 2 — Equivalence point versus end point
The equivalence point is where equivalent amounts have exactly reacted; the end point is where the indicator changes — the two must be matched by choosing the indicator for the curve.

## Sub lesson 3 — The four curves and Unit 1 review
Titration curves plot pH against volume added: strong acid–strong base jumps widely through pH 7; weak acid–strong base equivalence sits above 7; strong acid–weak base below 7; weak–weak has no sharp jump and no good indicator. Unit 1 closes with summary and review.
