You add alkali drop by drop to an acid, watching pH.

A titration adds a solution of known concentration from a burette to a measured volume of the other, to find its concentration by neutralisation calculation.

The equivalence point is where equivalent amounts have exactly reacted; the end point is where the indicator changes — the two must be matched by choosing the indicator for the curve.

Titration curves plot pH against volume added: strong acid–strong base jumps widely through pH 7; weak acid–strong base equivalence sits above 7; strong acid–weak base below 7; weak–weak has no sharp jump and no good indicator.

Now you. Say it back: What is the equivalence point?
Answer: Where equivalent amounts have exactly reacted.
