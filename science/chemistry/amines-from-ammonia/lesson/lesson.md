# Amines — from ammonia

## Do it first
Before you read: ammonia, NH₃, has three hydrogens on one nitrogen. Say out loud: what family would you get if you swapped those hydrogens, one at a time, for carbon groups? Then read on: Mauritania's 6AS book builds that family and sorts it by the number of swaps.

## Sub lesson 1 — From ammonia — the replacement ladder

The Chimie 6AS book presents the amines inside its functions chapter (printed p. 72): take ammonia and replace one of its hydrogens with an alkyl group — a primary amine; replace two — a secondary amine; replace all three — a tertiary amine. The nitrogen stays at the centre of the family; what changes is how many of its three places are taken by carbon groups.

## Sub lesson 2 — Naming from the substituents

An amine is named from the groups its nitrogen carries: the alkyl substituents are named, and the word amine closes the name. The book's worked case is trimethylamine — a tertiary amine whose nitrogen carries three methyl groups, its name simply reading off the three substituents and their count.

## Sub lesson 3 — One nitrogen, three classes

The ladder is the sorting: count the alkyl groups on nitrogen — one, primary; two, secondary; three, tertiary. The same count that builds the amine names its class, so structure, name and class are three readings of one fact about the nitrogen.

## The key ideas

- Amines are ammonia with one, two or three hydrogens replaced by alkyl groups.
- The replacement count gives the class: primary (1), secondary (2), tertiary (3).
- Names read off the substituents — trimethylamine: three methyls on nitrogen.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the lesson to your companion in your own words, working through its sub lessons in order (from ammonia — the replacement ladder; naming from the substituents; one nitrogen, three classes), using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Classify (CH₃)₂NH and CH₃NH₂. ((CH₃)₂NH carries two alkyl groups on nitrogen — secondary amine; CH₃NH₂ carries one — primary amine.)
2. Why is trimethylamine tertiary? (Its nitrogen carries three methyl groups — all three of ammonia's hydrogens have been replaced.)
3. A pupil calls NH₃ itself a primary amine. Correct them. (Ammonia is the parent, not a member: an amine begins when at least one hydrogen has been replaced by an alkyl group.)
