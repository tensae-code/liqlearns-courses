# Hands-on — Molar volume of a gas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Measure a mole of air (paper and a balloon)
Blow up a balloon and measure its circumference; work out its volume in litres from the sphere formulas your maths course gives, then divide by 22.4 L/mol to estimate the moles of air inside at room conditions. Write the conditions you assumed beside the number, as the books do.

## Two conditions, two volumes (paper only)
For 3 mol of a gas, compute the volume twice: at Vm = 22.4 L/mol and at Vm = 24 L/mol. Write one sentence on what the difference teaches about quoting a molar volume without its conditions.
