# Hands-on — Integrated rate equations and half-life

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The straight line and the curve (paper only)
A zero-order reaction starts at 0.80 mol L⁻¹ with k = 2.0 × 10⁻² mol L⁻¹ min⁻¹. Tabulate [R] every 10 minutes to 40 minutes and plot the points (draw the axes by hand). Then do the same for a first-order reaction with the same start whose half-life is 20 minutes, halving at each step. Write under each plot its shape in one word.

## Halving clock (paper only)
A first-order reaction has k = 4.62 × 10⁻² min⁻¹. Calculate its half-life from t½ = 0.693/k, then list how much of a 1.00 g sample remains after one, two, three and four half-lives. Check your last figure by the fraction rule (½)⁴.

## Which order? (paper only)
Two reactions each start at 1.0 mol L⁻¹. Reaction P reaches 0.50 in 10 min and 0.25 in 20 min. Reaction Q reaches 0.50 in 10 min and 0.00 in 20 min. Name each order and give the evidence in one sentence each.
