# Hands-on — Uses of common elements and compounds

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Walk through your home and list ten things, then sort them: which are elements (a metal pan, a copper wire) and which are compounds (water, salt, soap, plastic)? For each one, write the job it does — the use that gives it its value.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of uses of common elements and compounds — the elements and compounds around us earn their place by what they do — oxygen for breathing and burning, iron for building, water and salts for life — and each has a market value because people need what it does. — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
