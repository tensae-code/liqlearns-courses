# Uses of common elements and compounds — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: elements at work.
**Answer: Common elements do daily jobs: oxygen is breathed and supports burning; iron and aluminium are built into tools, roofs and vehicles; copper carries electricity in wires; carbon in its forms fuels fires and writes as graphite. Life itself is built from elements — carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen make up most of every living body.**

**2.** Explain: compounds at work.
**Answer: Compounds join elements into new materials with new uses: water for drinking, cooking and growing; common salt for food and preserving; limestone and cement for building; soaps and fertilisers are made compounds. A compound's uses come from its own properties, which are different from the elements inside it.**

**3.** Explain: use gives value.
**Answer: Because elements and compounds do needed work, they are bought and sold: metals, fuels, salts and fertilisers all carry a market value that rises and falls with how much people need them and how hard they are to get. Knowing what a substance is for is the first step to valuing it — and to not wasting it.**
