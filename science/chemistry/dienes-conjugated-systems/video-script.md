Today: **Dienes — conjugated systems** — Belarus's way: dienes as their own section, between the alkenes and the alkynes.

Start with two double bonds in one molecule. Where they stand is everything. Sharing one carbon — cumulated. Separated by two or more single bonds — isolated, each bond on its own. And standing double–single–double — conjugated, the arrangement that changes the chemistry.
In buta-1,3-diene the π electrons do not sit on two separate bonds. They spread over all four carbons in one shared cloud. The molecule is steadier than it should be, and when a reagent adds, it can land across the far ends — 1,4-addition. The system reacts as one thing.
And that one thing builds the material world: join butadiene units into chains and you have synthetic rubber; the rubber tree does the same sum with isoprene. Two double bonds, arranged just so, end up on every wheel.
