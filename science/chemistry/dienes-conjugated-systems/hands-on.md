# Hands-on — Dienes — conjugated systems

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort the arrangements (paper only)
Draw these four structures in full: CH₂=C=CH₂, CH₂=CH–CH=CH₂, CH₂=CH–CH₂–CH=CH₂, CH₃–C≡C–CH₃. Sort them into a row labelled cumulated, conjugated, isolated, not-a-diene, saying out loud the arrangement test you applied to each. Check against sub lesson 1 — note the fourth card's lesson: the formula CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ alone cannot do the sorting.

## Trace the cloud
Draw buta-1,3-diene large, with a p orbital (a dumbbell) on each of its four carbons. Shade the region the π electrons occupy in the conjugated system, then draw penta-1,4-diene the same way and shade its two separate regions. Under each drawing write one sentence: what the shading explains about addition products.

## From monomer to material
On one long strip of paper, draw six buta-1,3-diene units joining head-to-tail into a chain, marking the double bond that remains inside each unit of the chain. Beside it, note where sulfur could cross-link two such chains, and write two sentences on what the cross-links change about the material.
