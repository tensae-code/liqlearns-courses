# Lesson plan — Dienes — conjugated systems

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Dienes — conjugated systems
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the dienes as their own family, as Belarus' Grade 11 book sections them (Part 3 §14): hydrocarbons with two double bonds, sorted cumulated / conjugated / isolated; the conjugated system — π electrons delocalised over four carbons, steadiness and 1,4-addition as its evidences; and the polymerisation of conjugated dienes to the synthetic and natural rubbers

## Sub lessons
1. **Two double bonds in one molecule** — A diene is a hydrocarbon carrying two C=C double bonds in one molecule — general formula CₙH₂ₙ₋₂, the same as the alkynes, which makes the sorting by bond position, not formula, the real work: cumulated (the bonds share a carbon), conjugated (double–single–double) and isolated (the bonds kept apart by two or more single bonds).
2. **Conjugation — the shared cloud** — In a conjugated diene — buta-1,3-diene, CH₂=CH–CH=CH₂, is the type specimen — the single bond between the two double bonds is not an ordinary single bond: the π electrons spread over all four carbons, a shared cloud that changes the chemistry — addition can land at the ends (1,4-addition) as well as at one bond (1,2-addition), and the molecule is steadier than its formula suggests.
3. **From dienes to rubber** — Conjugated dienes polymerise: buta-1,3-diene links into the long elastic chains of synthetic rubber (buna), and its natural cousin isoprene is the monomer of natural rubber itself — the diene section of Belarus' book is where the rubber of tyres and gloves is born, by the same addition polymerisation the alkenes began.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Treats a conjugated diene as 'an alkene twice' → return to the cloud: draw the four p orbitals and ask where a reagent at carbon 1 can be answered from — the whole system, which is why carbon 4 answers too.
