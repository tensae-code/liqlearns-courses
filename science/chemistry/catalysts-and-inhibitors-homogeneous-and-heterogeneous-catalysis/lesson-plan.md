# Lesson plan — Catalysts and inhibitors — homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysis

## Sub lessons
1. **Catalysts, inhibitors, and the homogeneous road** — A **catalyst changes a reaction's rate and remains chemically unchanged**. **Positive** catalysts speed a reaction; **inhibitors** (negative catalysts) slow one — H⁺ speeds fat hydrolysis and esterification, while phosphoric acid slows peroxide decomposition. In **homogeneous catalysis** catalyst and reactants share one phase: Fe³⁺ ions catalysing H₂O₂ decomposition, its rate growing with catalyst concentration. The mechanism is **intermediate compounds**: in the NO cycle for SO₂ oxidation the catalyst is consumed into an intermediate and **regenerated** by the end.
2. **The heterogeneous road — adsorption** — In **heterogeneous catalysis** the catalyst is a solid surface — MnO₂ for peroxide. Reactant particles are **held and weakened on the surface (adsorption)**, so the **activation energy falls**; Bulgaria draws the energy diagram with and without the catalyst side by side. Catalysts are **specific**: CO + H₂ gives methanol over Cu/Ni but methanal over Co, as printed. Industry runs on catalysis — sulfuric and nitric acid, ammonia, fertilisers, plastics, synthetic rubber — and **catalytic converters** turn unburned hydrocarbons and toxic gases into water, nitrogen and CO₂.
3. **Biocatalysis — enzymes** — **Enzymes** are the body's catalysts: amylase, trypsin, pepsin, lipase and zymase run the hydrolyses **quickly, at low temperature, with great specificity**. The food industry hires the same workers — juice and wine clarification, cheese ripening, better bread.
