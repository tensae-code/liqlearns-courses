# Hands-on — Catalysts and inhibitors — homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysis

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes. (Paper work unless an activity says otherwise; any real bench work belongs to the school laboratory, with its safety kit and a teacher present.)

## Two roads, one peroxide
Paper practical only: write the two Bulgaria cases side by side — Fe³⁺ in solution (homogeneous) and solid MnO₂ (heterogeneous) decomposing H₂O₂. For each, draw where the catalyst sits during the reaction and circle the word that proves it survives: regenerated (solution) / unchanged surface (solid).

## Converter audit
List the gases a catalytic converter receives (unburned hydrocarbons, carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides) and the gases it releases (water, nitrogen, CO₂), matched arrow by arrow from the lesson. Then name the catalysis kind and say why a surface is the right shape for the job.
