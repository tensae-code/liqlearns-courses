# Catalysts and inhibitors — homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysis

## Do it first
A pinch of powder can make a slow reaction race — and come out of it exactly as it went in. Before reading, say out loud: if the powder is unchanged at the end, what did it actually do in the middle? Bulgaria's Grade 10 catalysis lesson answers in three theatres: in solution, on a surface, and in living tissue.

## Sub lesson 1 — Catalysts, inhibitors, and the homogeneous road

A **catalyst changes a reaction's rate and remains chemically unchanged**. **Positive** catalysts speed a reaction; **inhibitors** (negative catalysts) slow one — H⁺ speeds fat hydrolysis and esterification, while phosphoric acid slows peroxide decomposition. In **homogeneous catalysis** catalyst and reactants share one phase: Fe³⁺ ions catalysing H₂O₂ decomposition, its rate growing with catalyst concentration. The mechanism is **intermediate compounds**: in the NO cycle for SO₂ oxidation the catalyst is consumed into an intermediate and **regenerated** by the end.

## Sub lesson 2 — The heterogeneous road — adsorption

In **heterogeneous catalysis** the catalyst is a solid surface — MnO₂ for peroxide. Reactant particles are **held and weakened on the surface (adsorption)**, so the **activation energy falls**; Bulgaria draws the energy diagram with and without the catalyst side by side. Catalysts are **specific**: CO + H₂ gives methanol over Cu/Ni but methanal over Co, as printed. Industry runs on catalysis — sulfuric and nitric acid, ammonia, fertilisers, plastics, synthetic rubber — and **catalytic converters** turn unburned hydrocarbons and toxic gases into water, nitrogen and CO₂.

## Sub lesson 3 — Biocatalysis — enzymes

**Enzymes** are the body's catalysts: amylase, trypsin, pepsin, lipase and zymase run the hydrolyses **quickly, at low temperature, with great specificity**. The food industry hires the same workers — juice and wine clarification, cheese ripening, better bread.

## The key ideas

- Catalyst: changes the rate, chemically unchanged at the end. Speeding = positive catalyst; slowing = inhibitor.
- Homogeneous: same phase, works through intermediate compounds, catalyst regenerated. Heterogeneous: solid surface, adsorption weakens reactants, activation energy falls.
- Catalysts are specific (CO + H₂: methanol over Cu/Ni, methanal over Co). Enzymes: fast, low-temperature, highly specific biocatalysts.

Explain this lesson to your companion: catalysts, inhibitors, and the homogeneous road; the heterogeneous road — adsorption; biocatalysis — enzymes — in that order, in your own words, with the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. MnO₂ is tipped into hydrogen peroxide and stays MnO₂ when the fizzing ends. Classify it. (A positive, heterogeneous catalyst — it speeds the decomposition from its solid surface and is unchanged at the end.)
2. Phosphoric acid is added to stored peroxide to keep it from decomposing. What is it acting as? (An inhibitor — a negative catalyst slowing the decomposition.)
