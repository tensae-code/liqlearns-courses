# Lesson plan — Addition vs condensation polymerisation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Addition vs condensation polymerisation
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Polymers (Grade 12 Chemistry).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Addition polymerisation: monomers with C=C bonds open their double bonds and add on, one after another — nothing is lost.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 12 Chemistry lessons before it in Unit 4 — Polymers — and on the learner's earlier chemistry. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Addition polymerisation** — Addition polymerisation: monomers with C=C bonds open their double bonds and add on, one after another — nothing is lost.
2. **Condensation polymerisation** — Condensation polymerisation: monomers join with the loss of a small molecule, usually water, at each link.
3. **Named polymers and their uses** — The products' uses follow their structures — the section works named polymers one by one.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Grade 12 Chemistry lessons, especially the lessons of Unit 4 before this one. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Answer, in their own words: what is lost in condensation polymerisation?
2. Answer, in their own words: what bond opens in addition polymerisation?
3. Answer, in their own words: name an addition polymer.
4. Answer, in their own words: name a condensation polymer.
