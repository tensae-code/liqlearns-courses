# Addition vs condensation polymerisation

## Do it first
Two ways to build a chain: click the beads together whole, or snap them together discarding a small piece each time. Before reading, say which way loses water — and which polymer on the book's cover is made that way.

[Picture 21773 — The two routes side by side.]

## Sub lesson 1 — Addition polymerisation
Addition polymerisation: monomers with C=C bonds open their double bonds and add on, one after another — nothing is lost. Ethene → polyethene is the model; other named plastics follow the same route.

## Sub lesson 2 — Condensation polymerisation
Condensation polymerisation: monomers join with the loss of a small molecule, usually water, at each link. PET and nylon are built this way, with the structures presented.

## Sub lesson 3 — Named polymers and their uses
The products' uses follow their structures — the section works named polymers one by one. Teacher guide evidence stresses that the synthesis routes themselves are examinable content, kept inside this lesson.
