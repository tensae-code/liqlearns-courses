# Separating mixtures — decantation, filtration, distillation

## Do it first
Before you read: muddy water, and salt water, both need taking apart — but not by the same tool. Say out loud: what does each mixture let you exploit — settling, straining, or boiling away? Then read on: Mauritania's first chemistry unit matches three mixtures to three separations.

## Sub lesson 1 — Décantation — let it settle, pour it clear

The 2AS book's first separation (Unit I §I, printed p. 8) is décantation: muddy water left at rest lets its solid settle to the bottom, and the clear liquid above is poured off gently. No apparatus, no heating — gravity and patience do the work, because the mixture is heterogeneous: its parts were never dissolved into one another.

## Sub lesson 2 — Filtration — the residue and the filtrat

Filtration finishes what settling starts: pour the mixture through a filter and the solid stays on the filter — the residue — while the liquid passes through — the filtrat. The book names both products of the straining, because a separation is only done when each part is accounted for: what stayed, and what passed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Distillation — the most volatile part first

For a homogeneous liquid mixture, where nothing settles and nothing can be strained out, the book distils: heat the mixture and the most volatile part vaporises first, travels to the condenser, and condenses there into the distillate — approximately pure water in the book's working case. The unit's exercises run the same family of ideas on evaporation in the salt marshes: 40 L of seawater leaving 1 kg of salt.

## The key ideas

- Décantation: settle a heterogeneous mixture, pour the clear liquid off the settled solid.
- Filtration: the residue stays on the filter; the filtrat passes through.
- Distillation: the most volatile part of a homogeneous liquid mixture vaporises first and condenses as the distillate; evaporation in the salt marshes — 40 L of seawater → 1 kg of salt.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why can muddy water be decanted but salt water not? (Muddy water is heterogeneous — its solid settles under gravity and the clear water pours off. In salt water the salt is dissolved into a homogeneous mixture: nothing settles, so a boiling-family method (distillation/evaporation) is needed.)
2. In a filtration, name the two products and where each is found. (The residue — the solid left on the filter; the filtrat — the liquid that passed through the filter.)
3. What comes over first in a distillation, and where is it collected? (The most volatile part vaporises first; it condenses in the condenser and is collected as the distillate.)
