# Lesson plan — Separating mixtures — decantation, filtration, distillation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Separating mixtures — decantation, filtration, distillation
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** taking a mixture apart by its parts' own behaviour: décantation of muddy water by settling, filtration (the residue stays on the filter, the filtrat passes), and distillation of a homogeneous liquid mixture, where the most volatile part vaporises first and condenses to a distillate — with evaporation applied to the salt marshes, 40 L of seawater yielding 1 kg of salt (Mauritania, Grade 8 — *Sciences Physiques 2AS*, Unit I, printed p. 8)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Mixtures — homogeneous and heterogeneous is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
