# Preserving food

## Do it first
Every preservation method is an attack on spoilage microbes' living conditions. Before reading, match the methods to the conditions they remove: water, warmth, air, or the microbes themselves.

[Picture 21767 — Four preservation methods, four panels.]

Drying removes water microbes need; salting draws water out likewise.

Heating kills microbes (and canning seals them out); refrigeration slows their growth.

Chemical preservation (acids, sulfites and kin as presented) makes food hostile to microbes. Each method's chemistry is the section's content.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Preserving food to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
