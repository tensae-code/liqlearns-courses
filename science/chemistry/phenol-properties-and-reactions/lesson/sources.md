| §29 Phenol (Bulgaria, Grade 9) | Book 1 (Grade 9) — printed pp. 87–89 — the –OH group bound straight to the benzene ring (from benzene via chlorobenzene + NaOH): the ring polarises the O–H bond further, so phenol — unlike ethanol and glycerol — is a WEAK ACID (aqueous solution pink-red to litmus, pH < 7; C₆H₅OH ⇌ C₆H₅O⁻ + H⁺; "carbolic acid" of old medicine), reacting with active metals, basic oxides and bases (sodium phenolate); the ring in return substitutes more easily than benzene — bromine water throws down white 2,4,6-tribromophenol, nitration gives picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol, an explosive); phenol proved by FeCl₃ (violet); itself a protoplasmic poison and bactericide (5% solution once the standard disinfectant), a spill pollutant that biology cannot clean, and the parent of the first plastic — Bakelite from phenol–formaldehyde resin, which, unlike polyethylene, never softens again and cannot be recycled. |
| Classification §§42–43 (Bulgaria, Grade 10) | Book 2 (Grade 10) — printed p. 111 — Ethanol, glycerol and phenol compared through their hydroxyl group: glycerol's qualitative test with freshly precipitated Cu(OH)2 (bright blue solution), phenol's violet FeCl3 test, phenol's solution standing below pH 7 because the ring influences the –OH (which is why phenol reacts with sodium and with sodium hydroxide where ethanol does not), and the solubility of all three traced to their hydrogen bonding.
