# Phenol — properties and reactions

## Do it first
Ethanol's –OH group minds its own business — neutral. Bolt the same –OH straight onto a benzene ring and it turns acid. Before reading, say out loud: what must the ring be doing to the O–H bond? Bulgaria's books answer from both sides: the ring changes the group, and the group changes the ring.

## Sub lesson 1 — Phenol the weak acid

Phenol is made **from benzene via chlorobenzene + NaOH**. The ring **polarises the O–H bond further**, so phenol — unlike ethanol and glycerol — is a **weak acid**: its solution turns litmus pink-red, **pH < 7**, **C₆H₅OH ⇌ C₆H₅O⁻ + H⁺** — the 'carbolic acid' of old medicine. It reacts with **active metals, basic oxides and bases**, giving salts such as **sodium phenolate**. Bulgaria's Grade 10 comparison sets ethanol, glycerol and phenol side by side: phenol reacts with sodium and with sodium hydroxide **where ethanol does not**, all three's solubility traced to hydrogen bonding.

## Sub lesson 2 — The ring reacts back — tests, uses, dangers

The –OH group in return makes the ring **substitute more easily than benzene**: **bromine water throws down white 2,4,6-tribromophenol**, and **nitration gives picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol, an explosive)**. Phenol is **proved by FeCl₃ — violet**. It is itself a **protoplasmic poison and bactericide** (a 5% solution was once the standard disinfectant), a spill pollutant biology cannot clean, and the parent of the first plastic — **Bakelite**, from phenol–formaldehyde resin, which never softens again and cannot be recycled. Glycerol's own test stands in the comparison: freshly precipitated Cu(OH)₂ dissolving bright blue.

## The key ideas

- Phenol = –OH on the ring: a weak acid (pH < 7; C₆H₅OH ⇌ C₆H₅O⁻ + H⁺), reacting with metals, basic oxides and bases — where ethanol does not.
- Ring substitutions run easier than benzene's: bromine water → white 2,4,6-tribromophenol; nitration → picric acid. Test: FeCl₃ violet.
- Phenol is a poison and bactericide, an uncleanable spill pollutant, and Bakelite's parent.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does phenol react with NaOH when ethanol does not? (The benzene ring polarises phenol's O–H bond further, so its hydrogen leaves as H⁺ far more readily — phenol is a weak acid; ethanol is neutral.)
2. A colourless solution gives a violet colour with FeCl₃ and a white precipitate with bromine water. Name it. (Phenol — both are its signature tests in Bulgaria's books.)
