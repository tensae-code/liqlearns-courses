# Lesson plan — Phenol — properties and reactions

## Sub lessons
1. **Phenol the weak acid** — Phenol is made **from benzene via chlorobenzene + NaOH**. The ring **polarises the O–H bond further**, so phenol — unlike ethanol and glycerol — is a **weak acid**: its solution turns litmus pink-red, **pH < 7**, **C₆H₅OH ⇌ C₆H₅O⁻ + H⁺** — the 'carbolic acid' of old medicine. It reacts with **active metals, basic oxides and bases**, giving salts such as **sodium phenolate**. Bulgaria's Grade 10 comparison sets ethanol, glycerol and phenol side by side: phenol reacts with sodium and with sodium hydroxide **where ethanol does not**, all three's solubility traced to hydrogen bonding.
2. **The ring reacts back — tests, uses, dangers** — The –OH group in return makes the ring **substitute more easily than benzene**: **bromine water throws down white 2,4,6-tribromophenol**, and **nitration gives picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol, an explosive)**. Phenol is **proved by FeCl₃ — violet**. It is itself a **protoplasmic poison and bactericide** (a 5% solution was once the standard disinfectant), a spill pollutant biology cannot clean, and the parent of the first plastic — **Bakelite**, from phenol–formaldehyde resin, which never softens again and cannot be recycled. Glycerol's own test stands in the comparison: freshly precipitated Cu(OH)₂ dissolving bright blue.
