# Why the periodic table works — Unit 1 review

## Do it first
Before the review, test yourself: from its place alone, what could you predict about an element you have never handled — its size, its greed for electrons, whether it is a metal? If you can answer, the table has done its work.

Classification makes study systematic: learn one group member well and you know the family's pattern.

Properties of undiscovered or unmeasured elements can be predicted from their position — gaps in the early table were filled this way.

Unit 1's chain runs: atom → subatomic particles → quantum numbers → configurations → the table → its trends. This review lesson closes that chain.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Why the periodic table works — Unit 1 review to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
