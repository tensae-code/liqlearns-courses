# Lesson plan — Natural rubber and synthetic rubber

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Natural rubber and synthetic rubber
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rubber as Malaysia teaches it — natural rubber from the latex of the rubber tree, made hard-wearing by vulcanisation, and synthetic rubbers built in factories to copy and extend it — Malaysia's own historic industry taught as chemistry, inside its Form 5 polymer chapter

## Sub lessons
1. **Natural rubber — from latex** — Natural rubber begins as **latex**: the milky liquid that flows when the bark of the **rubber tree** is cut.
2. **Vulcanisation — sulfur locks the chains** — That idea is **vulcanisation**: heating raw rubber with **sulfur**.
3. **Synthetic rubber — built in factories** — **Synthetic rubbers** are rubber-like polymers **made in factories from petroleum-based monomers**, built to copy natural rubber's spring — and to go where natural rubber cannot.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the one element: rubber is a polymer in two sources — grown as latex in the rubber tree, or built in factories — and vulcanisation is the sulfur cross-linking that makes the grown kind hard-wearing.
- Thinks vulcanisation is just drying or heating → heating alone does not do it: the sulfur forms cross-links — bridges — between the chains; it is the locking together of the chains, not the warmth, that hardens and strengthens the rubber.
- Thinks synthetic rubber is fake or inferior rubber → synthetic rubbers are real polymers designed in factories; they copy natural rubber's spring and add properties it lacks, such as resisting oils — different jobs, not a fake version.
