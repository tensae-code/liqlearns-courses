# Hands-on — Natural rubber and synthetic rubber

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Spring and lock (home materials — a rubber band and a ball of string)
Stretch a rubber band and let it spring back; note how it returns. Now tangle a ball of string loosely and pull — the strands slide apart and do not return. Tie a few knots between strands and pull again. Write down what the knots changed and say it in lesson terms: cross-links stop chains sliding away while still letting them stretch and spring back — that is what sulfur does in vulcanisation. (Observation only.)

## The rubber hunt (observation only — home and street)
Find three rubber objects around your home or street — a tyre, a glove, a rubber band, a shoe sole, a hose. For each, write the job it does and the one property — springy, hard-wearing, heat-standing, oil-resisting — the job cannot do without, as Malaysia's section sets natural and synthetic rubber side by side.

## Natural or synthetic? (paper exercise)
List six rubber uses. Sort them into two columns — natural rubber (vulcanised) would serve / a synthetic rubber is the better build — and defend each placing in one line, naming the property that decides it.
