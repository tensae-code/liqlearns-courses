# Natural rubber and synthetic rubber — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is latex, and how does it become natural rubber?
**Answer: Latex is the milky liquid that flows from a cut rubber tree — rubber particles suspended in water. When it coagulates (treated with acid, the particles clump together), it becomes solid natural rubber, which is collected, rolled and dried.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What does sulfur do inside rubber during vulcanisation?
**Answer: The sulfur atoms form cross-links — bridges — between the long rubber polymer chains, locking the chains to each other so they can stretch and spring back but cannot slide away from each other.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is vulcanised rubber the rubber of tyres while raw rubber is not?
**Answer: Raw rubber is soft and sticky in heat and brittle in cold. Vulcanisation makes it harder, stronger, more elastic and more resistant to heat — the properties a tyre needs to survive years of hot roads.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What is a synthetic rubber, and what can it do that natural rubber cannot?
**Answer: A synthetic rubber is a rubber-like polymer made in factories from petroleum-based monomers. Because its chains are designed, it can be given properties natural rubber lacks — for example resisting oils and solvents that swell and spoil natural rubber.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does Malaysia give rubber two whole sections of its polymer chapter?
**Answer: Rubber is both a core polymer example and Malaysia's own historic industry: the natural polymer is tapped from trees in the country itself, so the chapter teaches the science through an industry its learners can see — then sets the factory-made synthetic rubbers beside it.**
