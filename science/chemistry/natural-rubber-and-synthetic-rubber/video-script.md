Today: **Natural rubber and synthetic rubber** — Malaysia's own industry, taught as chemistry.

Cut a rubber tree and out flows latex — white, milky, full of rubber particles swimming in water. Clump those particles together and you have natural rubber: soft, sticky, not yet a tyre. Heat it with sulfur and the sulfur locks the long chains to each other with little bridges — vulcanised rubber: hard, strong, springy, ready for the road. And in factories, chemists build rubbers of their own from petroleum, copying the spring and adding tricks the tree never learned, like standing up to oil.

One polymer, two sources — the tree and the factory — and one sulfur bridge between raw and ready.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
