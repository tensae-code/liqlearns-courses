# Air pollution

## Do it first
City air carries invisible cargo from exhausts, factories and fires. Before reading, list the major pollutants you expect — then check yours against the book's.

[Picture 21779 — City air with pollutant sources labelled.]

Major air pollutants include carbon monoxide, sulfur and nitrogen oxides, particulates and unburned hydrocarbons, from vehicles, industry and burning as presented.

Their effects: acid rain from SO₂/NOₓ dissolving in rain, smog over cities, and direct harm to lungs and health.

The lesson ties each pollutant to its sources and effects.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Air pollution to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
