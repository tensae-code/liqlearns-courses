# Lesson plan — The Born–Haber cycle

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** The Born–Haber cycle
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Born–Haber cycle — building an ionic compound's formation in measured steps (sublimation, dissociation, ionisation, electron affinity, lattice energy) whose energies must sum with the heat of formation to zero — as Laos's Grade 11 (ມ.6) textbook teaches it in Ch. 10 §2 for NaCl (the book spells it “Bon Haber Cycle”; taught corrected as Born–Haber)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. No other lesson is needed first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why a cycle — formation in steps you can measure** — An ionic compound's heat of formation cannot always be measured directly, but it can be reached by a detour: build the compound in separate steps, each with a known energy, and the energies around any closed loop must balance.
2. **The five steps for NaCl** — The book's steps, with its own numbers (printed pp.
3. **Closing the cycle — the sum is zero** — Around the closed cycle the steps must cancel: the book writes ΔHₛ + ½D + IE + E + U + (−ΔH_f) = 0, and its numbers obey it — 109 + 121 + 494 − 347 − 787 + 410 = 0.
