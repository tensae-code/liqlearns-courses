# Hands-on — The Downs cell — making sodium from its salt

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Draw the cell (paper only)
Draw the Downs cell in section from the lesson: graphite anode at the centre, iron cathode ring around it, iron gauze diaphragm between, the melt, chlorine leaving above the anode, and liquid sodium rising into its collecting chamber. Label each part and write the half-equation beside the electrode it belongs to.

## Two products, one current (paper only)
A classmate says the cell wastes half its work making 'useless' chlorine. Answer on paper from the course's own lessons: name two uses of chlorine from the halogens and chlor-alkali lessons, and one use of sodium from the alkali-metals lesson — the cell's two products both earn their place.
