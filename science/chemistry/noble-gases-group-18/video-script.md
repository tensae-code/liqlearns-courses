Today: **Noble gases — group 18** — India's way: the closing section of the Class XII p-block unit — the family that completes the table's right-hand edge.

Every noble gas carries a completed outer shell — eight electrons, helium's two — and a completed shell wants nothing: no electrons to gain, lose or share. So the family stands monoatomic and nearly inert, boiling at the lowest temperatures in the table, found free because nothing binds them — argon alone making almost one percent of the air itself.
What will not react is precious wherever reaction is the enemy. Helium lifts balloons and chills superconductors in its liquid form; neon glows orange-red in the advertising tube; argon blankets welding arcs and fills light bulbs so filaments and molten metal meet no oxygen. And then there is xenon, the traitor: in 1962 Bartlett made XePtF₆, and the xenon fluorides followed — the 'inert' family had compounds after all.
