# Noble gases — group 18 — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) The noble gases are monoatomic because…
a) They are metals  b) Their completed outer shells give them no drive to bond, so single atoms are their stable form  c) They are too heavy to pair  d) They boil too low to touch
**Answer: b) Their completed outer shells give them no drive to bond, so single atoms are their stable form**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Argon makes up about what share of dry air?
a) 21%  b) 0.03%  c) 0.93% — the third gas of the air  d) 5%
**Answer: c) 0.93% — the third gas of the air**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Helium for balloons is obtained mainly from…
a) Seawater  b) Natural gas, where underground radioactive decay has stocked it  c) Electrolysis  d) Coal tar
**Answer: b) Natural gas, where underground radioactive decay has stocked it**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) The orange-red glow of advertising signs comes from…
a) Argon  b) Helium  c) Neon in a discharge tube  d) Radon
**Answer: c) Neon in a discharge tube**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Bartlett attempted a xenon compound because…
a) Xenon was cheap  b) Xenon's ionisation enthalpy (1170 kJ mol⁻¹) nearly equals O₂'s (1175), and PtF₆ had already oxidised O₂  c) Xenon is radioactive  d) PtF₆ reacts with everything
**Answer: b) Xenon's ionisation enthalpy (1170 kJ mol⁻¹) nearly equals O₂'s (1175), and PtF₆ had already oxidised O₂**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) XeF₄ is prepared from the elements using…
a) A 1:5 Xe:F₂ mixture under pressure and heat  b) Electrolysis of Xe in HF  c) Hydrolysis  d) Burning xenon in air
**Answer: a) A 1:5 Xe:F₂ mixture under pressure and heat**
