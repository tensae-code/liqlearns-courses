# Hands-on — Noble gases — group 18

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Use detective (paper only)
Six jobs are listed: lifting a weather balloon; the orange-red glow of a shop sign; shielding a welding arc from air; the coolant that chills a hospital MRI magnet; the flash of a high-speed camera lamp; filling an ordinary light bulb. Assign each its noble gas from the lesson and write the property that makes the choice.

## Air's hidden percent (paper only)
Dry air is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, leaving about 1%. Write what nearly all of that remainder is, and explain from sub lesson 1 why it sat unnoticed in air until the 1890s — what two properties hid it from every earlier analysis.

## Bartlett's ledger (paper only)
Bartlett reasoned from O₂PtF₆ to XePtF₆. Write the two facts he matched — the first ionisation enthalpy of O₂ (1175 kJ mol⁻¹) against that of Xe (1170 kJ mol⁻¹) — and state in one sentence why that near-equality made a xenon compound worth attempting.
