# Lesson plan — Aluminium — a metal that fights acids and alkalis

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Aluminium — a metal that fights acids and alkalis
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Aluminium's reactions run systematically, as Yemen's Grade 11 book runs them: with oxygen to Al2O3; fast with dilute HCl but slowly with concentrated acid; SO2 from hot concentrated H2SO4; nothing with nitric acid, because the protective oxide coat forms — which is why aluminium tankers carry nitric acid; soluble aluminates plus hydrogen with NaOH or KOH; and compounds with sulfur, nitrogen, carbon and the halogens — the amphoteric metal behaviour done by hand in the practical workbook's first two activities, aluminium powder on the Bunsen flame and heated with sodium hydroxide solution
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Oxides — acidic, basic, amphoteric, neutral, peroxides is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
