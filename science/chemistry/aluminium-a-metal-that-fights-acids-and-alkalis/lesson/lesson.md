# Aluminium — a metal that fights acids and alkalis

## Do it first
Before you read: one metal is attacked by both an acid and an alkali, yet is trusted to carry the fiercest acid of all in road tankers. Say out loud: what could a metal wear that lets it refuse one acid completely while dissolving in another — and why would an alkali dissolve a metal at all? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 11 book runs aluminium's reactions one partner at a time.

## Sub lesson 1 — The reaction run — oxygen and the acids

The book runs aluminium's reactions **systematically**. With **oxygen** it gives **Al₂O₃**. With **dilute hydrochloric acid it reacts fast** — **2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂** — but with **concentrated acid only slowly**. With **hot concentrated sulfuric acid** it gives **SO₂**. And with **nitric acid — nothing**: a **protective oxide coat** forms on the metal and seals it, which is exactly **why aluminium tankers carry nitric acid**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The alkali attack — the amphoteric signature

Then the signature that makes aluminium **amphoteric**: with **sodium hydroxide or potassium hydroxide** it dissolves to **soluble aluminates plus hydrogen** — **2Al + 2NaOH + 2H₂O → 2NaAlO₂ + 3H₂↑**. A metal that fights acids *and* alkalis is playing both sides of the acid–base world, because its oxide and hydroxide do the same — the behaviour the oxides lesson met in Al₂O₃.

## Sub lesson 3 — The rest of the partners — and the practical proof

The run closes with the nonmetals: with **sulfur, nitrogen, carbon and the halogens** aluminium gives **Al₂S₃, AlN, Al₄C₃, AlCl₃ and AlF₃**. Yemen's practical workbook does the lesson by hand: **Activity 1** tips aluminium powder onto a non-luminous Bunsen flame to see how it burns, and **Activity 2** heats aluminium powder with sodium hydroxide solution and asks the student to name the gas evolved and write the equation — the amphoteric metal, proved at the bench.

## The key ideas

- Aluminium: **dilute HCl fast** (2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂), concentrated acid slow, hot concentrated H₂SO₄ gives **SO₂**, **nitric acid not at all** — the **protective oxide coat** is why tankers carry it in aluminium.
- With **NaOH/KOH** it dissolves to **aluminates + hydrogen** (2Al + 2NaOH + 2H₂O → 2NaAlO₂ + 3H₂↑) — the **amphoteric** signature.
- With sulfur, nitrogen, carbon, halogens: **Al₂S₃, AlN, Al₄C₃, AlCl₃, AlF₃**; the workbook's Activities 1–2 do the flame and the alkali by hand.

## Do It Yourself
1. A tanker of nitric acid is built of aluminium, yet aluminium dissolves in hydrochloric acid. Resolve the puzzle in two sentences. (Nitric acid does not attack aluminium because a protective oxide coat forms and seals the metal; hydrochloric acid gives no such protection — dilute HCl attacks fast, forming AlCl₃ and hydrogen. The metal's safety depends on which acid, not on the metal alone.)
2. Write the equation for aluminium with sodium hydroxide solution and name the gas and the salt type formed. (2Al + 2NaOH + 2H₂O → 2NaAlO₂ + 3H₂↑. The gas is hydrogen; the salt is an aluminate (sodium aluminate).)
3. What does the practical workbook's Activity 2 ask the student to do with the gas from aluminium and NaOH? (To name the gas evolved (hydrogen) and write the equation for the reaction — the amphoteric behaviour proved by hand, not only read.)
