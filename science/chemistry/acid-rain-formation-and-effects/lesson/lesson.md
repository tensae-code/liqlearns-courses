# Acid rain — formation and effects

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Sulfur(IV) oxide — the precursor

The strand opens with a study of **sulfur(IV) oxide, SO₂** (§1.9 spread, printed p. 26): the gas named as the **acid-rain precursor** — the substance in the air from which acid rain forms. The chemistry comes first: know the precursor, and the rain that follows it can be understood.

## Sub lesson 2 — Acid rain on stone — Armenia's heritage as the evidence

The book then sets **research on acid rain's effect on stone monuments and structures** (printed p. 28). Armenia is a country of carved stone — churches, monuments and buildings standing in the open air for centuries — so the damage acid rain does to stone is not an abstraction: it is investigated on **Armenia's own stone heritage**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Acid rains and environmental problems

The lesson closes with **acid rains and environmental problems** (§1.10, printed p. 30): the precursor, the damage and the wider problem held together as one environmental strand — the same Theme 1 that protects the air basin and studies water gives acid rain its own full treatment.

## The key ideas

- **Sulfur(IV) oxide (SO₂)** is the **acid-rain precursor** the strand studies first.
- The damage is researched on **stone monuments and structures** — Armenia's own stone heritage.
- Formation, damage and environmental problems are taught as **one strand** (§1.9–§1.10).
