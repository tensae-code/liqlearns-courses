# Planck, photons and the photoelectric effect

## Do it first
Shine a dim violet light on a certain metal and electrons fly off at once. Shine a very bright red light on it and nothing happens — ever. Brightness is not what matters. What else about light could decide it?

[Picture 21613 — Photons strike — electrons leave the metal.]

Planck proposed that energy comes in small packets called quanta. One packet's energy is E = hν, where h is Planck's constant and ν the frequency.

Einstein applied this to light: light travels as photons, each photon carrying energy E = hν.

In the photoelectric effect, photons knock electrons out of a metal surface.

Only frequency decides whether electrons are ejected and how much energy they carry — brighter light of too-low frequency ejects none.
