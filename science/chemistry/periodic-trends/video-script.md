# Video script — Periodic trends

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Sodium explodes in water; potassium explodes harder; lithium only fizzes fiercely.

Atomic radius shrinks across a period (greater nuclear charge pulls the same shells tighter) and grows down a group (new shells are added).

Ionisation energy — the energy to remove an electron — generally rises across a period and falls down a group.

Electron affinity and electronegativity (an atom's pull on shared electrons, greatest at fluorine) generally rise across a period; metallic character does the opposite.

Every trend is explained by the same two ideas: effective nuclear charge and shielding by inner electrons.

Now you. Say it back: Across a period, what happens to atomic radius?
Answer: It shrinks.
