# Periodic trends

## Do it first
Sodium explodes in water; potassium explodes harder; lithium only fizzes fiercely. Same family, growing violence down the group. Before reading, list what else you think changes in a regular way across a period or down a group.

[Picture 21628 — Trend arrows across a mini table.]

## Sub lesson 1 — Radius — across and down
Atomic radius shrinks across a period (greater nuclear charge pulls the same shells tighter) and grows down a group (new shells are added). Ionic radius follows the charge: cations are smaller than their atoms, anions larger.

[GIF 21629 — atoms shrinking across a period]

## Sub lesson 2 — Ionisation energy, affinity, electronegativity
Ionisation energy — the energy to remove an electron — generally rises across a period and falls down a group.

## Sub lesson 3 — Metallic character and the two explanations
Electron affinity and electronegativity (an atom's pull on shared electrons, greatest at fluorine) generally rise across a period; metallic character does the opposite.

## Sub lesson 4 — Comparing elements from their places
Every trend is explained by the same two ideas: effective nuclear charge and shielding by inner electrons.
