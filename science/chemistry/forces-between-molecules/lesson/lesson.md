# Forces between molecules

## Do it first
Water boils at 100 °C; hydrogen sulfide, a heavier molecule of the same family, is a gas far below zero. Covalent bonds inside both are strong. Before reading, name what else must be holding water's molecules together.

[Picture 21642 — Water molecules linked by hydrogen bonds.]

Between molecules act weaker forces than the bonds inside them. Dipole–dipole forces pull permanently polar molecules together.

Hydrogen bonding is the strong special case: hydrogen attached to N, O or F is attracted to a lone pair on a neighbouring N, O or F.

London dispersion forces arise from temporary, flickering dipoles in all molecules — the only forces in non-polar substances, growing with molecular size.

These forces, ranked hydrogen bonding > dipole–dipole > dispersion (in most comparisons), explain boiling points and solubility of covalent substances.
