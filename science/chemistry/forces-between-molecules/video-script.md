The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Water boils at 100 °C; hydrogen sulfide, a heavier molecule of the same family, is a gas far below zero.

Between molecules act weaker forces than the bonds inside them.

Hydrogen bonding is the strong special case: hydrogen attached to N, O or F is attracted to a lone pair on a neighbouring N, O or F.

London dispersion forces arise from temporary, flickering dipoles in all molecules — the only forces in non-polar substances, growing with molecular size.

These forces, ranked hydrogen bonding > dipole–dipole > dispersion (in most comparisons), explain boiling points and solubility of covalent substances.

Now you. Say it back: When does hydrogen bonding occur?
Answer: H on N, O or F attracted to a lone pair.
