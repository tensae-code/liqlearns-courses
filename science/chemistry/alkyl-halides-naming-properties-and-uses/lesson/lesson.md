# Alkyl halides — naming, properties and uses

## Do it first
Before you read: the plastic of a PVC pipe, an old anaesthetic, and the gases that once cooled fridges are all one chemical family. Say out loud: what single kind of atom do you think joins them — and where on a carbon chain would that atom sit? Then read on: Kuwait's Grade 12 book opens the family through its uses, then names it, sorts it and makes it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The family and its uses

A **halogenated hydrocarbon** carries a halogen in place of a hydrogen. Kuwait opens with the two branches and their uses (§1, printed pp. 65–72): **alkyl halides, R–X**, where X is a halogen on a carbon chain, and **phenyl (aryl) halides**, where the halogen sits on a benzene ring. The uses name the family before the rules do: **vinyl chloride**, made into **PVC pipes**; **chloroform**, the former anaesthetic; **CCl₄**; and the **CFCs**. The family is met first as things the world uses, then as structures to name.

## Sub lesson 2 — Naming both ways — and the three classes

Kuwait teaches **two namings**. The **IUPAC** way numbers the **longest chain** so the halogen's position is fixed, counts identical halogens with **di-, tri-, tetra-** prefixes, and lists different halogens in **alphabetical order** (after a table of the alkyl-group names the names are built from). The **common** way names the compound as an **alkyl halide** — the alkyl group plus the halide name (the book's Table 13). And the family is **classified by the carbon holding the halogen**: that carbon attached to one other carbon makes the halide **primary**, to two **secondary**, to three **tertiary** (Table 14).

## Sub lesson 3 — Making them — and how they behave as substances

**Preparation** in the book runs by **direct halogenation**: alkanes halogenated **under UV light**, and benzene halogenated **with a catalyst**. The **physical properties** mark the family off from the alkanes it comes from: alkyl halides are **nearly insoluble in water**; their **boiling points stand above the alkanes'** and **rise with the halogen's mass and with chain length**; and the **bromine and iodine compounds are denser than water** (Tables 15–16).

## Sub lesson 4 — Substitution reactions — halides as preparations

The family's working reactions are **substitutions** — the halogen swapped for another group — and Kuwait teaches them as **preparations**: with **aqueous bases**, an alkyl halide gives an **alcohol**; with **alkoxides**, it gives an **ether**; with **sodium amide**, it gives an **amine**. One family, three doors: the halide is the starting material from which three other families are made.

## The key ideas

- **Alkyl halides are R–X**; **aryl (phenyl) halides** carry the halogen on a ring. The uses open the family: **vinyl chloride → PVC**, **chloroform**, **CCl₄**, the **CFCs**.
- **Naming both ways**: IUPAC (longest chain numbered, di-/tri-/tetra- for identical halogens, alphabetical for different ones) and common alkyl-halide names; the class — **primary, secondary, tertiary** — is fixed by the carbon holding the halogen.
- **Preparation**: direct halogenation of alkanes **under UV**, of benzene **with a catalyst**. **Properties**: nearly insoluble in water, boiling points above the alkanes' and rising with halogen mass and chain length, Br/I compounds denser than water.
- **Substitution as preparation**: aqueous base → **alcohol**, alkoxide → **ether**, sodium amide → **amine**.
