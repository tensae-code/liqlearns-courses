# Lesson plan — Alkyl halides — naming, properties and uses

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Alkyl halides — naming, properties and uses
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)

## Sub lessons
1. **The family and its uses** — A **halogenated hydrocarbon** carries a halogen in place of a hydrogen. Kuwait opens with the two branches and their uses (§1, printed pp. 65–72): **alkyl halides, R–X**, where X is a halogen on a carbon chain, and **phenyl (aryl) halides**, where the halogen sits on a benzene ring. The uses name the family before the rules do: **vinyl chloride**, made into **PVC pipes**; **chloroform**, the former anaesthetic; **CCl₄**; and the **CFCs**. The family is met first as things the world uses, then as structures to name.
2. **Naming both ways — and the three classes** — Kuwait teaches **two namings**. The **IUPAC** way numbers the **longest chain** so the halogen's position is fixed, counts identical halogens with **di-, tri-, tetra-** prefixes, and lists different halogens in **alphabetical order** (after a table of the alkyl-group names the names are built from). The **common** way names the compound as an **alkyl halide** — the alkyl group plus the halide name (the book's Table 13). And the family is **classified by the carbon holding the halogen**: that carbon attached to one other carbon makes the halide **primary**, to two **secondary**, to three **tertiary** (Table 14).
3. **Making them — and how they behave as substances** — **Preparation** in the book runs by **direct halogenation**: alkanes halogenated **under UV light**, and benzene halogenated **with a catalyst**. The **physical properties** mark the family off from the alkanes it comes from: alkyl halides are **nearly insoluble in water**; their **boiling points stand above the alkanes'** and **rise with the halogen's mass and with chain length**; and the **bromine and iodine compounds are denser than water** (Tables 15–16).
4. **Substitution reactions — halides as preparations** — The family's working reactions are **substitutions** — the halogen swapped for another group — and Kuwait teaches them as **preparations**: with **aqueous bases**, an alkyl halide gives an **alcohol**; with **alkoxides**, it gives an **ether**; with **sodium amide**, it gives an **amine**. One family, three doors: the halide is the starting material from which three other families are made.
