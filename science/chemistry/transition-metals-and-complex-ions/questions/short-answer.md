# Transition metals and complex ions — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the four family behaviours the partly filled d subshell gives the transition metals.
**Answer: Variable oxidation states, coloured ions and compounds, magnetic behaviour from unpaired d electrons, and catalytic activity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does iron form both Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ while sodium forms only Na⁺?
**Answer: Iron's s and d electrons sit at close energies, so different numbers can be removed at similar cost; sodium has one outer electron over a noble-gas core, and removing a second would break that stable core.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Take [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ apart: centre, ligand, coordination number, charge.
**Answer: The centre is Fe³⁺; the ligand is water; six water molecules bind, so the coordination number is 6; the ligands are neutral, so the complex carries the centre's charge, 3+.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Copper sulfate solution turns a much deeper blue when ammonia is added. Explain, using ligands and Δ.
**Answer: Ammonia replaces water as the ligand on copper(II). Ammonia is a stronger-field ligand, so Δ grows, a different (higher-energy) colour is absorbed, and the complementary colour seen deepens to dark blue.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What decides whether a complex is high-spin or low-spin?
**Answer: The size of the splitting Δ against the cost of pairing: weak-field ligands give a small Δ, electrons spread unpaired (high-spin); strong-field ligands give a large Δ, electrons pair in the lower set (low-spin).**
