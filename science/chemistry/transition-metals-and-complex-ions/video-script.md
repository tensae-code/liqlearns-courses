Today: **Transition metals and complex ions** — the United States' way: OpenStax gives the d block a chapter of its own.

Iron, copper, nickel, gold — the transition metals live in the middle of the table, the d block. Their secret is an unfinished inner shell: the d subshell, still partly filled even in their ions. That one fact gives them changing charges — iron two-plus and three-plus — bright colours, magnetism, and the catalyst's gift: iron speeds the Haber process and is not used up.
Around these metals, other molecules gather. A ligand brings a lone pair and locks onto the metal centre. Four ammonias on copper make a complex ion, deep blue in solution. And the colour itself? The ligands split the metal's d orbitals into two levels. The electron drinks in one colour of light to jump the gap — and your eye sees the colour that is left over.
