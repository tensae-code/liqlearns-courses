# Hands-on — Transition metals and complex ions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## See a ligand change the colour (teacher-led, laboratory only)
With a teacher present and the laboratory's safety kit on: to a pale-blue copper sulfate solution in a test tube, add dilute ammonia drop by drop. The pale blue first clouds, then clears into a deep blue — the water ligands on copper are being replaced by ammonia ligands, Δ changes, and the colour deepens with it. Name the complex ion in the final solution and the coordination number of its centre. No part of this is done at home.

## Take a formula apart on paper
Write [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ and [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ large on a page. Under each, label: the central ion and its oxidation state, the ligands, the coordination number, and where the overall charge comes from. Then swap one ligand set for the other on paper and say what you would expect to happen to the colour — and why.

## Sort the catalysts
On cards, write: iron (Haber process), nickel (hydrogenation of oils), platinum (catalytic converter), sodium, calcium. Sort them into transition-metal catalysts and not, and for the three catalysts say in one sentence what the partly filled d subshell lets the metal do.
