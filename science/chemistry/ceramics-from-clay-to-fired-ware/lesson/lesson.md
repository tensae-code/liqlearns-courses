# Ceramics — from clay to fired ware

## Do it first
Wet clay slumps; fired clay rings and holds water for years. Before reading, list the journey's stages in order.

[Picture 21761 — Pottery stages, clay to kiln.]

Ceramics begin with clay preparation — cleaning, kneading and mixing to a workable body.

The clay is shaped (thrown, moulded or pressed), then dried to remove water before firing.

Firing in a kiln at high temperature hardens the ware permanently; glazing may finish it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain Ceramics — from clay to fired ware to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
