## Sub lesson 1 — Inspection — trial and adjustment

Three named balancing methods are taught in this lesson: the inspection method, the least common multiple (LCM) method, and the algebraic method. The inspection method comes first: you adjust the coefficients by trial — change one coefficient, recount the atoms on both sides, and keep adjusting until the atom counts match. It is the method to reach for first on a straightforward equation.

## Sub lesson 2 — The LCM method

The least common multiple (LCM) method balances by number: where the same element appears in different counts on the two sides, you use the least common multiple of those counts to choose coefficients that make the counts meet. It gives inspection a system when trial and adjustment alone keeps missing.

## Sub lesson 3 — The algebraic method

The algebraic method is the surest of the three: you assign an unknown coefficient to each substance, write one equation for each element saying its atoms balance, and solve the unknowns. The solved values become the coefficients of the balanced equation. Use it when inspection and LCM leave an equation stubborn.
