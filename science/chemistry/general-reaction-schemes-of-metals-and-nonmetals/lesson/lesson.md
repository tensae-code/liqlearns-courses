# General reaction schemes of metals and nonmetals

## Do it first
A good map replaces a hundred memorised roads. Before reading, say out loud: could one chart hold nearly every reaction of every metal you have met? Bulgaria's Grade 10 summary lesson tries exactly that — a scheme-chart for metals, mirrored by one for nonmetals.

## Sub lesson 1 — The metal scheme

A metal's general scheme, as Bulgaria charts it: **+ O₂ → basic oxide; + nonmetal → salt; + H₂O → hydroxide + H₂; + acid → salt + H₂; + base → salt + H₂ — for the amphoteric few only.** Each arrow is a family habit, not one element's trick: learn the chart and a new metal's behaviour is predicted before it is met.

## Sub lesson 2 — The nonmetal mirror — and oxidation states

The nonmetal's scheme mirrors it: **+ H₂ → hydrogen compound; + O₂ → oxide; + metal → salt; + H₂O → acid; + base → salt + H₂O.** Beside the charts sit the **characteristic oxidation states** (S: −2, +4, +6; P: −3, +3, +5), and the halogens are compared by displacement: **chlorine water displaces yellow bromine from KBr and brown iodine from KI** — the oxidising-power order read off the colours.

## The key ideas

- Metal scheme: +O₂→basic oxide; +nonmetal→salt; +H₂O→hydroxide+H₂; +acid→salt+H₂; +base→salt+H₂ (amphoteric few).
- Nonmetal scheme: +H₂→hydrogen compound; +O₂→oxide; +metal→salt; +H₂O→acid; +base→salt+H₂O.
- Oxidation states tabulated (S −2/+4/+6; P −3/+3/+5); chlorine displaces bromine and iodine — the halogen oxidising order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion: the metal scheme; the nonmetal mirror — and oxidation states — in that order, in your own words, with the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you one question about it, and answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Use the metal scheme to predict zinc with dilute acid, and with oxygen. (Acid arrow: salt + H₂ (ZnCl₂ + H₂ with HCl). Oxygen arrow: the basic oxide ZnO.)
2. Why is the '+ base' arrow marked 'for the amphoteric few'? (Most metals do not react with bases; only amphoteric metals such as aluminium and zinc take that arrow, giving salt + H₂.)
