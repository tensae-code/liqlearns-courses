# Lesson plan — General reaction schemes of metals and nonmetals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** General reaction schemes of metals and nonmetals
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Each family's whole chemistry on one scheme-chart: what a metal does with oxygen, nonmetals, water, acids and bases — mirrored by what a nonmetal does (Bulgaria, Grade 10 — §39, printed p. 103)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Classifying substances — the inorganic and organic family trees is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The metal scheme** — A metal's general scheme, as Bulgaria charts it: **+ O₂ → basic oxide; + nonmetal → salt; + H₂O → hydroxide + H₂; + acid → salt + H₂; + base → salt + H₂ — for the amphoteric few only.** Each arrow is a family habit, not one element's trick: learn the chart and a new metal's behaviour is predicted before it is met.
2. **The nonmetal mirror — and oxidation states** — The nonmetal's scheme mirrors it: **+ H₂ → hydrogen compound; + O₂ → oxide; + metal → salt; + H₂O → acid; + base → salt + H₂O.** Beside the charts sit the **characteristic oxidation states** (S: −2, +4, +6; P: −3, +3, +5), and the halogens are compared by displacement: **chlorine water displaces yellow bromine from KBr and brown iodine from KI** — the oxidising-power order read off the colours.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Memorises single reactions instead of the chart → cover the examples and rebuild the chart from the two questions: what does a metal give electrons to, and what does a nonmetal take them from?
