# Biological role of sodium, potassium, magnesium and calcium — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State magnesium's two biological posts named in the lesson.
**Answer: Centre of the chlorophyll molecule — the green pigment essential for photosynthesis — and, with calcium, catalyst of a number of enzymatic reactions, the ion holding and steadying the reacting partners.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is bone described as both structure and bank?
**Answer: Its calcium phosphate is the hard mineral that makes the skeleton rigid — structure. But the body also draws calcium from it to keep the blood's Ca²⁺ steady — a store that can be spent — which is the bank.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How does a metal ion help an enzyme, in the lesson's account?
**Answer: The small, highly charged ion holds the reacting molecules in position or steadies the charges of the step, so a reaction that would crawl instead runs at body temperature at living speed — many enzymes only work with their ion in place.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Describe the nerve impulse in the lesson's sodium–potassium terms.
**Answer: The impulse is a controlled crossing of the nerve membrane: Na⁺ rushes in and K⁺ moves out in a wave along the fibre. The standing division — Na⁺ chiefly outside, K⁺ chiefly inside — is what the wave spends and then restores.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Give the section's full calcium file: three jobs and one warning.
**Answer: Jobs: stored in bones as phosphate (structure and reservoir); catalysing enzymatic reactions (with Mg²⁺); triggering muscle contraction. Warning: Ca²⁺ deficiency in children causes malnutrition.**
