# Biological role of sodium, potassium, magnesium and calcium

## Do it first
Before you read: a leaf is green around one metal atom; your bones are hard with another; your muscles contract on a flood of the second, and your nerves fire on two more. Say out loud: how many metals can you name that your own body is using this minute, and at what jobs? Then read on: Bhutan's Class XI book closes its Group-2 chapter (§5.6) with exactly that staff list — sodium, potassium, magnesium and calcium at work in living bodies.

## Sub lesson 1 — Magnesium — the green centre, and calcium the builder

Bhutan's §5.6 opens with the ion a whole kingdom runs on: **Mg²⁺ stands at the centre of chlorophyll**, the green pigment essential for **photosynthesis** — take the magnesium out and the leaf's light-harvest has no heart; the plant's green is, atom for atom, a magnesium colour. **Calcium** the chapter presents first as the body's building store: calcium is present **in bones (and teeth) as phosphate** — the mineral that makes the skeleton hard — a store the body also draws on, which is why the section's closing warning belongs here: **Ca²⁺ deficiency in children causes malnutrition**, the growing skeleton and body starved of an ion they cannot substitute. Builder and reservoir in one element: bone is both structure and bank.

## Sub lesson 2 — The catalysts — Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ in enzymatic reactions

The section's second named job is quieter: **Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ ions catalyse a number of enzymatic reactions**. Enzymes are the body's own catalysts, and many of them only work with a metal ion in place — the ion holds the reacting molecules in position, or steadies the charges of the step, so the reaction runs at body temperature at living speed. The chemistry is the transition lesson's cousin read in biology: a small, highly charged ion is a powerful local organiser, and life spends exactly that power. Magnesium's double life — chlorophyll's centre in the leaf, enzyme partner in leaf and animal alike — is why the chapter gives the two group-2 ions a section of their own at the chapter's end.

## Sub lesson 3 — Calcium the signal, and sodium and potassium — fluids and nerves

Calcium's third job is motion: **Ca²⁺ ions play their role in muscle contraction** — the flood of Ca²⁺ into a muscle cell is the trigger its fibres answer by shortening, and its withdrawal lets them relax; the same ion that builds the bone moves the limb the bone carries. Around these two, the section sets **sodium and potassium**, the alkali pair whose ions run the body's waters and wires: Na⁺ chiefly in the fluids **outside** cells, K⁺ chiefly **inside** them, their balance holding the body's fluid volumes and its acidity steady, and their controlled crossing of nerve and muscle membranes carrying the **nerve impulse** itself — a wave of Na⁺ in and K⁺ out. Four ions, four kinds of post: pigment centre, building store, catalyst and trigger, fluid and signal — the chapter's closing argument that the s-block metals are not abstractions but staff.

## The key ideas

- **Mg²⁺ is at the centre of chlorophyll**, the green pigment essential for photosynthesis.
- **Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ catalyse a number of enzymatic reactions** — the ion holds and steadies the reacting partners.
- **Ca²⁺ triggers muscle contraction**, and calcium is stored **in bones as phosphate** — structure and reservoir together.
- **Ca²⁺ deficiency in children causes malnutrition** — the chapter's closing warning (Bhutan, Class XI, §5.6).
- **Na⁺ (outside cells) and K⁺ (inside cells)** hold the body's fluid balance and carry the nerve impulse across membranes.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why does a magnesium-deficient plant yellow, in the lesson's terms? (Chlorophyll's centre is a Mg²⁺ ion; without magnesium the plant cannot build its full stock of the green pigment, so the leaves pale — the light-harvesting heart of photosynthesis is missing its atom.)
2. One ion, three jobs: name calcium's three posts in the section and the fact that unites them. (Building store (bones as phosphate), enzymatic catalysis (with Mg²⁺), and trigger of muscle contraction. The uniting fact is Ca²⁺ itself — a small doubly charged ion that both precipitates as a hard mineral and organises reactions and signals in solution.)
3. A child's diet is short of calcium for years. State the chapter's consequence and where the body takes calcium from meanwhile. (The chapter names it directly: Ca²⁺ deficiency in children causes malnutrition. Meanwhile the body draws on its reservoir — the calcium phosphate of the bones — weakening the very structure the store was built to be.)
