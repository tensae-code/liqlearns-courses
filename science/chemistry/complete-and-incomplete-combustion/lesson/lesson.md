# Complete and incomplete combustion

## Do it first
Before you read: a Bunsen flame can burn blue and clean, or yellow and sooty, on the same gas. Say out loud: what single thing could you change to switch it from one to the other? Then read on: Syria's Grade 8 book runs exactly that test, on printed p. 26.

## Sub lesson 1 — Combustion — reaction with oxygen, tested on one flame

**Combustion is reaction with oxygen at a suitable temperature**: a fuel joins with oxygen, giving out heat and light. Syria tests the idea on a **Bunsen flame**, changing one thing only — the **air hole, open versus closed** — so the oxygen supply is the only difference between the two runs.

Same burner, same gas, two flames: whatever differs between them is the oxygen's doing. That is the experiment's whole design.

## Sub lesson 2 — Complete combustion — plentiful oxygen

With the air hole **open**, oxygen is **plentiful** and the combustion is **complete**: a **blue flame**, and the fuel's carbon and hydrogen burn fully — to **carbon dioxide and water**, nothing left half-burned.

Complete is a claim about the products as much as the flame: every carbon atom reaches carbon dioxide. The clean blue flame is the visible sign of that completeness.

## Sub lesson 3 — Incomplete combustion — oxygen short, and carbon monoxide

With the air hole **closed**, oxygen runs **short** and the combustion is **incomplete**: a **yellow, sooty flame** that **blackens a porcelain dish** held in it — unburned carbon (soot) escaping — and, more dangerously, **carbon monoxide** formed instead of carbon dioxide.

The book's enrichment box (printed p. 27) names the danger plainly: carbon monoxide **silently poisons the blood** — colourless and odourless, it is taken up by the blood where oxygen should ride. Incomplete combustion is not merely dirtier burning; it makes a poison.

## The key ideas

- **Combustion is reaction with oxygen at a suitable temperature**, tested on a Bunsen flame with the air hole open vs closed (U1 L4, printed p. 26).
- **Complete**: plentiful oxygen — **blue flame**, products **carbon dioxide + water**.
- **Incomplete**: oxygen short — **yellow sooty flame** blackening a porcelain dish, and **carbon monoxide**, whose silent poisoning of the blood the enrichment box (printed p. 27) warns of.
