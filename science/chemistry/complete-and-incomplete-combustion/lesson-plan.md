# Lesson plan — Complete and incomplete combustion

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Complete and incomplete combustion
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** combustion split into its two kinds the Syrian way, on one Bunsen flame: complete combustion with plentiful oxygen (blue flame, carbon dioxide + water) against incomplete combustion with oxygen short (yellow sooty flame, carbon monoxide) (Syria, Grade 8 — *الفيزياء والكيمياء للصف الثامن الأساسي (Physics & Chemistry, Grade 8 Basic)*, U1 L4, printed p. 26)

## Sub lessons
1. **Combustion — reaction with oxygen, tested on one flame** — **Combustion is reaction with oxygen at a suitable temperature**: a fuel joins with oxygen, giving out heat and light. Syria tests the idea on a **Bunsen flame**, changing one thing only — the **air hole, open versus closed** — so the oxygen supply is the only difference between the two runs.
2. **Complete combustion — plentiful oxygen** — With the air hole **open**, oxygen is **plentiful** and the combustion is **complete**: a **blue flame**, and the fuel's carbon and hydrogen burn fully — to **carbon dioxide and water**, nothing left half-burned.
3. **Incomplete combustion — oxygen short, and carbon monoxide** — With the air hole **closed**, oxygen runs **short** and the combustion is **incomplete**: a **yellow, sooty flame** that **blackens a porcelain dish** held in it — unburned carbon (soot) escaping — and, more dangerously, **carbon monoxide** formed instead of carbon dioxide.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the yellow flame is 'stronger' because it is brighter to look at → brightness here is glowing soot — unburned carbon; the blue flame is the complete burn, the yellow the starved one.
- Blames the fuel for the difference → the experiment changes ONE thing — the air hole, hence the oxygen; same fuel, two combustions.
- Treats soot as the only problem of incomplete combustion → soot is the visible problem; carbon monoxide — invisible, odourless, blood-poisoning — is the dangerous one.
