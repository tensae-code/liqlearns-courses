# Testing purity by melting and boiling point — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What makes a melting point a test of purity at all?
**Answer: A pure solid melts at one sharp, fixed temperature, so any sample can be judged against that point: melt sharply at the known value and it is pure; melt smeared or at the wrong value and it is not.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A sample of a solid whose pure melting point is 122 °C melts between 116 °C and 120 °C. Give the verdict and both reasons.
**Answer: The sample is impure: its melting point is lower than the pure value and the melting is spread over a range instead of sharp — the two signs of an impurity.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the chromatogram check back up the melting-point test?
**Answer: A pure substance gives a single spot on a chromatogram; extra spots show extra substances present — the same verdict the moved melting point gives, by an independent test.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must the heating in this test be slow?
**Answer: So the temperature at which melting or boiling actually happens can be read truly — fast heating overshoots and hides whether the point was sharp or spread, which is the whole evidence.**
