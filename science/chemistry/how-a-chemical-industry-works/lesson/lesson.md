# How a chemical industry works

## Do it first
A plant is sited neither beside the mine nor the market by accident. Before reading, list what a manufacturing industry needs before its first product leaves the gate.

[Picture 21750 — A plant flow in general form.]

A manufacturing industry is characterised by its raw materials, its process (the chemistry run continuously), and its products and by-products.

Siting weighs raw-material supply, energy, water, transport and markets.

Inputs, process and products in general terms are the frame every later process lesson fills in.

## Say it to your companion
Explain How a chemical industry works to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
