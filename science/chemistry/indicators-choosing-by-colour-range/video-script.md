# Video script — Indicators — choosing by colour range

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An indicator is itself a weak acid that changes colour as it loses its proton.

An acid–base indicator is a weak acid or base whose two forms (ionised and un-ionised) have different colours.

It changes colour over a characteristic pH range — roughly two pH units — not at one sharp point.

Choose the indicator whose range falls inside the titration's steep pH jump, so the colour change (the end point) lands at the equivalence point.

Now you. Say it back: What is an indicator, chemically?
Answer: A weak acid/base with two coloured forms.
