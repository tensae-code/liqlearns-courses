# Indicators — choosing by colour range

## Do it first
An indicator is itself a weak acid that changes colour as it loses its proton. Before reading, say why one indicator cannot serve every titration.

[Picture 21726 — One indicator in three colours.]

An acid–base indicator is a weak acid or base whose two forms (ionised and un-ionised) have different colours.

It changes colour over a characteristic pH range — roughly two pH units — not at one sharp point.

Choose the indicator whose range falls inside the titration's steep pH jump, so the colour change (the end point) lands at the equivalence point.
