# Lesson plan — Testing for water — anhydrous copper sulfate

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Testing for water — anhydrous copper sulfate
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the recognition test for water itself: white anhydrous copper sulfate turns blue when water is present — the test the Djibouti Grade 6 guide uses on foods and liquids, and the test acetone fails (teacher-guide evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Water in our environment is recommended first, never forced.
**Evidence standing:** this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Djibouti) — its sources name the guide and count it nowhere (acceptance 0).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The test — white powder, blue result** — The test chemical is **copper sulfate** in its **anhydrous** form — copper sulfate with no water in it.
2. **Using the test — foods, liquids, and the one that fails** — The guide works the test in its exercises (Exercices 5, 10, 13 and 22 of the chapter): pupils put the white powder to **foods and liquids** and read the result.
3. **Reading the test honestly** — The test answers one question only — **is water present?** It does not say how much water there is, and it does not say whether a liquid is pure water or a mixture containing water: both turn the powder blue.
