Today: **Testing for water — anhydrous copper sulfate**.

How do you prove water is there when you cannot see it? Djibouti's book teaches one test. Take copper sulfate with no water in it — a white powder. Touch it to a food or a liquid. If water is there, the powder drinks the water in and turns blue. If there is no water — like with acetone — the powder stays white. Blue means: water is present. White means: no water shown. One colour change, one clear answer. But remember: the test says water is there — it does not say how much, and it does not say the liquid is pure water.
