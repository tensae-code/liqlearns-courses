# Testing for water — anhydrous copper sulfate — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the recognition test for water as the guide teaches it.
**Answer: Put white anhydrous copper sulfate in contact with the substance. If it turns blue, water is present — the powder has taken up water and become blue (hydrated) copper sulfate.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do the exercises include acetone, a liquid with no water in it?
**Answer: As the negative case: acetone leaves the powder white, proving the test is specific — it responds to water, not to every liquid. Without a negative case, the blue result would prove nothing.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A clear liquid turns the powder blue. A pupil says 'this proves it is pure water.' Correct the pupil.
**Answer: The test only shows water is present. A mixture containing water turns the powder blue just as pure water does, so purity is not proved.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) What kind of book does this lesson stand on?
**Answer: The Djibouti Grade 6 teacher guide only — teacher-guide evidence, counted nowhere (acceptance 0); no Djibouti pupil book is held.**
