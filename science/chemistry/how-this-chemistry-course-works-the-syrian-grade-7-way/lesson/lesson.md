# How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 7 way

## Do it first
Before you read: every textbook teaches in its own way, and knowing the way helps you learn from it. Say out loud: when you open a new lesson in a book, what do you look at first — the title, the pictures, the questions? Then read on: Syria's Grade 7 book states its own way openly in its preface, on printed p. 3 — this lesson teaches that way as content.

## Sub lesson 1 — A research-and-experiment curriculum, tied to daily life

Syria's Grade 7 preface (printed p. 3) describes a **research-and-experiment curriculum**: chemistry is learned by investigating and by doing experiments, not by memorising lines. The questions start from what the learner can try, observe and test.

The same preface ties the curriculum to **daily life and the local environment**: the examples, materials and problems are drawn from the world around the learner, so each chemistry idea is met where it already lives — in the kitchen, the field, the street and the home.

## Sub lesson 2 — Every lesson opens with objectives and key words

Each Chemistry lesson in the book opens the same way: with its **printed objectives** — what the learner will be able to do by the end — and its **key words**, the lesson's own vocabulary named before the teaching starts.

That opening is a contract: the objectives say where the lesson is going, and the key words are the words the learner must own when it ends. A learner who reads the opening first knows what to watch for in everything that follows.

## Sub lesson 3 — “I try and I conclude” — the lesson's engine

The body of each lesson is built around bench experiments headed **“I try and I conclude”**: the learner tries the experiment first, watches what happens, and only then meets the conclusion — stated in an explicit **“I conclude” box**, so the finding is separated from the trying and can be checked against it.

Worked applications, enrichment boxes, unit questions and a closing hands-on project complete the shape: the idea is tried, concluded, applied, stretched, tested and finally used in a project of the learner's own. The Chemistry lessons of this book — the atom, and elements and compounds — all run in exactly this shape.

## The key ideas

- Syria's Grade 7 book states its own design in its preface (printed p. 3): a **research-and-experiment curriculum** tied to **daily life and the local environment**.
- Every Chemistry lesson opens with **printed objectives and key words**.
- The lesson's engine is **“I try and I conclude”** bench experiments with an explicit **“I conclude” box**, followed by worked applications, enrichment boxes, unit questions and a hands-on project.
