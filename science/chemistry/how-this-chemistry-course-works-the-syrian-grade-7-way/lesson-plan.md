# Lesson plan — How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 7 way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** How this chemistry course works — the Syrian Grade 7 way
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Syria's Grade 7 chemistry course-shape taught as content in its own right — the way the combined Physics & Chemistry book itself says its Chemistry lessons work: a research-and-experiment curriculum tied to daily life and the local environment, every lesson opening with printed objectives and key words and built around “I try and I conclude” bench experiments (Syria, Grade 7 — *الفيزياء والكيمياء للصف السابع الأساسي (Physics & Chemistry, Grade 7 Basic)*, preface, printed p. 3)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **A research-and-experiment curriculum, tied to daily life** — Syria's Grade 7 preface (printed p. 3) describes a **research-and-experiment curriculum**: chemistry is learned by investigating and by doing experiments, not by memorising lines. The questions start from what the learner can try, observe and test.
2. **Every lesson opens with objectives and key words** — Each Chemistry lesson in the book opens the same way: with its **printed objectives** — what the learner will be able to do by the end — and its **key words**, the lesson's own vocabulary named before the teaching starts.
3. **“I try and I conclude” — the lesson's engine** — The body of each lesson is built around bench experiments headed **“I try and I conclude”**: the learner tries the experiment first, watches what happens, and only then meets the conclusion — stated in an explicit **“I conclude” box**, so the finding is separated from the trying and can be checked against it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the preface is not lesson content → in Syria's Grade 7 book it is: the preface at printed p. 3 states the curriculum's design, and that design is what this lesson teaches.
- Mixes objectives and key words up → objectives say what the learner will be able to DO; key words are the lesson's vocabulary — the words, not the deeds.
- Reads the conclusion before trying → the book's order is the point: try first, conclude after — the “I conclude” box is checked against what the trying showed.
