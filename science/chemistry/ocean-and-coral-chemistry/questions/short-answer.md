# Ocean and coral chemistry — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the four forms that seawater's dissolved carbon takes, in chain order.
**Answer: Dissolved carbon dioxide (CO₂), carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), the hydrogen carbonate ion (HCO₃⁻) and the carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻) — one stock of carbon in equilibrium, shared according to the water's acidity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does acidity control the carbonate supply?
**Answer: Carbonate ions pick up hydrogen ions to become hydrogen carbonate, so the more acidic the water — the more hydrogen ions it carries — the more of the carbon stock is held as hydrogen carbonate and the less remains as carbonate.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How does a reef, kilometres long, come from animals no bigger than a thumbnail?
**Answer: Each polyp precipitates calcium carbonate from seawater ions as its skeleton; the skeletons stay when the polyps die, new polyps build on the old skeletons, and generation on generation the deposits accumulate into the reef.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Trace what extra carbon dioxide in the air does to a coral reef, step by step.
**Answer: The ocean absorbs the extra CO₂; it forms more carbonic acid, releasing more hydrogen ions, so the pH falls; the hydrogen ions convert carbonate ions to hydrogen carbonate, so coral builds more slowly for lack of carbonate, and existing calcium carbonate can dissolve back.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is a reef described in the lesson as a balance, not a finished monument?
**Answer: Because precipitation by the polyps and dissolution by the water run against each other every day, and which one wins is set by the chemistry of the seawater — change the water's acidity and the balance moves.**
