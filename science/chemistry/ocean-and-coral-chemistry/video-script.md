Today: **Ocean and coral chemistry** — Samoa's way: the reef taught as a storyline inside chemistry.

The sea holds carbon dioxide dissolved in its water — and the gas keeps changing its clothes: now carbonic acid, now hydrogen carbonate, now carbonate. How much wears each form? The water's acidity decides. More acid, less carbonate.
Now meet the builder: the coral polyp, a tiny animal with a chemical trick. It pulls calcium and carbonate out of the water and lays them down as stone — calcium carbonate — its own skeleton. Polyps on polyps, ages on ages, and the skeletons stack into a reef.
But the balance can tip. Extra carbon dioxide from the air dissolves in, the water grows more acidic, carbonate runs short — building slows, and the old stone itself can start to dissolve. A reef is an equilibrium you can stand on.
