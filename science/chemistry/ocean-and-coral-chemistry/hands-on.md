# Hands-on — Ocean and coral chemistry

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Trace one carbon atom (paper only)
Draw the journey of one carbon atom: start in the air as CO₂, dissolve into seawater, and draw each form it can take in the chain — carbonic acid, hydrogen carbonate, carbonate — with the equilibrium arrows between them. Finish the journey inside a coral skeleton as CaCO₃. Beside each arrow, write what pushes the atom that way (more acidity, less acidity, a building polyp).

## The lever test (paper only)
Two tanks of seawater stand side by side. Tank A is ordinary seawater; a measured extra dose of carbon dioxide is bubbled through Tank B. For each tank, write down: which way the carbonate chain shifts, what happens to the carbonate-ion supply, and what you would expect for a young coral growing in it and for a dead skeleton lying in it after a year. Check your answers against sub lessons 1 and 3.

## Reef walk (observation only — no collecting)
If you can visit a shore or a reef flat at low tide with a teacher or family: look, do not take. Find living coral and dead coral rock. Say out loud, for each one you find, which side of the lesson's balance it is on — building up or dissolving back — and what in the water decides it. Write three sentences when you return: what you saw, and the equation that explains the living one's growth.
