# Lesson plan — Biogas — from anaerobic digestion

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Biogas — from anaerobic digestion
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** biogas as a renewable alkane source — a sealed anaerobic digester in which bacteria break down manure, crop residues and food waste without air to a gas of about 60% methane and 40% carbon dioxide, which can be upgraded to biomethane for the gas grid or liquefied vehicle fuel, while the leftover digestate goes back to the fields as fertiliser
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Alkanes at work — fuels, and Where hydrocarbons come from — oil, gas, coal, are recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
1. **The anaerobic digester — sealed against air** — The sealed anaerobic digester; bacteria breaking down manure, crop residues and food waste.
2. **The gas itself — 60% methane, and upgrading it** — Biogas ≈ 60% methane / 40% CO₂; upgraded to biomethane for the gas grid or LNG vehicle fuel.
3. **Digestate — the leftover that feeds the fields** — The leftover digestate returned as fertiliser; biogas as a renewable cycle against fossil gas.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads 'anaerobic' as a detail → it is the whole design: air in the tank changes the bacteria's work and the gas; the seal is the lesson.
- Expects biogas to be pure methane → the digester's gas is about 60% methane and 40% carbon dioxide; biomethane is what upgrading makes of it.
- Forgets the second product → the digestate is not waste: it returns to the fields as fertiliser, closing the farm's loop.
