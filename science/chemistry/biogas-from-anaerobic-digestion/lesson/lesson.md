# Biogas — from anaerobic digestion

## Do it first
Before you read: a farm has manure, spoiled crops and kitchen waste piling up — and a fuel bill. Say out loud: could the pile itself pay the bill? Then read on: this is Dominica's CSEC unit B14.2's Caribbean answer — **biogas**, the renewable alkane made by shutting waste away from air and letting bacteria do the chemistry.

## Sub lesson 1 — The anaerobic digester — sealed against air

The apparatus is a sealed tank — the **anaerobic digester** — and its name carries the whole method: **anaerobic** means without air. Manure, crop residues and food waste are loaded in, the tank is sealed, and **bacteria** that work without oxygen break the waste down step by step. Because no air reaches them, their digestion ends not in full combustion but in a burnable gas: the waste's carbon leaves largely as methane, the simplest alkane — the same molecule the alkanes lessons burn, made here from this year's waste instead of ancient seas.

## Sub lesson 2 — The gas itself — 60% methane, and upgrading it

What collects above the digesting waste is **biogas: about 60% methane and 40% carbon dioxide**. Burned as it comes, it serves for cooking, heating and generating on the farm. Refined — the carbon dioxide and impurities removed — it is upgraded to **biomethane**, methane clean enough to feed into the **gas grid** like natural gas, or to be liquefied as **vehicle fuel**. The upgrading step is what turns a farm gas into a grid gas: same methane molecule, brought to the standard the network and the engine demand.

## Sub lesson 3 — Digestate — the leftover that feeds the fields

What stays in the tank when the gas has been drawn off is the **digestate** — the digested remains of the waste, still carrying the nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium the crops put there. Dominica's book returns it to the land **as fertiliser**, and that return is the lesson's closing point: the digester pays twice, once in gas and once in soil, from material that was a disposal problem. A fossil fuel is dug up, burned and gone; biogas is grown, digested, burned and grown again — a renewable alkane source inside the farm's own cycle.

## The key ideas

- **Biogas** is made in a sealed **anaerobic digester**, where bacteria break down manure, crop residues and food waste **without air**.
- The gas is **about 60% methane and 40% carbon dioxide** — a renewable source of the alkane methane.
- Upgraded (CO₂ removed), biogas becomes **biomethane** for the gas grid or liquefied vehicle fuel.
- The leftover **digestate** returns to the fields as fertiliser — the process pays in fuel and in soil from the same waste.
