# Where Bohr's model fails

## Do it first
Bohr's model predicts hydrogen's lines beautifully. Then scientists pointed it at helium — just one more electron — and it failed. Before reading, think: what does a model that works for exactly one atom tell us?

Bohr's model works only for one-electron species — hydrogen and ions with a single electron.

It fails to explain the spectra of multi-electron atoms.

It cannot account for fine structure — lines that split into close pairs under high resolution.

Its fixed orbits conflict with the uncertainty principle. A new model is needed — that need is this lesson's point.
