# Video script — Buffers — holding pH steady

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Blood holds its pH within a hair's width while acids pour in from the muscles.

A buffer is a solution of a weak acid with its salt (its conjugate base), or a weak base with its salt.

Added H⁺ is mopped up by the conjugate base; added OH⁻ is mopped up by the weak acid — the reservoir pair absorbs both attacks.

Buffer pH is calculated with the Henderson–Hasselbalch relation as presented.

Now you. Say it back: What two ingredients make a buffer?
Answer: A weak acid and its salt (conjugate base).
