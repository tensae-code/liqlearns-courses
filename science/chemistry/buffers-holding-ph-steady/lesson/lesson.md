# Buffers — holding pH steady

## Do it first
Blood holds its pH within a hair's width while acids pour in from the muscles. Before reading, guess the recipe: what two ingredients must a solution hold to swallow both added acid and added alkali?

[Picture 21720 — A buffer soaking up H⁺ and OH⁻.]

A buffer is a solution of a weak acid with its salt (its conjugate base), or a weak base with its salt. It resists pH change when small amounts of acid or alkali are added.

Added H⁺ is mopped up by the conjugate base; added OH⁻ is mopped up by the weak acid — the reservoir pair absorbs both attacks.

Buffer pH is calculated with the Henderson–Hasselbalch relation as presented. Buffers run through daily life and blood chemistry.
