# Systems, surroundings and specific heat

## Do it first
Before you read: a gram of iron and a gram of water sit in the same sun, and the iron is hot to touch while the water is barely warm. Say out loud: did the iron take in more heat — or does the same heat do more to it? Then read on: Yemen's Grade 12 book opens thermochemistry by fixing the words every later heat lesson will use.

## Sub lesson 1 — Energy, and the words for where it is

Energy is **one quantity in many forms** — heat, light, electrical, chemical, nuclear, kinetic, radiant — under the **conservation law**: never made or destroyed, only changed. **Chemical energy** is the **potential energy stored in a substance's structure** — the reason a litre of petrol holds more than a litre of water. The working vocabulary is fixed on a flask diagram: the **system**, the **surroundings**, and the **boundary** between them; **adiabatic** (no heat crosses) against **isothermal** (constant temperature); and **heat against temperature** — the flow against the reading.

## Sub lesson 2 — Heat capacity and specific heat — defined bilingually

**Heat Capacity and Specific Heat** are defined (in the book's bilingual style): the specific heat is the heat that raises **one gram** of a substance by **one degree** — the price, in joules, of warming a gram. Every substance names its own price, and the price decides how a given amount of heat shows itself as temperature.

## Sub lesson 3 — The worked copper block — and water's crown

The formula is worked on a **copper block: 95.49 g absorbing 849 J from 25 °C to 48 °C gives 0.387 J/g·°C**. The closing table teaches its one lesson by contrast: **water's specific heat is 4.18 J/g·°C — the highest common value — against iron's 0.449**: a gram of iron warms about **nine times faster** than a gram of water under the same heat. The sun puzzle of the opening is solved: the same heat, a ninefold difference in price.

## The key ideas

- Vocabulary: **system / surroundings / boundary**; **adiabatic** (no heat crosses) vs **isothermal** (constant temperature); **heat** (the flow) vs **temperature** (the reading); **chemical energy** is structure-stored potential energy, and energy is **conserved**.
- **Specific heat**: the heat to raise **1 g by 1 °C** — copper worked at **0.387 J/g·°C** (95.49 g, 849 J, 25→48 °C).
- The table's lesson: **water 4.18 J/g·°C**, the highest common value, against **iron 0.449** — iron warms ~**nine times** faster per gram.

## Do It Yourself
1. A flask of reacting mixture warms the bench it stands on. Name the system, the surroundings and the boundary. (System: the reacting mixture (in the flask). Surroundings: everything else that can trade heat with it — flask, bench, air. Boundary: the flask wall, the surface dividing the two.)
2. Use the book's copper figures to show the specific-heat calculation's shape: what is divided by what? (The heat absorbed (849 J) is divided by mass × temperature rise — 95.49 g × (48 − 25) °C — giving 0.387 J/g·°C: joules per gram per degree.)
3. Two pans, one of iron and one holding the same mass of water, take the same heat. Which is hotter, and about how many times hotter in temperature rise? (The iron: with specific heats 0.449 against water's 4.18 J/g·°C, the same heat buys about nine times the temperature rise in the iron.)
