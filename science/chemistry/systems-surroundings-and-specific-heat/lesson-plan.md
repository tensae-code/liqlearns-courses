# Lesson plan — Systems, surroundings and specific heat

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Systems, surroundings and specific heat
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Thermochemistry's working vocabulary fixed with a flask diagram, as Yemen's Grade 12 book fixes it: energy in its many forms with the conservation law, chemical energy as the potential energy stored in a substance's structure, system, surroundings and the boundary, adiabatic against isothermal, heat against temperature — then heat capacity and specific heat bilingually defined, the formula worked on a copper block (95.49 g absorbing 849 J from 25 °C to 48 °C gives 0.387 J/g·°C), and a specific-heat table whose lesson is water's 4.18 J/g·°C against iron's 0.449
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Exothermic and endothermic — enthalpy change is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
