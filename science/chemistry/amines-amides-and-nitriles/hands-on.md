# Hands-on — Amines, amides and nitriles

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The label hunt (observation only — home cosmetics and cleaners)
Read the ingredient labels of creams and lotions in your own home, as Brazil's chapter sets the student to do. Find a product that names ceramides. Write down the claim the jar makes for them, then say in lesson terms: what family ceramides belong to, and what that family does in the skin itself that the jar is borrowing.

## Three groups on paper (paper exercise)
Draw the three groups — –NH₂, –CONH₂, –C≡N — big, on one page. Under each, write: the family's name, the element all three share, and Brazil's meeting-place for the family (the nerve's messengers; the cosmetics label; the triple bond). Then draw the two arrows out of the nitrile: hydrolysis toward the amide and the acid, reduction toward the amine.

## The impulse restored (paper exercise)
Sketch a nerve membrane before, during and after an impulse as the lesson describes it: Na⁺ and K⁺ unevenly spread, the impulse passing, the differences restored. In three lines, say why the restoring matters — what could not happen next if the differences were left spent.
