# Amines, amides and nitriles — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What single element do amines, amides and nitriles all carry, and what group defines each family?
**Answer: All three carry nitrogen. The amine is defined by –NH₂, the amide by –CONH₂ (a carbonyl carbon joined to nitrogen), and the nitrile by –C≡N (a carbon triple-bonded to nitrogen).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the Na⁺/K⁺ differences across a nerve membrane be restored after an impulse?
**Answer: The impulse spends the concentration differences it runs on; until they are restored, the nerve cannot carry the next impulse — the restoring is what readies the nerve again.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a cosmetics jar print the word ceramides on its label?
**Answer: Because ceramides are the skin's own amides — the sealing lipids of its barrier — so the name promises the buyer the body's own barrier chemistry in the jar.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How do hydrolysis and reduction join the nitriles to the other two families?
**Answer: Hydrolysis walks a nitrile through the amide to the carboxylic acid; reduction takes the same nitrile to the amine — so one nitrogen compound can be carried into each of the other families by named reactions.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What does Brazil close its whole chemistry book with, and why does it belong in this chapter?
**Answer: UV-B radiation and skin cancer — the sun's damage to the very skin whose barrier this chapter's amides, the ceramides, protect: the nitrogen functions end where they work, on the body's surface under the sky.**
