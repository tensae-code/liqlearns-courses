# Carcinogenicity of polynuclear hydrocarbons — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why are polynuclear hydrocarbons hard for the body and the environment to get rid of?
**Answer: Their fused aromatic ring systems are chemically stable and unreactive, and the molecules are fat-soluble rather than water-soluble, so they break down slowly and lodge in tissue instead of being washed out.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the three sources of incomplete combustion NCERT names in §13.6.
**Answer: Tobacco, coal and petroleum.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Walk the three steps from a cigarette's smoke to a cancer, as NCERT gives them.
**Answer: The polynuclear hydrocarbons in the smoke enter the body; inside the body they undergo biochemical reactions; the products of those reactions damage DNA, and the damaged instructions, copied as the cell divides, give rise to cancer.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does §13.6 stand at the end of the hydrocarbons unit rather than in a health book?
**Answer: Because the carcinogens are the hydrocarbons unit's own subject — the largest members of the aromatic family taught in that unit (after benzene, §13.5) — and NCERT closes the unit with its own molecules' health verdict.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is benzo(a)pyrene singled out from the family?
**Answer: It is present in tobacco smoke and coal tar — the commonest exposures — and is among the most potent carcinogens of the family, so it serves as the type case for the whole class.**
