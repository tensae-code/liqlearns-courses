Today: **Carcinogenicity of polynuclear hydrocarbons** — India's way: the short closing section of the Class XI hydrocarbons unit — after the families are taught, NCERT turns to what the fused-ring members do to the human body.

Join benzene rings edge to edge and you get the polynuclear hydrocarbons — flat molecules of fused rings, naphthalene's family grown large. They are not made on purpose. They are born wherever carbon burns without enough air: in tobacco smoke, in coal tar and soot, in the exhaust of petroleum burned incompletely.
Breathed in or swallowed, they do not pass through unchanged. The body's own chemistry works on them, and the products of those reactions attack the DNA — the cell's instruction set. Damaged instructions, copied at every cell division, are how a cancer starts. That is why this family carries the name carcinogenic, and why benzo(a)pyrene, found in tobacco smoke and coal tar, is named in the book as its warning example.
