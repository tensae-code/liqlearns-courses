# Lesson plan — Carcinogenicity of polynuclear hydrocarbons

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Carcinogenicity of polynuclear hydrocarbons
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** NCERT's own health close to its Class XI hydrocarbons unit (§13.6): the polynuclear (polycyclic) hydrocarbons — benzene rings fused together into one flat molecule — are formed whenever organic matter burns incompletely, enter the body with smoke and tar, and there undergo biochemical reactions that damage DNA and cause cancer; benzo(a)pyrene of tobacco smoke and coal tar is the type case

## Sub lessons
1. **Fused rings — what polynuclear hydrocarbons are** — The polynuclear hydrocarbons are benzene rings fused edge to edge into one flat, stable, stubborn molecule — naphthalene's family grown large, through anthracene to the four- and five-ring members NCERT names: benzo(a)pyrene, benzo(e)pyrene, benzo(b)- and benzo(k)fluoranthene, dibenzo(a,h)anthracene. They are not manufactured; they arrive as by-products.
2. **Born in smoke — incomplete combustion** — They are formed on the incomplete combustion of organic matter — NCERT names tobacco, coal and petroleum — wherever burning is cut short: the cigarette's smoulder, coal tar and soot, exhaust, charred food. They enter the body on smoke's own routes, breathed in and swallowed, and being fat-soluble they lodge in tissue.
3. **From breath to DNA — how they cause cancer** — The damage is done in three steps: the hydrocarbon enters the body chemically quiet; the body's own biochemistry, working on the foreign molecule, converts it into reactive products; and those products damage DNA, whose damaged instructions are then copied at every division — cancer. Benzo(a)pyrene, in tobacco smoke and coal tar, is the family's type case.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Treats every hydrocarbon as a carcinogen → return to the structure: the danger named in §13.6 belongs to the fused-ring polynuclear family formed in incomplete combustion, not to the alkanes, alkenes and alkynes of the unit's earlier sections.
