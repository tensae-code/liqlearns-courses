# Hands-on — Carcinogenicity of polynuclear hydrocarbons

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Source hunt at home (kitchen materials only)
With an adult, list every place in and around the home where organic material burns without a plentiful air supply — a smoking pan, a charcoal fire, a candle burning sooty, an exhaust pipe. For each, record the visible sign of incomplete combustion (smoke, soot, tar smell). Say, from sub lesson 2, why that sign is also the sign that polynuclear hydrocarbons are being formed there.

## Two combustions compared (paper only)
Write the two equations for methane burning: completely (plentiful oxygen) and incompletely (limited oxygen, giving carbon/soot among the products). Under each, write where the carbon ends up. Then say why §13.6's carcinogens belong to the second fire and not the first.

## Label reading (kitchen materials only)
With an adult, read the health warnings on any tobacco product packaging present in the home or shown in a shop window (do not buy or handle the product itself). Record the warning's wording about cancer. Say which molecule family from this lesson the warning is, in the end, about.
