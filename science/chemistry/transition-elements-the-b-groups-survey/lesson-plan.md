# Lesson plan — Transition elements — the B-groups survey

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Transition elements — the B-groups survey
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The B groups toured one by one with their personalities, as Yemen's Grade 12 student book tours them (with the teacher guide's sharpening classification as evidence): IB the coinage metals with variable valency unlike IA's fixed +1; IIB zinc, cadmium, mercury at only +2; IIIB the most electropositive of the transition metals; IVB hard, high-melting, expensive; VB and VIB, chromium sealing itself under an oxide skin; VIIB manganese from +2 to +7; VIIIB the three horizontal families — with the chromium and copper 4s1 configuration exceptions and the inner-transition lanthanides and actinides tabled separately; the guide adds the definition (a partly filled d or f sub-shell, in the free state or in a compound), the A/B chemical-similarity bridge, and the characteristic traits — several oxidation states, coloured compounds, paramagnetism, complex-ion formation [teacher evidence]
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Transition elements — characteristic properties is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
