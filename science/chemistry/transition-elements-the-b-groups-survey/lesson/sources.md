| Unit 1: Transition elements (Yemen, Grade 12) | Book 1 (Grade 12) — Unit 1: Transition elements — printed pp. 11–15 — The B groups are toured one by one with their personalities: IB the coinage metals with variable valency (+1, +2, +3) unlike group IA's fixed +1; IIB zinc, cadmium and mercury, only +2 (mercury also Hg2²⁺); IIIB scandium, yttrium, lanthanum and the actinides, the most electropositive of the transition metals; IVB titanium, zirconium, hafnium — hard, high-melting, expensive, found chiefly in nature; VB vanadium, niobium, tantalum with their high oxidation states, vanadium resisting mineral acids and oxidisers except chlorine water; VIB chromium, molybdenum, tungsten, corrosion-resistant because chromium seals itself under an oxide skin; VIIB manganese with oxidation states from +2 to +7; VIIIB the three horizontal families (iron–cobalt–nickel; ruthenium–rhodium–palladium; osmium–iridium–platinum), the platinum metals serving jewellery and laboratory ware; the first-series configuration table shows the chromium and copper 4s¹ exceptions, and the inner-transition lanthanides (4f) and actinides (5f) are tabled separately. |
| Unit 1 background [teacher evidence] (Yemen, Grade 12) | Book 2 (Grade 12) [teacher evidence] — Unit 1 background [teacher evidence] — printed pp. 11–12 — The guide's background sharpens the classification the student book assumes: transition elements are divided into the main transition elements (d-Block elements) and the Inner transition elements whose two long series sit below the table; an element counts as transitional only if its d or f sub-shell is partially filled in the free state or in one of its compounds; and the naming bridge between the A and B group systems is made concrete in a chemical-similarity table — group (IVA) CCl4, SnCl4, CO2 set against group (IVB) TiCl4, TiO2; PO4³⁻/POCl3 against VO4³⁻/VOCl3; SO4²⁻/S2O7²⁻ against CrO4²⁻/Cr2O7²⁻; ClO4⁻/Cl2O7 against MnO4⁻/Mn2O7; NaCl against CuCl and AgCl — showing why each B group chemically echoes the A group of the same number; characteristic transition traits are listed (several oxidation states from one element, coloured compounds — most transition compounds are coloured — paramagnetism from unpaired d/f electrons, and a strong tendency to form complex ions). |
