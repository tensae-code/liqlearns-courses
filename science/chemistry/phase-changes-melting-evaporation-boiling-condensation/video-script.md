Today: **Phase changes** — Mongolia's way: the changes between the states, taught as the very first unit of chemistry.

Ice melts. A puddle dries. Steam beads back into drops on a cold lid. One story explains all three: the particles. In a solid they sit in fixed places, shaking. Heat them enough and they break loose — the solid melts. Cool the liquid and they settle back — it freezes. Same door, same temperature, both ways.
In a liquid, the fastest particles at the surface escape — that is evaporation, and it needs no boiling. Gas that meets a cold surface slows down and gathers back — condensation.
And boiling? That is the whole liquid turning at once, bubbles rising from inside, at one fixed temperature — and while it boils, the temperature will not climb, because the heat is busy pulling particles apart.
Through every change, remember: the particles themselves never change. Only how they are arranged, and how they move.
