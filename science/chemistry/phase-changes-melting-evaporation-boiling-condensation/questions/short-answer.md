# Phase changes — melting, evaporation, boiling and condensation — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do melting and freezing happen at the same temperature?
**Answer: They are the same change in opposite directions: at that temperature the particles have exactly the energy either to break free of their fixed positions (melting) or to settle back into them (freezing).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does evaporation cool the liquid left behind?
**Answer: The particles that escape are the fastest ones, so the particles remaining have a lower average energy — and a lower average particle energy is a lower temperature.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How is boiling different from evaporation?
**Answer: Evaporation is the escape of the fastest particles at the surface only, at any temperature; boiling happens at one fixed temperature with bubbles of gas forming throughout the whole liquid.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A puddle dries on a cool day without boiling. Explain how, in particle terms.
**Answer: At the puddle's surface, the fastest water particles escape into the air (evaporation). It needs no boiling point — it happens at any temperature, particle by particle, until the liquid is gone.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 1) What stays the same, and what changes, when ice melts into water?
**Answer: The particles stay the same — same kind, same number. What changes is their arrangement and motion: from fixed positions in the solid to moving past one another in the liquid.**
