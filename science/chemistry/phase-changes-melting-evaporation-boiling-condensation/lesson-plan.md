# Lesson plan — Phase changes — melting, evaporation, boiling and condensation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Phase changes — melting, evaporation, boiling and condensation
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the changes between the states of matter as one named unit — melting and freezing between solid and liquid, evaporation and condensation between liquid and gas, and boiling as the whole-liquid change — all read through the particle theory: in every phase change the particles themselves stay the same, and only their arrangement, spacing and motion change; Mongolia's Grade 7 book teaches phase transitions as a named unit inside its opening §1.1 States of matter, and its Grade 9 book opens with the same unit
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Properties of solids, liquids and gases, and The kinetic theory of matter, are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Melting and freezing — solid ↔ liquid** — Heat breaks a solid's particles out of their fixed positions at the melting point; cooling settles a liquid's particles back into place at the same temperature — one door, swinging both ways.
2. **Evaporation and condensation — liquid ↔ gas** — The fastest surface particles escape as evaporation, at any temperature and cooling what is left behind; gas meeting a colder surface gives its energy up and condenses back to liquid.
3. **Boiling — the whole liquid turns** — At the boiling point bubbles form throughout the liquid, not only at the surface, and the temperature holds steady while the change completes — the particle picture that closes the unit: the particles never change, only their arrangement and motion.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Properties of solids, liquids and gases**; **The kinetic theory of matter**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Melting and freezing — solid ↔ liquid.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Evaporation and condensation — liquid ↔ gas.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: Boiling — the whole liquid turns.
4. Name all four changes in the title, say which pair of states each joins, and state what happens to the particles themselves in every one.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the particle picture: same particles, different arrangement and motion — every change in the lesson is that one idea at a different door.
- Mixes evaporation up with boiling → evaporation is surface-only at any temperature; boiling is throughout the liquid at one fixed temperature.
- Thinks melting makes new particles → compare the particle drawings before and after: count them — the number and kind never change.
