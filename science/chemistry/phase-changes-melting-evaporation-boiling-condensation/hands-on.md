# Hands-on — Phase changes — melting, evaporation, boiling and condensation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The drying race (observation only — water and cloth)
Wet two identical cloths with the same amount of water. Hang one in moving air (near an open window or a fan, with a teacher), one in still air, both at the same temperature. Check every ten minutes and note which dries first and by how much. Explain the result from sub lesson 2: what the moving air does to the escaped particles, and why that speeds the escape of the next ones.

## Steam on a cold lid (teacher-led — hot water only, no chemicals)
With a teacher, hold a cold, dry metal lid or plate above — never touching — a cup of just-boiled water. Watch the underside: drops form where there were none. Name the change you are watching, say where the water in the drops came from, and say what the cold surface took from the steam's particles to make it happen.

## Draw the three states (paper only)
Draw one box of particles three times: packed in fixed rows (solid), close but disordered (liquid), far apart and scattered (gas). Under each arrow between the drawings, write the name of the change and whether heat is taken in or given out.
