# Water ionises itself — pH and pOH

## Do it first
Pure water is neutral, yet it conducts a whisper of electricity — ions exist in it, made by water itself. Before reading, say how many H₃O⁺ ions balance how many OH⁻ in pure water, and what single number their product always makes at 25 °C.

[Picture 21716 — The pH scale 0–14 with household examples.]

Water self-ionises: H₂O + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻. The equilibrium constant of that reaction is Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C.

pH = −log[H₃O⁺] and pOH = −log[OH⁻]; the scales run 0–14 in ordinary work, with pH + pOH = 14 at 25 °C.

Neutral water has pH 7; acids sit below 7, bases above. Each pH unit is a tenfold change in [H₃O⁺]. Calculations run in both directions — concentration to pH and pH back to concentration.
