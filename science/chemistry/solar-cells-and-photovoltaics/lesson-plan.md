# Lesson plan — Solar cells and photovoltaics

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Solar cells and photovoltaics
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a solar cell turns sunlight directly into electricity — the photovoltaic effect in a semiconductor junction, with no moving parts and nothing burned — met the way Iran meets it: pictured and questioned as an advanced technology inside the Grade 12 materials chapter, among the materials humanity reshapes for art, beauty and durability

## Sub lessons
1. **Light in, electricity out — the photovoltaic idea** — A solar cell produces electricity from sunlight directly: no flame, no steam, no spinning generator — the conversion happens inside the solid material itself, which is why Iran places it among advanced materials technologies.
2. **Inside the cell — the junction that separates charge** — The cell is a semiconductor (silicon) built as two differently treated layers; light frees electrons inside it, and the junction between the layers drives the freed charges apart to opposite faces, so the two faces become the cell's two terminals and a circuit joined across them carries a direct current.
3. **From one cell to a working technology** — Cells are joined into panels sized to the job — calculators and garden lights up to rooftop arrays and solar farms — giving electricity wherever the sun shines, silently and with no fuel, bounded by the limits the questions press on: night, cloud, and the need to store energy for when the light is gone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the one element: sunlight falls on a solid cell and electricity comes out — the light itself does the work inside the material, with nothing burned and nothing spinning.
- Mixes solar cells up with solar water heating → heating uses the sun's warmth to warm water directly; a photovoltaic cell makes electricity — the same sunlight, two different technologies, and only the cell's output can run a lamp or charge a battery.
- Thinks the cell stores the sunlight → the cell only converts while light falls on it; storing is a separate job, done by a battery the cell can charge.
