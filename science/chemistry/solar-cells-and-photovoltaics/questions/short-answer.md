# Solar cells and photovoltaics — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How does a solar cell's way of making electricity differ from a fuel-burning power station's?
**Answer: The station works by stages — burn fuel, boil water, spin a turbine, turn a generator. The solar cell skips every stage: sunlight falls on a solid wafer and electricity comes out of the wafer directly, by the photovoltaic effect.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Trace what happens from a photon striking the cell to a current in a wire.
**Answer: The photon frees an electron inside the silicon; the junction drives the freed electron one way and the gap it left the other way, separating charges to the cell's two faces; joined by a wire and a load, the separated charges flow round the circuit as a direct current.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why would the cell give no current if the junction were missing?
**Answer: Without the junction's built-in push, a freed electron would simply fall back where it came from; no charges would be separated to the two faces, so there would be no terminals and nothing to drive a current.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a solar lighting system need a battery if the panel already makes electricity?
**Answer: Conversion and storage are separate jobs: the panel converts only while light falls on it, so the battery stores the day's electricity for the dark hours when the panel gives nothing.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does Iran teach the solar cell inside a materials chapter?
**Answer: The chapter — "Chemistry, a face of art, beauty and durability" — is about the materials humanity reshapes from the Earth, and the solar cell is presented there as an advanced technology producing electricity from sunlight: a new material doing a new kind of work, pictured and questioned like the chapter's other materials.**
