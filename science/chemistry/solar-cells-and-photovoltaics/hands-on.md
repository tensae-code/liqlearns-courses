# Hands-on — Solar cells and photovoltaics

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The calculator test (observation only — a solar calculator or garden light)
Find a solar calculator or a solar garden light. Work it in bright light, then cover its cell strip completely with your hand and try again; then uncover only half the strip. Write down what happens at each step and say it in lesson terms: the cell converts only while light falls on it, and its output follows the light it receives.

## Light in, electricity out — the day chart (home observation)
If your home or street has a solar garden light, watch it across one day: note when it was charged (daylight) and when it gives its light back (dark). In three lines, separate the two jobs — conversion by the cell, storage by its battery — and name which part did each.

## One photon's journey (paper exercise)
Draw the cell as two layers with a join between them. Draw one photon arriving, one electron freed, an arrow for the junction's push, and a wire leaving each face to a small lamp. Label every step with the lesson's own words: photon, freed electron, junction, separated charges, direct current.
