# Simple and compound substances

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's thing is, in one sentence — then read on and check it against Armenia's way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Simple substances — one element only

A **simple substance** is built from atoms of **one element** only. However many atoms it holds, they are all the same kind. Armenia's Grade 7 book sets this sorting out as its own lesson (§3.2, printed p. 81), right after the molecule and the chemical formula have been introduced.

## Sub lesson 2 — Compound substances — different elements joined

A **compound substance** is built from atoms of **different elements** joined together. Its formula names more than one element because more than one element is really in it. The compound is a new substance, not a mixture of the elements sitting side by side — its atoms are joined, and its properties are its own.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sorting by the building

The lesson's test is a single question: **how many kinds of atom is it built from?** One kind — simple. More than one kind — compound. The sorting is done from the substance's composition, which the chemical formula (§3.1) writes down — the formula and the sorting lesson belong together, and Armenia teaches them in that order.

## The key ideas

- A **simple substance** is built from atoms of **one element**.
- A **compound substance** is built from atoms of **different elements** joined together — a new substance, not a mixture.
- The sorting test: **how many kinds of atom** is it built from?
