# The electron-sea model

## Do it first
One page, one picture — the model that explains metals. Before reading, picture a crowd of positive ions standing in the sea at the beach. What is the water, in a metal?

[Picture 21645 — Kernels in a sea of electrons.]

The electron-sea model pictures a metal as positive kernels arranged in a lattice, immersed in a 'sea' of delocalised electrons.

The electrons belong to no single atom — they are free to move through the whole structure.

This one picture is the explanation the next lesson spends on conductivity, bending and lustre.

## Say it to your companion
Explain The electron-sea model to your companion in your own words, using the lesson's own examples. Then let your companion ask you the questions from the end of the lesson until you can answer each one without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's key ideas in your own words, in a list, without opening the lesson.
2. Work one example of your own for each key idea — your own numbers, your own substances, the lesson's own rules.
3. Check your examples against the lesson. Fix anything that disagrees, and say what rule it broke.
4. Teach the hardest idea in the lesson to someone else, out loud, once.
