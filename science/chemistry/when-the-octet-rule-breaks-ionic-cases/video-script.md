# Video script — When the octet rule breaks — ionic cases

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Iron forms Fe²⁺ in one compound and Fe³⁺ in another.

The octet rule is a guide, not a law — real compounds break it.

Transition metals form ions of variable charge (Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ and similar), because d electrons can also be lost.

In such ionic cases, simple octet counting fails, and the actual charges must be learned from the compounds themselves.

Now you. Say it back: Is the octet rule a law?
Answer: No — a guide that can break.
