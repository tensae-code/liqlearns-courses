# Rocks and minerals — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is a mineral's streak steadier evidence than its surface colour?
**Answer:** Surface colour can be changed by impurities tinting the mineral, while the streak — the colour of its powder — stays characteristic of the mineral itself; identification uses the set of properties together, not one look.

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name any three properties used to identify a mineral.
**Answer:** Any three of: colour, streak, lustre, hardness, cleavage/fracture — tested one by one until the properties together name the mineral.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) State the mineral–rock distinction in one sentence.
**Answer:** A mineral is one definite substance; a rock is a natural mass made of one mineral or, usually, several minerals gathered together.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does slow cooling underground show itself in an igneous rock?
**Answer:** Crystals have time to grow large, so the rock is coarse-grained — the grain size is the visible record of the cooling speed.

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) A rock shows clear layers of gritty material. Which way of forming does the layering point to, and why?
**Answer:** Sedimentary formation: material was deposited layer on layer, then buried and compacted or cemented — the layering survives in the finished rock as the sign of that making.
