# Lesson plan — Rocks and minerals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Rocks and minerals
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rocks and minerals as a chemistry chapter — what a mineral is (a naturally occurring solid of definite composition and ordered structure) and how minerals are identified by their properties, what a rock is (minerals gathered into a natural mass) and how rocks are identified, and how rocks form in three ways (cooling from molten material, building up from deposited material, and remaking by heat and pressure); Uganda's Senior One prototype ends its chemistry course with the ground underfoot (Ch. 9)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

## Sub lessons
1. **Minerals — nature's definite solids, and how they are identified** — A mineral is a naturally occurring solid with a definite chemical composition and an ordered internal structure. Minerals are identified by their observable properties — colour, streak, lustre, hardness, cleavage and form — tested one by one until the properties together name the mineral.
2. **Rocks — minerals gathered into masses** — A rock is a natural mass made of one mineral or of several minerals gathered together. Rocks are identified by the minerals they contain, the size and arrangement of their grains, and their texture — the visible record of how the mass came together.
3. **How rocks form — three ways of making** — Rocks form in three great ways: igneous rocks cool and solidify from molten material; sedimentary rocks build up as material is deposited, buried and compacted or cemented; metamorphic rocks are older rocks remade by heat and pressure without melting. Each way of forming leaves its own signs in the rock, which is why formation and identification belong in one lesson.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes a mineral up with a rock → a mineral is one definite substance; a rock is a mass that may hold several minerals at once — count the substances, not the size of the piece.
