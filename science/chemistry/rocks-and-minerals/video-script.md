Today: **Rocks and minerals** — Uganda's way: Senior One chemistry ends in the ground underfoot.

Start with a mineral: a solid nature makes, always the same substance — quartz is always SiO₂, wherever you find it. You name a mineral by testing its properties together: its colour, the colour of its powder streak, how it shines, how hard it is, and how it splits.
A rock is different — it is a mass. One mineral, or usually several minerals gathered together, like the pale and dark grains locked side by side in granite.
And rocks are made three ways. Melted material cools and hardens — that is igneous. Worn material settles in layers, is buried and pressed into stone — sedimentary. And an old rock squeezed and heated without melting is remade — metamorphic. Read a rock's grains and layers, and they tell you which of the three made it.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
