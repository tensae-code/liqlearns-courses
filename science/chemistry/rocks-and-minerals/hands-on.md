# Hands-on — Rocks and minerals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Identify by properties (outdoors, no chemicals)
Collect three different stones from safe ground near your home or school (wash your hands afterwards). For each stone, record in a table: its colour, whether it looks glassy, metallic or dull, whether a fingernail or a steel nail scratches it, and whether it is made of one visible substance or of several grains of different colours. Using sub lessons 1 and 2, write under each stone: mineral-like (one definite substance) or rock-like (a gathered mass) — and the observation that decided you.

## Sort by formation story
On six cards write these formation stories: cooled slowly underground; lava cooled quickly at the surface; sand buried and cemented; shells pressed into layers; limestone remade by heat and pressure; an old rock squeezed deep without melting. Sort the cards into three piles — igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic — and say out loud, for each card, the sign sub lesson 3 says that way of forming leaves in the rock.

## Read one rock backwards
Choose the most layered or the most crystalline of your three stones. Write a short "reading" of it in three sentences: what you observe (minerals/grains/texture), which way of forming those observations point to, and what would change your mind. Read it to a companion and let them challenge the middle sentence.
