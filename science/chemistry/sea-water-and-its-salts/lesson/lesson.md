# Sea water and its salts

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's thing is, in one sentence — then read on and check it against Armenia's way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — The sea holds salts

Armenia's Grade 9 **teacher guide** names a lesson of its own: **the sea and its salts** (printed p. 71, contents). The sea is taught as a body of water that **holds salts in solution** — the salts are really in the water, invisible while dissolved, which is why sea water tastes of them and why they can be obtained from it. This lesson stands on that guide evidence, labelled as such.

## Sub lesson 2 — Salts obtained from the sea

The lesson's second half is the obtaining: **the salts got out of sea water**. Water that holds a salt in solution gives the salt back when the water is taken away — the sea is therefore a **salt source**, a store from which salts are won, not only a body of water to describe.

## Sub lesson 3 — A named lesson in the environment theme

The guide places the sea lesson inside the same teaching apparatus as the student book's environment theme — next to the water-hardness teaching (printed p. 61, contents). Water is studied whole in this course: hard water, sea water and its salts, each water with what it holds.

## The key ideas

- The guide names a lesson **the sea and its salts** (printed p. 71, contents) — sea water holds **salts in solution**.
- The salts can be **obtained from** the sea water — the sea is a **salt source**.
- It stands beside the guide's **water-hardness** teaching: each water studied with what it holds.
