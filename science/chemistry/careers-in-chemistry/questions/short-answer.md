# Careers in chemistry — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the placing of careers in Strand 1.1 matter?
**Answer: Careers are taught inside the introduction to chemistry itself, at printed p. 2 — so knowing what chemists do for a living counts as part of knowing what chemistry is, not as an extra.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) What exactly does the strand's careers exercise ask for?
**Answer: Pictures of eight chemistry-related careers, pasted in — each career matched to a person and the work they do.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Say what an agricultural scientist does, in lesson terms.
**Answer: An agricultural scientist applies chemistry to farming and food — testing soil, measuring what nutrients are missing and advising what fertiliser to add and how much.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Say what a biochemist does, in lesson terms.
**Answer: A biochemist studies the chemistry of living things — the substances life is made of and the reactions that keep it running, at work in medicine, food and farming.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) How do the two named careers differ in where their chemistry points?
**Answer: The agricultural scientist's chemistry points at the farm — soil and crops; the biochemist's points inside living things — the substances and reactions of life itself.**
