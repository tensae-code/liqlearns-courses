# Lesson plan — Careers in chemistry

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Chemistry · **Lesson:** Careers in chemistry
**Place in the curriculum:** Chemistry — appended to Unit 17 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** chemistry careers taught as content in their own right — the jobs chemistry opens, met the way Fiji's Year 11 book meets them: inside its opening Introduction to Chemistry strand, naming the agricultural scientist, the biochemist and others, and setting an exercise in which learners paste pictures of eight chemistry-related careers (Fiji, Grade 11 — *Chemistry for Year 11 (amended 2016)*, Strand 1.1, printed p. 2)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Chemistry, the central science is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Chemistry careers are content, not an afterthought** — Fiji's Year 11 book puts careers inside **Strand 1.1 Introduction to Chemistry** itself, at printed p.
2. **The agricultural scientist — chemistry at work on the farm** — An **agricultural scientist** uses chemistry where food is grown.
3. **The biochemist — chemistry inside living things** — A **biochemist** studies the chemistry of living things: the substances life is made of and the reactions that keep it running — how food is broken down for energy, how proteins are built, what the chemicals in blood and tissue say about health.

Biochemists work where chemistry meets medicine, food and farming — in laboratories testing samples, in food production, in research on crops and animals.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks careers are not lesson content → in Fiji's book they are: Strand 1.1 teaches them at printed p. 2, with an exercise, like any other topic.
- Mixes the two named careers up → the agricultural scientist works where chemistry meets farming (soil, fertiliser, crops); the biochemist works where chemistry meets living things (the substances and reactions of life).
- Cannot reach eight careers → start from the work, not the job title: who tests, who makes, who measures, who advises — in food, medicine, farming, industry and the environment.
