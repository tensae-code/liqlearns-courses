## Sub lessons
1. **Ions on the move — the heart's electricity** — The signals begin with **ions**: sodium (Na⁺), potassium (K⁺) and calcium (Ca²⁺) ions **move across the membranes of heart-muscle cells**. Every movement of charge is an electric current in miniature, and the coordinated movement across millions of heart cells adds up to a signal the body itself generates.
2. **The ECG — reading the signal from outside** — An **ECG (electrocardiogram)** records those electrical signals from electrodes on the skin. The trace's waves are the heart muscle's ion movements made visible: each beat's spread of excitation and recovery draws its own part of the line, so the machine reads the heart's electrical activity without touching the heart itself.
3. **When the balance slips — rhythm disturbance** — Because the signal is made by ions, **ion imbalance shows up as heart-rhythm disturbance**: too much or too little of the key ions in the blood changes how the heart muscle fires, and the ECG trace changes with it. That is why a chemistry idea — ions moving across a membrane — is also a medical instrument's whole foundation.
