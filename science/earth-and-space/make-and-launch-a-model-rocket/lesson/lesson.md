# Make and launch a model rocket

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — making a model rocket and launching it — the space unit's own build, China's Grade 6 closing project. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Making the rocket

China's Grade 6 book closes its space unit with a build: **make a rocket**. The model is made by the learner's own hands — a body, a nose, fins to steady it — a small stand-in for the flying machines the unit has studied, the real rockets and spacecraft that carry people and instruments into space.

Making the model teaches what the pictures cannot: a rocket is a designed thing, each part there for the flight — the pointed nose to part the air, the fins to keep it straight, the body to hold its power.

## Sub lesson 2 — Launching it

The made rocket is **launched** — safely, outdoors, with an adult — and its flight is the test of the build. A rocket that climbs straight has its parts in balance; one that veers or tumbles tells its maker what to change.

The launch closes the unit's circle: the class began by knowing the starry sky and the history of exploring it, and ends by flying a machine of its own — small, brief, and truly a flight.

## The key ideas

- A model rocket is **designed part by part**: a nose to part the air, fins to keep it straight, a body to hold its power.
- The **launch is the test**: a straight climb proves the build balanced; a veer or tumble names what to change.
- The build closes the space unit — from knowing the sky and its explorers to **flying a machine of the class's own**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Making the rocket, Launching it. Your companion may stop you once and ask one question about the hardest part; answer from the lesson, not by repeating it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw a model rocket and label its parts — nose, body, fins — with one line on what each part does in flight.
2. Write a launch-day checklist with an adult: a safe open place, the rocket checked, watchers standing back, and what you will observe.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how a launch tests a build.
