# Hands-on — The Lambda-CDM model of the universe

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The balloon universe
Draw six dots on a partly blown-up balloon. Measure the distance between two pairs of dots with a strip of paper. Blow the balloon up more and measure again. Every distance grew, no dot stayed the centre, and the far pairs grew fastest. Say aloud: the dots did not move across the surface — the surface itself stretched. That is the expanding universe, and it has no centre on the surface — just like the real one.

## The stretching wave
Draw a wavy line on a piece of elastic or a wide rubber band at rest. Now stretch it: the wave stretches too, longer and flatter. Say aloud: light travelling through expanding space is stretched the same way — toward the red. That stretching is the redshift astronomers measure.

## Weigh the invisible
Spin slowly with arms tucked in, then stretch your arms out and feel how your spin changes. A galaxy is the same physics written huge: its spin tells how much mass holds it — and the spin says there is far more mass than the shining stars can account for. Say aloud: dark matter is weighed by motion, not seen by light.
