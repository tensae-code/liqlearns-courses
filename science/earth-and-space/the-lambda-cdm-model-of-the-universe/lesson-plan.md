# Lesson plan — The Lambda-CDM model of the universe

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** The Lambda-CDM model of the universe
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — the cosmology capstone, after How the solar system formed — from cloud to Sun and The Sun — its scale, layers, spots and storms (CURRICULUM.md Unit 19), closing the unit at the largest scale there is.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the best-tested picture of the whole universe has a name you can take apart: the universe began hot and dense and has been expanding and cooling since (the Big Bang), most of its matter is cold dark matter (CDM) that no telescope sees directly, and a constant energy — Lambda, Λ — is speeding the expansion up; Lambda-CDM is that whole recipe in one name.
**This lesson's own level:** it is the capstone of the Earth-and-space unit — a learner can land here directly. How the solar system formed is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The universe is expanding — the Big Bang beginning** — galaxies are rushing apart, their light stretched red (redshift); run the film backwards and everything meets in a hot, dense beginning about 13.8 billion years ago, whose cooled afterglow — the cosmic microwave background — still fills the sky.
2. **CDM — the cold dark matter most of matter is** — galaxies spin too fast and clusters hold together too well for the matter we can see; the missing mass is dark matter — cold (slow-moving), dark (it does not shine or block light), known only by its gravity.
3. **Lambda — the dark energy speeding everything up** — distant exploding stars showed the expansion is not slowing but accelerating; Lambda (Λ), Einstein's cosmological constant, names the dark energy driving that acceleration — and the model's full name assembles: Lambda + CDM + the ordinary matter and light we know.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **How the solar system formed — from cloud to Sun** and **Planetary motion — ellipses and Kepler's laws**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the two pieces of evidence for the Big Bang picture: galaxies' redshifted light showing expansion, and the cosmic microwave background.
2. Say what "cold dark matter" means word by word, and name the observations that force it into the model.
3. Say what Lambda stands for and what observation put it there: the accelerating expansion found from distant supernovae.
4. Assemble the name: state the universe's rough recipe — a small part ordinary matter, a larger part dark matter, the largest part dark energy.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; the hands-on items in hands-on.md — a balloon and a marker, a stretchy band or piece of elastic. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner draws dots on a partly inflated balloon, inflates it more, and watches every dot move away from every other dot with no centre on the surface, before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the universe's recipe drawn as a simple pie chart — about 5% ordinary matter, about 27% dark matter, about 68% dark energy — the student points at the slice everything they have ever seen is made of.
   - ADD GIF: a 1–2 second GIF of a field of galaxy dots expanding — slowly at first, then faster and faster in the last stretch — the student watches the acceleration Lambda names.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: on the balloon, no dot is the centre, yet all dots separate — space itself stretches; that is the expansion the whole model is built on.
- Thinks dark matter is just faint ordinary matter → it cannot be: it neither shines nor blocks light, and there is far too much of it; it is known only because its gravity holds galaxies together.
- Thinks Lambda is a thing floating in space → Lambda is a constant in the equations — the name for the dark energy spread through all of space that pushes the expansion faster.
