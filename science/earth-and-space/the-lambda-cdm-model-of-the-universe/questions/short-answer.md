# The Lambda-CDM model of the universe — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) On the balloon of dots, no dot is the centre, yet all dots separate. What is the universe's version of that?
**Answer: Space itself expands everywhere at once: every galaxy moves away from every other, and there is no centre to the expansion — just as no point on the balloon's surface is the centre of its stretching.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name two independent witnesses that the universe began hot and dense.
**Answer: The redshift of distant galaxies — expansion that, run backwards, converges on a hot dense start — and the cosmic microwave background, the cooled afterglow of that beginning, still arriving from every direction.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the dark matter be cold rather than hot and fast?
**Answer: Fast particles would have streamed out of small clumps and smoothed them away; only slow, cold dark matter could gather into the clumps around which ordinary matter collected to form galaxies like ours.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What did astronomers expect to find when they measured distant supernovae, and what did they find instead?
**Answer: They expected the expansion to be slowing under gravity's pull. The supernovae were farther than a slowing universe allows — the expansion is accelerating, the observation that put Lambda, dark energy, into the model.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Write out the Lambda-CDM recipe and say which part everything we have ever seen belongs to.
**Answer: Roughly 5% ordinary matter, 27% dark matter and 68% dark energy. Everything we have ever seen — stars, planets, gas, ourselves — belongs to the smallest part, the 5% of ordinary matter.**
