# The rocky planets — Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars

## Do it first

Look at the four planets in the picture. Which one would burn you at noon and freeze you at midnight? Which one could you swim on? Guess first — then read and check.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mercury and Venus

[Picture 34505 — the four rocky planets in a row to relative scale, each with its signature feature drawn large]

**Mercury** — nearest the Sun and smallest — is Moon-like and **cratered**, with **3 km cliffs** read as wrinkles from a **shrinking iron core**. Its day reaches **425 °C** and its night **−170 °C**, and it has effectively **no atmosphere** — its trace gases are borrowed from the solar wind. **Venus** is Earth's twin in size and mass, hidden under **dense cloud**. The little sunlight that reaches its surface is trapped by **carbon-dioxide greenhouse heating** to **450–475 °C** — the strongest greenhouse case in the solar system.

## Sub lesson 2 — Earth and Mars

**Earth** — third out, at **1 AU (150 million km)** — is the only planet with **abundant liquid water and life**; its atmosphere burns up most meteors and shields radiation. **Mars** is the **red planet** — iron oxide in its soil — with **polar caps** of frozen water under frozen carbon dioxide, channels that may be old **water-carved valleys**, rift valleys, and **Olympus Mons**, the solar system's largest, probably extinct, volcano.
