# Hands-on — The Moon's surface — highlands, maria and craters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the lesson's work (household materials)
Work the lesson's central example yourself, the way Saudi Arabia's book sets it: Earth's Moon: until Galileo's telescope in 1609 the moon was thought smooth; its highlands are pocked with craters from crashing meteorites, and its dark flat areas, the maria, formed when lava flowed into low ground and cooled; in 2018 high-resolution pictures of the moon's surface were taken by the Saudi imaging system carried on the Chinese lunar exploration mission — the moon circles the earth every 27.3 days at an average 384,400 km Use only household materials, write down what you did and what it showed, and compare your result with the lesson.
