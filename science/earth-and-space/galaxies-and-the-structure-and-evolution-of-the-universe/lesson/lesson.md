# Galaxies and the structure and evolution of the Universe

## Do it first
Before you read: on the darkest night you can find, one fuzzy patch in the sky is not a star and not a cloud — it is another galaxy, so far away its light left it before humans existed. How many such islands are there, what shapes do they take, and is the space between them standing still? This lesson answers all three.

## Sub lesson 1 — Galaxies — islands of stars

A galaxy is a gravitationally bound system of stars, gas, dust and dark matter: our own Milky Way holds hundreds of billions of stars, with the Sun an ordinary one out in a spiral arm. Galaxies sort by shape — spirals with arms winding from a central bulge, barred spirals, ellipticals (smooth balls of mostly old stars, little gas, little new star-making) and irregulars with no settled shape. Galaxies gather in groups and clusters — the Milky Way and Andromeda are the two giants of the Local Group — and Andromeda, visible to the naked eye on a dark night, is on a slow collision course with us billions of years from now.

## Sub lesson 2 — The expanding Universe — Hubble's law

Look at distant galaxies and their light is shifted to the red: redshift grows with distance, which reads as space itself expanding, every galaxy receding from every other. Hubble's law prices it: recession speed v = H₀ × distance d — the farther the galaxy, the faster it recedes. Run the expansion backwards and everything converges on a hot, dense beginning about 13.8 billion years ago; the left-over heat of that beginning is still measurable everywhere as the cosmic microwave background, the Universe's oldest light.

## Sub lesson 3 — Structure and evolution of the Universe

The Universe's story runs in eras: a hot dense beginning; expansion and cooling until atoms could form and light could travel; gravity gathering gas into the first stars and galaxies; galaxies merging and growing into today's web of clusters and voids, with most of the matter dark — felt by its gravity, never seen — and the expansion itself now accelerating. Structure grew from near-uniformity because slightly denser patches pulled harder: the cosmic web is gravity's handwriting at the largest scale, and every galaxy — ours included — is a temporary pattern in that ongoing evolution.

## The key ideas

- A galaxy is a bound system of stars, gas, dust and dark matter; galaxies sort into spirals, ellipticals and irregulars, and gather into groups and clusters
- Distant galaxies are redshifted in proportion to distance — Hubble's law, v = H₀d — space itself is expanding
- Run backwards, the expansion reaches a hot, dense beginning about 13.8 billion years ago, still glowing faintly as the cosmic microwave background; gravity grew the near-uniform early Universe into today's cosmic web
