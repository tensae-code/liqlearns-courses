# Video script — Landforms and water on Earth

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Landforms and water on Earth** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Grade 2).

The land's shapes are its **landforms**, and the book names its pattern through examples: **valleys**, low land between higher ground; **canyons**, deep cuts through the land; **floodplains**, the flat land beside rivers (Grade 2, 2.1.1, printed p. 13). A landform is not a single hill or dip — it is a kind of shape that repeats across the Earth, and the pattern is what a model must show.

Earth's water belongs in the same model: water is held across the planet in its places, and in **two states — solid and liquid**. Ice is water standing solid on the land; rivers, lakes and seas are water liquid. One Earth, one water, two forms — and the model must carry both.

Utah's way is to **develop and use models** of the whole: a model that shows the patterns of landforms and water together — the shapes of the land and the places and states of the water in one view. The model is not the Earth; it is the Earth's pattern made small enough to point at.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
