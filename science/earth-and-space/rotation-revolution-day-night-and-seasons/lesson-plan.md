# Lesson plan — The Earth — rotation, revolution, day-night and seasons

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** The Earth — rotation, revolution, day-night and seasons
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — a new title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Earth, at General Science depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Earth spins on a 23.5°-tilted axis once in 24 hours (rotation — day and night), and travels around the Sun once in 365¼ days (revolution) — and the fixed tilt is what makes the seasons, opposite in the two hemispheres.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Earth — shape, evidence and dimensions: the learner knows the ball; this lesson sets the ball spinning and travelling.

## Sub lessons
1. **Rotation — day and night** — the Earth turns on its axis, tilted at 23.5°, once in 24 hours; the face toward the Sun has day, the face away has night.
2. **Revolution — the seasons** — one journey around the Sun in 365¼ days; the tilt stays pointing the same way, so the hemispheres lean toward and away from the Sun in turn: summer, winter, spring, autumn — opposite north and south.

- Recommended first: The Earth — shape, evidence and dimensions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what rotation is, how long one turn takes, and why it makes day and night.
2. State the axis tilt: 23.5°.
3. Say what revolution is and how long one journey takes.
4. Explain why, when the north has summer, the south has winter.

## What you need
A ball, a lamp for the Sun, and a stick through the ball for its axis.

## If the learner is stuck
- Swaps the two motions → anchor by time: the short one (24 hours) is the spin, rotation; the long one (365¼ days) is the journey, revolution.
- Thinks the seasons come from the Earth moving nearer the Sun → the distance barely changes; it is the TILT, held one way, that leans each hemisphere toward the Sun in turn.
- Forgets the tilt's number → 23.5° — say it with the picture: the axis leans about a quarter of a right angle.
