# Hands-on — The Earth — rotation, revolution, day-night and seasons

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your reading.

## Lamp and ball: day and night
Push a stick through a ball from top to bottom — that is the axis — and tilt it a little, about a quarter of a right angle (23.5°). Put a lamp on a table in a dark room: the Sun. Stick a small paper flag on the ball: your town. Turn the ball slowly on its tilted axis. Your town moves into the light (morning), faces the lamp (midday), leaves the light (evening) and crosses the dark side (night). One full turn = 24 hours.

## Walking the year
Keep the ball's axis tilted the SAME way — toward the same wall — and walk it slowly around the lamp, one full circle. At each quarter of the circle, stop: which end of the axis leans toward the lamp now? Write the season for the top half and the bottom half at all four stops. That is the year: 365¼ days, four stops, seasons opposite above and below.

## Your own shadow clock
Stand outside in the morning and mark where your shadow falls and how long it is. Do it again at midday and in the late afternoon. The shadow's turning and shrinking is rotation you can stand inside.
