# Time zones — why the hour differs around the Earth — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can it not be noon everywhere on Earth at the same moment?
**Answer: The Sun can only face one side of the Earth at a time. As the Earth spins, each place reaches the noon position in its turn — when one place is at noon, the opposite side is turned away in midnight.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) What does 'local time' mean?
**Answer: The hour belonging to a place — set by where that place stands on the Earth's spin, that is, by the Sun's position in its own sky.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) There are about 24 time zones. Why that number?
**Answer: Because the Earth's spin takes 24 hours: one zone for each hour of the turning, each differing from its neighbour by one hour.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A wide country spans four time zones. When it is 8 in the morning in its westernmost zone, what time is it in its easternmost zone, and why?
**Answer: 11 in the morning. The eastern zone is three zones to the east, and each eastward zone is one hour ahead: 8 + 3 = 11.**
