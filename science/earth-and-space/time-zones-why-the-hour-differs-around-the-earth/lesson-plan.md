# Lesson plan — Time zones — why the hour differs around the Earth

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Time zones — why the hour differs around the Earth
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space (CURRICULUM.md Unit 19), directly after The Moon — Earth's natural satellite and its phases in the Gabon 4e année Earth thread: the Earth's daily spin, already taught for day and night, now explains the world's clocks
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Earth spins once in 24 hours, so the Sun lights and leaves each place in turn: it cannot be noon everywhere at once; people therefore divide the Earth into time zones — bands running pole to pole, each keeping (roughly) one hour different from its neighbours — so that the clock everywhere agrees with the Sun's local story: morning where the Sun is rising, noon where it stands highest, night where the Earth has turned away

## Sub lessons
1. **One spinning Earth, many local times** — the Earth turns on its axis once in 24 hours, carrying each place around to face the Sun (day), away (night) and around again; a place's time of day is simply where it stands on that turn — so places east and west of each other are at different points of their day at the same moment, and their hours must differ
2. **Time zones — bands of one hour** — to make clocks agreeable and travel possible, the world is divided into about 24 time zones, one for each hour of the spin: each zone keeps the same time across its whole band, and crossing into the next zone eastward adds an hour, westward takes one away; big countries span several zones, and the zone lines bend to follow borders so neighbours share a clock

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: The Earth spins once in 24 hours; each place's hour is its position on that spin — local, not world-wide.
2. Say, in their own words: Places half a world apart have opposite hours at the same moment: one's noon is the other's midnight.
3. Say, in their own words: Time zones are pole-to-pole bands keeping one shared time; neighbouring zones differ by one hour.
4. Say, in their own words: Travelling east adds an hour; travelling west takes one away.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: your noon, their midnight, both correct. The spin is the only machinery needed.
- Thinks there is one true world time → if one clock ruled everywhere, half the world would eat 'breakfast' in darkness. The hour describes the Sun's local position, so it must be local.
- Mixes up east and west → the Sun reaches the east first, so eastern clocks are ahead: east adds, west subtracts.
