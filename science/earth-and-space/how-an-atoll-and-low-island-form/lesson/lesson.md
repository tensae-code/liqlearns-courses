# How an atoll and low island form

## Do it first
Before you read: some Pacific islands are high and mountainous; others are so low and flat you can see across them. How could an island be built almost flat, out in the open sea? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Micronesia's way in — its low-island reader tells how its own flat islands were built.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ring reef — an atoll

A low island begins with a reef that grows in a **ring** — an **atoll**: a circle of coral reef in the sea, holding a sheltered lagoon inside its ring.

The ring is the island's foundation. Everything that becomes land on a low island is built on and from that reef ring.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sand piles grow into narrow, flat islands

On the ring, **piles of coral and shell sand** gather — broken reef material heaped up by the sea until the piles rise above the water.

Those piles grow into the low islands themselves: **narrow and flat**, strips of sand land on the reef ring, with the lagoon on one side and the open ocean on the other. A low island is reef and sand, built up — not rock pushed up.

## The key ideas

- A low island forms on a **ring reef — an atoll** — with a lagoon inside the ring.
- **Piles of coral and shell sand** gather on the ring and rise above the water.
- The piles grow into **narrow, flat islands** — land built of reef and sand.
