# Lesson plan — How an atoll and low island form

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** How an atoll and low island form
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — appended to Unit 19 (Earth and Space) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a low island forms — a ring reef (an atoll) in the sea, and piles of coral and shell sand growing on it into narrow, flat islands — Micronesia's own way, in its PCEP low-island reader for Grades 6–8
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The ring reef — an atoll** — A low island begins with a reef that grows in a ring — an atoll: a circle of coral reef in the sea, holding a sheltered lagoon inside its ring.
2. **Sand piles grow into narrow, flat islands** — On the ring, piles of coral and shell sand gather — broken reef material heaped up by the sea until the piles rise above the water.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks a low island is the top of a volcano like a high island → return to sub lesson 1: the low island is built on a ring reef, from coral and shell sand piled up — compare the high island's lava building, stocked in the solar-system-formation lesson as Micronesia's other way.
- Thinks the islands fill the whole ring → return to sub lesson 2: the sand piles grow into narrow strips on parts of the ring, with lagoon inside and ocean outside.
