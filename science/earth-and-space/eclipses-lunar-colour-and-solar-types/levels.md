# Eclipses — lunar colour and solar types — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner states the lesson's one thing in their own words — an eclipse happens when Sun, Earth and Moon line up exactly: in a lunar eclipse the Earth stands between Sun and Moon and its shadow falls on the Moon — which does not go black but turns copper-red, because Earth's atmosphere bends and filters sunlight into the shadow, scattering the blue away and letting the red through; in a solar eclipse the Moon stands between Sun and Earth, and where its small shadow lands the eclipse is total if the Moon is near enough to cover the Sun's disc, annular — a bright ring — when it is too far to cover it fully; eclipses do not happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to the ecliptic, so most months the shadows miss — and proves it with the lesson's own activities, answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — doing the lesson's own experiments and sorts by hand, naming each idea aloud, and reasoning about new examples until the teaching is the learner's own.
