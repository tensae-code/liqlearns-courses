# Lesson plan — Eclipses — lunar colour and solar types

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Eclipses — lunar colour and solar types
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space (CURRICULUM.md Unit 19), closing Palestine's astronomy sequence after the Moon's journey: the shadow geometry that the celestial sphere and Moon lessons prepare
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an eclipse happens when Sun, Earth and Moon line up exactly: in a lunar eclipse the Earth stands between Sun and Moon and its shadow falls on the Moon — which does not go black but turns copper-red, because Earth's atmosphere bends and filters sunlight into the shadow, scattering the blue away and letting the red through; in a solar eclipse the Moon stands between Sun and Earth, and where its small shadow lands the eclipse is total if the Moon is near enough to cover the Sun's disc, annular — a bright ring — when it is too far to cover it fully; eclipses do not happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to the ecliptic, so most months the shadows miss
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Lunar eclipses — the Moon inside Earth's shadow** — Earth in the middle: its wide shadow swallows the Moon.
2. **Why the eclipsed Moon turns copper-red** — Red survives the atmosphere's filter; blue is scattered away.
3. **Solar eclipses and why they do not happen every month** — Tilted orbit, near misses most months; nodes give eclipse seasons.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A lunar eclipse: Earth between Sun and Moon; the Moon crosses the penumbra, then the umbra; totality can last over an hour and is seen from Earth's whole night side.
2. Say, in their own words: The eclipsed Moon turns copper-red because Earth's atmosphere bends sunlight into the shadow and scatters the blue away, passing the red.
3. Say, in their own words: A solar eclipse: Moon between Sun and Earth; total where the umbra lands, annular when the Moon is too far to cover the Sun's disc fully.
4. Say, in their own words: Eclipses do not happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to the ecliptic; they need a new or full moon near a node.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A lamp (the Sun), a large ball (Earth) and a small ball (the Moon) in a dark room, and a ring drawn on the floor for the Moon's tilted orbit.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the eclipsed Moon should be pitch black → Earth's atmosphere bends sunlight into the shadow and filters it red — the Moon is lit by the ring of all Earth's sunrises and sunsets.
- Thinks there should be an eclipse every full and new moon → the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to the ecliptic, so most months the Moon passes above or below the line and the shadows miss; only node crossings line up.
- Cannot start → return to Do it first. Hold that puzzle; the lesson is its solution.
