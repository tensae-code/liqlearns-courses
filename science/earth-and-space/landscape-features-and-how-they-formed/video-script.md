# Video script — Landscape features — and how they formed

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Today: **Landscape features — and how they formed** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**Samoa's special features and environments.** First, the mapping: Samoa's special landscape features — waterfalls, cliffs, caves, lava flows, the chain of volcanoes, blowholes, Sliding Rock, bays, lakes, pools, rivers and streams — and its special environments — rainforest, swamp forest, mangrove swamps and cloud forest. People have added features of their own: the National Park, Faleolo Airport, memorials, Apia township and the star mounds. A landscape lesson starts by naming what is actually there, on the learner's own ground (printed pp. 89–92).

**The field trip — evidence of forming and changing.** Then the class goes out and hunts evidence: volcanic ash and lava say a volcano built this; water-worn rock says water erosion is still cutting it; roots in cracks say plants are splitting rock; trees bent one way say the wind shapes growth; and cleared, built or damaged places say people have changed the landscape, for better and for worse. Every feature gets the same two questions: how did you form, and what is changing you now? (printed pp. 89–92).
