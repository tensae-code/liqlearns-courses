# Landscape features — and how they formed

## Do it first
A waterfall, a blowhole, a flat black lava flow with nothing growing on it yet. Each one is a clue: something happened here. What happened — and how could you prove it from what you can see? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Samoa's Year 11 way in — its Book 1 maps the islands' special features, then sends the class out to read them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Samoa's special features and environments

First, the mapping: Samoa's special landscape features — **waterfalls, cliffs, caves, lava flows, the chain of volcanoes, blowholes, Sliding Rock, bays, lakes, pools, rivers and streams** — and its special environments — **rainforest, swamp forest, mangrove swamps and cloud forest**. People have added features of their own: **the National Park, Faleolo Airport, memorials, Apia township and the star mounds**. A landscape lesson starts by naming what is actually there, on the learner's own ground (printed pp. 89–92).

## Sub lesson 2 — The field trip — evidence of forming and changing

Then the class goes out and hunts **evidence**: volcanic ash and lava say a volcano built this; water-worn rock says **water erosion** is still cutting it; roots in cracks say **plants are splitting rock**; trees bent one way say the **wind** shapes growth; and cleared, built or damaged places say **people have changed the landscape, for better and for worse**. Every feature gets the same two questions: how did you form, and what is changing you now? (printed pp. 89–92).
