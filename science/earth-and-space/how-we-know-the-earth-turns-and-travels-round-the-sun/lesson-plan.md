## Sub lessons
1. **The spin — evidence for rotation** — Three witnesses. First, shape: the Earth bulges at the equator and flattens at the poles, exactly as a spinning ball of once-soft material must. Second, the Foucault pendulum (1851): a free-swinging pendulum keeps its swing plane while the Earth turns beneath it, so the plane appears to rotate — at the pole a full turn in a day; no force on the pendulum can do that, only the ground's turning. Third, the air: winds and ocean currents are deflected — to the right in the northern hemisphere, left in the southern (the Coriolis effect) — the signature of motion on a rotating surface. Day and night alone prove nothing (a circling Sun would serve); these three prove the spin.
2. **The journey — evidence for the yearly orbit** — Again three witnesses. Stellar parallax: the nearest stars shift slightly against distant ones across six months, as the observer's viewpoint swings by the orbit's width — measured by Bessel in 1838, small because the stars are far. Aberration of starlight: every star appears tipped a little toward the direction of Earth's travel, as rain slants on a moving walker — Bradley's 1729 proof that the observer moves. And the seasons' geometry: the Sun's noon height and the day's length swing through the year exactly as a tilted axis carried round the Sun predicts — the Grade 9 book builds the chapter's history from these: ideas about the Universe changing as each witness arrived.
3. **Kepler's shape for the journey — and the honest order** — The journey's path is Kepler's: the orbit is an ellipse with the Sun at one focus (first law); and the same motion gives the seasons only through the axis tilt, not distance — Earth is nearest the Sun in January. The book's history runs honestly: geocentric models predicted much, but parallax's absence was their fair defence until instruments grew fine enough; heliocentrism won on evidence accumulated, not declared. This lesson's habit — name the witness, state what it rules out — is the astronomy chapter's method entire.
