# How we know the Earth turns and travels round the Sun — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the Foucault argument in two sentences.
**Answer: The pendulum's swing plane is not touched by any force that could rotate it, yet relative to the ground it steadily turns (fully, in a day, at the pole). The plane stays; therefore the ground — the Earth — is what turns.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why was the absence of observed parallax a fair defence of the geocentric view for so long?
**Answer: If Earth swung across its orbit, near stars should shift — none was seen. The heliocentric answer (stars too far for the shift to show) was true but unproven until instruments fine enough, in 1838, finally caught the tiny shift.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Use aberration to argue the observer moves.
**Answer: All stars appear tipped slightly toward the direction of Earth's travel, the tilt reversing over the year. A stationary observer has no travel direction to tip them toward; the slant is the signature of the observer's own motion, like slanted rain on a walker.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the three rotation witnesses and the three orbit witnesses as the lesson lists them.
**Answer: Rotation: polar flattening/equatorial bulge; Foucault pendulum; Coriolis deflection. Orbit: stellar parallax; aberration of starlight; the seasons' geometry under a tilted axis.**
