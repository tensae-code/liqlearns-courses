# Highlands and lowlands

## Do it first
Stand in your mind on flat ground by the sea. Now stand on high ground far above it, where the wind is colder. The land under your feet in the two places has two different names. Say what you think each name is. Then read on: Iceland's Grade 3 Earth chapter teaches getting to know the difference between highland and lowland.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lowland — the low ground

**Lowland** is low ground — the flat or gently rolling land down near the sea and along the valleys, where farms and towns most easily sit. Low is its one defining mark: height above the sea is small.

## Sub lesson 2 — Highland — the high ground, and the difference

**Highland** is high ground — the raised country above the lowland, higher, colder and emptier. The lesson's one thing is the **difference**: the same country's land sorts by height into lowland below and highland above, and Iceland's chapter teaches children to know the two apart on their own island. The fuller relief set (hillocks, hills, mountains, plains, plateaus) is stocked in `earth-and-space/landforms-hills-plains-and-valleys`.

## The key ideas

- Lowland is low ground — flat or gently rolling land down near the sea, small in height above it.
- Highland is high ground — the raised country above the lowland.
- A country's land sorts by height: lowland below, highland above — knowing the difference is the lesson.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Lowland — the low ground first, then Highland — the high ground, and the difference. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What single thing tells highland from lowland?" Answer: Height — how high the ground stands above the sea. Low ground is lowland; the raised country above it is highland.
