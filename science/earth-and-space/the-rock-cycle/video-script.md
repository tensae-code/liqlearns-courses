Today: **The rock cycle** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Grade 7).

Every rock belongs to one of three families, told by how it formed: **igneous** rock, born when melted rock cools and hardens; **sedimentary** rock, built from settled layers of material pressed together; **metamorphic** rock, an older rock remade by heat and pressure without melting (Grade 7, 7.2.1, printed p. 49). The families are the cycle's stations — the states rock matter can be in.

What moves rock between the families is the lesson's engine: **energy flow and matter cycling create igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks** — and destroy and remake them. Melted rock cools to igneous; igneous rock breaks down, settles and presses into sedimentary; buried and heated, either becomes metamorphic; melted again, the round restarts. There is no first rock and no last: it is a cycle, and energy flowing through the Earth is what drives it round.
