| fossils defined: a fossil is the remains of a living being, or of its biological activity, that has been embedded in a rock — the marks the first living beings left of their passage (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 119 |
| how the Earth's age is known: radioactivity is the proof — geologists calculate the age of rocks directly from their uranium and lead content, and the planet's age was estimated by lead-lead isotope dating on meteorites, including the Canyon Diablo meteorite, in work published in 1956 (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 119 |
| geological time is divided by great changes — movement of the continental masses, climate changes, formation of mountain ranges and the predominant type of life — into eras of very different durations (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 119 |
| the Azoic era ("without life"): from the Earth's appearance 4,600 million years ago, as the liquid magma materials cooled and the first igneous rocks formed; then the Archaean era, with volcanoes, oceans and high mountains appearing, and in the oceans the first manifestations of life — unicellular organisms and bacteria (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 120 |
| the Proterozoic era: great cratons gave rise to the continental platforms, and corals, marine sponges and the first rooted plants appeared; the Palaeozoic begins with shallow seas invading the continents, molluscs and fish predominating (some 360 million years), and later the lands rose, great forests of ferns and conifers formed, insects appeared in a humid climate, and the first amphibians arose (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 120 |
| the Mesozoic era, the age of the dinosaurs: great reptiles predominated on land and in lakes, with great volcanic activity; the highest mountains of today (Alps, Himalayas) formed, and the first mammals appeared; then the Cenozoic, the age of mammals, when the lands rose further, most domestic animals arose, and the glaciations determined the distribution of species — and the current era is registered from the appearance of Homo sapiens (Honduras — HN CN7) | Honduras, *HN CN7*, printed p. 120 |

| geological time runs from the Earth's formation some 4,600 million years ago and is measured in millions of years from evidence in the rocks (their age and disposition) and the fossils; fossils' three geological uses: interpreting the geological past, serving as important temporal indicators, and correlating rocks from different places (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN7 Guide) | Honduras, *CN7 Guide*, guide PDF p. 82 |
| the book's era scheme with its dates: Azoic (the oldest, without life), Archeozoic (great foldings and cataclysms forming the main mountain chains), Proterozoic (the great cratons — rigid continental masses since unaffected by orogenic movements), Paleozoic (began 570 million years ago, ended some 240 million ago), Mesozoic (the age of the dinosaurs, some 200 to 70 million years ago, beginning with intense volcanic activity), Cenozoic (the age of the mammals) and the Actual era (glaciers covering a quarter of the surface) (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN7 Guide) | Honduras, *CN7 Guide*, guide PDF p. 82 |
| dinosaurs in Honduras: in 1971 a fossil bone of a terrestrial dinosaur was discovered in Honduras, documented in studies of the stratigraphy, sedimentology and palaeoenvironment of the Esquías Formation; events key: Homo sapiens appeared in the Actual era, mammals arose in the Cenozoic, corals and marine sponges appeared at the beginnings of the Paleozoic, and the Azoic is "the era without life" (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN7 Guide) | Honduras, *CN7 Guide*, guide PDF p. 83 |
