# Lesson plan — Comparing the planets — a model of distance, size and behaviour

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Comparing the planets — a model of distance, size and behaviour
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — the title opened by the Grade 7 Earth lessons (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Solar System, at General Science Grade 8 depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** TEACHER-EVIDENCE LESSON: the guide teaches planet comparison as model-building — one scaled model, built on the playground or with string and a bead, carries every comparison of distance, size, position and behaviour. (The student book carries this material only as review questions.)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 8 planet lessons: the learner knows the planets one by one; this lesson, standing on teacher-guide evidence only, puts them all into one model that can be walked.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Building one model for every comparison** — building one model for every comparison part of the lesson.
2. **Reading the planets off the model** — reading the planets off the model part of the lesson.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Grade 8 Earth and Space lessons in this title, in unit order. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain why one model serves every comparison.
2. Fix a scale for distances and sizes and defend it.
3. Build the model on a playground or with string and a bead.
4. Summarise each planet's behaviour from the model.

## What you need
String, beads and balls of different sizes, and a playground or a long corridor.
