Today: **Volcanoes and earthquakes** — Australia's way, from its Grade 9 Pearson Science 9 (printed p. 414).

A **volcano** is an opening in the Earth's surface where melted rock from below pushes up and erupts — flowing out as lava, or bursting out with ash and gas. The melted rock was always there under the ground; the volcano is where the moving Earth lets it out. Australia's book teaches volcanoes inside its earth chapter (10.3) because a volcano is plate movement made visible: fire at the surface, driven from below.
An **earthquake** is the ground shaking suddenly. The plates of the section before move slowly and steadily, but where moving ground sticks and then lets go all at once, the stored movement is released in seconds — and the surface shakes. Same chapter, same cause family as the volcano: slow movement below, sudden event above.
Australia's order is the lesson: moving continents (10.1) say the ground travels; plate movements (10.2) say how; volcanoes and earthquakes (10.3) are what that travelling does where people live — one by opening the ground to fire, the other by shaking it. Two events, one moving Earth beneath both.
