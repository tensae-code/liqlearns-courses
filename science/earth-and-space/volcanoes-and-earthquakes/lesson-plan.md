# Lesson plan — Volcanoes and earthquakes

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Volcanoes and earthquakes
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — appended to Unit 19 (Earth and Space) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** volcanoes and earthquakes — the two sudden surface events of the Earth's slow movement below — taught together by Australia's Grade 9 Pearson Science 9 at the end of its moving-ground chapter (10.3), after moving continents (10.1) and plate movements (10.2)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Volcanoes — fire from below**
2. **Earthquakes — the ground lets go suddenly**
3. **Together at the chapter's end — what movement does**

## Before this lesson
- No required first lesson. Recommended, never forced: the neighbouring Earth and Space lessons that touch the same ground.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Volcanoes — fire from below.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Earthquakes — the ground lets go suddenly.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: Together at the chapter's end — what movement does.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Keeps the two events in separate boxes → return to the chapter's order: one moving Earth below; the volcano opens it, the earthquake shakes it.
