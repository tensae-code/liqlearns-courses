# Measuring distances in astronomy — the parallax method

## Do it first
Why does a near star shift more than a distant one between January and July photographs? — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Parallax — the shift of the near against the far

Hold a finger before your face and blink between eyes: the finger jumps against the wall. That jump is parallax — the apparent shift of a near object seen from two viewpoints, measured against far, unmoving references. In astronomy the two viewpoints are two places on Earth (for the Moon and planets) or two opposite points of Earth's orbit (for stars). The wider the baseline and the nearer the body, the bigger the shift; the whole method is turning that measured angle into a distance by triangulation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Horizontal parallax — the Solar System

For the Moon, Sun and planets the Kazakh books use horizontal parallax: the angle under which Earth's radius would be seen from the body — equivalently, the shift of the body against the stars between two observatories a known distance apart. The Moon's parallax is nearly a degree, a huge shift, giving its distance at once; the Sun's is under nine arcseconds, which is why the astronomical unit was the last Solar-System distance to be pinned down. Every planet distance in the course's tables stands on some rung of this ladder.

## Sub lesson 3 — Annual parallax and the parsec

For stars the baseline is Earth's orbit itself: photograph a near star, wait six months, photograph it again — it has shifted against the distant stars by twice its annual parallax. The angles are crumbs: the nearest star's parallax is 0.75 arcseconds. The parsec is defined by the method — the distance at which the parallax is exactly one arcsecond (about 3.26 light-years). The Kazakh books set the parsec as the working unit of the stellar world, and parallax remains the foundation rung: every other cosmic distance is calibrated against the stars parallax has measured.
