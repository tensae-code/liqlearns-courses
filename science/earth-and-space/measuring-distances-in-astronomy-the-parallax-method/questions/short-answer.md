# Measuring distances in astronomy — the parallax method — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain the finger-and-two-eyes demonstration and name the astronomical quantity each part stands for.
**Answer:** The finger is the near body, the wall the distant stars, the two eyes the two viewpoints, and the finger's jump against the wall the parallax angle. In astronomy the viewpoints are observatories or Earth at opposite sides of its orbit.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the baseline for stars Earth's orbit, when Earth's radius worked for the Moon?
**Answer:** Stellar parallax is so tiny that the widest possible baseline is needed to make it measurable. Earth's radius would give shifts thousands of times smaller still; the orbit's diameter (about 300 million km) is the largest baseline we have.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A star has a parallax of 0.25 arcseconds. Find its distance in parsecs and in light-years.
**Answer:** Distance = 1/p parsecs = 1/0.25 = 4 pc. In light-years: 4 × 3.26 ≈ 13 light-years.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does every other cosmic distance method lean on parallax?
**Answer:** Parallax is the only direct, geometry-only measurement — it assumes nothing about the body itself. Cepheid and other standard-candle distances are calibrated on nearby stars whose distances parallax fixed, so the whole ladder rests on it.
