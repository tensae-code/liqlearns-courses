# Video script — The geological eras

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **The geological eras** — Italy's way, from its Grade 9 Il globo terrestre e la sua evoluzione, Edizione blu (printed pp. 264–290).

Italy's history of the Earth divides deep time into named stretches. The **Phanerozoic** is the current **eon** — the largest stretch — and inside it the book teaches its eras in order, beginning with the **Palaeozoic era**, the first of the three. Hold the frame before the detail: an eon holds eras, and the Phanerozoic's story is told era by era.
The Phanerozoic continues: after the Palaeozoic comes the **Mesozoic era**, and after it the **Cenozoic era** — the era that runs up to the present. Three eras in one fixed order — Palaeozoic, Mesozoic, Cenozoic — carry the Phanerozoic from its beginning to today, and the book teaches them as one sequence inside the same stretch (printed pp. 264–276): the order itself is the skeleton every later date hangs on.
The book closes the eon with its most recent stretch: the **Quaternary — the last 2,600,000 years**. This near past is read by **isotope stratigraphy** — dating and matching layers by what their isotopes record. Then the whole history is gathered into a **synoptic history of the Earth** — the full sequence seen at once — and brought home: the **geological history of Italy**, the same deep-time story told for Italy's own territory. The lesson's order is Italy's order: eon, eras, the recent past, then home.
