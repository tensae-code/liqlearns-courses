# Searching for life elsewhere in the universe — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the search begin with a “shopping list” instead of pointing telescopes at random?
**Answer: Because life needs water, chemistry and energy together; listing those conditions first tells us which few places, among billions, are worth the closest look.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How can a probe “taste” Enceladus’s ocean without drilling through its ice?
**Answer: Enceladus sprays its ocean into space through geysers at its south pole; a probe flying through the spray can analyse the water directly.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What would make an atmosphere a strong — not yet conclusive — sign of life?
**Answer: Gases together in proportions non-living chemistry cannot maintain (such as oxygen with methane), persisting in the measurements — a chemical imbalance that life is the best explanation for.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the two main ways exoplanets are detected.
**Answer: The transit method (a regular dip in the star’s light as the planet crosses it) and the wobble method (the star’s spectrum shifting as the planet’s gravity tugs it).**
