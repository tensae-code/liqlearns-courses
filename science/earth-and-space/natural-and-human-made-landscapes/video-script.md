Today: **Natural and human-made landscapes** — Honduras's way.

Lesson 12: a natural landscape holds nature's elements (pine, mahogany and cedar trees, mountains, plateaus, valleys, rivers, lakes, seas, rocks, soil); a human-made (artificial) landscape is transformed by houses, buildings, hydroelectric dams, farming, roads and bridges; hurricanes and tropical storms also change landscapes. natural versus artificial elements: matter is everything we can touch and is classified into natural (resources found available in nature) and artificial (products created by human beings); raw materials (materias primas) are materials extracted from nature with a purpose — like the sheep's wool used to make textiles, or petroleum, a resource exploited to produce many synthetic materials. raw materials by origin, with their products: of plant origin (linen, cotton, wood, maize, seeds, wheat, fruits and vegetables), animal origin (wool, leather, silk, skins, milk and meat), mineral origin (iron, copper, gold, silver, marble, silicon, lead and tin) and fossil origin (petroleum and natural gas); clay — a type of soil or sedimentary rock, of diverse colourings according to its impurities, from orange-red to white when pure — is used to make bricks, kitchen utensils and art objects; iron is extracted fundamentally from the mines, for locks, tools, bridges and kitchen utensils; wood is obtained from the trees' trunks, for furniture, houses, toys, musical instruments and paper; and maize goes into cereals, flour, starches and biscuits.
