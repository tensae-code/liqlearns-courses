| natural versus artificial elements: matter is everything we can touch and is classified into natural (resources found available in nature) and artificial (products created by human beings); raw materials (materias primas) are materials extracted from nature with a purpose — like the sheep's wool used to make textiles, or petroleum, a resource exploited to produce many synthetic materials (guide evidence) (Honduras — HN CN1 Guide) | Honduras, *HN CN1 Guide*, printed p. 88 |
| raw materials by origin, with their products: of plant origin (linen, cotton, wood, maize, seeds, wheat, fruits and vegetables), animal origin (wool, leather, silk, skins, milk and meat), mineral origin (iron, copper, gold, silver, marble, silicon, lead and tin) and fossil origin (petroleum and natural gas); clay — a type of soil or sedimentary rock, of diverse colourings according to its impurities, from orange-red to white when pure — is used to make bricks, kitchen utensils and art objects; iron is extracted fundamentally from the mines, for locks, tools, bridges and kitchen utensils; wood is obtained from the trees' trunks, for furniture, houses, toys, musical instruments and paper; and maize goes into cereals, flour, starches and biscuits (guide evidence) (Honduras — HN CN1 Guide) | Honduras, *HN CN1 Guide*, printed p. 88 |
| humanised landscapes in three types: agrarian landscapes (natural spaces transformed to cultivate the land or feed livestock — the fields, villages, paths and roads), industrial landscapes (natural areas converted to install factories) and urban landscapes (natural spaces transformed into cities — gardens, plazas, parks) (guide evidence) (Honduras — HN CN1 Guide) | Honduras, *HN CN1 Guide*, printed p. 89 |
| rural and urban environments (guide evidence): human beings need to establish themselves in a determined place to perform the activities letting them survive — those sites are called settlements (asentamientos), and they can be rural or urban zones; the urban zones comprise the city and its surroundings, characterised by a high population concentration and continuous housing constructions, where activities linked to industry, commerce, public administration and government predominate, with more employment, a more developed economy, technology and science, and a faster life rhythm, with places for diversion and rest (parks, theatres, cinemas, museums); the rural zones have dispersed, scarce populations in small villages, where the inhabitants perform agricultural, livestock, forestry, fishing and mining economic activities (and beekeeping or reforestation), the communication routes are often of earth, houses are built of wood, bahareque (wattle-and-daub), stones and straw or zinc roofs, the environment suffers less damage and life is lived at a calmer rhythm, with less technology and fewer employment possibilities; and the two complement each other: in the villages the food consumed in the cities is cultivated and the raw materials extracted, while in the urban spaces the services and industries develop (Honduras — HN CN1 Guide) | Honduras, *HN CN1 Guide*, printed p. 152 |
