# Earth science — the Earth's materials

## Do it first
Before you read: step outside and pick up a stone. What is it made of? Where did it come from before it was a stone in your hand, and what will it become long after you put it down? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this lesson is Ghana's way in — its junior high curriculum files **Earth science** as a sub-strand of Strand 2, Cycles, at Basic 7.

## Sub lesson 1 — Earth science — studying the Earth itself

Every science takes one great thing as its study. **Earth science** takes the Earth itself — the whole planet as one subject: its land and what the land is made of, its waters, and the air wrapped around it.

Earth science asks the planet's own questions. What is the ground made of? Where does rain go? Why does a hillside slide? The answers begin with the Earth's materials — and with watching how those materials change, because on Earth nothing stays still for long.

That is why Ghana's curriculum places Earth science inside the **Cycles** strand: the Earth's story is a story of things going round — water rising and falling as rain, rock breaking down and building up again.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Earth's materials

The Earth is made of materials, and four great families of them meet the eye:

1. **Rock** — the hard solid of the ground itself, the mountains and the stones.
2. **Soil** — rock broken small over long ages, mixed with the remains of living things; the skin plants grow in.
3. **Water** — the seas, rivers, lakes and rain; the material that travels furthest and fastest.
4. **Air** — the gases around the Earth; unseen, but a material all the same — it pushes, it carries weather, it is breathed.

None of the four sits still. Water wears rock down; broken rock becomes soil; soil washes to the sea and settles; air moves water as clouds and rain. Earth science is the study of these materials and their endless changing — the Earth's materials, cycling.

## The key ideas

- **Earth science** studies the Earth itself: its land, its waters and its air.
- The Earth's great materials are **rock, soil, water and air**.
- The materials **cycle** — each one changes into the next's business, which is why Earth science stands in the Cycles strand.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Earth science first, then the four materials. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Which material am I holding?" — holding a stone, a handful of soil, a cup of water, or nothing visible at all. Answer each one.
