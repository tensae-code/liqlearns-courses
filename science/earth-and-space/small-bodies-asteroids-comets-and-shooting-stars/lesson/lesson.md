# Small bodies — asteroids, comets, and shooting stars that land

## Do it first

Watch a 'shooting star' in your mind: a streak, then gone. Was that thing a star? Where was it when it streaked — and what would it be called if it reached the ground? This lesson names it three times, once for each place.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pluto's demotion and the asteroid belt

[Picture 34508 — the three-in-one diagram]

**Pluto** was counted a planet for about **76 years** — then, in **2006**, it was reclassified a **dwarf planet**. Were scientists simply wrong before? No: the case shows how scientific categories **change when evidence grows** — Pluto turned out to be one of many small icy bodies, not a ninth planet like the eight. The **asteroids** are rocky or metallic bodies orbiting the Sun, most of them in the **belt between Mars and Jupiter**; the largest is about **a quarter of the Moon's diameter**, and some cross Earth's path.

## Sub lesson 2 — Comets, and the rock with three names

**Comets** are balls of ice and rock from the system's outer fringes. Near the Sun their ice turns **straight to gas**, and radiation and sunlight push that gas into a **tail that always points away from the Sun**. **Halley's comet** — recorded since **240 B.C.** and predicted by Halley for **1758** — returns every **75–76 years**. Last, the naming chain: a small rocky traveller in space is a **meteoroid**; burning in the atmosphere it is a **meteor** — the bright streak, whose old impacts cratered the Moon; surviving to strike the ground, it is a **meteorite**. The name follows the location.
