# Earthquakes and tsunamis

## Do it first
Before you read: Why are earthquakes and tsunamis taught as one lesson, not two? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is the Dominican Republic's way in — its Libro Abierto Ciencias de la Naturaleza books teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Earthquakes — the ground lets go

An **earthquake** is a sudden shaking of the ground. Deep in the Earth's crust, rock can be squeezed and strained until it breaks along a crack called a **fault** — and when the rock on the two sides of the fault slips, the ground shakes.

The shaking travels out from the break as **seismic waves** — waves through the rock itself, spreading in every direction from where the slip happened. The further the waves travel, the weaker the shaking they carry.

Because the waves can be felt and measured far from the break, scientists record them with an instrument called a **seismograph**, which draws the shaking as a wavy line. Reading that line tells them an earthquake has happened, how strong the shaking was, and where it came from. The Dominican Republic's Grade 5 book teaches the chain in that order: faults, seismic waves, seismographs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tsunamis — the sea wave that follows

When an earthquake happens under the sea, the sudden movement of the sea floor can push the water above it — and that push can set off a **tsunami**, called a **maremoto** in the Dominican Republic's books: a great, fast sea wave that travels across the ocean.

Out at sea the wave can pass almost unnoticed, but as it reaches shallow water at a coast it grows tall and rushes inland. That is why the Grade 3 book teaches earthquakes and tsunamis together: the shaking under the sea and the wave at the shore are two halves of one event.

For an island country, the pair matters together — the earthquake is the ground's event, and the tsunami is the sea's answer to it.

## The key ideas

- An **earthquake** is a sudden shaking of the ground when rock slips along a **fault**.
- **Seismic waves** carry the shaking through the rock, and a **seismograph** records them.
- A **tsunami (maremoto)** is a great sea wave set off by an earthquake under the sea.
- The Dominican Republic's books teach earthquakes and tsunamis together, as one joined hazard for an island.
