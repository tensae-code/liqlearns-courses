# Weathering, erosion and landforms

## Do it first
Look at any landscape — a hill, a valley, a plain. Two different kinds of work made it look as it does: work from inside the Earth that raised and formed it, and work from outside that has been reshaping it ever since. Which kind of work do you think you can see the marks of? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mexico's way in — relief taught through its generating and its modelling together.

## Sub lesson 1 — Internal factors that generate relief

Relief — the shapes of the land and the sea floor — is first **generated** from inside the Earth: the **internal factors** are the relief-makers. Before any surface can be worn or reshaped, it must be raised and formed, and that forming work comes from inside.

## Sub lesson 2 — Continental and ocean relief forms

The generated relief takes forms in two places: **continental relief** — the shapes on the land — and **ocean relief** — the shapes under the sea. One relief story, two settings: what stands on the continents and what lies on the ocean floor belong to the same lesson.

## Sub lesson 3 — External factors that model relief

Generated relief does not stay as it was made: **external factors model it** — working on it from outside, reshaping the forms over time. Hold the two hands together: internal factors generate relief; external factors model it. That pair is the whole lesson.

## The key ideas

- **Internal factors that generate relief** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Continental and ocean relief forms** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **External factors that model relief** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Did this factor generate the relief, or model it?" Answer: Generating is the internal work that forms relief; modelling is the external work that reshapes it afterwards.
