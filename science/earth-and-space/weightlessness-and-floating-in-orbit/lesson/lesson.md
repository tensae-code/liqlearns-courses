# Weightlessness and floating in orbit

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the thing this lesson is about, from your own life. Say out loud what you already know about it. Then read on: this is Pakistan (Punjab)'s way in — its Punjab Single National Curriculum *General Science* books teach this as its own lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Weight needs something to push against

Grade 7 first separates mass from weight (§12.2): mass is how much matter a thing has; weight is gravity's pull on it, felt because the ground, a chair or a floor pushes back and stops the fall. Stand still and you feel your weight in that push-back.

## Sub lesson 2 — Falling together — weightlessness

§12.3 teaches weightlessness with the falling-bottle activities: when a bottle and the water in it fall together, the water no longer presses on the bottle or pours as it does at rest — bottle, water and any falling rider fall at the same rate, nothing pushes back, and weight is not felt. That shared fall, kept going around the Earth, is the floating of orbit.

## The key ideas

- **Mass** stays the same; **weight** is gravity's pull, felt through the push-back of what holds you up.
- When everything **falls together** — bottle and water, craft and crew — nothing pushes back, and weight is not felt: **weightlessness**.
- Orbit is that fall kept going: falling around the Earth, so the floating never ends.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Does weightlessness mean gravity has stopped?" Answer from the lesson: No — from the lesson: gravity is still pulling; the falling-bottle activities show bottle and water falling together under gravity. Weight is not felt because nothing pushes back, not because the pull is gone.
