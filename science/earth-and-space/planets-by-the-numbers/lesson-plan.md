# Lesson plan — Planets by the numbers — the data-table investigation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Planets by the numbers — the data-table investigation
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — the title opened by the Grade 7 Earth lessons (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Solar System, at General Science Grade 8 depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one table — density, diameter, Sun-distance, day length and year length for all eight planets — is interrogated until it gives up the two families, the floating planet, the fastest spinner, the fastest orbiter, the planet whose year is shorter than its day, and the pattern behind Kepler's third law.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on both planet-tour lessons: the learner knows the planets; this lesson reads them as numbers. (The distance figures taught here are the corrected ones — see sources.)

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Grade 8 Earth and Space lessons in this title, in unit order. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Saturn as the only planet less dense than water (687 kg/m³).
2. Say which family is denser, and why.
3. Say which family spins fastest and which planet orbits fastest.
4. Identify Venus as the planet whose year is shorter than its day, and state the distance-cubed/period-squared pattern.

## What you need
A calculator or squared paper for the distance-cubed against period-squared plot.
