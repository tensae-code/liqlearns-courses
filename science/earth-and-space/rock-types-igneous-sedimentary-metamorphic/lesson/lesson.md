# Rock types — igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic

## Do it first
Pick up three different stones: one glassy and dark, one lying in flat layers, one banded and twisted. They are all rock — so why do they look as if they were made in completely different ways? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Costa Rica's way in — its Grade 8 Ciencias sorts all rocks into three types, each named by how it forms.

## Sub lesson 1 — Igneous rocks — born from melted rock

**Igneous rocks** form when **melted rock cools and hardens**. Melted rock below the surface, or lava poured out above it, loses its heat, solidifies, and becomes igneous rock.

Cooling is the making to hold: wherever rock was once molten and is now solid, an igneous rock stands.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sedimentary rocks — built from settled layers

**Sedimentary rocks** form from **sediment**: material worn from older rock, carried, and let fall in layers, which are then buried and pressed into solid rock.

The layered making is taught in full in the neighbouring lesson `earth-and-space/sedimentary-rocks-and-how-they-form`; here the sedimentary type takes its place as one of the three.

## Sub lesson 3 — Metamorphic rocks — changed by heat and pressure

**Metamorphic rocks** form when an **existing rock is changed** — buried, squeezed and heated until its material is remade into a new rock, without melting back into liquid.

So the three types are three makings: cooled from melt, built from sediment, changed by heat and pressure. Any rock a learner meets can be asked one question — how were you formed? — and its answer names its type.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Igneous rocks — born from melted rock first, then Sedimentary rocks — built from settled layers first, then Metamorphic rocks — changed by heat and pressure. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A rock's layers show it was built from settled sediment. Which type is it, and how do the other two form?" Answer: Sedimentary. Igneous rocks form when melted rock cools and hardens; metamorphic rocks form when existing rock is changed by heat and pressure.
