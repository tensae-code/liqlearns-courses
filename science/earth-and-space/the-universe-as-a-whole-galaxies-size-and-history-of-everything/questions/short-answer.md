# The universe as a whole — galaxies, size and history of everything — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the Milky Way seen as a pale band across the night sky?
**Answer: We are inside the galaxy's flat disc, looking along it: in that direction we look through the crowded plane of its stars, which merge into a continuous pale band; looking out of the plane, the stars thin out quickly.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) The observable universe is about 93 billion light-years across, yet the universe is only about 13.8 billion years old. How can both be true?
**Answer: Space itself expanded while the light was travelling: the matter that emitted the oldest light we receive has been carried far beyond 13.8 billion light-years from us by the expansion, so the observable sphere is wider than the age times the speed of light.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What is the evidence that the universe had a hot, dense beginning?
**Answer: Galaxies are all receding in an expanding universe whose history runs back to a single hot, dense state, and the sky still carries that state's cooled afterglow — the cosmic microwave background — exactly as the hot-beginning story predicts.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) On the balloon model of expansion, which dot is at the centre of the expansion?
**Answer: None of them: every dot sees every other dot recede, and the expansion happens everywhere on the surface at once — the model has no centre on the surface, just as the real expansion has no centre in space.**
