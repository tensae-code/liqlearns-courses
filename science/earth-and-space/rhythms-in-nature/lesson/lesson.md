# Rhythms in nature

## Do it first
Some things in nature happen once and never again. Others come back — morning after morning, spring after spring. Name one thing that comes back every day, and one that comes back every year. Then read on: this is Armenia's way in — its Grade 6 Nature book gives nature's repeating changes their own paragraph, §9, and calls the whole pattern nature's calendar.

## Sub lesson 1 — The short rhythms — the day and the year

Armenia's book opens with the rhythms everyone lives inside. The **daily rhythm**: light and dark, warming and cooling, waking and sleeping come back every day, driven by the Earth's turning. The **yearly rhythm**: the seasons return in their order — growth, ripening, rest — driven by the Earth's yearly travel round the Sun. A rhythm is not just a change: it is a change that **comes back**, in the same order, again and again.

## Sub lesson 2 — The long rhythms — centuries, and nature's calendar

Beyond the day and the year, the book names longer beats: **century rhythms** and **multi-century rhythms** — slow swings in nature that take lifetimes to complete a single turn. Short and long together make **nature's calendar**: the full pattern-book of when things happen in nature — and living things keep their lives by it, growing, breeding and resting to its beats.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Name one daily rhythm, one yearly rhythm, and one long rhythm." Answer: The day-and-night turn is daily; the seasons' return is yearly; the century and multi-century swings are the long rhythms — together, nature's calendar.
