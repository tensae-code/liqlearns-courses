# Earthquakes and the Richter scale

## Do it first
Two earthquakes shake two towns. One rattles the windows; the other brings down walls. Both are called by the same name — so how do we say, in a number, how much stronger one was than the other? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Costa Rica's way in — its Grade 8 Ciencias teaches earthquakes among the phenomena that alter the geosphere, and gives them a scale.

## Sub lesson 1 — Earthquakes — phenomena that alter the geosphere

An **earthquake** — a **sismo** — is a sudden shaking of the ground: rock below the surface moves, and the shaking spreads through the ground to the surface above.

Costa Rica's book places earthquakes among the **phenomena that alter the geosphere**: they are not only felt, they change the Earth's solid shell itself, and that is why they are studied with the Earth's layers, in the geosphere unit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Classifying earthquakes

Earthquakes are **classified** — sorted — so that one quake can be compared with another: by their strength, the shaking they cause, and the effects they leave.

Classification turns single frightening events into a study: once quakes are sorted, patterns can be seen and one event's size can be stated against another's.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Richter scale — measuring magnitude

The **Richter scale** is the scale that measures an earthquake's **magnitude** — its size as a number. A quake's Richter number lets any two earthquakes, anywhere, be compared on the one scale.

That number is the lesson's measuring tool: the bigger the Richter number, the stronger the earthquake — one agreed scale in place of 'big' and 'small' said by feel.
