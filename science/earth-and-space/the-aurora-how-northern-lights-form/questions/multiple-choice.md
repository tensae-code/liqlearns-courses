# The aurora — how northern lights form — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Every aurora begins with…
a) Charged particles streamed from the Sun — the solar wind  b) Moonlight on ice  c) Lightning in the upper air  d) Burning gases over the pole
**Answer: a) Charged particles streamed from the Sun — the solar wind**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A stormy Sun is usually followed, a night or two later, by…
a) Colder weather  b) A brighter aurora near the poles  c) Longer days  d) Eclipses
**Answer: b) A brighter aurora near the poles**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Auroras belong to the polar regions because…
a) It is colder there  b) The snow reflects the light  c) Earth's magnetic field lines dive in near the poles, funnelling particles down there  d) The Sun shines longer there
**Answer: c) Earth's magnetic field lines dive in near the poles, funnelling particles down there**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Auroras appear in rings around…
a) The equator  b) The magnetic poles  c) The tropics  d) Mountain ranges
**Answer: b) The magnetic poles**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) The aurora's green light comes from…
a) Nitrogen low down  b) Oxygen, given energy by particle collisions and shedding it as green light  c) Reflected moonlight  d) Ice crystals
**Answer: b) Oxygen, given energy by particle collisions and shedding it as green light**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) The gases of the upper air glow because…
a) They burn in the particle wind  b) They reflect the Sun like mirrors  c) Collisions hand them energy, which they shed as light  d) They are heated by friction only
**Answer: c) Collisions hand them energy, which they shed as light**
