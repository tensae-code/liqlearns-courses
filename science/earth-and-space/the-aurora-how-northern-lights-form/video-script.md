Today: **The aurora** — the northern lights, Finland's revontulet — how the sky itself can glow.

It starts at the Sun, which streams charged particles out into space. Earth's magnetic shield turns most of them aside — but near the poles, the field lines dive down, and the particles spiral down with them.
High over the polar night they crash into the air's gases — oxygen, nitrogen — and the gases give the energy back as light: green curtains, red tops, violet edges, rippling over the snow.
Particles, a magnetic funnel, glowing air. That is the whole recipe — and the far north is where it cooks.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
