# Hands-on — The aurora — how northern lights form

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The magnet's funnel
Lay a bar magnet under a sheet of paper and sprinkle iron filings on top. Tap gently. The filings draw the field: lines leaving one pole, curving round, and diving into the other. Say aloud: Earth's field is this picture at planet size — and where the lines dive in, at the poles, is where aurora particles come down.

## Colour cards
On paper, draw three aurora curtains: one green, one with a red top, one with a violet lower edge. Label each with its gas — oxygen for the green and the high red, nitrogen for the blue-violet edge — and with the collision that makes it glow.

## Watch brief
If you live — or are travelling — where auroras show, keep a one-week watch brief: note each night's sky (cloud, darkness) and any aurora seen, with its colours and shapes. Beside the brightest night, write the chain: what must have happened at the Sun, in the field, and in the air.
