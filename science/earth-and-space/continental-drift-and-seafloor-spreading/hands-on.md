# Hands-on — Continental drift and seafloor spreading

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The torn-page fit (paper and pencil)
Draw two rough continent shapes that face each other across a gap, in the style of a map torn in two. On each facing edge, mark the same fossil find and the same ancient-climate mark. Slide the two pieces together until the edges meet, and write one sentence under the drawing: what the matching marks prove, and what they cannot prove on their own.

## A spreading-speed sum (pencil only)
A band of ocean floor marked by a magnetic anomaly lies 100 km from the ridge it spread from, and the book's method dates that band at 10 million years. Divide distance by time: 100 km in 10 million years is 10 km per million years — 1 cm per year. Now do the same sum with your own two numbers, and say in one sentence why a speed this slow still opens an ocean.
