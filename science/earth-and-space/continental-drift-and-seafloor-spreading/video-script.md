Today: **Continental drift and seafloor spreading** — Italy's way, from its Grade 9 Il globo terrestre e la sua evoluzione, Edizione blu (printed pp. 201–212).

Italy's book opens the road to plate tectonics with **Wegener's "mobile Earth"**: the idea that the continents have not always stood where they stand now, but have moved. An idea this large needs proof, and the book gives two kinds. The **palaeontological proof**: the remains of the same ancient living things are found on continents now separated by whole oceans — the living things could not have crossed the ocean, so the continents must once have been joined. The **palaeoclimatic proof**: rocks carry the marks of ancient climates that do not fit where those rocks sit today — signs of climates out of place, explained if the continents themselves have travelled. Drift, in Italy's way, is not a guess: it is an idea carried by two independent lines of evidence.
The drift idea gains its engine on the ocean floor: **seafloor spreading**. The ocean floor is not still — it spreads, carrying the continents with it. This is the step that turns drift from a pattern on the map into a process: if the floor of the ocean spreads apart, the continents resting on the moving ground are carried apart too. Italy's book places spreading inside the same stretch as Wegener's proofs (printed pp. 201–208): first that the continents moved, then the moving floor that can move them.
The spreading floor keeps a record of itself. As ocean-floor rocks form, they become magnetised, and the ocean floor carries **magnetic anomalies** — bands where the magnetisation differs — laid out by the spreading itself. Because the record is written into the floor as it spreads, it can be read back as a measurement: from the magnetic anomalies, the book **calculates an ocean's spreading speed**. The proof of drift ends as a number — how fast an ocean opens.
