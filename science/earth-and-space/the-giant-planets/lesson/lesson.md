# The giant planets — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune

## Do it first

Look at the picture. Three giants stand upright. One is lying on its side, rolling. Find it before you read — that tipped planet is the one this lesson most wants you to remember.

## Sub lesson 1 — Jupiter and Saturn

[Picture 34506 — the four giants in a row to relative scale, rings on Saturn and tilted rings on sideways Uranus, Titan and the Great Dark Spot marked]

**Jupiter** is the largest planet, made of hydrogen and helium, with **16 moons as the book prints them** — the four Galilean moons above all: volcanic **Io**, rock-and-ice **Europa**, half-and-half **Ganymede**, icy **Callisto**. **Saturn** is the **lowest-density** planet: hydrogen-helium deepening to liquid over a possible rocky core. Its broad **rings** resolve into **thousands of ringlets** of ice and rock, from dust-specks to pieces tens of metres across. It has **at least 31 moons as printed**, and the standout is **Titan** — larger than Mercury, with an atmosphere of nitrogen, argon and methane.

## Sub lesson 2 — Uranus and Neptune

**Uranus**, found in **1781**, is blue-green because **methane absorbs red and yellow light**; it has a water-methane-ammonia mantle over rock, thin dark rings, and **at least 21 moons as printed**, cratered **Titania** among them. Its axis is **tipped nearly onto its side**, as if knocked over by a collision — it rolls around the Sun. **Neptune**, found in **1846**, has the same methane blue-green, an active changing atmosphere with an Earth-sized **Great Dark Spot** and bright fleeting clouds, **at least 11 moons as printed** including nitrogen-aired **Triton**, and young rings that will not last.
