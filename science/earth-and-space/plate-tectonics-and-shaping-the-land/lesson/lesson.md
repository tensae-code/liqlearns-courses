# Plate tectonics and shaping the land

## Do it first
Before you read: look at any land you know — a coast, a valley, a hill. Who or what made it that shape? Was it finished long ago, or is it still being shaped now? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Canada's way in — its Science First Peoples guides teach the shaping of the land twice over, once through the plates that built British Columbia and the hazards that come with them, and once through the thousands of years of First Peoples care that shaped the same land.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shaking and flooding — how plates build and shake the land

The ground is not one still sheet. It is made of great pieces — **tectonic plates** — and British Columbia was built where those plates meet. Off the coast, the **Juan de Fuca plate** is sliding under the land plate: one plate going down under another is called **subduction**.

That slow building is still at work. **Volcanism** — melted rock rising and erupting — belongs to the plate-built coast. And the surface is reworked after the building: **glaciation**, great ice moving over the land, and **erosion**, water and weather wearing it down, shape what the plates raised.

A land built by moving plates also moves suddenly. The same unit names the hazards plainly: **earthquakes**, when the stuck ground lets go; **tsunamis**, great sea waves raised by a quake under the ocean; **landslides**, slopes that give way; and **flooding**. So the unit does not end with the science — it ends with **preparedness**: knowing which hazards your plate-built land carries, and being ready for them before they come.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shaping the land by care — clam gardens, middens and burning

The Secondary guide asks the same question — how are landscapes shaped? — and answers it a second way: people have shaped this land for thousands of years, on purpose, and the shaping itself can be studied.

**Clam gardens** are shore places built and tended so that clams grow well — the shore itself reworked by care. **Shell middens** are the deep shell layers laid down where people lived and ate for generation after generation — a landscape record written in shells. And **landscape burning** is fire used deliberately, to manage what grows on the land.

Together these are **millennia of land management**: not accidents on the land, but shaping done with knowledge, repeated and handed down. Read with sub lesson 1, the lesson's whole point stands: the land is shaped from below by plates, at the surface by ice and water, and by people's long care — and a science of the land has to hold all three.

## The key ideas

- British Columbia was **built by tectonic plates**; the **Juan de Fuca plate** subducts off its coast.
- **Volcanism, glaciation and erosion** keep shaping the plate-built land.
- The moving ground brings **earthquakes, tsunamis, landslides and flooding** — so the unit closes on **preparedness**.
- People also shape land over **millennia**: **clam gardens**, **shell middens** and **landscape burning** are land management, studied as science.
