| Lesson part | Where it comes from |

| the crust as a dynamic system: the planet's surface is in constant change — rock disintegrates and decomposes, moves to lower zones by gravity and is transported by water, wind or ice, sculpting the Earth's physical landscape; some parts of the surface rise gradually by mountain formation and volcanism, processes carried out inside the Earth and fed by its internal energy, while contrary external processes constantly break the rock and displace the debris to lower zones (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 95 |
| the external processes named — gravitational processes, erosion and weathering (meteorización) — occur at or near the surface and are fed by solar energy; they are a basic element of the rock cycle, responsible for transforming solid rock into sediment (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 95 |
| gravitational processes defined: the downhill movement of rock, regolith and soil by the direct influence of gravity, distinguished from the erosive processes because they need no transporting medium like water, wind or ice; together with surface water currents, groundwater, wind, waves and glaciers they alter the terrestrial landscape; gravity controls them, but other factors favour them — tectonic instability (fractures and joints raise their probability), interstitial water (water retained in the spaces between the rock's particles, which reduces the materials' resistance and facilitates sliding), the rock's structure, and creep (reptación — the slow displacement whose evidence is the bent trunks of trees) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed pp. 95–96 |
| weathering (meteorización): the rock is fragmented mechanically or its chemical composition is altered, or both — illustrated with a sheet of paper, which can be disintegrated by tearing it into ever smaller pieces (mechanical) or decomposed by burning it (chemical); mechanical weathering breaks rocks into ever smaller fragments, each conserving the original material's characteristics (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 101 |
| the four mechanical weathering processes: expansion by decompression (when enormous masses of igneous rock, especially granite, are exposed by erosion they release in concentric slabs — lajeamiento, sheeting — because the great decrease of pressure lets the outer layers expand more than the rock beneath, eventually separating into exfoliation domes), thermal expansion, fragmentation by ice, and biological activity (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 101 |
| the rock cycle walked through whole: magma (molten rock formed at great depths) cools and solidifies — crystallisation — whether inside the crust or after a volcanic eruption at the surface, and the resulting rocks are igneous; exposed at the surface they suffer weathering — the atmosphere's elements slowly disintegrating and decomposing them — and the resulting materials are carried downhill by gravity and transported by surface waters, wind, glaciers or waves, and at last deposited as sediments in oceans, rivers, deserts, swamps and dunes; the sediments then undergo lithification, giving a sedimentary rock; buried deeply or caught in orogenesis or by a mass of magma, that rock is subjected to great pressures or intense heat and becomes a metamorphic rock, and under still greater pressure or temperature it melts into magma again (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 107 |
| the three rock families classified and the igneous intrusions pictured: igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic; an igneous body inside the crust is drawn as batholith (the great mass), dike, laccolith and sill (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 108 |
| sedimentary rocks: formed from sediment — the solid material deposited by a fluid, water or air (desert sand, swamp-bottom mud, riverbank and riverbed gravel, even household dust are sediments; the black beach sand of Amapala is formed of basalt fragments); the products of mechanical and chemical weathering are the basic material of the sedimentary rocks (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 113 |
| diagenesis and lithification: diagenesis is all the chemical, physical and biological changes occurring after the sediments' deposition, promoted by burial as temperatures and pressures rise; lithification — the transformation of unconsolidated sediments into solid sedimentary rock — has two basic processes: compaction (as sediment accumulates, the overlying material grows heavier and compresses the deeper sediments — the deeper the burial, the more compact) and cementation (materials of high cementing capacity carried in solution pass through the sediments and deposit between their pores, binding them into hard rock) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 113 |
| sediments have two principal origins: detrital (accumulations of material originated and transported as solid particles derived from mechanical and chemical weathering — the detritus forming detrital deposits) and chemical; the lesson's pictured sedimentary rocks are a lutita (shale) with a fossilised leaf and an arenisca (sandstone) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 114 |
| metamorphic rocks: metamorphism means "change of form" — the transformation of one rock type into another; metamorphic rocks form from pre-existing rocks — igneous, sedimentary or other metamorphic — altered by heat, pressure and chemically active fluids, a process that changes the minerals, the texture and often the chemical composition; unlike igneous and sedimentary rocks, which form at or near the surface, metamorphic rocks form mostly in deep zones of the Earth's interior (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 119 |
| the three metamorphism environments: contact or thermal (a rock in contact with a magma), hydrothermal (hot water rich in ions circulating through the rock's cracks, producing chemical alteration) and regional (during the formation of mountains, where great rock masses suffer directed pressures and elevated temperatures with large-scale deformations — where the greatest quantity of metamorphic rocks is produced); the most important factor is heat, which provides the energy promoting the chemical changes that recrystallise the existing minerals or form new ones; the pictured transformations: clay (sedimentary) into slate (pizarra), basalt (volcanic) into green schist (esquisto verde) (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 119 |
