# Identifying rocks by colour, grain and hardness

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — identifying collected rocks — granite, sandstone, shale, limestone and marble — by colour, grain, texture and hardness, with the scratch test. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Looking closely — colour, grain and texture

A rock is identified the way a detective works: by its marks. China's Grade 3 book has learners collect rocks and observe each one closely — its **colour**, its **grain** (the size of the bits it is made of), and its **texture** (how it feels and how its surface is put together).

Five rocks are the book's identification set: **granite, sandstone, shale, limestone and marble**. Each carries its own combination of marks, so careful looking already separates most of them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The scratch test — hardness

Where looking is not enough, the book adds a test: **hardness**, tried by scratching. A rock that scratches easily is softer; one that resists is harder — and hardness is a mark as telling as colour.

Colour, grain, texture and hardness together form the identification: the learner holds a collected rock, reads its four marks, and names it against the set of five — observation turned into a name, with evidence.

## The key ideas

- Rocks are identified by their **marks**: colour, grain, texture and hardness.
- The book's set is **granite, sandstone, shale, limestone and marble** — each with its own combination of marks.
- The **scratch test** tries hardness — a mark as telling as colour when looking alone cannot decide.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Looking closely — colour, grain and texture, The scratch test — hardness. Your companion may stop you once and ask one question about the hardest part; answer from the lesson, not by repeating it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Collect three safe stones (washed, with an adult's knowledge). Describe each one's colour, grain and texture in a line each, like the book's observers.
2. Try a gentle scratch test with an adult's help on your collected stones and rank them softest to hardest.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how four marks give a rock its name.
