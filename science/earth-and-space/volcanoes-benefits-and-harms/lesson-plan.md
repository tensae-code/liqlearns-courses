# Lesson plan — Volcanoes — benefits and harms

**Course:** Science · **Title:** earth-and-space · **Lesson:** Volcanoes — benefits and harms
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — appended to Unit 19 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Nicaragua's volcanoes — what they give and what they harm: volcanic ash that can fertilise the soil, set against fresh ash that harms health
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **What volcanoes give — ash that fertilises** — Nicaragua's Grade 8 book teaches its own country's volcanoes from both sides. On the giving side: **volcanic ash can fertilise** — ash fallen on the land feeds the soil, one reason farms thrive near volcanoes.
2. **What volcanoes harm — fresh ash and health** — On the harming side, the same book sets the **harm of fresh ash for health** — the ash that has just fallen is a danger to breathe and to live under, even though weathered ash later enriches the ground. Benefits and harms come from the same ash, at different times.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks volcanic ash is only a harm → return to sub lesson 1: ash can fertilise the soil — that is why people farm near volcanoes.
- Thinks the benefit cancels the harm → return to sub lesson 2: fresh ash harms health now; the fertilising comes later, as the ash weathers into the soil.
