# Hands-on — Rocks and minerals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The three sortings
Gather a handful of stones and pebbles from one place. Lay them out and sort them three times: once by colour, once by size, once by shape. Each time, say the feature out loud and count the groups. Write the three sortings down — same stones, three different groupings.

## The one-stuff test
Choose your most speckled stone. Look at it closely, turning it in the light. Count how many different-looking grains or colours you can see joined in it. That joined mixture is what makes it a rock. Now find the plainest, most even sample you have — the closest to one stuff throughout.

## A use for each
For three of your samples, name one use that fits it — the hardest for building, the flattest for a wall or a path, the prettiest for a collection — and say which feature (hardness you can feel, shape, colour) made it fit that use.
