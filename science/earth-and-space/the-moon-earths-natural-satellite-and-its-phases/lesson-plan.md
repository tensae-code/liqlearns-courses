# Lesson plan — The Moon — Earth's natural satellite and its phases

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** The Moon — Earth's natural satellite and its phases
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space (CURRICULUM.md Unit 19), beside The Earth — rotation, revolution, day-night and seasons and The Sun — its scale, layers, spots and storms: the Moon is the Earth's own companion body, and this lesson gives primary learners its whole story
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Moon is Earth's natural satellite — a rocky body that circles the Earth in about a month, held by gravity; it makes no light of its own but shines by reflecting the Sun's light; and its changing face — new moon, crescent, half, full and back — is not the Moon changing shape but our view of its sunlit half changing as it travels round us
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. The Earth — rotation, revolution, day-night and seasons is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Earth's natural satellite** — a satellite is a body that circles a bigger body; the Moon is Earth's natural satellite — natural because nobody launched it — circling the Earth once in about 27 days at about 384,000 km away, a rocky, airless world about a quarter of Earth's width, held on its path by Earth's gravity
2. **The Moon shines by reflected sunlight** — the Moon makes no light of its own: it is lit by the Sun like a ball lit by a lamp, exactly half of it bright at any moment — the half facing the Sun — and the moonlight we enjoy is sunlight bounced off its rocky surface to Earth
3. **The phases — our changing view** — as the Moon circles the Earth through the month, we see different amounts of its sunlit half: when the Moon is between Earth and Sun we see its dark side (new moon); as it moves on we see a crescent, then a half, then the full lit face (full moon) when Earth is between Sun and Moon — then half, crescent and new again; the cycle repeats every 29½ days

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: The Moon is Earth's natural satellite: a rocky body circling Earth in about 27 days, held by gravity.
2. Say, in their own words: The Moon makes no light of its own — it shines by reflecting sunlight; exactly half of it is sunlit at any moment.
3. Say, in their own words: Phases are our changing view of that sunlit half as the Moon circles us: new, crescent, half, full, and back.
4. Say, in their own words: New moon: Moon between Earth and Sun — we face its dark side. Full moon: Earth between Sun and Moon — we face its lit side.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: one Moon, many faces. Hold that puzzle; the lesson is its solution.
- Thinks the Moon changes shape → the ball experiment answers it: the Moon stays a round ball; only the lit part we can see changes.
- Thinks moonlight is the Moon's own light → a rock cannot shine; moonlight is sunlight reflected, which is why the lit half always faces the Sun.
