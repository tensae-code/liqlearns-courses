# Ulugbek's astronomical school — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why build an instrument 40 metres across to measure the sky?
**Answer: Angular precision: the same angle spans a longer arc on a bigger circle, so small angles become readable lengths — an arc-minute exceeds a centimetre at 40 m radius, against a hair's breadth on a hand instrument.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give two numbers by which Ulugbek's school can be judged world-class for its age.
**Answer: 1,018 star positions re-measured (the standard into the 1600s); the year at 365 d 6 h 10 min, within about a minute of the modern value (accept also: Earth's axial tilt, planetary tables).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What carried the school's work through after the observatory was destroyed?
**Answer: Its students: the school was a community, not just a building — Ali Qushji and others took the tables and methods abroad (Qushji to Istanbul), so the knowledge survived the stones.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why did the catalogue need re-measuring at all?
**Answer: Star positions drift slowly (precession) and old observations carry errors that grow across 1,300 years; a fresh measurement at Samarkand's precision reset the reference for everyone.**
