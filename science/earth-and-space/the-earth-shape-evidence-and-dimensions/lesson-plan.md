# Lesson plan — The Earth — shape, evidence and dimensions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** The Earth — shape, evidence and dimensions
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — a new title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Earth, at General Science depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Earth is a sphere slightly flattened at the poles and bulged at the equator — an oblate sphere — and we know it from evidence you can check: ships, different times, the eclipse shadow, and photographs from space; its size is fixed by Table 6.1's figures.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Location and Maps — the learner already knows the equator and the poles as places on the globe; this lesson says what shape the whole Earth is and how big.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The shape and its size** — a sphere flattened at the poles and bulged at the equator; the Table 6.1 dimensions: circumferences and diameters, equator against poles.
2. **The evidence** — four proofs from the student book (ships hull-first, different times in distant places, the round shadow in a lunar eclipse, photographs from space), then the longer evidence list and the old flat-Earth ideas, as teacher evidence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Location and Maps — Latitude Lines and the Equator. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the Earth's true shape in one sentence: a sphere slightly flattened at the poles and bulged at the equator.
2. Give the four Table 6.1 numbers: equatorial circumference 40,075 km, polar circumference 40,007 km, equatorial diameter 12,756.4 km, polar diameter 12,713.6 km.
3. Tell the four evidences and say what each one proves.
4. Name two more evidences from the longer list (teacher evidence) and the old flat-disc and flat-square ideas people once held.

## What you need
A globe or a ball, a toy boat or a bottle cap for the ship test, and a dark room with a lamp and a ball for the shadow test.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says the Earth is a perfect ball → the numbers disagree: the equator way around is 68 km longer than the pole way; that small difference IS the flattening and the bulge.
- Mixes the two circumferences → anchor them: the bigger number (40,075) belongs to the bulging equator; the smaller (40,007) to the flattened poles.
- Thinks the ship just gets too small to see → on a flat sea the whole ship would shrink together; hull-first vanishing means the sea itself curves away under it.
