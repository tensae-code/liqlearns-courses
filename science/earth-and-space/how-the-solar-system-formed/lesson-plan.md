# Lesson plan — How the solar system formed — from cloud to Sun

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** How the solar system formed — from cloud to Sun
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — the title opened by the Grade 7 Earth lessons (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Solar System, at General Science Grade 8 depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the solar system formed about 4.5 billion years ago from part of a nebula — a huge rotating cloud of gas and dust — whose densest part collapsed under gravity, flattened into a spinning disk, spun faster as it shrank, and heated until fusion lit the Sun.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 7 Earth and Space lessons: the learner knows the Earth and its motions; this lesson goes back to where the whole system came from.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons

2. **Fusion lights the Sun** — fusion lights the sun part of the lesson.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Grade 8 Earth and Space lessons in this title, in unit order. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a nebula is and what it is mostly made of.
2. Explain why the cloud flattened and spun faster as it shrank.
3. State the temperature at which fusion began and what fusion does.
4. Give the solar system's age: about 4.5 billion years.

## What you need
Paper and a handful of flour or dust to show a cloud, and room to spin.
