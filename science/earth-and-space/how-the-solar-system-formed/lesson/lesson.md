# How the solar system formed — from cloud to Sun

## Do it first

Look at a handful of flour dust in the air. Look again, closer. Now imagine that dust spread wider than the whole solar system, turning very slowly. This lesson tells how that turning cloud became the Sun and everything around it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The nebula — a cloud that collapses

[Picture 34500 — the nebula sequence in four panels]

The **solar system** is the Sun plus everything that orbits it: planets, moons, asteroids, comets, dust and gas. It is about **4.5 billion years old**, and nobody watched it form — so its origin is a **theory** built from what we can observe today.

The story starts with a **nebula**: an enormous cloud of gas and dust between the stars, mostly **hydrogen and helium**, with silicate, iron and carbon compounds and frozen water mixed in. One part of such a cloud — a few AU across, and about **twice the Sun's present mass** — began to collapse. **Gravity** pulled its densest part inward, perhaps set off by a nearby exploding star or by two clouds colliding. As the cloud shrank it **fragmented**, and each fragment flattened into a **spinning disk** with a dense centre — and, like a skater pulling in their arms, it **spun faster as it shrank**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fusion lights the Sun

At the centre of one disk, the falling gas piled up hotter and hotter. When the core reached about **10 million °C**, **nuclear fusion** began: **four hydrogen nuclei fuse into one helium nucleus**, releasing energy. A star had lit — our **Sun**. Everything that did not fall into the centre was left circling it, and that leftover matter is where the planets come from — the next lesson's story.
