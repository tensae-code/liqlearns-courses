# Hands-on — Telescopes — refracting, reflecting, radio and space

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the lesson's work (household materials)
Work the lesson's central example yourself, the way Saudi Arabia's book sets it: the means of watching the universe: the refracting telescope gathers light with a convex lens; the reflecting telescope with a concave mirror (the great European reflector's mirror is 8 m across); optical telescopes are housed in observatories — five Saudi ones are named — while the Hubble space telescope sits outside the atmosphere, away from the distortion it causes; radio telescopes gather radio waves day and night in all weathers, feeding receivers and computers that map the sky and search for signs of life — with the home experiment counting stars through a tube to feel the effect of light pollution Use only household materials, write down what you did and what it showed, and compare your result with the lesson.
