# Ocean waters and currents

## Do it first
Think of the sea on a map: it is usually drawn as empty blue. Mexico's Grade 7 book refuses that — it teaches ocean waters as moving, as placed around Mexico, and as a resource the country develops from. What could the sea give a country besides a border? Say your answer out loud. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Characteristics and dynamics of ocean waters

The lesson opens with the ocean itself: the **characteristics of ocean waters** and their **dynamics** — ocean water is not a still blue space on the map; it has its own properties and it moves, and that movement is part of what the waters are.

## Sub lesson 2 — The distribution of ocean waters around Mexico

Next, the book places those waters: the **distribution of ocean waters around Mexico**. A country with seas on its sides is taught to know where its ocean waters lie and how they are arranged around its coasts — the world ocean, read from Mexico's shore.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ocean waters as an economic resource

The book closes on use: **ocean waters in Mexico's economic development** — the sea as an **economic resource**. The waters around a country are not only nature to name; they are part of how the country lives and works, and Mexico teaches them that way.

## The key ideas

- **Characteristics and dynamics of ocean waters** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **The distribution of ocean waters around Mexico** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Ocean waters as an economic resource** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is this part about what ocean water is, where it lies, or what it gives?" Answer: Characteristics and dynamics say what it is; distribution says where it lies around Mexico; the economic-resource part says what it gives the country.
