# Terrestrial planets vs gas giants — the twelve-row contrast

## Do it first

Cover the right half of the lesson picture. Read only the rocky portrait and say the giant's version of each line before you uncover it. If you can predict the table, you already hold the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The terrestrial side of the table

[Picture 34504 — the comparison redrawn as two planet portraits]

The **terrestrial planets** — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — sit **close** to the Sun with orbits **tightly spaced**. They are **small** in mass and radius, made of **rock and metal**, with **solid surfaces** and high **density**. They spin **slowly**, have **few moons or none**, **no rings**, **thin atmospheres**, and are **warm**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The giant side of the table

The **gas giants** — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune — sit **far** out with orbits **widely spaced**. They are **huge** in mass and radius, made mostly of **light gases**, with **no solid surface**, low **density** for their size, and **fast** rotation. They carry **many moons**, **rings**, **thick atmospheres**, and are **cold**. Read the table row by row: on nearly every one of the twelve rows, the giant is the terrestrial planet's opposite.
