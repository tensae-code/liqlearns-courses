# Plate tectonics

## Do it first
Look at a map of the world in your mind: the continents look fixed and still. But Mexico's Grade 7 book says the ground itself is divided into great plates that move. What could 'a moving ground' mean — and what happens where two moving plates meet? Say your answer out loud. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Earth's internal structure and the dynamic plates

Mexico's book teaches the plates together with what lies under them: the **Earth's internal structure**. The plates are not still — they carry **dynamic characteristics**: the outer Earth is divided into plates that move, and that movement is the lesson's starting fact. Hold the frame: inside structure below, moving plates above, and everything in the next sub lessons is what that movement does where plates meet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plate dynamics — convergent, divergent and transform movements

Plate movement is sorted by direction where plates meet. **Convergent** movement is plates moving toward each other. **Divergent** movement is plates moving apart. **Transform** movement is plates sliding past each other sideways. Three directions, three names — toward, apart, past — and naming the direction is naming the kind of plate boundary.

## Sub lesson 3 — Distribution of plates in Mexico and the world

The plates are not arranged the same everywhere: the book closes by placing them — the **distribution of plates in the world**, and **in Mexico** in particular. Mexico's own position on that map is why this lesson matters there: a country is taught first on the world plate map, then at home, where its own plates are.

## The key ideas

- **The Earth's internal structure and the dynamic plates** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Plate dynamics — convergent, divergent and transform movements** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Distribution of plates in Mexico and the world** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Which movement is this — toward, apart, or past?" Answer: Name the direction first: toward each other is convergent, apart is divergent, sliding past is transform.
