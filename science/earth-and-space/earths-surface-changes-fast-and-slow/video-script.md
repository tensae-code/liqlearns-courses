# Video script — Earth's surface changes — fast and slow

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Earth's surface changes — fast and slow** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Grade 2, Grade 5 and Grade 7).

Some changes happen **quickly**: **volcanic eruptions** throwing up new land, **earthquakes** shaking and shifting the ground, **landslides** sending slopes down in minutes (Grade 2, 2.1.2, printed p. 26). A fast change can be witnessed in a single event, and the land before and after it is visibly different.

Other changes are **slow**: **weathering**, rock broken down where it stands, and **erosion**, the broken material carried away — by **water, ice, wind, gravity and vegetation**, at a **rate** that Utah's Grade 5 book (5.1.3, printed p. 27) has pupils investigate. No single day shows erosion happening, yet given years it lowers mountains and carves valleys: slow is not weak.

Utah's Grade 7 book (7.2.2, printed p. 56) sets the capstone: an **evidence-based explanation** of the processes that changed Earth's surface at **varying time and spatial scales**. The land itself is the record — every surface is fast changes and slow changes written over each other — and the scientist's job is to read which process, at which speed, made which mark.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
