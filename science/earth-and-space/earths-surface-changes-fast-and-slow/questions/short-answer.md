# Earth's surface changes — fast and slow — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Fast changes — eruptions, earthquakes, landslides.
**Answer:** Some changes happen quickly: volcanic eruptions throwing up new land, earthquakes shaking and shifting the ground, landslides sending slopes down in minutes (Grade 2, 2.1.2, printed p. 26).

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Slow changes — weathering and erosion.
**Answer:** Other changes are slow: weathering, rock broken down where it stands, and erosion, the broken material carried away — by water, ice, wind, gravity and vegetation, at a rate that Utah's Grade 5 book (5.1.3, printed p. 27) has pupils investigate.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Reading the evidence — changes at varying scales.
**Answer:** Utah's Grade 7 book (7.2.2, printed p. 56) sets the capstone: an evidence-based explanation of the processes that changed Earth's surface at varying time and spatial scales.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Fast changes — eruptions, earthquakes, landslides → Slow changes — weathering and erosion → Reading the evidence — changes at varying scales — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
