Today: **Moving continents and continental drift** — Australia's way, from its Grade 9 Pearson Science 9 (printed p. 392).

The continents are not fixed to the Earth like pins in a board. They **move** — so slowly that no one feels it, over spans of time so long that the movement adds up to whole oceans opening and closing. Australia's book opens its earth chapter here (10.1): before any machinery of plates, the fact itself — the continents have not always stood where they stand now.
The name for that movement is **continental drift**: the continents have drifted — travelled slowly — from earlier positions to the ones on today's map. The drift idea begins from the map itself: the continents' edges look as if they were once joined and have since been pulled apart, like pieces of a torn page drifting apart on a table. Drift is the observation the rest of the chapter must explain.
Australia's own way is the order: moving continents and drift first (10.1), plate movements second (10.2), volcanoes and earthquakes last (10.3). The observation comes before the mechanism — first that the continents move, then how the moving plates carry them, then what that movement does at the surface. A learner who keeps that order holds the whole chapter.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
