# Video script — Landforms and geological features of our island

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Landforms and geological features of our island** — the Dominican Republic's way: the geological characteristics of the island of Hispaniola.

The Dominican Republic shares one island — Hispaniola — and the Grade 5 book reads that island geologically: its land is not flat or featureless, it is built into mountain systems — long, joined ranges of high ground that run across the island and shape everything on it. A mountain system is more than one mountain: it is a whole family of peaks, ridges and valleys formed together and standing together. Naming the island's systems is the book's way of giving the learner a map of their own ground — where the high land runs, and how the island is put together. Once the mountain systems are named, the island can be read: high ground and low ground, the slopes between them, and the flat land near the coasts are the island's landforms — the shapes the Earth's surface takes. Landforms are geological features: they are what the island's long history of movement, breaking and wearing has left standing. The lesson keeps the book's order — first the island as a whole, then its mountain systems, then the features a learner can point to on their own island.
