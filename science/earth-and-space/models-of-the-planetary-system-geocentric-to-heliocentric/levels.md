# Models of the planetary system — geocentric to heliocentric — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner describes the geocentric model (Earth still at the centre, Moon, Sun, planets and stars circling it), names the planets' backward loops as the fact that strained it and tells how Ptolemy's epicycles saved it, then describes the heliocentric model (Sun at the centre, Earth spinning daily and circling yearly among the planets) and explains a backward loop as the Earth overtaking an outer planet — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has three sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — drawing both models from memory and labelling their centres, tracing an epicycle path until the loop appears, and acting out the overtaking with two walkers at different speeds until the learner can tell the whole story — senses, centre, loops, new centre — without the page.
