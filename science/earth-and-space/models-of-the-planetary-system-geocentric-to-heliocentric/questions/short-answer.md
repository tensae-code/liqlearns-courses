# Models of the planetary system — geocentric to heliocentric — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the geocentric model feel natural to anyone who simply watches the sky?
**Answer: The ground never seems to move, while the Sun, Moon and stars visibly rise, cross the sky and set — the senses themselves suggest a still Earth with everything circling it.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name, in order outward, what circles the Earth in the geocentric model.
**Answer: The Moon first, then the Sun and the five visible planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn), and farthest out the sphere of the fixed stars.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What observation could a single circle round a still Earth not explain, and how did the epicycle fix it?
**Answer: The planets' backward (retrograde) loops. Riding each planet on a small circle whose centre travels a larger circle makes the planet's path, seen from Earth, swing out and back — reproducing the loop.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What two motions does Copernicus give the Earth, and what does each one explain?
**Answer: A daily spin on its axis, which explains the rising and setting of everything in the sky; and a yearly journey round the Sun, which makes the Earth one planet among the others.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain Mars's backward loop the heliocentric way, using the road-and-carts comparison.
**Answer: The Earth travels faster on its smaller circle; as it catches up and passes Mars, Mars seems to slide backward against the distant stars — as a slower cart you overtake seems to drift backward against the hills — then drifts forward again once the Earth has passed. The planet never reverses; the loop is the view from a moving Earth.**
