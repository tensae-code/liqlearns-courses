Today: **Models of the planetary system** — the story of what stands at the centre of everything.

Look up: the Sun rises, crosses the sky and sets; at night the stars wheel overhead. For most of history, people drew exactly what the senses show: the Earth, still, at the centre, with everything circling it. One trouble: the planets sometimes loop backwards in the sky. To save the picture, Ptolemy set each planet on a small circle riding a bigger circle — circles on circles, until the loops came out right.
Then Copernicus moved the centre. The Sun stands in the middle; the Earth spins each day and travels round the Sun each year, one planet among the rest. And the loops? They are just what we see when our faster Earth overtakes a slower planet outside it — no extra circles needed. Same sky, new centre — and astronomy was never the same again.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
