# Hands-on — Models of the planetary system — geocentric to heliocentric

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Draw the old picture
On the ground or on paper, draw the geocentric model: a dot for the Earth at the centre, then circles around it for the Moon, the Sun, the planets and the fixed stars. Stand on the Earth dot and turn slowly: everything you drew circles you. Say aloud: this is the picture my own senses hand me — still ground, wheeling sky.

## Trace an epicycle
Draw a big circle. Pick a point travelling round it, and around that point draw a small circle with a second point riding it. Now move both points together — the carrier round the big circle, the planet round the small one — marking the planet's position step by step. Join the marks: the path loops backward against its own travel. Say aloud: circles on circles make the loop the sky really shows.

## Act the overtaking
Two walkers circle a lamp (the Sun) on chalk circles: the inner walker (Earth) on a small circle, walking faster; the outer walker (Mars) on a bigger circle, walking slower. The inner walker keeps an eye on the outer one against the far wall: while passing, the outer walker seems to drift backward against the wall, then forward again. Say aloud: nobody looped — the backward loop is what overtaking looks like from the faster circle.
