# Hands-on — The daily motion of the Sun, Moon and stars

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Shadow clock and Moon watch
Push a straight stick upright into open ground on a sunny day. Mark its shadow tip every hour with a small stone and note the time beside each stone. By the afternoon the stones draw an arc — your own shadow clock, driven by the Earth's turning. That evening, if the Moon is up, log its direction and height and the shape of its lit part; repeat at the same time two nights later and compare the two log entries aloud: same daily parade, one slow extra step eastward.
