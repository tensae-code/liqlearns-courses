# The daily motion of the Sun, Moon and stars — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A stick's shadow sweeps west to east during the day. Explain this using the Earth's rotation.
**Answer: The Earth turns west to east, so the Sun appears to move east to west across the sky; the shadow, cast opposite the Sun, therefore sweeps the other way, west to east.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the two motions an observing log of the Moon over several nights shows.
**Answer: The daily east-to-west parade caused by the Earth's spin, and the Moon's own eastward step in its orbit around the Earth, which delays its rising and changes its phase.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the Pole Star barely move while other stars wheel around it?
**Answer: The Earth's axis points almost at it, so it sits near the centre — the celestial pole — of the sky's apparent turning.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Day and night, in one sentence each — what are they?
**Answer: Day is the side of the turning Earth facing the Sun; night is the side turned away from the Sun.**
