# Polar day and polar night — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the midnight sun?
**Answer: The polar day seen near a pole in its summer — the Sun stays above the horizon all through the turning, still shining at midnight, and at the pole itself the daylight lasts for months.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the turning Earth fail to bring night to a pole tilted toward the Sun?
**Answer: Because every part of that pole's turning stays in the sunlight — the tilt keeps it out of the Earth's shadow for the whole season, so the Sun circles the sky without setting.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How do the two poles share polar day and polar night?
**Answer: They take opposite turns: when one pole is tilted toward the Sun and has its polar day, the other is tilted away and has its polar night; half a year later they exchange.**
