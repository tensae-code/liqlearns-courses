# Two families of planets — rocky inside, giants outside

## Do it first

Look at the picture of the two families. Before reading on, sort the eight planets by eye into 'small and rocky' and 'huge and light' — then ask: what single thing could have sorted them this way?

## Sub lesson 1 — Leftover matter sticks together

[Picture 34501 — a temperature-gradient bar from the Sun outward, rocky planets in the hot zone and gas giants in the cold zone]

Not all of the cloud was pulled into the core. The matter left circling the young Sun **collided and stuck together**, lump on lump, until it built the **planets** and the **asteroids**. One disk, one process — but two very different results, because the disk was not the same temperature everywhere.

## Sub lesson 2 — Heat near the Sun, cold far away

Close to the hot young Sun, easily **vaporised** elements could not condense — the heat drove them off. So the inner planets — **Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars** — are **small and rocky**, with **iron cores**, poor in light elements. Farther out, where it was cold, lighter substances — **hydrogen, helium, methane and ammonia** — could gather in huge amounts, so the outer planets — **Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune** — grew **much larger** and light for their size. One temperature gradient explains the solar system's whole two-family layout.
