# Hands-on — Global wind belts and the Coriolis effect

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the lesson's work (household materials)
Work the lesson's central example yourself, the way Saudi Arabia's book sets it: global air currents: the sun's rays fall vertically on the equator and slanting on the poles, so equatorial air heats more, rises and moves poleward while cold polar air sinks equatorward; the earth's rotation bends the moving air right in the northern hemisphere and left in the southern — the Coriolis effect Use only household materials, write down what you did and what it showed, and compare your result with the lesson.
