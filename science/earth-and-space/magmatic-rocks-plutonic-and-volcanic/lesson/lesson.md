# Magmatic rocks — plutonic and volcanic

## Do it first
Deep under a volcano, melted rock cools so slowly it has time to grow big crystals; the same melt, thrown out at the surface, freezes fast and fine. Are the two rocks the same family or two different ones? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its SVT books teach the magmatic family by its two cooling places.

## Sub lesson 1 — Magmatic rocks — born from molten rock

Mauritania's SVT books teach **magmatic rocks**: rocks formed when molten rock — magma below the surface, lava at it — cools and hardens. They are one family by birth: every magmatic rock was once melt.

Within the family, the books split the rocks by **where the melt cooled**, and the split is the lesson: cooled deep, or cooled at the surface. The cooling place writes itself into the rock — slow cooling grows large crystals; fast cooling leaves them small or none.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plutonic rocks — cooled slowly, deep down

**Plutonic rocks** (Grade 9, printed p. 101; Grade 10, printed p. 89) are the magmatic rocks that cooled **below the surface**, inside the Earth's crust. Buried in hot rock, the melt loses heat slowly, and slow cooling gives the crystals time to grow — a plutonic rock shows its minerals as visible grains.

They reach the surface only later, when the rock above them is worn or lifted away: a plutonic rock in a cliff today began as a slow-cooling melt far below it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Volcanic rocks — cooled quickly, at the surface

**Volcanic rocks** (Grade 9, printed p. 110; Grade 10, printed p. 93) are the magmatic rocks that cooled **at the surface**, from lava poured or thrown out by volcanoes. In open air and water the melt cools fast, crystals have no time to grow, and the rock is fine-grained or glassy.

Same family, same melt-born birth — the difference between a volcanic and a plutonic rock is the cooling place and the crystal size it leaves behind. That is the pair Mauritania teaches, in Grade 9 and again in Grade 10.

## The key ideas

- **Magmatic rocks** are born from molten rock cooled and hardened — one family by birth.
- **Plutonic rocks** cooled slowly **below the surface** — time for visible crystals to grow.
- **Volcanic rocks** cooled quickly **at the surface** from lava — fine-grained or glassy.
- The cooling place is the split Mauritania's Grade 9 and Grade 10 books both teach.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Two magmatic rocks, one with big visible crystals and one fine and glassy — which cooled where?" Answer from the lesson: The big-crystalled one is plutonic: it cooled slowly deep below the surface, with time for crystals to grow. The fine, glassy one is volcanic: its lava cooled fast at the surface.
