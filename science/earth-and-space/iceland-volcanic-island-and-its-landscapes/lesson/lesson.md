# Iceland — the volcanic island and its landscapes

## Do it first
Some islands are old and quiet. Iceland is not: it is an island still being built by volcanoes. If your whole country sat on new, dark lava ground, what would the land under your school look like? Say your picture out loud. Then read on: Iceland's Grade 3 book opens its Earth chapter with the home island itself — "ELDFJALLAEYJAN ÍSLAND", Iceland the volcanic island.

## Sub lesson 1 — An island built by volcanoes

Iceland is a **volcanic island**: an island raised and still shaped by volcanic eruptions, its ground made of lava. That is the country's first Earth fact about itself, and its book teaches it first — the home island before any other land.

## Sub lesson 2 — The volcanic island's landscapes

Because the island is volcanic, its **landscapes** carry the marks of that making — lava ground and volcanic shapes across the country the children live in. The chapter pairs the island lesson with highlands and lowlands (`earth-and-space/highlands-and-lowlands`) and with kinds of maps: know your own island's making, its heights, and how maps show it.

## The key ideas

- Iceland is a volcanic island — raised and still shaped by eruptions, its ground made of lava.
- The island's landscapes carry the marks of that volcanic making.
- Iceland's book teaches the home island first — the country's own opening to Earth study.
