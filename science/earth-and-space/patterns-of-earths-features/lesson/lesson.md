# Patterns of Earth's features

## Do it first
Plot every earthquake and volcano on a world map. Would the dots scatter evenly, like rain — or would they line up? And where would the great mountain chains fall? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 5 book teaches Earth's features by their patterns, analysed from data.

## Sub lesson 1 — Earthquakes and volcanoes occur in bands

The data shows a pattern, not a scatter: **most earthquakes and volcanoes occur in bands along continent–ocean boundaries** (Grade 5, 5.1.1, printed p. 13). Where a continent meets an ocean, the Earth is active in a line; away from those boundaries, the map is quiet. The band is the fact the analysis finds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mountain chains — inside continents or near their edges

The mountains pattern too: **mountain chains lie inside continents or near their edges**. Chains — not lone peaks — are the unit: mountain building runs in lines, and those lines sit within continents or along their margins. Together with the earthquake–volcano bands, the lesson's claim stands: Earth's great features are arranged in patterns, and the patterns can be read from data.

## The key ideas

- **Earthquakes and volcanoes occur in bands** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Mountain chains — inside continents or near their edges** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
