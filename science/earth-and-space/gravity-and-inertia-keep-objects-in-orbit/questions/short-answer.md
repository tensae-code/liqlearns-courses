# Gravity and inertia keep objects in orbit — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Gravity — the inward pull.
**Answer:** Gravity pulls the orbiting object toward the body it circles (Grade 6, 6.1.2, printed p. 36).

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Inertia — the onward carry.
**Answer:** Inertia is the object's tendency to keep moving in a straight line at its speed.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Orbit — the two together.
**Answer:** An orbit is what the two make together: the object forever falls toward the centre under gravity while forever travelling onward under inertia, so its path closes into a circle or ellipse around the body.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Gravity — the inward pull → Inertia — the onward carry → Orbit — the two together — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
