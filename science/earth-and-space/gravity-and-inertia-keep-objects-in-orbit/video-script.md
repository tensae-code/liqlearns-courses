Today: **Gravity and inertia keep objects in orbit** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Grade 6).

**Gravity** pulls the orbiting object toward the body it circles (Grade 6, 6.1.2, printed p. 36). Left to gravity alone, the Moon would simply fall to Earth: the pull is always inward, always bending the object's path toward the centre. Gravity is the orbit's inward half.

**Inertia** is the object's tendency to keep moving in a straight line at its speed. Left to inertia alone, the Moon would fly off straight and be gone. Inertia is the orbit's onward half — the motion gravity must bend, and never stops trying to bend.

An **orbit** is what the two make together: the object forever falls toward the centre under gravity while forever travelling onward under inertia, so its path closes into a circle or ellipse around the body. Utah's way is a **model** of the solar system's motions: every planet's orbit is this same balance, gravity and inertia, neither winning.
