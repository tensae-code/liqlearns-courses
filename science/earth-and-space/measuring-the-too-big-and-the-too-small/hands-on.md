# Hands-on — Measuring the too-big and the too-small

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your reading.

## The globe and the string
Wrap a string around a globe's equator, mark it, and measure the string with a ruler. Measure the globe's diameter too. Now the scaling: if the globe's diameter stands for the Earth's 12,756.4 km, every centimetre on your string stands for the same share of the Earth's 40,075 km. Work it out and check how close your string-and-ruler answer lands to the printed 40,075 km.

## The too-slow race
Mark the edge of a shadow at 9 o'clock, again at 10, again at 11. The shadow crept while you were not watching — measure the hour-by-hour movement in centimetres. That is the drift method in miniature: too slow to watch, easy to measure as change over time.

## Fingernail drift
Your fingernails grow a few centimetres a year — about the speed continents drift. Measure a nail today and again in a month: the month's growth, multiplied up, is the year's. Indirect measurement on your own hand.
