# Measuring the too-big and the too-small — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** Indirect measurement is used when a quantity is —
a) exactly one metre  b) too big, too small or too slow to measure directly  c) already known  d) made of water
**Answer: b).**

**2.** To find the Earth's circumference with a globe, you measure —
a) the globe's weight  b) a string around the globe and the globe's diameter, then scale up  c) the globe's colour  d) nothing; it cannot be estimated
**Answer: b).**

**3.** Continental drift is measured by —
a) watching it for one minute  b) measuring positions a long time apart and dividing the change by the years  c) listening to the ground  d) weighing a continent
**Answer: b)** — a few centimetres per year.

**4.** A movement of 40 cm in 10 years is a drift of —
a) 40 cm a year  b) 4 cm a year  c) 0.4 cm a year  d) 400 cm a year
**Answer: b) 4 cm a year.**

**5.** A globe's string measures 40 cm around and the globe's diameter is 12.7 cm. The string stands to the diameter as the Earth's circumference stands to 12,756.4 km. The worked-out circumference is closest to —
a) 4,000 km  b) 40,000 km  c) 400,000 km  d) 1,270 km
**Answer: b) 40,000 km** — about forty thousand kilometres, the string-and-proportion road to Table 6.1's 40,075 km.
