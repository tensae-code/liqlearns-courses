# Lesson plan — Seasons and the Sun's path — the ecliptic

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Seasons and the Sun's path — the ecliptic
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space (CURRICULUM.md Unit 19), after the celestial sphere lesson in Palestine's astronomy sequence: the tilted yearly journey that the celestial-sphere coordinates make readable
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** as the Earth travels round the Sun through the year, the Sun appears to travel a great circle on the celestial sphere — the ecliptic — tilted 23.5° to the celestial equator because the Earth's axis is tilted 23.5° to its orbit; that fixed tilt, not any change in distance, makes the seasons: each hemisphere in turn leans toward the Sun, receiving its rays more directly and for longer days, then leans away into winter
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **The ecliptic — the Sun's road across the sphere** — The Sun's yearly road is a great circle tilted 23.5° to the sky's equator.
2. **The Sun's changing declination and the turning year** — One number — the Sun's declination — drives the whole seasonal cycle.
3. **Why the tilt, not the distance, makes the seasons** — The axis never changes direction; the lean does the work.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: The ecliptic is the Sun's apparent yearly path on the celestial sphere, tilted 23.5° to the celestial equator.
2. Say, in their own words: The Sun crosses the celestial equator at the two equinoxes and reaches declination ±23.5° at the two solstices.
3. Say, in their own words: Seasons are caused by the fixed tilt of the Earth's axis: each hemisphere in turn leans toward the Sun.
4. Say, in their own words: Leaning toward the Sun means higher noon Sun (rays more concentrated) and longer days — summer; leaning away means the opposite — winter.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A ball on a stick (the tilted Earth), a lamp in the middle of a dark room, and room to walk the ball round the lamp.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says summer is when Earth is closest to the Sun → Earth is nearest the Sun in early January — northern winter; and one distance cannot give both hemispheres opposite seasons at the same moment, while the tilt does exactly that.
- Thinks the axis tips back and forth through the year → the axis keeps one direction all year (toward Polaris); it is the Earth's position on its orbit that changes which hemisphere the fixed tilt leans sunward.
- Cannot start → return to Do it first. Hold that puzzle; the lesson is its solution.
