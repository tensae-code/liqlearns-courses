# Karst — limestone caves and sinkholes

## Do it first
Before you read: rain falls on a limestone hill for a hundred thousand years, and one day the hill has a cave inside it and a hole in its top — yet no one ever dug or blasted. Where did the rock go? Say your answer out loud. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The solvent action of water — water dissolves limestone

Water does not only wash rock away grain by grain; on limestone it works as a **solvent**: it **dissolves** the rock itself. Italy's relief unit names this plainly — **the solvent action of water** — as one of the shaping forces of the Earth's surface. The dissolving is slow and invisible while it happens, but it never stops: wherever water meets limestone, the rock is being taken away in solution.

## Sub lesson 2 — Karst — the caves and sinkholes the dissolving shapes

A landscape shaped by that dissolving is called **karst**. Underground, the dissolved rock leaves hollow spaces — **caves**. At the surface, where the dissolved rock gives way, the ground opens into **sinkholes**. Cave and sinkhole are the same work seen in two places: the solvent action of water, hollowing limestone below and opening it above. Italy's book files karst in one page of its relief unit (printed p. 430) — a small page for a whole landscape kind, and this lesson keeps it at exactly that size.
