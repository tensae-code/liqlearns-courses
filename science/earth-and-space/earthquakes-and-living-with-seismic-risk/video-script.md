Today: **Earthquakes and living with seismic risk** — Mexico's way, from its Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades (printed pp. 136–150).

The lesson opens with the cause: earthquakes in Mexico are tectonic plate activity made felt. The plates of the plate-tectonics lesson move — and where that movement releases suddenly, the ground shakes. Mexico is taught this cause on its own ground, because its plates are the ones under its own cities.
Mexico's book teaches its earthquakes by their shared date: 19 September. The 1985 earthquake and the 2017 earthquake fell on the same calendar day, a generation apart, and the book holds them together — the country's seismic memory is dated, named and local, not an abstract risk. (This sub lesson is a summary of what the book teaches about those events, told as the book's own history lesson; no detail beyond the placement is added here.)
Inside that history stands the Colegio Rébsamen tragedy, which the book carries as a safety lesson: a school is the place where safety must hold, and its failure is taught so that it is not repeated. The lesson the book draws is about safety itself — that buildings where children learn must be safe, and that seismic risk is answered by preparation and by safe building, not by forgetting. (Carried exactly as filed: as a safety lesson, in summary only.)
