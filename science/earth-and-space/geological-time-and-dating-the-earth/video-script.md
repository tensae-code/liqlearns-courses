# Video script — Geological time and dating the Earth

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Geological time and dating the Earth** — Mexico's way, from its Grade 7 Ética, naturaleza y sociedades (printed pp. 284–291).

The lesson opens with what was found: human fossil remains in Mexican territory, and the evidence they carry. Remains are the dating lesson's raw material: a human past far older than any written record is read from what the ground preserved, and Mexico's book starts from its own territory's finds.
Not every find settles its question. The book teaches the "Man of Tepexpan" controversy: a named Mexican find whose meaning has been argued over — the controversy itself is the lesson, that dating the past is done from evidence, and evidence can be disputed. The book does not hide the argument; it teaches it. (Carried as filed: the controversy is named and taught as a controversy; no side is invented here.)
The book closes with a person: Naia, a woman of Mexican prehistory. Against the disputed find stands a named individual from Mexico's deep past — prehistory taught, in Mexico's way, not as anonymous ages but through a woman with a name, recovered from Mexico's own ground.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
