# Mauritania's geological ensembles

## Do it first
Every country stands on its own ground: some old and hard, some built of younger basins of layered rock. If you had to describe your whole country's ground in a few great regions, what would they be? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 11 book teaches the country's own geological ensembles as a lesson of their own.

## Sub lesson 1 — The great geological ensembles of Mauritania

Mauritania's Grade 11 série D *Sciences Naturelles* book teaches **Mauritania's great geological ensembles** (printed p. 129): the country's ground sorted into its large rock regions — the great ensembles, each a vast area sharing one geological character and history.

An ensemble is geology at the country's scale: not one rock or one cliff, but the regional frame — the old foundations and the younger basins laid over and against them — from which every smaller study of the country's ground begins.

## Sub lesson 2 — A country's own geology, in its own book

The lesson's place in the book is its meaning: it stands after the geological eras (printed p. 128) and before Mauritania's mining resources (printed p. 132) and its oil and gas (printed p. 137). First the Earth's long ages, then this country's ground, then what that ground holds.

That order is Mauritania's own way — world geology made local. The learner who knows the ensembles can place everything else the book teaches about their own land: where its minerals lie, where its underground water waits, and why.

## The key ideas

- **Geological ensembles** are a country's great rock regions — vast areas each sharing one geological character and history.
- Mauritania's Grade 11 série D book teaches **its own country's ensembles** as a lesson of their own (printed p. 129).
- The lesson stands between the **geological eras** and Mauritania's **mining resources and oil and gas** — world time, then home ground, then what the ground holds.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why does the book teach Mauritania's ensembles between the eras and the mining resources?" Answer from the lesson: Because the ensembles are the frame everything else hangs on: the eras give the long ages of the Earth, the ensembles give this country's ground built through those ages, and the resources are then read as what that ground holds.
