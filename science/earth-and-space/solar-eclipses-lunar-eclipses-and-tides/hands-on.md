# Hands-on — Solar eclipses, lunar eclipses and tides

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Shadows with a lamp and two balls
In a darkened room, set a lamp 2 m from a big ball (Earth). Hold a small ball (Moon) between them, at arm's length from the Earth ball, and find where its shadow spot falls: that spot is a total solar eclipse — note how small it is. Then move the Moon ball behind the Earth ball until it enters the Earth's shadow: it disappears into shade across its whole face — a lunar eclipse, 'visible' from the whole dark side of your Earth ball. Swap roles with a partner and explain each arrangement aloud: new Moon, full Moon, which shadow, whose view.
