# Solar eclipses, lunar eclipses and tides — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why don't we get a solar eclipse every new Moon?
**Answer: The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to the Earth's orbit, so at most new Moons the Moon's shadow passes above or below the Earth; only when the alignment is also near a node of the two orbits does the shadow land.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does totality last only minutes for a solar eclipse but over an hour for a lunar one?
**Answer: The Moon's shadow spot on Earth is small and sweeps quickly as both bodies move; the Earth's shadow at the Moon's distance is much wider than the Moon, so the Moon takes over an hour to cross it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain the second tidal bulge, on the side of Earth away from the Moon.
**Answer: The Moon pulls Earth's centre harder than the far-side oceans, so the far side is effectively left behind relative to the centre — the water there bulges outward, away from the Moon, just as the near-side water bulges toward it.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Vietnam's book teaches eclipses and tides in one lesson. What single fact of geometry unites them?
**Answer: Both peak when Sun, Moon and Earth fall into one line: the line-ups cast the shadows (eclipses) and stack the Sun's and Moon's tidal pulls into spring tides.**
