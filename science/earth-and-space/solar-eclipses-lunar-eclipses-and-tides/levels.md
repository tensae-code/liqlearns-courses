# Solar eclipses, lunar eclipses and tides — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Solar eclipse: at new Moon, the Moon's shadow falls on Earth — total in the narrow umbra track, partial in the penumbra; visible only locally; Lunar eclipse: at full Moon, the Earth's wide shadow falls on the Moon — total lunar eclipses are long, red, and visible from the whole night hemisphere; Tides come from the unevenness of the Moon's pull across the Earth: two bulges, two high tides a day at most coasts; Spring tides (biggest range) occur at new and full Moon — the eclipse alignments; neap tides at the quarters — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
