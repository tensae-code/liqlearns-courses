# Metamorphic rocks and the rock cycle

## Do it first
Take a rock and squeeze and heat it — not enough to melt it, just enough to remake its minerals and bend its layers. Is the result the same rock, or a new one? And can that new rock ever become sediment again? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its SVT books close the rock families with the changed rocks, then join all three families in the cycle.

## Sub lesson 1 — Metamorphic rocks — rocks changed, not melted

Mauritania's SVT books teach **metamorphic rocks** by their characteristics (Grade 9, printed p. 129; Grade 10, printed p. 97): rocks remade in the solid state — buried rock subjected to heat and pressure until its minerals and texture change — **without melting**. Melting would make it magmatic; change short of melting makes it metamorphic.

The changed rock keeps a memory of what it was, written in its new minerals and its banding: metamorphic rocks are read as transformations, one rock family's answer to the Earth's inner pressure and heat.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gneiss — the worked example

The Grade 9 book studies **gneiss** as the worked example (printed p. 131): a metamorphic rock showing the family's signature — light and dark mineral bands, the mark of intense change under pressure, in a rock that was once another rock entirely.

One rock studied closely carries the whole family: see the banding, name the change that made it, and every other metamorphic rock in the book's closing survey (printed p. 137) is read by the same rule.

## Sub lesson 3 — The rock cycle — all three families, turning

The books close by linking everything in the **rock cycle** (Grade 9, printed p. 137; Grade 10, printed p. 101): magmatic rocks are worn to sediment and pressed into sedimentary rock; any rock, buried and changed, becomes metamorphic; any rock, melted, returns to magma and begins again as magmatic.

No family is an end. The cycle is the Earth's own recycling: every rock in the crust is a stage, not a finish, and the three families are three moments of one turning system.

## The key ideas

- **Metamorphic rocks** are remade by heat and pressure **without melting** — change in the solid state.
- **Gneiss**, banded light and dark, is Mauritania's Grade 9 worked example of the family.
- The **rock cycle** links all three families: melting, cooling, wearing, pressing, changing — every rock a stage, not an end.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A buried rock melts completely and later hardens. Is the new rock metamorphic?" Answer from the lesson: No — melting ends the metamorphic story. Melted rock that hardens is magmatic. Metamorphic change happens in the solid state, under heat and pressure short of melting.
