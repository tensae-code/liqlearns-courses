# The Solar system — planets and the modern picture of the Universe

## Do it first
Before you read: draw the Solar system from memory — most drawings make the planets evenly spaced beads. The real system is emptier, stranger and far more lopsided. And beyond it? Keep both scales ready.

## Sub lesson 1 — The planets — two families around one star

Eight planets, in order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — the rocky family, small, dense, close to the Sun — then Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune — the giants, vast balls of gas and ice with rings and retinues of moons (Jupiter's Io of Rømer's measurement among them). The pattern is a birth certificate: near the young hot Sun only rock and metal could condense; beyond the frost line, gas and ice piled into giants. Earth sits in the temperate band where water can be liquid — the fact under everything else in this course.

## Sub lesson 2 — Moons, and the system's small change

Planets are not the whole household: moons by the hundred (ours unusually large for its planet, tidally locked so one face always turns to us, and the maker of tides and eclipses), the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, and beyond Neptune the comet reservoirs whose visitors — comets, meteors and meteorites — the book treats in this same chapter's strand stocked in the small-bodies lesson. The system's architecture — one star holding 99.86% of the mass, planets sharing the rest in near-circular, near-planar orbits in the same direction — is the fingerprint of its birth from one spinning cloud.

## Sub lesson 3 — The modern picture — a system inside a Universe

Zoom out as the Grade 9 book closes: the Sun is one star among hundreds of billions in the Milky Way galaxy; the galaxy is one among billions, gathered in clusters; and the whole structure is expanding, its galaxies receding as space itself grows — read from the redshift of their light — back toward a hot, dense beginning about 13.8 billion years ago. That is the modern picture the book's chapter builds to: the Solar system as our one well-mapped household, and the honest scale of the street it stands on.

## The key ideas

- Eight planets, two families: rocky (Mercury–Mars) near the Sun, giants (Jupiter–Neptune) beyond the frost line
- Moons, asteroids and comets complete the household; the Sun holds 99.86% of the system's mass
- Near-circular, same-direction orbits are the fingerprint of birth from one spinning cloud
- The modern picture: one star in a galaxy of billions of stars, one galaxy among billions, in an expanding Universe ≈ 13.8 billion years from a hot dense beginning

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: eight planets circle the Sun in two families — small rocky worlds near in, giant gas and ice worlds far out, each with its moons — and the Solar system itself is one small household in a universe of galaxies, expanding since a hot dense beginning. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.
