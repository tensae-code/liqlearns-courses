# The Solar system — planets and the modern picture of the Universe — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain the two-family pattern from the young Solar system's heat.
**Answer: Near the hot young Sun, only rock and metal could condense into solids — small rocky planets. Beyond the frost line it was cold enough for ice and gas to gather and be held, growing the giants. Composition maps the birth temperature.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What three features of the orbits betray a birth from one spinning cloud?
**Answer: The planets orbit in nearly the same plane, in nearly circular orbits, all in the same direction — the flattened, one-way spin of the original cloud, preserved.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Place the Solar system in the modern picture in three zoom steps.
**Answer: Solar system → one household around one star; the Sun → one star among hundreds of billions in the Milky Way; the Milky Way → one galaxy among billions in an expanding Universe about 13.8 billion years from a hot dense beginning.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can Earth have liquid water oceans while Mercury and Neptune cannot (in the lesson's frame)?
**Answer: Earth orbits in the temperate band: not so near the Sun that water boils away, not so far that it locks as ice — distance sets surface temperature, and Earth's is the middle case.**
