# Hands-on — Fossils and how they form

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## A print in settling sediment (kitchen materials)
Press a shell, a leaf or a small coin firmly into soft mud or wet clay in a tray, then lift it out carefully. Look at the print it leaves. Write two sentences: the print is the record the remains leave in sediment — and what must happen next, by diagenesis, for a record like this to survive as a fossil in rock.

## From sediments to rock — a story in four frames
Draw four frames: (1) remains settling among sediments in water; (2) layers burying them; (3) diagenesis — the sediments becoming rock; (4) the rock opened, the preserved remains — the fossil — inside. Label frames 3 and 4 with the lesson's two key words: diagenesis and fossilisation.

## Hunt the evidence
If you can visit a museum, a layered cliff or a stone with a shell or print in it, sketch what you find and write underneath: which stage of the sediments-to-rock story are you looking at, and what tells you so?
