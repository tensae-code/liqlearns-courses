Today: **The celestial sphere and sky coordinates — declination and right ascension**.

The night sky looks like a dome — so astronomers draw it as one: the celestial sphere, with Earth's axis stretched to its poles and Earth's equator stretched to its equator. Every star gets a fixed address on that sphere. Declination says how far north or south of the sky's equator it sits, in degrees, like latitude. Right ascension says how far east it sits, counted in hours, like longitude — 24 hours round the whole sky. The sphere turns; the addresses never change.
