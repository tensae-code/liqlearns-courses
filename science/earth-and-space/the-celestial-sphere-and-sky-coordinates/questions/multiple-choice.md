# The celestial sphere and sky coordinates — declination and right ascension — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) The celestial sphere is…
a) A real glass ball around the Earth  b) An imaginary ball around the Earth on which the stars are pictured as fixed  c) The Moon's surface  d) The Earth's atmosphere
**Answer: An imaginary ball around the Earth on which the stars are pictured as fixed**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) The north celestial pole lies almost exactly at…
a) The Moon  b) Polaris, the pole star  c) The Sun  d) The March equinox point
**Answer: Polaris, the pole star**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A star is at its highest in your sky when it crosses…
a) The horizon  b) The celestial equator  c) Your meridian  d) Its declination circle
**Answer: Your meridian**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Declination is measured from…
a) The horizon  b) The celestial equator, north (+) or south (−)  c) The meridian  d) Polaris
**Answer: The celestial equator, north (+) or south (−)**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Right ascension is counted in…
a) Degrees only  b) Hours, minutes and seconds, eastward along the celestial equator  c) Kilometres  d) Days of the year
**Answer: Hours, minutes and seconds, eastward along the celestial equator**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) One hour of right ascension equals…
a) 1°  b) 10°  c) 15°  d) 24°
**Answer: 15°**
