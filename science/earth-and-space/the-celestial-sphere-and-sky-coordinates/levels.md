# The celestial sphere and sky coordinates — declination and right ascension — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner states the lesson's one thing in their own words — astronomers map the sky as if the stars were fixed on a great imaginary ball around the Earth — the celestial sphere — whose equator and poles are the Earth's own equator and poles projected outward; every star then has two fixed coordinates, declination (its angle north or south of the celestial equator, like latitude) and right ascension (its angle eastward along the equator, measured in hours, like longitude) — and proves it with the lesson's own activities, answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — doing the lesson's own experiments and sorts by hand, naming each idea aloud, and reasoning about new examples until the teaching is the learner's own.
