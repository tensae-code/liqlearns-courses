# Hands-on — The Earth — four spheres, one system

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your reading.

## One spot, four spheres
Stand at a stream bank, a farm edge or your schoolyard. List ten things you can see, hear or feel. Sort your list into four piles: rock and soil / water / living things / air. If a thing will not sort, ask what it is made of — a wooden house is biosphere timber standing on lithosphere ground under atmosphere sky.

## Chain building
Take a handful of stones or bottle caps as tokens, one per sphere link. Start a chain aloud: "The trees are cut (biosphere) —" pass a token: "the rain hits bare soil (lithosphere) —" pass: "the river runs muddy (hydrosphere) —" pass: "the fish sicken (biosphere again)." Build two more chains of your own, at least three links each.

## Map your town as a system
Draw your town or village as four overlapping circles, like the lesson picture. Write inside each circle what belongs to that sphere in your town — its river, its fields, its people and animals, its winds and smoke. On each overlap, write one thing that passes between the two spheres.
