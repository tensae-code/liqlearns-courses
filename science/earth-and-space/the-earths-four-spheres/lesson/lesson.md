# The Earth — four spheres, one system

## Do it first

Stand outside in one spot — by a stream if you can, in a schoolyard if not. List everything around you: the ground, the soil on your shoes, any water, the plants, any animals, the air on your face, the clouds. Now sort your list into four piles by what each thing is made of. You have just built this lesson. The four piles have names.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four spheres

- **Lithosphere** — the rock sphere: the land, the landforms and the **soil**.
- **Hydrosphere** — the water sphere: **oceans, lakes, glaciers, groundwater** and **water vapour**.
- **Biosphere** — the living sphere: **all living things**, from grass to people.
- **Atmosphere** — the air sphere: the **air** wrapped around the Earth.

[Picture 33104 — the four spheres overlapping: change one and the others change]

The four spheres are not four separate boxes. They overlap and lean on one another everywhere — the soil holds water, the air carries vapour, living roots grip the rock — and they are **so interdependent that a change in one sphere changes the others**.

## Sub lesson 2 — How they interact

Follow the chains link by link:

- **Biosphere → lithosphere.** Trees (biosphere) are cut from a hillside. Their roots no longer hold the soil (lithosphere), so the rain washes the soil away — **deforestation erodes the soil**.
- **Atmosphere → biosphere.** Smoke rises into the air (atmosphere). People and animals breathe that air, and the smoke **harms their lungs** (biosphere).
- **A great dam, read sphere by sphere.** Building the GERD dam moves rock and soil (lithosphere), holds back a river into a new lake (hydrosphere), changes the homes of living things up and down the river (biosphere), and the new lake's surface gives off more vapour into the air (atmosphere) — one work, four spheres touched.
- **A lake evaporating.** Water (hydrosphere) rises as vapour into the air (atmosphere), where it may fall later as rain on soil (lithosphere) that living things (biosphere) depend on.

The way to study any place, then, is the way this lesson ends: **map your own town as a system** — draw its four spheres, and write what passes between them.

## Say it to your companion

Name the four spheres and one member of each from the place you are standing in. Then take one change — "the wood on the hill is cut" — and walk your companion through what it does to the other three spheres, one at a time.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort these into their spheres: a glacier, a ploughed field, a fish, a cloud, groundwater, a mountain, pollen in the wind, a farmer. (Some hold two: the cloud is hydrosphere water riding in the atmosphere — say both.)
2. Build a three-link chain that starts in the hydrosphere: a pond dries up. What changes in two other spheres?
3. Draw your town as four overlapping circles and fill each circle with three members from your town. On one overlap, write what passes between those two spheres.
