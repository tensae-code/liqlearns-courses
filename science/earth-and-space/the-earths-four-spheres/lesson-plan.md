# Lesson plan — The Earth — four spheres, one system

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** The Earth — four spheres, one system
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — a new title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Earth, at General Science depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Earth works as four joined spheres — lithosphere (rock and soil), hydrosphere (water), biosphere (living things) and atmosphere (air) — so interdependent that a change in any one of them changes the others.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Inside the Earth — crust, mantle, core and on The Earth — shape, evidence and dimensions: the learner knows the solid ball and its coverings; this lesson names the four working spheres at and above the surface and how they lean on each other.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The four spheres** — each sphere named and filled: what belongs to the lithosphere, the hydrosphere, the biosphere and the atmosphere.
2. **How they interact** — a change in one sphere changes the others: cutting the biosphere's forest erodes the lithosphere's soil; the atmosphere's smoke harms the biosphere's lungs; a great dam and a lake's evaporation read sphere by sphere; your own town mapped as a system.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Inside the Earth — crust, mantle, core. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four spheres and give two members of each.
2. Trace one chain: deforestation in the biosphere changing the lithosphere.
3. Say which sphere smoke belongs to and which sphere it harms, and how.
4. Take one local change — a new dam, a drained pond, a planted wood — and name what it does in all four spheres.

## What you need
A walk outside: one place where all four spheres meet — a stream bank, a farm, a schoolyard tree.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands in one outdoor spot and lists everything there, then sorts the list into rock-and-soil, water, living and air piles before the sphere names are given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the four spheres named over the learner's own sorted lists; then the interaction chains, one link at a time (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 33104]: a picture of the four sphere circles overlapping over one landscape — atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, biosphere — the student points to where each pair overlaps and says what passes between them.

3. **Say it to your companion** — pick one change and walk it through all four spheres for your companion (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts by the thing, not the sphere → rivers are water wherever they run (hydrosphere); the riverBED is rock and soil (lithosphere). Ask "what is it made of?"
- Treats the spheres as separate boxes → the overlaps in the picture are the lesson: every sphere touches every other, so no change stays in one.
- Cannot start the town map → begin with water: where does the town's water sit, flow and fall? Then ask what lives by it, what it cuts through, and what hangs over it.
