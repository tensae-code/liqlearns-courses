# Fossils — evidence of life and environments long ago

## Do it first
High in the cold Arctic, scientists find fossils of tropical plants. Seashells are dug from rock far inland, on dry hills. What are these remains actually evidence of — and what would you argue from them? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 4 book teaches fossils not as curiosities but as evidence to analyse and to argue from.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fossil data — organisms and environments of long ago

A **fossil** is read as **evidence**: the book (4.1.3, printed p. 41) has pupils **analyse fossil data as evidence of organisms and environments from long ago** — its worked example is the **trilobites and their modern relatives**. Match a fossil to the living things it resembles, and the fossil tells you both what lived then and what its world was like: the organism and its environment are read together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rock layers and their fossils — environments change over time

One fossil is a fact; **patterns in rock layers and the fossils in them** are an argument (4.1.4, printed p. 56): argue from those patterns that **environments change over time**. The book's own case is the **tropical plant fossils in the Arctic** — plants of warm places, fossilised where no such plants can live now. The layers order the story, and the fossils inside them prove the place itself was once different.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What do tropical plant fossils in the Arctic prove?" Answer: That environments change over time: the Arctic once held an environment where tropical plants lived — the fossils in the rock layers are the evidence.
