## Sub lessons
1. **Redshift — galaxies receding, space expanding** — The light of distant galaxies is shifted toward the red end of the spectrum, and the farther the galaxy, the greater the shift: every galaxy is receding, at a speed that grows with its distance. Run the film backwards and the galaxies converge — the universe was once far smaller, denser and hotter. The expansion is of space itself, not an explosion into pre-existing emptiness: every observer, in every galaxy, sees the same pattern of recession.
2. **The cosmic microwave background — the afterglow** — A hot beginning should leave heat behind, cooled by the expansion to a faint glow everywhere. In 1965 exactly that was found: a microwave glow arriving equally from every direction, at a temperature of about 2.7 kelvin — the cosmic microwave background (CMB). It is the oldest light there is, released when the universe first became transparent, and its near-perfect evenness, with tiny ripples that match where galaxies later grew, fits the hot-beginning account in detail.
3. **Two evidences, one account** — Redshift says the universe expands; the CMB says it was once hot. Neither was arranged to support the other, yet both fall out of one story — a hot, dense beginning about 13.8 billion years ago, expanding and cooling ever since. That is how evidence works in science: independent lines, measured by different instruments on different principles, converging on the same account. Later observations — the abundances of the lightest elements, the growth of galaxies — have added further independent lines to the same convergence.
