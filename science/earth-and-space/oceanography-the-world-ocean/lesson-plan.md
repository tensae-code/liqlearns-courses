# Lesson plan — Oceanography — the world ocean

**Course:** Science · **Title:** earth-and-space · **Lesson:** Oceanography — the world ocean
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — appended to Unit 19 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** oceanography — the world ocean: its exploration, the ocean floor and its mapping, the physical properties of seawater, and the life distributed through it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The oceans, their exploration and the ocean floor** — Malaysia's Additional Science Form 5 book opens oceanography with **early ocean exploration and its purposes** — the earliest explorers named are **Ibn Battuta, Christopher Columbus and Vasco da Gama** — and sets out **the main oceans on the world map and the main seas of Southeast Asia**. The **ocean floor's structure** is sketched and tabulated zone by zone, and **mapping the ocean floor** is taught with what seafloor maps give science. Continental drift and plate tectonics are linked to how the ocean floor evolves.
2. **Seawater — temperature, pressure, density — and ocean life** — The book's physical oceanography plots **ocean depth against water temperature** and identifies the **surface temperatures of the Pacific, Atlantic and Southern Oceans**. **Pressure and density differences in seawater** are followed into the living world: the **adaptations of creatures in the ocean's dark zone**. Ocean life itself is classified by **size, mobility and location** — pleuston and the other groups. The chapter closes with **careers in oceanology** and a design task: invent a product solving an ocean problem.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks the ocean floor is flat and unknown → return to sub lesson 1: its structure is mapped zone by zone, and the maps themselves are science's tool.
- Forgets what changes with depth → return to sub lesson 2: temperature, pressure and density — and the dark zone's creatures are adapted to them.
