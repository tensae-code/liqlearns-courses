# Identifying minerals by their properties

## Do it first
Two grey stones look alike. How do you tell which mineral each one is? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Sudan's way in — its Grade 9 Natural Sciences teaches minerals in its Earth unit by their properties, with a table of common minerals to identify them by.

## Sub lesson 1 — Telling minerals apart — lustre, streak and hardness

Minerals are told apart **by their properties**, not by a first look. The book's first tests: **lustre** (how the surface shines), **streak** (the colour of the mark the mineral leaves) and **hardness** (how hard it is to scratch) — three properties, each checked in its own way, narrowing which mineral a sample can be.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fracture and magnetism — and the properties table

Two more tests complete the set: **fracture** (how the mineral breaks) and **magnetism** (whether a magnet pulls it). The book gathers the properties in a **table of common minerals' properties** — the identification is made by matching the sample's tested properties against the table, property by property.

## The key ideas

- **Telling minerals apart — lustre, streak and hardness** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.
- **Fracture and magnetism — and the properties table** — as taught above, in the lesson's own order.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "By what five properties does Sudan's book tell minerals apart?" Answer: Lustre, streak, hardness, fracture and magnetism — tested one by one and matched against the book's table of common minerals' properties.
