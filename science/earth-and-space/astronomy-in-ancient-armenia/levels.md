# Astronomy in ancient Armenia — a country's own sky history — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A farming highland people lives by the sky's clock: sowing, harvest, festivals and travel all need the year measured, and measuring the year means watching the Sun, Moon and stars return; The name the tradition keeps is Anania Shirakatsi (7th century), mathematician and astronomer, whose writings — including a geography and works on the cosmos, the calendar and arithmetic — gathered the sky knowledge of his age in Armenian; The course-way point, the same one the Grade 7 book makes with its physicists: astronomy arrives to the Armenian pupil as an inheritance before it arrives as a syllabus — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
