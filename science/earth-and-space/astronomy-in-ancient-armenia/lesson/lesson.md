# Astronomy in ancient Armenia — a country's own sky history

## Do it first
Keep a one-week sky log: at the same time each clear evening, sketch the Moon's shape and its place among the stars, and note the time of sunset. At the week's end, read your own log the way an ancient calendar-keeper would: the Moon has changed shape and moved against the stars, and the Sun's setting has shifted. You have repeated, in miniature, the observations Armenia's calendar was built from. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Watching the sky in ancient Armenia

A farming highland people lives by the sky's clock: sowing, harvest, festivals and travel all need the year measured, and measuring the year means watching the Sun, Moon and stars return. Ancient Armenia did that watching on its own ground — its calendar, its feast days and its stone-carved signs of the heavens are the records of a sky science practised at home long before telescopes. The chapter's first claim is simply that astronomy has an Armenian address, and an old one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Anania Shirakatsi — the cosmos written in Armenian

The name the tradition keeps is Anania Shirakatsi (7th century), mathematician and astronomer, whose writings — including a geography and works on the cosmos, the calendar and arithmetic — gathered the sky knowledge of his age in Armenian. He wrote of the Earth's sphericity, the Moon's borrowed light and the causes of eclipses in the scientific language of his time. One scholar with books matters to a course: he proves the watching was not folklore only — it was written, taught and argued science.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the chapter starts at home

The course-way point, the same one the Grade 7 book makes with its physicists: astronomy arrives to the Armenian pupil as an inheritance before it arrives as a syllabus. The stars over Armenia are the same stars the chapter will price in light-years — but they are first the stars its own calendar-keepers timed. From that ground the chapter walks outward: Sun, planets, Moon, stars, galaxies, the Universe — the local sky opening onto everything.
