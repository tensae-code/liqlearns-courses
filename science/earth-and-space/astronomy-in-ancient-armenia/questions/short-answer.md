# Astronomy in ancient Armenia — a country's own sky history — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Give two everyday needs that forced ancient peoples to measure the sky's cycles.
**Answer:** Timing sowing and harvest (the year's length and seasons) and fixing festivals/calendar dates — also navigation and travel timing are acceptable: all are read off the Sun, Moon and stars.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is a written astronomy (like Shirakatsi's) a step beyond sky-watching folklore?
**Answer:** Writing fixes the knowledge outside one memory: it can be taught, checked, corrected and built on by others — that is what makes it science rather than custom.

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) What does the Moon do, over one week of same-time evening sketches, that a calendar-keeper can use?
**Answer:** It changes its lit shape (phases) and shifts its position against the background stars from night to night — a visible clock and calendar in one.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the course-way claim of §46 in one sentence.
**Answer:** Astronomy is presented first as something Armenia itself has practised for over a thousand years, so the pupil meets the science as their own inheritance before meeting its modern content.
