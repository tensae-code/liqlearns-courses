# Lesson plan — Astronomy in ancient Armenia — a country's own sky history

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Astronomy in ancient Armenia — a country's own sky history
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space (CURRICULUM.md Unit 19), inside the astronomy strand — Armenia's Grade 9 astronomy chapter opens with the development of astronomy in Armenia itself (§46) before the solar system
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Armenian Grade 9 book opens its astronomy chapter at home: Armenians watched, timed and wrote about the sky for well over a thousand years — a calendar built from the stars, and in Anania Shirakatsi a 7th-century scholar who set down the cosmos in Armenian — before the chapter turns to planets and galaxies

## Sub lessons
1. **Watching the sky in ancient Armenia** — A farming highland people lives by the sky's clock: sowing, harvest, festivals and travel all need the year measured, and measuring the year means watching the Sun, Moon and stars return. Ancient Armenia did that watching on its own ground — its calendar, its feast days and its stone-carved signs of the heavens are the records of a sky science practised at home long before telescopes. The chapter's first claim is simply that astronomy has an Armenian address, and an old one.
2. **Anania Shirakatsi — the cosmos written in Armenian** — The name the tradition keeps is Anania Shirakatsi (7th century), mathematician and astronomer, whose writings — including a geography and works on the cosmos, the calendar and arithmetic — gathered the sky knowledge of his age in Armenian. He wrote of the Earth's sphericity, the Moon's borrowed light and the causes of eclipses in the scientific language of his time. One scholar with books matters to a course: he proves the watching was not folklore only — it was written, taught and argued science.
3. **Why the chapter starts at home** — The course-way point, the same one the Grade 7 book makes with its physicists: astronomy arrives to the Armenian pupil as an inheritance before it arrives as a syllabus. The stars over Armenia are the same stars the chapter will price in light-years — but they are first the stars its own calendar-keepers timed. From that ground the chapter walks outward: Sun, planets, Moon, stars, galaxies, the Universe — the local sky opening onto everything.
