# Landforms — hills, plains and valleys

## Do it first
Look out at the land around your home. Is it flat, or does it rise? Does a river cut through it? Every shape of that land has a name, and together the shapes are the land's relief. Say one shape you can see from where you stand. Then read on: this is Serbia's way in — the relief of my region.

## Sub lesson 1 — What relief is — and height above sea level

Serbia's book begins with the word itself: **relief** is the shape of the land — all its rises and hollows together. A place's height is measured as its **height above sea level**: how high the land stands above the sea. That measure is what lets one place be called higher or lower than another.

## Sub lesson 2 — The high forms — hillocks, hills and mountains

The land rises in steps of size: a small rise is a **hillock**, a bigger rise a **hill**, and the greatest rises are **mountains**. Hillock, hill, mountain — the same kind of form, growing: Serbia's way is to name them in that climbing order, on the learner's own region.

## Sub lesson 3 — The hollows and flat lands — river beds, gorges, canyons, plains, basins, valleys and plateaus

Against the rises stand the low forms: a **river bed**, where the water runs; deep cuts — **gorges** and **canyons**; wide flat **plains**; **basins**, low ground gathered inside higher ground; **valleys**, the low road a river makes between rises; and **plateaus**, high flat ground. Rises and hollows together — that full set is the relief of a region.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is this form a rise, a hollow, or flat land?" Answer: Name its size and shape: a small rise is a hillock, a bigger one a hill, the greatest a mountain; a river bed, gorge and canyon are cuts and hollows; a plain is wide and flat, a valley is the low way between rises.
