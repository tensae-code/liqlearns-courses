# Constellations and the stars we navigate by

## Do it first
Before you read: go to a window, or picture the night sky. The stars are too many to count. How could anyone learn them, or find their way by them? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Vanuatu's way in — its Year 3 science guide's Term 3 sky lessons name the constellations and the stars island children learn first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Constellations — the sky's named patterns

Stars are not learned one by one. They are learned in **patterns**.

A **constellation** is a group of stars that makes a pattern in the sky, with a name. When you know a constellation — its shape and its name — you can find that part of the sky again, night after night. The guide's lessons give the names of the constellations and look at the constellations up in the sky: naming the patterns is the first step to reading the sky at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — The first stars — the Southern Cross, the Morning and Evening Stars, and Sirius

Out of all the patterns, island children learn some stars first — the ones people know the sky by.

**The Southern Cross** — *Saoten kros* — the cross of stars in the southern sky, the best-known pattern of the southern seas.

**The Morning Star and the Evening Star** — *Moning sta mo Ivening sta* — the one bright star that shows near the morning and the one that shows near the evening; each is known by the time it keeps.

**Sirius** — *Sirus*, in the Big Dog — among the brightest stars in the whole sky, named in the guide beside its constellation.

These first stars are signposts. Sailors and travellers find their way by stars they know — a known star tells you where you are and which way you are facing. That is why the guide teaches them by name, in Bislama, before anything else about the sky: the stars we navigate by are learned first because they are used first.

## The key ideas

- A **constellation** is a named pattern of stars; learning the patterns is how the sky is first read.
- The first stars island children learn are the **Southern Cross**, the **Morning Star** and **Evening Star**, and **Sirius**.
- Each first star is known by something: the Cross by its shape, the Morning and Evening Stars by the time they show, Sirius by its brightness.
- Known stars are **signposts** — people navigate by the stars they know.
