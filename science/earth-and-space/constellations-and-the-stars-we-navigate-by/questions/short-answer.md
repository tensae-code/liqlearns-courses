# Constellations and the stars we navigate by — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is a constellation, and why are stars learned that way?
**Answer:** A constellation is a group of stars making a pattern in the sky, with a name. Stars are learned in patterns because a known shape and name lets you find that part of the sky again, night after night.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the first stars Vanuatu's Year 3 guide teaches.
**Answer:** The Southern Cross (Saoten kros), the Morning Star and the Evening Star (Moning sta mo Ivening sta), and Sirius, the bright star of the Big Dog.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How do you tell the Morning Star from the Evening Star?
**Answer:** By the time each one keeps: the Morning Star shows near the morning, and the Evening Star shows near the evening.

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why are known stars called signposts?
**Answer:** Because sailors and travellers find their way by stars they know — a known star tells you where you are and which way you are facing.
