# Hands-on — Constellations and the stars we navigate by

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## A first-stars night watch
On a clear night, go outside with an adult and look up for ten minutes without a torch. Let your eyes settle. Find the brightest star you can see, and any pattern of stars you can trace with a finger. Say which of the lesson's first stars you think you found, and what makes you think so.

## Draw the Southern Cross
Draw the Southern Cross from the lesson, then check your drawing against the sky or a star picture the next clear night. Count its stars, mark the two brightest, and write its Bislama name — Saoten kros — under your drawing.

## Morning star, evening star diary
For three days, note the bright star you can see just after sunset (the Evening Star) and, if you wake before sunrise, the bright star then (the Morning Star). Write the day, the time and where in the sky each one stood.
