# Rockets — and how they fly — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: What a rocket is — a machine that carries its push with it.
**Answer:** Eritrea's Grade 8 book opens its Space Travel unit with the atmosphere (printed p. 148), then teaches **rockets** (printed p. 151) as their own topic, before satellites and space exploration (printed p. 155). A rocket is the machine that makes space travel possible at all: unlike a car it needs no road and unlike a plane it needs no air to push against or to burn — it **carries everything it needs with it**, its fuel and the oxygen to burn it, so it can work even in the emptiness above the atmosphere where nothing outside can help it.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: How a rocket flies — gases down, rocket up.
**Answer:** A rocket flies by the law this same book taught earlier among Newton's laws: **for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction**. The rocket's engine throws burning gases **down** and back at great speed; the gases' push throws the rocket **up** and forwards in answer. The push is called **thrust**, and it does not come from pushing on the ground or the air — a rocket would fly in empty space exactly as it flies off the launch pad, which is why rockets, and only rockets, can leave the Earth.

**3.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** What a rocket is — a machine that carries its push with it → How a rocket flies — gases down, rocket up — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
