# Inside the Earth — crust, mantle, core

## Do it first

Look at a boiled egg cut in half. It has three parts, one inside another: a thin shell, a thick white, and a yolk at the centre. Hold that picture in mind. Nobody can cut the Earth in half — but everything we can measure says the Earth is built on the same three-part plan. This lesson takes the layers in order, from the one you stand on to the one at the centre.

## Sub lesson 1 — The crust

The **crust** is the Earth's outermost layer — the shell of the egg. It is made largely of **silicon and aluminium** compounds, and it is not the same thickness everywhere:

- **Continental crust** (under the land): **20–90 km thick**.
- **Oceanic crust** (under the oceans): only **5–10 km thick** — and **denser** than the continental crust.

Thin as it is, the crust carries everything you have ever seen: every mountain, farm, town and ocean floor is part of it or rests on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The mantle

Under the crust lies the **mantle** — the white of the egg, and by far the **thickest layer**: about **2,900 km** thick. It holds **83% of the Earth's whole volume**. The mantle is rich in **iron and magnesium**. When you read that the crust is measured in tens of kilometres and the mantle in thousands, you see the egg comparison is honest: a thin shell, then a great thick middle.

## Sub lesson 3 — The core

At the centre is the **core** — the yolk. It is made of **iron and nickel** and holds **16% of the Earth's volume**. It has two parts:

- an **outer core that is liquid**, and
- an **inner core that is solid**.

The core is also the source of the **Earth's magnetic field** — the invisible field that turns a compass needle and wraps the whole planet.

## Say it to your companion

Start at your feet and travel down. Tell your companion each layer as you reach it: its name, one number that belongs to it, and what it is made of. Your companion checks the order against the egg: shell, white, yolk — crust, mantle, core.
