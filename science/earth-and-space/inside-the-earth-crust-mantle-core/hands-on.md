# Hands-on — Inside the Earth — crust, mantle, core

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your reading.

## The egg dissection
A teacher or an adult cuts a boiled egg in half lengthways. Lay the two halves cut-side up. Point and name: shell, white, yolk. Then say the Earth sentence for each: "shell — crust, the layer we stand on; white — mantle, the thick middle; yolk — core, the centre." Draw the egg half in your book and write the Earth name beside each part.

## Layers in clay or dough
Make three balls of clay, dough or mud: a big orange one (mantle), a yellow one to fit inside it (core), and flatten a thin brown sheet (crust). Wrap the sheet around the orange ball, then cut your model Earth in half with a thread or a knife (adult helps) and check: does your model show the layers in the egg's order?

## How thin is the crust?
On a drawing of the Earth 12.7 cm across (the polar diameter shrunk a billion times), the thickest continental crust would be less than a tenth of a millimetre — thinner than your pencil line. Draw the circle, then try to draw the crust to scale. That is how thin the layer we live on really is.
