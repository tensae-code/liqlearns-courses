# Lesson plan — Inside the Earth — crust, mantle, core

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** Inside the Earth — crust, mantle, core
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — a new title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Earth, at General Science depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Earth has three layers — crust outside, mantle in the middle, core at the centre — each with its own thickness, contents and share of the whole, learned through the boiled-egg comparison.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Earth — shape, evidence and dimensions: the learner knows the ball and its size; this lesson opens the ball and looks inside.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The crust** — the outermost layer: what it is made of, and how its thickness differs under continents (20–90 km) and under oceans (5–10 km, and denser).
2. **The mantle** — the thickest layer: about 2,900 km thick, 83% of the Earth's volume, rich in iron and magnesium.
3. **The core** — iron and nickel at the centre: 16% of the volume, a liquid outer part and a solid inner part, and the source of the Earth's magnetic field.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Earth — shape, evidence and dimensions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three layers in order from outside to centre.
2. Give the crust's two thicknesses and say which is denser.
3. Give the mantle's thickness and its share of the volume.
4. Say what the core is made of, which part is liquid and which solid, and what the core gives the Earth.

## What you need
A boiled egg, a knife for the teacher to cut it with, and a picture of the cut Earth.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the mantle outside → go back to the egg: shell first, white next, yolk last. The Earth keeps the same order.
- Mixes the volume shares → the mantle is the thick layer, so it holds the big share (83%); the core, deep and small on the drawing, holds 16%.
- Thinks the crust is one thickness everywhere → the lesson's own contrast: thick under continents, thin and denser under oceans.
