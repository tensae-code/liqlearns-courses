# Hands-on — The Earth — the atmosphere and its five layers

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your reading.

## The hundred-breath pie
Draw a circle and divide it by eye: a little more than three quarters for nitrogen, most of the rest for oxygen, and the thinnest sliver you can draw for everything else together (argon, carbon dioxide and the rare gases). Your drawing is rough — the lesson picture is the measured one — but the sizes should shock you the same way: the gas we live on is only about a fifth of the air.

## Layer ladder on the wall
On a long strip of paper, mark five bands from the floor up: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere. Write on the borders between them: tropopause, stratopause, mesopause. Add three labels where they belong: "we live here" (band 1), "planes cruise here" (band 2, low), "−90 °C, the coldest top" (band 3, top). Stand at the strip and climb it aloud.

## Cloud watch
Watch clouds for a few minutes: all the weather you can see — clouds forming, rain falling, wind moving — happens in the lowest layer only. If a plane passes high and steady above the clouds, you are watching the second layer at work.
