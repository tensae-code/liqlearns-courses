# Lesson plan — The Earth — the atmosphere and its five layers

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Earth and Space · **Lesson:** The Earth — the atmosphere and its five layers
**Place in the curriculum:** Earth and Space — a new title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 6 — The Earth, at General Science depth (teacher-guide evidence).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the atmosphere — the air around the Earth — known two ways: by what it is made of (mostly nitrogen, then oxygen, with argon, carbon dioxide and the rare gases) and by its five stacked layers, from the troposphere we live in to the exosphere.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Earth — four spheres, one system: the learner knows the atmosphere as the air sphere; this lesson opens that sphere up — its mixture and its layers. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only: the student book does not carry this subtopic, and the lesson is labelled so wherever it is used.

Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **What the air is made of** — the composition: nitrogen about 78%, oxygen about 21%, argon as printed in the guide, carbon dioxide about 0.03%, plus neon, helium, krypton and xenon.
2. **The five layers** — troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere and exosphere, lowest to highest, with their boundaries (tropopause, stratopause, mesopause), the mesosphere's cold top (about −90 °C), the layer we live in and the layer planes fly in.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Earth — four spheres, one system. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the air's composition in order, largest share first.
2. Name the five layers from the ground up, with the three named boundaries between them.
3. Say which layer we live in and which layer planes cruise in.
4. Say which layer's top is the coldest place in the atmosphere, and how cold.

## What you need
Nothing but the sky, a window or doorway to watch clouds from, and (if one passes) an aeroplane to spot.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the stack top-down → always climb from your feet: you live in layer 1, the troposphere; that fixes the order.
- Swaps stratosphere and mesosphere → the plane is the anchor: planes cruise in the stratosphere (layer 2); the coldest top (−90 °C) caps the mesosphere (layer 3).
- Asks where this lesson comes from → it is labelled honestly: teacher-guide evidence (see lesson/sources.md); the student book does not carry this subtopic.
