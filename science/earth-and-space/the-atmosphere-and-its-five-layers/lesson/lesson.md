# The Earth — the atmosphere and its five layers

## Do it first

Go outside and look up for a full minute. Everything you see happening — clouds building, wind moving them, perhaps a plane crossing high above — happens inside one blanket: the **atmosphere**, the air wrapped around the Earth. This lesson takes that blanket apart two ways: what it is made of, and how it is stacked. *(This whole subtopic comes from the teacher guide — the student book does not carry it — and the lesson is labelled so in its sources.)*

## Sub lesson 1 — What the air is made of

The air is a mixture of gases, in these shares:

- **Nitrogen — about 78%**: most of every breath you take.
- **Oxygen — about 21%**: the share living things use.
- **Argon — printed as 0.009%** in the guide's table (the lesson teaches the guide's table as printed; that figure is flagged in the sources).
- **Carbon dioxide — about 0.03%**: a small share with a large job in the carbon cycle.
- Plus traces of **neon, helium, krypton and xenon**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five layers

From the ground upward, the atmosphere is stacked in **five major layers**:

1. **Troposphere** — the lowest layer: **we live in it**, and weather happens in it. Its top boundary is the **tropopause**.
2. **Stratosphere** — above it; **planes cruise in its lower part**, above the weather. Its top boundary is the **stratopause**.

[GIF 33114 — a plane cruising in the lower stratosphere above the weather clouds]

3. **Mesosphere** — above that; **its top is the coldest place in the atmosphere, about −90 °C**, under the **mesopause** boundary.
4. **Thermosphere** — the fourth layer up.
5. **Exosphere** — the fifth, outermost layer, thinning away into space.

[Picture 33105 — the five layers stacked, with their boundaries]

Learn the climb with its anchors: your feet in the troposphere, the plane in the stratosphere, the deepest cold at the mesosphere's top.

## Say it to your companion

Climb the atmosphere for your companion without looking: troposphere — tropopause — stratosphere — stratopause — mesosphere — mesopause — thermosphere — exosphere. Then tell them what the air is made of, biggest share first.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the composition pie from memory and label the four main shares. Check against Sub lesson 1.
2. Write the five layers in order, ground up, and mark: where we live, where planes cruise, where the −90 °C cold sits.
3. A plane passenger looks down on clouds. Which layer is the plane in, which layer are the clouds in, and what is the boundary between them called?
