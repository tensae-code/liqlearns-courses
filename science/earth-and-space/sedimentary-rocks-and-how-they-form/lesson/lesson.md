# Sedimentary rocks and how they form

## Do it first
Before you read: look at a cliff, a road cutting, or a photograph of one, where the rock lies in flat bands, one on top of another like the layers of a cake. Where could bands like that come from? Were they poured, grown, or laid down one at a time? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Haiti's way in — its Grade 8 Géologie sequence defines a sedimentary rock by where it forms and by the layers it forms in, then teaches the whole cycle that makes it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a sedimentary rock is — surface-made, in horizontal layers

A **sedimentary rock** is a rock **formed at the Earth's surface** — not deep inside the Earth, but out where land, air and water meet.

Its forming places are waters: **oceans, seas, lakes and rivers**. In those waters, material settles — and it does not settle in a heap. It settles **in horizontal layers**, flat sheet on flat sheet. Those layers are called **strata**.

That is the definition to hold: surface-formed, water-laid, layered. A rock that wears its strata openly is announcing how it was born.

## Sub lesson 2 — From erosion to sedimentation — the formation cycle

The layers were not always layers. The sequence teaches their making as a **formation cycle**, and the cycle runs in order:

- **Erosion** — older rock and land at the surface are worn away, broken into material that can be moved.
- **Carrying** — the worn material is transported, chiefly by water, away from where it was worn.
- **Sedimentation** — in an ocean, a sea, a lake or a river the carrying slows, and the material is let fall and settles — flat, in horizontal layers, stratum upon stratum.
- **Burial and hardening** — later layers bury the earlier ones, and in time the buried sediment becomes solid sedimentary rock — the cycle's end, and the layered rock of sub lesson 1.

So the cycle and the definition are one story told two ways: erosion to sedimentation makes the strata, and the strata in a finished rock are the proof the cycle ran.

## The key ideas

- A **sedimentary rock** forms **at the Earth's surface**, in **oceans, seas, lakes and rivers**.
- It is laid down in **horizontal layers** called **strata**.
- Its **formation cycle** runs from **erosion** — material worn away — through carrying, to **sedimentation** — material let fall in water, layer on layer — and on to buried, hardened rock.
- Layers are evidence: strata in a rock record the cycle that made it.

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — what a sedimentary rock is first, then the cycle from erosion to sedimentation. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Where did that layer settle?" Answer with one of the four waters, not by repeating.
