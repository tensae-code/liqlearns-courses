# Hands-on — Sedimentary rocks and how they form

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Layers in a jar (kitchen materials)
Half-fill a clear jar with water. Add a spoonful each of sand, soil and small gravel, shake gently, then let it stand undisturbed. Watch what settles, in what order, and how the settled material lies — in flat, horizontal layers. Label a drawing of your jar with the word strata, and write one sentence: this settling is sedimentation, the middle of the formation cycle.

## Read a layered rock or bank
Find a road cutting, river bank or layered stone where flat layers show. Count the layers you can see, note whether they lie horizontal, and write the cycle backwards for the bottom layer: where was this material worn from, how was it carried, and in what water — ocean, sea, lake or river — could it have settled?

## Cycle cards
On five cards write: erosion, carrying, sedimentation, burial in layers, rock. Shuffle them, then lay them in the cycle's order and say each step aloud in one sentence before you turn to the next card.
