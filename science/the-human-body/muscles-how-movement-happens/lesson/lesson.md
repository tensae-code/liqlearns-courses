# Muscles — how movement happens

## Do it first
Put one hand flat on the front of your upper arm. Bend the elbow slowly, bringing your fist toward your shoulder. Feel the front of the arm swell and harden. Now straighten the arm slowly and feel the front soften while the back of the arm works. Something is trading places in there. The lesson names the traders.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three kinds of muscle
Muscle is tissue that can contract — shorten and thicken — and so pull. The body runs three kinds:

- **Skeletal muscle** — fixed to the bones; it moves the skeleton. This is the muscle of arms, legs, face and trunk.
- **Cardiac muscle** — found only in the heart; it squeezes the heart's chambers and never tires.
- **Smooth muscle** — in the walls of inner organs and tubes: the gut that squeezes food along, the vessels, the bladder.

Same trick — contraction — three workplaces.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pairs that pull — and who gives the orders
A muscle can only **pull**; it cannot push. So at every joint, muscles are arranged in **pairs** that work against each other. At the elbow: the **flexor** (the biceps, in front) pulls the forearm up, bending the joint; the **extensor** (the triceps, behind) pulls it back down, straightening it. When one contracts, the other relaxes — then they trade. Bend, straighten, bend: the trade you felt in the Do It First.

Who gives the orders? Some muscle is **voluntary** — you order it: lifting, walking, writing (skeletal muscle). Some is **involuntary** — it runs itself, unasked: the heart's cardiac muscle, the gut's smooth muscle. You cannot order your heart to stop, and it would be a burden if you had to order every squeeze of your gut.

## Sub lesson 3 — When muscles fail — and the grass-pea warning
Muscles and their joints have their own troubles:

- **Strain** — a muscle pulled too far or torn by overwork.
- **Sprain** — the ligament at a joint stretched or torn, as in a twisted ankle.
- **Spasm** — a muscle contracting suddenly and painfully, out of control (a cramp).
- **Dystrophy** — a disorder in which muscle wastes and weakens over time.

And this country carries one stark case of its own. **Grass pea (guaya)** is a hardy crop whose seeds hold a **neurotoxin**. People who eat it heavily over a long time can be crippled by it — the legs lose their working, and the harm does not heal. Local beliefs about the cause are weighed against the evidence: the paralysis follows heavy eating of the seed, and where eating is lighter or preparation is careful, it is rare.

The mitigations are concrete: cook in **metal pots**; add **antioxidant seasonings**; **soak the seeds in lemon water** before cooking; and **avoid unripe seeds**. The crop is not cursed and it is not useless — it is a food to be prepared with knowledge.

## Do It Yourself
1. Pair check: at your knee, feel which muscle contracts as you bend the leg back and which as you straighten it. Name flexor and extensor for the knee.
2. Sort the actions: heartbeat, chewing, the gut squeezing food, raising a hand, the pupil shrinking in bright light — write V (voluntary) or I (involuntary) beside each.
3. Warning card: write the grass-pea warning as a card for a cook: the harm in one line, the four safer steps in order.
