This is the story of a pea — and a warning.

In hard years, when other crops fail, one crop still grows: the **grass pea**, called **guaya**.
It survives drought. It fills the pot. Families lean on it — sometimes for months, eating little else.

But the seeds carry a hidden thing: a **neurotoxin** — a poison that attacks the nerves.
Eat grass pea heavily, for a long time, and the toxin can cripple the legs.
People who could walk — cannot. This is **grass-pea paralysis**. It does not heal.

Some say: it is fate. Some say: it is a curse.
But look at the evidence. The sickness follows the eating — heavy eating, long eating, of this one seed.
Where people eat little of it, or prepare it carefully, the sickness is rare.
Belief must answer to evidence. The cause is in the seed.

So — how do people protect themselves? Four ways. Say them with me.

**One.** Cook in **metal pots** — the metal helps draw the toxin down.
**Two.** Add **antioxidant seasonings** to the food.
**Three.** Soak the seeds in **lemon water** before cooking.
**Four.** Do not eat the **unripe seeds**.

Grass pea is food — in a hard year, it can be life.
But eat it with knowledge. Prepare it safely. Do not let one food carry the whole diet for months.

The pea is not cursed. It is chemistry — and chemistry can be handled wisely.
