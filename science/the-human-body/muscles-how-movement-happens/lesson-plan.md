# Lesson plan — Muscles — how movement happens

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Muscles — how movement happens
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Human Body Systems and Health, the movement system, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** muscles move the body by pulling in pairs — skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle; a flexor contracts while its extensor partner relaxes — and the lesson carries the injuries (strain, sprain, spasm), the disorders, and the local grass-pea (guaya) paralysis warning with its safer preparation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The human body — systems working together and beside Skin — the integumentary system; it is learned best together with The skeleton — frame, joints and fractures, the frame the muscles pull on. The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (Cardiac, skeletal and smooth muscle; Muscle contraction — sliding filaments); this lesson is the Grade 8 version, built new.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Three kinds of muscle** — skeletal muscle on the bones, cardiac muscle in the heart, smooth muscle in the walls of inner organs.
2. **Pairs that pull — and who gives the orders** — flexor/extensor pairs trading contraction at a joint; voluntary control (you order it) against involuntary control (it runs itself).
3. **When muscles fail — and the grass-pea warning** — strain, sprain, spasm and dystrophy; and grass pea (guaya): the neurotoxin in its seeds, the local beliefs weighed against evidence, and the safer preparation — metal cooking pots, antioxidant seasonings, lemon-water soaking, avoiding unripe seeds.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three muscle kinds and where each works.
2. Show and explain a flexor/extensor pair trading contraction as a joint bends and straightens.
3. Tell voluntary from involuntary muscle action with an example of each.
4. Name strain, sprain and spasm, and retell the grass-pea story — toxin, harm, and the safer preparation — weighing local belief against evidence.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks muscles push → muscles only pull; that is exactly why they are arranged in pairs — one pulls the joint each way.
- Mixes strain and sprain → strain hurts the muscle itself; sprain hurts the ligament at a joint.
