# Lesson plan — Fertilisation, pregnancy and birth

**Course:** Science · **Title:** the-human-body · **Lesson:** Fertilisation, pregnancy and birth
**Place in the curriculum:** The Human Body — appended to Unit 3 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fertilisation, pregnancy and birth — how one sperm and one ovum form the zygote, the new individual's first cell, how the baby grows protected in the womb, and how birth brings it out — with the flowering plants' double fertilisation as the parallel the same books teach
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

## Sub lessons
1. **Fertilisation — one sperm enters the ovum and forms the zygote** — Nicaragua's Grade 6 book teaches it plainly: **one sperm enters the ovum and forms the zygote, the new individual's first cell**. From that one cell, division after division builds the whole new being.
2. **Pregnancy and birth — the amniotic sac, the placenta, and the way out** — In pregnancy the **amniotic sac protects the baby**. The Grade 9 book draws the stages — **morula, blastula, gastrula** — stage by stage: the **chorion's villi enter the endometrium to form the placenta**, passing **nutrients, oxygen and antibodies** from mother to child. At **birth** the sac breaks, contractions come, and the baby is expelled.
3. **Double fertilisation in flowering plants — the plant parallel** — The Grade 9 Módulo Autoformativo teaches the plants' own version: in **double fertilisation** in angiosperms, **one antherozoid (male cell) joins the oosphere to form the zygote and embryo, and the second forms the nutritive tissue** that will feed it — two joins in one event, which is why it is called double.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks many sperm form the baby → return to sub lesson 1: one sperm enters the ovum; that join forms the zygote, the first cell.
- Mixes the human and plant stories up → return to sub lesson 3: double fertilisation is the flowering plants' parallel — in people, one join forms the zygote; in flowering plants, two joins form the embryo and its food tissue.
