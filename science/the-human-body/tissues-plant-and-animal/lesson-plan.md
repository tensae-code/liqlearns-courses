# Lesson plan — Tissues — plant and animal

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Tissues — plant and animal
**Place in the curriculum:** The Human Body — appended to Unit 3 (The Human Body) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a tissue is — a group of like cells joined to do one job — and how tissues are classified, plant tissues against animal tissues, with the animal tissues named in the Dominican Republic's Grade 6 Ciencias de la Naturaleza book, nervous and reticular connective tissue among them
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **What a tissue is** — a single cell does its work alone, but a living thing's bigger jobs are done by cells working together.
2. **Plant tissues and animal tissues** — **Plant tissues** are the cell teams that make up a plant's parts — the teams of the root, the stem and the leaf that the learner has already met as plant organs: a plant's organs are built of its tissues, as a wall is built of bricks.

**Animal tissues** are the cell teams of an animal's body, and the book names kinds among them: **nervous tissue**, the team's signalling cells that carry messages, and **reticular connective tissue**, a net-like supporting tissue that holds other cells in place.
