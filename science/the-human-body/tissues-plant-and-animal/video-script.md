Today: **Tissues — plant and animal** — the Dominican Republic's way: what a tissue is.

A single cell does its work alone, but a living thing's bigger jobs are done by cells working together. A tissue is a group of like cells, joined together, that do one job as a team. The Grade 6 book defines tissues and then classifies them — sorts them into kinds. The first great sorting is by the living thing they belong to: plant tissues in plants, animal tissues in animals. Same idea — cells teamed for one job — in two different living worlds. Plant tissues are the cell teams that make up a plant's parts — the teams of the root, the stem and the leaf that the learner has already met as plant organs: a plant's organs are built of its tissues, as a wall is built of bricks. Animal tissues are the cell teams of an animal's body, and the book names kinds among them: nervous tissue, the team's signalling cells that carry messages, and reticular connective tissue, a net-like supporting tissue that holds other cells in place. Naming the kinds is the classification at work: each tissue known by its cells and its job.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
