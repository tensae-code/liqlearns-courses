# Tissues — plant and animal

## Do it first
Before you read: How can one word — tissue — cover both a leaf and a nerve? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is the Dominican Republic's way in — its Libro Abierto Ciencias de la Naturaleza books teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a tissue is

A single cell does its work alone, but a living thing's bigger jobs are done by cells working together. A **tissue** is a group of like cells, joined together, that do one job as a team.

The Grade 6 book defines tissues and then **classifies** them — sorts them into kinds. The first great sorting is by the living thing they belong to: **plant tissues** in plants, **animal tissues** in animals. Same idea — cells teamed for one job — in two different living worlds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plant tissues and animal tissues

**Plant tissues** are the cell teams that make up a plant's parts — the teams of the root, the stem and the leaf that the learner has already met as plant organs: a plant's organs are built of its tissues, as a wall is built of bricks.

**Animal tissues** are the cell teams of an animal's body, and the book names kinds among them: **nervous tissue**, the team's signalling cells that carry messages, and **reticular connective tissue**, a net-like supporting tissue that holds other cells in place. Naming the kinds is the classification at work: each tissue known by its cells and its job.

## The key ideas

- A **tissue** is a group of like cells joined to do one job.
- Tissues are classified first as **plant tissues** and **animal tissues**.
- An organ is built of tissues, as a wall is built of bricks.
- Named animal tissues in the book include **nervous tissue** and **reticular connective tissue**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How can one word — tissue — cover both a leaf and a nerve?" Answer from the lesson's definition: in both, like cells are joined to do one job — the leaf's teams and the nerve's signalling cells are the same idea in two living worlds.
