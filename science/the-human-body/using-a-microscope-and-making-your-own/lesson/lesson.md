# The microscope — using it well, and making your own

## Do it first
Before you touch a real microscope (or the diagram), practise the carry in the air: one hand grips an imaginary arm, the other slides flat underneath as the base. Hold it level. That two-handed carry is the first skill of this lesson — try it three times.

## Sub lesson 1 — Carrying and care rules
- **Carry by the arm and the base together** — one hand on the arm, the other flat under the base. Never carry it by the tube alone.
- **Never touch the lens glass** with your fingers — grease blurs the image and is hard to clean.
- **Never aim the mirror at direct sun** — the concentrated light can damage your eye and the specimen.

Treat it as borrowed treasure: clean, level, two hands.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mounting and focusing
Mounting:
1. Put a **hair or a fibre** on the middle of a clean slide.
2. Add **one drop of water** over it.
3. Lower a **cover slip** gently at an angle over the drop — **always use a cover slip**.

Focusing:
1. Start on the **lowest power** objective.
2. Turn the **coarse adjustment knob** to bring the specimen into first focus.
3. Move to **medium, then high power** — and there use the **fine adjustment knob only** to sharpen.

## Sub lesson 3 — Building a bottle microscope
You can build a working **simple microscope** from things at home:
1. Cut the top section from a **clear plastic bottle** to make a stand that holds a slide strip at its base (an adult helps with cutting).
2. Use **clear plastic strips as slides** — put your fibre or hair on one, with a drop of water.
3. For the lens, rest a single **drop of water** in a small hole or loop above the specimen — the round drop bulges like a convex lens and **magnifies** what is under it.
4. Hold it up to the light and look through the drop: the specimen appears bigger — a simple microscope, made by you.

## Say it to your companion
Talk your companion through the whole job without touching anything: carry, mount, focus low, sharpen high — then how the bottle microscope's water drop works as a lens.

## Do It Yourself
1. Carry check: lift and carry the microscope (or a stand-in object of similar shape) across the room by arm and base, level, twice.
2. Mount and focus a hair or fibre: water drop, cover slip, low power + coarse, then fine only. Draw what you see.
3. Build the bottle microscope and view the same fibre through its water-drop lens. Compare the two views in two sentences.
