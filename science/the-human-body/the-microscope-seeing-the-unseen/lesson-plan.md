# Lesson plan — The microscope — seeing the unseen

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** The microscope — seeing the unseen
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Cell as the Basis of Life, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a microscope makes tiny things big enough to see — pond life, parasites in clinic samples, and cells themselves were all unseen before it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's naked-eye world — everything they have seen with their own eyes so far. The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (The microscope — seeing the small world); this lesson is the first-depth Grade 7 version, not a copy of it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student’s guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Why we need a microscope** — what the naked eye cannot see: pond life, parasites in clinic stool and blood samples, and cells.
2. **The first compound microscope — Janssen and Hooke** — the Janssen father-and-son lens tube of the 1590s, and Hooke's improved microscope of 1665.
3. **Leeuwenhoek's single lens** — Leeuwenhoek's single-lens instruments of the 1670s, which out-resolved the compound ones.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: any earlier Human Body lesson — the outside parts first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a microscope does: it enlarges what the naked eye cannot see.
2. Name three things only a microscope can show: pond life, parasites in clinic samples, and cells.
3. Tell the history in order: Janssen's tube (1590s), Hooke's improvement (1665), Leeuwenhoek's single lens (1670s).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a glass of pond or tap water to look at with the naked eye first.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks bigger is always newer → Leeuwenhoek's tiny single lens (1670s) saw more than the bigger compound tubes before it; size of the instrument is not sharpness.
- Mixes Janssen and Hooke → Janssen made the first compound tube in the 1590s; Hooke improved the compound microscope much later, in 1665.
