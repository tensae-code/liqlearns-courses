# Teeth: two sets, four jobs

## Do it first
Count your teeth with your tongue. A child who does it carefully finds fewer teeth than a grown person — because you are still on your first set. This lesson names both sets, and the four jobs teeth do.

## Two sets in one life
A life holds **two sets of teeth**. First comes the **milk set: 20 teeth**, with three kinds of tooth. After it comes the **permanent set: 32 teeth**, the set a grown person keeps. The last molars of the permanent set arrive only after childhood — they are the latecomers at the very back.

## Four kinds, four jobs
The permanent set holds **four kinds** of tooth, and each kind has its own job. The **incisors** at the front **bite and cut**. The **canines** beside them **pierce and tear**. The **premolars** **crush**. The **molars** at the back **grind** the food small. Cutting, tearing, crushing, grinding — the mouth's workshop does all four before the food is swallowed.

## Caring for teeth
Teeth are cared for by **cleaning them morning and evening**. The book pictures two good tools side by side: the **local chewing stick** and the **factory toothbrush**. Either one, used morning and evening, keeps the workshop clean.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the two sets and their numbers, then walk across one row of teeth naming each kind and its job — bite and cut, pierce and tear, crush, grind.

## Do It Yourself
1. Mirror count: count your upper teeth and your lower teeth and add them. Which set are you on — and how can you tell?
2. Job test at a meal: bite with the front teeth, tear, crush, grind — notice which teeth do which job, in the book's order.
3. Morning-and-evening cleaning: clean your teeth with a chewing stick or a brush, morning and evening, for a week — the book's own care rule.
