# Adolescence and its changes

## Do it first
A 9-year-old and a 15-year-old are both 'children' to a stranger — but their bodies and feelings are far apart. Between them lies adolescence, the fast bridge from child to adult. This lesson walks the bridge with the book's own table.

## A transition of fast change
**Adolescence (ወጣትነት)** arrives at about **14–16 years for boys and 12–14 for girls**. It is a transition of **fast physical and emotional change**: quick emotionality, a new valuing of music and film, and new dreams for the future. The book sorts its marks in two: the **primary** characteristics — **the reproductive organs themselves** — and the **secondary** characteristics — the physical and emotional changes that arrive with adolescence.

## The book's table of changes
**Boys:** hair at the genitals, armpits and face (a beard); a growth spurt; the voice deepening; muscles growing and shoulders broadening. **Girls:** hair at the genitals and armpits; breasts developing; hips widening; a growth spurt; the voice softening; and menstruation beginning. Every change in the table is normal, and none of them is a defect or a disease.

## Say it to your companion
Read the table to your companion, one side each: you take boys, they take girls, then swap. Finish by naming the changes both share.

## Do It Yourself
1. Two-column chart: copy the book's table into your notebook — boys on one side, girls on the other — from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. Primary or secondary? Sort these: the ovaries; a deepening voice; the testes; widening hips; a growth spurt. (The organs themselves are primary; the rest are secondary.)
3. Timeline: mark the two age bands — girls about 12–14, boys about 14–16 — on a line from age 8 to age 20.
