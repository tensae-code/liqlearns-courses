# Lesson plan — Adolescence and its changes

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Adolescence and its changes
**Place in the curriculum:** The Human Body — growing up, in the book's plain table (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** adolescence (ወጣትነት) is the fast change from child to adult — about 14–16 for boys and 12–14 for girls — and its changes split into the primary characteristics (the reproductive organs themselves) and the secondary ones the book's table lists
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The human reproductive system — those organs are the primary characteristics; this lesson adds the changes that arrive with them.

## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Human Body — The human reproductive system. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say when adolescence arrives: about 14–16 years for boys, about 12–14 for girls.
2. Tell the primary characteristics (the reproductive organs themselves) apart from the secondary characteristics (the changes of adolescence).
3. Recite the book's table: the changes in boys and the changes in girls, side by side.

## What you need
Only the lesson; the changes are normal, expected, and shared by everyone growing up.

## If the learner is stuck
- Ashamed of a change → every change in the table happens to everyone growing up; the book prints them plainly because they are normal.
- Mixes primary and secondary → primary = the organs themselves; secondary = the changes that arrive around them.
