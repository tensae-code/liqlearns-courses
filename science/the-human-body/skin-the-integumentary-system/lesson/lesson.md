# Skin — the integumentary system

## Do it first
Pinch the skin on the back of your hand and lift it gently. Look at a fingernail. Pull one loose hair and look at its root end. Before any names are given: what jobs is this covering already doing for you, right now? List everything you can — the lesson will check your list.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three layers, and keratin — hair and nails
Skin is built in three layers, like a wall in three courses.

On top lies the **epidermis** — the thin outer layer you see and touch, always renewing itself. Beneath it lies the **dermis** — the thick middle layer, holding the roots of hairs, the sweat glands, and the sense endings that feel touch. Deepest lies the **subcutaneous fat** — a soft layer that cushions the body and keeps its warmth in.

**Hair** and **nails** are skin's own building work. Both are built of **keratin**, a strong protein: hair grows from roots set in the dermis, and nails grow from their base, plate by plate, of hardened keratin.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five jobs of skin
Skin is not a passive cover. It does five jobs:

1. **Protection** — it is the body's first wall against germs, knocks and harmful rays.
2. **Temperature control** — it helps hold the body at its working warmth: sweat cools the skin as it dries, and the fat layer holds warmth in.
3. **Sensation** — endings in the dermis feel touch, pressure, heat, cold and pain, and warn the body.
4. **Sweating** — sweat glands in the dermis carry water and some wastes out through the skin.
5. **Holding water in** — the outer layer keeps the body's water from escaping; without skin, the body would dry out.

## Sub lesson 3 — When skin is in trouble — and caring for it
Each common trouble has a cause and a care:

- **Burns** — heat, sun or chemicals destroy skin layers; cool the burn with clean cool water and cover it, and take deep or wide burns to a clinic.
- **Athlete's foot** — a fungus that grows between the toes where feet stay warm and damp; keep feet clean and dry.
- **Ultraviolet light and skin cancer** — too much strong sun damages skin over years and can end in skin cancer; shade and covering protect.
- **Acne** — blocked skin pores, common in the teenage years; gentle washing helps, squeezing makes it worse.
- **Eczema** — inflamed, itchy skin that flares with irritants and allergens.

For inflamed skin, the care routine taught to teachers runs in order: wash with cool water daily; apply cool compresses; limit contact with common allergens; use wet wipes gently; avoid smoking and alcohol; keep the affected area covered and the skin moist; avoid long sun exposure. Redness can be a sign of eczema or of rosacea — if it stays, the follow-up is a clinic visit. Teachers' assessment lists also name chickenpox and latex allergy among skin conditions.

And one mechanism worth knowing: **hair insulates two ways** — it stands as a physical barrier between cold air and skin, and it traps a layer of warm air against the skin.
