# The social self

## Do it first
You are one person alone — and somehow also a slightly different person with your friends, your family, your class. Australia's Step-up chapter closes with the social self. What part of you could be 'social'? Say your answer out loud. Then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The social self — the self among others

The **social self** is the self as it exists among other people: the part of who we are that is formed, shown and changed in our relations with others — how we see ourselves through others' eyes, and how we present ourselves to them. Australia's book gives it the closing place in its psychology chapter (11.3): after the mind and the brain, the person placed back among people.

## Sub lesson 2 — The chapter's arc — mind, brain, society

Australia's own order carries the meaning: what psychology is (11.1) names the science; the brain and cognition (11.2) give the mind its organ; the social self (11.3) completes the person — a self is not finished inside one skull, but in the company it keeps. The Step-up chapter that began by asking what psychology is ends where psychology always ends: people, together.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why does the social self close the chapter?" Answer: Because the person is completed among people: mind first (11.1), then its organ, the brain (11.2), then the self among others (11.3).
