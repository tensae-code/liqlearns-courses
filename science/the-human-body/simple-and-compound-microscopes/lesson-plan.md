# Lesson plan — The microscope — simple and compound, and total magnification

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** The microscope — simple and compound, and total magnification
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Cell as the Basis of Life, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a simple microscope has one lens and magnifies about 10–20×; a compound microscope multiplies eyepiece by objective — total magnification is their product.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The microscope — seeing the unseen (the tool and its history). The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (Parts of the light microscope); this lesson is the first-depth Grade 7 version.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The simple microscope — one lens** — one convex lens: the hand lens, reading glasses, Leeuwenhoek's lens — about 10–20×; Practical 4.1 views of insects and book letters.
2. **The compound microscope and total magnification** — eyepiece (usually 10×) times objective (4×–100×) gives the total magnification.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The microscope — seeing the unseen. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a simple microscope is and name two examples.
2. State the usual simple-microscope magnification: about 10–20×.
3. Work out total magnification as eyepiece × objective, e.g. 10× × 40× = 400×.

## What you need
A hand lens or reading glasses if available; a book with small print; paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds eyepiece and objective instead of multiplying → total magnification is a product: 10× × 40× = 400×, not 50×.
- Thinks reading glasses are not a microscope → one convex lens that magnifies IS a simple microscope; reading glasses are one.
