# Lesson plan — Cells — respiration: releasing energy from food

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Cells — respiration: releasing energy from food
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Cell as the Basis of Life, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** cells release energy from glucose with oxygen in the mitochondria, making ATP; without enough oxygen they ferment instead — making alcohol or lactic acid, with far less energy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Cells — inside a cell: organelles and their jobs (the mitochondrion). The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (Cellular respiration; Anaerobic respiration and fermentation); this lesson is the first-depth Grade 7 version.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Aerobic respiration — the mitochondrion as engine** — glucose + oxygen → energy (ATP) + carbon dioxide + water, in the mitochondria, the cell's powerhouses.
2. **Anaerobic respiration — two kinds of fermentation** — alcoholic fermentation (alcohol + CO₂) and lactic-acid fermentation (lactic acid + CO₂); human muscles switch to it during hard exercise when oxygen runs short; far less energy than aerobic.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write the aerobic respiration word equation and name where it happens.
2. Say why aerobic respiration gives far more energy than anaerobic.
3. Name both fermentation kinds and say when human muscles use lactic-acid fermentation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks respiration is just breathing → breathing brings the oxygen in; cellular respiration is the chemistry inside the mitochondria that releases the energy.
- Thinks fermentation gives the same energy → it gives far less; that is why hard exercise cannot be kept up for long without oxygen.
