# Cells — respiration: releasing energy from food

## Do it first
Run hard on the spot for one full minute. Feel your breathing race and your leg muscles burn and tire. Your muscles are asking for more energy than your breathing can supply. Remember that burn — this lesson explains exactly what is happening inside your cells.

## Sub lesson 1 — Aerobic respiration — the mitochondrion as engine
Every cell needs energy to live, grow and work. It gets that energy by breaking down **glucose** (a sugar from food) with **oxygen**. This is **cellular respiration**, and it happens in the **mitochondria** — the cell's **"powerhouses"**:

**Glucose + oxygen → energy (ATP) + carbon dioxide + water**

The energy is stored in a carrier called **ATP**, ready for the cell to spend. The carbon dioxide and water are the waste — you breathe the carbon dioxide out.

Respiration **with** oxygen is called **aerobic** respiration, and it yields **far more energy** from each glucose than respiration without oxygen.

## Sub lesson 2 — Anaerobic respiration — two kinds of fermentation
Without oxygen, cells can still release a little energy — **anaerobic** respiration, or **fermentation**. It comes in two kinds:
- **Alcoholic fermentation** — glucose → **alcohol + carbon dioxide**. Yeast does this: it is how tella, tej and bread dough get their alcohol and their rising gas.
- **Lactic-acid fermentation** — glucose → **lactic acid + carbon dioxide** (as the lesson's book presents it). Your **muscles switch to this during hard exercise**, when the blood cannot deliver oxygen fast enough — that switch is the burn you felt in the Do It First, and the tiredness that forces you to rest.

Both kinds yield **far less energy** than aerobic respiration — a short-term backup, never a full replacement.
