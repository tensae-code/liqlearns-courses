# Cells — inside a cell: organelles and their jobs

## Do it first
Think of your school. It has a fence or gate, rooms, an office that runs it, a kitchen or store, messengers. Now hold that picture: this lesson shows that a cell is organised the same way — each part with its own job. As you read, match every cell part to the school part it works like.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four features every cell has
Every cell — plant, animal, bacterium — has these four:
- **Cell membrane** — the **gatekeeper**: a thin skin around the cell that controls what enters and leaves. (The school gate.)
- **Cytoplasm** — the **jelly** filling the cell, holding all the working parts (organelles) inside it. (The school compound.)
- **DNA** — the **instructions**: the information for making new cells and for running all the cell's functions. (The office's plans and registers.)
- **Ribosomes** — the **protein makers**: tiny builders that make proteins, the cell's working materials. (The workshop.)

## Sub lesson 2 — The organelles and their jobs
Inside the cytoplasm, each **organelle** — each little organ — does one job:
- **Nucleus** — the control centre; it holds the DNA and directs the cell. (The head's office.)
- **Mitochondria** — release energy from food (see the respiration lesson). (The kitchen fires.)
- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** — a folded membrane network that makes and moves materials. (The corridors and store paths.)
- **Golgi body** — finishes, packs and sends out proteins. (The packing and dispatch desk.)
- **Vacuole** — a storage space; in plant cells it is one large central vacuole holding cell sap. (The store room.)
- **Lysosome** — digests worn-out parts and invaders with enzymes. (The cleaning and repair crew.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Plant-only parts, and building a cell model
Plant and animal cells share everything above. A **plant cell** has two more parts an animal cell never has:
- **Cell wall** — a strong outer wall outside the membrane, giving shape and support.
- **Chloroplasts** — the green parts where photosynthesis happens (see the photosynthesis lesson).

In the picture, the shared parts wear one colour and the plant-only parts wear a second colour — that colour split IS the plant/animal difference.

**Build a cell model** from local materials: a **plastic bag** as the membrane filled with jelly-like material as cytoplasm; **cardboard** as the plant cell wall; **beans or stones** as mitochondria; a **button** as the nucleus; string as the ER. Label every material with the part it stands for.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion around your model (or the picture): name each part, give its job, and finish by naming the two plant-only parts and their colour in the picture.

## Do It Yourself
1. Build the cell model from a plastic bag, cardboard, beans/stones, buttons and local materials; label every part.
2. Write the school-as-cell matching as a list: gate, compound, office, workshop, kitchen, store — each beside its cell part.
3. Cover the picture's labels and name all the parts of both cells; mark the two plant-only ones with a star.
