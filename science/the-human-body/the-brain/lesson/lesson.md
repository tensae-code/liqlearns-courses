# The brain

## Do it first
Tap your head, gently. Under the skin your fingers meet something hard — bone. Why would the body build a hard case at the very top? Because inside it sits the softest, most important part of all: the brain.

## The control centre in its bone case
The **brain** is the body's **control centre**: everything you think, decide, remember and command begins there. When your eyes see and your ears hear, the news travels to the brain; when you lift a hand or say a word, the order travels out from the brain.

The brain is built in **three parts**, each with its own work in the control centre. And because the brain is soft and precious, the body keeps it inside the **skull** — the hard bone case you felt in the Do It First. The skull is the brain's helmet, grown by the body itself.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion three things: what the brain does, how many parts it has, and what guards it — then tap your head and name the guard.

## Do It Yourself
1. Control-centre test: a companion calls out actions — clap, blink, count to three, stand. After each one, say where the order came from.
2. News in, orders out: cover your eyes and listen — name what you hear (news in). Then point at it when you open your eyes (an order out).
3. Helmet thinking: name two things people wear or build to protect precious soft things, the way the skull protects the brain.
