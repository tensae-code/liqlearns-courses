# Video script — The skeleton — frame, joints and fractures

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Stand up. Feel your skull. Feel your backbone. Feel your ribs.
That is the **axial skeleton** — the centre. It guards your brain, your spine, your heart and lungs.
Now move your arms. Move your legs.
That is the **appendicular skeleton** — the limbs and their girdles. It carries you and moves you.

A baby is born with about **three hundred** bones — much of the skeleton still soft **cartilage**.
Growing up, cartilage turns to bone, and many bones **fuse** — they join into one.
An adult has **two hundred and six**. Nothing was lost. Bones joined.

Where bones meet is a **joint**. **Ligaments** tie bone to bone. **Cartilage** cushions the ends.

Bones can break. If the skin is broken — an **open** fracture. If not — **closed**.
And in our towns, the **bone setters** have set broken bones for generations. Ask them how. Listen well.

Two hundred and six bones. One frame. Carry it carefully.
