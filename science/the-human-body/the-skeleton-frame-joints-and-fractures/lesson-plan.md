# Lesson plan — The skeleton — frame, joints and fractures

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** The skeleton — frame, joints and fractures
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Human Body Systems and Health, the frame system, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the skeleton is one frame in two parts — axial (centre) and appendicular (limbs) — joined by joints with ligaments and cartilage; bones break as open and closed fractures, local bone setters treat them, and the guide's key explains why a baby's ~300 bones become an adult's 206.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The human body — systems working together and pairs with Muscles — how movement happens (the muscles pull on this frame). The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (Axial and appendicular skeletons; Types of joints); this lesson is the Grade 8 version, built new, with the guide's 300 → 206 explanation filed as labelled teacher evidence inside it.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Two skeletons in one frame** — the axial skeleton (braincase, backbone, ribs) guarding the centre, the appendicular skeleton (limbs, shoulder girdle, pelvis) carrying weight and movement; and the guide's count: ~300 bones at birth, 206 in the adult, because cartilage turns to bone and separate bones fuse.
2. **Joints, ligaments and cartilage** — where bones meet: ligaments tie bone to bone, cartilage cushions the ends, and joints let the frame bend.
3. **Breakages, bone setters and bone diseases** — open and closed fractures; interviewing local bone setters about their practice; osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, rickets and Paget's disease; the clay-skeleton project.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Human Body — Muscles — how movement happens. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort named bones into axial and appendicular, and say each part's main work.
2. Explain the 300 → 206 bone count (teacher-guide evidence, labelled as such).
3. Say what ligaments and cartilage do at a joint.
4. Tell an open fracture from a closed one, name the four bone diseases, and say what pupils ask local bone setters.

## What you need
Clay or mud for the skeleton model; your own body for finding joints.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the two parts → centre guards, limbs move: if it protects the brain, spine or chest organs it is axial; if it reaches, walks or carries, appendicular.
- Thinks bones are lost as we grow → nothing is lost; bones join — two or more become one, so the count falls.
