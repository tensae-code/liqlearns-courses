Today: **The sense of taste** — Kuwait's way: how we know the taste of things with the tongue — sweet, sour, salty and bitter — and how taste warns us that food has spoiled.

The tongue is the organ of taste. With it we know the tastes of things: sweet like honey, sour like lemon, salty like salt, and bitter like some medicines and leaves. Kuwait's Grade 1 book teaches taste together with smell, because the two work as a pair: much of what we call the taste of food is really its smell reaching the nose while we eat. A strange or bad taste is a warning. Food that has spoiled often tastes sour or unpleasant, and the right answer to that warning is to stop eating and spit the food out — never to swallow and hope. We keep the tongue and mouth safe by not tasting unknown things, not sharing food that may have spoiled, and telling an adult when food tastes wrong. Taste guards the gate of the body: everything we swallow must pass it first.
