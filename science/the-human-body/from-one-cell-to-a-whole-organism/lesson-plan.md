# Lesson plan — Cells — from one cell to a whole organism

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Cells — from one cell to a whole organism
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Cell as the Basis of Life, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** some organisms live as one cell doing everything, and multicellular organisms divide the labour across five levels: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Cells — shape, size and function (cells specialised by shape and job). The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (From cell to organism); this lesson is the first-depth Grade 7 version, built new.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student’s guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **One cell or many** — unicellular organisms (amoeba, paramecium, protozoa) do everything in one cell; multicellular ones divide labour between specialised cells.
2. **The five levels of organisation** — cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism, worked for an animal (circulatory system) and for a plant.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Cells — shape, size and function. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name two unicellular organisms and say how one cell does everything.
2. Give the five levels in order, with an example at each level.
3. Trace the levels for a plant as well as for an animal.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts organ before tissue → tissues build organs, never the other way: cells → tissue → organ.
- Thinks a one-celled organism is not alive → the amoeba feeds, moves and reproduces — all of life, in one cell.
