# Cells — from one cell to a whole organism

## Do it first
Think of a one-person shop and a big market town. In the one-person shop, one person buys, sells, cleans and guards — every job. In the town, jobs are divided: bakers bake, drivers drive. Living things come in both kinds. Guess which kind you are — then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — One cell or many
Some organisms are **unicellular** — made of **one cell only**:
- **amoeba**, **paramecium**, and the other **protozoa**.

That one cell does **everything**: it moves, feeds, breathes, excretes and reproduces. It is a complete living thing in a single cell — the one-person shop.

**Multicellular** organisms — like you, a horse, or a tree — have many cells, and the cells **divide the labour**: each kind of cell does one job well, as the shaped cells of the last lesson showed.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five levels of organisation
In a multicellular organism, the labour is organised in **five levels**, each built from the one below:

1. **Cell** — one specialised unit (a heart muscle cell).
2. **Tissue** — similar specialised cells working together (heart muscle tissue; **liver tissue**).
3. **Organ** — different tissues together doing one big job (the **heart**; a leaf).
4. **Organ system** — organs working together (**heart + blood + blood vessels = the circulatory system**).
5. **Organism** — the whole living thing (a horse, a rat — or a whole plant).

The staircase works for a **plant** too: cell → tissue → leaf (organ) → shoot system → whole plant.

## Say it to your companion
Climb the staircase aloud for your companion, once for an animal (ending in the circulatory system) and once for a plant. Your companion checks the order.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the five levels on five cards, shuffle, and re-order them; write an example on each.
2. List three jobs the amoeba's single cell must do alone.
3. Trace your own levels: a blood cell → blood tissue → heart → circulatory system → you.
