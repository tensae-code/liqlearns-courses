# Early pregnancy — and its risks

## Do it first
A body must finish growing itself before it can grow a baby well. What could go wrong when pregnancy comes early, before that growing is done? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 6 *Ciências da Natureza* book, after reproduction and sexual life, teaches early pregnancy (A gravidez precoce) as its own lesson, before the infections lesson and the closing health run.

## Sub lesson 1 — What early pregnancy is

**Early pregnancy** (gravidez precoce) is pregnancy that comes **early** — while the girl's or young woman's own body is **still growing**. Angola's Grade 6 book names it as its own subject, after teaching reproduction: the same event that, in a grown body, is the beginning of a child, in a still-growing body loads two growings onto one body — the mother's own unfinished growth, and the baby's.

## Sub lesson 2 — The risks of early pregnancy

The risks follow from that double load. For the **young mother**: her body must share what it still needs for itself, so pregnancy and birth press harder on her health. For the **baby**: a body still growing may not give all the baby needs, so the baby's start is at risk. And for her **life path**: early pregnancy commonly interrupts schooling and the future being built on it. The lesson teaches the risks plainly so they can be avoided — early pregnancy is a risk to be prevented, not a fate to be accepted.

## The key ideas

- **Early pregnancy** is pregnancy while the mother's own body is **still growing** — two growings loaded onto one body.
- Its risks fall on the **young mother's health**, on the **baby's start**, and on her **schooling and future**.
- The lesson's purpose is prevention: the risks are taught plainly **so they can be avoided**.
