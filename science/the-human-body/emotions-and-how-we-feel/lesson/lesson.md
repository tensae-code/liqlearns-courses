# Emotions — and how we feel

## Do it first
Think of a day when you felt very happy, and a day when you felt sad. What was different inside you? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 3 *Estudo do Meio* book, after the body's systems, closes the human-body run with my emotions (As minhas emoções) — the body's feelings, named as the body's own subject.

## Sub lesson 1 — Naming my emotions

An **emotion** is a feeling that rises in us — **joy** when something good comes, **sadness** when we lose or miss, **anger** when something strikes us as wrong, **fear** when something threatens. Angola's Grade 3 book teaches **my emotions** (As minhas emoções) as their own lesson: the feelings are mine, they are real, and each one has a name. Naming an emotion is the first skill — a feeling I can name is a feeling I can talk about.

## Sub lesson 2 — Emotions show — and they change

Emotions **show**: in the face, in the body, in what we do next — and other people can read them, as we read theirs. And emotions **change**: no feeling stays at its strongest for ever; joy settles, anger cools, fear passes when the threat goes. The lesson's place in the book is the point: after the digestive, breathing, blood, urinary and reproductive systems, the emotions complete the picture of a person — a body that feels as well as works.

## The key ideas

- An **emotion** is a feeling that rises in us — joy, sadness, anger, fear — and each one **has a name**.
- Emotions **show** in the face and body, and others can read them.
- Emotions **change** — no feeling stays at its strongest for ever.
