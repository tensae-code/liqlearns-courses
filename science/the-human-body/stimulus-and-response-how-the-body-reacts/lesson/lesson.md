# Stimulus and response — how the body reacts

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — stimulus and response: how the body senses a change and reacts to it — the Hebei Grade 5 book's opening of the body-sensing topic. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Stimulus — the body senses a change

A **stimulus** is a change the body can sense — light reaching the eyes, a sound reaching the ears, heat touching the skin. China's Hebei Grade 5 book opens its body topic here: before any organ is named, the learner meets the event itself — something changes, and the body **senses** it.

The senses are the body's receivers: each one is tuned to its own kind of change, and together they keep the body informed about the world around it and about the body itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Response — the body reacts

Sensing is only the first half. The second half is the **response**: the body reacts to what it sensed — pulling back from the hot thing, turning toward the sound, shading the eyes from the light.

Stimulus and response are one event in two halves: a change is sensed, and the body answers it. The Hebei book teaches the pair together because neither half makes sense alone — a body that sensed but never reacted could not protect itself, and reactions without sensing would be blind.

## The key ideas

- A **stimulus** is a change the body can sense — light, sound, heat and their kin, picked up by the senses.
- A **response** is the body's reaction to what it sensed — pulling back, turning toward, shading against.
- Stimulus and response are **one event in two halves**: sense, then answer — the pair that lets the body protect and guide itself.
