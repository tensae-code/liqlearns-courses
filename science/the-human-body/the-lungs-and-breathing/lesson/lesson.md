# The lungs and breathing

## Do it first
Put a hand on your ribs. Now breathe in, slowly and deeply. Your chest swells under your hand. Breathe out — it falls. Something inside is filling with air and emptying again, like two sponges. Those are your lungs.

## A spongy pair at work
The **lungs** are a **pair** — two of them — sitting inside the chest, one on each side of the heart. They are **spongy**: soft, full of tiny air spaces, made to fill and empty.

Their work is the exchange at the heart of breathing: when you breathe **in**, the lungs take in **oxygen** from the air — the part of air the body needs. When you breathe **out**, they give out **carbon dioxide** — the part the body has finished with.

In with the good air, out with the used air, all day and all night: that is breathing, and the lungs are its organs.

## Grade 5 — the breathing road, part by part
Breathing in (**ምጋት**) and out (**ኢ-ምጋት**) are named, and the air's road is walked part by part: **nose** (two nostrils, hairs that filter dust and microbes — mouth-breathing compared), **pharynx** (the shared food/air crossing, guarded by the **epiglottis/ትንመዝ**), **trachea (ትንቧ)**, **bronchi (ባላ ትንቧ)**, **bronchioles (ደቂቅ ትንቧ)** and **alveoli (ትንከረቶች)**, where gases are exchanged. The Grade 6 teacher guide's respiratory subtopic (teacher guide — evidence) sets the same identification: the main parts of the respiratory system and their functions.

## Grade 5 — Experiment 2.2: the bottle-lung model; the air in and out; smoking
**Experiment 2.2** builds working lungs from a **cut plastic bottle, a Y-tube, two balloons and a rubber-sheet diaphragm**: pulling the sheet inflates the balloons — the model's whole proof. Then **Table 2.1** prints the numbers: inhaled air is **20.95% oxygen, 0.04% carbon dioxide, 78% nitrogen**; exhaled air is **16% oxygen, 4.0% carbon dioxide, 78% nitrogen** — plus water vapour, proven by breathing on a mirror. Breathing health: polluted air and airborne diseases (**TB, corona, flu, trachoma** named). Then **cigarette smoke** — it damages the airways' filtering linings, builds mucus, causes the smoker's cough — with **Experiment 2.3**, a smoking-machine model (bottle and cotton) showing tar staining, and the **seven printed harms of smoking**: lung disease; smell on breath, clothes and home; blackened lips and yellowed teeth and fingers; family money lost; harm to bystanders; harm to an unborn baby; fire risk from discarded butts. (The smoking machine is a teacher demonstration in the book's spirit — no pupil smokes anything, ever.)

## Grade 8 — the air path, step by step
At Grade 8 depth the road is walked in full: **nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**, and it is at the alveoli that gas exchange happens — oxygen passes into the blood, carbon dioxide passes out. The pumping is done by the **diaphragm and the ribs**: the lungs themselves have **no muscle** — they are filled and emptied by the chest that works around them, exactly as the bottle-and-balloon model lung shows. A ventilator note connects the model to hospitals, where a machine does this pumping for a patient who cannot.

## Grade 8 — the disease survey; and COVID-19 (teacher guide — evidence, dated to the guide)
The Grade 8 book closes the system with a health-centre survey pupils complete locally. The diseases: **asthma, sinusitis, influenza, COPD, bronchitis, pneumonia, TB (tuberculosis) and lung cancer**. The teacher guide adds one row the student book does not carry: **COVID-19** — taught, as the guide directs, as transmission-and-prevention: a dangerous worldwide respiratory disease, transmitted by close contact with an infected person or contaminated objects and carried through the air in sneezing and coughing. That row is **teacher-guide evidence dated to the guide** (the guide is a federal new-syllabus book, implemented from 2014 E.C.); it is not presented as current information. At this depth the lesson cross-refers to Biology (The breathing system — air's path to the alveoli; COVID-19 — the spiked virus; Tuberculosis and the BCG vaccine); it does not copy or grow a Biology file.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: how many lungs there are, what they feel like (spongy), what they take in, and what they give out.

## Do It Yourself
1. Rib watch: hand on ribs, breathe slowly five times. Count how many breaths you take in one quiet minute.
2. Breath words: say a long sentence on one breath out — the air leaving carried your voice. That is the out-breath at work.
3. Pair drawing: draw the chest, the two lungs, and the heart between them — the book's own picture, from memory.
