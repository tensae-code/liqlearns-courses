# Video script — The lungs and breathing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Two **lungs**, soft and **spongy**, fill your chest.
Breathe in — **oxygen** comes in.
Breathe out — **carbon dioxide** goes out. All day, all night.

## Grade 8 — build-along: the bottle-and-balloon lung (FULL script, added 2026-11-12)

Build with me. We are making a lung you can work with your hand.

You need: a clear plastic bottle with its bottom cut off (a teacher does the cutting). Two small balloons. One larger balloon or rubber sheet, cut open. A Y-shaped tube or a straw. Sticky tape and a stopper or clay.

**Step one.** Push the Y-tube through the stopper. On each lower arm of the Y, fix a small balloon. These two balloons are the **lungs**. The tube is the **trachea** and its two branches, the **bronchi**. — PAUSE —

**Step two.** Put the stopper in the bottle's neck, balloons inside the bottle. The bottle is the **chest**. Seal it well — no air may sneak in or out except through the tube. — PAUSE —

**Step three.** Stretch the opened balloon across the cut bottom of the bottle and tape it tight. This sheet is the **diaphragm**. — PAUSE —

**Step four.** Pull the sheet **down**. Watch the balloons. They **fill** — air rushes in through the tube, because the space inside grew and the air pressure pushed air in. This is breathing **in**. — PAUSE —

**Step five.** Let the sheet go. The balloons **empty**. This is breathing **out**. — PAUSE —

Now say it back: the balloons did not pull themselves. The **sheet** moved, and the air followed.
That is your body: the **diaphragm and ribs** do the pumping. The lungs have no muscle of their own.
