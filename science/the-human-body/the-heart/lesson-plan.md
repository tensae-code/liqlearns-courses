# Lesson plan — The heart

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** The heart
**Place in the curriculum:** The Human Body — the inside systems, after the outside parts and senses (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the heart is a muscle that pumps blood around the body by squeezing (contracting) and relaxing — and you can feel it work
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Parts of the body and the five senses — the outside first; the heart is the first inside part the child can feel working.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Human Body — Parts of the body and the five senses. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the heart is: a muscle that pumps blood.
2. Say how it pumps: by contracting and relaxing, again and again.
3. Feel your own heartbeat: jump, then rest a hand on your chest, the book's way.

## What you need
Room to jump on the spot; a quiet moment to feel the chest.

## Grade 8 depth (added 2026-11-12)
The Grade 8 General Science book adds the water-tower analogy, valves, the pulmonary exceptions, blood as plasma plus cells made in bone marrow, double circulation named as two loops, and the diseases (hypertension, heart attack, stroke, heart failure), with an outdoor circuit game using petals as blood cells. - ADD AUDIO: the pulmonary-exception rule read aloud — the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood — the student repeats it and says why the name still fits. (Described slot only — no audio file is made in this build.)

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot feel the beat → press the flat hand firmly at the left of the chest and be still; jumping first makes the beat strong.
- Thinks the heart is a pump like a bicycle pump, outside the muscles → it IS a muscle: its squeezing is its own muscle work.
