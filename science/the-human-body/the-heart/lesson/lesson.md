# The heart

## Do it first
Jump up and down ten times. Now put your hand flat on your chest and stand still. Something in there is thumping — faster than before you jumped. That is the heart, and it never stops, day or night.

## A muscle that pumps
The **heart** is a **muscle** — a working muscle that **pumps blood** around the body. It pumps by **contracting and relaxing**: it squeezes, and the blood is pushed on; it relaxes, and fills again. Squeeze, relax, squeeze, relax — that rhythm is your **heartbeat**.

You proved it in the Do It First: after jumping, the heart beats faster, because the body is asking for more blood. At rest it slows again. The heart works while you run, while you sit, and while you sleep.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: what is the heart made of, what does it pump, and how — contracting and relaxing — and how you know it beats faster after jumping.

## Do It Yourself
1. Beat check: feel your heartbeat sitting still and count the beats in half a minute. Then jump twenty times and count again. Say which count is bigger, and why.
2. Hand pump: squeeze a fist gently, open it, squeeze again — that squeeze-and-relax is the heart's own movement, in small.
3. Quiet listening: in a quiet room, put your ear near a companion's chest (with their leave) and listen for the beat.
