# The heart

## Do it first
Jump up and down ten times. Now put your hand flat on your chest and stand still. Something in there is thumping — faster than before you jumped. That is the heart, and it never stops, day or night.

## A muscle that pumps
The **heart** is a **muscle** — a working muscle that **pumps blood** around the body. It pumps by **contracting and relaxing**: it squeezes, and the blood is pushed on; it relaxes, and fills again. Squeeze, relax, squeeze, relax — that rhythm is your **heartbeat**.

You proved it in the Do It First: after jumping, the heart beats faster, because the body is asking for more blood. At rest it slows again. The heart works while you run, while you sit, and while you sleep.

## Grade 5 — the heart in full; vessels; blood; two circulations
The Grade 5 book gives the system its three actors — **heart, vessels and blood** — moving food, air and goods around the body and carrying waste away. The **heart has four chambers** (the upper pair receive, the lower pair deliver; **two right, two left**), is about **fist-sized** — clench a fist to feel its size — and beats by **contracting and relaxing**, faster or slower with age, activity, heat and illness; pupils feel and count their own beat and meet the **stethoscope** by name. **Blood vessels**: **arteries** carry oxygen-rich blood **away** from the heart, **veins** carry oxygen-poor blood **back**, and **capillaries** make the exchange with the body's cells.

## Grade 5 — blood groups and cells; the two circulations
**Blood** comes in **four groups (A, B, AB, O)**, found by **serum testing**; giving blood through the Red Cross/Red Crescent is presented as a human duty. Its **three cell kinds**: **red cells carry oxygen and food, white cells fight disease, platelets clot the blood**. And blood runs **two circuits**: the **pulmonary** circuit between heart and lungs (carbon dioxide out, oxygen in) and the **systemic** circuit between heart and the whole body. The Grade 6 teacher guide's circulation subtopic (teacher guide — evidence) sets the same outcomes: draw the circulation and label it, explain the types of blood and their uses, identify the heart's parts and their uses.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: what is the heart made of, what does it pump, and how — contracting and relaxing — and how you know it beats faster after jumping.

## Do It Yourself
1. Beat check: feel your heartbeat sitting still and count the beats in half a minute. Then jump twenty times and count again. Say which count is bigger, and why.
2. Hand pump: squeeze a fist gently, open it, squeeze again — that squeeze-and-relax is the heart's own movement, in small.
3. Quiet listening: in a quiet room, put your ear near a companion's chest (with their leave) and listen for the beat.
