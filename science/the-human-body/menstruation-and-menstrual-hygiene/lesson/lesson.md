# Menstruation and menstrual hygiene

## Do it first
What have you heard about a girl's monthly bleeding? Some of what is said about it is fear talking. The book speaks plainly, and this lesson repeats it plainly: it is a healthy sign, and it can be managed with simple care.

## What it is — and what it is not
The book states it without flinching: the appearance of menstruation means **a healthy girl has matured** — it is **not a disease and not a defect**. Blood flows **from the uterus through the vagina for 4–6 days, about every 28 days**. That is the whole fact — regular, expected, and shared by women everywhere.

## Managing it with care
Hygiene has two honest routes. **Factory-made pads** exist and serve well. And where price or supply is a problem, a **clean homemade cloth of 100% cotton** can be prepared and used. The cloth route is the book's own answer to a real problem — no girl should miss school for want of a bought pad.

## Fatuma's story (plot summary only)
**Fatuma, 12**, gets her first period at school: she sees blood on her legs, panics, tells her teacher and then her mother that she is sick, and misses a test; the next morning, finding much blood, she stays in bed. Her older brother sees the blood and accuses her of having had sex. Her **aunt** arrives, rebukes the brother — "you boys know nothing" — explains that menstruation is natural and a sign of maturity, and Fatuma moves **from fear and shame to relief and happiness**. The aunt notes that nobody had told Fatuma beforehand — some people are shy about such topics, but there is nothing shameful in them. (The story is placed here as a plot summary only.)

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the book's ruling in one sentence — menstruation is a healthy sign of maturity, not a disease — then the pattern (4–6 days, about every 28 days) and the two ways to manage it.
