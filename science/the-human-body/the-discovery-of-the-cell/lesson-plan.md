# Lesson plan — Cells — the discovery of the cell

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Cells — the discovery of the cell
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Cell as the Basis of Life, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** cells are the building blocks of all living things — Hooke saw dead cork cell walls in 1665 and coined the word 'cell', and Leeuwenhoek saw living single-celled animalcules in pond water in 1674.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The microscope — seeing the unseen (no microscope, no cells seen). The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (Cell theory; The cell — life's smallest unit); this lesson is the first-depth Grade 7 version.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student’s guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Cells — the building blocks of life** — all living things are built of cells; a human body holds trillions of them.
2. **Hooke and the cork — 1665** — Hooke's honeycomb compartments in cork — dead plant cell walls — and the word 'cell'.
3. **Leeuwenhoek and living cells — 1674** — living single-celled animalcules in pond water, plus bacteria, yeast and red blood cells — the first sight of living cells.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The microscope — seeing the unseen. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a cell is: a building block of all living things.
2. Tell what Hooke saw in cork in 1665 and what he named it.
3. Tell what Leeuwenhoek saw in pond water in 1674, and name two more things he saw.

## What you need
A piece of cork if available; a drop of pond water; paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Hooke saw living cells → cork cells are dead plant walls — empty boxes. Living cells were Leeuwenhoek's sight, nine years later.
- Thinks one cell is big → a human body holds trillions of cells; each one is microscopic.
