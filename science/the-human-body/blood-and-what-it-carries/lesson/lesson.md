# Blood — and what it carries

## Do it first
Before you read: this lesson teaches one thing — blood — what it is made of and what it carries around the body — as placed from Papua New Guinea's science books (see lesson/sources.md for the exact books and pages). What do you already know about it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Papua New Guinea's way in — its Department of Education books teach this as its own topic, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Blood's solid and liquid parts

Papua New Guinea's Grade 6 textbook opens blood by its make-up: blood has **solid parts and a liquid part** together in one stream. It looks like one red liquid, but it is a mixture — solids carried in a liquid, each with its own job.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carrying oxygen and carbon dioxide

Blood's first carrying job is gases: it carries **oxygen** to every part of the body, and carries **carbon dioxide** back — the Grade 6 manual teaches blood by exactly this exchange, the load picked up and the load returned on the same round.

## Sub lesson 3 — Carrying nutrients and wastes

The same stream carries more: **nutrients** — the useful part of food — out to the body, and **wastes** away from it. Blood is the body's transport: gases, food and wastes all travel in it, which is why its solid and liquid parts matter together.
