## Sub lesson 1 — One cell becomes two

Every living body begins as one cell, and it grows because that cell **divides**: the cell copies its hereditary material and splits into two cells, each with a full copy. Those two divide again — two, four, eight — and growth is nothing but this multiplying, organised.

Kuwait's Grade 9 book places cell division at the head of its reproduction unit: before the human reproductive system, the learner must hold the engine underneath it — division is how a fertilised cell becomes a body, and how the cells that begin reproduction are made.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why division matters

Division does three jobs. It makes **growth**: a child from a single cell, a plant from a seed's first cells. It makes **repair**: new cells fill wounds and replace worn-out cells — skin and blood are renewed by division all life long. And it makes **reproduction** possible, in the special divisions that prepare the reproductive cells.

This general lesson teaches division as the body's engine of more-cells; the Biology title's lesson on mitosis teaches the same division step by step at secondary depth, and the two cross-refer, as this course's twin lessons do.

## The key ideas

- In cell division, one cell copies its hereditary material and splits into two.
- Growth is cell division, multiplied and organised.
- Division repairs wounds and renews worn-out cells like skin and blood.
- The Biology mitosis lesson is this lesson's secondary-depth twin.
