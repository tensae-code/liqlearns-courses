Today: **Cell division** — Kuwait's way: cell division — how one cell divides to become two, so living things grow, repair themselves and reproduce — the general lesson, with the Biology title's mitosis lesson as its secondary-depth twin.

Every living body begins as one cell, and it grows because that cell divides: the cell copies its hereditary material and splits into two cells, each with a full copy. Those two divide again — two, four, eight — and growth is nothing but this multiplying, organised. Kuwait's Grade 9 book places cell division at the head of its reproduction unit: before the human reproductive system, the learner must hold the engine underneath it — division is how a fertilised cell becomes a body, and how the cells that begin reproduction are made. Division does three jobs. It makes growth: a child from a single cell, a plant from a seed's first cells. It makes repair: new cells fill wounds and replace worn-out cells — skin and blood are renewed by division all life long. And it makes reproduction possible, in the special divisions that prepare the reproductive cells. This general lesson teaches division as the body's engine of more-cells; the Biology title's lesson on mitosis teaches the same division step by step at secondary depth, and the two cross-refer, as this course's twin lessons do.
