# Experiment 2.1: saliva and the starch test

## Do it first
Chew a piece of bread for a long time without swallowing. It softens — and it even starts to taste different. Something in your mouth is working on it. This lesson runs the book's experiment to catch that worker in the act.

## The two glasses
The book's **Experiment 2.1** sets bread in **two glasses of water**. Into one glass goes **saliva** as well. Both glasses are warmed to **body temperature — 37 °C** — by a lamp or a candle, with an adult in charge of the warming. Then the class watches: in the glass with saliva, the bread **breaks down**; saliva is doing chemical digestion, the same work it does in the mouth while the teeth chew.

## The starch test
The same experiment day carries a second test. Drops of **iodine** are put on a piece of **potato**. The iodine test is the test **for starch**: it shows whether the food holds starch — the very food-stuff saliva starts to break down.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the two glasses to your companion: what is the same in both, what is different, and what the difference proves about saliva.

## Do It Yourself
1. Run the two glasses exactly as the book sets them out, with an adult warming them. Record what each glass looks like at the start and at the end.
2. Iodine-test three foods at home (potato first, as the book does). Write which foods the test marks as holding starch.
3. Slow-chew test: chew bread slowly for a full minute and describe, in words, how it changes — the experiment happening in your own mouth.
