# Lesson plan — Experiment 2.1: saliva and the starch test

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Experiment 2.1: saliva and the starch test
**Place in the curriculum:** The Human Body — a doing-lesson on the mouth's work (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** saliva does real chemical work on food — shown by the book's two-glass experiment — and iodine tests a food for starch

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Set up the two-glass experiment: bread in water, and bread in water with saliva, warmed to body temperature (37 °C).
2. Say what the experiment shows: saliva breaks food down — digestion begins in the mouth.
3. Test a potato with iodine drops and say what the test looks for: starch.

## What you need
Two glasses, bread, water, a way to warm the glasses to body temperature (a lamp or candle, with an adult), a potato and iodine.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks digestion starts in the stomach → the two glasses prove the mouth starts it: teeth chew (physical) while saliva works on starch (chemical), both at once.
- Forgets why the glasses are warmed → saliva works at the body's own warmth, 37 °C; the warming keeps the test true to the mouth.
