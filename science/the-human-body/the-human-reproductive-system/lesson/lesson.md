# The human reproductive system

## Do it first
You know the system that digests food, the system that moves blood, the system that breathes. The body has one more system this course must name plainly, the way the book names it: the system by which new life begins. It is taught here with the same calm words as every other system.

## The male organs
The male system's basic organs are the **testes (ቆለጥ)**, which make the **sperm cells (ነባዘር)**. The testes are held in the **scrotum**, with the duct system that carries the sperm.

## The female organs, ovulation and fertilisation
The female system's basic organs are the **ovaries (እንቁልጢ)**, which make the **eggs**. An egg is released — **ovulation (ውፃት)** — about every 14 days from the first menstruation. If intercourse occurs, a sperm meets the egg in the **uterine tube**; that meeting is **fertilisation (ፅንሰት)**, and the fertilised cell becomes a **zygote**, then an **embryo** in the **uterus (ማህፀን)**. If no fertilisation occurs, the egg dies and the prepared lining of the uterus breaks down and leaves the body with blood: **menstruation (የወር አበባ)**. The cycle repeats about every **28 days** until pregnancy.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion, plainly and in order: what the testes make, what the ovaries make, what ovulation is, where fertilisation happens, and what menstruation is. No giggling — these are the book's own words for a normal system.
