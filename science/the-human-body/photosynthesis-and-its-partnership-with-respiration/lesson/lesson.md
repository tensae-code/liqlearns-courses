# Cells — photosynthesis and its partnership with respiration

## Do it first
Hold a green leaf up to the light. It is quietly doing something no animal can do: making food out of air, water and light. Before you read on, guess: what gas does the leaf give out while it works — the same gas you must breathe in?

## Sub lesson 1 — Photosynthesis — the chloroplast as food factory
Inside the **chloroplasts** of plant cells sits a green pigment, **chlorophyll**. It **traps light energy**, and the plant uses that energy to build food:

**Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen** (in light, using chlorophyll)

This is **photosynthesis** — making things with light. The glucose is the plant's food (and, eaten, the food of nearly every other living thing). The **oxygen** is released into the air — **photosynthesis feeds nearly all life and supplies the oxygen it breathes**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The great exchange — partnership with respiration
Now put the last lesson beside this one:

- **Photosynthesis:** carbon dioxide + water → glucose + **oxygen out**
- **Respiration:** glucose + oxygen → energy + **carbon dioxide and water out**

Each process's products are the other's raw materials. The leaf gives oxygen to the animal; the animal's respiration returns carbon dioxide and water to the leaf. Between organisms and their environment, the gases run in **one great exchange cycle** — leaf → animal → leaf, round and round.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion both equations, then narrate the loop: start at the leaf, follow the oxygen to the animal, follow the carbon dioxide back, and say what keeps the loop turning (light, trapped by chlorophyll).

3. Answer in two sentences: why would animal life fail without photosynthesis? (Food and oxygen both come from it.)
