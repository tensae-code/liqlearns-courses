# Cells — shape, size and function

## Do it first
Hold two balls in your mind: a small marble and a big football. If you had to pass food and air to the very middle of each through its skin, which middle would be fed sooner? Keep your answer — it is the key to why cells are the size they are.

## Sub lesson 1 — How small is a cell?
Cells are measured in **micrometres (µm)** — one micrometre is a thousandth of a millimetre.

- A typical cell is **10–20 µm** across.
- **Bacteria** are smaller still: **1–10 µm** long.

Why so small? Because everything a cell needs — **nutrients and gases** — must cross its membrane, and everything it must lose — **wastes** — must cross out. In a small cell nothing inside is far from the surface, so materials cross **easily and quickly**. A giant cell would starve at its centre. **Cells stay small so materials can cross them easily** — just as the marble's middle is reached sooner than the football's.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shape follows function
Cells are not all one shape. Each shape fits its job:
- **Red blood cells** are **biconcave discs** — dented in on both sides — a shape that helps them **carry oxygen** as they travel in the blood.
- **Muscle cells** are **spindle-shaped** — pointed at both ends, wide in the middle — a shape that **contracts** (shortens) to move the body.
- **Nerve cells** are **long and branched** — a shape that **transmits messages** over long distances and connects to many other cells.

**Form follows function**: look at a cell's shape, and you can guess its job.
