# Lesson plan — Reproduction, puberty and staying safe

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Reproduction, puberty and staying safe
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Human Body Systems and Health, the closing system of the unit, inside The Human Body (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** puberty brings primary and secondary characteristics (girls about 11–13, boys about 14–16 as printed); the male and female organs do the work of reproduction; menstruation runs about a 28-day cycle with 3–5 days of bleeding, managed practically; and sexually transmitted infections split into bacterial and parasitic ones, treatable with antibiotics, and viral ones, which have no cure.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The human reproductive system, Adolescence and its changes and Menstruation and menstrual hygiene (the earlier, younger lessons) and gathers them at Grade 8 depth with the sexual-health content added; it cross-refers to Keeping Healthy — HIV and AIDS. The same ground is met again at secondary depth in Biology (Growing up — puberty; The male / female reproductive system; The menstrual cycle; Bacterial, viral and parasitic STIs); this lesson is the Grade 8 version, built new — it never copies or grows a Biology file, and it does not rewrite the three earlier lessons, whose own books gave different printed ages for adolescence (see sources).

Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Primary and secondary characteristics** — the reproductive organs themselves (primary) against the other changes of puberty (secondary); girls about 11–13, boys about 14–16, as printed.
2. **The organs and what they do** — male and female reproductive organs with their function tables.
3. **Menstruation — the practical facts** — the ~28-day cycle, 3–5 days of bleeding, pads and care, and the school questions pupils actually ask, answered plainly.
4. **Infections that pass sexually — treatable and not** — syphilis, gonorrhea, chancroid and HIV/AIDS named; the key split: bacterial and parasitic infections are treatable with antibiotics, viral infections have no cure.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell primary from secondary characteristics and give the printed age ranges.
2. Name the main male and female reproductive organs with their jobs.
3. State the menstrual pattern (~28 days; 3–5 days of bleeding) and its practical management.
4. Name four sexually transmitted infections and state the treatable / no-cure split correctly.

## What you need
Only the lesson itself, read plainly and without embarrassment — this is a body system like any other; a factory pad to show, where the class can show one plainly.

## If the learner is stuck
- Embarrassment blocks the learning → keep the plain words of the lesson; these are body parts and body facts, handled like any other system.
- Mixes primary and secondary → primary IS the organs; secondary is everything else that changes around them.
