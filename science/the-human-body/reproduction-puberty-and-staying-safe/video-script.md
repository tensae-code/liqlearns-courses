Let us answer, plainly, the questions pupils actually ask about menstruation.

**What is happening?**
Once in about every **twenty-eight days**, the womb sheds its lining. The blood flows out through the vagina. It lasts about **three to five days**. This is menstruation. It is a healthy sign that the body is maturing. It is not a sickness. It is not dirt. It is not shameful.

**When does it start?**
Girls begin at different ages — commonly somewhere in the years of puberty, about **eleven to thirteen** for many. Earlier or later can still be normal. Every body has its own timing.

**What do I use?**
A **pad**, worn inside the underwear, changed regularly through the day. Where pads are hard to get, a clean cloth, folded and kept only for this, washed well and dried fully in the sun. Change often. Wash your hands before and after.

**Can I go to school? Can I play?**
Yes. Menstruation does not stop school, work, sport or bathing. Bathe as usual — keeping clean matters more, not less, in these days.

**What about pain?**
Some cramping is common. Moving, warmth on the belly, and rest can help. If pain is severe, or bleeding is very heavy, or the days stretch far beyond five — that is a question for the clinic, and asking it is right.

**Can anyone tell?**
No. With a pad or a clean cloth, changed on time, nobody can tell. It is your body's business, and it is ordinary — half the world knows these days, every month, for many years of life.

Remember the numbers: a cycle of about **twenty-eight days**; bleeding of about **three to five days**.
And remember the ruling: healthy, normal, and nothing to hide.
