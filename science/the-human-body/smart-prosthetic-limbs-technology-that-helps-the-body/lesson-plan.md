# Lesson plan — Smart prosthetic limbs — technology that helps the body

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Smart prosthetic limbs — technology that helps the body
**Place in the curriculum:** The Human Body — appended to Unit 3 (The Human Body) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a prosthetic limb is a made limb that stands in for a missing limb, and smart prosthetics use technology to help the body do the limb's work
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. It follows Jordan's Grade 2 book, where it is an enrichment inside the human-body-and-health unit.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **A made limb that helps the body** — Jordan's enrichment: when a limb is missing, people make a prosthetic limb to stand in for it, and smart prosthetics add technology so the made limb can help the body move and do things; the guide's lesson plan frames it as appreciating the role of science and technology.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Human Body — Parts of the body and the five senses. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a prosthetic limb is and what job it does.
2. Say what makes a smart prosthetic different: technology helping the made limb work with the body.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner answers the opening question out loud before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's sub lesson (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a prosthetic limb in use — the student names the limb it stands in for and the job it helps with.
   - ADD GIF: a 1–2 second GIF of a prosthetic limb in motion — the student says what movement the technology helps with.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson in their own words (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/, served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a prosthetic limb is a body part that grew → it is a made thing: technology standing in for the missing limb, not a grown part.
