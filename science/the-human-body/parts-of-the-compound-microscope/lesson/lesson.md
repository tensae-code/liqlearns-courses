# The microscope — parts and what each does

## Do it first
Look at the diagram in this lesson before you read any labels. Try to guess, from its shape alone, which part you look through, which part holds what you look at, and which part catches the light. Then read on and check your guesses.

## Sub lesson 1 — The viewing parts — eyepiece, tube, objectives, nosepiece
- **Eyepiece** — the top lens you look through; it magnifies, usually 10×.
- **Body tube** — the tube holding the eyepiece above the objectives, keeping them the right distance apart.
- **Revolving nosepiece** — the turning disc under the tube; turn it to swing a different objective into place.
- **Objective lenses** — the lenses nearest the specimen: **4×, 10×, 40×, and 100× (oil)**. Longer objectives magnify more.
- **Arm** — the upright part joining tube to base; you carry the microscope by the arm (with the base — next lesson).

## Sub lesson 2 — The stage and the light parts
- **Stage** — the flat table where the slide rests.
- **Clips** — they hold the slide steady on the stage.
- **Coarse adjustment knob** — moves the stage (or tube) a long way, for first focusing.
- **Fine adjustment knob** — moves it a very little way, for sharpening the image.
- **Mirror** — reflects light up through the specimen.
- **Condenser** — gathers and concentrates the light onto the specimen.
- **Iris diaphragm** — opens and closes to control how much light passes.
- **Base** — the foot the whole microscope stands on.

Learn them in the light's path: **mirror → condenser → diaphragm → specimen on the stage → objective → tube → eyepiece → your eye.**

## Say it to your companion
Name every part to your companion in the light's-path order, with its job. Your companion follows on the diagram and stops you if a job is missing.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw the microscope from memory and label all twelve parts.
2. Write the light's path as one line of arrows, mirror to eye.
3. Cover the diagram's labels and name each part aloud; note any you missed and learn those two again.
