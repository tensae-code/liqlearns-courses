# Parts of the body and the five senses

## Do it first
Point to a part of your body. Now another. How many can you name already? Point to the part you see with. Point to the part you hear with. In this lesson we name all the outside parts, one by one, and learn what each one does.

## Sub lesson 1 — Parts of the body and what they do

Look at the person in the picture. Every part you can see from outside has a name. Start at the top: the **head**, with **hair** on it; the **face**, with the **forehead** above, the **eyes**, the **nose**, and the **lips** of the mouth. Below the head is the **neck**, then the **shoulders**, the **arms** and **hands**, the **chest** and the **belly**, the **waist**, and down the legs: the **knee**, the **shin**, the **leg** and the **heel** of the foot.

Each part has its job. Hair covers the head and protects it. The hands work — they hold, carry, write and build. The legs walk and run and carry the whole body. The eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin have special jobs of their own — they are the sense organs, and the next part is about them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five senses and what they do

Five of our parts are **sense organs** — the parts that tell us about the world around us. The **eyes** see. The **ears** hear. The **nose** smells. The **tongue** tastes. The **skin** feels — it tells us rough and smooth, hot and cold.

The senses work together. You smell food cooking, then you taste it. You hear a sound, then your eyes look for what made it. Try it in class the way the book does: with the eyes shut, can you still smell and hear? With the ears covered, can you still see and taste? Each sense does its own job, and no other sense can do it for it.

The student book carries a poem about the senses — a short celebration of what each sense does: seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, feeling. Learn it as a song of thanks for the five helpers.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the parts of the body from the head down, then the five senses and what each one does. Your companion points to each part as you name it, and catches any part you miss.

## Do It Yourself
1. Stand in front of a mirror or a friend. Name every outside part you can see, from the head to the heel.
2. Close your eyes. Let a friend touch your hand with something soft, then something rough. Which sense told you? Name it.
3. Draw a person. Mark the five sense organs on your drawing.
