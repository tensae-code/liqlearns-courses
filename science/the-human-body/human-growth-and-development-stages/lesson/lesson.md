# Human growth and development stages

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the people you know — a baby, a child your own age, a teenager, and a grown-up. What is different about their bodies? What can each one do that the one before could not? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is the Bahamas' way in — its Grade 1 science curriculum teaches the human body by starting with a human life itself, stage by stage.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four stages of a human life

Every human life passes through the same stages, in the same order:

1. **Infant** — the baby, in the first stage of life.
2. **Young child** — the child who has left babyhood behind.
3. **Teenager** — the young person between child and adult.
4. **Adult** — the fully grown person.

The stages cannot be swapped or skipped. An infant becomes a young child, the young child becomes a teenager, and the teenager becomes an adult. Naming the stage tells you roughly how big the body is — and, as the next sub lesson shows, what that body can do.

## Sub lesson 2 — What changes, stage by stage

**The infant** grows faster than at any later time. The body gets bigger and stronger month by month, and the abilities arrive in order: first holding up the head, then sitting, then standing, then the first steps of walking. The infant cannot feed or wash alone — grown people do those things for the baby.

**The young child** grows steadily. Walking is sure now, and running, jumping and climbing join it. The child talks in full sentences, learns, plays, and does more and more things alone — dressing, eating, washing — that the infant could not do at all.

**The teenager** grows in a spurt: height shoots up and the body's shape changes as it matures toward the adult form. The teenager is stronger, can do harder and longer work, and takes on more of an adult's jobs — while still growing.

**The adult** is fully grown: the body has reached its full height and mature form. The adult can do the full range of a grown person's work and care for others — including caring for a new infant, where the whole circle begins again.

So development is two growths together: the **body changes** — size, shape, strength — and the **abilities change** — what the person can do at that stage.

## The key ideas

- A human life passes through four stages in one order: **infant, young child, teenager, adult**.
- Each stage is known by the body's **physical changes** and by the person's **abilities** at that stage.
- The infant grows fastest and learns to sit, stand and walk; the young child walks, runs, talks and learns; the teenager grows in a spurt and matures; the adult is fully grown.
- Development is the body changing and the abilities growing — together.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the four stages first, then what changes at each one. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How do you know which stage a person is in?" Answer from the changes and the abilities, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw four figures in a row — infant, young child, teenager, adult — and label each stage.
2. Under each figure, write one physical change and one ability that belong to that stage.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the stage you are in now, and the change that comes next for you.
