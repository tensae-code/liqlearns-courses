# Human growth and development stages — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the four stages of human development, in order.
**Answer: Infant, young child, teenager, adult — in that order, and nobody skips a stage.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give one physical change and one ability of the young-child stage.
**Answer: Physical change: the child grows steadily, bigger and stronger than the infant. Ability: any one of walking surely, running, jumping, talking in full sentences, or doing things alone such as dressing and eating.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the teenager stage differ from the young-child stage?
**Answer: The teenager grows in a spurt, the body's shape changes and matures toward the adult form, and the teenager is stronger and takes on more of an adult's jobs — while the young child grows steadily and is still learning the basic abilities.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the lesson teach abilities together with physical changes?
**Answer: Because development is both growths together: the body changes in size, shape and strength, and what the person can do changes at every stage — a stage is known by both.**
