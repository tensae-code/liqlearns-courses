# Lesson plan — Human growth and development stages

**Course:** Science · **Title:** The Human Body · **Lesson:** Human growth and development stages
**Place in the curriculum:** The Human Body — appended to Unit 3 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the stages of human development — infant, young child, teenager, adult — and the physical changes and abilities that come with each stage — the Bahamas' own way, in its Grade 1 Primary Science Curriculum Guidelines, is to teach a child's own growth as the first human-body science
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The four stages of a human life** — A human life passes through named stages in one order: infant, young child, teenager, adult — each stage known by the size and shape of the body and by what the person can do.
2. **What changes, stage by stage** — The physical changes and abilities at each stage: the infant grows fast and learns to sit, stand and walk; the young child grows steadily, walks, runs, talks and learns; the teenager grows in a spurt and the body matures toward the adult form; the adult is fully grown and can do the full work of a grown person.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks the stages can come in any order → return to sub lesson 1: the stages follow one after another, infant first and adult last, and nobody skips one.
- Thinks growing is only getting taller → return to sub lesson 2: abilities grow with the body — what a person can do changes at every stage, not only their height.
