# Lesson plan — Water problems: sharing, pollution and the lake weed

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Soil and Water · **Lesson:** Water problems: sharing, pollution and the lake weed
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water — the region's three water problems (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** East Africa's water faces three printed problems: shared rivers with no agreement (Ethiopia contributes 86% of the Nile yet downstream countries obstruct its use; Ethiopia's position is equality and fairness); pollution from industrial waste, household waste and farm chemicals, with silt shrinking the water itself; and the water hyacinth (እንቦጭ) covering lakes Tana and Victoria, blocking sunlight and oxygen and harming navigation, irrigation and fish
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The great lakes and the Nile — the wealth is measured; now its three troubles are faced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State problem 1 fairly in the book's framing: upper-basin against lower-basin positions, and Ethiopia's printed position — use its water with equality and fairness while advancing the GERD.
2. Name problem 2's sources: industrial chemical waste, household liquid and solid waste, farm fertiliser, pesticide and herbicide chemicals — plus siltation.
3. Explain problem 3: how the water hyacinth (እንቦጭ) turns lake stretches to 'desert' — blocking sunlight and oxygen, harming navigation, irrigation and fish production.

## What you need
The class debate needs only the lesson; the weed can be observed at any pond edge where a floating mat forms.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the downstream countries' pressure is about the water's amount alone → the book frames it as positions over use: no agreement, obstruction, and Ethiopia's equality-and-fairness answer.
- Calls the hyacinth harmless because it is green and flowering → green can strangle: it blocks light and oxygen under the mat, and boats, irrigation and fish all suffer.
