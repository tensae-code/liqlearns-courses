# Water problems: sharing, pollution and the lake weed

## Do it first
A lake can be rich and still be in trouble. East Africa's water has three troubles, and the book names them in order — a quarrel, a poisoning, and a weed. This lesson takes them one by one.

## Problem 1 — shared rivers, no agreement
The first water problem is political: **transboundary rivers with no agreement** between the countries. On the Abbay/Nile, upper-basin and lower-basin positions block proper use; the downstream countries obstruct Ethiopia although it contributes **86% of the water**, and Ethiopia's firm printed position is to use its water **with equality and fairness** while advancing GERD construction. The book sets the discussion itself: what position should Ethiopia take, and why do the two downstream countries pressure it?

## Problem 2 — pollution
Water is dirtied by **industrial chemical waste, household liquid and solid waste, and farm fertiliser, pesticide and herbicide chemicals** — and heavy **siltation** shrinks the water itself. One river drawn clean above a town and dirty below it carries the whole problem: everything the town lets go, the river must carry.

## Problem 3 — the water hyacinth (እንቦጭ)
An invasive water weed, the **water hyacinth (እንቦጭ)**, now widespread on **Lake Tana** — and causing the same problem on Lake Victoria — turns stretches of lake to **"desert"**: it **blocks sunlight** from entering the water, **starves the water of oxygen** and its organisms of life, and **harms navigation, irrigation and fish production**.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the three problems in the book's order, Ethiopia's printed position on the first, the pollutant list on the second, and the hyacinth's four harms on the third.

## Do It Yourself
1. The debate, the book's own: split the class — upstream and downstream. Each side states its position from the lesson; the class judges which arguments the book itself prints.
2. One river audit: walk a local stream (with an adult) and list everything entering it — household, farm, workshop. Sort your list into the book's pollution kinds.
3. Mat watch: if a pond near school has floating plants, sketch its edge monthly. Is the mat growing? The hyacinth's spreading, watched at safe small scale.
