Today: **Desalination — making seawater drinkable** — Egypt's way, from its ministry books.

**Seawater holds salt — the problem by the sea.** Sea water is full of **dissolved salt**. That salt is why a land beside the sea can still be short of drinking water: the water is there, but as it is, living things cannot use it the way they use fresh water. Egypt's Primary 5 book teaches water as a precious resource first — its sources, its uses, and why it must be protected and saved — and then asks the harder question for people who live by the sea: can the sea itself become a source?

**Desalination — taking the salt out.** **Desalination** is getting fresh water from seawater: separating the water from the salt it holds, so what remains can be drunk and used like any fresh water. Egypt's book sets it as the interdisciplinary project **Water by the Sea** — learners work on the problem as a project, joining their science of water and matter to a real need of coastal life. The lesson the project teaches is the shape of the solution itself: the salt does not disappear; it is separated out, and the fresh water is what is collected.
