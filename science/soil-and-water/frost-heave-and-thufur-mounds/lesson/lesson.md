# Frost heave and þúfur mounds

## Do it first
A heath is covered in little mounds, hummock after hummock, as if the ground itself bubbled up. No animal dug them and nobody piled them. What made them? Say your best idea out loud. Then read on: Iceland's heath chapter proves the answer in a freezer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Þúfur — the mounds of the heath

The mounds are **þúfur** (thufur): the little hummocks that cover Iceland's heath ground. They are a frost-made landform — the ground itself lifted into mounds, winter work written on the heath.

## Sub lesson 2 — The freezer experiment — moist soil lifts as it freezes

The guide proves it with an experiment: **moist soil put in a freezer expands and lifts as it freezes**. Wet soil, frozen, takes more room and pushes up — and that same lifting, repeated winter after winter in the heath's moist ground, raises the þúfur. Freezer first, heath second: the small proof carries the big landform.

## The key ideas

- Þúfur are the little frost-made mounds that cover Iceland's heath ground.
- Moist soil expands and lifts as it freezes — proved by putting moist soil in a freezer.
- That same lifting, winter after winter, raises the þúfur in the heath.
