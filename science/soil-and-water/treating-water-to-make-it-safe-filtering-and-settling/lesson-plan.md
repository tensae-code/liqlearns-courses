# Lesson plan — Treating water to make it safe — filtering and settling

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Soil and Water · **Lesson:** Treating water to make it safe — filtering and settling
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water (CURRICULUM.md Unit 7), beside Water — why it is precious and how to save it and Water problems — sharing, pollution and the lake weed: this is the lesson that shows, by hand, how dirty water is made cleaner
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** river and well water carries mud, sand and germs you cannot always see; two simple physics treatments make it safer step by step — first SETTLING, letting the heavy dirt sink quietly to the bottom, then FILTERING, pouring the water through clean sand, gravel or cloth that traps what is left — and treated water is then kept covered and clean, because clear water that is stored badly becomes dirty again

## Sub lessons
1. **Settling — let the dirt sink** — muddy water left perfectly still slowly clears from the top down: the grains of sand and soil are heavier than water, so gravity pulls them down and they settle into a layer at the bottom — this is settling (sedimentation), the first and easiest treatment, and it works only if the water is left undisturbed
2. **Filtering — trap what is left** — pouring the settled water slowly through a filter — layers of clean gravel, clean sand, or a folded clean cloth — traps the fine dirt the settling missed: the water passes, the dirt stays in the filter; the slower and finer the filter, the clearer the water that comes through
3. **Keeping treated water safe** — treated water stays safe only if it is kept safe: store it in a clean, covered container, draw it with a clean cup instead of dipping hands, and remember that boiling is the surest home treatment of all — filtering and settling make water cleaner, cleanliness keeps it that way

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Settling: muddy water left still lets its heavy dirt sink to the bottom; the clearer water is poured off gently.
2. Say, in their own words: Filtering: water poured slowly through cloth, sand and gravel traps the fine dirt that settling missed.
3. Say, in their own words: Pour slowly, and keep the filter clean — a dirty filter adds dirt instead of trapping it.
4. Say, in their own words: Store treated water covered, and draw it with a clean cup — never with hands.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Two clear bottles or jars, muddy water (water stirred with a spoon of soil), a clean cloth or coffee filter, clean sand and small gravel if you have them, and a clean covered container.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the dirt in the still bottle sinks by itself. Settling is that sinking, given time and quiet.
- Thinks clear water is automatically safe water → clear means the dirt is out; germs are too small to see and partly pass a filter — that is why boiling and clean storage are in the lesson.
- Pours fast and gets cloudy water → the water shoved the dirt through. Slow pouring is not politeness; it is how the trap works.
