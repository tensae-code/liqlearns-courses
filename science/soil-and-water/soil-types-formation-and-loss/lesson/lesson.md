# Soil — types, formation and loss

## Do it first

Take three handfuls of different soils if you can: one gritty, one smooth and sticky, one in between. Rub each between finger and thumb. The gritty one is made of BIG pieces, the sticky one of tiny pieces — and that one difference, particle size, decides how each soil holds water, feeds plants and washes away. This lesson proves it with water.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three soils — particle size and the water test

**Sand** — the biggest particles; it feels gritty, lets water run straight through and holds almost none.

**Clay** — the smallest particles; it feels smooth and sticky, and holds the most water — puddles sit on it.

**Loam** — the in-between mixture; it holds water moderately, and it is the most fertile soil: it holds more nutrients, moisture and humus, drains air and water well and is easy to till.

The lab: put equal soil in three funnels over cups, pour equal water gently on each, and compare what the cups collect. The keyed result: **clay holds the most water, sand holds essentially none, loam is intermediate.** Silt, the guide adds, is more fertile than the other types for cropping because it promotes water retention and air circulation, and clay is the source of many properties that make soil a growth medium and a waste-disposal medium.

## Sub lesson 2 — How soil forms — and how it is lost

Soil begins as **parent rock**, broken down by **weathering** — water, heat, roots and time cracking and crumbling it — and mixed with the remains of living things.

Five factors govern the forming: **parent material** (what rock it came from), **climate**, **organisms**, **relief** (the shape and slope of the land) and **time**. Soil forms slowly — a handful is the work of ages.

**Erosion** is soil being carried away, chiefly by water and wind. Its causes split in two: **natural** causes — heavy rain, floods, wind, steep slopes — and **man-made** causes — clearing trees, overgrazing, ploughing that leaves soil bare.
