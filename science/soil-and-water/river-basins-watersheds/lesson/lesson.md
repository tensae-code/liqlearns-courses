# River basins — watersheds

## Do it first
Rain falls on a hill. Where does it go — and does it join the same river as rain falling on the next hill, or a different one? The line that decides that question divides one basin from another. Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Mexico's book teaches the whole country basin by basin.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hydrographic basins — definition and characteristics

A **hydrographic basin** is the land a river system drains: the waters of an area gather and run together through its rivers. The book opens with the basin's **definition and characteristics** — a basin is known as one whole drainage unit, not as single rivers listed one by one.

## Sub lesson 2 — The distribution of Mexico's basins

Next, the book maps them: the **distribution of Mexico's basins** across the country. Read as basins, a country's map changes: instead of separate rivers, you see the great drainage wholes that organise its waters from source to sea.

## Sub lesson 3 — Basins in economic development

The book closes on use: basins **in economic development**. The waters gathered in a basin serve the life and work of the people inside it, so a basin is taught in Mexico as part of the country's development — a natural unit with an economic life.
