# Modern irrigation — saving water in farming

## Do it first
A farmer has one well and a field of thirsty crops under a hot sun. Half the water she pours never reaches a root. Where does it go — and how could a better system deliver the water to the plants instead? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Egypt's Primary 6 way in — its interdisciplinary project: a new modern irrigation system (printed p. 80).

## Sub lesson 1 — Farming drinks water — and loses much of it

**Irrigation** is bringing water to crops. Egypt's Primary 6 book arrives here through soil and environmental change: how soil forms, changes and is lost, and how it is protected. Water spread openly over a field is shared with the sun and the wind — much evaporates or runs past the roots before a plant can drink. In a dry land, every lost bucket is a loss the farm and the aquifer both feel; saving water in farming is protecting soil and water together.

## Sub lesson 2 — A new modern irrigation system — the project

The interdisciplinary project is to design **a new modern irrigation system**: a system whose measure of success is how much of its water reaches the plants' roots. Designing it joins the unit's science — water movement, soil, evaporation under heat — to the farmer's need. As with every design project in these books, the design is a claim to be tested: how much water did the old way lose, how much does the new system save, and what proves it?
