# Groundwater and springs

## Do it first
Dig a hole at a riverbank and water seeps in — though nobody poured it there. Where was that water hiding? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Armenia's way in — after the surface waters, its Grade 6 book goes underground, §23: groundwater, springs and artesian waters.

## Sub lesson 1 — Groundwater — the water under the ground

Rain does not all run away: part of it **soaks into the ground** and collects there, in the spaces of soil and rock, as **groundwater**. It is the land's hidden water store — out of sight, moving only very slowly, and feeding the surface waters from below.

## Sub lesson 2 — Springs and artesian waters — where the hidden water comes out

Where the ground's surface cuts into the groundwater, the water comes out by itself as a **spring**. And where groundwater lies held under pressure between layers, a well bored into it makes the water rise on its own — **artesian water**. Spring and artesian well are the two doors by which the hidden water reaches people and rivers.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Where does groundwater come from, and how does it come back out?" Answer: Rain soaks into the ground and collects as groundwater; it comes out where the surface cuts into it, as a spring, or rises under pressure in an artesian well.
