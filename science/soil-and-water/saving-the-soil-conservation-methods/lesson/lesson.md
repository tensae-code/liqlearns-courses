# Saving the soil — conservation methods

## Do it first

Rain is coming to two hillsides. One is bare; the other is stepped like stairs, lined with trees and ploughed along its curves. In one storm, which hillside keeps its soil? You know already. This lesson names each protection on the second hillside — and the cause each one defeats.

## Sub lesson 1 — Holding the slope — terraces, contours, dams and cover

**Terracing** — cutting a steep slope into flat steps. Rain that would rush down now rests on each step and sinks in; the soil stays. It defeats erosion by running water on slopes.

**Contour ploughing** — ploughing along the level curves of a slope, not up and down it. Each furrow is a small wall that slows water instead of a channel that speeds it.

**Check dams** — small barriers built across a gully. They slow the flood water, trap the soil it carries, and heal the gully step by step.

**Leaving crop residue as cover** — stalks and leaves left on the field after harvest shield the soil from rain's beating and sun's drying, and rot into the soil's food.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planting against loss — forests and shelter belts

**Afforestation** — planting trees where there were none (or where they were lost). Roots hold the soil, leaves break the rain's force, and fallen leaves feed the soil. It defeats erosion on bare land and slopes alike.

**Shelter belts** — lines of trees planted around fields to break the wind. Slower wind cannot lift and carry the topsoil; the belt also shelters the crop inside it.

The matching rule for the whole toolkit: first name the cause — running water, wind, bare beating rain, a growing gully — then choose the method built against that cause. A method matched to the wrong cause protects little.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — holding the slope — terraces, contours, dams and cover first, then planting against loss — forests and shelter belts. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Show me where that is in the picture." Answer by pointing, not by repeating.
