# Lesson plan — Soil fertility, erosion, and why soil matters

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Soil and Water · **Lesson:** Soil fertility, erosion, and why soil matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water — what makes soil good, what carries it away, and the chain that hangs on it (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fertility is decided by parent rock, chemical content, land shape, climate, remains and small living things — and fine smooth soil is carried away by water and wind more easily than coarse soil, while everything we eat hangs on soil: no soil, no plants; no plants, no animals; no animals, no food
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Loam, clay and sand — the kinds are known; now their goodness and their danger.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name what decides fertility: parent rock, chemical content, land shape, climate, amount of plant/animal remains, small living things.
2. Say why fine smooth soil erodes more easily: rain cannot soak in, floods wash it off, and its light particles blow away — while coarse soil lets water in and resists both water and wind.
3. Recite the food chain that hangs on soil, and name soil's other uses: building, pottery, household goods.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks rain soaks into every soil alike → fine smooth soil sheds water over its surface; coarse soil drinks it. The soak test settles it.
- Thinks erosion is only water → wind takes the light particles of fine soil too; the book names both carriers.
