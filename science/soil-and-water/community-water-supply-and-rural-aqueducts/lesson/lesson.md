# Community water supply and rural aqueducts

## Do it first
A city turns a tap and water comes. But who brings safe drinking water to a small rural community, far from the city's pipes — and who decides how it is shared and kept clean? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Costa Rica's own way — its Grade 7 Ciencias teaches the rural aqueducts, the ASADAS, as the country's own answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rural aqueducts — a community's own water system

A **rural aqueduct** is a water system that brings **drinking water** to a rural community: water is taken from a source, carried through pipes, and supplied to the homes of the community it serves.

In Costa Rica these systems are run for the community by its own water association — an **ASADA** (Asociación Administradora de Sistemas de Acueductos y Alcantarillados Sanitarios), the community body that administers the local aqueduct. The supply is not a private tap for one house; it is a shared system that a whole community depends on together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Run under official rules — Costa Rica's own way

Costa Rica teaches its rural aqueducts as systems with **official rules**: the ASADAS supply community drinking water under the country's own regulations, so the taking, carrying and sharing of the water is organised and answerable, not left to chance.

That rule-bound, community-run shape IS the lesson's content — Costa Rica's own way of teaching water supply: the community administers its aqueduct itself, inside rules the country sets, so that safe drinking water reaches rural homes fairly and keeps reaching them.
