# Hard and soft water

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — hard and soft water. Hold it in your mind, say what you already know about it out loud. Then read on: this is Bhutan's way in — Bhutan's Class VI book teaches water hardness inside its chemical-change chapter, in three steps: what hard and soft water are, how hardness is removed, and the advantages and disadvantages of hard water.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hard water and soft water — the lather test

Not all water washes the same. **Soft water** gives a good lather with soap at once — the bubbles rise easily and the washing is quick. **Hard water** fights the soap: the lather comes slowly and poorly, soap is wasted, and a scum can form instead of bubbles. Water picks up its hardness on its way through the ground — from the rocks and soil it has passed through and dissolved a little of — so two places drawing water from different ground can have different water in their buckets.

## Sub lesson 2 — Removing the hardness of water

Hardness is not in the water for good; it can be taken out — the water can be **softened**. Boiling drives out one kind of hardness, and water can be treated and filtered through softening materials that take the hardness out as the water passes. Softened water lathers freely again: the proof that the hardness is gone is the soap test itself, run a second time.

## Sub lesson 3 — Advantages and disadvantages of hard water

Bhutan's book weighs hard water honestly, on both sides. Against it: it wastes soap, leaves scum, and can fur up kettles and pipes with the hardness it carries. For it: the same dissolved minerals that make it hard are among the things living bodies use, and many people prefer its taste. Hard or soft is not good or bad by itself — it is a property of the water, to be known, tested, and softened where soft water serves better.

## The key ideas

- **Soft water** lathers easily with soap; **hard water** lathers poorly, wastes soap and can leave scum.
- Water takes its hardness from the rocks and soil it passes through.
- Hardness can be **removed** — by boiling, and by softening treatments — and the lather test proves it is gone.
- Hard water has both **advantages and disadvantages** — the lesson weighs both, as Bhutan's book does.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How can you tell hard water from soft water without any instrument?" Answer from the lesson: Wash with soap in each: the soft water lathers freely at once, while the hard water gives a poor lather, wastes soap and may leave a scum. The soap itself is the test.

## Do It Yourself
1. Collect water from two sources your home uses and run the lather test on each — same soap, same shaking, in two clear bottles. Record which is harder and what you saw.
2. Boil a sample of your harder water, let it cool, and run the lather test again. Write what changed, or did not change.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the two kinds of water, and the honest weighing of hard water.
