# Drought: what it is and how we hold it back

## Do it first
One hot week is not drought. Drought is when the **rain fails for a long time**, the streams shrink, the wells sink, and the land itself cracks. It does not strike and leave like a storm — its harm **lasts long**, striking people, plants, animals and the land's surface together. This lesson names it exactly, and then names the works that hold it back.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rain failed a long time — the definition and the causes
**Drought** is **when rain fails for a long time in an area and water sources dry up**.

Its **main cause is an irregular climate**: the rainfall amount and spread fall, the earth's moisture vanishes, and temperature rises. And drought **can also follow deforestation** — cut the forest, and the dry harm comes easier.

It differs from other natural events in one grim way: **its harm lasts long**, striking people, plants, animals and the land's surface all at once and for a long time.

## Sub lesson 2 — Holding it back — the works
The book's answer is work, before and during:

- **Care for and use the forest resource carefully; plant trees and improve plant cover — plant many trees for every one cut.**
- **Watershed work: soil terraces, stone terraces, soil-and-gabion works, water harvesting.**
- **Stop free grazing**, so the cover can recover.

And in **drought-prone places**, plant **drought-resisting trees**, grow **plants that use little water**, and **cover the ground with remains and straw — mulch — to cut evaporation** in high heat.

Drought cannot be ordered away. But land that is terraced, covered and harvested-for-water meets it standing.

## Grade 5 — drought: causes, effects, the rainfall map, satellites
**Drought** is the event of too little rain: rainfall below what a place should get, so moisture fails and production shrinks or vanishes, and famine follows in its train. **Four printed causes**: rain shortage or total failure; land-use change (deforestation, free grazing, backward farming); surface waters drying up; climate change and global warming. Two root causes underlined for Ethiopia: the north's long misuse of its natural resources, and the Sahara desert spreading south. **Effects** come in three families: **environmental** (crop failure, a disturbed ecosystem, danger to biodiversity, death and displacement), **economic** (no food grain to eat, and no grain means no money) and **social** (health problems, conflicts over water, a degraded life) — a chain activity maps the cascade: drought → famine → disease → … Reading the rainfall map against the land heights: highlands generally get enough rain, lowlands little — so the **eastern and south-eastern lowlands** are the drought-prone side. The fix named: **harvesting rainwater** and using ground and surface water well, with irrigation beside the meher harvest. And modern defence: geographic information systems and meteorological satellite data — Ethiopia's own satellites are celebrated: **ETRSS-1**, a 72 kg remote-sensing satellite launched from China on **Tahsas 10, 2012 E.C.**, for climate monitoring, agricultural research and infrastructure — and the second, **ET-Smart-RSS**, launched Tahsas 13, 2013 E.C.

## Grade 6 — drought and famine remembered; the Rift; coping together
The Grade 6 book dates Ethiopia's unforgettable drought-famine events: **1966 E.C. and 1977 E.C.**, with terrible loss of human and animal life; the phenomenon is expected on average about **every ten years**. Drought's listed causes there: **deforestation, using land improperly, exhaustion of the land's fertility, overgrazing, and ploughing drought-prone areas widely**. The **Rift Valley** — stretching from Syria to Mozambique, low and hot — leaves the areas it borders often meeting severe drought; drought strikes chiefly at **agriculture, transport, wildlife's way of life, human health, the environment and ecosystem, and the clean-water supply**. Against the memory stands one gain: through forest, soil and water conservation work, Ethiopia's forest cover is known to have passed **10%**. Coping, in the chapter's closing work: East African countries must **act together**; Ethiopia's wide **tree-planting** explained; the yearly **soil and water conservation** work of farmers set out; and the "water tower" question posed — Ethiopia's rivers have flowed out for ages without serving it, so what must citizens do to use the water wealth to end drought and famine?

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the definition, the two causes, and four holding-back works — then the three special rules for drought-prone places.

## Do It Yourself
1. Terrace spotting: find a terraced slope near home or on a journey. Say what the terraces do when the rains finally come.
2. Mulch trial: cover two patches of bare ground — one with straw or dry remains, one bare. After a hot day, feel both soils. Which kept its moisture?
3. Many-for-one pledge: count the trees cut near home this year (ask). The book's rule: many planted for every one cut. Count the seedlings planted too.
