# Ethiopia's rivers and lakes

## Do it first
Most of Ethiopia's rivers are born in the western highlands and run outward like fingers from a hand. The book calls the country Africa's water tower. This lesson reads the tower's plumbing — three systems, three kinds of lake — and then asks the hard question: why is so much of it unused?

## Three drainage systems
Ethiopia, rich in rivers and lakes, is called **Africa's water tower**; most rivers rise in the **western highlands** and run to lowlands, seasonal in flow, winding and steep with waterfalls in their upper courses. By drainage they form **three systems**: the **western (Mediterranean)** system — **Baro, Abbay and Tekeze**, joining to become the **Nile** in Sudan; the **south-eastern (Indian Ocean)** system — **Wabi Shebele and Genale**; and the **inland (Rift Valley)** system — **Awash and Omo**, ending in Rift lakes. Pupils complete a source/direction/use table for Abbay, Baro, Tekeze, Awash and Omo.

## Lakes — three kinds
Lakes were formed by Rift and volcanic processes, not by climate. **Highland lakes**: **Tana — the largest**; the volcanic Bishoftu, Ziquala, Zengena, Ashenge. **Rift Valley lakes** running from Abe (Djibouti border) to Rudolf/Turkana (Kenya border) — Abe, Ziway, Langano, Shala, Abiyata, Awasa, Abaya, Chamo: **Abaya the biggest of them, Ziway the shallowest, Shala the deepest**. **Man-made lakes** built for power, irrigation and fish: **Koka, Fincha, Melka Wakana, Abba Samuel, and the GERD reservoir**. The Grade 5 teacher guide's water subtopics (teacher guide — evidence) carry the same map teaching — lake types and characteristics beside the main river-basin system, naming Abbay, Tekeze, Baro, Gibe and Awash, and noting most rivers rise in high places.

## Why the water wealth is under-used
Apart from the Awash, no river yet gives its full economic worth. Three printed reason-groups: **policy and strategy** failures (no prioritised, realistic national water plan or study); **backwardness** (technology, money, trained people, weak irrigation, weak protection and management); and **external factors** — water politics in the Horn of Africa, with foreign interests (Egypt, Sudan, America and other Western countries named over the GERD) pressing against Ethiopia's right to use its water.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the three systems with a river each, the three lake kinds with a lake each and its superlative if it has one, and the three reason-groups for under-use.
