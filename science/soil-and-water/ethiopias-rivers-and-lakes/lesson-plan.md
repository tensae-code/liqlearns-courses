# Lesson plan — Ethiopia's rivers and lakes

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Soil and Water · **Lesson:** Ethiopia's rivers and lakes
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water — the country's water wealth, Grade 5 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia — Africa's water tower — drains three ways: the western system (Baro, Abbay, Tekeze → the Nile), the south-eastern (Wabi Shebele, Genale → the Indian Ocean) and the inland Rift system (Awash, Omo); its lakes are highland, Rift Valley and man-made; and apart from the Awash its water wealth is under-used for three printed reasons
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Water: why every living thing needs it — water's uses are known; now the country's whole water map is read.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three drainage systems with their rivers: western — Baro, Abbay, Tekeze; south-eastern — Wabi Shebele, Genale; inland — Awash, Omo.
2. Sort Ethiopia's lakes into highland, Rift Valley and man-made, with the book's superlatives: Tana largest, Abaya biggest of the Rift lakes, Ziway shallowest, Shala deepest.
3. State the three reason-groups why the water wealth is under-used: policy and strategy, backwardness, external factors.

## What you need
A map of Ethiopia (the class wall map) for tracing the three systems in three colours.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls every river a Nile river → only the western system joins the Nile; the south-eastern system ends in the Indian Ocean and the inland system never leaves — it ends in Rift lakes.
- Sorts the GERD reservoir with the Rift lakes → it is man-made, built for power: kind is decided by how the lake was made, not where it sits.
