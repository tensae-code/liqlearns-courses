# Swamps and glaciers

## Do it first
A river runs. A lake stands. But water has two slower forms still: ground so wet it never dries, and ice so great it covers mountains. Name a wet place and a frozen place you know. Then read on: this is Armenia's way in — after rivers and lakes, its Grade 6 book closes the land waters with swamps and glaciers, §22, and asks what each one means for nature.

## Sub lesson 1 — Swamps — water that stands in the land

A **swamp** is land where water stands and soaks the ground for most or all of the year — neither open water like a lake nor dry land, but the wet ground between. Swamp plants and animals are fitted to that standing water, and the swamp holds water back, releasing it slowly — its standing is its work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Glaciers — water held as ice, and what both mean

A **glacier** is a great mass of ice on the land, built from snow pressed over long years, holding fresh water frozen in place and feeding rivers as it melts at its edges. Swamp and glacier are the land's two slow stores: one holds water **wet**, the other holds it **frozen** — and that holding is the significance of both for the waters around them.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is the difference between a swamp and a glacier?" Answer: A swamp is land where water stands and soaks the ground; a glacier is a great mass of ice holding fresh water frozen. Both are slow stores of the land's water.
