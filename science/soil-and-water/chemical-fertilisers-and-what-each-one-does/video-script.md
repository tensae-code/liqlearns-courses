Today: **Chemical fertilisers and what each one does** — Nepal's way, from its Curriculum Development Centre books.

Chemical (inorganic) fertilisers by the nutrient they give: nitrogen fertilisers (urea, ammonium sulphate, ammonium nitrate — fast leafy growth; lack yellows the leaves and shrinks flower, fruit and seed), phosphatic fertilisers (ammonium phosphate, superphosphates — root growth and early, full grain; lack weakens roots and delays ripening), and potassic fertilisers (potassium chloride, sulphate, nitrate and carbonate, and ash — strong stems and roots and disease resistance; lack shrinks and sheds leaves), opening with the book's own story of a farmer whose maize failed until a soil test showed each part of his field lacked a different element.

Chemical fertilisers by nutrient: nitrogen fertilisers (ammonium nitrate, urea, ammonium sulphate) for fast growth, green leaves and protein, their lack showing in yellow leaves and small fruit; phosphorus fertilisers (ammonium phosphate, superphosphate, bone meal) for roots, ripening and grain; potassium fertilisers (potassium chloride, nitrate and sulphate, and ash) for food-making, their lack wilting leaves and weakening disease resistance; NPK as the three mixed together (same proposed lesson as in the Science and Technology Grade 9 section)
