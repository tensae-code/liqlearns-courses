# Famine, and how it differs from drought

## Do it first
Imagine the rains fail. The fields give nothing. Do the people starve — always? Not always. One word makes the difference: **stores**. A community with full grain stores can eat through a dry year. A community without them falls into famine. The two harms are cousins, not twins — this lesson separates them.

## Drought's further harm — and its other roots
**Famine** can **follow drought's spread**: when drought comes, **food and water for animals and people run short**, and hunger takes hold.

But mark the book's careful line: **drought alone does not mean famine**, because **stored food reserves can carry a community through**. The store is the bridge over the dry year.

And famine has **other roots** that are not weather at all:

- farmers **not producing enough with improved crops and animals**,
- **modern farming not spreading**,
- **political instability and civil war**.

So famine is fought on three fields: the sky's rain, the farmer's production, and the country's peace — with the grain store standing guard between drought and hunger.

## Grade 5 — famine and its causes; coping with drought, Ethiopia's own methods
**Famine** is the event of severe food shortage from natural or human-made problems. Printed causes: **climate change (drought and rain failure), high population, crop loss (pests, locusts, hail), poverty, war, price inflation and political pressure**. The book's discussion: in drought-time markets, which falls in price — grain or animals — and why? (Grain rises as it vanishes; animals fall as everyone sells and no one can feed them — the reasoning the discussion teaches.) Then the **indigenous coping list**: sow productive, fast-maturing and drought-resistant crops; build market links; keep small animals (sheep, goats, chickens), bees and fodder plots; strengthen community natural-resource management and planting; **harvest rainwater**; care for lakes, rivers, ponds and wetlands; use water sparingly with varied irrigation; improve environmental care (environmental education, improved fuel-saving stoves); and make sure farmers **hear climate forecasts** from the experts.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion what famine is, why drought does not always bring it, and the three other roots the book names.

## Do It Yourself
1. Store audit (respectful): ask at home what food reserves the household keeps and how long they would last. That number is your family's bridge.
2. Root sorting: here are three causes of famine — failed rain, low production, civil war. Which are weather? Which are people's doing? The book counts all three.
3. Two villages tale: tell a short story of two villages in the same drought — one with stores, one without — and how their years differed.
