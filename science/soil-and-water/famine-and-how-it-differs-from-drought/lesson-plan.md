# Lesson plan — Famine, and how it differs from drought

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Soil and Water · **Lesson:** Famine, and how it differs from drought
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water — the lesson after drought: when the food runs out, and why it need not (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** famine can follow drought — food and water for animals and people run short — but drought alone does not mean famine, because stored food reserves can carry a community through; famine's other roots are too little production with improved crops and animals, modern farming not spreading, and political instability and civil war
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Drought: what it is and how we hold it back — drought named and answered; famine is the further harm, and it has further causes.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what famine is: when drought spreads, food and water for animals and people run short.
2. Say the key difference: drought alone does not mean famine — stored food reserves can carry a community through.
3. Name famine's other roots: not producing enough with improved crops and animals, modern farming not spreading, political instability and civil war.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses drought and famine as one word → drought is the rain failing; famine is the food failing. Stores stand between them, and the book insists on the difference.
