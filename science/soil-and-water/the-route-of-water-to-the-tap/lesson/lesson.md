# The route of water to the tap

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's title — The route of water to the tap. Say out loud anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on: this is Venezuela's way in — its Grade 2 *Comunidad con Ciencia* follows the water on its journey through the community: the route the water takes before it reaches the tap.

## Sub lesson 1 — Water travels to reach us

The water at the tap did not begin at the tap. It **travels a route**: through **pipes**, held in **storage tanks**, and lifted by **pumps** where it must climb. Following that route — from its source to the tap — is the lesson's journey.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two kinds of consumption

At the end of the route, water is **consumed** — used — in two named kinds: **domestic** consumption, the water used in homes, and **industrial** consumption, the water used in workplaces and making. The book teaches the two apart in a **building drawing**: trace the water through the building and say, at each use, which kind it is.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Water travels to reach us first, then Two kinds of consumption. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What does water pass through on its route to the tap?" Answer: Pipes, storage tanks and pumps — and at the end it is consumed, domestically in homes or industrially in workplaces.
