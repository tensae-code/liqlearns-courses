# Pesticides — bio and chemical — and using them safely

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — pesticides — bio and chemical — and using them safely. Then read on: this is Nepal's way in — its Curriculum Development Centre books teach this thing at the places named in this lesson's sources, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Insecticides

Insecticides: bio-insecticides made from plants insects dislike — the fermented jholmol of nettle, neem, timur and other strong-smelling leaves with cow urine and dung, sprayed diluted; ash dusted on fields; neem leaves stored with grain — against chemical insecticides made in factories (DDT, BHC, methoxychlor, aldrin, dieldrin, malathion)

## Sub lesson 2 — The harm chemical insecticides do and the care their use demands

The harm chemical insecticides do and the care their use demands: residues entering the body (DDT dust causing breathing disease), damage to nerves and glands, pollution of air, water and soil, useful insects killed along with harmful ones, and harm to fish, birds and the whole ecosystem; the precautions — study the method first, wear protective clothing, keep the spray from eyes and mouth, spray with the wind, store leftovers safely, and never wash spraying equipment near a water source (same proposed lesson)

## Sub lesson 3 — Insecticides in this book

Insecticides in this book: DDT, BHC, methoxychlor, aldrin and dieldrin kill useful insects along with harmful ones, unbalance the ecosystem, and harm birds and fish — fewer eggs, infertile eggs, thin shells — and the human breathing system (same proposed lesson as in the Science and Technology Grade 9 section)

## Sub lesson 4 — What pesticides do, good and bad

What pesticides do, good and bad (guide evidence; lesson first proposed in the Grade 9 Nepali student book above): used against harmful insects, they can also destroy useful insects, harm the health of the person who sprays them when care is lacking, and harm the people who eat the sprayed vegetables and fruit; the kinds are sorted three ways — by their effect on the environment, by the organism they target, and by how they work; DDT, BHC, aldrin and malathion named in the unit test.

## Sub lesson 5 — Storing and using poisonous chemicals with care

Storing and using poisonous chemicals with care (guide evidence; lesson first proposed in the Grade 9 Nepali student book above): pesticides are not medicines but poisons; used without proper care they damage the environment gravely, unbalance the ecosystem and harm the life processes of every living thing, people included — the guide collects the class's own list of storage and use precautions.
