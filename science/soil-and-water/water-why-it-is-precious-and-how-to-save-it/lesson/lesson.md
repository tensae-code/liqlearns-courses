# Water — why it is precious and how to save it

## Do it first

Picture all the water on Earth in one big jar. Almost the whole jar — over 96 parts in 100 — is salty sea, undrinkable. The fresh water of every river, lake and ground store is the small cup beside the jar. Your own body is about 60% that fresh water's work. This lesson guards the small cup.

## Sub lesson 1 — How much water — and how little of it we can use

Water covers **71%** of the Earth's surface — the planet looks blue and water-rich.

But **over 96%** of that water is **saline** — salty sea water that people, crops and most living things cannot use as it is. The fresh share is small, and not all of even that is reachable.

Your own body is **about 60% water**: water carries food and wastes in you, cools you, and fills every cell. A resource that is both scarce in usable form and built into your body is precious by both counts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saving it — the conservation practice

**Prevent pollution** — dirty water is water lost: keeping waste out of rivers and wells keeps the fresh share usable.

**Reuse wastewater** and **plug leaks** — water used twice, and water never dripping away, are both water saved at home.

**Harvest rainwater** — catch roof rain in tanks and jars in the wet season for the dry one; **dam and store** water the same way at community scale.

**Use irrigation water efficiently** — give crops the water they need, where and when they need it, not more.

**Protect groundwater** — the stored water underground is the dry-season reserve; do not waste or pollute it. And **plant trees**: trees hold water in the land, feed springs and steady the rain.
