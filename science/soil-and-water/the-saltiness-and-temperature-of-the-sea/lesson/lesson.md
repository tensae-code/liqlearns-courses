# The saltiness and temperature of the sea

## Do it first
A litre of seawater is not a litre of water. Something is dissolved in it — how much? And is the sea the same temperature at the bottom as at the top? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Samoa's Year 11 way in — an appendix that measures the sea itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Salinity — how salty, and why it changes

**Salinity** is the sea's dissolved load: the ocean is **96.5% water and 3.5% dissolved solids**, measured in **parts per thousand**, and the dissolved part is a mix of common **ions**. Salinity is not the same everywhere: **rainfall** freshens the surface and **evaporation** concentrates it, so salinity runs lower near the equator and off river coasts where rain and rivers dilute it. For the animals living in it, salinity changes the water itself — its **viscosity** and the **osmotic pressure** at their skins and gills (printed p. 114).

## Sub lesson 2 — Ocean temperature — warm top, cold deep

Around Samoa the sea sits at **26–28°C all year**. Sunlight reaches down about **60 metres**, warming the top; below that the water is cold. The surface does not stay separate: it **mixes with the cooler deeper water**, and **evaporation** carries heat away from the surface. The book reads these two measures together off a graph of a lagoon — the Great Barrier Reef lagoon's temperature and salinity (printed pp. 94, 114).
