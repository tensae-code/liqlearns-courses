Today: **The saltiness and temperature of the sea** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**Salinity — how salty, and why it changes.** Salinity is the sea's dissolved load: the ocean is 96.5% water and 3.5% dissolved solids, measured in parts per thousand, and the dissolved part is a mix of common ions. Salinity is not the same everywhere: rainfall freshens the surface and evaporation concentrates it, so salinity runs lower near the equator and off river coasts where rain and rivers dilute it. For the animals living in it, salinity changes the water itself — its viscosity and the osmotic pressure at their skins and gills (printed p. 114).

**Ocean temperature — warm top, cold deep.** Around Samoa the sea sits at 26–28°C all year. Sunlight reaches down about 60 metres, warming the top; below that the water is cold. The surface does not stay separate: it mixes with the cooler deeper water, and evaporation carries heat away from the surface. The book reads these two measures together off a graph of a lagoon — the Great Barrier Reef lagoon's temperature and salinity (printed pp. 94, 114).
