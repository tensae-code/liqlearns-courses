# Drinking water — access as a human right

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Drinking water — access as a human right. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Venezuela's Grade 9 Colección Bicentenario book teaches water fully — what drinking water is, how it is made drinkable and how it reaches the home — and then names the principle underneath: access to drinking water is a human right, in the community's keeping.

In your mind's eye: access to safe drinking water is a human right — something every person is owed simply for being a person — and with the right comes a duty: water that is a right for everyone must not be wasted by anyone. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**A right, not a luxury.** Because no life is possible without water, access to drinking water is named a human right: every person, rich or poor, town or country, is owed enough safe water to drink and live. A right is not bought by merit and cannot fairly be refused.

**The community's duty.** A shared right carries a shared duty. The book's saying is plain: water you do not have to drink, do not let it run to waste — "agua que no has de beber… no la dejes perder". Wasting water takes from the same store that everyone else's right is drawn from.

**Rights need systems.** The right reaches a home through real work: sources protected, water made drinkable, pipes and cisterns maintained. Knowing how water is treated and delivered — the rest of the book's water reading — is knowing what a community must keep working so that the right is real and not only said.
