# Drinking water — access as a human right — writing

Write your answer out in full sentences.

**1.** Write two or three sentences explaining drinking water — access as a human right, using the lesson's key facts.
**Model answer: Access to safe drinking water is a human right — something every person is owed simply for being a person — and with the right comes a duty: water that is a right for everyone must not be wasted by anyone. Because no life is possible without water, access to drinking water is named a human right: every person, rich or poor, town or country, is owed enough safe water to drink and live. A right is not bought by merit and cannot fairly be refused. A shared right carries a shared duty. The book's saying is plain: water you do not have to drink, do not let it run to waste — "agua que no has de beber… no la dejes perder". Wasting water takes from the same store that everyone else's right is drawn from. The right reaches a home through real work: sources protected, water made drinkable, pipes and cisterns maintained. Knowing how water is treated and delivered — the rest of the book's water reading — is knowing what a community must keep working so that the right is real and not only said.**
