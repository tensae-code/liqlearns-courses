# Drinking water — access as a human right — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: a right, not a luxury.
**Answer: Because no life is possible without water, access to drinking water is named a human right: every person, rich or poor, town or country, is owed enough safe water to drink and live. A right is not bought by merit and cannot fairly be refused.**

**2.** Explain: the community's duty.
**Answer: A shared right carries a shared duty. The book's saying is plain: water you do not have to drink, do not let it run to waste — "agua que no has de beber… no la dejes perder". Wasting water takes from the same store that everyone else's right is drawn from.**

**3.** Explain: rights need systems.
**Answer: The right reaches a home through real work: sources protected, water made drinkable, pipes and cisterns maintained. Knowing how water is treated and delivered — the rest of the book's water reading — is knowing what a community must keep working so that the right is real and not only said.**
