# Hands-on — Drinking water — access as a human right

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Count how many times your household uses drinking water in one day — drinking, cooking, washing food, cleaning teeth. Mark each use: needed, or could it use less? Write one change that wastes less, and who else benefits when your home saves.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of drinking water — access as a human right — access to safe drinking water is a human right — something every person is owed simply for being a person — and with the right comes a duty: water that is a right for everyone must not be wasted by anyone. — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
