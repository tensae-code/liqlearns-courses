# Lesson plan — The great lakes and the Nile

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Soil and Water · **Lesson:** The great lakes and the Nile
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water — the region's waters at East Africa scale (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** most of Africa's lakes lie in East Africa — Victoria (66,400 km², third in the world by area, shallow at 92 m), Malawi and Tanganyika (deepest at 1,435 m) — and the region's rivers crown in the Nile, the world's longest at 6,650 km, whose Blue branch rises in Ethiopia at Gish Abbay carrying 86% of its water, meeting the White Nile from Lake Victoria at Sudan's capital
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Ethiopia's rivers and lakes — one country's systems read; now the whole region's.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Measure the three great lakes: by area Victoria, Malawi, Tanganyika — and by depth the flipped order, Tanganyika 1,435 m deepest, Victoria only 92 m.
2. Trace the Nile: Blue Nile from Gish Abbay, White Nile from Lake Victoria, joining at Sudan's capital to run to the Mediterranean — 6,650 km, with the Blue Nile carrying 86% of the water.
3. Say how water works for the region — Koka, Fincha, Gilgel Gibe, Tana Beles, Tekeze, Owen Falls (Jinja) and the GERD — and why water transport stays small, with its exceptions.

## If the learner is stuck
- Assumes the biggest lake is also deepest → Victoria wins area and loses depth: 66,400 km² but only 92 m deep; Tanganyika is the deep one at 1,435 m.
- Puts the Nile's source in one place → it has two births: Blue at Gish Abbay in Ethiopia, White from Lake Victoria — one name only after they meet.
