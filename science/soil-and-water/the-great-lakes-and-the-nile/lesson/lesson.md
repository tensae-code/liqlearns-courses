# The great lakes and the Nile

## Do it first
One lake you could sail for days. One river that walks through six countries to the sea. East Africa's waters are world-sized — this lesson measures them with the book's own numbers, and follows the great river from both its births to their meeting.

## The great lakes, measured
Most of Africa's lakes lie in East Africa, most of them in the Rift Valley (Victoria and Tana sit on high land). The giants are measured: **Lake Victoria 66,400 km²** — third in the world by area, seventh by volume, shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania; **Malawi 38,800 km²**; **Tanganyika 32,890 km²**. By depth the order flips: **Tanganyika 1,435 m is deepest**, then Malawi 706 m, then shallow **Victoria at 92 m**. The Rift and Ethiopian lakes are named too — Kivu, Edward, Albert, Turkana, Meru, Langano, Chamo and Abaya.

## Rivers and the Nile
The region's rivers are named — Tana and Ewaso in Kenya, Shebele in Somalia, Rufiji and Ruvuma in Tanzania — before the book crowns one: **the Nile, the world's longest river at 6,650 km**. The **Blue Nile** rises in Ethiopia at **Gish Abbay**; the **White Nile** flows from **Lake Victoria**; the two meet at **Sudan's capital** to take the name Nile and run through Sudan and Egypt to the **Mediterranean**. The Blue Nile carries **86% of the Nile's water**; its biggest beneficiaries are Egypt and Sudan, while Ethiopia, contributing the 86%, is — as the book prints it — "not a beneficiary".

## Water at work — and water transport
Water works through dams: **Koka Dam** on the Awash, built mainly for power, which also enabled irrigation in the lower Awash valley; irrigation farming around the **Fincha, Gilgel Gibe, Tana Beles and Tekeze** dams; Uganda's dam at **Owen Falls (Jinja)**; and the **GERD**, which when finished will be a major power source for Ethiopia and all East Africa, with irrigation besides. Transport on the region's water stays **mostly small**, and the book says why: heavy silt and plants at river mouths, flow that rises and falls, and many narrow deep gorges. The exceptions: the **Baro** river (Ethiopia) and **Tana** river (Kenya); Lakes **Victoria, Malawi and Tanganyika**; Lake **Tana** linking Gojjam and Gondar; Lake **Ziway** linking Meki, Ziway, Asela and Bulbula; and Zambia's man-made **Lake Kariba**, serving transport all year.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lakes' two orders — area and depth — then walk the Nile from both births to the meeting and the sea, with the 86% said at the right place.

## Do It Yourself
1. Two orders drill: write Victoria, Malawi, Tanganyika in area order; shuffle; write them in depth order. Why does the order flip? (Victoria is wide but shallow.)
2. The 86% in your hands: fill a jug to 86% and a cup with the rest. If the jug is Ethiopia's contribution, who does the book say benefits most — and what does it call Ethiopia? ("Not a beneficiary" — argue fairly about that sentence in class.)
3. Transport verdicts: for Baro, a gorge-cut river, Lake Tana and Lake Kariba, say boat or no boat — with the book's reason for each.
