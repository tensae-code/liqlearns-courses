# Water as a coolant

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — water used as a coolant — the liquid that carries heat away — in engines and in the body. Then read on: this is Sri Lanka's way in — its Grade 7 Science Part 1 places water as a coolant inside its water chapter, between water as a solvent and water as a medium of life.

## Sub lesson 1 — The coolant's job — carrying heat away

A **coolant** is a liquid that takes heat away from something hot and carries it off, so the hot thing stays at a safe temperature. Water does this job well: it can take in a great deal of heat as it passes the hot part, then carry that heat away as it flows on.

## Sub lesson 2 — Water as a coolant in engines

In an **engine**, moving parts and burning fuel make heat faster than the engine can lose it. Water circulated through the engine — the radiator system — picks the heat up and carries it away, which is why an engine whose coolant runs out overheats and is damaged.

## Sub lesson 3 — Water as a coolant in the body

The **body** uses water the same way: blood — mostly water — carries heat from the working inside of the body to the skin, and sweat, which is water, takes heat away as it evaporates. Sri Lanka's book names engines and the body side by side: one coolant, two homes.
