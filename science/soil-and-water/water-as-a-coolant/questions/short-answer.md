# Water as a coolant — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: The coolant's job — carrying heat away.
**Answer:** A **coolant** is a liquid that takes heat away from something hot and carries it off, so the hot thing stays at a safe temperature. Water does this job well: it can take in a great deal of heat as it passes the hot part, then carry that heat away as it flows on.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Water as a coolant in engines.
**Answer:** In an **engine**, moving parts and burning fuel make heat faster than the engine can lose it. Water circulated through the engine — the radiator system — picks the heat up and carries it away, which is why an engine whose coolant runs out overheats and is damaged.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Water as a coolant in the body.
**Answer:** The **body** uses water the same way: blood — mostly water — carries heat from the working inside of the body to the skin, and sweat, which is water, takes heat away as it evaporates. Sri Lanka's book names engines and the body side by side: one coolant, two homes.

**Answer:** The coolant's job — carrying heat away → Water as a coolant in engines → Water as a coolant in the body — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
