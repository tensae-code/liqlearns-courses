# Loam, clay and sand — the squeeze test

## Do it first
Take a handful of damp soil. Squeeze it — hard. Open your hand. What have you got? A smooth ball? A rough lump that is already crumbling? Or nothing at all, just grains slipping away? Your hand has just done the oldest soil test in the book — and its answer names the soil.

## Three soils, three answers in the hand
The book's three main soils, and the **squeeze test** that tells them:

- **Loam (ለም አፈር)** — the **fertile** soil, a **mix of sand, clay and humus**. Squeezed, it **holds together**; released, it **half crumbles, leaving a rounded lump**.
- **Clay soil (ሸክላ)** — **very fine and smooth**. Squeezed, it forms **a ball** and keeps it.
- **Sandy soil (አሸዋማ)** — **big, rough grains**. It **will not hold together** at all.

The book's way of learning them is a **field trip**: collect local soils and compare them, handful by handful. The hand is the instrument; the three results are the reading.

Tell your companion the three soils, their Amharic names, and exactly what each does in the squeezed hand.

## Do It Yourself
1. Field-trip collection, the book's own: gather soils from three places, squeeze-test each damp, and label them loam, clay or sand by the result.
2. Fertile finding: which of your collected soils is loam — the mix with humus? That is the farmer's favourite; say why in one line.
3. Blind test: a companion hands you damp soil with your eyes closed. Squeeze, feel the grains, name the soil — then open your eyes and check.
