# Lesson plan — Loam, clay and sand — the squeeze test

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Soil and Water · **Lesson:** Loam, clay and sand — the squeeze test
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water — the main soil types, known by the hand (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the three main soils are told apart by the squeeze test: fertile loam (ለም አፈር) holds then half-crumbles into a rounded lump; clay (ሸክላ) squeezes smooth into a ball; sandy soil (አሸዋማ) will not hold together at all
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What soil is and what is in it — soil is now sorted into its main kinds, the book's field way.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three main soils: loam (ለም አፈር), clay (ሸክላ), sandy soil (አሸዋማ).
2. Say what loam is: a fertile mix of sand, clay and humus.
3. Do the squeeze test and read its three results: loam — holds, then half-crumbles to a rounded lump; clay — a smooth ball; sand — falls apart.

## What you need
Three handfuls of different soils, slightly damp — gathered on the book's field trip to collect local soils.

## If the learner is stuck
- Tests dry soil and everything crumbles → the test needs the soil slightly damp; dry clay will lie to you.
- Swaps loam and clay → both hold, but differently: clay keeps a smooth ball; loam half-crumbles into a rounded lump and is the fertile mix with humus.
