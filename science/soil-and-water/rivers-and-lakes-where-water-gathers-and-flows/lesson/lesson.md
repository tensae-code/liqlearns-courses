# Rivers and lakes — where water gathers and flows

## Do it first
Where does the water near your home go? Some water runs — in a river. Some water gathers and stands — in a lake or a pond. Say which kind is nearer to you. Then read on: this is Serbia's way in — the surface waters of my region.

## Sub lesson 1 — The parts of a river — source, course, bed, mouth and banks

A river is learned part by part: its **source**, where it begins; its **course**, the way it runs; its **bed**, the hollow it runs in; its **mouth**, where it ends by joining bigger water; and its **banks**, the land along its sides. Source to mouth — a river is one journey with named stops.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lowland and mountain rivers

Rivers are sorted by the land they cross: **lowland rivers** run through flat low ground, and **mountain rivers** run through the mountains. The land the river crosses names the river's kind.

## Sub lesson 3 — Lakes and ponds — water that gathers

Not all surface water runs: **lakes** and **ponds** are waters that gather and stand in one place. Running water and standing water together are a region's surface waters.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is this water running, or gathered and standing?" Answer: Running water is a river — name it part by part, source to mouth. Gathered, standing water is a lake or a pond.
