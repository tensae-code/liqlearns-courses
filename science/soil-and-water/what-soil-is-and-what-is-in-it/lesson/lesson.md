# What soil is and what is in it

## Do it first
Scratch the ground at your feet. The dark, crumbly stuff at the top — how deep does it go? A finger's depth? A hand's? Dig a little, or look where a path cuts through a bank, and find where the living ground ends. The book has an exact answer, measured by roots.

## The root-deep layer, and what it holds
**Soil** is **the layer of the ground from the surface down to where plant roots reach**. Where roots can go, there is soil; below that, the ground is no longer soil in the book's sense.

And soil is not one thing — it is a holding-together of many:

- **minerals** from the rock below,
- **tiny living things**, too small to see one by one,
- **plant and animal remains**, rotted into the layer,
- **air**, in the spaces between crumbs,
- **water**, held in the layer for the roots.

Soil's **colour differs by place — black, red, grey, brown, white** — and colour is one of its **identifying marks**: you can know a soil, in part, by the colour it wears.

## Do It Yourself
1. Root-depth check: find a pulled-up plant or a bank and see how deep the roots reached. That depth was soil, by the book's measure.
2. Colour survey: collect tiny soil samples from three places (in bottle caps). Line them up — how many colours has your neighbourhood got?
3. Crumbs close-up: rub soil between your fingers over white paper. Look for the remains — tiny leaf bits and dark rotted pieces among the grains.
