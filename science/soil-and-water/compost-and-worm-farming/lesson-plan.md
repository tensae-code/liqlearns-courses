# Lesson plan — Compost and worm farming

**Course:** Science · **Title:** soil-and-water · **Lesson:** Compost and worm farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Soil and Water — appended to Unit 7 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** feeding the soil with organic fertiliser — building a compost pile layer by layer, and farming worms (vermiculture) whose humus improves the soil
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Compost — organic fertiliser built layer by layer** — Nicaragua's Grade 9 book teaches organic fertiliser (abono orgánico) as the way to feed the soil in ecological agriculture: build a **compost pile layer by layer**, with an **air column** through it so the material can breathe and rot down into fertiliser instead of waste.
2. **Worm farming — vermiculture and worm humus** — The same book sets out **vermiculture (lombricultura)**: worm beds of **1 × 1.5 m and 60 cm high**. The Grade 9 Módulo Autoformativo adds the module's own way of producing **worm humus**: beds on **dry, well-drained ground near organic waste** — the humus is a blackish acidic substance that **improves soil structure and supplies nutrients**.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks compost is just a rubbish heap → return to sub lesson 1: the pile is built layer by layer with an air column, on purpose, to make fertiliser.
- Mixes compost and worm humus up → return to sub lesson 2: compost rots down in a pile; worm humus is what worms make in their beds — both feed the soil.
