# Water: why every living thing needs it

## Do it first
Since you woke up today, how many times has water worked for you? You drank. You washed. Food was cooked. Count honestly — most of us stop counting at ten. The book draws water's work as a mind-map, and this lesson walks every branch of it.

## The mind-map of water's work
The book teaches water's uses as a **mind-map** — one resource, many branches:

**For the body:** **drinking**; **cooking and cleanliness**; **digesting food**; and **carrying waste out of the body in sweat and urine**.

**Beyond the body:** **growing plants** — no water, no crops; **making electricity** — the **Hidase (Grand Ethiopian Renaissance) dam** turns the great river's water into Ethiopia's power; **transport** — boats carry people and goods on water; and **fish breeding** — water is the farm where fish are raised.

Follow one jerrycan through your day and you will meet every branch.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the mind-map without looking: body uses first, then the uses beyond the body, ending with the dam's name.

## Do It Yourself
1. Jerrycan diary: for one day, mark every use of water at home with a tally on paper. Group your tallies under the book's branches.
2. Dam talk: ask at home what people know about the Hidase dam — where it is, what it makes. Bring one sentence back.
3. No-water hour (imagined): list what would stop first, second, third if the water failed for a day. Order teaches value.
