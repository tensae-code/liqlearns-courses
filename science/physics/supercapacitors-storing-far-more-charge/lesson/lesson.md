# Supercapacitors — storing far more charge

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why they are super — area up, separation down

The parallel-plate rule, C = εA/d, names the two levers. A supercapacitor pulls both hard: its electrodes are made of porous carbon with an internal surface area like a football field folded into a thimble, and the charge separation is the thickness of a layer of ions — nanometres, not the micrometres of a plastic film. Area up by thousands and distance down by thousands multiply into farads where ordinary capacitors have microfarads.

## Sub lesson 2 — What they store and how they behave — between capacitor and battery

Stored energy is still ½CV², so a supercapacitor bank holds real, usable energy — far more than any ordinary capacitor, though still less per kilogram than a battery. It charges and discharges in seconds, survives hundreds of thousands of cycles, and delivers large currents — but its voltage falls steadily as it discharges, unlike a battery's steady plateau, and it self-discharges faster.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where they work — the applications Jordan names

The supercapacitor's place is the burst and the bridge: capturing a braking vehicle's energy in seconds, smoothing power, backing up memory and starting systems, paired with batteries so each does what it is good at — the battery the marathon, the supercapacitor the sprint. Jordan closes its capacitance lesson with them because they are the lesson's own formula, engineered to its limit.

## The key ideas

- A supercapacitor stores charge on facing surfaces, like any capacitor — but in farads, not microfarads
- Its electrodes are porous carbon: enormous area in a small volume
- Its charge separation is a layer of ions — nanometres thick
- Energy is still ½CV²; charging takes seconds and cycles number in the hundreds of thousands
- Its work is bursts and bridges beside a battery: the sprint beside the marathon
