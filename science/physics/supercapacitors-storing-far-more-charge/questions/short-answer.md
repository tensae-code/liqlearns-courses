# Supercapacitors — storing far more charge — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Use C = εA/d to explain the supercapacitor's design in two clauses.
**Answer: Its porous carbon electrodes maximise A — an internal surface thousands of times the device's size — while the ion layer minimises d to nanometres; C grows with A and shrinks with d, so both levers multiply the capacitance.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Compute the energy in a 100 F supercapacitor charged to 2.5 V, and compare it with a 1,000 µF capacitor at the same voltage.
**Answer: Supercapacitor: ½ × 100 × 2.5² ≈ 313 J. Ordinary: ½ × 0.001 × 6.25 ≈ 0.003 J — about a hundred thousand times less; the comparison is the lesson's point.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why pair a supercapacitor with a battery instead of using either alone?
**Answer: They excel oppositely: the battery stores much energy and gives it steadily (the marathon), the supercapacitor takes and gives power fast for many cycles (the sprint) — pairing lets each cover the other's weakness.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two working places of supercapacitors.
**Answer: Capturing braking energy in vehicles; power smoothing and backup/starting support — any two burst-or-bridge jobs where seconds-scale charge and discharge and long cycle life matter.**
