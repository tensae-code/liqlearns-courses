## Sub lessons
1. **Why they are super — area up, separation down** — The parallel-plate rule, C = εA/d, names the two levers. A supercapacitor pulls both hard: its electrodes are made of porous carbon with an internal surface area like a football field folded into a thimble, and the charge separation is the thickness of a layer of ions — nanometres, not the micrometres of a plastic film. Area up by thousands and distance down by thousands multiply into farads where ordinary capacitors have microfarads.
2. **What they store and how they behave — between capacitor and battery** — Stored energy is still ½CV², so a supercapacitor bank holds real, usable energy — far more than any ordinary capacitor, though still less per kilogram than a battery. It charges and discharges in seconds, survives hundreds of thousands of cycles, and delivers large currents — but its voltage falls steadily as it discharges, unlike a battery's steady plateau, and it self-discharges faster.
3. **Where they work — the applications Jordan names** — The supercapacitor's place is the burst and the bridge: capturing a braking vehicle's energy in seconds, smoothing power, backing up memory and starting systems, paired with batteries so each does what it is good at — the battery the marathon, the supercapacitor the sprint. Jordan closes its capacitance lesson with them because they are the lesson's own formula, engineered to its limit.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A supercapacitor stores charge on facing surfaces, like any capacitor — but in farads, not microfarads
2. Its electrodes are porous carbon: enormous area in a small volume
3. Its charge separation is a layer of ions — nanometres thick
4. Energy is still ½CV²; charging takes seconds and cycles number in the hundreds of thousands
5. Its work is bursts and bridges beside a battery: the sprint beside the marathon
