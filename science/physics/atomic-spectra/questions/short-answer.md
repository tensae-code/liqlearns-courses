# Atomic spectra — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a hot solid not give a line spectrum?
**Answer: In a dense solid the atoms' levels are smeared by their constant interactions into effectively continuous bands, so photons of every energy are emitted — a continuous spectrum; isolated atoms in a thin gas keep sharp, separate levels and so give sharp lines.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) An electron absorbs a photon and rises two levels. What must be true of that photon?
**Answer: Its energy h f must equal exactly the difference between the two levels involved — no other photon energy can make that jump, which is why absorption lines sit at the same wavelengths as emission lines.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How can astronomers know what a star is made of without a sample?
**Answer: They spread the starlight into a spectrum and match its absorption (or emission) lines against the known fingerprints of elements measured in laboratories; matching line positions name the elements present.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) State the photon rule for a level jump, in symbols and words.
**Answer: h f = E_upper − E_lower: the photon's energy equals the difference between the electron's starting and finishing energy levels — emitted on a fall, absorbed on a rise.**
