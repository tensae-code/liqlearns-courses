# Lesson plan — Amount of substance — counting molecules with the mole

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Amount of substance — counting molecules with the mole
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the molecular-kinetic strand — Armenia's Grade 11 §2, sizes and masses of molecules and the amount of substance, before the gas laws
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** molecules are too small to count and too many to imagine, so physics counts them the way shops count eggs — in a standard parcel: one mole is 6.02 × 10²³ particles, and the amount of substance ν = m/M turns any weighing into a molecule count

## Sub lessons
1. **Why molecules must be counted by weighing** — §2 of the Grade 11 book starts with sizes and masses: a molecule is some 10⁻¹⁰ m across and some 10⁻²⁶ kg heavy, and even a drop of water holds more of them than there are drops in an ocean. No counting by ones is possible — but a balance is honest. If the mass of one molecule (or of a standard crowd of them) is known, weighing the sample counts it. All of amount-of-substance machinery is that one swap: mass for number.
2. **The mole and Avogadro's number** — The standard crowd is the mole: 1 mol of any substance contains the same number of particles, Avogadro's number N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ — chosen so that one mole of a substance weighs its relative molecular mass in grams (12 g of carbon, 18 g of water). Amount of substance ν is measured in moles, and the headcount follows at once: N = ν × N_A. One unit, every substance: a mole of hydrogen and a mole of lead are the same count of particles at wildly different masses.
3. **Molar mass — the working bridge** — The bridge between the balance and the count is the molar mass M, the mass of one mole, read off the periodic table's relative masses with 'grams per mole' written after them. Then ν = m/M: weigh the sample, divide by its molar mass, and you hold its amount of substance — and × N_A its number of molecules. This is the arithmetic the whole molecular-kinetic chapter will spend: the gas laws' n, the kinetic theory's densities, the electrolysis lab's charge per mole all begin as ν = m/M.
