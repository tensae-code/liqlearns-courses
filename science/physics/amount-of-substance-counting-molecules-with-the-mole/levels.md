# Amount of substance — counting molecules with the mole — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — §2 of the Grade 11 book starts with sizes and masses: a molecule is some 10⁻¹⁰ m across and some 10⁻²⁶ kg heavy, and even a drop of water holds more of them than there are drops in an ocean; The standard crowd is the mole: 1 mol of any substance contains the same number of particles, Avogadro's number N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ — chosen so that one mole of a substance weighs its relative molecular mass in grams (12 g of carbon, 18 g of water); The bridge between the balance and the count is the molar mass M, the mass of one mole, read off the periodic table's relative masses with 'grams per mole' written after them — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
