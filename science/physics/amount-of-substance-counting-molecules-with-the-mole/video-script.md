# Video script — Amount of substance — counting molecules with the mole

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **molecules are too small to count and too many to imagine, so physics counts them the way shops count eggs — in a standard parcel: one mole is 6.02 × 10²³ particles, and the amount of substance ν = m/M turns any weighing into a molecule count**. §2 of the Grade 11 book starts with sizes and masses: a molecule is some 10⁻¹⁰ m across and some 10⁻²⁶ kg heavy, and even a drop of water holds more of them than there are drops in an ocean. Then the lesson builds its three parts in order — why molecules must be counted by weighing; the mole and avogadro's number; molar mass — the working bridge — each one first met in the hand, then said in words.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
