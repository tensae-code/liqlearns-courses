# Amount of substance — counting molecules with the mole — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) How many molecules are in 2 mol of a gas?
**Answer:** N = νN_A = 2 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What does it mean that N_A is 'the same for every substance'?
**Answer:** A mole is defined as a count, not a mass: 6.02 × 10²³ water molecules and 6.02 × 10²³ carbon atoms are both one mole — their masses differ (18 g against 12 g) because the particles' masses differ.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Find the mass of 0.5 mol of oxygen gas (M = 32 g/mol).
**Answer:** m = νM = 0.5 × 32 = 16 g.

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the mole the natural unit for the gas laws that follow in the book?
**Answer:** The gas laws speak about the particles' behaviour — pressure and temperature come from the crowd's motion — so their equations are written per count of particles; the mole is that count made weighable.
