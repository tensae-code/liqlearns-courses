## Sub lesson 1 — A shunt makes an ammeter

A moving-coil galvanometer detects tiny currents.

A low-resistance shunt in parallel converts it into an ammeter.

[Picture 22061 — galvanometer with its shunt resistor drawn]

## Sub lesson 2 — A series resistor makes a voltmeter

And a high resistance in series converts it into a voltmeter — both conversions are worked numerically (e.g. a 5 mA movement into a 10 A ammeter.

A 40 Ω, 3 mA movement into a 0–10 V voltmeter).
