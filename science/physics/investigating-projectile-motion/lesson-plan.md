# Lesson plan — Investigating projectile motion

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Investigating projectile motion
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), in the projectile strand of Compulsory Book 2 — the experiment between combining motion and the projectile laws
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before the projectile laws are stated, the flat-throw is **investigated**: a ball launched horizontally is photographed (or timed through gates) mark by mark, and the records show the two signatures the laws will be built from — equal horizontal steps in equal times, and vertical falls of 1 : 4 : 9 — one motion uniform, the other accelerated, running independently
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Setting the throw — a clean horizontal launch** — The investigation needs a projectile whose story starts purely horizontal. A small ball rolls down a curved ramp and leaves a level lip: at the lip its velocity is horizontal, its speed read from the ramp's height or a photogate at the exit. Beyond the lip nothing pushes it but gravity. To capture the flight, the school arrangements are two: a **flash photograph** at equal time intervals, printing the ball's positions as a row of images on one frame, or a **grid backdrop** with a camera recording frames at a known rate. A second ball, dropped from the lip's height at the launch instant, flies the control flight — straight down — for comparison.
2. **Reading the record — the two signatures** — Measure the photograph. Horizontally, the ball's positions step forward by **equal distances in equal times** — the horizontal motion is uniform, untouched by gravity. Vertically, the falls between successive marks grow as **1 : 3 : 5** (the distances fallen from rest in each equal beat), giving total falls of 1 : 4 : 9 — the fingerprint of uniformly accelerated motion from rest, with the same g as free fall. And the dropped control ball stands at the projectile's height in every frame: the two land together. Three facts, all read off the record with a ruler — no formula has been used yet, which is the point.
3. **What the investigation entitles the book to say** — The next section's laws are this record written in symbols: horizontal motion x = v₀t at constant v₀; vertical motion y = ½gt² under gravity alone; the two independent, sharing only the clock — exactly the independence principle the previous lesson stated, now measured in a real flight. The investigation also teaches its own cautions: launch speed must be steady between trials (same ramp, same release point), air resistance must stay negligible (dense ball, modest speeds), and the photograph's scale must be calibrated against the grid. An experiment whose result the next law merely transcribes — that is China's order of teaching, again: measure first, legislate after.
