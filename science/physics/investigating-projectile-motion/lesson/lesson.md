# Investigating projectile motion

## Do it first
Before you read: flick a coin off a table's edge and, at the same instant, drop an identical coin from the same height beside it. Listen. One click or two? Your ears have just run the control experiment of this investigation — now do it with instruments, and read the whole flight, mark by mark.

## Sub lesson 1 — Setting the throw — a clean horizontal launch

The investigation needs a projectile whose story starts purely horizontal. A small ball rolls down a curved ramp and leaves a level lip: at the lip its velocity is horizontal, its speed read from the ramp's height or a photogate at the exit. Beyond the lip nothing pushes it but gravity. To capture the flight, the school arrangements are two: a **flash photograph** at equal time intervals, printing the ball's positions as a row of images on one frame, or a **grid backdrop** with a camera recording frames at a known rate. A second ball, dropped from the lip's height at the launch instant, flies the control flight — straight down — for comparison.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the record — the two signatures

Measure the photograph. Horizontally, the ball's positions step forward by **equal distances in equal times** — the horizontal motion is uniform, untouched by gravity. Vertically, the falls between successive marks grow as **1 : 3 : 5** (the distances fallen from rest in each equal beat), giving total falls of 1 : 4 : 9 — the fingerprint of uniformly accelerated motion from rest, with the same g as free fall. And the dropped control ball stands at the projectile's height in every frame: the two land together. Three facts, all read off the record with a ruler — no formula has been used yet, which is the point.

## Sub lesson 3 — What the investigation entitles the book to say

The next section's laws are this record written in symbols: horizontal motion x = v₀t at constant v₀; vertical motion y = ½gt² under gravity alone; the two independent, sharing only the clock — exactly the independence principle the previous lesson stated, now measured in a real flight. The investigation also teaches its own cautions: launch speed must be steady between trials (same ramp, same release point), air resistance must stay negligible (dense ball, modest speeds), and the photograph's scale must be calibrated against the grid. An experiment whose result the next law merely transcribes — that is China's order of teaching, again: measure first, legislate after.

## The key ideas

- A clean flat-throw starts with a purely horizontal velocity — a ramp and level lip provide it
- Equal times, equal horizontal steps: the horizontal motion is uniform
- Vertical falls grow 1 : 3 : 5 per beat (totals 1 : 4 : 9): uniformly accelerated motion at g
- The dropped control ball keeps the projectile's height in every frame — they land together; the laws of the next section are this record in symbols

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: before the projectile laws are stated, the flat-throw is **investigated**: a ball launched horizontally is photographed (or timed through gates) mark by mark, and the records show the two signatures the laws will be built from — equal horizontal steps in equal times, and vertical falls of 1 : 4 : 9 — one motion uniform, the other accelerated, running independently. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. On a flash photograph with beats of 0.1 s, a projectile's horizontal steps are 12 cm each and its total vertical falls after 1, 2, 3 beats are 5, 20 and 45 cm. Verify both signatures and find the launch speed.
2. The same photograph shows the dropped control ball level with the projectile in every frame. What principle does that single fact prove?
3. A student repeats the launch but releases the ball from halfway down the ramp. Why must those photographs be thrown out of the comparison?
