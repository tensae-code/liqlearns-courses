# Project work in physics — the Armenian project method — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Sort a project from a practical: both use apparatus — what differs?
**Answer:** A practical follows a given route to a known result to learn a technique or confirm a law; a project starts from a question whose answer the pupil does not yet hold, with choices about route and method in the pupil's hands, and ends in a presented, defensible result.

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) A pupil proposes 'a project on electricity'. Use the guides' first function to fix it.
**Answer:** Frame it smaller until it is investigable: e.g. 'which arrangement of two batteries and bulbs gives the longest useful light from one set of batteries?' — a question with something measurable inside it.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the presentation be to an audience that can question it?
**Answer:** Questioning is the audit: claims must survive other people's 'how do you know?' — that pressure is what turns a display into evidence and the presenter into someone who owns the reasoning.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the two things the guides have the teacher assess in project work.
**Answer:** The product (what was found or made, and its presentation) and the process (planning, evidence-keeping, revision, persistence) — process marks stop a lucky result or a polished copy from outscoring real investigation.
