# Project work in physics — the Armenian project method

## Do it first
Run the method in miniature: in one session, take the question 'which paper makes the best parachute?' — write the plan (what varies, what is measured, what is kept the same), do three timed drops, and present a two-sentence answer with its evidence. You have walked every guide-step — choice, plan, evidence, presentation — at classroom scale; a term project is this, longer. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — What project work is — the essence

The guides define the project method by its essence: learning organised around a task the learners carry from question to presented result, over days or weeks, with real choices in their hands. It sits in every grade's guide because the method is grade-independent: a Grade 8 project on insulating a box and a Grade 12 project on a home-made instrument are the same species — an owned question, a plan, evidence, a public answer.

## Sub lesson 2 — The steps — from topic choice to presentation

The guides walk the steps in order: choosing a topic that is genuinely investigable; planning (what will be measured, built or compared, and how); carrying out the work, with the plan revised when the evidence demands; and presenting the result to an audience that can question it. Sample project topics are printed in the guides so the first choice is never a blank page, and the steps are taught, not assumed — a first project is scaffolded, later ones are freer.

## Sub lesson 3 — The teacher's functions — guide, not ghost

The method does not retire the teacher; it re-deploys them. The guides name the teacher's functions inside project work: helping frame a question small enough to answer, checking plans for safety and feasibility, supplying resources and deadlines, coaching through stalls, and assessing both the product and the process. Pupil ownership with teacher structure — that balance, stated in the chapter, is what separates the Armenian project method from both a lecture and a free-for-all.

Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: each Armenian guide carries a chapter on educational project work: pupils take a real question, plan their own route to its answer in steps, do the work over time, and present what they found — with the teacher's functions named at every stage — because some physics is only learned by carrying an investigation of your own (teacher-guide evidence only). If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.
