# The six simple machines — and compound machines

## Do it first
Take a bicycle in your mind and strip it down: wheels, pedals, brakes, chain. Name the simple machine in each part.

[Picture 21839 — six types in a two-family tree; bicycle with component machines labelled]

## The six simple machines — and compound machines — the teaching
The six simple machines fall into two families. The inclined-plane family: the inclined plane itself, the wedge, and the screw — each is a slope, still or moving or wrapped. The lever family: the lever, the wheel and axle, and the pulley — each turns about a point or axle. A compound machine combines simple machines into one tool: a bicycle joins wheel and axle, levers and a screw or two; wire cutters join levers to wedges; a car is a compound machine of many parts.

## The key ideas

- Inclined-plane family: inclined plane, wedge, screw.
- Lever family: lever, wheel and axle, pulley.
- A compound machine combines simple machines — bicycle, wire cutters, car.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the six simple machines — and compound machines to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
