# Lesson plan — Biological effects of electromagnetic radiation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Biological effects of electromagnetic radiation
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the United States placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** electromagnetic radiation's effect on living tissue is decided photon by photon — **E = hf** splits the spectrum into **non-ionising** radiation (radio to low ultraviolet: warming, no bond-breaking) and **ionising** radiation (high ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays: electrons knocked off, DNA breakable), whose harm is counted in **dose** as deterministic effects above thresholds and stochastic cancer risk rising with dose — and protection is **time, distance (inverse square) and shielding**, doses kept as low as reasonably achievable, medical exposure weighed against its benefit
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **The spectrum split — photon energy decides** — Each photon carries E = hf; below high ultraviolet the energy cannot ionise atoms, so radio, microwaves, infrared, visible and low UV are non-ionising (they warm, they do not break bonds); high UV, X-rays and gamma rays are ionising.
2. **How ionising radiation harms living tissue** — Ionisation breaks molecules, DNA included: heavy doses kill cells (deterministic effects above a threshold — burns, sickness), while surviving mutated cells carry the stochastic risk of later cancer, probability rising with dose.
3. **Protection — time, distance, shielding, and honest weighing** — Halve the time, halve the dose; distance cuts intensity by the inverse square; shielding absorbs. Doses are kept as low as reasonably achievable, medical exposure is weighed against benefit, and non-ionising waves are not feared on ionisation grounds — their photons cannot ionise.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Place any named radiation on the spectrum and state, from E = hf, whether it is ionising.
2. Explain deterministic and stochastic effects and the role of dose in each.
3. Apply time, distance and shielding to a stated exposure, including an inverse-square calculation.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
