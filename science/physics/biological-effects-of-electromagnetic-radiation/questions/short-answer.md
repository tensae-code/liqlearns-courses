# Biological effects of electromagnetic radiation — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A very bright visible lamp is still non-ionising, while a faint gamma source is ionising. Explain in two sentences.
**Answer: Ionising power is a per-photon property set by frequency (E = hf), not by how many photons there are. Brightness adds photons; it does not raise any single visible photon to ionising energy, while every gamma photon already carries more than enough.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why are children and dividing tissues more sensitive to ionising radiation?
**Answer: The serious damage is to DNA, and dividing cells are copying their DNA often — more copying means more chances for an unrepaired break to become a lasting mutation, and children have more dividing tissue and more years for a stochastic effect to appear.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the three protection levers and one everyday ultraviolet example of each.
**Answer: Time — shorter exposure (less time in midday sun); distance — farther from the source (the inverse square law; for the sun, fixed, so shade-seeking replaces it); shielding — matter in the way (clothing, shade, sunscreen against UV; lead and concrete against X- and gamma rays).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is a needed CT scan not refused on radiation grounds alone?
**Answer: Medical exposure is weighed by benefit: the scan's small, managed dose is set against the illness it can find and guide treatment for — protection means doses as low as reasonably achievable for the image needed, not zero imaging at any cost.**
