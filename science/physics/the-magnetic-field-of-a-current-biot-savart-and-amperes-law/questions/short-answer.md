# The magnetic field of a current — Biot–Savart and Ampère's laws — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A current element points north; the point of interest lies due east of the element, in the horizontal plane. Which way does dB point at that point?
**Answer: Vertically — perpendicular to both the element (north) and the line to the point (east): the cross product dl × r̂ points up or down; by the right-hand rule, with dl north and r̂ east, dB points downward (dl × r̂ = north × east = down in a right-handed frame) — the magnitude follows dB = (μ₀/4π) I dl sinθ/r² with θ = 90°.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Doubling a straight wire's current and doubling the distance: what happens to B?
**Answer: B = μ₀I/(2πr): the doubled current doubles B and the doubled distance halves it — the two changes cancel and B is unchanged.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does Ampère's law not directly give the field of a short finite wire segment?
**Answer: The law is always true, but it is only useful when symmetry fixes B along a chosen loop. A finite segment has no such symmetry — B varies around every loop in a complicated way — so the field must be built element by element with Biot–Savart (and the segment's field is part of its whole circuit's).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Two parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. Without calculation: does the field of wire 1 at wire 2, with the force it causes, pull the wires together or push them apart — and which law priced the field?
**Answer: The field at wire 2, from B = μ₀I/(2πr) (either law's result for a long wire), points so that the force on wire 2's current is toward wire 1: parallel currents attract. (The companion lesson `two-wires-pull-or-push-each-other` works the force itself.)**
