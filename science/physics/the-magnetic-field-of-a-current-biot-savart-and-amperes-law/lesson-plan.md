# Lesson plan — The magnetic field of a current — Biot–Savart and Ampère's laws

## Sub lessons
1. **The Biot–Savart law — the field element by element** — A current makes a magnetic field everywhere around it, and the Greek book prices it piece by piece. A short element of wire of length dl carrying current I contributes, at a point a distance r away, a field dB = (μ₀/4π) · I dl sinθ / r², where θ is the angle between the element and the line to the point, and μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A is the permeability of free space. The direction is given by the cross product's right-hand rule: dB is perpendicular to both the element and the line joining it to the point. The law has the same inverse-square shape as Coulomb's — it is Coulomb's law for magnetism's true source, the moving charge — and the field of any whole circuit is the sum (integral) of its elements' contributions.
2. **Summing the elements — wire, loop and solenoid** — Applied to a long straight wire, the elements' contributions gather into circles around the wire: B = μ₀I / (2πr), falling as 1/r, directed by the grip rule — thumb along the current, fingers curling with the field. At the centre of a circular loop of radius R the elements all point the same way and sum to B = μ₀I / (2R); N turns multiply it by N. Inside a long solenoid the contributions add to a nearly uniform B = μ₀ n I (n = turns per metre), almost zero outside. These three results — wire, loop, solenoid — are the Greek chapter's working fields, each one the Biot–Savart sum over a simple geometry.
3. **Ampère's law — the field taken whole** — Ampère's law reaches the same fields by a global statement: travel round any closed loop and add up the field's component along your path; the total (the circulation) equals μ₀ times the total current passing through the loop: ∮B·dl = μ₀ I_through. For the straight wire, symmetry says B is constant on a circle of radius r around it, so B × 2πr = μ₀I and B = μ₀I/(2πr) falls out in one line — no element-by-element sum needed. The law's power is exactly this: where symmetry makes B constant (or zero) along a chosen loop, Ampère gives the field immediately; where there is no symmetry, Biot–Savart remains the honest route. The two laws are one physics, stated element-wise and whole-wise.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the Biot–Savart law with the meaning of each symbol, and give dB's direction by the right-hand rule
2. Sum the elements for a long straight wire to B = μ₀I/(2πr), and quote the loop-centre and solenoid results
3. State Ampère's law, ∮B·dl = μ₀ I_through, and use symmetry to recover the wire's field in one line
4. Choose the right law for a geometry: Ampère where symmetry fixes B on a loop, Biot–Savart otherwise
5. Keep the direction discipline: grip rule for the wire, cross-product sense for the element
