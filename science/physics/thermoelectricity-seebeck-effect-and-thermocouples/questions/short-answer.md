# Thermoelectricity — Seebeck effect and thermocouples — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the two metals of a thermoelectric circuit be different?
**Answer: With a single metal (or an identical pair) the junction effects cancel around the circuit and no net EMF appears; the Seebeck EMF arises from the unlike pair — its size set by how far apart the metals stand in the thermoelectric series.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give two advantages of a thermocouple over a liquid-in-glass thermometer for furnace work.
**Answer: Range and toughness: it survives temperatures that would burst a glass thermometer or boil its liquid. Speed and size: its tiny junction responds quickly and can measure the temperature at a small point or in a narrow space. (Its electrical signal can also be read at a distance.)**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A thermocouple reads the EMF for a junction in a furnace against a reference in ice. The operator moves the reference to a warm bench and the reading changes although the furnace is steady. Explain.
**Answer: The EMF measures the difference between the junctions. Warming the reference shrinks the difference, so the EMF falls and the instrument reports a lower 'furnace' temperature — the reference must be held at its known temperature (or compensated) for the calibration to hold.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Roughly how big is a thermocouple's EMF per degree, and what does measuring it therefore demand?
**Answer: Tens of microvolts per degree for common pairs — so it demands a sensitive millivoltmeter or potentiometer drawing negligible current, and usually a calibration table for the pair.**
