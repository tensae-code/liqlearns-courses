# Thermoelectricity — Seebeck effect and thermocouples — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) The Seebeck effect produces an EMF when…
a) A magnet moves near a coil  b) Two junctions of unlike metals are at different temperatures  c) A current heats a wire  d) Light falls on a metal
**Answer: b) Two junctions of unlike metals are at different temperatures**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) If the hot and cold junctions of a thermoelectric circuit are swapped, the EMF…
a) Doubles  b) Vanishes permanently  c) Reverses direction  d) Becomes alternating
**Answer: c) Reverses direction**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) In a thermocouple thermometer the reference junction is traditionally kept…
a) In the furnace  b) At a known temperature — melting ice, 0 °C  c) At room temperature, unmeasured  d) Disconnected
**Answer: b) At a known temperature — melting ice, 0 °C**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A thermocouple junction can measure a point temperature well because it can be made…
a) Very large  b) Tiny — a small welded bead, responding quickly  c) Of glass  d) Magnetic
**Answer: b) Tiny — a small welded bead, responding quickly**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) The Peltier effect is…
a) The Seebeck effect run backwards — a driven current heats or cools a junction  b) Heating by friction  c) A magnetic effect  d) The resistance rise of a hot wire
**Answer: a) The Seebeck effect run backwards — a driven current heats or cools a junction**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) A thermopile increases the measured EMF by…
a) Using one large junction  b) Joining many thermocouple junctions in series so their EMFs add  c) Cooling the junctions  d) Using thicker wire
**Answer: b) Joining many thermocouple junctions in series so their EMFs add**
