# Equilibrium of a solid under two forces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the three parts of the two-force equilibrium condition.
**Answer: The two forces must have the same line of action, opposite senses, and equal intensities — together, F₁ + F₂ = 0.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How do the Mauritanian manuals establish the condition experimentally?
**Answer: A light card is pulled by two dynamometers; it rests only when the two readings are equal and the pulls lie on one straight line in opposite senses — varying any one of the three makes it move or turn.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A spring (K = 200 N/m) is stretched 0.05 m by a hanging body at rest. What is the body's weight?
**Answer: T = K·Δl = 200 × 0.05 = 10 N; at rest tension equals weight, so the body weighs 10 N.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is floating taught inside this lesson in the 4AS manual?
**Answer: Because a floating body is held by exactly two forces — weight down and Archimedes' thrust up — and rests precisely when they are equal, opposite and on one line: F_A = P.**
