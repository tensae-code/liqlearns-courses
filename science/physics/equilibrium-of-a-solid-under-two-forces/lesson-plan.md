# Lesson plan — Equilibrium of a solid under two forces

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Equilibrium of a solid under two forces
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 — Physics expansion) — a new lesson built for the Mauritania placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a solid held by exactly two forces rests only when the two forces cancel completely: same line of action, opposite senses, equal intensities — F₁ + F₂ = 0 — a condition Mauritania's manuals establish experimentally with two dynamometers and then spend on bodies on tables, on threads, on springs and afloat

## Sub lessons
1. **The condition — one line, opposite senses, equal sizes** — Take a light card pulled by two dynamometers. It stays put only when the two pulls share one line of action, point in opposite senses and read the same intensity: then F₁ + F₂ = 0 as vectors. Break any one of the three — shift a line, turn a sense, unbalance the sizes — and the card moves or turns. The condition is found on the bench, not asserted: the two readings and the ruler-straight line are the evidence.
2. **Bodies resting and hanging — reaction and tension** — Most two-force equilibria hide one of the forces. A body on a table is pulled down by its weight and held up by the table's reaction: two forces, one line (the vertical), opposite, equal. A body hanging from a thread pairs its weight with the thread's tension. In each case the equilibrium condition works backwards: measure or know one force and the other is fully determined — same size, same line, opposite sense.
3. **Springs and floating — T = K·Δl and the pirogue** — Hang the body from a spring instead: the spring's tension grows with its stretch, T = K·Δl, where K is the stiffness constant, until tension balances weight. Afloat, a Mauritanian pirogue on the river rests in the same two-force balance: its weight downward, Archimedes' thrust upward, equal and opposite — floating taught as this lesson's equilibrium, F_A = P.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Two-force equilibrium: same line of action, opposite senses, equal intensities — F₁ + F₂ = 0
2. The condition is established on the bench with two dynamometers on a light card
3. On a table: weight and the table's reaction balance; hanging: weight and tension balance
4. On a spring: tension T = K·Δl grows until it equals the weight
5. Floating is two-force equilibrium: Archimedes' thrust equals the weight (F_A = P)
