# Heat transfer at home — the thermos flask and radiation absorbers — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Fill a cube with hot water whose four faces are finished differently — dull black, shiny black, dull white, polished metal — and hold a detector at equal distances: the dull black face radiates most, the polished face least; A vacuum between the double glass walls kills conduction and convection (no matter, no transfer); silvering on the walls reflects radiation back; a plastic or cork stopper of poor conductor seals the top, and the narrow neck shrinks the opening; Punjab closes with the choices the physics dictates: light-coloured, loose clothing in summer reflects radiation and lets air convect; dark surfaces absorb for solar heating; shiny teapot and foil-backed insulation borrow the flask's silvering — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
