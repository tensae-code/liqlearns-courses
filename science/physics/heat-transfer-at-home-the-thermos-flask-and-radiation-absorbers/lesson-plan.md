# Lesson plan — Heat transfer at home — the thermos flask and radiation absorbers

## Sub lessons
1. **The Leslie cube — surfaces vote on radiation** — Fill a cube with hot water whose four faces are finished differently — dull black, shiny black, dull white, polished metal — and hold a detector at equal distances: the dull black face radiates most, the polished face least. Good absorbers are good emitters; the cube makes the surface, not the temperature alone, decide how fast heat leaves by radiation.
2. **The thermos flask — three transfers, three defeats** — A vacuum between the double glass walls kills conduction and convection (no matter, no transfer); silvering on the walls reflects radiation back; a plastic or cork stopper of poor conductor seals the top, and the narrow neck shrinks the opening. Hot stays hot and cold stays cold by the same three defeats, run in both directions.
3. **Everyday consequences — clothing and houses** — Punjab closes with the choices the physics dictates: light-coloured, loose clothing in summer reflects radiation and lets air convect; dark surfaces absorb for solar heating; shiny teapot and foil-backed insulation borrow the flask's silvering. The unit's three transfers end as decisions about what to wear and how to build.
