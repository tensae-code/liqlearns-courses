# The micrometer screw gauge and the screw principle — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Derive the least count of a micrometer whose pitch is 0.5 mm with 100 thimble divisions.
**Answer: One turn advances 0.5 mm shared among 100 divisions, so each division is 0.5 ÷ 100 = 0.005 mm — that is the least count.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the object never be tightened down with the thimble itself?
**Answer: Hand force on the thimble can compress the object (and strain the screw), giving too small a reading; the ratchet slips at a fixed, gentle pressure so every measurement is taken at the same force.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Closed, a micrometer reads +0.03 mm. A sheet measures 1.24 mm on it. What is the sheet's true thickness?
**Answer: 1.24 − 0.03 = 1.21 mm; the positive zero error is subtracted from every reading.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Which scale is read first on a micrometer, and why?
**Answer: The sleeve (main) scale first — it gives the whole and half millimetres; the thimble then adds the hundredths, and reading in that order avoids mistaking which sleeve mark the thimble has uncovered.**
