# The micrometer screw gauge and the screw principle

## Do it first
Before you read: turn a screw once and it creeps forward by one thread's spacing — half a millimetre. Now mark fifty steps around that turn: each step is a fiftieth of the creep, a hundredth of a millimetre. A gauge built on nothing but that idea measures a hair. That idea — the screw principle — is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The screw principle — pitch and least count

A screw advances by exactly its **pitch** — the distance between neighbouring threads — in one complete turn. The micrometer's screw has a pitch of 0.5 mm, and its thimble carries **50 divisions** around its rim, so one thimble division advances the spindle by 0.5 ÷ 50 = **0.01 mm**: the least count. The principle is general: least count = pitch ÷ number of thimble divisions. Rotation you can see and count is traded for a forward motion too small to see — that trade is the whole instrument.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the micrometer

The object sits between the **anvil** and the **spindle**. The sleeve (barrel) carries the main scale — millimetres above the line, half-millimetres below. Read the sleeve scale uncovered by the thimble first, then the thimble division that stands against the sleeve's line, times the least count, and add: sleeve 3.5 mm with thimble division 27 gives 3.5 + 0.27 = **3.77 mm**. The last turns are always made with the **ratchet**, which slips at the right pressure — squeezing with the thimble itself crushes the object and falsifies the reading. With the jaws closed, check the zero: a closed reading that is not zero is a **zero error** to correct every measurement by.

## Sub lesson 3 — Measuring small thicknesses well

Measure a wire's diameter at several places and in two directions at each place (a wire is rarely perfectly round), and average the corrected readings. The micrometer's range is small — a school gauge spans 25 mm — but within it nothing else in the school laboratory reads finer. Keep the faces clean, close gently on paper to test the grip, and never force the frame: the instrument's accuracy is its screw, and a strained screw is a ruined gauge.

## The key ideas

- **Screw principle**: one turn advances the spindle by the pitch; least count = pitch ÷ thimble divisions = 0.5 mm ÷ 50 = 0.01 mm.
- Reading = sleeve reading + (thimble division × least count), corrected for zero error; the last turns belong to the ratchet, never to force.
- Measure at several places and directions and average — the object, not the gauge, is usually the uneven one.

## Do It Yourself
1. A micrometer's screw has pitch 1 mm and 100 thimble divisions. What is its least count?
2. Sleeve reading 5.0 mm, thimble division 38, least count 0.01 mm, zero error −0.02 mm (closed, it reads −0.02). What is the corrected thickness?
3. A wire reads 0.84, 0.86 and 0.85 mm at three places. What diameter should be reported, and why not just the first reading?
