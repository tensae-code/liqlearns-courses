## Sub lessons
1. **The steady point — k = 1 held by design** — A chain reaction is counted by its multiplication: how many fissions the neutrons of one fission go on to cause. Above one, the reaction runs away; below one, it dies. The reactor's whole art is holding the number at exactly one for years — a balance struck between the fuel's urge to multiply and the absorbers' power to trim, adjustable minute by minute.
2. **The parts — moderator, control rods, coolant** — Fresh fission neutrons are fast, and uranium-235 fissions far more readily with slow ones, so the moderator (water, heavy water or graphite) slows them by collisions without swallowing them. Control rods of boron or cadmium do the opposite — they swallow neutrons greedily, and sliding them in or out trims the multiplication to one. The coolant carries the fission heat out to raise steam and turn the generators — the heat is the product; the chain reaction is only its fire.
3. **Safety in layers — the controlled fire kept** — Around the physics stand the layers: the fuel's own ceramic and cladding, the steel pressure vessel, the concrete containment, and the rule that the reactor is designed to fail toward shutdown — heat it and the reaction naturally slackens. Jordan closes its nuclear unit here: fission's chain reaction, first met as an uncontrolled multiplication, ends as the most deliberately balanced fire in technology — with the fusion reactor named as the still-experimental counterpart.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A reactor holds the chain reaction at exactly one onward fission per fission — the steady point
2. The moderator slows fast neutrons so U-235 fissions readily, without absorbing them
3. Control rods of boron or cadmium absorb neutrons and trim the reaction
4. The coolant carries the fission heat away to raise steam for generation
5. Safety is layered — fuel, cladding, vessel, containment — and designed to fail toward shutdown
