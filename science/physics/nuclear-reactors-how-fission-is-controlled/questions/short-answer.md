# Nuclear reactors — how fission is controlled — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Define the steady point and say what trims the reactor to it.
**Answer: Each fission's neutrons cause exactly one further fission on average, so power is constant. Control rods, slid in or out, absorb more or fewer neutrons and trim the multiplication to exactly that point.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do thermal reactors need a moderator at all?
**Answer: Fission neutrons are born fast, but U-235's probability of fissioning is far higher for slow (thermal) neutrons; without slowing, too few neutrons would cause fission and the chain could not sustain itself in natural or low-enriched fuel.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Distinguish the moderator's and the rods' treatment of neutrons.
**Answer: The moderator slows neutrons by collisions while absorbing as few as possible — it prepares them for fission. The rods absorb neutrons outright — they remove them from the account. Preparation and removal are opposite tools.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What does Jordan's closing contrast — the fusion reactor — add?
**Answer: It names the counterpart still in experimental development: fusion's fire is not a self-multiplying chain but must be continuously fed and confined — control there means sustaining conditions, not trimming a multiplication.**
