## The seven-unit journey

This course runs in seven units, in this order. First, **Unit 1 — Physics and Human Society**: what physics is and where it works in daily life. Then **Unit 2 — Physical Quantities**: measuring things properly, with standards. Then **Unit 3 — Motion in a Straight Line**: describing how things move. Then **Unit 4 — Force, Work, Energy and Power**: pushes, and what pushes can do. Then **Unit 5 — Simple Machines**: levers, pulleys and the other helpers that multiply force. Then **Unit 6 — Mechanical Oscillation and Sound Wave**: things that swing and vibrate, and the sound they make. Last, **Unit 7 — Temperature and Thermometry**: hot and cold, and how we measure them.

Society first, then measure, move, push, machines, waves, heat — that order is the journey. Each unit builds on the ones before it: you cannot talk about force before you can measure, and you cannot measure heat before you know what a quantity is.

## How every unit is taught — brainstorm first

Every unit follows the same rhythm. It opens with **Brainstorming Questions drawn from daily life** — you think and talk first, before any new idea is named. Then the unit's outcomes are stated, so you know where you are going. Each numbered section opens with the words "at the end of this section you should be able to…" and then works through **Activities, Exercises and worked Examples**, with **KEY CONCEPT** boxes picking out the ideas that must stick. Every unit closes with a **Unit Summary** and a multi-part **Unit Review**.

So in every lesson of this course: think first from your own life, then learn the idea, then practise it on worked examples and exercises. That brainstorm-first pattern is not decoration — it is how the learning happens.

## Ethiopian anchors — physics starts at home

The examples come from home. Harar Jugol, Fasilides Castle, the Axum obelisks and the Lalibela churches show physics holding up heritage buildings. Measuring starts in the local market, where measures differ from place to place. The Afar region's heat appears in an expansion problem. The kirar — the Ethiopian lyre — is one of the oscillating instruments in the sound unit. When a lesson asks you for your own example, it is following the book's own way: start from what you already know.

## The key ideas

- The Grade 9 course has seven units, in order: Physics and Human Society; Physical Quantities; Motion in a Straight Line; Force, Work, Energy and Power; Simple Machines; Mechanical Oscillation and Sound Wave; Temperature and Thermometry.
- Every unit opens with Brainstorming Questions drawn from daily life — think first, then learn.
- Each section opens with "at the end of this section you should be able to…", and units close with a Unit Summary and a Unit Review.
- Ethiopian anchors run throughout: Harar Jugol, Fasilides Castle, the Axum obelisks, Lalibela, market measuring, Afar heat and the kirar.
