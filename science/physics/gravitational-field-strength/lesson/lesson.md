# Gravitational field strength

## Do it first
Before you read: stand on a bathroom scale and it reads your weight — Earth's pull on you. Now imagine the scale under you on the Moon: it would read about a sixth as much, though you are exactly the same you. Nothing about you changed; something about the place did. That property of the place — how hard gravity pulls each kilogram there — is this lesson's quantity.

## Sub lesson 1 — The field idea — gravity without touching

The gain is practical: one field map serves every visitor. The Earth's field at a point is the same for a falling apple and a falling astronaut — what each feels differs, because their masses differ, not because the field does.

## Sub lesson 2 — Field strength — force per kilogram

Because g depends only on M and r, it is a property of the place: at the Moon's surface g ≈ 1.6 N/kg (smaller M, smaller r); at twice Earth's radius from Earth's centre, g falls to a quarter — the **inverse-square** fall-off the formula carries. Vietnam's lesson (Bài 2, Cường độ trường hấp dẫn — literally 'intensity of the gravitational field') works exactly these variations: g with height, g with depth of latitude, g on other worlds.

## The key ideas

- A mass creates a gravitational field around it; field lines point toward the mass and crowd where the field is stronger
- Field strength g = F/m — the force on each kilogram placed at the point, in N/kg
- For a spherical mass, g = GM/r²: independent of the test mass, falling with the inverse square of distance
- Surface g (Earth ≈ 9.8 N/kg, Moon ≈ 1.6 N/kg) is also the free-fall acceleration at that place — the same quantity in two units

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: A mass changes the space around it: the gravitational field strength g at a point is the force the field exerts on each kilogram placed there, g = F/m = GM/r² — measured in newtons per kilogram — pointing toward the mass, growing stronger closer in, and independent of the test mass that measures it. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find g at a point 3 Earth-radii from Earth's centre (surface g = 9.8 N/kg).
2. A planet has twice Earth's mass and the same radius. Find its surface g.
3. An astronaut's weight on a planet is 240 N and her mass is 80 kg. Find the field strength at that planet's surface and her mass's weight back on Earth.
