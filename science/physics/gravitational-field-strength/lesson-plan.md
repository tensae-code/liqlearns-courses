# Lesson plan — Gravitational field strength

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Gravitational field strength

## Sub lessons
1. **The field idea — gravity without touching** — Universal gravitation states a force between two masses; the **field** view restates it in two steps: a mass M creates a **gravitational field** in the space around it, and any mass m placed in that field feels a force. The field exists whether or not a second mass is there to feel it — it is a property of the space around M, drawn with field lines that point **toward** M (gravity only attracts) and crowd closer where the field is stronger. Vietnam's Grade 11 study-topics book builds its whole gravitation topic on this view: Bài 1 the field, Bài 2 its strength, Bài 3 its potential.
2. **Field strength — force per kilogram** — The **gravitational field strength** g at a point is defined as the force per unit mass placed there: **g = F/m**. Put Newton's law inside it — F = GMm/r² — and the test mass cancels: **g = GM/r²**, in **newtons per kilogram**. Near the Earth's surface this is the familiar 9.8 N/kg — the same number as the acceleration of free fall, and no coincidence: a mass released in the field accelerates at a = F/m = g, so field strength in N/kg and free-fall acceleration in m/s² are one quantity wearing two units.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define gravitational field strength and state its unit.
2. Derive g = GM/r² from Newton's law and the definition g = F/m.
3. Compute g at a given distance and on another planet's surface.
4. Explain why g and free-fall acceleration are numerically the same.

## What you need
No equipment — the hands-on is a thought-and-calculation exercise with a bathroom scale's reading imagined on other worlds.
