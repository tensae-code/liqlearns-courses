# Gravitational field strength — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the test mass cancel out when g = GM/r² is derived?
**Answer: Because the force itself is proportional to the test mass (F = GMm/r²), so dividing by m to get force-per-kilogram removes it — the field strength characterises the field of M alone.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) At what height above Earth's surface (in Earth radii) is g about 2.45 N/kg?
**Answer: g must fall to a quarter of 9.8, so r must double: at r = 2 Earth radii from the centre, i.e. one Earth radius above the surface.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain why the Moon's surface g is smaller than Earth's even though the Moon's radius is also smaller.
**Answer: g = GM/r²: the Moon's much smaller mass (about 1/81 of Earth's) outweighs its smaller radius (about 1/3.7, whose square boosts g by ~13.7) — net effect ≈ 13.7/81 ≈ 1/6 of Earth's g.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Vietnam's topics book places field strength (Bài 2) before gravitational potential (Bài 3). What is gained by the field-first order?
**Answer: The field view makes gravity a property of space around a mass, computable point by point; strength (force per kg) and then potential (energy per kg) become two measurements of the same field, instead of forces computed pair by pair for every new mass.**
