# Light — a wave you can see

## Do it first
In a dusty or smoky room, watch a sunbeam or torch beam hold its straight edge. You are seeing the ray model with your own eyes.

[Picture 21929 — ray from a candle to an eye, straight-line path drawn]

## Light — a wave you can see — the teaching
Visible light is the one EM band our eyes detect. It travels through vacuum as happily as through air, water or glass, and in a uniform medium it travels in straight lines — the ray model, where a ray is the path of light drawn as a straight line with an arrow. Its speed in vacuum is c ≈ 3.00×10⁸ m/s — exactly 2.99792458×10⁸ m/s — and it slows in materials, the fact refraction is built on.

**Keep the formula:** c ≈ 3.00×10⁸ m/s

## The key ideas

- Light is the visible band of the EM spectrum.
- In a uniform medium light travels in straight lines (the ray model).
- c ≈ 3.00×10⁸ m/s in vacuum; light slows in materials.

## Say it to your companion
Explain light — a wave you can see to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
