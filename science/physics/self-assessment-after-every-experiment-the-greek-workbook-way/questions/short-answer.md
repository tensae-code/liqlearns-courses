# Self-assessment after every experiment — the Greek workbook way — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Describe the order of a Greek workbook worksheet from start to finish.
**Answer: The experiment's working record comes first — measurement tables, graph space, conclusion prompts and homework, all completed by the pupil — and the worksheet closes with the self-assessment block, the pupil's written evaluation of their own effort.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Write the three questions a pupil answers in the self-assessment block.
**Answer: What did I do well? Where did I struggle (apparatus, method, maths or idea)? What will I do better or differently next time?**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain the workbook's claim that a learner who can judge their work can improve it.
**Answer: Improvement needs a diagnosis. The pupil who can name precisely what went wrong — a badly chosen graph scale, a rushed table — has already identified the change that makes the next experiment better; the written block turns that diagnosis into a plan.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the block placed after the conclusion rather than before the experiment?
**Answer: Self-assessment judges finished work while it is still remembered in detail — the table, graph and conclusion just produced. Before the experiment there is nothing of the pupil's own to judge.**
