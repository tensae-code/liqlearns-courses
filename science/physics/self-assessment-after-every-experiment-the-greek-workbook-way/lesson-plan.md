# Lesson plan — Self-assessment after every experiment — the Greek workbook way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Self-assessment after every experiment — the Greek workbook way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a country's-own-way lesson: the Greek Grade 9 workbook ends every one of its thirteen lab worksheets with the pupil evaluating their own effort
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in Greece's Grade 9 workbook, an experiment is not finished when the last measurement is taken: every worksheet closes with a self-assessment block in which the pupil evaluates their own effort — what they did well, where they struggled, what they would do better — making honest self-judgement a timetabled part of learning physics
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **The worksheet — record, conclude, then judge** — The Greek Grade 9 workbook is the paper companion to its lab guide: one worksheet for each of the thirteen labs, in the same order. Each worksheet carries the pupil through the experiment's working record — measurement tables to fill, graph space to plot on, conclusion prompts to answer, homework to attempt. The pupil is the author of the whole page: nothing arrives pre-concluded. Only when that working record is complete does the worksheet's final block appear — and it is not about the physics. It is about the pupil.
2. **The self-assessment block — evaluate your effort** — Every worksheet ends with the same demand, in the book's words: evaluate your effort. The pupil looks back over the worksheet they have just produced and judges it: Was the table complete and honestly filled? Does the graph actually show what the conclusion claims? Where did I get stuck — the apparatus, the method, the maths, the idea? The judgement is written, not just thought, and it is the pupil's own: no marks are attached to it, because a self-assessment that is graded stops being honest. It is a mirror held up at the exact moment the work is finished and still remembered in detail.
3. **Why it is a way of teaching, not an extra** — Put at the end of all thirteen worksheets, self-assessment becomes a habit of the course, not a one-off reflection exercise. Its claim is the Greek workbook's own: a learner who can judge their work can improve it — the pupil who writes 'my graph's scale hid the pattern; next time choose the scale before plotting' has already done the next lab better. The teacher reads the block too, and learns where the class actually struggled rather than where the marks say they did. Judging your own effort, experiment by experiment, is presented as part of what doing physics is.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the Greek workbook's shape: one worksheet per lab, pupil-authored tables, graphs and conclusions
2. State what the closing self-assessment block asks, and that it is written and ungraded
3. Explain why attaching marks would destroy the block's honesty
4. Use a self-assessment to name a concrete improvement for the next experiment
5. Say what the teacher learns from reading the class's self-assessments
