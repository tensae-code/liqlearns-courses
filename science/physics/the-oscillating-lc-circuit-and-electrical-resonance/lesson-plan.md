# Lesson plan — The oscillating LC circuit and electrical resonance

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The oscillating LC circuit and electrical resonance

## Sub lessons
1. **Energy swapping between capacitor and coil** — Charge a capacitor, disconnect the supply and connect it across an inductor. The capacitor discharges through the coil: as its charge and electric-field energy fall, the coil's current and magnetic-field energy rise. When the capacitor is empty the current is greatest — and does not stop, because the coil's inductance keeps it flowing, recharging the capacitor with the opposite polarity. Then the discharge runs backwards. The energy sloshes from capacitor to coil and back, electric to magnetic to electric, twice per cycle.
2. **The natural frequency of the LC circuit** — The trade has one natural rate, set by the two components alone: f = 1/(2π√(LC)) — the same shape as the pendulum's law, with inductance playing the mass (reluctance to change current) and capacitance the springiness (reluctance to change voltage). Double L or C and the frequency falls by √2; the oscillation's period is T = 2π√(LC).
3. **Resonance and the tuned radio circuit** — Connect the LC pair to the aerial. Every station's signal pushes tiny currents at its own frequency through the circuit — but the circuit answers strongly only to the push arriving at its own natural frequency: electrical resonance. At that one frequency the response builds up large (limited only by the circuit's resistance), giving a usable signal; all other frequencies are answered feebly and are rejected.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: An LC circuit oscillates by trading energy: capacitor's field energy Q²/2C ↔ inductor's magnetic energy ½LI², the sum constant in the ideal circuit.
2. Say, in their own words: The natural frequency is f = 1/(2π√(LC)) — set by L and C alone, independent of amplitude; resistance damps the oscillation away.
3. Say, in their own words: At resonance the circuit responds strongly to a driving signal at its own frequency and feebly to all others.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the capacitor 'uses up' its energy in the coil → energy is traded, not spent: the coil stores it magnetically and returns it, recharging the capacitor with reversed polarity; only resistance truly spends it.
- Expects amplitude to set the frequency → f = 1/(2π√(LC)) contains only L and C; like the pendulum, the LC circuit keeps its period at any amplitude.
