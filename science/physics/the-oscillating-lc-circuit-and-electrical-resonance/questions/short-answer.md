# The oscillating LC circuit and electrical resonance — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Describe one full cycle of energy in an LC circuit, starting with the capacitor fully charged.
**Answer: Capacitor charged: all energy is Q²/2C, current zero. Discharge: charge falls, current rises, energy moves into the coil's field. Capacitor empty: current maximum, all energy ½LI². The current continues, recharging the capacitor with opposite polarity until all energy is back in its field — half a cycle. The second half mirrors it, restoring the original state: the energy sloshes electric ↔ magnetic twice per full period.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) An oscillator uses L = 0.1 mH and C = 100 pF. Find its frequency. (1/2π ≈ 0.159.)
**Answer: f = 1/(2π√(LC)) = 0.159 ÷ √(0.0001 × 1×10⁻¹⁰) = 0.159 ÷ √(1×10⁻¹⁴) = 0.159 ÷ 1×10⁻⁷ ≈ 1.59 × 10⁶ Hz ≈ 1.6 MHz.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do real LC oscillations die away, and how does a radio's tuned circuit nevertheless keep answering a steady station?
**Answer: The circuit's resistance dissipates a little energy as heat each cycle, so a free oscillation damps down. In the receiver the aerial signal at the resonant frequency continuously feeds energy in step with the oscillation — resonance keeps replenishing what resistance spends — so the response holds at a steady, large amplitude for that one frequency.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A tuning capacitor is halved. What happens to the received station's frequency, and why?
**Answer: f varies as 1/√C, so halving C multiplies f by √2 ≈ 1.41: the circuit now resonates with a station at about 1.41 times the previous frequency. Smaller capacitance, faster energy trade, higher natural frequency.**
