# Quantization of electric charge

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The elementary charge — nature's coin

Millikan's oil-drop experiment found the unit: charged drops carried many different charges, but every one was a whole multiple of a single smallest value, e. The carrier is the electron with charge −e and the proton with +e. A body charged to −4.8 × 10⁻¹⁹ C holds exactly three extra electrons — the arithmetic of charging is counting, in units of e.

## Sub lesson 2 — Quantisation stated — Q = ne

The law is one line: the charge on any body is Q = ne, with n a whole number (positive, negative or zero). Half-charges and third-charges do not occur on free bodies — a measured charge that is not a whole multiple of e has simply not been measured finely enough. In school-sized charges the unit is so tiny that charge feels continuous, exactly as water feels continuous though it is made of molecules.

## Sub lesson 3 — Beside conservation — the two rules of charge

Jordan places quantisation next to the charge unit's other rule, conservation: in charging by rubbing, conduction or induction, charge is only ever moved, never made — and what moves is whole electrons, so both rules are one picture: electrons, each carrying −e, changing address. The electron count behind a coulomb is the scale of it: one coulomb is about 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons' worth — a vast number of very small coins.

## The key ideas

- Every free charge is a whole multiple of the elementary charge e
- Q = ne, with n a whole number; fractional charges do not occur on free bodies
- e ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; the electron carries −e and the proton +e
- Millikan's oil drops established the unit experimentally
- One coulomb is about 6.25 × 10¹⁸ elementary charges

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion, in your own words: electric charge comes in whole units of one elementary charge e: every free charge ever measured is a whole-number multiple of e — +e, −2e, +3e — never a fraction of one. Jordan's Grade 11 book states the quantisation beside conservation: charge is neither created nor destroyed, and it is dealt only in whole coins of e ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ coulombs.
