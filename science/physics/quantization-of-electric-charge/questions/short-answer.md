# Quantization of electric charge — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does charge feel continuous in everyday circuits if it is quantised?
**Answer: Because e is so small that ordinary charges contain astronomical numbers of units; the steps are far below anything a school meter can resolve — as water feels continuous though it is molecular.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A body is measured with charge +8.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. How many electrons has it lost?
**Answer: n = Q/e = 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ ÷ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 5 — it has lost exactly 5 electrons (a positive charge is an electron deficit).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What would a single reliable measurement of a free charge of 0.5e do to the law?
**Answer: It would break it — quantisation claims every free charge is a whole multiple of e; one verified fraction would falsify the claim as stated. (Quarks carry fractional charge but are never found free.)**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State Jordan's two rules of charge together, in one sentence.
**Answer: Charge is conserved — only moved, never created or destroyed — and it is quantised — carried only in whole units of e by whole electrons.**
