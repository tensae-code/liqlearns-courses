# Lesson plan — Quantization of electric charge

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Quantization of electric charge
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the electrostatics strand — Jordan's Grade 11 completion of the charge story
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** electric charge comes in whole units of one elementary charge e: every free charge ever measured is a whole-number multiple of e — +e, −2e, +3e — never a fraction of one. Jordan's Grade 11 book states the quantisation beside conservation: charge is neither created nor destroyed, and it is dealt only in whole coins of e ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ coulombs

## Sub lessons
1. **The elementary charge — nature's coin** — Millikan's oil-drop experiment found the unit: charged drops carried many different charges, but every one was a whole multiple of a single smallest value, e. The carrier is the electron with charge −e and the proton with +e. A body charged to −4.8 × 10⁻¹⁹ C holds exactly three extra electrons — the arithmetic of charging is counting, in units of e.
2. **Quantisation stated — Q = ne** — The law is one line: the charge on any body is Q = ne, with n a whole number (positive, negative or zero). Half-charges and third-charges do not occur on free bodies — a measured charge that is not a whole multiple of e has simply not been measured finely enough. In school-sized charges the unit is so tiny that charge feels continuous, exactly as water feels continuous though it is made of molecules.
3. **Beside conservation — the two rules of charge** — Jordan places quantisation next to the charge unit's other rule, conservation: in charging by rubbing, conduction or induction, charge is only ever moved, never made — and what moves is whole electrons, so both rules are one picture: electrons, each carrying −e, changing address. The electron count behind a coulomb is the scale of it: one coulomb is about 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons' worth — a vast number of very small coins.
