# Standing waves in air columns — longitudinal standing waves

## Do it first
Before you read: hum into an empty bottle, then into a tube open at both ends. Different notes — yet nothing inside either one is moving from end to end; the air just trembles in place. This lesson explains the pattern that trembling makes, and why the two containers are allowed different notes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Longitudinal standing waves — the pattern

Sound in air is **longitudinal**: particles oscillate back and forth along the direction the wave travels. When a sound wave reflects back on itself inside a pipe, the two travelling waves superpose into a **standing wave**: the pattern does not travel. At **nodes** the air never moves (displacement node, pressure antinode); at **antinodes** the air swings through its widest displacement. The distance between neighbouring nodes — or neighbouring antinodes — is half a wavelength, and a node to the next antinode is a quarter wavelength: those two facts are the whole arithmetic of pipes.

## Sub lesson 2 — The closed pipe — odd harmonics only

A pipe closed at one end forces a **displacement node at the closed end** (the air cannot move there) and an **antinode at the open end**. The shortest pattern that fits runs node-to-antinode: a quarter wavelength, so the **fundamental has λ₁ = 4L** and f₁ = v/4L. The next patterns that fit add half a wavelength each time — 3λ/4, 5λ/4 — so a closed pipe sounds only the **odd harmonics: f₁, 3f₁, 5f₁…** That missing even harmonics give the clarinet family its hollow colour.

## Sub lesson 3 — The open pipe — every harmonic

A pipe open at both ends has a **displacement antinode at each open end**. The shortest fitting pattern is antinode-to-antinode: half a wavelength, so the **fundamental has λ₁ = 2L** and f₁ = v/2L — an octave above a closed pipe of the same length. Every whole number of half-wavelengths fits, so the open pipe sounds the **complete harmonic series: f₁, 2f₁, 3f₁…** — the flute and the open organ pipe. Same air, same speed of sound; the ends decide the music.

## The key ideas

- A standing wave does not travel: nodes stay still, antinodes swing widest; node-to-node is λ/2, node-to-antinode is λ/4.
- Closed pipe: node at the closed end, antinode at the open end; fundamental λ = 4L; odd harmonics only (f, 3f, 5f).
- Open pipe: antinodes at both ends; fundamental λ = 2L; the full harmonic series (f, 2f, 3f).
- Same pipe length: the open pipe's fundamental is twice the closed pipe's frequency.

## Do It Yourself
1. A closed pipe is 0.85 m long and the speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find its fundamental frequency and its next two sounding harmonics.
2. An open pipe has the same length. Find its fundamental and explain the ratio to the closed pipe's in one sentence.
3. A closed pipe sounds its third harmonic at 600 Hz. What is its fundamental, and which harmonics will it never sound?
