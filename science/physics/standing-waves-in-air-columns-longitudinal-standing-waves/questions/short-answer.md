# Standing waves in air columns — longitudinal standing waves — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is a sound standing wave called longitudinal?
**Answer: Because the air particles oscillate back and forth along the same line as the direction of propagation — the displacement is parallel to the travel direction, unlike a transverse wave on a string.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why can a closed pipe not sound its second harmonic?
**Answer: Its patterns must run from a node at the closed end to an antinode at the open end, which fits only odd quarter-wavelength counts — λ/4, 3λ/4, 5λ/4 — giving frequencies f, 3f, 5f; 2f would need a pattern ending in a node at the open end.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) An organ builder wants a lower fundamental from the same length of pipe. What change does this lesson suggest?
**Answer: Close one end: a closed pipe of length L has fundamental v/4L, an octave below the open pipe's v/2L.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A closed pipe's fundamental is 100 Hz. List its first three sounding resonances.
**Answer: 100 Hz, 300 Hz and 500 Hz — the odd harmonics only.**
