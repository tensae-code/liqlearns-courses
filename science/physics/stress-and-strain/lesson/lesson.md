# Stress and strain

## Do it first
Pull a thick rubber band and a thin one with the same force. The thin one stretches much more — same force, smaller area, greater stress.

[Picture 21891 — wire under load with force, area, original length and extension labelled]

## Stress and strain — the teaching
Stress is the deforming force divided by the area it acts on: stress = F/A, measured in pascals (N/m²). Strain is the fractional change the stress produces — extension divided by original length for a stretched wire — and it has no unit. The pair comes in forms: tensile (pulling), compressive (squeezing) and shear (sliding layers). Same force, thinner wire: more stress, because the area carrying it is smaller.

**Keep the formula:** Stress = F / A; Strain = extension / original length

## The key ideas

- Stress = F/A, unit pascal (N/m²).
- Strain = extension ÷ original length — unitless.
- Forms: tensile, compressive, shear.

## Say it to your companion
Explain stress and strain to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
