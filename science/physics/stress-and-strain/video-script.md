# Video script — Stress and strain

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Stress is the deforming force divided by the area it acts on: stress = F/A, measured in pascals (N/m²).
Strain is the fractional change the stress produces — extension divided by original length for a stretched wire — and it has no unit.
The pair comes in forms: tensile (pulling), compressive (squeezing) and shear (sliding layers).
Same force, thinner wire: more stress, because the area carrying it is smaller.

Remember: **Stress = F/A, unit pascal (N/m²).**
Remember: **Strain = extension ÷ original length — unitless.**
Remember: **Forms: tensile, compressive, shear.**
Keep the formula: **Stress = F / A; Strain = extension / original length**
