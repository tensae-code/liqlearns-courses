# Lami's theorem for three concurrent forces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A ring is held by three forces but the point slowly drifts to the left. What does the drift tell you about the forces, and what drawing would show it?
**Answer: They are not in equilibrium — their vector sum is not zero; drawn head-to-tail the three arrows would not close into a triangle, and the gap in the chain points along the unbalanced part of the force, the way the point drifts.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) State Lami's theorem in words, naming exactly which angle belongs to each force.
**Answer: For three concurrent forces in equilibrium, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two forces: F₁/sin α = F₂/sin β = F₃/sin γ, where each angle is the one at the point that does not touch the force it is paired with.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A 60 N weight hangs with the angles at the ring: between the tensions 90°, facing T₁ the angle 135°, facing T₂ the angle 135°. Find each tension (sin 90° = 1, sin 135° ≈ 0.707).
**Answer: T₁ = 60 × sin 135° / sin 90° ≈ 60 × 0.707 ≈ 42.4 N, and T₂ the same, ≈ 42.4 N — equal tensions, as the symmetric angles require.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can Lami's theorem not be used directly on a point held by four strings?
**Answer: The theorem is stated for exactly three concurrent forces: with four, there is no single chain of three equal ratios and the three-angles-around-a-point picture no longer matches; the four-force case is handled by resolving into components instead.**
