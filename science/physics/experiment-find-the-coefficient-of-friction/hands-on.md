# Hands-on — Experiment: find the coefficient of friction

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the practical
1. Bench experiment: place a block on a board, load and pull it, and find coefficients from the forces at slipping and during sliding; the results feed exercises on friction on inclines.

Work with a partner, write down what you measured or observed, and say what the result proves. If apparatus is missing, walk through the steps with drawings and say what you would expect to see.

## Feel inertia
Put a book on a sheet of paper and pull the paper out fast. The book stays. Then pull slowly — the book rides. Name the law that explains both.

## See it and say it
Study this lesson's picture — a block, spring balance and weights on a board — and name every part of it out loud. Then close the picture and draw it from memory; check your drawing against the lesson.

## Teach it back
Explain this lesson to someone at home using one thing in the room as your example. Their questions show you which part to re-read.
