# Thin-film interference — colours in soap films — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain why the two reflections can interfere though they come from one incoming ray.
**Answer: They are two parts of the same wave train — one reflected at the front surface, one transmitted, reflected at the back and transmitted out again — so they are coherent, with a path difference fixed by the film's thickness.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the very thinnest film black rather than bright?
**Answer: Its path difference is nearly zero, but the front reflection's half-cycle phase change puts the two waves out of step anyway — they cancel. Without the phase change they would reinforce; the black patch is the phase change's signature.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A film region looks green. What is true there?
**Answer: At that region's thickness, the path difference (with the phase change counted) reinforces green and comparatively cancels the other colours — the thickness is the one that fits green's condition.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the same phenomenon in one other daily place and its cause.
**Answer: Oil on a wet road — the oil layer's varying thickness reinforces different colours place by place, by the same two-reflection interference.**
