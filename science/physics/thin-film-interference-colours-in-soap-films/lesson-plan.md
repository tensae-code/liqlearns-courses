# Lesson plan — Thin-film interference — colours in soap films

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Thin-film interference — colours in soap films
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the wave-optics strand — Jordan's Grade 11 lesson on interference in thin layers
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the colours sweeping across a soap bubble or an oil patch are interference in a layer almost as thin as light's wavelength: light reflecting from the film's front surface interferes with light reflecting from its back surface, and the film's varying thickness selects, place by place, which colour the path difference reinforces

## Sub lessons
1. **Two reflections, one film — setting up the interference** — A soap film is two surfaces a fraction of a micrometre apart. Incoming light reflects twice: part at the front surface, part after crossing the film at the back surface. The two reflected waves recombine in the eye. Their path difference is twice the film's thickness (down and back) — so thickness alone decides whether they arrive in step or out of step, for each colour separately.
2. **The phase change — why the rule flips at the front surface** — Reflection off a denser medium flips the wave's phase by half a cycle; reflection inside the film, off the rarer air beyond, does not. So the front reflection carries an extra half-cycle the back one lacks, and the interference rule is reversed compared with a naive path count: a film so thin it is nearly nothing reflects destructively — the black patch seen at the top of a draining soap film, where it is thinnest.
3. **Colours by thickness — reading the film** — Where the film thickens, the reinforcing colour changes: each thickness picks its colour, so a draining vertical film draws itself in horizontal bands of changing colour as the top thins toward black. Oil on wet roads does the same with its own thickness map. The phenomenon is a measuring instrument in disguise — the colours are a contour map of thickness in units of wavelengths of light.
