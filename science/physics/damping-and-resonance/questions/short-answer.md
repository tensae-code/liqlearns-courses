# Damping and resonance — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A plucked guitar string goes quiet even though nobody touches it. Where did its energy go?
**Answer: Answer: Damping drained it: the string's energy left as sound carried away by the air, as friction where the string is held, and as movement of the air around it, so the amplitude shrank swing by swing until the oscillation died.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Two identical pendulums hang side by side. One is driven by small pushes at its natural frequency, the other by the same pushes at a much lower frequency. Which answers bigger, and why?
**Answer: Answer: The one driven at its natural frequency: its pushes arrive in step and each adds energy, so it resonates and its amplitude builds to a maximum; the other's pushes arrive out of step and mostly cancel.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does lighter damping make resonance more dangerous for a building?
**Answer: Answer: With light damping, little energy leaves the sway each cycle, so a driver at the natural frequency (wind gusts, an earthquake) can build the amplitude swing by swing to a large, damaging value; heavy damping drains the energy as fast as it arrives.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Give one use of resonance and one place where damping is deliberately added, naming the job each does.
**Answer: Answer: Use of resonance: tuning a radio — the circuit is made to resonate at one station's frequency so only that signal builds up. Deliberate damping: a car's shock absorbers, set near critical damping, drain bounce energy so the car settles after a bump instead of bouncing on.**
