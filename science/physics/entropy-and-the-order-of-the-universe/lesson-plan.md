# Lesson plan — Entropy and the order of the universe

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Entropy and the order of the universe
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics — Thermodynamics neighbourhood, after the heat-engine lessons (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** some processes run one way only, and entropy — the measure of how spread out and disordered energy and matter have become — is why: the second law of thermodynamics says the total entropy of the universe never decreases, so the universe's order is always, slowly, running down.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on everyday sights — melting ice, cooling coffee, a shuffled room — a learner can land here directly. What heat is and Steam and motion deepen it, but nothing must come first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Processes that run one way only** — heat flows hot to cold, ice melts in a warm room, perfume spreads through the air — and none of these ever runs backwards by itself; time's arrow is built into physics.
2. **Entropy — measuring disorder** — entropy measures how disordered and spread out things are: concentrated, ordered states have low entropy; spread out, jumbled states have high entropy — and spreading is what nature does at every chance.
3. **The order of the universe** — the second law: in every real process the universe's total entropy grows or stays the same, never falls; local order (a crystal, a living body) can rise only by spreading more disorder around it, and the far end of the road is a universe with everything spread evenly, warm and still.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **What heat is** and **Steam and motion — heat engines and the industrial revolution**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give three everyday processes that run one way only, and say what the reverse would look like.
2. Define entropy as the measure of disorder — of how spread out energy and matter have become.
3. State the second law: the total entropy of the universe never decreases.
4. Explain how a living body or a growing crystal can build local order without breaking the law — it exports more disorder than the order it builds.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → go back to Do it first: has a smell ever gathered itself back into the bottle? The one-wayness is the whole lesson; entropy is its measure.
- Thinks entropy means things 'want to be messy' → nothing wants anything: there are simply vastly more ways to be spread out than to be concentrated, so spreading is what happens at every chance.
- Thinks living things break the second law → they obey it at a price: a body builds its own order by taking in concentrated energy and throwing out more disorder (heat and waste) than the order it keeps.
