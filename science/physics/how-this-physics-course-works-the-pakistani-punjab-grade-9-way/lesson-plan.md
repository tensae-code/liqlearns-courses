# Lesson plan — How this physics course works — the Pakistani (Punjab) Grade 9 way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** How this physics course works — the Pakistani (Punjab) Grade 9 way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the measurement strand — Pakistan (Punjab)'s own way of opening every unit of Physics IX
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every Punjab Grade 9 unit is built the same way: Student's Learning Outcomes and a Major Concepts list first, a Conceptual Linkage box tying it to earlier and later classes, Investigation Skills and Science–Technology–Society boxes with Quick Quiz, Tidbits and For Your Information sidebars through it, and a five-part question set — MCQ, review, conceptual and numerical questions — to close
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Outcomes and concepts first, linkage around them** — Every unit of Physics IX opens by telling the learner exactly what they will be able to do: a Student's Learning Outcomes list, then the unit's Major Concepts in one place. Around them sits the Conceptual Linkage box, which names what the unit is built on from earlier classes and what it leads to in later ones — the learner always knows where the unit stands in the whole course before meeting any detail.
2. **Investigation, society and sidebars while you learn** — Through the unit run Investigation Skills boxes, where the learner does the measuring and testing, and Science–Technology–Society boxes, where the physics meets the world it serves. Quick Quiz checks, Tidbits and For Your Information sidebars ride alongside the main text — small, named pauses that test, surprise and extend without breaking the unit's line.
3. **Five ways of being questioned at the close** — Each unit closes with a summary and then questions in five registers: multiple-choice questions, review questions, conceptual questions and numerical questions — the same physics asked as recognition, as recall, as understanding and as calculation. A learner who can answer in all the registers owns the unit; the Punjab pattern makes that the test of finishing.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
