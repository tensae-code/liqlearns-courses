# Electrostatic applications — inkjet printing and spray painting — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Trace one drop through an inkjet printer, naming each physics step.
**Answer: The drop breaks off and is given a chosen charge; it flies between deflecting plates; the field exerts F = qE sideways for its flight time, so its landing height is proportional to its charge; it strikes the page at the position the charge encoded.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give two practical advantages of electrostatic painting that follow from the physics.
**Answer: Wrap-around coverage (field lines reach the back and edges, so less re-spraying) and less waste (droplets are drawn to the object instead of drifting past), with an even coat from the droplets' mutual repulsion.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must the drops' charges be controlled precisely in the printer?
**Answer: Because charge is the steering instruction: the deflection is proportional to q, so any error or spread in q lands drops off their intended positions and blurs the image.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What do the two machines have in common, in one sentence?
**Answer: Both charge small parcels of matter and use an electric field's force on the charge to place the parcels exactly — ink on paper, paint on the workpiece.**
