# Electrostatic applications — inkjet printing and spray painting

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The inkjet printer — charged drops, steered by a field

A print head fires a stream of identical ink drops. As each drop breaks off it is given a charge chosen by the electronics — the amount of charge is the instruction. The drop then flies between deflecting plates whose field pushes it sideways in proportion to its charge: heavily charged drops land high, uncharged drops fly straight into a gutter for recycling. The page's image is written by charge amounts and timing alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — Electrostatic painting — droplets that wrap around

In the spray booth the paint leaves the gun charged (commonly negative) and the workpiece is earthed. The droplets repel each other into a fine, even cloud, then follow the field lines to the object — including its back and edges, the wrap-around that a straight spray cannot reach. Less paint misses, less is wasted, and the coat is even — the reasons car bodies are painted this way.

## Sub lesson 3 — One physics in both — charge, field, force

Both machines run the same unit: charge a small parcel of matter, let a field exert F = qE on it, and use the force to place it. The applications are Jordan's chosen evidence that electrostatics is not only a laboratory chapter — the same force that moves a test charge in the theory lesson steers ink onto this page and paint onto a car door.

## The key ideas

- Inkjet drops are given a measured charge that encodes where they must land
- Deflecting plates steer each drop in proportion to its charge; uncharged drops are recycled
- Spray-paint droplets are charged and the workpiece earthed, so the field carries paint to all faces
- Charged droplets repel each other into a fine, even cloud
- Both applications place matter with the force F = qE on a charged parcel
