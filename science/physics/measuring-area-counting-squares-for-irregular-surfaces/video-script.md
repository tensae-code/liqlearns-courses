# Video script — Measuring area — counting squares for irregular surfaces

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **how big is a surface?** You measure it by covering it with squares and counting. A rectangle is easy — the squares stand in rows, so length times width does the counting for you. But a leaf has no rows. So Greece's lab counts honestly: every whole square is one, and the squares the border cuts through are counted in pairs, or as halves — the slivers you over-count cancel the slivers you miss. Count twice, by two routes, and average. That mean, with its square unit, is the area. Even a hand. Even a country on a map.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
