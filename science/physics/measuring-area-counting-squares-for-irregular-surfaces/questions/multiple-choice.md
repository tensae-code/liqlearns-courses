# Measuring area — counting squares for irregular surfaces — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Area is fundamentally…
a) A length  b) A count of unit squares covering the surface  c) A perimeter  d) A volume
**Answer: b) A count of unit squares covering the surface**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A rectangle 5 cm by 3 cm covers how many 1 cm² squares?
a) 8  b) 16  c) 15  d) 30
**Answer: c) 15**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A border square with more than half its area inside the outline is counted, in the more-than-half rule, as…
a) Zero  b) One  c) Two  d) A quarter
**Answer: b) One**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Part squares over-counted and under-counted along a border…
a) Always add up  b) Roughly cancel each other  c) Must be ignored completely  d) Double the area
**Answer: b) Roughly cancel each other**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Two counting routes give 46 cm² and 50 cm². The reported area is…
a) 46 cm²  b) 50 cm²  c) The mean, 48 cm², with the spread showing the uncertainty  d) 96 cm²
**Answer: c) The mean, 48 cm², with the spread showing the uncertainty**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) Using finer (millimetre) squared paper improves the measurement because…
a) It changes the shape  b) The border slivers left over are smaller  c) It is cheaper  d) Squares become rectangles
**Answer: b) The border slivers left over are smaller**
