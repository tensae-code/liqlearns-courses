# Measuring area — counting squares for irregular surfaces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain why length × width gives an area, in the language of counting squares.
**Answer: The rectangle covers a grid of unit squares standing in rows: width squares in each row and length rows of them. Multiplying counts the whole grid at once — the rule is the count, collapsed.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A pupil counts only the whole squares inside a leaf and reports that as its area. What is wrong?
**Answer: The count leaves out every part square along the border, so it under-measures — it is a lower bound, not the area. The border squares must be estimated in (pairs, halves, or the more-than-half rule).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State the Greek lab habit this lesson shares with its length and time measurements.
**Answer: Never trust a single reading: repeat by a second route or a second count and report the mean value, with the spread between counts showing the uncertainty.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) One count of a footprint gives 61 cm² counting part squares as halves, and 59 cm² by the more-than-half rule. Report the area.
**Answer: Mean = (61 + 59)/2 = 60 cm², with a spread of about ±1 cm² between the routes — reported as 60 cm².**
