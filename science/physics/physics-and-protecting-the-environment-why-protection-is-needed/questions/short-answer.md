# Physics and protecting the environment — why protection is needed — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does scale matter — humans have burned wood for thousands of years without wrecking the climate?
**Answer: Because the cycles could absorb the old scale of output: today's burning releases fossil carbon millions of years in the making at a rate the oceans and forests cannot match, so the excess accumulates — it is the rate and amount, not the act, that overwhelms the system.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Name one environmental change that is measured, with the quantity used to state it.
**Answer: Rising atmospheric carbon dioxide, stated as a concentration in parts per million (or global average temperature change in degrees, or radiation dose in sieverts).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Trace one pollutant in your area back to an energy conversion.
**Answer: For example: motorbike exhaust gases come from burning petrol in an engine — the conversion of chemical energy to kinetic energy, whose necessary by-products are the exhaust gases and waste heat.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does Vietnam's course put this lesson before the pollutants and renewable-energy lessons?
**Answer: It establishes the need and the method first — what is happening, at what scale, and how physics judges it — so the later catalogues of pollutants and technologies are read as answers to a stated problem, not as lists.**
