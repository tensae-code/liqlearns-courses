# Lesson plan — Physics and protecting the environment — why protection is needed

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Physics and protecting the environment — why protection is needed
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — the opener of Vietnam's Grade 10 study-topics Topic 3, Physics with environmental-protection education: the topic's framing lesson, before pollutants and renewable energy
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the environment needs protecting because human activity — above all the production and use of energy, which is physics' own subject — now changes air, water, land and climate at a scale the natural cycles cannot absorb; protection is needed not as decoration on the science but as a consequence of it: the same laws that let us release energy govern where its waste heat, gases and materials must go
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **The environment we live inside — and what is happening to it** — The environment is not scenery: it is the **air** breathed, the **water** drunk, the **land** farmed and the **climate** that sets the seasons — a system of cycles (water, carbon, nutrients) that recycles everything nature produces. What has changed is **scale**: human population and industry now move matter and energy in amounts comparable to the cycles themselves — burning in a year fossil carbon that took millions of years to lay down, spreading materials (plastics, fertilisers, industrial chemicals) that the cycles have no route to absorb. The results are measured, not guessed: rising greenhouse-gas concentrations, warming, polluted air and water, land degraded and species lost.
2. **Why this is a physics question** — Nearly every driver of the damage runs through physics: **energy** — its production (burning, fission, falling water), conversion (engines, motors, heaters) and waste (every conversion sheds heat; combustion sheds gases); **radiation** — its uses and its leaks; **materials** — how long they persist and where they travel. A physicist's quantities are the debate's quantities: concentrations in parts per million, energy in joules and kilowatt-hours, temperature change in degrees, dose in sieverts. Protection therefore begins as a physics habit: **measure** the change, **trace** it to its source process, and **judge** the source by its numbers.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the environment as a system of cycles and state what has changed in scale.
2. Name the main measured signs that protection is needed.
3. Explain why environmental protection is partly a physics question, naming energy, radiation and materials.
4. State what physics can and cannot decide in the protection debate.

## What you need
No equipment — the hands-on is a home energy-and-waste audit on paper.
