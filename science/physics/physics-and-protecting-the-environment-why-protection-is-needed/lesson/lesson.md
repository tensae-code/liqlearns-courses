# Physics and protecting the environment — why protection is needed

## Do it first
Before you read: list everything your home threw away or burned in the last day — packaging, food scraps, exhaust from a journey, ash, wastewater. For each item ask: where does it go next, and does anything in nature's cycles take it back? Some items vanish into cycles; some accumulate. That difference is this lesson's whole subject.

## Sub lesson 1 — The environment we live inside — and what is happening to it

Vietnam's study-topics course opens its environment topic with exactly this framing lesson (Bài 7 of the Grade 10 topics book): before any pollutant is named or any clean technology surveyed, the learner is asked to see **why protection is needed at all** — the topic's thesis, stated first.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why this is a physics question

That is also the honest limit of the science: physics can state what is happening and what each remedy would do — it cannot by itself choose for society. Vietnam's topic structure honours exactly that division: this lesson establishes the need, the next lessons name the pollutants and survey the renewable technologies, and the choosing is left where it belongs — with informed people.

## The key ideas

- The environment is the working system of air, water, land and climate — not scenery; human energy use now rivals the natural cycles in scale
- The core driver is energy: producing and converting it sheds waste heat, gases and persistent materials that cycles cannot absorb
- Physics' role is to measure the change, trace it to its source process, and judge remedies by their numbers
- Protection is a consequence of the science, not an ornament on it — the lesson that opens Vietnam's environment topic

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: The environment needs protecting because human activity — above all the production and use of energy, which is physics' own subject — now changes air, water, land and climate at a scale the natural cycles cannot absorb; protection is needed not as decoration on the science but as a consequence of it: the same laws that let us release energy govern where its waste heat, gases and materials must go. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Give two measured (not slogan) signs that the environment is changing under human pressure.
2. Pick one pollutant source in your town. Trace it back to the energy conversion that produces it.
3. A speaker says 'protecting the environment is politics, not physics.' Answer in two sentences, one for what physics contributes and one for what it cannot decide.
