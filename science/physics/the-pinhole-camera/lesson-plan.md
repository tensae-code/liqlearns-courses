# Lesson plan — The pinhole camera

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The pinhole camera
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the light lessons — Light travels in straight lines, Things that block light and shadows and Laws of reflection: this is the lesson where straight-line travel alone, with no lens and no mirror, makes a picture
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a tiny hole in a dark box projects an inverted image of a bright scene onto a screen, because light travels in straight lines and the rays cross at the hole — and the image's size is set by magnification = image distance ÷ object distance, while the hole's size trades sharpness against brightness
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Light travels in straight lines and Things that block light and shadows are recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **A hole that makes a picture** — Stand a screen inside a dark box and let light from a bright scene reach it only through one tiny hole in the opposite side: a picture of the scene appears on the screen — upside down and left-for-right reversed. No lens does this. Light travels in straight lines, so the ray from the top of the object passes straight through the hole and lands low on the screen, and the ray from the bottom lands high: the crossing at the hole is what turns the image over. Every point of the object sends one such ray through the hole to one point of the screen, which is why the picture is a picture at all.
2. **Sharpness, brightness and the size of the hole** — The hole's size trades two things against each other. A smaller hole lets through a thinner bundle of rays from each object point, so the spots on the screen are smaller and the image is sharper — but less light gets through, so the image is dimmer. A bigger hole gives a brighter image whose spots overlap, so it looks blurred. Moving the screen farther from the hole makes the image bigger and dimmer; bringing it closer makes the image smaller and brighter. There is no best hole — only the balance a maker chooses.
3. **Magnification — how big the picture is** — Magnification compares the picture with the thing pictured: magnification = image height ÷ object height, and the same number falls out of the distances — magnification = distance from hole to screen ÷ distance from hole to object. So a tree 10 m from the hole whose screen sits 0.20 m behind the hole is pictured at magnification 0.20 ÷ 10 = 0.02: an 8 m tree makes an image 0.16 m tall, upside down. Slide the screen back and the magnification, and the image, grow in proportion.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Light travels in straight lines and Things that block light and shadows**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A pinhole camera forms an inverted image because straight rays from the object cross at the hole — top lands low, bottom lands high.
2. Say, in their own words: A smaller hole gives a sharper but dimmer image; a larger hole gives a brighter but blurred image.
3. Work magnification both ways: m = image height ÷ object height = image distance ÷ object distance.
4. Predict what happens to the image when the screen is moved farther from the hole.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a lens must be hiding somewhere → cover the hole with a finger: the whole image vanishes at once, though a lens image would only be blocked in part. One hole, no lens — straight lines do all the work.
