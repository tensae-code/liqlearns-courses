# Hands-on — The pinhole camera

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Build the camera
Make a box light-tight (tape the seams), prick one clean small hole in the centre of one end with a pin, and tape tracing paper across the open other end as the screen. In a dim room, point the hole at a bright window. Move the box until the inverted picture of the window is clear on the paper. Say aloud: straight rays crossed at the hole — that crossing is why the picture is upside down.

## Hole size and screen distance
Widen the hole a little with the pin and look again: the picture brightens and blurs. Cover it and prick a fresh smaller hole in foil taped over the end: sharper, dimmer. Then slide the screen (or the box's back) farther from the hole and watch the picture grow. Say aloud the two trades: hole size trades sharpness against brightness; screen distance trades size against brightness.
