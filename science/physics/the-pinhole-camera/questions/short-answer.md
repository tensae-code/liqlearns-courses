# The pinhole camera — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A friend says the image must be made by a lens hidden in the box. Give the one-finger test that proves there is no lens at work.
**Answer: Cover the hole with a finger: the whole image vanishes at once. A lens forms its image from light gathered over its whole surface, so blocking part of it would only dim part of the picture; here one tiny hole carries the whole image, so straight-line travel through the hole does all the work.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a bigger hole blur the image instead of just brightening it?
**Answer: Each point of the object sends a bundle of rays through the hole; the wider the hole, the wider the bundle and the bigger the spot each point makes on the screen. Neighbouring points' spots overlap, and that overlap is the blur — the extra light that brightens the image is the same light that blurs it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) An object 4 m from the hole is 1.5 m tall and its screen is 0.20 m behind the hole. Find the image height.
**Answer: Magnification = 0.20 ÷ 4 = 0.05, so image height = 0.05 × 1.5 m = 0.075 m (7.5 cm), inverted.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State two ways to make a pinhole image bigger, and what each costs.
**Answer: Move the screen farther from the hole, or bring the object closer to the hole; both raise the magnification, and both make the image dimmer because the same light is spread over a bigger picture (moving the screen) or the geometry spreads the rays wider.**
