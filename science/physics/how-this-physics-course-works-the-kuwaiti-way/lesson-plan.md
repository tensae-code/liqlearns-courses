**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Start from the objectives** — Every unit opens with its **objectives** — a short list of what you will be able to do when the unit ends: name, explain, calculate, compare. Read them first and read them as promises you can test. As you work through the unit, tick each objective off only when you can actually do it without looking — an objective you cannot yet do tells you exactly which lesson to revisit. The objectives are the unit's own exam of itself, printed before the teaching starts.
2. **Discover it yourself — try before you are told** — Each unit carries a **"discover it yourself" inquiry box**: a small activity or puzzling observation — a telescope picture, a wind turbine's numbers, a falling object — that you meet *before* any explanation. Do it, or picture it honestly, and write what you think will happen. The lessons that follow are then the story of that try: when the explanation arrives you already have a result of your own to compare it with, and a wrong first guess is not a failure — it is the thing the lesson corrects, which is why you remember the correction.
3. **The sidebars — Physics in the lab, Physics and technology** — Down the side of the unit run two kinds of sidebar. **"Physics in the lab"** points to the practical work that belongs with the lesson — the experiment that proves or measures the idea. **"Physics and technology"** points outward, to where the same idea works in the world: physics in medicine, wind turbines, solar cells and satellites in the very first unit. A closing sustainable-development feature and the unit review questions end the unit: do the review only after the objectives are ticked, and use its misses to choose what to re-read.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first and do the opening activity with real objects or numbers before reading any explanation; the explanation is the story of that activity.
- Mixes the sub lessons up → re-read the key ideas in order; each key idea is one sub lesson compressed to a line.
