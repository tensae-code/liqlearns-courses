# Lesson plan — Mirage and optical phenomena in daily life

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Mirage and optical phenomena in daily life
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the light strand — beside refraction and total internal reflection
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a mirage is refraction at work in air itself: hot air near the ground is less dense, light from the sky bends gradually as it crosses the layers, and the traveller sees the sky's light arriving from below — reading it as water on the road. ## Sub lessons
1. **Air in layers — density changes with temperature** — Air hugging sun-heated ground is hotter and less dense than the air above it. Light crossing from denser to rarer air bends away from the normal, and crossing many thin layers it bends a little at each one — its path curves smoothly instead of kinking once. The hotter the ground, the stronger the bending.
2. **The inferior mirage — sky seen on the road** — A ray from the sky heading slightly downward curves as the layers grow rarer, until it turns upward again — total internal reflection in a gradual, layered form — and reaches the eye travelling upward. The eye projects it straight back and sees sky-light coming from the road surface: a bright, shimmering patch that looks exactly like water reflecting the sky. It is the sky, not water, and it retreats as you approach because the geometry moves with you.
3. **Other daily phenomena from the same bending** — The same layered refraction shimmers the air above a stove or a hot roof, makes stars twinkle as their light crosses moving air, lifts or sinks the Sun's image at sunset, and — with the temperature gradient reversed over cold water — can raise a superior mirage, an image floating above the horizon. Each is one rule — light bends toward denser air — wearing different weather.

## What the learner can do when it ends

2. A mirage is sky light curved up into the eye and projected onto the ground — it looks like water

4. Shimmering air, twinkling stars and the flattened setting Sun are the same bending
5. Over cold surfaces the gradient reverses and a superior mirage can float above the horizon
