# Lesson plan — Quantum physics foundations

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Quantum physics foundations
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the modern-physics lessons — Inside the nucleus, Photons and the photoelectric effect and Special relativity — basic principles: this is the course's foundations lesson for the whole quantum picture, the one the photoelectric-effect lesson grows from
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the quantum world began when Planck quantised energy to fit a black body's glow — E = hf — and everything since confirms the packets: photons freeing electrons one-for-one in the photoelectric effect, photons carrying momentum in Compton scattering, matter itself behaving as waves, and the uncertainty principle Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π setting nature's own limit on what can be known at once
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. The electromagnetic spectrum and Photons and the photoelectric effect are recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Black-body radiation and quantisation** — the heated body's humped glow-spectrum that classical physics missed entirely (the ultraviolet catastrophe), and Planck's fix: energy exchanged only in quanta of E = hf
2. **The photoelectric effect and Compton scattering — photons with energy and momentum** — Einstein's photons freeing electrons one photon at a time above a threshold frequency, and Compton's X-ray photons recoiling off electrons like colliding balls: light carries momentum as well as energy
3. **Wave–particle duality and the uncertainty principle** — light and electrons each drawing wave patterns and each arriving as particles; de Broglie's λ = h/p for matter; Heisenberg's Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π as a law of nature that also explains why atoms hold their size — with quantum cryptography and nanomaterials as the applications the foundations already carry

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **The electromagnetic spectrum** and **Photons and the photoelectric effect**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A black body's glow defeated classical physics, and Planck's quantisation — energy in packets of E = hf — fits it.
2. Say, in their own words: The photoelectric effect shows photons are real: one photon frees one electron, and only if hf pays the work function.
3. Say, in their own words: Compton scattering shows photons carry momentum, bouncing off electrons like colliding balls.
4. Say, in their own words: Wave–particle duality means quantum things show the side the experiment asks about; the uncertainty principle limits position and momentum together, by nature, not by instruments.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the iron bar's colour changes with heat, and the old maths said it should flood the room with ultraviolet. Hold that puzzle; the lesson is its solution.
- Thinks quantisation is just "small pieces" → the packet size is set by frequency, E = hf: that is why dim ultraviolet beats bright red light at freeing electrons — size of packet, not number of packets, pays the price.
- Thinks uncertainty means bad instruments → squeeze a marble's position and its momentum is still exactly knowable; squeeze an electron's and nature itself spreads the momentum — the limit is in the thing, not the tool.
