# Quantum physics foundations — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a hotter black body glow with its peak at a shorter wavelength, and why does the ultraviolet not run away?
**Answer: Higher temperature means more energy available per packet; since high-frequency light comes only in big quanta of E = hf, the peak shifts shorter as the body can afford bigger packets — but the packet price is exactly what stops any runaway: frequencies whose quanta the body cannot pay for are scarcely emitted.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Photons of 4.5 eV strike a metal of work function 2.0 eV. Find the fastest ejected electrons' kinetic energy, and state what changes if the light is made twice as bright.
**Answer: KE_max = hf − φ = 4.5 − 2.0 = 2.5 eV. Twice the brightness sends twice as many photons, so twice as many electrons are ejected — at the same maximum speed.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Describe Compton's experiment and what it settled.
**Answer: Compton scattered X-ray photons off electrons and found the photons recoiling with longer wavelength — less energy — exactly as colliding balls would, the electron taking the difference. It settled that photons carry momentum: light behaves as particles in collisions.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Use the uncertainty principle to explain why an electron does not fall into the nucleus and sit there.
**Answer: An electron pinned to the nucleus would have an almost exactly known position, so its momentum would have to spread enormously — giving it far more kinetic energy than staying in the atom costs. The atom's size is the compromise: confined enough to be bound, loose enough to keep the momentum spread, and energy, down.**
