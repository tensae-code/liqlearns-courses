## Sub lessons
1. **The junction rule — charge is conserved** — At any junction, current cannot pile up or vanish: the sum of currents entering equals the sum leaving. If 3 A and 2 A flow into a junction and one branch carries 4 A out, the remaining branch carries exactly 1 A out. This is Kirchhoff's first law, and it is charge conservation wearing a circuit's clothes. It also fixes directions: assume a direction for every branch current, and a negative answer at the end simply means the current really runs the other way.
2. **The loop rule — energy is conserved** — Walk round any closed loop and the books must balance: rises across sources (from − to + inside a source, +E) and drops across resistors (in the current's direction, −IR) sum to zero. This is Kirchhoff's second law — energy conservation per coulomb. The EMF strand it stands on — a source's EMF, its internal resistance and its terminal voltage — is stocked in the lesson EMF, internal resistance and terminal voltage; this lesson takes the loop rule itself as its tool. Sign discipline is the whole craft: pick a walk direction, give every gain and drop its sign by the rule, and never change your mind mid-loop.
3. **Solving a branching circuit** — The method: label every branch current (junction rule cuts the unknowns down), write one loop equation for each independent loop, solve the simultaneous equations, then check — junction sums at every node, loop sums round a loop you did not use. Worked shape: a 12 V source across two parallel branches of 2 Ω and 4 Ω gives branch currents 6 A and 3 A by the junction-and-loop pair, total 9 A from the source. When a second source enters a loop, its EMF enters with its sign — that is the case series–parallel rules could never touch, and Kirchhoff's laws close it.
