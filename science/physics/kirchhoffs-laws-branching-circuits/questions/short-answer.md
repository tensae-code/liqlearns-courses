# Kirchhoff's laws — branching circuits — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can charge not pile up at a junction?
**Answer: Charge is conserved and a junction stores none — every coulomb arriving must leave, so the entering and leaving currents balance at every instant.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A loop holds a 6 V source and drops of 2 V and 4 V across two resistors. Show the loop rule holds.
**Answer: +6 − 2 − 4 = 0 — gains and drops balance, as the loop rule requires.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do series–parallel rules fail on a circuit with two sources in different branches?
**Answer: The branches share junctions and loops with currents fixed by both sources at once; no single equivalent resistance exists — Kirchhoff's simultaneous equations are needed.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) In the worked parallel case (12 V; 2 Ω and 4 Ω), what total current leaves the source, and which rule gives it?
**Answer: 9 A — the junction rule: 6 A + 3 A recombine to 9 A in the main line.**
