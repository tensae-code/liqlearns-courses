# Apparent weight — overweight and weightlessness — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in one or two sentences, why a scale can read more than mg without your true weight changing.
**Answer: The scale reads the floor's push N. To accelerate you upward the floor must push harder than gravity pulls, N = m(g + a), so the reading grows while mg is unchanged.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A 50 kg passenger (g = 10 m/s²) is in a lift accelerating downward at 3 m/s². Find N and the scale reading.
**Answer: N = m(g − a) = 50 × 7 = 350 N; the scale reads 35 kg.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A parachutist in free fall before opening the parachute feels weightless, yet gravity is “on”. Reconcile the two facts.
**Answer: Weightlessness is the absence of a supporting push, not of gravity: with a = g there is nothing to push back, N = 0, while gravity supplies the whole acceleration.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A rocket accelerates upward at a = 2g. A 70 kg astronaut's scale (seat) force is how many times her still weight?
**Answer: N = m(g + 2g) = 3mg — three times.**
