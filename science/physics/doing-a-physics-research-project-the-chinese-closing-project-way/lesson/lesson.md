# Doing a physics research project — the Chinese closing-project way

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one thing near your home you have always wondered about — why the kettle lid rattles, which stair creaks loudest, how far a paper plane flies from the balcony. Say it as a question a measurement could answer. That question, shaped and investigated, is a research project — and it is how every senior physics book in China ends.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing a question worth investigating

A research project begins with a question that measurement can answer: not “about friction” but “how does the stopping distance of a bicycle grow with speed?” The PEP closing unit shows the shape with a worked sample project first — one real investigation carried from question to conclusion — and then offers a topic list for students who are stuck. A good project question is small, safe, and testable with the instruments at hand: it names what will be changed, what will be measured, and what will be kept the same.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planning and measuring — evidence before opinion

The plan comes before the apparatus: list the variables, choose the measuring instruments and their ranges, decide how many repeats each measurement gets, and design the results table before the first reading is taken. Then measure honestly — record what the instruments say, including the awkward readings, repeat the doubtful ones, and keep the raw numbers. The project's physics is only as good as this evidence: a conclusion the table cannot support is not a conclusion, however beautiful it sounds.

## Sub lesson 3 — Arguing, presenting and judging the work

The last part turns measurements into knowledge: plot the graph, state the pattern in words, compare it with the physics the course has taught, name the biggest source of error honestly, and say what you would do better. Then present it — poster, talk or report — and answer questions from the class. That closing ritual is the point of the Chinese way: every senior volume, from Compulsory 1 to Selective Compulsory 3, ends by sending its students out to do physics, not only to read it.

## The key ideas

- Every PEP senior volume closes with a research project unit (课题研究), with a worked sample project and suggested topics
- A project question must be small, safe and testable: what changes, what is measured, what is kept the same
- Plan first — variables, instruments, repeats, results table — then measure honestly and keep the raw readings
- Finish by arguing from the evidence: graph, pattern, comparison with taught physics, honest errors, presentation

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: every PEP senior physics volume in China ends the same way: not with another chapter to read, but with a **research project (课题研究)** — the students choose a real question, plan how to investigate it, measure, argue from their own evidence, and present what they found; the book even carries a worked sample project and a list of suggested topics to start from. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Turn “pendulums” into a project question a measurement can answer.
2. A team measures a trolley's speed once, gets a strange value, and quietly deletes it before writing the table. What does the Chinese project way say to do instead?
3. Name the four moves of the closing part of a project, in order.
