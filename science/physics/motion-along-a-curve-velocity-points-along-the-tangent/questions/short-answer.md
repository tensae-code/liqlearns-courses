# Motion along a curve — velocity points along the tangent — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A hammer thrower releases the hammer at the top of its circle, where the tangent points toward the field. Describe the flight's first direction and why it is not toward the circle's centre.
**Answer: It leaves toward the field along the tangent, because velocity on a curve is tangential; the centre was the direction of the pull (the force), not of the velocity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A satellite's velocity and the gravity on it: state each direction, and what each does.
**Answer: Velocity along the tangent to its orbit; gravity across it (inward) — curving the path continuously without (for a circular orbit) changing the speed.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A ball rolls straight, then enters a region where a steady sideways push acts. Describe the path and justify it with the component rule.
**Answer: It curves toward the push: the force is purely across the velocity at first, so direction changes while speed (at first) does not — a curved path is the signature of an across-component.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the book teach tangents before the projectile formulas?
**Answer: Curved motion is first a geometry of directions — knowing where velocity points at each instant is what the component-splitting of the next lessons is built on.**
