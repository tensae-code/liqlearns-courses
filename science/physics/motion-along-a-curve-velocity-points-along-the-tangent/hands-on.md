# Hands-on — Motion along a curve — velocity points along the tangent

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Tangents you can throw
(1) String and soft ball: whirl a soft ball on a string above a clear floor, release at a chosen moment, and mark where it lands relative to the circle — repeat from different release points and sketch each straight flight line against the circle; every line should sit as a tangent. (2) Coin on a curve: roll a coin so it curves across a table (a gentle sideways brush as it starts) and track its path with chalk dots every half-second — draw the direction arrows at three dots and check they lie along tangents of the chalk curve. (3) Sparks study: watch (from a photo or a teacher demonstration, never your own grinder work) the spray from a grinding wheel and note its straight, tangential fans. Record one sentence: what the release direction proves about where velocity points on a curve.
