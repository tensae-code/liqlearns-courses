# Gas pressure — pressure from molecular collisions — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the rain of molecular hits feel like a steady push rather than separate taps?
**Answer:** The hits are so numerous — billions every second on every small area — that they merge into a constant average force; no instrument (and no hand) can follow them one by one.

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) A balloon is taken up a high mountain and swells. Explain with collisions.
**Answer:** Outside air pressure falls with altitude while the inside collisions keep their rate at first, so the inside push wins and stretches the balloon until the two balance again.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Two identical bottles of gas stand in sun and shade. Which has the higher pressure and why?
**Answer:** The sunlit one: its particles move faster, striking the walls harder and more often, so the same volume of gas presses more.

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the difference between how a gas and a liquid fill a container, in particle terms?
**Answer:** Liquid particles stay close and slide, so a liquid keeps its volume and pools at the bottom; gas particles fly free, so a gas spreads through the whole volume and presses on every wall.
