# Lesson plan — Gas pressure — pressure from molecular collisions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Gas pressure — pressure from molecular collisions
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the pressure strand — Armenia's Grade 7 §41 gives gas pressure its own paragraph, explained molecularly, before Pascal's law
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a gas pushes on its container because its particles never stop flying: billions of molecular collisions with the walls every second add up to the steady push we call gas pressure — squeeze the gas or heat it and the collisions answer at once

## Sub lessons
1. **Gas particles never rest** — The Grade 7 book gives gas pressure a paragraph of its own because a gas is not a quiet filling: its molecules fly freely in every direction at hundreds of metres per second, crossing the container and striking its walls again and again. A solid holds its shape and a liquid holds its volume, but a gas's particles own the whole space they are given — and they keep hitting its boundaries the whole time.
2. **Pressure is the collisions, added up** — One molecular hit is nothing; the pressure we feel is the sum of an uncountable rain of hits on every square centimetre of wall, so steady that it reads as a smooth push. That is what pressure means for a gas: force spread over area, delivered by collisions. It pushes in all directions at once — up on the lid as much as down on the base — because the particles fly in all directions.
3. **Squeeze it or heat it — the collisions answer** — Halve the volume and the same particles cross a smaller space, hitting the walls more often: pressure rises. Heat the gas and the particles fly faster, hitting harder and more often: pressure rises again. Cool it or let it spread and the pressure falls. Every later gas law in the Armenian course — Boyle, Gay-Lussac, Charles — is this one collision picture with numbers put on it.
