# Coulomb's law — how strong the charge force is

## Do it first
Bring two rubbed balloons near each other and watch them push apart; move them slowly closer and feel the push grow — Coulomb's law tightening with distance, in your hands.

## Coulomb's law — how strong the charge force is — the teaching
Coulomb's law measures the force between two point charges: F = kq₁q₂/r². The force grows with the product of the charges and falls with the square of their separation — double the distance and the force drops to a quarter. Like charges repel, unlike charges attract; the law gives the force's size, the signs give its direction. It is the electrostatic twin of the gravity law's shape: a product on top, a square below.

**Keep the formula:** F = kq₁q₂ / r²

## The key ideas

- F = kq₁q₂/r² — force grows with charge product, falls with distance squared.
- Double the distance → force falls to a quarter.
- Like charges repel; unlike charges attract.
