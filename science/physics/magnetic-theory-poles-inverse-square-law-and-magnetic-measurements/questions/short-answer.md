# Magnetic theory — poles, inverse-square law and magnetic measurements — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Two poles, 2 units and 3 units, are 1 m apart (force F). State the force if both pole strengths double at the same distance.
**Answer: F ∝ m₁m₂/r², so doubling each strength multiplies the force by 2 × 2 = 4: the force is 4F.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the pole model still used if isolated poles do not exist?
**Answer: Because outside a magnet the field it predicts is the field actually measured; poles are a calculating model seated at the magnet's ends, and within that domain the inverse-square mathematics gives correct fields and forces.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A magnetometer needle deflects 30° with tan 30° ≈ 0.58. What does that say about the magnet's field B?
**Answer: By the tangent law B = H tan θ ≈ 0.58 H — the magnet's field at the needle is a little over half the Earth's horizontal field there.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) How do the fields of a bar magnet's two poles combine at a point outside it?
**Answer: As vectors: each pole contributes a field along the line joining it to the point (away from north, toward south), and the resultant is their vector sum — strongest near the poles where the contributions reinforce the familiar field pattern.**
