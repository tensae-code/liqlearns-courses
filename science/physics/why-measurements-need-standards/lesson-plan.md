# Lesson plan — Why measurements need standards — the SI base quantities

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Why measurements need standards — the SI base quantities
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Physical Quantities — Grade 9 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A standard must be accessible, reliable, and give the same result anywhere.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 9 lessons before it in Unit 2 and on everyday observation; it needs nothing else to start.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A standard must be accessible, reliable, and give the same result anywhere.
2. The seven SI base quantities are length, mass, time, temperature, current, amount of substance and luminous intensity.
3. The second is defined by caesium-133 vibrations; the metre by light travel time.
4. Answer the lesson's questions, including any calculation, with working shown.
