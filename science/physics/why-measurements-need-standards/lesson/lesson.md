## Why measurements need standards — the SI base quantities — the teaching
Local and traditional measures vary from place to place: six pupils in different parts of Ethiopia measuring the same object with local measures get different values. So measurement needs standards — measures that are accessible, reliable, and give the same result anywhere. The world standard system is SI, with seven base quantities: length, mass, time, temperature, electric current, amount of substance and luminous intensity. The metre is defined by how far light travels in a set time, the kilogram through the Planck constant, and the second by caesium-133 vibrations.

## The key ideas

- A standard must be accessible, reliable, and give the same result anywhere.
- The seven SI base quantities are length, mass, time, temperature, current, amount of substance and luminous intensity.
- The second is defined by caesium-133 vibrations; the metre by light travel time.
