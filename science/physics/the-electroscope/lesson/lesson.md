## The electroscope — seeing charge — the teaching
An electroscope is a sensitive charge detector: a metal rod ending in two light leaves (or a needle) inside a case. Bring charge to its cap — or touch it with a charged body — and both leaves receive the same kind of charge, repel each other, and diverge. The wider the spread, the more the charge. Used carefully, it not only detects charge but helps identify its kind. The book sets you to design and build one from locally available materials — a jar, wire and foil will do.

## The key ideas

- An electroscope detects charge by its leaves diverging.
- Both leaves get the same charge and repel each other apart.
- Wider divergence means more charge.

## Say it to your companion

Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
