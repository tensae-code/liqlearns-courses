# Friction — the grip that resists

## Do it first
Slide a book across a rough mat and then across a smooth table or tray. The same book, two journeys — the difference is friction.

[Picture 21831 — shoe gripping rough ground vs sliding on a smooth floor; car stuck in mud]

## Friction — the grip that resists — the teaching
Friction is the surface resistance that opposes motion — or attempted motion — where two surfaces touch. It grows with roughness and shrinks on smooth, slippery surfaces. Friction is both friend and cost: it lets you walk without slipping and gives vehicles their grip, yet it wears parts down and can mire a car in mud, where the wheels find too little useful grip to bite with.

## The key ideas

- Friction opposes motion where surfaces touch.
- Rougher surfaces give more friction; smooth ones less.
- Friction is useful (walking, grip) and costly (wear, stuck in mud).

## Say it to your companion
Explain friction — the grip that resists to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
