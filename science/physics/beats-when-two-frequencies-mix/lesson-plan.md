# Lesson plan — Beats — when two frequencies mix

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Beats — when two frequencies mix
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the waves strand — PECTAA Grade 11's interference of two close tones
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** play two tones of slightly different frequencies together and the sound swells and fades in a slow rhythm — beats — at a rate equal to the difference of the frequencies, f_beat = |f₁ − f₂|, the effect tuners use to bring instruments into tune

## Sub lessons
1. **Two tones, one pulsing sound** — Superpose two waves of close frequencies and their sum alternates between agreement (loud) and opposition (quiet) as the faster wave gains on the slower. The ear hears one tone of the average frequency whose loudness pulses — the beats — once for every cycle the faster wave gains.
2. **The beat frequency is the difference** — Because the gain of one full cycle takes 1/|f₁ − f₂| seconds, the pulsing repeats at f_beat = |f₁ − f₂|: 256 Hz against 254 Hz gives 2 beats per second; identical frequencies give none. The formula turns a heard rhythm into a measured frequency difference — precision from counting swells.
3. **Tuning by beats — the musician's instrument** — A tuner sounds a reference and the instrument together and listens: beats mean out of tune, and their slowing tracks the approach to unison; when the beats vanish, the frequencies match. PECTAA's application is exact — beats in musical instruments are not a curiosity but the working tool of everyone who tunes by ear.
