# How this Physics course works — the Iranian two-branch way

## Do it first
Before you read: imagine a country where every pupil takes physics, but the class splits in two — one group gets the full core course, the other gets the same core plus whole extra chapters. How would you organise the books so neither group needs a different course? Keep the question: what is shared, what is extra, and how does a learner know how to work a problem?

## Sub lesson 1 — One national course, two branches

Every Iranian pupil studies the same national Physics course — but from Grade 10 the course splits into two branches. The **Experimental Sciences** book and the **Mathematical Sciences** (Mathematics-and-Physics) book share one core: chapter for chapter, the Mathematical book contains everything the Experimental book teaches. On top of the core, the Mathematical branch adds whole territories the Experimental branch never meets: **thermodynamics at Grade 10, free fall, uniform circular motion, wave diffraction, and nuclear fission and fusion at Grade 12**. One course, two depths — and the branch difference is written into the books themselves, not into separate syllabuses.

## Sub lesson 2 — The order of the journey, and the four-step method

The journey is deliberately ordered: physics begins at Grade 10 with **measurement and modelling**, **electricity and magnetism fill Grade 11**, and mechanics returns — with waves and modern physics (photoelectric effect, the Bohr atom, the laser, core for BOTH branches) — at Grade 12. Every book opens with the same apparatus: a word to students, a word to teachers, and a printed **four-step problem-solving method — identify the concepts, set up the problem, solve it, then evaluate the answer**. Chapter openers are anchored in Iranian life and craft: measurement through Iranian architecture, capacitors through the touchscreen, so the physics starts from what the learner already knows.

## The key ideas

- Iran's Physics is one national course in two branches: Experimental Sciences and Mathematical Sciences, sharing one core
- The Mathematical book contains the whole core plus extra territories: thermodynamics (Grade 10), free fall, circular motion, diffraction, fission and fusion
- The order: measurement and modelling first (Grade 10), electricity and magnetism (Grade 11), then mechanics, waves and modern physics (Grade 12) — modern physics is core for both branches
- Every book teaches a printed four-step problem-solving method: identify the concepts → set up → solve → evaluate
