# Lesson plan — Fields — interacting objects and field characteristics

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Fields — interacting objects and field characteristics
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the United States placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** objects interact at a distance through **fields** — a source sets up a field in the space around it and a second object feels the field's force where it sits — and a field is fully characterised by its **strength** (force per unit visitor: g = F/m in N/kg, E = F/q in N/C), its **direction**, and its **pattern** of field lines (pointing the push's way, never crossing, crowding where strong); gravity only attracts and cannot be screened, electric fields attract and repel and cancel inside a conductor, and overlapping fields **superpose** as vectors
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Interacting through a field** — A source object sets up a field in the space around it (gravity from mass, electric field from charge, magnetic field from magnets and currents); a second object interacts by sitting in the field and feeling its force. Changes in a field travel outward at the speed of light.
2. **Field characteristics — strength, direction, pattern** — Strength is force per unit visitor: g = F/m in N/kg, E = F/q in N/C, falling with distance from a point source by the inverse square. Field lines give the pattern: they point the way a visitor is pushed, never cross, and crowd where the field is strong.
3. **Fields compared, combined and shut out** — Gravity only attracts and cannot be screened; electric fields attract and repel and cancel to zero inside a hollow conductor (Faraday cage); magnetic fields form closed loops. Overlapping fields superpose — they add as vectors at each point.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the two-step field story of an interaction and name the speed at which field changes travel.
2. Calculate g or E from a force and a mass or charge, with correct units, and read strength and direction off a field-line pattern.
3. Contrast gravity, electric and magnetic fields — attraction/repulsion, screening, pattern — and add two fields as vectors.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
