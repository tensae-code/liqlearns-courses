# Non-inertial frames and apparent forces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does no experiment inside a smoothly cruising train reveal its speed?
**Answer: Because the train is an inertial frame: the laws of physics take the same form in every inertial frame (Galilean relativity), so uniform motion is only ever relative to something outside.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A car accelerates away from traffic lights and a passenger is pressed into the seat back. Name the apparent force's direction and its formula.
**Answer: Backward — opposite the car's forward acceleration — with magnitude F = m·a_frame (F_apparent = −m·a_frame).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A passenger says: “The centrifugal force threw me against the door — it is as real as gravity.” Give the two-frame reply.
**Answer: In the car (rotating) frame the centrifugal apparent force must be counted and its effects are real there. In the ground frame there is no outward force at all: the passenger's inertia carried them straight while the car turned, and the door's real inward (centripetal) push is the only contact force. The apparent force has no agent and vanishes in the inertial account.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must weather forecasters count a force that “does not exist” in the ground frame?
**Answer: Because forecasting is done in Earth's rotating frame, which is non-inertial: without the Coriolis apparent force, predicted wind paths are wrong — it deflects winds right in the northern hemisphere and left in the southern, shaping cyclones and trade winds.**
