# Non-inertial frames and apparent forces — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A frame is inertial when it…
a) Moves at constant velocity without rotating  b) Accelerates steadily  c) Rotates slowly  d) Is attached to the Earth, whatever the Earth does
**Answer: a) Moves at constant velocity without rotating**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Inside a bus that brakes hard, a standing passenger lurches forward. In the ground frame the cause is…
a) The passenger's inertia — their body keeps its speed while the bus slows  b) A forward force from the brakes  c) Gravity turning forward  d) The apparent force, acting also in the ground frame
**Answer: b) A forward force from the brakes**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) The apparent force added inside a frame accelerating at a is…
a) F = −m·a, opposite the frame's acceleration  b) F = m·g always  c) F = +m·a, along the acceleration  d) Zero — Newton's laws need no changes
**Answer: c) F = +m·a, along the acceleration**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A lift accelerating upward makes a passenger feel heavier because the apparent force points…
a) Downward, adding to the passenger's weight as felt in the lift frame  b) Sideways  c) Nowhere — apparent forces have no direction  d) Upward, adding to gravity
**Answer: d) Upward, adding to gravity**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) The push a turning car's passenger feels toward the outer door is the…
a) Real centripetal force  b) Centrifugal apparent force of the rotating frame  c) Coriolis force  d) Force of the door pulling outward
**Answer: a) Real centripetal force**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) On Earth, the Coriolis apparent force deflects moving air…
a) To the left in both hemispheres  b) To the right in both hemispheres  c) To the right in the northern hemisphere and to the left in the southern  d) Not at all — it acts only on solids
**Answer: b) To the right in both hemispheres**
