# Hands-on — Non-inertial frames and apparent forces

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Brake-test with a hanging key
Hang a key (or any small weight) from a short string tied where you can watch it — a handrail, a companion's raised hand in a slowly walking start-and-stop in a safe open space. Walk forward steadily: the key hangs straight (your frame is near-inertial). Stop suddenly: the key swings forward. Walk and turn a corner: it swings outward. Say aloud each time: which way the frame accelerated, and which way the apparent force points — opposite, every time.

## Two accounts of one slide
Put a coin on a smooth book. Slide the book forward and stop it sharply — the coin slides forward off the front edge. Write the two accounts side by side: road frame (inertia — the coin kept its speed) and book frame (an apparent forward force −m·a). Say aloud: the event is one; the forces named depend on the frame chosen.
