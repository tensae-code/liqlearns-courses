# Lesson plan — Non-inertial frames and apparent forces

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Non-inertial frames and apparent forces
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the circular-motion and Newton's-laws lessons — the frame-dependence completion of the dynamics story
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In a frame that accelerates or rotates, Newton's laws need extra **apparent forces** — the centrifugal push outward in a turning car, the Coriolis deflection on a rotating Earth — which are not pushes from any object but the bookkeeping of the frame's own acceleration
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what makes a frame inertial, and why a cruising car qualifies but a braking or turning car does not.
2. Give both accounts — inertial and non-inertial — of the braking-bus lurch and the turning-car slide.
3. Write the apparent force in an accelerating frame (−m·a_frame) and use it in a lift example.
4. Name the centrifugal and Coriolis forces and one real effect of each.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
