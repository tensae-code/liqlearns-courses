# Lesson plan — Reading a chronophotograph — velocity and acceleration from a dot record

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Reading a chronophotograph — velocity and acceleration from a dot record
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 — Physics expansion) — a new lesson built for the Mauritania placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before defining velocity and acceleration from formulas, Mauritania's Grade 12 manuals measure them: a puck gliding on an air table (or a ticker timer) stamps dots at equal time steps τ, and the record itself gives the velocity at a point from its two neighbours over 2τ, and the acceleration from the change of two such velocities over 2τ — even a friction force can be read back out of a record

## Sub lessons
1. **The dot record — motion written down** — A chronophotograph (an enregistrement) is motion turned into a row of dots: the moving point is marked at equal time intervals τ — by a spark timer on an air table, where a puck floats on air jets almost without friction, or by a ticker timer's vibrating pin. Equal times mean the spacing between dots is the speed drawn to scale: crowded dots, slow; stretched dots, fast; evenly stretched, accelerating steadily.
2. **Velocity at a point — from its two neighbours** — The velocity at dot Mᵢ is read from the dots around it: vᵢ = (distance Mᵢ₋₁Mᵢ₊₁) / (2τ) — the segment straddling the point, over the two time steps it spans. Using the neighbours, not just the previous dot, centres the measurement on the point itself. Direction comes from the record too: the velocity vector is tangent to the path at that point, drawn at a chosen scale.
3. **Acceleration — and reading friction out of a record** — Take two velocities two steps apart, draw their difference Δv, and the acceleration is a = Δv / (2τ), pointing as Δv points. Then dynamics runs backwards: from a computer-recorded motion of a puck on a real (slightly frictional) table, the constant small deceleration is measured off the record and the friction force follows, f = m·a — the 7AS-D exercises extract f = 0.8 N exactly this way. The record is not an illustration of the theory; it is where the numbers come from.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A chronophotograph stamps the moving point at equal time steps τ — spacing is speed drawn out
2. Velocity at Mᵢ = Mᵢ₋₁Mᵢ₊₁ / 2τ — from the two neighbours, tangent to the path
3. Acceleration = Δv / 2τ — from the change between two velocity vectors
4. The air table (puck on air jets) makes the record nearly friction-free
5. Run backwards, a record yields forces: friction f = m·a read from the deceleration in the dots
