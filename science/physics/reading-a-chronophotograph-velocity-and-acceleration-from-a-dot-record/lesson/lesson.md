# Reading a chronophotograph — velocity and acceleration from a dot record

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The dot record — motion written down

A chronophotograph (an enregistrement) is motion turned into a row of dots: the moving point is marked at equal time intervals τ — by a spark timer on an air table, where a puck floats on air jets almost without friction, or by a ticker timer's vibrating pin. Equal times mean the spacing between dots is the speed drawn to scale: crowded dots, slow; stretched dots, fast; evenly stretched, accelerating steadily.

## Sub lesson 2 — Velocity at a point — from its two neighbours

The velocity at dot Mᵢ is read from the dots around it: vᵢ = (distance Mᵢ₋₁Mᵢ₊₁) / (2τ) — the segment straddling the point, over the two time steps it spans. Using the neighbours, not just the previous dot, centres the measurement on the point itself. Direction comes from the record too: the velocity vector is tangent to the path at that point, drawn at a chosen scale.

## Sub lesson 3 — Acceleration — and reading friction out of a record

Take two velocities two steps apart, draw their difference Δv, and the acceleration is a = Δv / (2τ), pointing as Δv points. Then dynamics runs backwards: from a computer-recorded motion of a puck on a real (slightly frictional) table, the constant small deceleration is measured off the record and the friction force follows, f = m·a — the 7AS-D exercises extract f = 0.8 N exactly this way. The record is not an illustration of the theory; it is where the numbers come from.

## The key ideas

- A chronophotograph stamps the moving point at equal time steps τ — spacing is speed drawn out
- Velocity at Mᵢ = Mᵢ₋₁Mᵢ₊₁ / 2τ — from the two neighbours, tangent to the path
- Acceleration = Δv / 2τ — from the change between two velocity vectors
- The air table (puck on air jets) makes the record nearly friction-free
- Run backwards, a record yields forces: friction f = m·a read from the deceleration in the dots

## Do It Yourself
1. Dots are stamped every τ = 0.05 s. Around M₄, the neighbours M₃ and M₅ are 12 cm apart. What is the velocity at M₄?
2. On a record, v at one point is 0.40 m/s and two steps later (2τ = 0.1 s) it is 0.30 m/s in the same line. Find the acceleration and say what kind of motion this is.
3. A 0.20 kg puck's record shows a steady deceleration of 4 m/s² on a real table. What friction force does the record report?
