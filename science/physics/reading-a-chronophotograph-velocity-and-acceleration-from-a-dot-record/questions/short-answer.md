# Reading a chronophotograph — velocity and acceleration from a dot record — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the air table matter for the Grade 12 kinematics record?
**Answer: The puck floats on air jets, so friction is nearly removed: the record shows the motion the forces (often none, or a chosen pull) actually produce, cleanly enough that velocities and accelerations measured from the dots are trustworthy.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Dots every 0.02 s; M₂M₄ measures 8.0 cm. Find the velocity at M₃.
**Answer: v₃ = M₂M₄ / (2τ) = 0.080 m / 0.040 s = 2.0 m/s.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Two velocity vectors at points two steps apart are 1.2 m/s and 0.8 m/s in the same direction, τ = 0.05 s. Find the acceleration and interpret it.
**Answer: a = Δv/(2τ) = (0.8 − 1.2)/0.10 = −4 m/s²: a deceleration of 4 m/s² — the point is slowing steadily, as friction alone would make it.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How do the 7AS-D exercises get a friction force of 0.8 N from a position record?
**Answer: Velocities are read from successive dot spacings, the (constant) acceleration from their change; with the puck's mass known, the backwards run of the RFD gives the only horizontal force: friction f = m·a = 0.8 N for the record's numbers.**
