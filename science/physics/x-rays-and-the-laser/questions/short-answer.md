# X-rays and the laser — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What decides the penetrating power of the X-rays from a tube?
**Answer: The tube voltage: it sets the electrons' energy, and so the maximum photon energy — harder (shorter-wavelength) X-rays from a higher voltage penetrate further.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does stimulated emission need a triggering photon of one exact energy?
**Answer: The photon must match the excited atom's level difference exactly, h f = E_upper − E_lower; only then can it stimulate the fall and leave with an identical twin — that matching is what makes the copies identical.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Give one medical use of each tool and the property that suits it.
**Answer: X-rays image bones because they penetrate soft tissue but are absorbed by dense bone. Lasers perform eye and skin surgery because a collimated, coherent beam concentrates energy on one tiny, controllable spot.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must a laser beam never be pointed at an eye?
**Answer: Because the beam is collimated and concentrated, the eye's lens focuses nearly all its power onto one small retinal spot, which can be permanently damaged in an instant.**
