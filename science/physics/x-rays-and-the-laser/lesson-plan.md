# Lesson plan — X-rays and the laser

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** X-rays and the laser
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Syria placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two tools made from the atom's light — **X-rays**, very short-wavelength electromagnetic radiation produced when fast electrons strike a target, penetrating soft tissue and stopped by bone and metal; and the **laser**, light made coherent by **stimulated emission**, in which one photon clones itself through excited atoms so the whole beam travels in step, in one direction and one colour
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **X-rays — production and properties** — **X-rays** are electromagnetic waves of very short wavelength (roughly 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻¹¹ m), produced in an X-ray tube when fast electrons, accelerated through a high voltage, strike a metal target and are violently decelerated or knock inner electrons out of target atoms.
2. **The laser — stimulated emission** — A laser begins with atoms raised to excited states — a **population inversion**, more atoms up than down.
3. **Uses — and the safety that comes with them** — X-rays image bones and teeth, scan luggage, inspect welds, and — aimed and dosed — treat cancers.
