# Video script — Young's modulus — how stiff a material is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Young's modulus Y = stress ÷ strain.
It belongs to the material, not to the specimen: a thick steel wire and a thin steel wire share the same Y, because stress and strain both scale together.
It is found in a wire-stretching experiment — load a long wire in steps, measure each extension, divide stress by strain — and then it predicts: given Y, how much will any wire of that material extend under a stated load?
Copper and steel under the same load stretch by different amounts because their moduli differ.

Remember: **Y = stress ÷ strain — a property of the material, not the specimen's size.**
Remember: **Y is measured in a wire-stretching (Young) experiment.**
Remember: **Given Y, extensions of wires under load can be predicted.**
Keep the formula: **Y = stress / strain**
