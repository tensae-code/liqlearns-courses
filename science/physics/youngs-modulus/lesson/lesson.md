# Young's modulus — how stiff a material is

## Do it first
Stretch two different 'wires' — a rubber band and a string — under the same hanging mass and compare extensions. Different stiffness, felt by hand: the idea of a modulus.

## Young's modulus — how stiff a material is — the teaching
Young's modulus Y = stress ÷ strain. It belongs to the material, not to the specimen: a thick steel wire and a thin steel wire share the same Y, because stress and strain both scale together. It is found in a wire-stretching experiment — load a long wire in steps, measure each extension, divide stress by strain — and then it predicts: given Y, how much will any wire of that material extend under a stated load? Copper and steel under the same load stretch by different amounts because their moduli differ.

**Keep the formula:** Y = stress / strain

## The key ideas

- Y = stress ÷ strain — a property of the material, not the specimen's size.
- Y is measured in a wire-stretching (Young) experiment.
- Given Y, extensions of wires under load can be predicted.
