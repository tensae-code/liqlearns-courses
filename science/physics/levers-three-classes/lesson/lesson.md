# Levers — three classes

## Do it first
Make a lever from a ruler over a pencil fulcrum and lift a heavy book with one finger. Slide the fulcrum and feel the VR change.

[Picture 21842 — three lever classes with F, L, E marked in different orders]

## Levers — three classes — the teaching
A lever is a rigid bar turning about a fixed point, the fulcrum. Three points matter: fulcrum (F), load (L) and effort (E) — and the class is set by which sits in the middle. First class, fulcrum in the middle: seesaw, scissors, crowbar. Second class, load in the middle: wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener. Third class, effort in the middle: the load moves faster and farther, at the price of force. Lever ratios come from the arms: VR = effort arm ÷ load arm. Archimedes boasted that with a place to stand and a long enough lever he could move the Earth.

**Keep the formula:** VR = effort arm / load arm

## The key ideas

- The lever's class is set by which of F, L, E sits in the middle.
- First class: fulcrum middle (seesaw). Second: load middle (wheelbarrow). Third: effort middle.
- Lever VR = effort arm ÷ load arm.

## Say it to your companion
Explain levers — three classes to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
