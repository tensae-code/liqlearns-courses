# Three-part assessment — diagnostic, formative, summative, with test specifications

## Do it first
Take one topic and build its three assessments on one page: three diagnostic questions for the day before it starts; two formative checkpoints mid-topic with 'if they fail this, I will…' written beside each; and a specification grid for the end test — outcomes down the side, item numbers across. The page you have made is exactly the apparatus the Armenian guides ask for. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diagnostic — before the teaching starts

The guides' assessment chapter opens before the topic does: diagnostic assessment finds the starting point — which prerequisites are firm, which misconceptions are already in the room. Its marks do not count against anyone; its information counts for everything, because it tells the teacher where the topic must actually begin rather than where the book assumes it begins. A five-minute diagnostic can move a whole week's planning.

## Sub lesson 2 — Formative — assessment that steers

During the topic, formative assessment is the steering wheel: questions, short tasks, lab observations whose results change what happens next — re-teach this, practise that, let this group run. The guides treat it as part of teaching, not an interruption of it: evidence gathered while there is still time to act on it is the only evidence that improves the learning it measures.

## Sub lesson 3 — Summative — and the specification behind the test

After the topic, summative assessment certifies: the thematic test. The guides' discipline is the specification (the test blueprint) printed with it — a table matching items to outcomes and levels, so the test provably samples what the topic promised to teach, at the demand it promised. A sample thematic test with its specification is printed in the guides as the model: assessment designed, not assembled — and fair because its coverage can be checked before a single pupil sits it.

## The key ideas

- The guides' assessment chapter opens before the topic does: diagnostic assessment finds the starting point — which prerequisites are firm, which misconceptions are already in the room.
- During the topic, formative assessment is the steering wheel: questions, short tasks, lab observations whose results change what happens next — re-teach this, practise that, let this group run.
- After the topic, summative assessment certifies: the thematic test.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: the Armenian guides assess in three tenses: diagnostic assessment before teaching finds what the class already holds, formative assessment during teaching steers it, and summative assessment after a topic certifies it — with the summative tests built to a printed specification so the test matches the outcomes it claims to measure (teacher-guide evidence only). If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.
