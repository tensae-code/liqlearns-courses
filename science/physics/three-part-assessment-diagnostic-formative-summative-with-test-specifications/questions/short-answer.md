# Three-part assessment — diagnostic, formative, summative, with test specifications — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) A mid-topic checkpoint shows half the class cannot yet use ρ = m/V. What does formative assessment demand happens next?
**Answer:** The plan bends while the topic is still alive: re-teach or re-practise the weak point (a worked example, a lab measurement) before building higher — evidence that arrives in time to act on.

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is a test without a specification suspect, in the guides' terms?
**Answer:** Nobody can check whether it samples the outcomes the topic promised, at the promised levels — it may over-test one corner and miss another, so its certificate is unfair in a way nobody can see until the specification is demanded.

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) Diagnostic, formative, summative: give each its tense in one phrase.
**Answer:** Diagnostic — before: what do they hold? Formative — during: how is it going, and what changes? Summative — after: what has been learned, certified?

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) A teacher uses the diagnostic test's marks in the term grade. What has gone wrong?
**Answer:** Diagnosis has been turned into judgement: pupils are punished for the starting point the test existed to discover, and next time they will hide what they don't know — destroying the information the diagnostic runs on.
