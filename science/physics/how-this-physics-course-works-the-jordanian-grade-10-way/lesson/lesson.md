# How this physics course works — the Jordanian Grade 10 way

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The same NCCD pattern, one year on

The Grade 10 terms keep the Grade 9 architecture exactly: each unit opens with an introductory experiment — Term 1's vectors unit opens by finding the resultant of two forces practically, Term 2's motion work opens on the air track — followed by the general idea, lessons with the English terms glossed at first use, and the closing review and application spreads.

## Sub lesson 2 — New tools for a harder course

Grade 10 is where Jordan's course turns mathematical: vectors are drawn, added and resolved; motion is graphed and written in equations; Newton's laws are applied through free-body diagrams. The book's pattern carries the load — every new tool is first met in the introductory experiment or a worked practical before it is used in problems.

## Sub lesson 3 — STEAM enrichment in every unit

Each unit closes with a STEAM enrichment that joins the physics to design and making — in the workbook, the wave unit's enrichment builds a floating electricity-generation station. The enrichment is the course's way of saying the physics is finished only when it has been built into something.

## The key ideas

- Grade 10 keeps the Grade 9 NCCD pattern: experiment first, general idea, glossed terms, review
- The air track is Term 1's introductory instrument for motion
- Vectors, motion graphs and equations are the year's new mathematical tools
- Free-body diagrams arrive with the applications of Newton's laws
- Every unit closes with a STEAM enrichment joining physics to design
