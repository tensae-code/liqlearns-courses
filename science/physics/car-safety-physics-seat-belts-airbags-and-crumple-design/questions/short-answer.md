# Car safety physics — seat belts, airbags and crumple design — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A passenger's momentum change in a crash is 1,050 kg·m/s. Compare average forces for stopping times of 0.015 s and 0.3 s.
**Answer: F = J/Δt: 1,050 ÷ 0.015 = 70,000 N against the hard interior; 1,050 ÷ 0.3 = 3,500 N against belt and bag — the same impulse at a twentieth of the force.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the belt be worn even in a car with airbags?
**Answer: The bag does not restrain the body: without the belt the occupant keeps travelling during the crash, meets the bag (or the interior) at the wrong time and place, and loses both the belt's time-stretching and its force-spreading.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What does a head restraint add, physically?
**Answer: In a rear impact it catches the head so head and torso decelerate together, preventing the neck's whiplash stretch — the same impulse logic applied to the head's own stopping.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Summarise Jordan's reading of the whole car in one sentence.
**Answer: Sacrificial, progressively folding structure outside buys time and distance; a rigid cell inside protects the occupants; belts, bags and restraints stretch each body's impulse payment and spread its force.**
