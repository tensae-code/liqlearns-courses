# How this Physics course works — the Belarusian Grade 8 way

## Do it first
Before you read: open any paragraph of the Belarusian Grade 8 book in your mind. What three things will you always find at its end? What does the star beside an exercise mean? And what does the frontispiece tell you to do with a derivation — copy it, or reproduce it? Settle those three, and you hold the Belarusian way.

## Sub lesson 1 — The frontispiece — the method before the physics

The frontispiece — «How to work with this textbook» — teaches the study method itself, before any physics: first read the paragraph quickly, to get its shape; then read it deeply, slowly; reproduce every derivation in your own notebook, in your own hand, until the steps are yours; and repeat the book's experiments at home with what you have. The answers to the exercises are printed at the back, so the book can check you — but the method says work first, check after.

Belarus's own way, shared by the whole series: the method chapter is itself taught, not assumed — a pupil who has never met physics is first shown how a physicist reads, writes and tests.

## Sub lesson 2 — The design of every paragraph

The book is built from numbered paragraphs (§). Every paragraph ends the same way: a «Main conclusions» (Галоўныя вывады) block that compresses the paragraph to its points, then control questions to check them. The exercises are graded by difficulty, and the hardest carry a star. Material beyond the core is printed in small print and marked as optional, so the main road through the book is never blocked by an extra.

Because every paragraph has the same shape, you always know where you are: new material, then the conclusions, then the check. The shape is the same in every book of the series, Grade 7 to Grade 10.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 8 map — what the year holds

The Grade 8 year keeps the Grade 7 shape and points it at new matter: Ch.1 Thermal phenomena (§§1–12) — internal energy, heat transfer, melting, evaporation and boiling; Ch.2 Electromagnetic phenomena (§§13–34) — charge, fields, circuits, Ohm's law, magnets and electromagnets; Ch.3 Light phenomena (§§35–43) — sources, reflection, refraction, lenses and the eye; and Ch.4, the closing laboratory-experiment chapter, with a density appendix at printed p. 179 and the answers at p. 180.

## The key ideas

- The frontispiece teaches the method first: quick read, then deep read; reproduce every derivation in your notebook; repeat the experiments at home
- Every paragraph (§) ends with «Main conclusions» (Галоўныя вывады) and control questions
- Exercises are graded by difficulty and the hardest are starred; optional material is small print
- The book closes with a laboratory-experiment chapter, and the answers to the exercises are printed at the back — the Grade 8 road is Ch.1 Thermal phenomena, Ch.2 Electromagnetic phenomena, Ch.3 Light phenomena, Ch.4 Laboratory experiment

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: the Belarusian Grade 8 book teaches its method on the frontispiece — quick read, deep read, derivations reproduced in your notebook, experiments repeated at home; every § ends with Main conclusions and control questions, the hardest exercises are starred, and the year runs Ch.1 Thermal phenomena, Ch.2 Electromagnetic phenomena, Ch.3 Light phenomena, Ch.4 Laboratory experiment. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take any one paragraph idea from the Grade 8 map. Run the frontispiece method on it in order: state the quick-read version in one line, then the deep-read version in three lines.
2. A classmate copies a derivation from the book word for word and says he has studied. Name the frontispiece move he skipped, and what he should do instead.
3. You meet a starred exercise and a small-print paragraph on the same page. Which is required, which is the hardest, and which is optional? Say how you know.
