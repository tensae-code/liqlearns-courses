# The hydrogen spectral series — Lyman, Balmer, Paschen and the Rydberg formula

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Levels and falls — where lines come from

Hydrogen's electron lives on the ladder Eₙ = −13.6/n² eV: ground at n = 1, excited rungs above, freedom (ionisation) at 0. A line is born when the electron falls from a higher rung n to a lower one p, emitting one photon of exactly the difference: hν = 13.6 × (1/p² − 1/n²) eV. Absorption runs the film backwards — only a photon of exactly a rung difference can be taken in; a too-energetic photon ionises the atom and keeps the change as the electron's kinetic energy.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Rydberg formula — one law for every line

Turn the energy difference into a wavelength and one formula prices the whole spectrum: 1/λ = R_H (1/p² − 1/n²), with the Rydberg constant R_H ≈ 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹. Give it the landing level p and the starting level n and it returns the line — the formula the Mauritanian chapter prints for each series separately, one law wearing six names.

## Sub lesson 3 — The six series — one family per landing level

Fix the landing level and a series appears. Falls to p = 1: the Lyman series, ultraviolet. To p = 2: Balmer, the visible series — Hα red is its 3 → 2 fall. To p = 3: Paschen; p = 4: Brackett; p = 5: Pfund; p = 6: Humphreys — the rest infrared, each series crowding toward its own limit (the fall from n = ∞, the ionisation edge of that level). The spectrum's apparent chaos is a table of landings.

## The key ideas

- A spectral line is a fall between levels: hν = 13.6 × (1/p² − 1/n²) eV
- Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R_H(1/p² − 1/n²), R_H ≈ 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹
- Lyman series: falls to p = 1 — ultraviolet
- Balmer series: falls to p = 2 — visible (Hα: 3 → 2, red)
- Paschen (3), Brackett (4), Pfund (5), Humphreys (6) — infrared series
