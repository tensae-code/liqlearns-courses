# Charge distribution and the electric field inside conductors — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does no excess charge stay in the interior of a charged conductor?
**Answer: Like charges repel, so each charge pushes away from the others until it can go no further — the outer surface is the farthest they can get; the interior is left empty of excess charge.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A charged conductor has a sharp point and a smooth round part. Compare the surface charge density at the two places.
**Answer: The density is greatest at the sharp point and smaller on the smooth part — charge crowds where the surface curves most sharply.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the electric field inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium be zero?
**Answer: A non-zero field would exert forces on the conductor's free charges and set them moving; equilibrium means the charges have settled, which is only possible when the field inside is zero.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) What is meant by saying a conductor in equilibrium is an equipotential?
**Answer: Every point of the conductor — surface and interior — is at the same potential; there is no potential difference between any two of its points, so no charge flows anywhere in it.**
