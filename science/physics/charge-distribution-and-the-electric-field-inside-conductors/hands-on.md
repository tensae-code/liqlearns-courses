# Hands-on — Charge distribution and the electric field inside conductors

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Charge hunts for the surface
Rub a balloon and hold it near small paper pieces near a metal spoon lying flat, then near the spoon's tip: the pull is strongest near the pointed part, where surface charge crowds. Charge a plastic comb and bring it near a thin stream of tap water — the stream bends toward the comb, showing charge gathering on the near surface of the water. A teacher demonstrates any mains-connected shielding example (a phone inside a closed metal biscuit tin loses its signal) — the tin's conductor surface keeps fields out of the inside, the Faraday-cage idea.
