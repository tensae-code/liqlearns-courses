# Charge distribution and the electric field inside conductors

## Do it first
Before you read: charge a metal spoon by rubbing it with a charged balloon, then test with paper scraps where the charge seems strongest — along the flat bowl, or at the pointed handle end? And is there any charge hiding inside the metal itself? Keep the question: where does charge go when it is free to move in a conductor?

## Sub lesson 1 — Where charge sits on a charged conductor

Give a conductor extra charge and the charges repel each other — so they push apart until they can go no further: they end up spread over the conductor's **outer surface**, with none left in the interior. The sharper the surface curves, the more crowded the charge: at a pointed part the surface charge density is greatest, which is why pointed conductors leak charge into the air and why lightning rods are pointed. ## Sub lesson 2 — The electric field inside a conductor

Iran's Mathematical Grade 11 book treats the same section, §1-9, from the field side. In electrostatic equilibrium the electric field inside a conductor is **exactly zero**: if it were not, the free charges would still be moving, and the situation would not be equilibrium. The field at the surface stands perpendicular to the surface, and the whole conductor is one equipotential — no potential difference between any two of its points. A hollow conductor shields its inside: external fields end on the outer surface, the cavity stays field-free — the principle of the Faraday cage.

## The key ideas

- Excess charge on a conductor resides entirely on its outer surface; the interior holds none
- Surface charge crowds where the surface is sharpest — points leak charge and attract lightning
- In electrostatic equilibrium, E = 0 everywhere inside a conductor
- The conductor's surface field is perpendicular to the surface, and the whole conductor is at one potential; a hollow conductor shields its cavity (Faraday cage)

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: charge placed on a conductor spreads over its outer surface only, thickest at sharp points, and with the charges settled the field inside the conductor is zero and the whole conductor is one potential.
