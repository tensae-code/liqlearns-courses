# Lesson plan — Charge distribution and the electric field inside conductors

## Sub lessons
1. **Where charge sits on a charged conductor** — Give a conductor extra charge and the charges repel each other — so they push apart until they can go no further: they end up spread over the conductor's outer surface, with none left in the interior. The sharper the surface curves, the more crowded the charge: at a pointed part the surface charge density is greatest, which is why pointed conductors leak charge into the air and why lightning rods are pointed. Iran's Experimental Grade 11 book teaches this as its own section, §1-9: the distribution of charge in conductors.
2. **The electric field inside a conductor** — Iran's Mathematical Grade 11 book treats the same section, §1-9, from the field side. In electrostatic equilibrium the electric field inside a conductor is exactly zero: if it were not, the free charges would still be moving, and the situation would not be equilibrium. The field at the surface stands perpendicular to the surface, and the whole conductor is one equipotential — no potential difference between any two of its points. A hollow conductor shields its inside: external fields end on the outer surface, the cavity stays field-free — the principle of the Faraday cage.
