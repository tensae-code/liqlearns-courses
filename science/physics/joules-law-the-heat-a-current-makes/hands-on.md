# Hands-on — Joule's law — the heat a current makes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Watch I²R warm water
Put a low-voltage resistance coil (or a length of resistance wire on a former) in a small insulated cup of water with a thermometer, wired to a variable supply and an ammeter. (1) Run 1 A for three minutes, stirring gently, and record the temperature rise. (2) Fresh water, same coil: run 2 A for three minutes — Joule's law predicts roughly four times the rise, not two; check. (3) Fresh water: run 2 A for six minutes and watch the rise roughly double against run 2 — the t in the law. Keep voltages low, never mains, and let the coil cool in the water, not in air. Finish by computing Q = I²Rt for run 2 and comparing it with the water's mcΔT — close, with the difference lost to the cup and the air, which is itself a lesson in where heat goes.
