# Pendulum and spring — timing oscillations

## Do it first
Hang a stone on a string, time 20 swings, halve the string, time again. The shorter pendulum hurries — exactly as the formula promises.

[Picture 21853 — swinging pendulum and a bouncing mass on a spring, timers beside both]

## Sub lesson 1 — The simple pendulum
The simple pendulum — a mass on a string — swings with a period set by its length: T = 2π√(l/g). Longer string, slower swing; the mass itself does not matter. Time many swings, divide, and the same formula turned around (g = 4π²l/T²) lets a pendulum measure your local value of g.

**Keep the formulas:** Pendulum T = 2π√(l/g); Spring T = 2π√(m/k)

## Sub lesson 2 — The mass on a spring
The mass–spring system bounces with period T = 2π√(m/k), where k measures the spring's stiffness. Here mass does matter: a heavier mass bounces more slowly, and a stiffer spring bounces faster. Both systems are timed the same practical way — count many oscillations, divide by the count.

## The key ideas

- Pendulum period T = 2π√(l/g) — set by length, not by the bob's mass.
- A pendulum can measure local g: g = 4π²l/T².
- Spring period T = 2π√(m/k) — heavier mass, slower bounce; stiffer spring, faster.
