# Video script — Pendulum and spring — timing oscillations

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

The simple pendulum — a mass on a string — swings with a period set by its length: T = 2π√(l/g).
Longer string, slower swing; the mass itself does not matter.
Time many swings, divide, and the same formula turned around (g = 4π²l/T²) lets a pendulum measure your local value of g.
The mass–spring system bounces with period T = 2π√(m/k), where k measures the spring's stiffness.
Here mass does matter: a heavier mass bounces more slowly, and a stiffer spring bounces faster.
Both systems are timed the same practical way — count many oscillations, divide by the count.

Remember: **Pendulum period T = 2π√(l/g) — set by length, not by the bob's mass.**
Remember: **A pendulum can measure local g: g = 4π²l/T².**
Remember: **Spring period T = 2π√(m/k) — heavier mass, slower bounce; stiffer spring, faster.**
Keep the formula: **Pendulum T = 2π√(l/g); Spring T = 2π√(m/k)**
