# Stability and the position of the centre of gravity — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Tip a body a little and watch its centre of gravity: while the weight's line of action falls inside the base, gravity itself pulls the body back upright; Two changes make the line harder to push out: lowering the centre of gravity (the body must be lifted further before the line escapes, and the restoring moment grows) and widening the base (the line has further to travel); Set a body on a slope of its own shape and three fates appear: displaced, a stable body returns (its centre of gravity rises when tipped); an unstable body falls further (its centre of gravity drops); a neutral body stays where left (a ball, a cylinder on its side — the centre of gravity neither rises nor falls) — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
