# Stability and the position of the centre of gravity

## Do it first
Stand a bottle upright, half-full of water, and tip it gently both ways — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — The line that decides — centre of gravity over the base

Tip a body a little and watch its centre of gravity: while the weight's line of action falls inside the base, gravity itself pulls the body back upright. Tip past the edge, the line falls outside the base, and the same gravity now topples it. Stability is geometry: where the line lands decides which way the moment acts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lower and wider stand firmer

Two changes make the line harder to push out: lowering the centre of gravity (the body must be lifted further before the line escapes, and the restoring moment grows) and widening the base (the line has further to travel). Racing cars, table lamps and loaded trucks are all built on this pair of rules — weight low, feet wide.

## Sub lesson 3 — Stable, unstable and neutral

Set a body on a slope of its own shape and three fates appear: displaced, a stable body returns (its centre of gravity rises when tipped); an unstable body falls further (its centre of gravity drops); a neutral body stays where left (a ball, a cylinder on its side — the centre of gravity neither rises nor falls). Punjab's section names all three by what the centre of gravity does.

## The key ideas

- Tip a body a little and watch its centre of gravity: while the weight's line of action falls inside the base, gravity itself pulls the body back upright.
- Two changes make the line harder to push out: lowering the centre of gravity (the body must be lifted further before the line escapes, and the restoring moment grows) and widening the base (the line has further to travel).
- Set a body on a slope of its own shape and three fates appear: displaced, a stable body returns (its centre of gravity rises when tipped); an unstable body falls further (its centre of gravity drops); a neutral body stays where left (a ball, a cylinder on its side — the centre of gravity neither rises nor falls).

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: a body is stable while a vertical line from its centre of gravity falls inside its base; lower the centre of gravity and widen the base and the body stands harder to tip — Punjab's Grade 9 section makes the position of the centre of gravity the whole story of stability. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Why is a half-full bottle steadier than an empty or a full one?
2. A bus carries luggage on its roof. What happens to stability, and why do drivers load low instead?
3. Classify: a cone on its base, a cone on its tip, a ball on a table — stable, unstable or neutral, with the centre-of-gravity reason.
