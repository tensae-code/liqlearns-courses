# Lesson plan — Investigating acceleration against force and mass

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Investigating acceleration against force and mass
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the motion-and-force strand — the experiment that earns Newton's second law, placed directly before it
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before Newton's second law is stated, it is **earned**: keep the mass fixed and double the pull — the acceleration doubles; keep the pull fixed and double the mass — the acceleration halves; the control-variables design of this trolley experiment is what entitles the book to write a = F/m in the next section

## Sub lessons
1. **The design — one variable at a time** — The question has two suspects: acceleration might depend on the force F and on the mass m. The **control-variables** design separates them. A trolley on a low-friction track is pulled by a hanging weight over a pulley; a ticker timer (or photogates) records its motion. Run A holds the trolley's mass fixed and changes only the pull; Run B holds the pull fixed and changes only the mass loaded on the trolley. Friction is first compensated — the track is tilted a touch until the trolley, nudged, keeps a steady speed with no pull at all — so the measured acceleration belongs to the pull alone.
2. **Run A and Run B — what the numbers say** — With mass fixed, the tapes give accelerations in the same ratio as the pulls: double F, double a; triple F, triple a — **a ∝ F** at fixed m. With the pull fixed, doubling the mass halves the acceleration: **a ∝ 1/m** at fixed F. Two clean proportionalities, each earned by holding the other suspect still. Plotted, a against F is a straight line through the origin; a against 1/m is too — the graph shape is the proportionality made visible, and any curve or intercept is a message about friction not fully compensated.
3. **From the pattern to the law** — Join the two results: a ∝ F/m. Choose the unit of force so the constant is one — that choice is exactly how the newton is defined — and the pattern becomes the law of the next section, **F = ma**. The order matters and is China's way: the law is not announced and then illustrated; the experiment speaks first, the law is the summary of what the tapes already said. The same design discipline — change one, hold the rest, read the graph — is the template for every quantitative investigation in the senior course.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Control variables: change one suspect at a time, hold the other fixed; compensate friction first
2. Fixed mass: acceleration is proportional to the force — a ∝ F
3. Fixed force: acceleration is proportional to 1/mass — a ∝ 1/m
4. Joined: a ∝ F/m — and choosing the force unit to make the constant 1 gives F = ma, stated only after the experiment
