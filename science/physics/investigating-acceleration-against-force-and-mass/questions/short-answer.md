# Investigating acceleration against force and mass — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why are masses moved from the trolley to the hanger (not added from the bench) when the pull is changed in Run A?
**Answer: So the total mass of the moving system stays constant — only the pull changes; otherwise two variables would change at once and the proportionality could not be read cleanly.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A 2 kg trolley accelerates at 1.5 m/s² under a pull. The same pull on a 6 kg trolley gives what acceleration?
**Answer: a ∝ 1/m: tripling the mass thirds the acceleration → 0.5 m/s².**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State the two proportionalities and the joined statement they earn.
**Answer: At fixed mass a ∝ F; at fixed force a ∝ 1/m; joined, a ∝ F/m — with the newton defined to make the constant 1, F = ma.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A team's a against 1/m plot is straight but their a–F plot curves. Give one setup fault that could bend the second plot.
**Answer: Friction not compensated (or growing with speed/load) steals a growing share of the larger pulls; or the hanger's mass was ignored at small pulls. Accept any single-variable fault named with its effect.**
