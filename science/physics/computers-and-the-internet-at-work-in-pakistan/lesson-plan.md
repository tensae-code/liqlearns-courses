# Lesson plan — Computers and the internet at work in Pakistan (Punjab)

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Computers and the internet at work in Pakistan (Punjab)
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the ICT strand — Punjab Grade 10's ICT-in-life survey, applications and risks together
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Punjab Grade 10 closes its ICT unit where the technology lands: computers at work in offices, schools and services — word processing the named example — the internet's services on top of them, and, in the same breath, the risks ICT brings to society and the environment
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Computers at work — the applications** — The unit surveys what computers actually do in Punjab's working life: word processing for documents, records kept and searched, calculations, communication and control. Each application replaces a slower hand process, and the survey names them plainly so learners can point to the computer's work in any office they enter.
2. **Internet services — the network on top** — On connected machines come the internet's services: the web, electronic mail, messaging, shared files and information search — information flowing between people and institutions at a distance and a speed the standalone computer cannot offer. The services, not the wires, are what users actually meet.
3. **Risks to society and the environment** — The same unit refuses to end on praise: it names ICT's risks — to society (dependence, misuse, unequal access, privacy) and to the environment (power demand, electronic waste). Punjab's way is to teach the tool and its shadows in one unit, so the learner's judgement grows with their skill.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
