# Computers and the internet at work in Pakistan (Punjab)

## Do it first
Survey your own surroundings for one day — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Computers at work — the applications

The unit surveys what computers actually do in Punjab's working life: word processing for documents, records kept and searched, calculations, communication and control. Each application replaces a slower hand process, and the survey names them plainly so learners can point to the computer's work in any office they enter.

## Sub lesson 2 — Internet services — the network on top

On connected machines come the internet's services: the web, electronic mail, messaging, shared files and information search — information flowing between people and institutions at a distance and a speed the standalone computer cannot offer. The services, not the wires, are what users actually meet.

## Sub lesson 3 — Risks to society and the environment

The same unit refuses to end on praise: it names ICT's risks — to society (dependence, misuse, unequal access, privacy) and to the environment (power demand, electronic waste). Punjab's way is to teach the tool and its shadows in one unit, so the learner's judgement grows with their skill.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: Punjab Grade 10 closes its ICT unit where the technology lands: computers at work in offices, schools and services — word processing the named example — the internet's services on top of them, and, in the same breath, the risks ICT brings to society and the environment. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.
