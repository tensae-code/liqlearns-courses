# Hands-on — Magnetic properties of substances — dia-, para- and ferromagnetism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The three answers, felt in the hand
You need a strong magnet (a neodymium magnet from a dead hard drive or a craft shop), an iron nail, a piece of aluminium foil scrunched and hung from thread, and — if you can get it — a bismuth sample or a grape (mostly water, diamagnetic). Hang each sample from the thread so it can swing freely, and bring the magnet near without touching. The iron leaps; the aluminium drifts in, barely; the grape, on a still day, edges away. Record the three answers in three rows and label them ferro-, para- and diamagnetic — you have just run the classification this lesson teaches. Keep hands and the strong magnet away from phones and cards.
