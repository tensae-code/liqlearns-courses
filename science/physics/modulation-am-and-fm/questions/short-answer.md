# Modulation — AM and FM — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How does giving each station its own carrier frequency keep stations separate?
**Answer: Receivers tune to one carrier frequency and read only the modulation written on that carrier; other stations' carriers sit at different frequencies and are rejected by the tuning, so their patterns never reach the demodulator together.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Sketch in words the carrier of an AM broadcast of a note that gets steadily louder.
**Answer: The carrier keeps its frequency but its peaks grow steadily taller — its envelope swells in step with the note's loudness, tracing the audio wave's shape above and below the axis.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a limiter help an FM receiver but would destroy an AM signal?
**Answer: FM writes its message in frequency and holds amplitude constant, so clipping all amplitude variation removes noise while leaving the message untouched; in AM the message IS the amplitude variation, so a limiter would clip the music itself away.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two advantages of FM and the two costs that come with them.
**Answer: Advantages: clean, noise-resistant sound and room for high-fidelity audio. Costs: each station needs a wide channel, and the VHF signals used reach only about as far as the horizon, so more transmitters are needed to cover a country.**
