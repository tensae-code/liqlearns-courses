# Sensors — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must a sensor come before an amplifier in any sensing system?
**Answer: The amplifier can only enlarge a signal that exists; the sensor is what creates the electrical signal from the physical quantity in the first place — amplification without sensing has nothing to amplify.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A divider holds an LDR (upper arm) and a fixed resistor (lower arm); the output is taken across the lower arm. What happens to the output as the room darkens?
**Answer: Darkness raises the LDR's resistance, so a larger share of the supply voltage falls across the LDR and a smaller share across the lower arm — the output voltage falls (the sense can be flipped by swapping the arms).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Two temperature sensors cover −50 °C to +150 °C and 35 °C to 42 °C respectively. Which suits a fever thermometer, and which characteristic decided it?
**Answer: The narrow-range one: the job needs precision inside a small span, and a sensor ranged 35–42 °C spends its whole sensitivity there — range matched to the job is the deciding characteristic.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Vietnam's electronics topic runs sensors → op-amp → complete circuits. Why is that order the natural one?
**Answer: It follows the signal: the sensor creates it, the op-amp enlarges or compares it, and the complete circuit acts on it — input, processing, output, in the order information actually flows.**
