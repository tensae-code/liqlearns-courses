# Using solar energy — thermal and photovoltaic generation

## Do it first
Before you read: a black water container left in the sun is hot by midday — that is one way to use sunlight. A calculator that works in a lit room with no battery change is using the other way. Heat, or electricity straight from light — learn to tell the two routes apart and say where each is used.

## Sub lesson 1 — Collecting the Sun's heat — thermal uses

The simplest solar technology collects sunlight as heat: a dark surface absorbs the radiation and warms up, and that warmth is put to work — heating water for washing, warming air, drying crops and cooking. A flat collector with a dark absorber under glass traps the heat the way a greenhouse does. Nothing is converted into another form first: sunlight in, useful heat out — the thermal route in its direct form, and the one the Grade 12 companion experiment sets out to collect and exploit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Thermal generation — heat first, then electricity

Scaled up, collected heat can make electricity indirectly: mirrors or large collectors concentrate sunlight until a fluid boils, and the steam drives a turbine and generator exactly as in a fuel-burning power station — with the Sun doing the burning's job. This is thermal (solar-thermal) generation: the energy chain runs light → heat → motion → electricity, and it works best where sunlight is strong and steady and can be concentrated.

## Sub lesson 3 — Photovoltaic generation — light straight to electricity

The second route skips heat entirely: a photovoltaic (solar) cell converts light directly into electricity, by the same semiconductor physics as the diode — photons free charge carriers in the cell, and the junction separates them into a current. Cells join into panels, panels into arrays: silent, with no moving parts, working at any scale from a calculator to a power plant. Thermal or photovoltaic — the question is always which chain fits the job: heat where heat is wanted, cells where electricity must come straight from light.

## The key ideas

- Thermal uses collect sunlight as heat — dark collectors warm water and air directly, with no conversion first
- Thermal generation concentrates sunlight to raise steam, driving a turbine and generator: light → heat → motion → electricity
- Photovoltaic cells convert light straight into electricity in a semiconductor junction — no heat stage, no moving parts, any scale

If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Put two identical bottles of water in the sun, one painted black, one white. After an hour, feel both and explain the difference in absorption words.
2. Write the energy chain for a solar-thermal power station and for a rooftop photovoltaic panel, side by side. Circle the stage the photovoltaic route skips.
3. Choose the route — thermal or photovoltaic — for: heating bath water; powering a remote radio; drying fruit; running lights in a village far from the grid. Give a reason for each.
