# The generator as an active dipole — EMF, internal resistance and the operating point

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The falling characteristic — U = E − rI

Plot a battery's terminal voltage against the current it delivers and the points fall on a straight line sloping down: U = E − rI. At I = 0 the line meets the axis at E, the electromotive force — the voltage the generator shows when it delivers nothing. The slope is −r: the internal resistance, the generator's own material resisting its own current, dropping rI volts inside the source for every ampere drawn.

## Sub lesson 2 — The power balance — EI = UI + rI²

Multiply the characteristic by I and the energy account appears: EI is the total power the generator's chemistry produces; UI is the useful power delivered to the circuit; rI² is the power lost as heat inside the generator itself. Efficiency follows at once: ρ = U/E — the share of each volt that survives to the terminals, falling as the current rises and rI eats more of E.

## Sub lesson 3 — The operating point — where the two characteristics meet

Connect the generator to a receiver (a resistor, say of 200 Ω): the circuit must satisfy both characteristics at once, so it settles where the generator's falling line crosses the receiver's rising line U = RI — the operating point, one voltage and one current. Mauritania's manuals find it by the two standing methods: graphically (draw both lines, read the crossing) and algebraically (solve E − rI = RI, so I = E/(R + r)).

## The key ideas

- A generator's characteristic falls: U = E − rI
- E is the open-circuit voltage (I = 0); r is the internal resistance — the slope's size
- Power balance: generated EI = useful UI + internal Joule loss rI²
- Efficiency ρ = U/E — it falls as the drawn current grows
- The operating point is where generator and receiver characteristics cross: I = E/(R + r)

## Do It Yourself
1. A generator has E = 12 V, r = 2 Ω. What current does it drive through a 10 Ω resistor, and what is the terminal voltage at the operating point?
2. For the same generator short-circuited (R = 0), what current flows, and where does all the power go?
3. Why does a car battery's headlight dip while the starter motor turns? Answer with U = E − rI.
