# Lesson plan — The generator as an active dipole — EMF, internal resistance and the operating point

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The generator as an active dipole — EMF, internal resistance and the operating point

## Sub lessons
1. **The falling characteristic — U = E − rI** — Plot a battery's terminal voltage against the current it delivers and the points fall on a straight line sloping down: U = E − rI. At I = 0 the line meets the axis at E, the electromotive force — the voltage the generator shows when it delivers nothing. The slope is −r: the internal resistance, the generator's own material resisting its own current, dropping rI volts inside the source for every ampere drawn.
2. **The power balance — EI = UI + rI²** — Multiply the characteristic by I and the energy account appears: EI is the total power the generator's chemistry produces; UI is the useful power delivered to the circuit; rI² is the power lost as heat inside the generator itself. Efficiency follows at once: ρ = U/E — the share of each volt that survives to the terminals, falling as the current rises and rI eats more of E.
3. **The operating point — where the two characteristics meet** — Connect the generator to a receiver (a resistor, say of 200 Ω): the circuit must satisfy both characteristics at once, so it settles where the generator's falling line crosses the receiver's rising line U = RI — the operating point, one voltage and one current. Mauritania's manuals find it by the two standing methods: graphically (draw both lines, read the crossing) and algebraically (solve E − rI = RI, so I = E/(R + r)).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A generator's characteristic falls: U = E − rI
2. E is the open-circuit voltage (I = 0); r is the internal resistance — the slope's size
3. Power balance: generated EI = useful UI + internal Joule loss rI²
4. Efficiency ρ = U/E — it falls as the drawn current grows
5. The operating point is where generator and receiver characteristics cross: I = E/(R + r)
