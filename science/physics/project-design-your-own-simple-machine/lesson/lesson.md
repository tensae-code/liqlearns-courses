# Project: design your own simple machine

## Do it first
Walk your compound with the project's eyes: find one job that hurts — a heavy lift, a long carry — and hold it in mind as your design brief.

[Picture 21850 — pupil-made machine sketch beside the built version]

## Project: design your own simple machine — the teaching
This project lesson closes the unit by designing. Choose a real local need — lifting water, moving stones, splitting wood, opening, carrying. Sketch a simple machine for it, naming its type, its purpose (force, speed or direction), and its predicted MA and VR from your measurements. Build it from local materials, test it against the need, measure its real efficiency, and say honestly where friction taxed you. A design that is measured beats a design that is only admired.

## The key ideas

- Start a design from a real local need.
- Predict MA and VR before building; measure efficiency after.
- Friction explains the gap between ideal and real performance.

## Say it to your companion
Explain project: design your own simple machine to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Complete the full design-build-test cycle and present sketch, machine and measurements together.
2. Write the key ideas in your own words — one line each — without looking back. Then check them against the lesson and fix any line that drifted.
