# Resistivity and how resistance changes with temperature — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Two wires of the same metal differ in resistance if their lengths or areas differ; the quantity that does not differ is the resistivity ρ, defined by R = ρL/A and measured in ohm-metres; Heat a metal and its ions vibrate harder; drifting electrons collide more often, and ρ climbs — roughly linearly over ordinary ranges, ρ = ρ₀(1 + αΔT) with α ≈ 0; In a semiconductor, heat's gift of extra free carriers beats the extra collisions: ρ falls steeply as temperature rises (the thermistor's negative coefficient) — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
