# Video script — Average velocity and instantaneous velocity

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Average velocity is displacement divided by the time taken.
Because displacement is a vector, average velocity carries a direction too.
The round trip is the warning case: walk out and back to your start and your displacement is zero, so your average velocity is zero — even though your average speed was not.
Grade 10 sharpens the same idea: position is fixed against a frame of reference, and average velocity is still displacement over time, now read against that origin.
Instantaneous velocity is the velocity at one instant — the value with its direction at a single moment.
On a position–time graph it is read from the slope: the slope of the chord between two times gives the average velocity, and the slope of the tangent at one point gives the instantaneous velocity there.

Remember: **Average velocity = displacement ÷ time, with direction.**
Remember: **A round trip back to the start gives zero average velocity.**
Remember: **Instantaneous velocity at a point on a position–time graph is the tangent's slope.**
Keep the formula: **Average velocity = displacement / time**
