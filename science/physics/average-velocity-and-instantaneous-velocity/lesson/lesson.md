# Average velocity and instantaneous velocity

## Do it first
Walk 10 steps east, then 10 steps west back to your mark, timed. Compute both average speed and average velocity and feel why they differ.

[Picture 21817 — out-and-back journey with the displacement arrow shrinking to zero; position–time graph with chord and tangent]

## Sub lesson 1 — Average velocity
Average velocity is displacement divided by the time taken. Because displacement is a vector, average velocity carries a direction too. The round trip is the warning case: walk out and back to your start and your displacement is zero, so your average velocity is zero — even though your average speed was not. Grade 10 sharpens the same idea: position is fixed against a frame of reference, and average velocity is still displacement over time, now read against that origin.

**Keep the formulas:** Average velocity = displacement / time

## Sub lesson 2 — Instantaneous velocity
Instantaneous velocity is the velocity at one instant — the value with its direction at a single moment. On a position–time graph it is read from the slope: the slope of the chord between two times gives the average velocity, and the slope of the tangent at one point gives the instantaneous velocity there.

## The key ideas

- Average velocity = displacement ÷ time, with direction.
- A round trip back to the start gives zero average velocity.
- Instantaneous velocity at a point on a position–time graph is the tangent's slope.

## Say it to your companion
Explain average velocity and instantaneous velocity to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
