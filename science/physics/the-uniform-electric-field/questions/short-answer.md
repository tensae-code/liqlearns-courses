# The uniform electric field — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Plates at 50 V are moved from 1.0 cm apart to 2.0 cm apart. What happens to the field, and why?
**Answer: The field halves, from 5,000 V/m to 2,500 V/m: E = V/d, and the same potential difference spread over twice the distance gives half the field.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the uniform field compared to gravity near the Earth's surface?
**Answer: Both are (near-)constant fields giving a constant force and so a constant acceleration — g for masses near the ground, qE/m for charges between the plates — which is why the motions in them share the same equations and the same parabola.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A negative charge is placed between the plates. Which way does it accelerate, and what happens to its speed?
**Answer: It accelerates against the field direction — toward the positive plate — with constant acceleration a = qE/m in size, so its speed changes steadily (growing if released from rest toward the plate).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two instruments whose working parts are charges moving in uniform fields.
**Answer: The cathode-ray tube (electron beam deflected by plate pairs) and Millikan's oil-drop apparatus (charged droplets balanced and driven between horizontal plates to find the electron's charge).**
