# The uniform electric field

## Do it first
Before you read: rub a balloon and hold it near a thin stream of water from a tap — the stream bends toward the balloon, more as you bring it closer. The bend is uneven because that field is uneven. Between two charged plates, this lesson shows, the field can be made perfectly even — the same push everywhere — and that evenness is what makes its physics exactly computable.

## Sub lesson 1 — Between the plates — a field that does not change

Its strength follows from the plates' potential difference V and separation d: **E = V/d**, in volts per metre — the same quantity as newtons per coulomb. Double the voltage and the field doubles; double the gap at the same voltage and the field halves. A 100 V difference across 2.0 cm gives E = 100 ÷ 0.020 = **5,000 V/m** everywhere between the plates.

## Sub lesson 2 — A charge in the field — a constant force

The work the field does moving a charge between the plates is W = qV, which is where the electronvolt comes from: one electron crossing 1 V gains 1 eV of energy — the energy unit of the atomic world, earned here in the uniform field.

## Sub lesson 3 — Flight through the field — the parabola

The deflection grows with the field and the time spent between the plates, and falls with the particle's mass and entry speed — every term in that sentence is a knob an instrument designer turns. Vietnam's lesson closes the field strand's core: field as pattern (Bài 17), field uniform and computable (Bài 18), then the energy view — potential (Bài 19–20).

## The key ideas

- A uniform field lives between large parallel plates: straight, parallel, evenly spaced field lines; the same E everywhere
- E = V/d (volts per metre = newtons per coulomb): raise V or shrink d and the field strengthens
- A charge in the field feels the constant force F = qE and so moves with constant acceleration a = qE/m
- A charge fired across the field follows a parabola — free flight along its entry direction, constant acceleration across it

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: Between two large parallel plates at a potential difference V, separated by d, the electric field is uniform — the same strength and direction everywhere between the plates: E = V/d, pointing from the positive plate to the negative — and a charge q inside it feels a constant force F = qE, so a free charge moves through it with constant acceleration, curving on a parabola like a projectile in gravity. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Plates 5.0 cm apart are held at 200 V. Find E between them, and the force on a charge of 2.0 µC placed inside.
2. An electron enters horizontally between plates where E = 1,000 V/m. Find its acceleration across the field (e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 9.1×10⁻³¹ kg) and say why its forward velocity does not change.
3. Why do the field lines near the edges of real plates spoil the uniformity, and how do instrument makers reduce the spoilage?
