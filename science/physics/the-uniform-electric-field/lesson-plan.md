# Lesson plan — The uniform electric field

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The uniform electric field
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the electric-field strand — after Electric fields and field lines and before Electric potential: the field between parallel plates, the one field whose strength is the same everywhere
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** between two large parallel plates at a potential difference V, separated by d, the electric field is uniform — the same strength and direction everywhere between the plates: E = V/d, pointing from the positive plate to the negative — and a charge q inside it feels a constant force F = qE, so a free charge moves through it with constant acceleration, curving on a parabola like a projectile in gravity

## Sub lessons
1. **Between the plates — a field that does not change** — Bring two large metal plates face to face, connect them to a supply so one is positive and one negative, and the field between them is the simplest in all of physics: **uniform**. Its field lines are **straight, parallel and evenly spaced** — the drawing of a field whose strength and direction are the same at every point between the plates (edge effects at the rims aside). Vietnam's Grade 11 book gives the uniform field its own lesson (Bài 18) between the field concept and potential — the field you can compute exactly, before fields in general.
2. **A charge in the field — a constant force** — A charge q placed in the field feels **F = qE** — a **constant** force, because E is constant: a positive charge is pushed along the field (positive plate to negative), a negative charge against it. Constant force means **constant acceleration** a = qE/m — the electric twin of falling under gravity's constant g, and the direct bridge from the field concept to motion: the field is no longer just a pattern of lines, it is a pusher with a calculable push.
3. **Flight through the field — the parabola** — Fire a charged particle **across** the field — enter an electron horizontally between vertical plates, or a charged droplet between horizontal ones — and its motion splits in two, exactly like a projectile: **unchanged velocity along its entry direction** (no field force that way) and **constant acceleration across it** (the field's steady push). The two combine into a **parabola** — the path of electrons in a cathode-ray tube, steered by plate voltages, and of Millikan's oil drops, balanced and driven between plates to measure the electron's charge.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw the field lines between parallel plates and state what 'uniform' means there.
2. Compute E from V and d, in V/m, and the force on a given charge.
3. Explain why a free charge in a uniform field has constant acceleration.
4. Describe the parabolic path of a charge entering across the field, with the two component motions.

## What you need
Two metal plates or foil sheets, a battery pack, and (for the hands-on) a balloon and a charged comb or rod to feel a field's push.
