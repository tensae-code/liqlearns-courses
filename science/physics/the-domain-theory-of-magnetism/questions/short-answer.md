# The domain theory of magnetism — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Use the domain theory to explain why a magnet's strength has a limit — why stroking a bar a hundred times does not make it a hundred times stronger.
**Answer: Once every domain that can turn has lined up, there are no more domains to add to the line: the bar is saturated. Further stroking turns nothing new, so the strength stops growing.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does stroking back and forth fail to magnetise a bar?
**Answer: Each return stroke drags the domains back toward the opposite alignment, undoing what the forward stroke did. Only strokes in one direction, with the magnet lifted clear on the return, keep coaxing more domains into the same line.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A coil carrying current magnetises the bar inside it, but a soft-iron bar loses its magnetism when the current stops while a steel bar keeps its. Explain both halves.
**Answer: The current's field lines both bars' domains up while it flows. Soft iron's domains fall back to pointing at random as soon as the aligning field is removed, so its magnetism is temporary; steel's domains resist turning in both directions, so they stay lined up after the field goes.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the three demagnetising treatments and give the domain reason they share.
**Answer: Heating (strong vibration of the particles breaks the domain order), hammering or dropping (mechanical shocks shake domains out of line), and a slowly fading alternating-current field (the reversing field leaves domains pointing at random as it dies away). All three end with the domains pointing every which way, so their effects cancel.**
