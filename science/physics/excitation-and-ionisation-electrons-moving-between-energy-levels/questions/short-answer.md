# Excitation and ionisation — electrons moving between energy levels — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) Distinguish excitation from ionisation in one sentence each.
**Answer: Excitation: the electron absorbs a level difference of energy and jumps to a higher level but stays in the atom. Ionisation: the electron absorbs at least the ionisation energy and leaves the atom, which becomes a positive ion.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is an ionised atom charged but an excited atom neutral?
**Answer: Ionisation removes a negative electron, unbalancing the atom's charges (positive ion); excitation only rearranges the electron on the levels — nothing leaves, so the totals still balance.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do the levels crowd together toward the top of the ladder, and what lies above the top level?
**Answer: The higher levels approach the energy at which the electron escapes, so their spacings shrink toward it; above the top level there is no bound level at all — the electron is free (ionised).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A gas glows with sharp coloured lines when excited electrically. Explain the lines using levels.
**Answer: Excited electrons fall back between fixed levels; each fall emits one photon of exactly that level difference, so only certain energies — certain colours — appear: sharp lines, each element's own ladder giving its own pattern.**
