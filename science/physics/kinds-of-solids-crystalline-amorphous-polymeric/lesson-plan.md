# Lesson plan — Kinds of solids — crystalline, amorphous, polymeric

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Kinds of solids — crystalline, amorphous, polymeric
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the solids strand — the classification of solids by atomic arrangement that Punjab teaches in both Grade 11 and Grade 12
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** solids sort by how their particles are arranged: crystalline solids repeat a regular lattice, amorphous solids hold their particles in a frozen disorder, and polymeric solids are built from long chain molecules — and the arrangement decides melting behaviour, cleavage and strength

## Sub lessons
1. **Crystalline solids — the repeating lattice** — In a crystal, atoms, ions or molecules sit in a pattern that repeats in three dimensions — the lattice (salt, metals, quartz). The regularity gives crystals their sharp melting point (the whole lattice lets go at one temperature), flat cleavage planes and geometric crystal shapes: order inside, order visible outside.
2. **Amorphous solids — order that never formed** — Glass, rubber-like solids and many plastics have particles arranged without long-range order — a liquid's disorder, frozen. With no single lattice to collapse, an amorphous solid softens over a range of temperatures instead of melting sharply, and breaks with curved, shell-like surfaces rather than flat cleavage.
3. **Polymeric solids — the chain-built kind** — Polymers are solids whose molecules are long chains of repeating units — natural (rubber, cellulose) and synthetic (polythene, nylon). The chains can slide, tangle and cross-link, which is why polymeric solids stretch, flex and soften as they do; Punjab lists them as the third great kind beside crystalline and amorphous, sorted by structure, not by hardness.
