# Mirrors — plane, concave, convex

## Do it first
Face a mirror and raise your right hand; the person facing you raises their left. Lateral inversion, demonstrated by your own body.

[Picture 21933 — ray diagrams for the three mirrors with the same object]

## Sub lesson 1 — Plane mirrors
A plane mirror gives an image that is virtual (behind the mirror), erect, the same size as the object, and laterally inverted — left and right swapped. Image distance equals object distance.

**Keep the formulas:** 1/f = 1/v + 1/u; m = v/u; f = R/2

## Sub lesson 2 — Concave mirrors
A concave mirror curves inward and gathers rays: parallel rays reflect through its focal point, with focal length f = R/2, half the radius of curvature. It serves in car headlights, shaving mirrors and solar furnaces.

## Sub lesson 3 — Convex mirrors
A convex mirror curves outward and spreads rays, giving a wide field of view with small, erect, virtual images — the rear-view mirror's bargain. Both curved mirrors are computed with the mirror equation 1/f = 1/v + 1/u and magnification m = v/u, with ray diagrams to check the arithmetic.

## The key ideas

- Plane mirror image: virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted.
- Concave mirror focuses rays; f = R/2.
- Convex mirror spreads rays — wide view, small erect images.
