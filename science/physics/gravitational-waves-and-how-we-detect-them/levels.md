# Gravitational waves and how we detect them — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Just as an accelerated charge radiates electromagnetic waves, an accelerated mass radiates gravitational waves — disturbances of space-time's geometry travelling outward at c; A gravitational wave stretches space in one direction while squeezing it in the perpendicular direction, then reverses — distances between free objects breathe by a fractional amount called the strain; LIGO's answer is the laser interferometer: two perpendicular arms, kilometres long, with light bouncing between mirrors; a passing wave lengthens one arm and shortens the other, shifting the interference pattern — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
