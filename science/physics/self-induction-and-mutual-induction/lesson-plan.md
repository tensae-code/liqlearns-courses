# Lesson plan — Self-induction and mutual induction

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Self-induction and mutual induction
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Egypt placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a coil induces an EMF in itself whenever its own current changes (self-induction, ε = −L dI/dt, with L the inductance in henrys), and in a neighbouring coil whenever its flux reaches across (mutual induction) — the pair of effects behind the induction coil, the choke and the transformer

## Sub lessons
1. **Self-induction — a coil fights its own changing current** — Current growing in a coil builds a magnetic flux through the coil's own turns. By Faraday's law a changing flux induces an EMF — and here the flux and the circuit are the same coil, so the induced EMF appears in the coil itself, opposing the change that makes it (Lenz's law): it fights the current's rise switch-on and fights its fall at switch-off, which is why the lamp flashes and the spark jumps when the circuit breaks. This is self-induction.
2. **Inductance and the henry** — The constant L is the coil's self-inductance, measured in henrys (H): a coil has an inductance of one henry when a current changing at one ampere per second induces one volt in it. L is fixed by the coil's construction — it grows with the square of the number of turns, with the cross-sectional area and with an iron core, and it does not depend on the current itself.
3. **Mutual induction — one coil induces in another** — Place a second coil near the first so that part of the first coil's flux links it. Now change the current in the first coil: the shared flux changes, and an EMF is induced in the second coil — mutual induction, ε₂ = −M (ΔI₁/Δt), with M the mutual inductance, also in henrys. Nothing connects the coils but the field itself.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Self-induction: a coil induces an EMF in itself while its current changes, ε = −L (ΔI/Δt), opposing the change — nothing is induced by a steady current.
2. Say, in their own words: Inductance L is measured in henrys (1 H = 1 V per A/s of current change) and is set by turns², area and core — not by the current.
3. Say, in their own words: A coil stores energy in its field, U = ½L I², paid back at switch-off — the spark and the lamp flash are that energy leaving.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Expects induction from a steady current → a constant current makes a constant flux, and only changing flux induces. The effects live at switch-on, switch-off and on AC.
- Drops the minus sign's meaning → the induced EMF always opposes the change (Lenz): back-EMF against a rising current, forward push sustaining a falling one.
