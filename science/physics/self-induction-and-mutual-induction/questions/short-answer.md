# Self-induction and mutual induction — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A current in a 0.2 H coil is switched off, falling from 3 A to zero in 0.01 s. Find the average induced EMF and explain the spark seen at the switch.
**Answer: ε = L ΔI/Δt = 0.2 × 3/0.01 = 60 V — far above the battery that drove the coil. The collapsing field drives this EMF across the opening contacts, enough to break down the air gap for an instant: the spark, powered by the stored ½LI² = 0.9 J leaving.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does inserting an iron core into a coil raise its inductance?
**Answer: The core multiplies the flux produced by the same current (its permeability concentrates the field through the turns). Since L measures flux linkage per ampere, the same current now links far more flux, so L rises — and with it both the back-EMF for a given current change and the stored energy.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Two coils are wound on the same iron core. The primary current is steady at 2 A. What EMF appears in the secondary, and why?
**Answer: None. Mutual induction needs a changing flux; a steady primary current gives a constant flux through the secondary, so Δflux/Δt = 0 and ε₂ = 0. Only when the primary current changes — switched, chopped or alternating — does the secondary answer.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the difference between L and M as constants of a pair of coils.
**Answer: L (self-inductance) belongs to one coil alone: the EMF it induces in itself per unit rate of change of its own current. M (mutual inductance) belongs to the pair: the EMF induced in coil 2 per unit rate of change of current in coil 1, and it depends on how much flux the two coils share.**
