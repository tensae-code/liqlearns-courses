# When waves meet — superposition and standing waves

## Do it first
Two people shake a rope from opposite ends with the same rhythm. Watch the pattern settle into place — big swing spots and still spots you can point to.

[Picture 21861 — two waves adding into a bigger wave, cancelling, and standing still in a rope]

## When waves meet — superposition and standing waves — the teaching
When waves meet, their displacements add — the superposition principle. Arriving in step (in phase), crests add into a bigger wave: constructive interference. Arriving opposite, crest meets trough and they cancel: destructive interference. Send two identical waves against each other along a rope and the result is a standing wave — a pattern that vibrates in place, with nodes that never move and antinodes that swing widest, a quarter-wavelength apart from each other.

## The key ideas

- Superposition: displacements of meeting waves add.
- In phase = constructive (bigger); opposite = destructive (cancel).
- A standing wave has still nodes and widest-swinging antinodes, λ/4 apart.
