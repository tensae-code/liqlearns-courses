# Video script — When waves meet — superposition and standing waves

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

When waves meet, their displacements add — the superposition principle.
Arriving in step (in phase), crests add into a bigger wave: constructive interference.
Arriving opposite, crest meets trough and they cancel: destructive interference.
Send two identical waves against each other along a rope and the result is a standing wave — a pattern that vibrates in place, with nodes that never move and antinodes that swing widest, a quarter-wavelength apart from each other.

Remember: **Superposition: displacements of meeting waves add.**
Remember: **In phase = constructive (bigger); opposite = destructive (cancel).**
Remember: **A standing wave has still nodes and widest-swinging antinodes, λ/4 apart.**
