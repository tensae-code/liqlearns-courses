# EMF, internal resistance and terminal voltage of a source — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is 'electromotive force' a misleading name for EMF?
**Answer: EMF is not a force at all. It is an energy per unit charge — joules per coulomb — the energy the source gives each coulomb that passes through it, so it is measured in volts, not newtons.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) An old battery reads almost full voltage on a voltmeter alone but runs a lamp dimly. Explain.
**Answer: With no current drawn, no volts are lost inside, so the meter reads nearly the EMF. Under load, the aged battery's large internal resistance spends most of the EMF as lost volts Ir inside the battery, leaving little terminal voltage for the lamp.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A source is short-circuited by a plain wire. Why is the current so large, and what limits it at all?
**Answer: The outside resistance is nearly zero, so I = E/(R + r) becomes I ≈ E/r. Only the source's own small internal resistance limits the current — which is why short circuits heat and damage sources.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Two identical 1.5 V cells, each r = 0.5 Ω, are joined in series. What are the battery's EMF and internal resistance?
**Answer: The EMFs add: 3.0 V. The internal resistances also add: 1.0 Ω. Series sources give more push but also more resistance inside.**
