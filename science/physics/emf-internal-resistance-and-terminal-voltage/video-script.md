Today: **where a battery's voltage goes**.

A battery promises, say, one and a half volts. But connect a lamp and the voltage across the battery drops — the lamp gets less than the promise. Nothing is cheating: the battery has resistance inside itself. Its EMF is the whole push — the energy it gives every coulomb. Part of that push is spent pushing charge through the battery's own inside — those are the lost volts. What is left at the terminals is the terminal voltage, and it shrinks the harder you draw current. Measure it, graph it, and the graph hands you both secrets: the EMF where the line starts, the internal resistance in its slope.
