# Lesson plan — Electronic tuning — the varactor diode

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Electronic tuning — the varactor diode

## Sub lessons
1. **A diode whose capacitance you can dial** — Recall the p–n junction: at the join, mobile carriers clear out, leaving a depletion layer — a thin insulating region of uncovered fixed charges, with conducting p and n material on its two sides. Conductors separated by an insulator are a capacitor, so every junction has a capacitance set by that layer's width: C behaves as area ÷ width, exactly like a parallel-plate capacitor whose plate spacing is the depletion width.
2. **Tuning an LC circuit by voltage** — The tuned circuit of the resonance lesson answers at f = 1/(2π√(LC)). Put a varactor in place of the variable capacitor and C — and therefore f — follows the tuning voltage: raise the reverse bias, the depletion layer widens, C falls, and the resonant frequency climbs; lower the bias and the circuit tunes down the band. One DC voltage, set by a knob, a button or a processor, now does the travelling that meshing metal plates used to do.
3. **Electronic tuning at work — no moving parts** — The gains are everything mechanical tuning could not do: no plates to bend, corrode or drift out of alignment; tuning by a voltage that a remote control, a preset button or an automatic frequency control (AFC) circuit can supply — the AFC senses a station drifting off-tune and nudges the varactor voltage to pull it back, a self-correcting loop impossible with a hand-turned knob. Channel presets are simply stored voltages; a scanning tuner sweeps a voltage ramp and stops where a station answers.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A reverse-biased p–n junction's depletion layer is an insulator between conducting regions — a capacitor whose plate spacing (and so capacitance) the reverse voltage controls: wider layer, smaller C.
2. Say, in their own words: The varactor diode is built to exploit exactly this: a clean, sizeable voltage-variable capacitance, used in reverse bias only.
3. Say, in their own words: In an LC circuit, varactor C makes the resonant frequency f = 1/(2π√(LC)) voltage-controlled: higher reverse voltage → smaller C → higher f.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Expects the diode to conduct for tuning → the varactor works in reverse bias, ideally conducting nothing; it is used purely as a voltage-set capacitor.
- Gets the direction backwards → more reverse voltage = wider depletion layer = plates further apart = SMALLER capacitance = higher resonant frequency.
