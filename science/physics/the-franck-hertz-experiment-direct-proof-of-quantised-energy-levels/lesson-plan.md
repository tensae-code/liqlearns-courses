# Lesson plan — The Franck–Hertz experiment — direct proof of quantised energy levels

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The Franck–Hertz experiment — direct proof of quantised energy levels
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the atomic-physics strand — beside the Bohr model and atomic spectra
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in 1914 Franck and Hertz fired electrons through mercury vapour and watched the collected current climb, then drop, then climb again — dropping every 4.9 volts of accelerating voltage. The drops are the atoms' signature: an electron can hand a mercury atom exactly 4.9 eV of energy, exciting it to its first level, or nothing at all — the first direct, electrical proof that atomic energy levels are quantised

## Sub lessons
1. **The tube — electrons fired through mercury vapour** — The apparatus is a sealed tube of mercury vapour with three electrodes: a hot cathode emitting electrons, a grid that an accelerating voltage pulls them toward, and a collecting plate held at a small retarding voltage just below the grid's. An electron that reaches the grid region still carrying at least the small retarding energy crosses to the plate and registers as current. Collisions on the way are the whole experiment: if an electron loses its energy to an atom mid-flight, it arrives too weak to cross, and the current falls.
2. **The curve — current that climbs and drops every 4.9 volts** — Raise the accelerating voltage from zero and the current climbs smoothly — until 4.9 V, where it suddenly drops: electrons now reach the mercury atoms' first excitation energy just before the grid, collide inelastically, hand over exactly 4.9 eV, and arrive too weak to pass. Raise the voltage further and the current climbs again — the electron excites one atom midway and still arrives with energy to spare — until 9.8 V, where it drops again: now it can excite two atoms, or one atom after a longer run. The drops repeat at multiples of 4.9 V, as regular as rungs.
3. **What it proves — atoms take energy only in fixed amounts** — Below 4.9 eV the collisions are elastic — electrons bounce off mercury atoms keeping essentially all their energy, which is why the current first climbs unhindered: the atoms simply refuse smaller energy offerings. At exactly 4.9 eV they accept — the atom jumps to its first excited level, and later sheds the same 4.9 eV, in mercury's case as ultraviolet light of the matching frequency (E = hf). Bohr's model had predicted such levels from spectra; Franck and Hertz measured one directly, with a voltmeter and an ammeter, in collisions — quantisation proved as an electrical measurement, not only as lines of light.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Below the excitation energy, electron–atom collisions are elastic — atoms refuse smaller amounts of energy
2. At 4.9 V (for mercury) electrons excite atoms inelastically, lose exactly 4.9 eV, and the collected current drops
3. The drops repeat at multiples of 4.9 V — one atom excited twice over, or two atoms once each
4. The excitation energy matches the light the excited atom later emits: E = hf
5. Franck–Hertz is direct electrical proof of quantised energy levels, confirming Bohr's picture by collision
