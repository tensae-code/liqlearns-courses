# STEAM project — build a floating power station

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Float first — the platform is a fluids problem

A floating station begins with Archimedes: the platform must displace its own weight of water plus its equipment, and its centre of mass must sit low and centred so it rights itself instead of capsizing. The builder chooses the hull — a wide, shallow platform spreads the load and steadies the station — and tests it loaded before any generator is fitted.

## Sub lesson 2 — Generate — motion into electricity on water

The station's moving water or waves drive the generator: a turbine or oscillating body turns a coil in a magnetic field, and electromagnetic induction does the rest. The model builder chooses the drive (falling water through a small turbine, a rocking float working a linkage, a hand-cranked stand-in for trials) and measures what the station makes — voltage and current on a meter, a lamp lit.

## Sub lesson 3 — Present the system — physics, design and evidence together

The STEAM close is a presentation, not only a model: the physics named (upthrust, induction), the design choices justified (hull shape, drive, materials), and the measurements reported (load floated, output measured, what failed and was changed). Jordan's enrichment grades the join — science, technology, engineering, art and mathematics in one artefact that floats and generates.

## The key ideas

- The platform must float by Archimedes' principle and stay stable, centre of mass low
- Moving water drives a generator; induction turns the motion into electricity
- Output is measured — voltage, current, a lamp lit — not merely claimed
- The project joins science, technology, engineering, art and mathematics
- The presentation must name the physics, justify the design and report the measurements
