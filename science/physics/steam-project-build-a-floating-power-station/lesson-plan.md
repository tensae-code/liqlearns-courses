## Sub lessons
1. **Float first — the platform is a fluids problem** — A floating station begins with Archimedes: the platform must displace its own weight of water plus its equipment, and its centre of mass must sit low and centred so it rights itself instead of capsizing. The builder chooses the hull — a wide, shallow platform spreads the load and steadies the station — and tests it loaded before any generator is fitted.
2. **Generate — motion into electricity on water** — The station's moving water or waves drive the generator: a turbine or oscillating body turns a coil in a magnetic field, and electromagnetic induction does the rest. The model builder chooses the drive (falling water through a small turbine, a rocking float working a linkage, a hand-cranked stand-in for trials) and measures what the station makes — voltage and current on a meter, a lamp lit.
3. **Present the system — physics, design and evidence together** — The STEAM close is a presentation, not only a model: the physics named (upthrust, induction), the design choices justified (hull shape, drive, materials), and the measurements reported (load floated, output measured, what failed and was changed). Jordan's enrichment grades the join — science, technology, engineering, art and mathematics in one artefact that floats and generates.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

5. The presentation must name the physics, justify the design and report the measurements
