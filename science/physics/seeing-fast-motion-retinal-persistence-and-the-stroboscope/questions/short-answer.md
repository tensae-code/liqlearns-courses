# Seeing fast motion — retinal persistence and the stroboscope — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What sets the eye's limit for following fast motion, according to the 7AS-D chapter?
**Answer: Retinal persistence: each image is held about 1/15–1/20 s, so changes arriving faster than that fuse into one another — the chapter grounds the limit in the retina's chemistry (rhodopsin).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A wheel turns at 30 Hz. A strobe flashes at 10 Hz. What is seen and why?
**Answer: Three fixed images (k = 3): the flashes catch the wheel every third period, at three successive phases, and persistence holds all three — the motion is dissected into k = N/N′ = 3 positions.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A vibrating string at 100 Hz is viewed with a strobe at 99 Hz and then at 101 Hz. Describe each view.
**Answer: At 99 Hz (N′ < N): the string appears to vibrate slowly in its true sense, at the 1 Hz mismatch. At 101 Hz (N′ > N): it appears to vibrate slowly reversed — each flash catches it slightly earlier in its cycle.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the waves chapter open with the eye and the strobe before any equation?
**Answer: Because a periodic motion must first be made visible to be measured: persistence explains what the eye can and cannot follow, and the stroboscope turns an invisible-fast vibration into a frozen or slowed one whose frequency can be read.**
