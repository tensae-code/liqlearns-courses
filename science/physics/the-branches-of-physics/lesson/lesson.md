# The branches of physics

## Do it first
Name six things happening around you — a falling cup, music, a lamp, a hot pot. Try to give each one a branch before you read on.

## The branches of physics — the teaching
Physics is organised into branches, and each branch has its own territory. Mechanics studies motion, with and without force, and splits into classical and quantum mechanics. Acoustics studies sound, optics studies light, thermodynamics studies heat and energy, electromagnetism studies electricity and magnetism together, and nuclear physics studies the centre of the atom. When you meet a new phenomenon, the first physics question is: which branch does it belong to?

## The key ideas

- Mechanics studies motion and splits into classical and quantum mechanics.
- Acoustics is the branch about sound; optics is the branch about light.
- Thermodynamics studies heat, and nuclear physics studies the centre of the atom.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the branches of physics to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
