# Lesson plan — The Faraday cage

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The Faraday cage
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the electrostatics strand — PECTAA Grade 11's shielding lesson
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a closed conducting shell keeps its inside free of external electric fields: charge rearranges on the conductor's outer surface so the interior field cancels to zero — the Faraday cage that shields instruments, buildings and people in lightning

## Sub lessons
1. **Charge lives on the outside** — Put a conductor in an external field and its free charges move until they cancel the field inside the material — which piles the charge on the outer surface. Inside a hollow in the conductor there is nothing left to make a field: the interior is field-free however strong the field outside.
2. **Why the inside is exactly zero** — The induced surface charges arrange themselves so their field is everywhere equal and opposite to the applied field within the cavity. It is not that the field is blocked like light by a wall; it is cancelled by charges that moved for precisely that purpose — which is why a mesh with holes much smaller than the job requires still works as a cage.
3. **Cages at work** — Sensitive electronics live in metal cases; buildings and aircraft let lightning's charge run over their outer skin while the inside stays safe; a car struck by lightning protects its occupants the same way. PECTAA's lesson names the cage as electrostatics' most useful consequence: a place where the field is not.
