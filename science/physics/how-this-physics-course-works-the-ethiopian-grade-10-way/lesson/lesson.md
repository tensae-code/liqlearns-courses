## The six-unit journey

This course runs in six units, in this order. First, **Unit 1 — Vector Quantities**: quantities with direction — arrows you can add. Then **Unit 2 — Uniformly Accelerated Motion**: motion that speeds up steadily, with equations to match. Then **Unit 3 — Elasticity and Static Equilibrium of Rigid Body**: stretch, moduli, and bodies that balance. Then **Unit 4 — Static and Current Electricity**: charge, Coulomb's law, fields and circuits. Then **Unit 5 — Magnetism**: poles, fields and electromagnets. Last, **Unit 6 — Electromagnetic Waves and Geometrical Optics**: waves, light, mirrors, lenses and colour.

Arrows, then faster motion, then stretch, spark, magnet, light. The rhythm matches Grade 9 — a Brainstorming question to open, unit outcomes, sections opening with "by the end of this section you should be able to…", Exercises, worked Examples, KEY CONCEPT boxes, Section and Unit summaries, and Review and End-Unit questions — but the mathematics steps up: components, equations of motion, moduli, Coulomb's law, and mirror and lens calculations.

Ethiopian anchors hold the course at home: the Entoto Observatory telescope appears as a question, and birds standing on Ethiopian transmission lines open a famous electricity puzzle.

## The virtual-lab habit — PhET

Every unit closes with **Virtual Labs**: named PhET simulations to run on the soft copy of the book, under the teacher's guidance. The named simulations include Geometric Optics, Bending Light, Color Vision and Radio Waves. A virtual lab is not a game at the end — it is this book's laboratory habit: run the simulation, change one thing at a time, watch what happens, and check it against what the unit taught. When a Grade 10 lesson in this course sends you to a simulation, it is following the book's own closing step for the unit.

## How your lessons are staged — the same steps every time

Behind every unit sits a framing you can feel in the classroom. The curriculum is **competency-based** and **learner-centred**: what matters is what you can do — your knowledge, your values and attitudes, and your skills — and science itself is presented as a dynamic, growing body of knowledge, not a list of fixed facts. Methods are mixed inside one lesson: a discussion to open, then discovery or practical work.

Each unit's lessons follow the same staging: **Starting Off → Input and Model → Guided Practice → Check for Understanding → Where next → Closure** — with period counts per section and an answer checklist to mark your work against. The year's general objectives are the six units themselves: vectors, accelerated motion, bulk-matter properties and equilibrium, charge, Coulomb's law and fields, circuits, magnetism, and light and optics. Knowing the staging helps you learn: at Starting Off, say what you already think; at Check for Understanding, test it honestly before Closure.
