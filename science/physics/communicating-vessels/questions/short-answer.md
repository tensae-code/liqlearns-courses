# Communicating vessels — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A teapot's spout is shorter than the body's top. Why can the pot never be filled above the spout's tip?
**Answer:** Body and spout are communicating vessels: the level in both is one — past the spout's tip the water simply runs out of the spout as fast as it is poured.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, step by step, why the levels must match when one liquid stands in joined vessels.
**Answer:** Suppose one column were higher: at the join its ρgh would exceed the other's, so liquid would be pushed from the high side to the low side until the heights — and so the pressures — equalise. Rest is possible only at one level.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) An unknown liquid balances water with heights 10 cm (unknown) against 8.5 cm (water) measured from the join. Find its density.
**Answer:** ρ_unknown × 10 = 1000 × 8.5, so ρ_unknown = 850 kg/m³.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two working devices that are communicating vessels in disguise.
**Answer:** Any two of: the spirit/water level, the U-tube manometer, canal locks, a water tower and its pipes, a boiler's water gauge glass.
