# Resolving and combining forces — resultant and the parallelogram law

## Do it first
Before you read: two people pull a stuck cart, one straight ahead, one off to the side. The cart feels neither pull separately — it feels one combined pull, in one direction. And a single pull on a slope does two jobs at once, dragging along the slope and pressing into it. Splitting forces and joining forces are the same skill seen from two ends — this lesson teaches both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Resolving a force into components

A force F at an angle θ to a chosen direction is replaced, exactly, by two forces at right angles: the component **along** the direction, F cos θ, and the component **across** it, F sin θ. Nothing is lost or added — the pair does everything the single force did. Resolution is chosen to fit the problem: on a slope, resolve weight into mg sin θ down the slope and mg cos θ into the slope; for a projectile, velocity and force split into horizontal and vertical parts that can be handled one at a time.

## Sub lesson 2 — The parallelogram law and the resultant of two forces

The **parallelogram law**: if two forces A and B acting at a point are drawn as the two sides of a parallelogram, their **resultant** — the single force with the same effect — is the **diagonal** through that point. Its size is R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cos θ), where θ is the angle between the forces when placed tail to tail; its direction makes an angle φ with A given by tan φ = B sin θ ÷ (A + B cos θ). Check the law at its edges: θ = 0 gives R = A + B (pulling together), θ = 180° gives R = |A − B| (opposed), and θ = 90° gives the Pythagoras form R = √(A² + B²).

## Sub lesson 3 — Combining several forces by components

For three or more forces, do not chain parallelograms — resolve every force into x- and y-components, add all the x-components into one ΣFx and all the y-components into ΣFy, and combine that pair once: R = √(ΣFx² + ΣFy²), at tan φ = ΣFy/ΣFx. This is the working method of statics and dynamics alike, and it carries its own check: if ΣFx = ΣFy = 0 the resultant is zero — the forces balance, which is exactly the equilibrium condition the equilibrium lesson builds on.

## The key ideas

- Resolution: a force F at angle θ becomes F cos θ along and F sin θ across the chosen direction — the pair is exactly equivalent to the one force.
- Parallelogram law: the resultant of A and B is the parallelogram's diagonal, R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cos θ).
- Many forces: resolve all, sum the components (ΣFx, ΣFy), combine once — and ΣF = 0 is equilibrium.

## Do It Yourself
1. A 100 N force acts at 30° to the horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical components.
2. Two forces of 30 N and 40 N act at right angles at a point. Find their resultant's size and its angle to the 30 N force.
3. Forces of 60 N and 80 N pull at 60° between them. Use R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cos θ) to find the resultant.
