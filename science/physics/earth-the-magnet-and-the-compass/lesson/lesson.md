# Earth the magnet — and the compass

## Do it first
Watch any compass (or a phone's) while you turn in a circle: the needle refuses to turn with you — it keeps faith with the Earth instead.

[Picture 21914 — Earth with field lines and a compass needle aligning to them]

## Earth the magnet — and the compass — the teaching
The Earth itself behaves as a giant magnet, wrapped in a field that reaches far into space. A compass is a small, free-turning magnetised needle: released, it swings until it settles along the Earth's field — that settling is what 'north' on a compass means. The field also shields life, deflecting harmful charged particles from space. The magnetic poles and the geographic poles are near each other but not the same points, and the angle between their directions at a place is its declination — the compass's small, known lie.

## The key ideas

- Earth behaves as a giant magnet with a field reaching into space.
- A compass needle aligns with Earth's field.
- Magnetic and geographic poles differ; the local angle between them is declination.

## Say it to your companion
Explain earth the magnet — and the compass to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
