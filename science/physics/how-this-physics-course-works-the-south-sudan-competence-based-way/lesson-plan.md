# Lesson plan — How this Physics course works — the South Sudan competence-based way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** How this Physics course works — the South Sudan competence-based way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), as the framing lesson of South Sudan's four-year Physics course — the Part 1 introduction every Teacher's Guide (Secondary 1–4) opens with
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** South Sudan's Physics course is competence-based: every topic in every year opens with key inquiry questions, teaches through activities, and is judged by learning outcomes in three strands — knowledge and understanding, skills, and attitudes and values — with cross-cutting issues and special-needs inclusion written into the lessons themselves (teacher-guide evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
1. **A competence-based curriculum — what a Physics lesson must build** — All four South Sudan Teacher's Guides open with the same Part 1: the organisation of the book, the background to the **new competence-based curriculum**, the basic requirements for an effective Physics lesson, and assessment. A competence is more than knowing: it is knowledge **used** — in skills practised with the hands and in attitudes and values carried out of the classroom. So a South Sudan Physics lesson is not finished when the facts are copied; it is finished when the learner can *do* something with them — measure, build, argue, decide. The guides state the requirements plainly: an effective lesson needs its inquiry opened, its activities resourced (with local materials where the kit is thin), and its outcomes checked — the framing this lesson records on teacher-guide evidence.
2. **The shape of every topic — inquiry questions, activities, three-strand outcomes** — Open any unit of any guide and the shape repeats. First a **'Learn about / Key inquiry questions' table**: the questions the topic must answer, posed before the content. Then **learning outcomes in three strands** — Knowledge and understanding / Skills / Attitudes and values — so a topic promises manipulation and judgement alongside facts. Then the teaching itself, **activity by activity**, keyed to the student book's numbered activities, with assessment in the S2 guide's three modes — **Observation, Conversation, Product**: watch them do it, hear them explain it, mark what they made. And the student books mirror the shape from the learner's side: every topic closes with a **Topic summary, a 'new words' list and a Topic Test**. Inquiry in, activity through, outcomes checked, summary and test to close — S1 to S4, the same skeleton.
3. **Cross-cutting issues and inclusion — written into the Physics itself** — The guides refuse to bolt the rest of education onto Physics from outside. **Cross-cutting issues — environment and sustainability, peace education, life skills** — are written into the topics themselves: energy sources carry their environmental effects inside the physics lesson (S2 Topic 5), sound carries pollution and rights (S2 Topic 8.10), electricity carries safety as examined content (S4 Topic 8). **Special-needs inclusion** is likewise written into the activities: the S2 guide's equilibrium work includes **touch-based activities for learners with sight problems**, so the same competence is reached by a different sense. The competence-based way, whole: physics content, human content and access planned as one lesson, not three.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State what competence-based means here: outcomes in knowledge and understanding, skills, and attitudes and values.
2. Describe the shape of every South Sudan topic, from inquiry questions to Topic Test, and the three assessment modes.
3. Name the cross-cutting issues and say how inclusion is written into the activities.

## What you need
One stocked lesson with a South Sudan source and paper: the class rebuilds a real topic into the South Sudan shape — questions, three-strand outcomes, assessment modes, summary and test.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first and do the opening activity with real objects before reading any explanation; the explanation is the story of that activity.
- Mixes the sub lessons up → re-read the key ideas in order; each key idea is one sub lesson compressed to a line. The usual confusion is the three strands — if an outcome is only something a learner can recite, two strands are still missing.
