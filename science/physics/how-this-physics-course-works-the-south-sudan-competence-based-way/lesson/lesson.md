# How this Physics course works — the South Sudan competence-based way

## Do it first
Before you read: pick any Physics topic you have met in this course and ask — how was it opened, what were you promised you would be able to do, and how would anyone know you could do it? South Sudan's course answers those three questions the same way in every topic of every year. This lesson is that answer, taken from the Teacher's Guides' own introductions.

## Sub lesson 1 — A competence-based curriculum — what a Physics lesson must build

The course's contract, from Part 1 of the guides: Physics is taught to build **competences** — knowledge and understanding, skills, and attitudes and values together — under a competence-based curriculum introduced across South Sudan's schools. The teacher's guides, not the student books, carry this framing, so this lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only, as the Zambia and Uganda course-way lessons do.

## Sub lesson 2 — The shape of every topic — inquiry questions, activities, three-strand outcomes

The skeleton, named once for all four years: **key inquiry questions** open the topic; **outcomes in three strands** (knowledge and understanding / skills / attitudes and values) set its promises; **per-activity teaching guidelines** carry it, assessed by **observation, conversation and product**; and the student book closes each topic with **summary, new words and Topic Test**. A learner moving from S1 to S4 never has to relearn how a topic works — only what it contains.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cross-cutting issues and inclusion — written into the Physics itself

Two commitments distinguish the South Sudan way in its own guides' words: **cross-cutting issues inside the topics** (environment, peace education, life skills — placed where the physics touches them, from energy's environmental effects to sound pollution), and **inclusion inside the activities** (adaptations such as touch-based equilibrium work for learners with sight problems, written into the guide's own teaching guidelines). This lesson records both on the guides' evidence — the framing every South Sudan placement in this course was taught under.

## The key ideas

- South Sudan's Physics is competence-based: outcomes are competences — knowledge and understanding, skills, and attitudes and values together (teacher-guide evidence)
- Every topic opens with a Learn about / Key inquiry questions table and closes, in the student book, with a Topic summary, new words list and Topic Test
- Assessment in the guides works three ways: Observation, Conversation, Product
- Cross-cutting issues (environment, peace education, life skills) and special-needs inclusion are written into the topics and activities themselves

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: South Sudan teaches Physics the competence-based way: key inquiry questions open each topic, outcomes are promised in three strands — knowledge, skills, attitudes and values — assessment watches, listens and marks products, and every topic closes with summary, new words and a Topic Test. Environment, peace education, life skills and inclusion live inside the lessons, not beside them. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take one topic from this course and write its three-strand outcomes yourself: one knowledge outcome, one skill, one attitude or value.
2. A teacher says 'my learners can recite the pressure formula.' Using the three assessment modes, say what else the South Sudan guides would ask her to check.
3. Find the cross-cutting issue inside one topic you know (energy, sound or electricity) and write the sentence that joins it to the physics.
