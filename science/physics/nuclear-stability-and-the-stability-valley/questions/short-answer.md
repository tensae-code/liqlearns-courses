# Nuclear stability and the stability valley — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain the valley's bend with the two forces.
**Answer: The strong force binds only between near neighbours, so its total binding grows roughly with nucleon number; proton–proton repulsion acts across the whole nucleus and grows faster with Z. Extra neutrons add binding without repulsion, so stability at high Z demands a rising N/Z ratio — the bend.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A nucleus has too many protons for the valley. Name its two possible decays and the change each makes.
**Answer: Beta-plus decay or orbital electron capture; both turn a proton into a neutron — Z falls by 1, N rises by 1 — stepping the nucleus down-left toward the valley.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does alpha decay suit the heaviest nuclei better than beta decay?
**Answer: Beta decay changes Z by one while leaving the nucleus just as heavy and crowded; alpha emission removes two protons and two neutrons at once, cutting both the repulsion and the size — the large step a far-off-valley heavy nucleus needs.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A nucleus is plotted far above the valley. Predict its fate without any table.
**Answer: It is neutron-rich and radioactive: it will beta-minus decay, possibly in a chain of steps, each moving it toward the valley, until a stable nucleus is reached.**
