# Nuclear stability and the stability valley

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The valley — where the stable nuclei live

The N–Z chart plots each nucleus by its neutron number (vertical) and proton number (horizontal). Stable nuclei are not scattered at random: they lie in a narrow band, the stability valley. For light nuclei the valley runs along N = Z; as Z grows, the valley bends upward — by lead, stability needs about one and a half neutrons per proton.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the valley bends — two forces in competition

Protons repel each other electrically at every distance across the nucleus, while the strong nuclear force binds only near neighbours. Adding neutrons adds binding without adding repulsion — the glue dilutes the repulsion. The heavier the nucleus, the more neutron glue per proton stability demands — which is the bending, in one sentence. Beyond bismuth, no recipe works: every heavier nucleus is radioactive.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reading decay off the map — the way back to the valley

A nucleus plotted off the valley decays toward it: too many neutrons for its Z and it beta-minus decays (a neutron becomes a proton, moving it down-right toward the valley); too many protons and it beta-plus decays or captures an electron (moving down-left); the very heavy shed alpha particles, cutting both numbers. The valley turns the decay kinds from a list into directions on a map.

## The key ideas

- Stable nuclei occupy a narrow band — the stability valley — on the N–Z chart
- Light stable nuclei have N ≈ Z; heavier ones need more neutrons per proton
- The valley bends because proton repulsion grows while the strong force binds only neighbours
- Beyond bismuth (Z = 83) no nucleus is stable
- Nuclei decay toward the valley: neutron-rich by beta-minus, proton-rich by beta-plus or capture, the heaviest by alpha emission
