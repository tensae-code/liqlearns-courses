# Lesson plan — The ripple tank — seeing waves

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The ripple tank — seeing waves
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the waves strand — Punjab Grade 10's wave-visualisation apparatus
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a shallow tray of water, lit from above and rippling from a vibrating bar, projects waves you can watch, measure and steer: the ripple tank is Punjab Grade 10's way of making reflection, refraction and diffraction visible before they are believed
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **The apparatus — water, light and a shadow screen** — A shallow glass-bottomed tray holds a thin layer of water; a lamp above casts the ripples' shadows onto a white screen below, and a motor-driven bar (or a dipper for single pulses) sets the surface moving. Crests appear as bright lines, and the projected pattern can be drawn, timed and measured as it moves.
2. **Straight and circular waves on demand** — The straight bar makes plane waves — parallel crests marching together; a point dipper makes circular waves spreading from a centre. Frequency is set by the vibrator, wavelength read off the screen with a ruler, and the wave speed checked against v = fλ on real, visible waves.
3. **Barriers and shallows — steering the waves** — Place a barrier and the waves reflect with angle in equal to angle out; leave a gap and they diffract through it, spreading wider as the gap narrows toward the wavelength; lay a plate under part of the tray to make a shallow region and the waves slow and bend — refraction, seen. Every later wave rule in the unit is first met here, as something the eye caught.
