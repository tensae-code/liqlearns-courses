# The ripple tank — seeing waves

## Do it first
Build the school ripple tank (or a baking tray under a bright lamp) — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — The apparatus — water, light and a shadow screen

A shallow glass-bottomed tray holds a thin layer of water; a lamp above casts the ripples' shadows onto a white screen below, and a motor-driven bar (or a dipper for single pulses) sets the surface moving. Crests appear as bright lines, and the projected pattern can be drawn, timed and measured as it moves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Straight and circular waves on demand

The straight bar makes plane waves — parallel crests marching together; a point dipper makes circular waves spreading from a centre. Frequency is set by the vibrator, wavelength read off the screen with a ruler, and the wave speed checked against v = fλ on real, visible waves.

## Sub lesson 3 — Barriers and shallows — steering the waves

Place a barrier and the waves reflect with angle in equal to angle out; leave a gap and they diffract through it, spreading wider as the gap narrows toward the wavelength; lay a plate under part of the tray to make a shallow region and the waves slow and bend — refraction, seen. Every later wave rule in the unit is first met here, as something the eye caught.

## The key ideas

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: a shallow tray of water, lit from above and rippling from a vibrating bar, projects waves you can watch, measure and steer: the ripple tank is Punjab Grade 10's way of making reflection, refraction and diffraction visible before they are believed. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.
