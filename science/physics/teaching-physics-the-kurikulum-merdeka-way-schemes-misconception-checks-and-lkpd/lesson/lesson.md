# Teaching physics the Kurikulum Merdeka way — schemes, misconception checks and LKPD

## Do it first
Before you read: imagine you must teach a topic tomorrow — say, why a ship floats. On a scrap of paper, write the three things you would want decided before you walk in: how the time is divided, what wrong idea your learners probably bring, and what they will hold in their hands while they work. Keep your three answers beside you; this lesson is about a teaching guide that decides exactly those three things, for every chapter, in advance.

## Sub lesson 1 — The scheme first — Skema Pembelajaran

In this way of teaching, no chapter is improvised. For each chapter the guide first lays out a **Skema Pembelajaran** — a learning scheme: how many meetings the chapter takes, how the hours fall, and which strategy each meeting uses — opening, investigation, discussion, check. The scheme is the chapter seen whole before it is taught in parts. Its value is order: the teacher knows, before meeting one, what meeting four must be ready to do, which activity needs apparatus booked ahead, and where the checks fall — so the learners meet a journey, not a sequence of surprises.

## Sub lesson 2 — Misconception checks — teach against the named wrong idea

The guide names, for every chapter, the **miskonsepsi** — the misconceptions learners commonly carry into that topic: that a moving object needs a force to keep moving, that current is used up by a lamp, that heat and temperature are the same thing. Teaching is then planned **against** the named wrong idea. The misconception is surfaced at the chapter's opening — learners commit to an answer before teaching — the chapter's activities are chosen so the evidence argues with that answer, and the closing checks ask, in effect, whether the wrong idea has moved. A misconception that is never named survives the chapter untouched; a named one becomes the chapter's real target.

## Sub lesson 3 — LKPD and the people around the lesson — worksheets, parents, reflection

The learners' work rides on **LKPD** — student worksheets that structure each activity: what to do, what to record, what to conclude — so the investigation in the learner's hands is the same investigation the scheme planned. Around the lesson, the guide adds two more pieces. **Parent-interaction** notes bring the home into the chapter — what parents are told, and what a learner can show or ask at home, so learning does not stop at the school gate. And each chapter closes with **teacher reflection** and, where the first route missed some learners, **alternative-learning** routes — a second way into the same idea — so the next time this chapter is taught, it is taught better than the last.

## The key ideas

- Every chapter is planned whole first, in a Skema Pembelajaran: meetings, hours and strategies laid out before teaching starts.
- The guide lists each topic's common misconceptions (miskonsepsi), and teaching is planned against them: surface the wrong idea, let the activity argue with it, check it has moved.
- LKPD worksheets carry the learners' work; parent-interaction notes, alternative-learning routes and teacher reflection surround and improve the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
1. Choose a topic you could teach. Write its Skema Pembelajaran in miniature: three meetings, one line each — the meeting's job and its strategy.
2. For that topic, list two miskonsepsi learners commonly bring, and for each one name the activity or demonstration whose evidence argues against it.
3. Draft the front of an LKPD for your first meeting: the activity's aim in one sentence, a table with the columns learners will record in, and the concluding question they must answer in their own words. Add one parent-interaction line: what the learner shows or asks at home that evening.
