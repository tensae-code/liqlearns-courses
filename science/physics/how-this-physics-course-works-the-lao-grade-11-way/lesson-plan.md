## Sub lessons
1. **Five parts, twenty-five lessons** — The year's arc: Part I Structure and Properties of Solids, Liquids and Gases (Lessons 1–5), Part II Electrostatics (6–10), Part III Direct Current (11–15), Part IV Electric Current in Various Media (16–21), Part V Geometrical Optics (22–25). Against Grade 10's mechanics year, mechanics has vanished — the year belongs to matter, electricity and light. The arc is a widening: from what matter is, to charge at rest, to charge flowing in wires, to charge flowing in everything else, to light's geometry.
2. **Opening with molecules — evidence first** — The book opens with the composition of matter: molecules, and Brownian motion — pollen grains jiggling in water — with Perrin's work, as the visible evidence that molecules exist and move; Avogadro's number (6.02×10²³), atomic diameters in picometres and molar mass are worked in the opening lesson's body. Structure first, properties after: solids, liquids and gases are then treated by what their particles do, and the gas laws and gas energy close the part. Physics here starts from evidence for the invisible, not from definitions.
3. **Current in various media — the six-lesson part** — Part IV is Laos' signature: current taught medium by medium in six lessons — in metals (the electron sea), in electrolytes, in gases, in air/rarefied air, and in semiconductors (with vacuum conduction in the strand). Most courses teach current once, in wires; the Lao course asks how charge moves in each kind of matter in turn, and only then turns to light. The teacher guide parallels the book lesson for lesson and adds its learner-centred framing (see the WALT and WILF lesson).
