# How this Physics course works — the Lao Grade 11 (ມ.6) way

## Do it first
Before you read: most physics years open with motion. Laos' Grade 11 year opens with jiggling pollen grains in water — specks you can see, dancing for no visible reason. Why would a national course choose that dance as its first page? This lesson is the map of that year: its five parts, its evidence-first opening, and its most distinctive part.

## Sub lesson 1 — Five parts, twenty-five lessons

The year's arc: Part I Structure and Properties of Solids, Liquids and Gases (Lessons 1–5), Part II Electrostatics (6–10), Part III Direct Current (11–15), Part IV Electric Current in Various Media (16–21), Part V Geometrical Optics (22–25). Against Grade 10's mechanics year, mechanics has vanished — the year belongs to matter, electricity and light. The arc is a widening: from what matter is, to charge at rest, to charge flowing in wires, to charge flowing in everything else, to light's geometry.

## Sub lesson 2 — Opening with molecules — evidence first

The book opens with the composition of matter: molecules, and Brownian motion — pollen grains jiggling in water — with Perrin's work, as the visible evidence that molecules exist and move; Avogadro's number (6.02×10²³), atomic diameters in picometres and molar mass are worked in the opening lesson's body. Structure first, properties after: solids, liquids and gases are then treated by what their particles do, and the gas laws and gas energy close the part. Physics here starts from evidence for the invisible, not from definitions.

## Sub lesson 3 — Current in various media — the six-lesson part

Part IV is Laos' signature: current taught medium by medium in six lessons — in metals (the electron sea), in electrolytes, in gases, in air/rarefied air, and in semiconductors (with vacuum conduction in the strand). Most courses teach current once, in wires; the Lao course asks how charge moves in each kind of matter in turn, and only then turns to light. The teacher guide parallels the book lesson for lesson and adds its learner-centred framing (see the WALT and WILF lesson).

## The key ideas

- The Lao Grade 11 course: 5 parts / 25 lessons — matter, electrostatics, DC, current in media, geometrical optics
- The year opens with Brownian motion and Perrin as visible evidence for molecules; Avogadro's number is worked in Lesson 1
- Part IV teaches conduction medium by medium in six lessons — metals, electrolytes, gases, air, semiconductors
- The teacher guide parallels the book lesson for lesson (titles cited from it where the student file's legacy encoding garbles its own)
