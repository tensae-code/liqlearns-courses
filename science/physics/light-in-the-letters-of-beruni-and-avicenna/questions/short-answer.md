# Light in the letters of Beruni and Avicenna — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Give one everyday fact that supports the receiving theory of vision.
**Answer: We cannot see in a dark room though our eyes are open and unchanged — if the eye sent out the seeing rays, darkness would not stop them. Light must arrive from the object.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is there no rainbow at noon in summer (in the scholars' frame)?
**Answer: The bow stands opposite the Sun at a fixed angle; with the Sun high, the opposite point and its bow lie below the horizon — the geometry has nowhere in the visible sky to stand.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How does the water sphere both magnify and burn?
**Answer: Its curved surfaces refract rays toward the centre-line: rays from a page spread as if from a larger object (magnification), and parallel sunlight converges to one small focus where its energy concentrates enough to kindle tinder.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) What makes the Beruni–Avicenna letters physics, though no instrument appears in them?
**Answer: They proceed by claim, counter-case and reply — each claim is tested against pointed examples (dazzle, darkness, after-images) and revised or defended. Argument disciplined by cases is the written form of experiment.**
