# Light in the letters of Beruni and Avicenna

## Do it first
Before you read: when you look at a candle, does something leave your eye and touch the flame, or does something leave the flame and enter your eye? Two of the sharpest minds of their age argued this by letter. Decide your side before you read theirs.

## Sub lesson 1 — How do we see? — the letters' first question

The old Greek view sent visual rays out of the eyes to feel the world, like fingers of light. Avicenna argued the reverse, and argued it hard: the eye receives; light from the object enters the eye and forms the image — if rays poured out of our eyes, we would see in the dark and tire like lamps. Beruni pressed him with cases — the dazzle of bright light, the after-image, the squint — testing where the receiving theory strains. The correspondence is physics by post: claim, counter-case, reply. Modern optics sides with receiving: the eye is a camera, not a torch.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rainbow — a bow with a geometry

Both scholars treated the rainbow as a thing to be explained, not a sign: sunlight meeting rain. In the letters and their works the bow is tied to the Sun's position — it stands opposite the Sun, morning and evening, never at noon in summer — and to the spray of droplets each bending and bouncing the light. The full account (refraction in, reflection inside the drop, refraction out, colours split by bending) came later, but the frame was theirs: a rainbow is geometry plus droplets, and its place in the sky is predictable from the Sun's.

## Sub lesson 3 — The water sphere — a lens in a bowl

The letters' most hands-on exhibit: a glass sphere filled with water gathers sunlight to a burning point and magnifies what is seen through it — a lens before lenses were understood. Beruni and Avicenna discuss how the sphere bends rays toward its centre-line, concentrating the Sun's heat enough to kindle tinder. It is the seed of the light chapter's whole second half: refraction bending light, spheres and lenses forming images, instruments built from bent light — argued out, a thousand years ago, in letters between Khwarizm and Bukhara.

## The key ideas

- The correspondence argues vision: Avicenna — the eye receives light; the old view — rays leave the eye. Experiment sides with receiving
- The rainbow, for both scholars, is sunlight in rain — standing opposite the Sun, a matter of geometry, not omen
- A water-filled sphere bends light to a focus and magnifies — a working lens, discussed in the letters
- The letters model the method: claim, counter-case, reply — physics done by argument and test cases

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: in their famous correspondence, Beruni and Avicenna argued the physics of light itself: does seeing send rays out of the eye or receive them in, what makes the rainbow's bow, and how a sphere of water bends light like a lens — questions the Grade 6 book keeps at the heart of its light chapter. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.
