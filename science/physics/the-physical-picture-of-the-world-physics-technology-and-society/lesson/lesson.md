# The physical picture of the world — physics, technology and society

## Do it first
Before you read: you have spent three books learning physics topic by topic — motion, heat, light, atoms. What is it all a picture of? The book's final chapter steps back and answers, and then asks what the picture is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — One picture — matter, field, law

The physical picture assembles the course: the world is matter in motion — particles building atoms, atoms building everything — interacting through fields (gravitational, electromagnetic) by laws that measurement keeps correcting. Each age's picture replaced the last: a clockwork of forces, then fields, then quanta and nuclei. The picture is called physical because its test is physical — experiment — and it is honestly a picture: the best current map, not the territory's final truth. Holding it whole, after three years of parts, is the chapter's first job.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the picture builds — physics into technology

Turn the picture outward and it becomes the modern world: power stations and grids from electromagnetism, electronics from semiconductors, medicine from radiation and optics, flight from mechanics, communication from waves. The chapter traces the chain in both directions — technology also feeds physics, giving it instruments (the telescope, the accelerator) that open the next picture. Society is in the loop: what gets built is chosen, funded and used by people, so the picture carries responsibilities — safety, environment, access — alongside its powers.

## Sub lesson 3 — Physics in Uzbekistan — the picture at home

The book closes at home: Uzbekistan's own physics — the solar furnace and heliotechnics of its sunny plateau, nuclear research at its institutes, the physics departments and the school course itself, this book in the reader's hands. The series that began with Beruni ends with the student's own country as a working part of the picture: measuring sunlight, splitting atoms, teaching the next class. The final claim of the three books is quiet and total: physics is not a foreign subject imported into Uzbekistan — it is one of Uzbekistan's own oldest trades, and the reader is its newest apprentice.

## The key ideas

- The physical picture: matter in motion, interacting through fields, under laws that experiment keeps correcting — a map, honestly revisable
- Technology is the picture applied: power, electronics, medicine, flight, communication — and instruments flow back, opening the next picture
- Society chooses and funds what gets built, so the picture carries responsibilities with its powers
- The series closes at home: Uzbekistan's solar, nuclear and university physics — the reader as the picture's newest apprentice

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: the Grade 9 book closes by gathering everything into one physical picture of the world — matter, fields and their laws — and tracing what that picture builds: the technology of modern life and physics' own work in Uzbekistan, from solar furnaces to nuclear research. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Assemble the picture in one paragraph: name matter's building order and the two great fields of the course.
2. Trace one technology backwards to its physics (choose: the grid, the phone, the X-ray) in three links.
3. The chapter claims physics is one of Uzbekistan's oldest trades. Give two pieces of evidence from this series (one historical, one modern).
