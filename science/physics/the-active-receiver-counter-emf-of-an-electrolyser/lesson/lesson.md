# The active receiver — counter-EMF of an electrolyser

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — A receiver that pushes back

Feed an electrolyser from a variable supply and plot its characteristic: below a threshold voltage almost nothing flows; above it the points settle on a rising straight line that, extended back, meets the voltage axis not at zero but at E′ — the counter-electromotive force. The receiver is active: part of every volt applied must first match E′ before any current-driven chemistry proceeds — the open-circuit voltage the device holds of itself, like a generator facing the wrong way.

## Sub lesson 2 — The law — U = E′ + r′I

Above threshold the line is straight: U = E′ + r′I. The slope r′ is the receiver's internal resistance (its electrolyte and electrodes resisting); the intercept E′ is fixed by the chemistry being driven. Compare the generator's U = E − rI: same furniture, opposite signs — the generator's line falls from E as it gives power out; the receiver's line rises from E′ as it takes power in.

## Sub lesson 3 — The energy account — chemistry stored, heat lost

Multiply by I: UI = E′I + r′I². Of the power taken in, E′I is stored as chemical energy (the decomposition actually performed — gases evolved, metal plated), and r′I² is wasted as Joule heat in the receiver's resistance. The useful share E′/U rises as the current falls — run an electrolyser gently and more of each joule becomes chemistry.

## The key ideas

- An active receiver (electrolyser) has a counter-EMF E′ — it pushes back against the applied voltage
- Its characteristic is a rising line NOT through the origin: U = E′ + r′I
- E′ is the intercept (the open-circuit voltage it keeps); r′ is the slope — its internal resistance
- Mirror of the generator: generator U = E − rI falls; receiver U = E′ + r′I rises
- Energy: UI = E′I stored as chemistry + r′I² lost as heat
