# Lesson plan — The active receiver — counter-EMF of an electrolyser

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The active receiver — counter-EMF of an electrolyser
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 — Physics expansion) — a new lesson built for the Mauritania placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an electrolyser is not a passive resistor: driven by a current, it pushes back with its own voltage — a counter-EMF E′ — so its characteristic is a rising straight line that does NOT pass through the origin, U = E′ + r′I, where E′ is the open-circuit voltage the receiver keeps and r′ its internal resistance

## Sub lessons
1. **A receiver that pushes back** — Feed an electrolyser from a variable supply and plot its characteristic: below a threshold voltage almost nothing flows; above it the points settle on a rising straight line that, extended back, meets the voltage axis not at zero but at E′ — the counter-electromotive force. The receiver is active: part of every volt applied must first match E′ before any current-driven chemistry proceeds — the open-circuit voltage the device holds of itself, like a generator facing the wrong way.
2. **The law — U = E′ + r′I** — Above threshold the line is straight: U = E′ + r′I. The slope r′ is the receiver's internal resistance (its electrolyte and electrodes resisting); the intercept E′ is fixed by the chemistry being driven. Compare the generator's U = E − rI: same furniture, opposite signs — the generator's line falls from E as it gives power out; the receiver's line rises from E′ as it takes power in.
3. **The energy account — chemistry stored, heat lost** — Multiply by I: UI = E′I + r′I². Of the power taken in, E′I is stored as chemical energy (the decomposition actually performed — gases evolved, metal plated), and r′I² is wasted as Joule heat in the receiver's resistance. The useful share E′/U rises as the current falls — run an electrolyser gently and more of each joule becomes chemistry.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. An active receiver (electrolyser) has a counter-EMF E′ — it pushes back against the applied voltage
2. Its characteristic is a rising line NOT through the origin: U = E′ + r′I
3. E′ is the intercept (the open-circuit voltage it keeps); r′ is the slope — its internal resistance
4. Mirror of the generator: generator U = E − rI falls; receiver U = E′ + r′I rises
5. Energy: UI = E′I stored as chemistry + r′I² lost as heat
