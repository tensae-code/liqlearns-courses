# The active receiver — counter-EMF of an electrolyser — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is an electrolyser called an ACTIVE receiver while a resistor is passive?
**Answer: A resistor's characteristic passes through the origin (U = 0 when I = 0) — it only receives. The electrolyser keeps its own voltage E′ with no current and pushes back against the supply: it carries a source-like term of its own, so it is active.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) An electrolyser (E′ = 3 V, r′ = 1.5 Ω) runs at 4 A. Find the applied voltage and split the power.
**Answer: U = E′ + r′I = 3 + 6 = 9 V. Power in: UI = 36 W; stored chemically: E′I = 12 W; lost as heat: r′I² = 24 W.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, from U = E′ + r′I, why the current stays near zero until the applied voltage approaches E′.
**Answer: Until U covers E′, the counter-EMF is not matched and no driving margin (U − E′ = r′I) exists to push current through r′; only the excess of U over E′ drives current.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does the useful share of an electrolyser's energy change with current, and why?
**Answer: It falls as current rises: the stored share is E′/U and U = E′ + r′I grows with I — the r′I² heat term grows quadratically while chemistry grows only linearly.**
