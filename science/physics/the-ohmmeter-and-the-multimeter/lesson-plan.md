# Lesson plan — The ohmmeter and the multimeter

## Sub lessons
1. **Measuring resistance directly — the ohmmeter** — An ohmmeter is a moving-coil meter movement, its own internal battery, a variable zero-adjust resistor and the unknown resistance, all in series. Connected across the unknown resistor, the battery drives a current I = E/(R_internal + R_unknown) through the coil: the bigger the unknown resistance, the smaller the current and the smaller the deflection. The pointer's position therefore prices the resistance directly — no separate supply, no calculation.
2. **Zero adjustment and the crowded scale** — Between those ends the scale is badly non-linear: equal steps of resistance crowd together toward the high end, because deflection follows 1/(R_internal + R_unknown), not R_unknown itself. Readings are trustworthy only near the centre of the scale, so the ohmmeter offers ranges (×1, ×10, ×100, ×1k) that swap the internal resistance and move the unknown's value toward mid-scale.
3. **The multimeter — one instrument, many jobs** — The multimeter (avometer) packages the three instruments of the electricity course in one case: a voltmeter (high resistance, in parallel), an ammeter (low resistance, in series) and the ohmmeter, in AC and DC versions of the ranges, selected by one rotary switch. The same meter movement serves them all — shunts make the current ranges, multipliers make the voltage ranges, the internal battery powers the ohms ranges.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: The ohmmeter puts its own battery in series with the meter movement and the unknown resistor: deflection falls as resistance rises.
2. Say, in their own words: Its scale is reversed (0 Ω at full deflection, right; ∞ at rest, left) and non-linear — accurate only near mid-scale, so ranges are chosen to put the reading there.
3. Say, in their own words: Short the probes and re-zero with the zero-ohms adjuster on every range and as the battery ages; never measure a live circuit or a component still wired in.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the ohms scale like the voltage scale → it is reversed and non-linear: right end is 0 Ω, left end is ∞, and only mid-scale readings are precise.
- Measures a resistor in a live or wired-in circuit → the ohmmeter's own battery drives its test current; outside voltage can wreck the movement, and parallel paths falsify the value. Dead and disconnected, always.
