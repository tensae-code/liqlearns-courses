# The ohmmeter and the multimeter — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain why the ohmmeter's scale runs in the opposite direction to its voltmeter scale.
**Answer: Deflection grows with current. In the ohmmeter the current is E/(R_internal + R_unknown): maximum when the unknown is 0 (probes shorted) and zero when the unknown is infinite — so 0 Ω sits at the full-deflection end and ∞ at the rest end, the reverse of a voltmeter's rising scale.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does changing the ohms range force you to re-zero the instrument?
**Answer: Each range swaps the internal resistance in series with the movement, changing the current that flows with the probes shorted, and the battery's voltage also drifts as it ages. The zero-ohms adjuster trims the circuit so that the shorted-probes current is once again exactly full deflection.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A resistor measures 330 Ω alone but 165 Ω when measured across its position in a dead circuit board. Account for the two numbers.
**Answer: On the board, a second 330 Ω path sits in parallel with the resistor through the board's wiring; the ohmmeter prices the combination, 330 ∥ 330 = 165 Ω. The component must be lifted (at least one end disconnected) for its own value.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the three instruments combined in a multimeter and the connection rule for each.
**Answer: Voltmeter — in parallel across the part, high resistance; ammeter — in series in the branch, low resistance; ohmmeter — across the dead, disconnected component only, powered by the meter's own battery.**
