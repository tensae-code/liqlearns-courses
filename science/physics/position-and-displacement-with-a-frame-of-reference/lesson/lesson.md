# Position and displacement — with a frame of reference

## Do it first
Mark a 'home' stone as origin. Give the positions of three landmarks from it; then move the origin and watch every position change while journeys between them stay the same.

## Position and displacement — with a frame of reference — the teaching
Position is meaningless until you fix a frame of reference — an origin and directions to measure from. The same car sits at different positions in two frames that use different origins, yet its displacement — the vector change of position, final minus initial — tells the journey's true step. Grade 10 restates Grade 9's ideas at exactly the level the accelerated-motion unit needs: fix the frame, read positions, subtract for displacement.

**Keep the formula:** Displacement = final position − initial position

## The key ideas

- Position needs a frame of reference: origin plus directions.
- Displacement = final position − initial position (a vector).
- Changing the origin changes positions, not the displacement.

## Say it to your companion

Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
