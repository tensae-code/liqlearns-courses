# Video script — Photons and the photoelectric effect

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **light arrives in packets**.

Shine a bright red lamp on metal — nothing. Shine a dim ultraviolet lamp — electrons fly off at once. If light were only a wave, bright should always win. It does not, because light comes in packets called photons. Each packet carries energy set by its frequency: red packets are small, ultraviolet packets are big. An electron takes its escape energy from one single packet, so only a big enough packet can free it — that is the photoelectric effect, and Einstein's little equation keeps the accounts: the photon's energy pays the metal's escape price, and whatever is left becomes the electron's speed.
