# Photons and the photoelectric effect

## Do it first
Before you read: picture two lamps shining on a clean metal plate in the dark. The first is a **bright red lamp** — as bright as you like, shining for as long as you like. Nothing happens: not one electron leaves the metal. The second is a **dim ultraviolet lamp**, far weaker to look at. The instant it switches on, electrons fly off the plate. Dim beats bright, instantly, completely. If light were just a wave pouring energy onto the metal, brightness should win. Something else is going on. Keep that puzzle; this lesson solves it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Photons — light in packets

In 1900, trying to explain the glow of hot objects, Max Planck proposed the **quantum hypothesis**: energy is not exchanged in a smooth flow but in small packets called **quanta** (quantum = 'how much', one packet). For light of frequency f, every packet carries the same energy:

**E = hf**

where h is **Planck's constant**, a tiny fixed number, h ≈ 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s. In 1905 Albert Einstein took the hypothesis at full strength for light itself: a beam of light is a stream of these packets, later named **photons**.

Two ideas in E = hf run everything that follows:

- **Frequency sets the size of each packet.** Double the frequency, double the energy of every photon. Ultraviolet photons are energetic; red photons are feeble — because ultraviolet's frequency is higher.
- **Brightness (intensity) sets how many packets there are.** A brighter lamp sends more photons per second — but each one carries exactly the same E = hf as before.

Work one number. Ultraviolet light of frequency 1.0 × 10¹⁵ Hz carries photons of E = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 1.0 × 10¹⁵ ≈ **6.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J** each — about 4.1 electron-volts (1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J), the energy unit atomic physics prefers because photon energies are so small in joules.

## Sub lesson 2 — The photoelectric effect — light knocking electrons out

The **photoelectric effect** is the ejection of electrons from a metal surface when light of high enough frequency falls on it. Photographers' light meters, automatic doors and solar cells all descend from it. Three observed facts define it — and each is a blow to the pure wave picture:

1. **There is a threshold frequency.** Below a certain frequency f₀ — different for each metal — **no electrons are ejected at all**, however bright the light or however long it shines. Above f₀, ejection happens even with feeble light. A wave should eventually pile up enough energy at any frequency; reality says frequency alone decides.
2. **Ejection is instant.** The first electrons leave the moment the light arrives, with no build-up delay — as if each electron is struck once, cleanly, not slowly heated by a wave.
3. **Brightness changes the count, not the speed.** Brighter above-threshold light ejects **more** electrons per second, but the fastest electrons fly no faster. Only raising the **frequency** makes the ejected electrons faster.

The photon picture settles all three at a stroke. An electron is freed by absorbing **one photon, whole**. If that photon's energy hf is less than the energy needed to escape the metal, the electron simply cannot get out — and a million such photons arriving one by one change nothing, because no electron collects them. That is the threshold, the instant ejection, and the brightness rule, all from one idea.

## Sub lesson 3 — Einstein's equation — the energy account

Einstein wrote the account in one line. The photon brings hf. Escaping the metal costs a fixed fee for that metal, the **work function φ** — the minimum energy an electron needs to break free of the surface. Whatever is left becomes the ejected electron's kinetic energy, at most KE_max for electrons that escape without wasting energy on the way out:

**hf = φ + KE_max**

- If hf < φ, the right side cannot be paid: **no emission**. The threshold frequency is the photon that just breaks even: **hf₀ = φ**.
- If hf > φ, electrons emerge with up to KE_max = hf − φ; higher frequency means a bigger leftover, so faster electrons.
- More photons (brighter light) means more of these one-to-one deals per second — more electrons, same maximum speed.

Work it. A metal has work function φ = 2.0 eV. Ultraviolet photons of energy 5.0 eV strike it. Then KE_max = 5.0 − 2.0 = **3.0 eV**: the fastest electrons leave carrying 3.0 eV of kinetic energy. Red photons of 1.8 eV also strike it — brightly, for an hour. 1.8 < 2.0, so **none** are ejected, ever. The account forbids it.

This effect proved the quantum hypothesis the way few experiments ever prove anything: it earned Einstein his Nobel Prize, and it is why physics now holds both pictures of light at once — waves for how light travels and interferes, photons for how it trades energy with matter.

## The key ideas

- Light arrives in packets — **photons** — each of energy **E = hf**; frequency sets the packet's size, intensity sets how many packets arrive.
- The **photoelectric effect**: light ejects electrons from a metal only above that metal's **threshold frequency** f₀, instantly, with brightness changing the number ejected, never their top speed.
- **Einstein's equation hf = φ + KE_max** keeps the account: the photon pays the **work function** φ to free the electron, and the remainder is the electron's kinetic energy.
- hf₀ = φ defines the threshold: below it, no brightness and no waiting time can free an electron, because each electron is served by one photon alone.
