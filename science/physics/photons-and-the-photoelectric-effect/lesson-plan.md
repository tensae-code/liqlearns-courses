# Lesson plan — Photons and the photoelectric effect

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Photons and the photoelectric effect
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the modern-physics lessons — Inside the nucleus, Wave–particle duality of light and Discovering the nucleus: this is the course's first photoelectric-effect lesson, the experiment that proved light arrives in packets
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** light does not only behave as a wave — it arrives in packets called photons, each carrying energy E = hf; when photons strike a metal, a photon of high enough frequency gives all its energy to one electron and knocks it out — the photoelectric effect — and Einstein's equation hf = φ + KE_max, built on Planck's quantum hypothesis, explains every feature of it

## Sub lessons
1. **Photons — light in packets** — the quantum hypothesis: energy is exchanged in packets (quanta) of size E = hf, where f is the frequency and h is Planck's constant; a photon is one such packet of light — brighter light means more photons, higher-frequency light means more energy in each photon
2. **The photoelectric effect — light knocking electrons out** — ultraviolet light ejects electrons from a metal surface instantly, red light never does however bright it is, and brighter ultraviolet ejects more electrons but no faster ones: the three facts no wave picture can explain, all settled by a threshold frequency for each metal
3. **Einstein's equation — the energy account** — one photon gives all its energy to one electron: hf = φ + KE_max, where φ is the work function — the least energy that frees an electron from that metal — and KE_max is the fastest ejected electron's kinetic energy; the same physics runs photocells, light meters and solar cells

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **How waves travel — transverse and longitudinal** and **The electromagnetic spectrum**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A photon is a packet of light energy, E = hf — frequency sets the size of the packet, brightness sets how many packets there are.
2. Say, in their own words: The photoelectric effect is electrons ejected from a metal by light, and it happens only above the metal's threshold frequency, however bright the light.
3. Say, in their own words: Einstein's equation hf = φ + KE_max keeps the energy account: photon in, work function to escape, the rest as the electron's kinetic energy.
4. Say, in their own words: Raising the intensity raises the number of electrons ejected, not their speed; raising the frequency raises their speed.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the bright red lamp that frees nothing and the dim ultraviolet lamp that frees electrons at once. Hold that puzzle; the lesson is its solution.
- Thinks brighter light must free electrons 'by force' → brightness is numbers, not strength: a million weak packets cannot do what one strong packet does, because each electron takes its energy from one photon only.
- Mixes up intensity and frequency → say it as money: frequency is the size of each coin, intensity is how many coins per second. The work function is a price — only a big enough coin pays it.
