# Measuring volume of solids and liquids — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Calculate the volume of a cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 10 cm (use π = 3.14).
**Answer:** V = πr²h = 3.14 × 4 × 10 = 125.6 cm³.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the eye be level with the meniscus when reading a cylinder?
**Answer:** Because the scale is read through the curved glass and liquid; looking down or up shifts the apparent level against the scale (parallax), giving a wrong volume. Level reading puts the meniscus bottom exactly against its true mark.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Describe how to find the volume of a small irregular metal figure.
**Answer:** Part-fill a measuring cylinder and note the water volume V₁. Lower the figure in on a thread until it is fully under the water and note the new level V₂. The figure's volume is V₂ − V₁.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A smartwatch must not be put in water. How could you still find its volume by displacement?
**Answer:** Make or use an exact same-size waterproof model (or seal the watch in a thin waterproof wrap whose own volume is measured and subtracted) and displace with that — the Kazakh book sets this as a puzzle; any method that displaces the same volume without wetting the watch earns the answer.
