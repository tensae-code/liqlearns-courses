# Lesson plan — Measuring volume of solids and liquids

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Measuring volume of solids and liquids
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the measurement strand — Kazakhstan's Grade 7 books give measuring volume its own named section in every edition
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** volume is measured three honest ways — by formula for regular shapes, by reading a measuring cylinder for liquids, and by displacement for irregular solids — and every way starts by finding the scale's division value

## Sub lessons
1. **Volume by formula — regular shapes** — A regular solid's volume comes from its lengths. A cuboid (parallelepiped): V = l × w × h. A cylinder: V = πr²h. A sphere: V = (4/3)πr³. A pyramid: one third of its base area times its height. The Kazakh books drill these beside the instruments that check them — the caliper and the micrometer read the lengths to a tenth or a hundredth of a millimetre, so the formula's answer can be trusted. Units climb the ladder mm³ → cm³ → m³, a thousand at each step.
2. **Liquids in a measuring cylinder** — A liquid takes its vessel's shape, so its volume is read, not calculated. Pour it into a measuring cylinder and read the scale — first finding the division value: the volume between two marked lines divided by the number of gaps between them. Read the bottom of the meniscus (the curved surface) with the eye exactly level with it; reading from above or below shifts the answer — that shift is parallax error, and the Kazakh section names the scale division value in its key-terms box because every later measurement stands on it.
3. **Irregular solids — displacement** — A stone has no formula. Lower it into a measuring cylinder of water: the level rises by exactly the stone's volume, V = V₂ − V₁. For a body too big for the cylinder, an overflow vessel does the same job — fill it to the spout, lower the body in, and measure the water that runs out. The Kazakh books give this displacement method its own section in every Grade 7 edition, and set it puzzles — how to size a smartwatch that must not be wetted is left for the learner to solve (wrap it, or measure a same-shaped model).
