# Measuring volume of solids and liquids — video script

**Opening (the hook):** Volume is measured three honest ways — by formula for regular shapes, by reading a measuring cylinder for liquids, and by displacement for irregular solids — and every way starts by finding the scale's division value.

**Walk-through:** Take the sub lessons in order. For 'Volume by formula — regular shapes': A regular solid's volume comes from its lengths. For 'Liquids in a measuring cylinder': A liquid takes its vessel's shape, so its volume is read, not calculated. For 'Irregular solids — displacement': A stone has no formula. 
**The picture:** [Picture 36858] — a picture of a drawn diagram — a measuring cylinder with a meniscus line read at eye level, a stone lowered in on a thread raising the level, and beside it a cuboid, a cylinder and a sphere each labelled with its volume formula. Walk the learner across it slowly, naming each part as the sub lessons name it.

**Close:** Say the one thing once more, plainly: volume is measured three honest ways — by formula for regular shapes, by reading a measuring cylinder for liquids, and by displacement for irregular solids — and every way starts by finding the scale's division value. Then send the learner to the Do It Yourself items.
