# Semiconductors — intrinsic and extrinsic, the p-n junction diode

## Do it first
Before you read: a torch LED, a phone charger, a radio — all of them steer current with parts that conduct only when pushed the right way, made from a material that barely conducts at all until it is deliberately spoiled with a pinch of impurity. This lesson is that material's story: pure, then doped two ways, then joined.

## Sub lesson 1 — Conductors, semiconductors, insulators — and the intrinsic crystal

The sort comes first: conductor, semiconductor, insulator. **Intrinsic** means pure: silicon's crystal holds its electrons in bonds, warmth frees a few, and each freed electron leaves a **hole** — the two carriers always in equal numbers, both few, both rising with temperature. South Sudan teaches the intrinsic crystal carefully because everything in the topic is a modification of it: doping does not invent carriers, it outnumbers them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Extrinsic semiconductors — doping makes n-type and p-type

**Extrinsic** = doped. A donor impurity (five outer electrons in a four-electron crystal) donates free electrons → **n-type**, majority carriers electrons. An acceptor impurity (three outer electrons) accepts electrons and leaves holes → **p-type**, majority carriers holes. Both types remain electrically neutral — 'n' and 'p' name the sign of the *moving* carriers, not a charge on the material. South Sudan's Topic 4 teaches intrinsic and extrinsic as a pair precisely so the junction that follows makes sense.

## Sub lesson 3 — The p-n junction diode

The **p-n junction**: carriers cancel at the join, leaving a **depletion region** that blocks flow; forward bias thins it and current passes, reverse bias widens it and current stops. The diode therefore conducts **one way only** — the simplest electronic device and the building block: South Sudan's sequence (intrinsic → extrinsic → junction → transistor) is the ladder every electronics course since has climbed, taught here in one topic.

## The key ideas

- Semiconductors (silicon, germanium) conduct between conductors and insulators, and their conductivity can be engineered
- Intrinsic: pure crystal — electrons and holes born in equal, few pairs; warmth makes more carriers
- Extrinsic: doping — donors make n-type (majority electrons), acceptors make p-type (majority holes); both stay neutral
- The p-n junction diode: a depletion region at the join; forward bias conducts, reverse bias blocks — a one-way gate for current

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: A pure semiconductor is intrinsic: few carriers, electrons and holes in equal pairs. Dope it with donors and it is n-type, rich in electrons; dope it with acceptors and it is p-type, rich in holes. Join the two and the p-n junction conducts one way only — the diode, electronics' one-way gate. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.
