# Semiconductors — intrinsic and extrinsic, the p-n junction diode — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does an intrinsic semiconductor conduct better when hot, unlike a metal?
**Answer: Warmth breaks more bonds and creates more electron–hole pairs — more carriers — so conductivity rises; a metal already has all its carriers, and warmth only jostles them (more resistance).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Make the comparison: n-type against p-type — impurity kind, majority carrier.
**Answer: n-type: donor impurity (extra electron), majority carriers electrons. p-type: acceptor impurity (one electron short), majority carriers holes. Both remain neutral overall.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain how the depletion region makes the diode one-way.
**Answer: At the junction, electrons and holes cancel into a carrier-free depletion region that blocks current; forward bias pushes carriers in and thins it so current flows, while reverse bias widens it and blocks flow — hence one way only.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the bias in each case: a diode circuit where the lamp lights, and where it stays dark.
**Answer: Lamp lit: forward bias (p-side to positive). Lamp dark: reverse bias — the depletion region has widened and blocks the current.**
