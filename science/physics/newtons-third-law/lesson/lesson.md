# Newton's third law — action and reaction

## Do it first
Press your palm hard against a wall and notice the wall pressing back — you cannot push without being pushed. Then walk and attend to the ground's answer at each step.

[Picture 21830 — hand on a wall with two equal opposite arrows]

## Newton's third law — action and reaction — the teaching
Newton's third law: forces come in pairs. If body A pushes body B, then B pushes A back with an equal force in the opposite direction. The two forces act on different bodies, so they never cancel on one body. Push a wall and the wall pushes your hand back; walking works because your foot pushes the ground backward and the ground pushes you forward; a recoiling gun is the same pair read from the other side.

## The key ideas

- Action and reaction are equal, opposite, and act on different bodies.
- Push a wall and the wall pushes back equally.
- Walking: foot pushes ground back, ground pushes walker forward.

## Say it to your companion
Explain newton's third law — action and reaction to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find three action–reaction pairs in sport or farm work and name both bodies in each pair.
2. Write the key ideas in your own words — one line each — without looking back. Then check them against the lesson and fix any line that drifted.
