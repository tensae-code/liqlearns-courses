# Motion of a body thrown vertically upward

## Do it first
Throw a ball straight up and clap the rhythm: catch the instants — launch, top, return to your hand. Time the whole flight with a phone stopwatch (several tries, keep the best): half the time is the rise, so v₀ = g × (T/2) prices your throw, and h_max = v₀²/(2g) its height. Compare throws with a partner: the stopwatch has turned your arm into a measuring instrument. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Up against gravity — speed spent at g per second

The throw's only force is gravity, pulling down at g, so the upward speed falls in a straight line: v = v₀ − gt. A body thrown at 30 m/s is moving at 20 after one second, 10 after two — the same steady subtraction whatever the body is made of, air set aside. Height grows meanwhile but ever slower: h = v₀t − ½gt². The Armenian book isolates this motion in its own paragraph because every rule in it is the sign discipline the rest of mechanics will reuse.

## Sub lesson 2 — The top, and the mirror-image fall

At t = v₀/g the speed reaches zero — not the acceleration: g acts at the top as at every other instant, which is exactly why the body does not stay there. The top height is h_max = v₀²/(2g). Then the motion replays in mirror: the body passes each height downward with the same speed it had there upward, and lands back at launch level after 2v₀/g with speed v₀. Up-time equals down-time; the throw is symmetric around its still instant.

## Sub lesson 3 — Solving vertical throws — one sign convention, held

Every vertical-throw problem is bookkeeping: choose up as positive, write a = −g, and let the algebra speak — a negative v late in the problem simply means 'moving down'. Height questions use h = v₀t − ½gt², speed questions v = v₀ − gt, top questions v = 0. The errors pupils make here are almost never physics errors; they are sign errors, and the paragraph exists to train the convention before projectiles split the motion into two directions at once.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: throw a body straight up and gravity edits its speed by the same g every second: v = v₀ − gt on the way up, one still instant at the top (h_max = v₀²/2g), then the mirror-image fall — one motion, signed carefully, whose whole story is free fall read upwards first. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A body is thrown up at 20 m/s. Where is it and how fast is it moving after 3 s? (g = 10 m/s²)
2. Why is the acceleration at the very top NOT zero, even though the velocity is?
3. A throw's total flight (up and back to launch level) lasts 4 s. How high did it go? (g = 10 m/s²)
