# Lesson plan — Motion of a body thrown vertically upward

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Motion of a body thrown vertically upward
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the projectile strand — Armenia's Grade 10 book gives the vertical throw its own paragraph (§26) between free fall and the horizontal launch
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** throw a body straight up and gravity edits its speed by the same g every second: v = v₀ − gt on the way up, one still instant at the top (h_max = v₀²/2g), then the mirror-image fall — one motion, signed carefully, whose whole story is free fall read upwards first

## Sub lessons
1. **Up against gravity — speed spent at g per second** — The throw's only force is gravity, pulling down at g, so the upward speed falls in a straight line: v = v₀ − gt. A body thrown at 30 m/s is moving at 20 after one second, 10 after two — the same steady subtraction whatever the body is made of, air set aside. Height grows meanwhile but ever slower: h = v₀t − ½gt². The Armenian book isolates this motion in its own paragraph because every rule in it is the sign discipline the rest of mechanics will reuse.
2. **The top, and the mirror-image fall** — At t = v₀/g the speed reaches zero — not the acceleration: g acts at the top as at every other instant, which is exactly why the body does not stay there. The top height is h_max = v₀²/(2g). Then the motion replays in mirror: the body passes each height downward with the same speed it had there upward, and lands back at launch level after 2v₀/g with speed v₀. Up-time equals down-time; the throw is symmetric around its still instant.
3. **Solving vertical throws — one sign convention, held** — Every vertical-throw problem is bookkeeping: choose up as positive, write a = −g, and let the algebra speak — a negative v late in the problem simply means 'moving down'. Height questions use h = v₀t − ½gt², speed questions v = v₀ − gt, top questions v = 0. The errors pupils make here are almost never physics errors; they are sign errors, and the paragraph exists to train the convention before projectiles split the motion into two directions at once.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
