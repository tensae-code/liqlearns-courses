# Motion of a body thrown vertically upward — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) A body is thrown up at 25 m/s (g = 10 m/s²). Find the top height and the time to reach it.
**Answer:** t = v₀/g = 2.5 s; h_max = v₀²/(2g) = 625/20 = 31.25 m.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain why up-time equals down-time in a vertical throw.
**Answer:** The fall from the top is free fall from rest over the same height the rise covered; the motion is time-symmetric — each height is passed with the same speed in both directions, so the two halves take equal times.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A pupil writes a = +g with up positive and gets a negative height. What went wrong?
**Answer:** The sign convention was broken: with up positive, gravity's acceleration must enter as −g in both v = v₀ − gt and h = v₀t − ½gt²; mixing signs, not physics, produced the nonsense.

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Two identical balls are thrown up at 10 m/s and 20 m/s. Compare their top heights.
**Answer:** Height goes as v₀²: the second reaches (20/10)² = 4 times the height of the first — doubling the throw quadruples the climb.
