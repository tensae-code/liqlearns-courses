# Video script — Adding light, subtracting light — colour mixing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Adding light (additive mixing) combines the primaries: red + green = yellow, green + blue = cyan, blue + red = magenta, and all three together = white.
Stage lights do this in the open air of a theatre.
Subtracting light works with filters and pigments, which absorb some primaries and transmit (filters) or reflect (pigments) the rest.
Yellow absorbs blue, magenta absorbs green, cyan absorbs red.
Stack filters and each removes its primary, until a pair leaves only one primary — or none: paints mixed together darken toward black.
A surface that looks magenta reflects red and blue and absorbs green — which is why objects change colour under coloured light, and vanish into darkness when their colour is absent from the light.

Remember: **Additive: R+G = yellow, G+B = cyan, B+R = magenta, R+G+B = white.**
Remember: **Subtractive primaries cyan, magenta, yellow each absorb one light primary.**
Remember: **A magenta surface reflects red and blue and absorbs green.**
Keep the formula: **R+G=Y; G+B=C; B+R=M; R+G+B=white**
