# Adding light, subtracting light — colour mixing

## Do it first
Overlap coloured cellophane or phone torches with colour filters on a wall: add lights toward white, then stack the filters toward darkness — both mixings in one minute.

[Picture 21940 — stage lights mixing to white on one side; stacked filters darkening to black on the other]

## Sub lesson 1 — Adding light
Adding light (additive mixing) combines the primaries: red + green = yellow, green + blue = cyan, blue + red = magenta, and all three together = white. Stage lights do this in the open air of a theatre.

**Keep the formulas:** R+G=Y; G+B=C; B+R=M; R+G+B=white

## Sub lesson 2 — Subtracting light — filters and pigments
Subtracting light works with filters and pigments, which absorb some primaries and transmit (filters) or reflect (pigments) the rest. Yellow absorbs blue, magenta absorbs green, cyan absorbs red. Stack filters and each removes its primary, until a pair leaves only one primary — or none: paints mixed together darken toward black. A surface that looks magenta reflects red and blue and absorbs green — which is why objects change colour under coloured light, and vanish into darkness when their colour is absent from the light.

## The key ideas

- Additive: R+G = yellow, G+B = cyan, B+R = magenta, R+G+B = white.
- Subtractive primaries cyan, magenta, yellow each absorb one light primary.
- A magenta surface reflects red and blue and absorbs green.
