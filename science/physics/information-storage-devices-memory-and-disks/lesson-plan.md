# Lesson plan — Information storage devices — memory and disks

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** information must live somewhere between uses: Punjab Grade 10 sorts the storage family into the computer's own primary memory and the removable, magnetic and flash devices that carry information between machines, people and years

## Sub lessons
1. **Primary memory — the machine's own desk** — Primary memory is the storage the processor works on directly: fast, close, and — for RAM — emptied when the power dies. It is the desk, not the archive: what is being used now lives here, and anything that must survive the night must be written out to a device.
2. **Magnetic disks — the hard disk and its ancestors** — Magnetic storage writes information as patterns of magnetisation: the hard disk spins platters under read-write heads and holds the machine's archive; magnetic disks and audio/video cassettes before it used the same principle on slower, removable media. Capacity grew; the idea — magnetised spots standing for data — stayed.
3. **Flash drives — storage with no moving parts** — The flash drive stores charge in solid-state memory: small, silent, nothing to spin or scratch, plugged in anywhere. Punjab's survey ends with the device its learners carry — and the rule the whole family teaches: keep information in more than one place, because every device can fail, be lost or be left behind.
