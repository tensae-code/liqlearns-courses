# Information storage devices — memory and disks

## Do it first
Inventory the storage you can touch — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Primary memory — the machine's own desk

Primary memory is the storage the processor works on directly: fast, close, and — for RAM — emptied when the power dies. It is the desk, not the archive: what is being used now lives here, and anything that must survive the night must be written out to a device.

## Sub lesson 2 — Magnetic disks — the hard disk and its ancestors

Magnetic storage writes information as patterns of magnetisation: the hard disk spins platters under read-write heads and holds the machine's archive; magnetic disks and audio/video cassettes before it used the same principle on slower, removable media. Capacity grew; the idea — magnetised spots standing for data — stayed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Flash drives — storage with no moving parts

The flash drive stores charge in solid-state memory: small, silent, nothing to spin or scratch, plugged in anywhere. Punjab's survey ends with the device its learners carry — and the rule the whole family teaches: keep information in more than one place, because every device can fail, be lost or be left behind.

## The key ideas

- Primary memory is the storage the processor works on directly: fast, close, and — for RAM — emptied when the power dies.
- Magnetic storage writes information as patterns of magnetisation: the hard disk spins platters under read-write heads and holds the machine's archive; magnetic disks and audio/video cassettes before it used the same principle on slower, removable media.
- The flash drive stores charge in solid-state memory: small, silent, nothing to spin or scratch, plugged in anywhere.
