# Experiment — estimating a molecule's size with an oil film — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Oil spreads on water to a film one molecule thick: the film's thickness is the molecule's size, d = V/S; Dilute the oil (e.g. 1:500) and price one drop by counting drops per millilitre, so a single drop carries a truly tiny volume of oil; Dust the water with lycopodium powder so the film's edge shows; count grid squares for the area S; The result lands at the molecular scale — 10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻⁹ m — an order-of-magnitude victory, errors carried honestly — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
