# Hands-on — Experiment — estimating a molecule's size with an oil film

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Measure a molecule on a tray of water
You need a large tray of still water, lycopodium powder (or fine talc), oil solution diluted 1:500 in petroleum ether, a calibrated dropper, and a transparent cm grid. (1) Count your dropper: drops per 1 mL of solution — twice, and average. (2) Dust the water's surface very lightly and evenly; let one drop fall at the centre; wait for the film to finish spreading and the solvent to evaporate. (3) Lay the grid over the patch; count squares (whole + more-than-half) for S in cm². (4) Compute the oil volume in one drop and d = V/S in metres; repeat with a fresh tray and compare. (5) Write the audit: your value's order of magnitude, the known scale 10⁻¹⁰ m, and your two largest error sources. Teacher handles the solvent; no flames anywhere near — it is flammable, and the film is the only thing meant to spread.
