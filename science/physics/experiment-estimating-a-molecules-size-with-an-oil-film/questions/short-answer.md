# Experiment — estimating a molecule's size with an oil film — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A 1:400 solution, 40 drops per mL, spreads one drop to 200 cm². Find d.
**Answer: Oil per drop = (1/40) ÷ 400 mL = 6.25 × 10⁻⁵ cm³. d = 6.25 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 200 = 3.1 × 10⁻⁷ cm ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁹ m.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the method give only an order of magnitude, and why is that still a triumph?
**Answer: Drops vary, film borders are judged by eye, spreading may be incomplete, and the molecule is not a neat sphere — factors of a few are unavoidable. But landing at 10⁻⁹–10⁻¹⁰ m from bench apparatus measures a world no microscope of the era could see: the scale itself is the discovery.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A student's film keeps spreading past the tray's edge. Name the fault and the fix.
**Answer: Too much oil in the drop — the dilution was too weak (or two drops fell). Fix: dilute further (or use a finer dropper) so one drop's oil fits the tray as a monolayer.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the experiment's chain of divisions in order.
**Answer: Dilution ratio shrinks the oil in the solution; drop-counting shrinks the solution in one drop; the film area then divides the tiny volume down to a molecular thickness: d = V/S.**
