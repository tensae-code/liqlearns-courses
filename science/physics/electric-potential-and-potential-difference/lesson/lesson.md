# Electric potential and potential difference

## Do it first
Before you read: a battery is labelled 9 V, a wall socket 220 V. Volts are not current and not energy — so what does the number price? If one end of a wire sits at a higher number than the other, what flows, and which way? Decide here.

## Sub lesson 1 — Electric potential — work per unit charge

Carrying a positive charge toward a charged body takes work against the repulsion. The electric potential at a point is that work divided by the charge carried: V = W/Q, in volts (1 V = 1 joule per coulomb). Potential is a property of the point, not of the visiting charge — double the test charge and the work doubles, but the potential stays the same. Earth is taken as zero potential, the sea level of electricity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Potential difference — the push between two points

What circuits run on is not potential at one point but the difference between two: the potential difference (p.d.) V = W/Q is the energy given to each coulomb moving between the points. A source raises charge from low potential to high; the charge then falls through the components, spending the energy as light, heat or motion. A voltmeter measures p.d. and is connected in parallel across the component. No difference, no current — current flows from higher potential to lower through the outside circuit.

## Sub lesson 3 — Potential and field — E = V/d

Potential and field strength are two views of the same landscape. Between parallel plates the field is uniform and E = V/d: a p.d. of 100 V across plates 0.05 m apart gives E = 2000 V/m (the same unit as N/C). Lines of equal potential — equipotentials — cross field lines at right angles and themselves feel no field along them: moving along an equipotential costs no work. Steep potential slope, strong field.

## The key ideas

- Electric potential V = W/Q at a point, in volts (joules per coulomb); Earth is the zero
- Potential difference between two points drives current; a voltmeter reads it in parallel
- In a uniform field E = V/d — field strength is potential difference per metre

## Do It Yourself
1. A charge of 2 C is carried to a point at a cost of 30 J of work. What is the potential at the point?
2. A 12 V battery drives charge round a circuit. How much energy does each coulomb gain from the battery, and where is it spent?
3. Two plates 0.02 m apart carry a p.d. of 240 V. Find the field strength between them.
