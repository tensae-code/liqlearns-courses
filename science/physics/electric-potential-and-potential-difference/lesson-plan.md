## Sub lessons
1. **Electric potential — work per unit charge** — Carrying a positive charge toward a charged body takes work against the repulsion. The electric potential at a point is that work divided by the charge carried: V = W/Q, in volts (1 V = 1 joule per coulomb). Potential is a property of the point, not of the visiting charge — double the test charge and the work doubles, but the potential stays the same. Earth is taken as zero potential, the sea level of electricity.
2. **Potential difference — the push between two points** — What circuits run on is not potential at one point but the difference between two: the potential difference (p.d.) V = W/Q is the energy given to each coulomb moving between the points. A source raises charge from low potential to high; the charge then falls through the components, spending the energy as light, heat or motion. A voltmeter measures p.d. and is connected in parallel across the component. No difference, no current — current flows from higher potential to lower through the outside circuit.
3. **Potential and field — E = V/d** — Potential and field strength are two views of the same landscape. Between parallel plates the field is uniform and E = V/d: a p.d. of 100 V across plates 0.05 m apart gives E = 2000 V/m (the same unit as N/C). Lines of equal potential — equipotentials — cross field lines at right angles and themselves feel no field along them: moving along an equipotential costs no work. Steep potential slope, strong field.
