# Lesson plan — Wave–particle duality of light

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Wave–particle duality of light
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics — Light and modern physics neighbourhood, after Light — a wave you can see (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** light refuses to be only one thing: it spreads, bends and interferes like a wave, and it arrives and knocks electrons loose in separate packets — photons — like a particle; wave–particle duality is the honest answer: light shows the face the experiment asks for.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the light lessons' wave picture, which it completes — a learner can land here directly. Light — a wave you can see is recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **Light behaves as a wave** — light spreads around corners (diffraction) and two light beams crossing make interference patterns of bright and dark bands — behaviour only waves can show.
2. **Light behaves as particles — photons** — in the photoelectric effect, light knocks electrons out of a metal one packet at a time: each packet (photon) carries energy E = hf, set by the frequency, so dim high-frequency light can do what bright low-frequency light cannot.
3. **Both at once — the duality** — neither picture alone is the truth: light is light, and it answers wave questions as a wave and particle questions as a particle; matter itself (electrons) shows the same two faces.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Light — a wave you can see** and **Electromagnetic waves — light's family**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give two pieces of evidence that light behaves as a wave: diffraction and interference.
2. Describe the photoelectric effect and say what it proves about how light arrives.
3. Use E = hf: say which light's photons carry more energy — red or ultraviolet — and why brightness cannot substitute for frequency in the photoelectric effect.
4. State the duality plainly: light shows wave behaviour in wave experiments and particle behaviour in particle experiments; neither picture alone is complete.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the CD rainbow is light spreading and overlapping like ripples — hold that wave picture, then let the photoelectric effect argue back.
- Thinks light must be one or the other → the experiments disagree with each single picture: slits give bands (waves), the photoelectric effect gives packets (particles). Nature's answer is both — the duality.
- Thinks brighter light always knocks out faster electrons → in the photoelectric effect each electron takes its energy from one photon: frequency sets the energy per photon; brightness only sets how many photons arrive.
