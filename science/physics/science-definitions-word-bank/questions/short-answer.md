# Science definitions — the physics word bank — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a definition usually carry the quantity's unit inside it?
**Answer: Because the unit is part of what the word means — weight in newtons and mass in kilograms are different quantities, and the unit in the definition is what keeps them, and their calculations, apart.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Write the exact definitions of pressure and power, with units.
**Answer: Pressure is force per unit area, measured in pascals (Pa). Power is the rate of doing work (energy transferred per second), measured in watts (W).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A learner says "the heat of the water is 80 degrees." Correct the sentence using the definitions of heat and temperature.
**Answer: 80 degrees is the water's temperature — how hot it is. Heat is energy in joules passing between bodies because of a temperature difference; a body does not "contain" a temperature of heat.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Libya's Grade 11 Physics book is written in Arabic, yet its "Definitions" appendix (Appendix 1, p. 211) is printed in English. What support does that build into the book itself?
**Answer: Bilingual support: the learner studies in Arabic but holds the science's shared technical words in English inside the same book — ready for exams, further study and science beyond one language.**
