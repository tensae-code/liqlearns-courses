# Science definitions — the physics word bank

## Do it first
Before you read: define "weight" in one sentence, out loud. Most people say something like "how heavy a thing is" — and a physics exam would mark it wrong, because in physics *weight* is a force, measured in newtons, and "heavy" is not a measurement. The gap between the everyday word and the science word is what this lesson closes — with the tool Libya's Grade 11 Physics book prints at its own back: a "Definitions" appendix, a word bank, in English, inside the Arabic book.

## Sub lesson 1 — A definition is a tool

A physics **definition** fixes a word to one exact meaning that can be measured or tested — and it usually names the word's **unit**, because the unit is part of the meaning. *Speed* becomes *velocity* the moment a direction is added to the definition. *Mass* (kilograms: the amount of matter, the measure of inertia) and *weight* (newtons: gravity's force on that mass, W = mg) are two different words because their definitions differ — muddle them and whole calculations fall. This is why serious textbooks end with a glossary: the definitions are the settled law of the course's language. Libya's book makes the support bilingual — the learner studies physics in Arabic and holds the science's shared words in English on the same shelf, Appendix 1 (ملحق 1), printed p. 211.

## Sub lesson 2 — Words of motion and force

Say each one exactly: **quantity** — something that can be measured. **Unit** — the standard amount a quantity is measured against. **Scalar** — a quantity with size only. **Vector** — a quantity with size and direction. **Displacement** — distance in a stated direction from a start point. **Velocity** — rate of change of displacement, in m/s. **Acceleration** — rate of change of velocity, in m/s². **Force** — a push or a pull, in newtons (N). **Mass** — the amount of matter in a body, in kilograms (kg). **Weight** — the force of gravity on a mass, W = mg, in newtons. **Friction** — the force opposing relative motion where surfaces touch. **Pressure** — force per unit area, in pascals (Pa). **Work** — force × the distance moved along it, in joules (J). **Energy** — the capacity to do work, in joules. **Power** — the rate of doing work, in watts (W).

## Sub lesson 3 — Words of heat, waves and electricity — and using the bank

The second shelf: **temperature** — how hot a body is, in °C or kelvin (K). **Heat** — energy passing from hotter to colder because of the temperature difference, in joules. **Latent heat** — heat that changes a body's state without changing its temperature. **Wave** — a disturbance carrying energy without carrying matter along. **Wavelength** — the distance from one crest to the next, in metres. **Frequency** — oscillations per second, in hertz (Hz). **Refraction** — the bending of a wave when its speed changes at a boundary. **Current** — rate of flow of charge, in amperes (A). **Voltage** — energy per unit charge between two points, in volts (V). **Resistance** — a conductor's opposition to current, in ohms (Ω). And the method that makes the bank yours: **find the word → say the definition exactly, unit included → use it in a sentence about something real → check yourself against the bank.** Four passes, and the word stops being the book's and becomes yours.

## The key ideas

- A physics definition fixes a word to one exact, measurable meaning — the unit is part of the meaning
- Mass and weight, speed and velocity, heat and temperature differ because their definitions differ
- Libya's Grade 11 book prints an English "Definitions" appendix (p. 211) inside the Arabic book — bilingual support built into the book itself
- The word-bank method: find the word, say it exactly with its unit, use it in your own sentence, check against the bank
