# Lesson plan — Science definitions — the physics word bank

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Science definitions — the physics word bank
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), as the course's reference lesson — the appendix habit of Libya's Grade 11 book, which prints an English "Definitions" glossary at the back of the Arabic text (Appendix 1, printed p. 211)

## Sub lessons
1. **A definition is a tool** — Physics words are not ordinary words used loosely: each one is **defined** — fixed to one exact meaning that can be measured or tested. *Velocity* is not "speed" but speed in a stated direction; *weight* is not "heaviness" but the force of gravity on a mass, in newtons. A definition usually carries the quantity's **unit** inside it, because the unit is part of what the word means. When a problem turns on a word, the definition is the tool that settles it — which is why textbooks print glossaries at all, and why Libya's Grade 11 Physics book ends with an English **"Definitions" appendix** (ملحق 1, printed p. 211): the Arabic-medium learner is given the science's own words in the science's international language, inside the same book.
2. **Words of motion and force** — The mechanics shelf of the bank, each word in one exact line: **quantity** — something that can be measured; **unit** — the standard amount a quantity is measured against; **scalar** — a quantity with size only; **vector** — a quantity with size and direction; **displacement** — distance in a stated direction from a starting point; **velocity** — rate of change of displacement; **acceleration** — rate of change of velocity; **force** — a push or a pull, in newtons; **mass** — the amount of matter in a body, in kilograms, the measure of its inertia; **weight** — the gravitational force on a mass, W = mg, in newtons; **friction** — the force that opposes relative motion at surfaces in contact; **pressure** — force per unit area; **work** — force × distance moved in the force's direction; **energy** — the capacity to do work, in joules; **power** — rate of doing work, in watts.
3. **Words of heat, waves and electricity — and using the bank** — The second shelf: **temperature** — how hot a body is, in °C or K; **heat** — energy passing from a hotter body to a colder one because of the temperature difference; **latent heat** — the heat that changes a body's state without changing its temperature; **wave** — a disturbance that carries energy without carrying matter; **wavelength** — the distance between two neighbouring crests; **frequency** — oscillations per second, in hertz; **refraction** — the bending of a wave as its speed changes at a boundary; **current** — rate of flow of charge, in amperes; **voltage (potential difference)** — energy per unit charge between two points, in volts; **resistance** — how much a conductor opposes current, in ohms. And the bank's method, which is the Libyan appendix's method: **find the word → say the definition exactly, with its unit → use the word in a sentence about a real thing → check yourself against the bank.** A definition learned this way is available in an exam, in a lab and in an argument — it has become the learner's own word.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a physics definition is: a word fixed to one exact, measurable meaning, usually carrying its unit.
2. Give exact one-line definitions, with units, for the motion-and-force words: scalar, vector, displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, mass, weight, pressure, power.
3. Give exact one-line definitions, with units, for the heat, waves and electricity words: temperature, heat, latent heat, wavelength, frequency, current, voltage, resistance.
4. Use the word bank's method — find, say exactly, use, check — on a word they do not yet own.

## What you need
Any physics textbook with a glossary, or this lesson's own lists; a notebook page ruled into two columns — the learner starts their own word bank during the hands-on.
