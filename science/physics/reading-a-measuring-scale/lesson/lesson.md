# Reading a measuring scale

## Do it first
Take a ruler. Lay a pencil along it so its end falls between two marks. Read the length out loud, including your estimated last digit.

[Picture 21804 — ruler scale zoomed — the estimated last digit sits between two marks]

## Reading a measuring scale — the teaching
A scale is the set of marks on a measuring device. The more marks there are, the finer the precision — you can read smaller differences. To read a scale, find the last numbered mark the pointer or edge has passed, then estimate one extra decimal place for how far it sits between that mark and the next. That estimated last digit is part of the reading; length, mass and temperature scales are all read this way.

## The key ideas

- A scale is the set of marks on a measuring device.
- More marks on a scale means finer precision.
- A reading takes one extra estimated decimal place between two marks.

## Say it to your companion
Explain reading a measuring scale to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Read three scales at home — a ruler, a jug, a thermometer if you have one — estimating the last digit each time.
2. Write the key ideas in your own words — one line each — without looking back. Then check them against the lesson and fix any line that drifted.
