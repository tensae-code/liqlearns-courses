# Combining and splitting motion — components move independently — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A boat crosses at 4 m/s in a 3 m/s current, heading straight across a 120 m river. Find the crossing time and the downstream drift.
**Answer: Time = 120 ÷ 4 = 30 s (the across component alone sets the time). Drift = 3 × 30 = 90 m downstream.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain why the current does not change the crossing time in that example.
**Answer: Independence: the crossing time belongs to the across account, driven by the boat's own 4 m/s; the current works only in the along account, adding drift during the same 30 s.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Rain falls vertically at 6 m/s past a train moving at 8 m/s. At what speed does it strike the window, and slanted which way?
**Answer: Speed = √(6² + 8²) = 10 m/s, slanted — combining the rain's downward motion with the train's forward motion (equivalently the rain's backward slant relative to the train).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the three-step rhythm this lesson teaches for two-dimensional problems.
**Answer: Split the motion (or velocity) into components; solve each one-dimensional account separately; combine the results as vectors.**
