# Hands-on — Combining and splitting motion — components move independently

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Two motions, one diagonal
(1) Walk-and-carry: pace across the classroom at a steady rate while a partner slides a long paper strip sideways under a marker you hold down to it — the trace on the paper is straight across; the trace on the floor (mark start and end) is diagonal. Measure both and check the diagonal against √(across² + along²). (2) Drop-and-throw: set two balls at a table's edge; knock one straight off so it falls, and launch the other horizontally with a ruler flick at the same instant — listen for one landing sound, not two; repeat at a harder flick and confirm the tie survives. (3) Ferryman problem on paper: a river 200 m wide, current 2 m/s, boat speed 4 m/s — compute the crossing time heading straight across (200/4 = 50 s) and the downstream drift (2 × 50 = 100 m), then find the upstream aiming angle that lands exactly opposite (sin θ = 2/4, θ = 30°).
