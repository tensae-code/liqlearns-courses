# Magnetic properties of iron — permeability and hysteresis

## Do it first
Before you read: wrap a coil round a nail and the nail lifts clips the coil alone never could — iron has amplified the field enormously. Switch off, and the nail still lifts a clip or two: iron remembers. Lift, amplify, remember, resist letting go — iron's whole magnetic character is in those words, and this lesson gives them their measures: permeability and hysteresis.

## Sub lesson 1 — Permeability — how much iron multiplies a field

Put iron in a magnetising field H (from a coil, say) and the flux density inside it, B, is far larger than the coil alone would give. **Permeability** measures the multiplication: μ = B ÷ H, and the **relative permeability** μᵣ compares it with empty space — a few hundred to several thousand for soft iron, meaning the iron concentrates the field by that factor. Permeability is not a constant of the iron: it grows, peaks, and falls as the iron approaches **saturation**, where its magnetisation can grow no further and extra H adds only the vacuum's small contribution. Electromagnets, relay cores and transformer cores all live off high permeability — flux guided and multiplied by the iron path.

## Sub lesson 2 — The hysteresis loop

Magnetise iron up, reverse the magnetising force, and bring it back: plot B against H and the return path does **not** retrace the outward path — B **lags** H, and the closed curve is the **hysteresis loop**. Reading the loop: when H returns to zero a flux remains — the **remanence** (residual magnetism); a reverse H, the **coercive force**, must be applied to wipe B to zero; and the **area of the loop is energy lost as heat in each cubic metre for every cycle** — the hysteresis loss, paid on every cycle of every AC core. Hysteresis is the iron remembering where it has been: magnetisation depends on history, not on H alone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing irons — soft, hard, and AC losses

The loop's shape is the iron's character reference. **Soft iron**: narrow loop, low coercivity, high permeability, small loop area — magnetises and demagnetises easily, cheap in energy per cycle; the choice for electromagnet and transformer cores, where AC runs the loop fifty times a second and a fat loop would cook the core (cores are also laminated, to cut the separate eddy-current loss). **Steel and hard magnetic alloys**: wide loop, large remanence and coercivity — hard to magnetise, harder to demagnetise; the choice for **permanent magnets**, where remembering forever is the whole job. Same element, opposite virtues — the application picks the loop.

## The key ideas

- Permeability μ = B/H (relative μᵣ against vacuum) measures how strongly iron multiplies and guides a field; it varies with the field and fails at saturation.
- Hysteresis: B lags H round a cycle; remanence is the flux left at H = 0, coercive force the reverse H that wipes it, and the loop's area is the energy lost as heat per cycle.
- Soft iron (narrow loop, low coercivity) for electromagnets and AC cores; hard steel (wide loop, high remanence and coercivity) for permanent magnets.
