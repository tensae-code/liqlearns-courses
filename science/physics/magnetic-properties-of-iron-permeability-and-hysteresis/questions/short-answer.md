# Magnetic properties of iron — permeability and hysteresis — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is permeability not a fixed constant for a sample of iron?
**Answer: Because B does not grow in proportion to H over the whole range: μ rises, peaks, and falls as the iron nears saturation, where magnetisation can grow no further — so μ depends on the magnetising field at which it is measured.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What is the coercive force, and why does a permanent-magnet material need a large one?
**Answer: The reverse magnetising force that must be applied to reduce the flux density to zero. A permanent magnet must survive stray fields, knocks and its own demagnetising effects without losing its magnetisation, so it needs a large coercive force resisting exactly that wiping-out.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why are transformer cores laminated, and is that a hysteresis measure?
**Answer: Lamination (thin insulated sheets) breaks up eddy currents in the core, cutting the eddy-current loss. It is a separate loss from hysteresis: hysteresis loss is set by the loop's area and is cut by choosing a narrow-loop (soft) iron, not by laminating.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) An AC core runs at 50 Hz. Why does its hysteresis loop area matter commercially?
**Answer: Every cycle of the alternating magnetisation pays the loop-area energy as heat. At 50 cycles a second, a fat loop wastes power continuously, heats the core, and can force costlier cooling — so core iron is bought for a small loop area.**
