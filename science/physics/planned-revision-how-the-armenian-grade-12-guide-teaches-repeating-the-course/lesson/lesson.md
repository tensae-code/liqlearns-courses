# Planned revision — how the Armenian Grade 12 guide teaches repeating the course

## Do it first
Build the guide's move for yourself, at small scale: take three topics you studied months apart, and — book shut — write everything you can still produce about each (laws, formulas, one worked example). Mark what would not come. Your marked page is a revision plan: the gaps are located, and they are where the next study hour goes. That is §3.6 in one sitting. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Revision is taught, not left to chance

Every guide in the Armenian set shares one architecture — but the Grade 12 guide adds §3.6: the methodology of repeating the study material in the second semester. Its claim is plain: revision is a teaching task with a method of its own, deserving the same planning as new material. The final-year teacher does not merely finish the syllabus and wish the class luck; the guide schedules the return journey over everything the course has built.

## Sub lesson 2 — How the repetition is organised

Planned revision revisits the course by structure, not by panic: topics come back in their families — mechanics, molecular physics, electricity, optics, the quantum and nuclear close — with problem-solving and questions doing the revisiting, so each return is also a retrieval. Old material is met again in mixed company, the way examinations will mix it, and each pass is aimed: find the gaps while there is still a semester left to close them in.

## Sub lesson 3 — What good revision does

A taught revision does three things raw re-reading does not: it forces recall (the pupil must produce the physics, not recognise it), it reconnects topics that were learned in separate months into one subject, and it diagnoses — every failed retrieval is a located gap with time still attached to it. That is why the guide gives repetition a methodology chapter of its own: in the final year, the return over the course is half the course.
