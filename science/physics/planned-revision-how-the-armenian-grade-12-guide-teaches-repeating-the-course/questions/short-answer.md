# Planned revision — how the Armenian Grade 12 guide teaches repeating the course — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State §3.6's central claim in one sentence.
**Answer:** In the final year, revising the course is itself teaching work with its own method and timetable — it must be planned and taught, not left to pupils to improvise.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the repetition belong to the second semester in the guide's design?
**Answer:** By then the whole course's content exists to be revisited; the second semester is where the final examinations can still be prepared for with time to close the gaps the revision finds.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A pupil says: 'I read the chapter three times and knew it all; in the test I could write nothing.' Diagnose in the guide's terms.
**Answer:** Recognition is not retrieval: re-reading makes the page familiar, but the test demands production from memory. Revision must be built from attempts to recall and solve — with the book shut — so the gaps show before the examination does.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does revision 'reconnect' the course, and why does that matter for the final examination?
**Answer:** Topics learned months apart (say, energy in mechanics and in thermodynamics) are met together again, so the pupil files them as one subject with shared tools — the form in which mixed examination questions will demand them.
