## Sub lessons
1. **Revision is taught, not left to chance** — Every guide in the Armenian set shares one architecture — but the Grade 12 guide adds §3.6: the methodology of repeating the study material in the second semester. Its claim is plain: revision is a teaching task with a method of its own, deserving the same planning as new material. The final-year teacher does not merely finish the syllabus and wish the class luck; the guide schedules the return journey over everything the course has built.
2. **How the repetition is organised** — Planned revision revisits the course by structure, not by panic: topics come back in their families — mechanics, molecular physics, electricity, optics, the quantum and nuclear close — with problem-solving and questions doing the revisiting, so each return is also a retrieval. Old material is met again in mixed company, the way examinations will mix it, and each pass is aimed: find the gaps while there is still a semester left to close them in.
3. **What good revision does** — A taught revision does three things raw re-reading does not: it forces recall (the pupil must produce the physics, not recognise it), it reconnects topics that were learned in separate months into one subject, and it diagnoses — every failed retrieval is a located gap with time still attached to it. That is why the guide gives repetition a methodology chapter of its own: in the final year, the return over the course is half the course.
