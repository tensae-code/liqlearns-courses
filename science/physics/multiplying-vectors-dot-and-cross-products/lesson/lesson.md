# Multiplying vectors — dot and cross products

## Do it first
Before you read: push a heavy box. Pushing along its motion does the most work; pushing at right angles to its motion does none at all; pushing at 45° does something in between. One pair of vectors — force and displacement — and the answer depends only on the angle between them. That angle-dependence is exactly what the two vector products are built to capture.

## Sub lesson 1 — The dot product — a scalar that measures working together

The **dot product** of A and B is **A·B = AB cos θ**, where θ is the angle between them when placed tail to tail. The answer is a scalar — a number with a unit, no direction. Cos θ does the sorting: parallel vectors (θ = 0°) give the full AB; perpendicular vectors (θ = 90°) give exactly zero; opposite vectors give −AB. In components, A·B = AₓBₓ + A_yB_y + A_zB_z. Its home in physics is **work: W = F·s** — only the component of force along the displacement, F cos θ, does work, which is why the pushing-at-an-angle feeling in Do it first is a dot product.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cross product — a vector perpendicular to both

The **cross product** is **A×B**, a *vector*. Its size is **AB sin θ** — largest when the vectors are perpendicular, zero when they are parallel. Its direction is perpendicular to the plane of A and B, given by the **right-hand rule**: point the fingers of your right hand along A, curl them toward B, and your thumb points along A×B. Swapping the order flips the sign: B×A = −A×B. In components along the unit vectors, î×ĵ = k̂, ĵ×k̂ = î, k̂×î = ĵ, and each unit vector crossed with itself is zero.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing the product — and where each one works

Ask what kind of answer the physics needs. A number — work, the angle between two vectors (from cos θ = A·B/AB), whether two vectors are perpendicular (dot product zero) — calls for the **dot** product. A new direction — the torque τ = r×F turning a spanner, the magnetic force F = qv×B bending a moving charge's path, the angular momentum L = r×p — calls for the **cross** product. Check every result twice: the dot product must have no direction to state, and the cross product must be perpendicular to *both* parents — if it is not, it is wrong.

## The key ideas

- Dot product: A·B = AB cos θ — a scalar; zero for perpendicular vectors; in components AₓBₓ + A_yB_y + A_zB_z; work W = F·s is a dot product
- Cross product: size AB sin θ, direction perpendicular to both vectors by the right-hand rule; B×A = −A×B
- Torque τ = r×F, magnetic force qv×B and angular momentum r×p are cross products
- Choose by the answer needed: a number → dot; a new direction → cross

## Do It Yourself
1. A force of 10 N acts at 60° to a displacement of 4 m. Calculate the work done, and state which product you used.
2. Vectors A (3 î + 4 ĵ) and B (4 î − 3 ĵ) (in newtons and metres as appropriate): calculate A·B and say what the result tells you about the angle between them.
3. A spanner of length 0.30 m is pulled at its end with 40 N at right angles to the spanner. Calculate the torque's size, name the product used, and give its direction in words.
