# Ocean currents and water density — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Warming a sample of water (above 4 °C) does what to its density?
a) It increases  b) It decreases, because the water expands so the same mass fills more volume  c) It stays exactly the same  d) It becomes zero
**Answer: b) It decreases, because the water expands so the same mass fills more volume**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Adding dissolved salt to water raises its density because…
a) Salt adds volume but no mass  b) Salt adds mass without adding much volume  c) Salt heats the water  d) Salt removes water
**Answer: b) Salt adds mass without adding much volume**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Water sinks to the deep ocean floor where it is…
a) Warm and fresh  b) Cold and salty, so its density is high  c) Full of air bubbles  d) Moving fastest
**Answer: b) Cold and salty, so its density is high**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) The density-driven overturning of the oceans is called…
a) The tide  b) Thermohaline circulation  c) Evaporation  d) The water cycle
**Answer: b) Thermohaline circulation**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Upwelling zones are rich fishing grounds because rising deep water brings up…
a) Warmth  b) Nutrients  c) Salt only  d) Sand
**Answer: b) Nutrients**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) Flooding the surface with fresh meltwater can slow the overturning because…
a) Fresh water is too cold  b) The surface water becomes less dense and sinks less readily  c) Fresh water stops the wind  d) The ocean becomes shallower
**Answer: b) The surface water becomes less dense and sinks less readily**
