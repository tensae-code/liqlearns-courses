# Ocean currents and water density — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) An egg sinks in fresh water but floats in strong brine. Explain, without saying the egg changed.
**Answer: The egg's density stays between the two waters' densities: fresh water is less dense than the egg, so the egg sinks; brine is denser than the egg, so the egg floats. Floating is a contest between the object's density and the liquid's.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Trace one parcel of surface water through the thermohaline circulation.
**Answer: In a cold, salty region the parcel grows dense, sinks to the deep floor and spreads toward warmer regions; eventually it rises (for example in upwelling), warms at the surface and travels back toward the cold region — a slow loop driven by density differences.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do warm ocean currents soften the climate of a cold coast?
**Answer: The current carries heat gathered in warm regions and releases it along the cold coast, raising air temperatures there — the ocean moves heat that the local sunlight did not supply.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A hydrometer is placed in three liquids and floats highest in liquid C. Rank A (sinks deepest), B and C by density.
**Answer: Floating highest means the liquid is densest, so C is densest; sinking deepest means least dense, so A is least dense: density order A < B < C.**
