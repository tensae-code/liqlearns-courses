# Lesson plan — Ocean currents and water density

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Ocean currents and water density
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the density lessons — Density, Density and specific gravity and Density — why things sink or float: this is the lesson that takes density out of the beaker and into the whole ocean
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** water's density is not fixed — it falls as water warms and rises as water cools or grows saltier — and those small differences are an engine: light warm water floats and spreads on top, cold salty water sinks and creeps along the deep floor, and together the sinking and rising drive the great ocean currents

## Sub lessons
1. **Density changes — warm, cold and salty water** — warm water expands, so the same mass fills more volume and its density falls; cold water contracts and grows denser; dissolved salt adds mass without adding much volume, so salty water is denser than fresh water at the same temperature — a hydrometer sinks less in denser water, and an egg that sinks in fresh water can float in strong brine
2. **Sinking and rising — the engine of the currents** — surface water in cold, salty places grows dense enough to sink to the deep floor and spread toward the equator, while warm light water spreads over the surface toward the poles — this slow overturning, the thermohaline circulation (thermo for temperature, haline for salt), is a current driven by density alone, running alongside the wind-driven surface currents
3. **What the currents carry — climate, food and coasts** — the moving water carries heat, oxygen and nutrients with it: warm currents soften cold coasts, cold upwelling water brings nutrients up where deep water rises and feeds rich fishing grounds, and a change in surface temperature or salt — extra meltwater freshening the surface, for instance — can slow the whole overturning by making the surface water too light to sink

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Warming lowers water's density and cooling or adding salt raises it, and floating is a contest between the object's density and the water's.
2. Say, in their own words: Cold, salty surface water sinks and spreads deep while warm light water spreads on top — that density-driven overturning is the thermohaline circulation.
3. Say, in their own words: Ocean currents carry heat, oxygen and nutrients, so they shape coastal climates and fishing grounds.
4. Use a hydrometer/egg-and-brine result to rank liquids by density.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the current is only wind → wind drives surface currents, but wind cannot push water along the deep floor; only density can sink surface water down there, and what sinks somewhere must rise somewhere else — that loop is the lesson's current.
