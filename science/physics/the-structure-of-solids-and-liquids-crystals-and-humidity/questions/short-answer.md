# The structure of solids and liquids — crystals and humidity — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) One heating test separates crystalline from amorphous solids. State it and both outcomes.
**Answer: Heat the solid: a crystal melts at one sharp temperature; an amorphous solid softens gradually over a range, having no single melting point.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a falling raindrop pull itself nearly spherical?
**Answer: Surface tension shrinks the surface to the smallest area for its volume — a sphere — because the surface acts like a stretched film.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does evaporation cool the liquid that remains?
**Answer: The particles that escape are the fastest ones; losing them lowers the average kinetic energy — the temperature — of those left behind.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Can saturated vapour's density at a fixed temperature be raised by squeezing more vapour in? Say why.
**Answer: No — at dynamic equilibrium any extra vapour condenses back; the saturated density is fixed by temperature alone (it rises only when the temperature rises).**

**5.** (Sub lesson 4) A psychrometer's wet bulb reads much lower than its dry bulb. What is the air like?
**Answer: Dry — low relative humidity; fast evaporation from the wet bulb cools it strongly, so a wide difference means dry air (the readings draw together near saturation).**
