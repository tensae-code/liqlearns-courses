# How this physics course works — the Kazakhstan humanities-track way — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does this track rebuild the epoch of a discovery instead of going straight to the formula?
**Answer:** Because on this way a law is understood as something thinkable in its time: knowing the instruments, arguments and world around it shows why it was discovered then and what it replaced — the formula is remembered inside its story.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the project differ from ordinary homework exercises?
**Answer:** Exercises are short and closed, practising one skill. The project is one growing work kept all year: each topic adds its epoch and evidence, so the learner builds — and finally defends — their own reconstruction of the historical-physical picture of the world.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What must a learner show at the defence?
**Answer:** Three things: the physics stated correctly, the discovery placed in its epoch (its people, arts and politics), and answers to questions about the joins — how one step led to the next.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name one risk of this way and how the book guards it.
**Answer:** The story could swallow the science — all epoch, no physics. The guard is the defence itself plus the track's ordinary sections and laboratory works: the physics must still be stated, used and measured correctly, or the coursework fails.
