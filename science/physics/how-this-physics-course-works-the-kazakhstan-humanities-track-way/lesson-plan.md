## Sub lessons
1. **Physics through history — rebuild the epoch** — The OGN preface is explicit: this track meets physics through the scientific method and through history. A discovery is not a formula dropped from the sky — it is something a person in a particular time could think, with that time's instruments and arguments. So the learner rebuilds the epoch of a discovery: who wrote then, who painted, who ruled, what people feared and hoped. Newton stands in plague-year England; the laws of thermodynamics open, as the book itself opens, with the old quarrel between the caloric theory and the motion theory of heat.
2. **The year-long project** — One creative project runs the whole year, chosen early and grown lesson by lesson: each new topic adds its epoch and its evidence to the project. The project is the track's memory made visible — a growing reconstruction of the historical-physical picture of the world, in the learner's own arrangement. Keeping it is the homework habit of this way: not exercises alone, but a work that accumulates, the way the science itself accumulated.
3. **The defence — coursework** — At the year's end the project is defended as coursework: presented, explained and questioned, like the dissertations it imitates at school scale. The defence is the assessment of this way — the learner must show the physics correctly, place it in its epoch, and answer for the joins. What is being certified is the track's whole claim: that a person who can tell how humanity came to know a thing, and prove the knowing, knows the thing.
