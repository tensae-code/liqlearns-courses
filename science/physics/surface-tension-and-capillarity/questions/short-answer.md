# Surface tension and capillarity — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain why a pond skater can stand on water but a person cannot.
**Answer: The skater's light feet rest in dents on the surface skin; its weight is spread over the tight surface without breaking it. A person's weight on two feet breaks through the surface at once — surface tension can only carry very small loads spread gently.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Drawn in words: describe the meniscus of water and of mercury in a glass tube and give the cause of each.
**Answer: Water's meniscus curves up at the edges: adhesion to glass beats cohesion, so the edge creeps up. Mercury's curves down: cohesion beats adhesion, so mercury pulls away from the glass and its level is depressed.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does water spread quickly through a paper towel but sit as beads on a waxed surface?
**Answer: The towel is full of fine spaces that water wets — adhesion wins and capillarity pulls water through. Wax is not wetted: cohesion wins, the water pulls itself into beads and does not spread.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A farmer wants soil water to reach roots from a moist layer below. What property of the soil's pore spaces decides how high it can climb?
**Answer: The fineness of the pores: narrower spaces give a higher capillary rise, like narrower tubes, provided the water wets the soil grains.**
