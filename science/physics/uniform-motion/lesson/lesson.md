# Uniform motion

## Do it first
Walk as steadily as you can while a partner claps each second; mark where each clap finds you. Even marks mean you nearly managed uniform motion.

[Picture 21820 — equally spaced position dots along a straight track]

## Uniform motion — the teaching
Uniform motion is motion at constant velocity in a straight line: equal displacements in equal times, again and again. Nothing speeds up, nothing slows down, nothing turns. It is the baseline case of motion — the calm pattern against which accelerated motion is compared. Mark a uniformly moving body's position every second and the marks sit evenly spaced along the track.

## The key ideas

- Uniform motion means constant velocity in a straight line.
- Equal displacements happen in equal times.
- Position marks every second sit evenly spaced.

## Say it to your companion
Explain uniform motion to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find one near-uniform motion at home (a dripping tap's fall is not — a moving belt or escalator is) and justify it.
2. Write the key ideas in your own words — one line each — without looking back. Then check them against the lesson and fix any line that drifted.
