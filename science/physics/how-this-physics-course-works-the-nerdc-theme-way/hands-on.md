# Hands-on — How this Physics course works — the NERDC theme way

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes. No apparatus is needed — paper, a pen, and a companion if you have one.

## Sort the topics onto the map
Write these twelve topic names on separate scraps of paper: position and displacement; work and energy; heat energy; electric charges; sound waves; the human eye; the gravitational field; electric fields; fluids in motion; units of measurement; lenses; musical instruments. Now lay out six large sheets labelled with the six themes and sort every scrap onto its theme. Check yourself against the lesson: measurement and motion sit in Theme 1, work/heat/charges open Theme 2, sound and the eye live in Theme 3, the fields in Theme 4, fluids close Theme 5's Book 1 run, and lenses with musical instruments finish the course in Theme 6. Say aloud, for each scrap: the theme's name, and the one sentence that makes the topic belong there.

## Read a topic like a scheme of work
Pick any topic you have already studied in this Physics title. Write its performance objectives yourself: three sentences beginning "By the end, I can…" that a teacher could honestly check. Compare them with what the lesson's questions actually asked of you. Say aloud: an objective is a promise the lesson makes before it starts — and the questions are where the promise is kept or broken.

## Hunt the missing theme
Take the six theme names and, from the lesson, mark which themes the first book carries and which one it leaves out; then do the same for the second book. Say aloud: a year's book is one dealing of the course, not the whole course — the missing theme is named openly in the book itself, and it belongs to another year.
