# Lesson plan — How this Physics course works — the NERDC theme way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** How this Physics course works — the NERDC theme way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the other how-this-course-works lessons — the Ethiopian Grade 9, Grade 10 and Grade 12 ways and the Zambia CDC module way: this is Nigeria's way, where the whole of Physics is organised by six unifying **themes** set by the Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council (NERDC)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Nigeria's Physics course is not a list of apparatus topics — it is built on six themes that hold the whole subject together: **interaction** of matter, space and time; **conservation** principles; **waves** — motion without material transfer; **fields** at rest and in motion; **energy quantization and duality** of matter; and **Physics in technology** — and every topic is taught through a scheme of work with performance objectives the learner must be able to show
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. No earlier lesson is needed; it is a map of the country this whole Physics title walks through, in Nigeria's arrangement

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why themes — the course's promise** — the Introduction's purpose and goals: Physics organised by the big ideas that keep returning, so that a topic is never a lonely fact; the teacher's role and the time allocation that the scheme of work lays down for each theme and topic
2. **The six themes — the map of the whole subject** — Theme 1 Interaction of matter, space and time; Theme 2 Conservation principles; Theme 3 Waves — motion without material transfer; Theme 4 Fields at rest and in motion; Theme 5 Energy quantization and duality of matter; Theme 6 Physics in technology — and how familiar topics (motion, heat, sound, lenses, musical instruments) find their homes inside the themes
3. **The scheme of work — how a topic is taught** — each topic arrives with performance objectives: statements of what the learner will be able to do; the year's topics are dealt out across the themes, and a theme a year's book does not carry is left out openly, not silently — Book 1 carries no Theme 3, Book 2 carries no Theme 4

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: none needed. The other how-this-course-works lessons (the Ethiopian ways, the Zambia CDC module way) make a good comparison afterwards. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name all six NERDC themes in order and say, in their own words, what kind of physics each one holds.
2. Place a given topic — motion, heat energy, sound waves, lenses, musical instruments — inside its theme and say why it lives there.
3. Say what a scheme of work is and what performance objectives do for a learner.
4. Explain why a year's book can honestly carry only five of the six themes, and name which theme each of the first two books leaves out.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
For the hands-on: nothing but the lesson itself and a notebook — the activity is sorting real topic names onto the six-theme map, on paper or aloud with a companion.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: sort the six topic cards by what they share, before any theme is named. The themes are only names for the piles the learner already made.
- Mixes up Themes 4 and 5 → fields are about influence reaching across space (gravity, electric and magnetic fields); quantization is about energy and matter coming in grains — light and matter behaving as both wave and particle.
- Thinks a missing theme is a printing error → it is the curriculum's own dealing of the year: each book states openly which theme it does not include, and the theme returns in another year's book.
