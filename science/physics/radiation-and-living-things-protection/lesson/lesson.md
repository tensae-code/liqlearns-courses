# Radiation and living things — protection

## Do it first
Before you read: the same radiation that can find a tumour can also cause one. The difference is dose and control. What three simple rules — no instruments needed — cut any dose down?

## Sub lesson 1 — What radiation does inside a body

Alpha, beta and gamma radiation are ionising: passing through tissue they knock electrons off atoms, breaking molecules — including DNA. A cell usually repairs the damage; a large dose overwhelms repair, killing cells (radiation sickness at whole-body doses) or corrupting instructions so that a cancer may start years later. Children and dividing tissues are most sensitive. The Grade 9 book states this plainly because it is the reason for everything that follows: radiation is a tool with a price, and the price is charged by dose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dose — the measure that decides

Harm follows dose: the energy absorbed per kilogram of tissue, counted with the radiation's kind, in sieverts for protection work. Background radiation — rocks, sky, our own bodies — gives everyone a few millisieverts a year, inescapably. Medicine adds chosen doses: an X-ray's tiny fraction against the information it buys. The rule of the field is ALARA — keep every dose As Low As Reasonably Achievable: no dose without a reason, and no reason accepted at a higher dose than needed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Time, distance, shielding — the three rules

Protection needs no magic, only the three rules. Time: dose grows with minutes near a source — work briskly, plan before you start. Distance: intensity falls with the square of distance — double the distance, quarter the dose; tongs and remote tools are distance in disguise. Shielding: matter absorbs — lead for X-rays and gamma, a sheet of metal or even paper for alpha, glass or plastic for beta; hospitals build the rules into walls, aprons and badges that record each worker's dose. Time, distance, shielding: the whole of practical protection, and the lesson's closing account.

## The key ideas

- Ionising radiation breaks molecules in cells, DNA included; harm follows dose
- Dose is absorbed energy per kilogram, weighted by radiation kind — sieverts in protection work; background gives a few mSv a year
- ALARA: every dose as low as reasonably achievable — none without a reason
- Protection = time (less), distance (inverse-square), shielding (matter absorbs)

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: ionising radiation damages living tissue by knocking electrons from atoms inside cells — the harm depends on dose, and protection rests on three rules anyone can use: shorten the time, increase the distance, and put shielding between the source and the body. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A worker halves her time near a source, doubles her distance, and adds shielding that absorbs half of what reaches her. By what factor is her dose cut? Show each rule's factor.
2. Why is a chest X-ray acceptable while the same dose 'for no reason' is not? Answer with ALARA.
3. Match the shield to the radiation: alpha, beta, gamma — paper, plastic/glass, lead. Justify each in one phrase.
