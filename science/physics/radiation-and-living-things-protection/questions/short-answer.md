# Radiation and living things — protection — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why are children and dividing tissues most sensitive to radiation?
**Answer: Because the damage that matters most is to DNA instructions; tissues that divide often copy those instructions often, so an unrepaired error is multiplied — and a child has more years of division ahead.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A nurse stands 1 m from a source for 10 minutes. Give two changes that each cut her dose, with the physics of each.
**Answer: Halve the time → half the dose (dose ∝ time). Step back to 2 m → quarter intensity (inverse-square law). (Shielding with a lead apron also counts: absorption in matter.)**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do hospital radiation workers wear dose badges if the three rules already protect them?
**Answer: The badge checks the rules worked: it records each worker's accumulated dose so any failure of time, distance or shielding practice is caught and corrected — protection verified, not assumed.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) State the difference between a dose that sickens at once and a dose that raises cancer risk.
**Answer: Large whole-body doses kill so many cells that organs fail — radiation sickness, within days. Small doses kill few cells but may corrupt DNA in survivors, raising a statistical cancer risk years later.**
