# Drawing vectors — arrows with scale and direction

## Do it first
Draw a 40 N force at a bearing you choose, using 1 cm = 10 N. Measure your own arrow back and recover 40 N — your drawing is now data.

## Drawing vectors — arrows with scale and direction — the teaching
A vector is drawn as an arrow. The arrow's length is its magnitude, drawn to a stated scale — for instance 1 cm stands for 10 N — and its direction is given by an angle or a bearing from a stated reference. In symbols, vectors are written bold or with an arrow over the letter, so a reader always knows direction is part of the quantity. A drawing without a scale is a sketch; with a scale and bearing it is data.

## The key ideas

- A vector is drawn as an arrow: length = magnitude, to a stated scale.
- Direction is given by angle or bearing.
- Vector symbols are written bold or with an arrow over the letter.

## Say it to your companion
Explain drawing vectors — arrows with scale and direction to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
