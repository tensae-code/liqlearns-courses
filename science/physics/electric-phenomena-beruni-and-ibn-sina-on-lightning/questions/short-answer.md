# Electric phenomena — Beruni and Ibn Sina on lightning — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name two electric phenomena known to Beruni's world besides lightning.
**Answer: Rubbed amber attracting straw and chaff; the stunning touch of certain electric fish (also: storm-fire behaving unlike hearth-fire).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What makes Ibn Sina's lightning account science-shaped, even though its mechanism is wrong?
**Answer: It gives lightning a place (inside storm clouds), a natural cause (the cloud's violent discharge) and a linked effect (thunder as the crash) — a testable, natural story instead of an omen or a wonder.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the Grade 8 book open electricity with history at all?
**Answer: Because the stance is the lesson: the phenomena of nature and the phenomena of the bench are one subject, approached by collecting and comparing before measuring — the method the whole chapter then uses.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) A classmate says rubbed-amber attraction is 'just a trick, not physics'. Answer with Beruni's method in one or two sentences.
**Answer: It is a repeatable fact — rub, attract, record — and repeated, compared facts are exactly what physics is built from; the electroscope chapter that follows is this same trick made measurable.**
