# Hands-on — What gravitation theory achieved — weighing the Earth, predicting planets

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Weigh a planet with a moon's clock
Work the audit yourself on paper. (1) Earth: take g = 9.8 m/s², R = 6.37 × 10⁶ m, G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ SI; compute M = gR²/G and compare with the accepted 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg. (2) Jupiter: its moon Io orbits at r = 4.22 × 10⁸ m with period T = 1.77 days = 1.53 × 10⁵ s. Compute M = 4π²r³/(GT²) (you should land near 1.9 × 10²⁷ kg) — you have weighed a planet you will never stand on, using a moon as its scale-pan. (3) Neptune audit: read the timeline — Uranus found 1781, drift documented by the 1840s, Adams and Le Verrier compute 1845–46, Galle observes 23 September 1846 — and write two sentences on why this counts as the theory predicting, not merely explaining. Keep all three computations in your notes: they are the achievements section in working form.
