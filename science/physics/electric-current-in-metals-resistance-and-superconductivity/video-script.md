# Video script — Electric current in metals — resistance and temperature, superconductivity

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **the river inside a wire — and the day it stopped resisting**. A metal is full of free electrons, a sea moving fast at random and going nowhere. A battery adds a creep — a drift slower than an ant — yet the lamp lights at once, because the signal that starts the creep races near light speed. What fights the drift is the lattice, and the hotter it vibrates the harder it fights: resistance rises with temperature. But cool some metals far enough and the fight stops dead. In 1911 Onnes found mercury at four degrees above absolute zero with **zero** resistance — currents that flow forever. That is superconductivity.
