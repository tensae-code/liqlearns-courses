# Hands-on — Electric current in metals — resistance and temperature, superconductivity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Cold wire, hot wire
With a multimeter (or a teacher's), measure the resistance of a long thin coil of wire at room temperature; then measure again after the coil has been gently warmed (warm water bath, teacher-supervised). Compare: the warm coil reads higher. Say aloud: nothing about the wire's length or thickness changed — the lattice's vibration scattered the drift harder.

## Drift-speed sense-check
Take a torch battery circuit: note the lamp lights essentially instantly when the switch closes, anywhere in the circuit. Say aloud: the signal raced around at near light speed, but each electron drifted slower than a crawling ant — the sea was already everywhere in the wire, and it all began to creep at once.
