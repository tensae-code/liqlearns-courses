# Lesson plan — Electric current in metals — resistance and temperature, superconductivity

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Electric current in metals — resistance and temperature, superconductivity
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the current and resistance lessons — the microscopic completion of the conduction story
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In a metal, current is a slow drift of a vast sea of free electrons; **resistivity** grows with temperature because the vibrating lattice scatters the drift harder — until, in a **superconductor** cooled past its critical temperature, resistance falls to exactly zero and a current, once started, flows forever
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the electron sea, and distinguish random motion, drift and signal speed.
2. Use ρ ≈ ρ₀(1 + αΔT) in a worked number and explain the lattice-scattering cause.
3. State what Onnes found in 1911, what a critical temperature is, and the Meissner effect.
4. Name applications that depend on resistance being exactly zero.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
