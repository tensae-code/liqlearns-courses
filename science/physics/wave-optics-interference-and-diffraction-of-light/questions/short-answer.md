# Wave optics — interference and diffraction of light — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the path-difference condition for a bright fringe and for a dark fringe.
**Answer: Bright (constructive) where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths — 0, λ, 2λ, …; dark (destructive) where it is a half-odd number — λ/2, 3λ/2, 5λ/2, … — because crest then meets trough and the waves cancel.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Double-slit fringes are 2.4 mm apart, the screen is 2 m from slits 0.5 mm apart. Find the wavelength.
**Answer: λ = w·a/D = (2.4×10⁻³ × 0.5×10⁻³) ÷ 2 = 6.0×10⁻⁷ m = 600 nm.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the central maximum of a single-slit pattern twice as wide as the side maxima?
**Answer: The first minima sit at sin θ = ±λ/a on either side of the centre, so the central maximum spans 2λ/a in angle; each side maximum is squeezed between neighbouring minima only λ/a apart — half the width (and far fainter).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A grating has 500 lines per millimetre. What is its grating spacing d?
**Answer: d = 1 ÷ 500,000 lines per metre = 2×10⁻⁶ m = 2 μm — the spacing is the reciprocal of the lines per unit length.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a grating beat a prism for separating two very close wavelengths?
**Answer: The grating's maxima are extremely sharp (thousands of slits interfering), and each wavelength peaks at its own angle d sin θ = nλ, so two close wavelengths give two separate narrow peaks instead of one smeared band.**

**6.** (Sub lesson 4) Why do polarizing sunglasses cut the glare from a wet road so well?
**Answer: Light reflected from a flat surface like a wet road is partly polarized horizontally; the sunglasses' filters have vertical transmission axes, so they block that horizontally polarized glare while still passing enough ordinary light to see by.**
