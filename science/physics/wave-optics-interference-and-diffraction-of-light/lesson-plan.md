# Lesson plan — Wave optics — interference and diffraction of light

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Wave optics — interference and diffraction of light
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Fiji placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** light proves it is a wave by doing the two things only waves do — **interference** (two coherent light waves add crest-to-crest into brightness or cancel crest-to-trough into darkness, decided by the **path difference**: a whole number of wavelengths means bright, a half-odd number means dark) and **diffraction** (light spreads as it passes a slit, most strongly when the slit is about a wavelength wide) — and the double slit, the single slit and the diffraction grating (**d sin θ = nλ**) turn those two effects into a way of measuring the wavelength of light itself
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Interference — two waves in step** — Waves from two **coherent** sources (same wavelength, constant phase difference) overlap and superpose: where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths they reinforce into a bright fringe, where it is a half-odd number they cancel into a dark fringe — Young's double slit makes the pattern stand still on a screen, with fringe width **w = λD/a**.
2. **Diffraction of light — one slit spreads the beam** — A single narrow slit spreads light into a wide central maximum flanked by dark minima where **a sin θ = nλ**; the spread is large only when the slit width is close to the wavelength, which is why light's wave nature hid for so long — its wavelength is tiny.
3. **The diffraction grating — interference as a measuring tool** — Hundreds of slits per millimetre sharpen the double-slit pattern into bright, narrow maxima at **d sin θ = nλ** for each order n; each wavelength takes its own angle, so the grating spreads white light into spectra and reads an unknown wavelength from an angle.
4. **Polarization — light with a chosen direction** — A polarizing filter passes only vibrations parallel to its axis, halving unpolarized light; a second filter follows Malus's law (I = I₀ cos²θ) and crossed filters pass nothing; reflected glare is partly polarized, so polarizing sunglasses cut it — polarization is possible only for a transverse wave, fixing light's wave geometry.
