# Satellites and escape velocity — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the satellite's own mass not appear in v = √(GM/r)? Show the cancelling.
**Answer: Setting gravity equal to the centripetal need gives GMm/r² = mv²/r; the satellite's mass m stands on both sides and cancels, leaving v² = GM/r — the speed is fixed by the planet's mass and the orbit's radius alone.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What does it mean, in energy terms, to say a body launched at escape velocity has total energy of zero?
**Answer: Its kinetic energy exactly equals the gravitational potential energy binding it (GMm/r): as it climbs it trades all its motion for height, slowing towards zero speed only at infinity, so it never falls back — zero is the boundary between being held and being free.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A weather satellite must photograph the same region continuously. Which orbit should it use, and at roughly what height and period?
**Answer: A geostationary orbit: about 35,800 km above the equator, circling in Earth's turning direction with a period of 24 hours, so it stays over the same spot and can watch the same weather systems without interruption.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) The Moon's escape velocity is much smaller than Earth's. What long-term consequence does the lesson give for the Moon?
**Answer: Gas molecules can reach escape speed and leave, so over time the Moon could not hold onto an atmosphere — its gravity well is too shallow to keep gases bound.**
