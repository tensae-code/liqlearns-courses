# Damped and forced oscillations — the torsion and gravity pendulums — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How does a torsion pendulum measure an unknown moment of inertia?
**Answer: Time its period with a body of known I to find the wire's κ from T = 2π√(I/κ); then fit the unknown body, time the new period, and solve the same equation for its I.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must a gravity pendulum's swings stay small for T = 2π√(L/g)?
**Answer: The restoring torque is proportional to sin θ, which equals θ only for small angles; only then is the motion simple harmonic with that period — large swings run slower than the formula.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A bridge is crossed by marching soldiers who break step on purpose. Which idea are they avoiding, and why?
**Answer: Resonance: marching in step would drive the bridge at a steady rhythm; if that rhythm matched a natural frequency of the structure, small repeated pushes would build a dangerously large amplitude.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Distinguish damped from forced oscillation in one sentence each.
**Answer: Damped: friction drains energy each cycle so the amplitude decays. Forced: an external periodic force feeds energy in, sustaining or growing the oscillation, most strongly at resonance.**
