# Red, green, blue — how eyes build colour

## Do it first
Stare at a bright red patch for half a minute, then look at a white wall: a greenish ghost appears — your red cones, tired, letting their partners answer louder.

## Red, green, blue — how eyes build colour — the teaching
The retina carries two kinds of light sensors. Rods work in dim light without colour; cones work in bright light and come in three kinds, sensing red, green and blue. The brain builds every colour you see from how strongly those three cone kinds answer: equal answers from all three read as white; red and green together read as yellow. Red, green and blue are therefore the primary colours of light — the eye's own alphabet of colour.

## The key ideas

- Rods sense dim light; cones sense colour in bright light.
- Three cone kinds sense red, green and blue.
- Red, green and blue are the primary colours of light.

## Say it to your companion
Explain red, green, blue — how eyes build colour to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
