# The speed of light, the light-year and the astronomical unit — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does light seem instantaneous in everyday life but not in astronomy?
**Answer: Its speed is so large that earthly delays are unmeasurably small; over astronomical distances the same speed gives delays of minutes, years and millions of years.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Compute the light-year in metres from c = 3.00×10⁸ m/s.
**Answer: Distance = c × t = 3.00×10⁸ × (365.25 × 24 × 3600) ≈ 3.00×10⁸ × 3.16×10⁷ ≈ 9.46×10¹⁵ m.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A planet is 2 AU from the Sun. How long does sunlight take to reach it?
**Answer: About 2 × 8 minutes = 16 minutes (light crosses each AU in roughly 8 minutes).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Correct this sentence: 'The star is 10 light-years old.'
**Answer: The star is 10 light-years AWAY: the light-year measures distance. The light we receive left it 10 years ago — which is a statement about the light's journey, not the star's age.**
