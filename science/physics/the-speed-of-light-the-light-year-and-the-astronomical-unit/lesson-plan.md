# Lesson plan — The speed of light, the light-year and the astronomical unit

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The speed of light, the light-year and the astronomical unit

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** light travels at a finite speed, c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s — so fast that earthly journeys seem instant, yet slow enough that astronomy measures its distances in the time light takes: the light-year (the distance light covers in one year) and the astronomical unit (the Earth–Sun distance, which light crosses in about 8 minutes)

## Sub lessons
1. **A finite speed — 3×10⁸ metres every second** — Light is not instant: it travels at c ≈ 3.00×10⁸ m/s in vacuum. Across a classroom the delay is far too small to notice, which is why light once seemed instantaneous; across space it is not. Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes to reach us — we always see the Sun as it was 8 minutes ago, and every distant thing is seen in its past.
2. **The light-year — a distance wearing a time's name** — Because c is constant, a travel time can be turned into a distance: distance = c × time. One light-year is the distance light travels in one year: 3×10⁸ m/s × (365.25 × 24 × 3600 s) ≈ 9.46×10¹⁵ m. It is a distance, not a time — the ruler astronomers use for the stars, whose kilometres would need absurd numbers of digits.
3. **The astronomical unit — the Earth–Sun step** — Inside the solar system the handier ruler is the astronomical unit: 1 AU = the mean Earth–Sun distance ≈ 1.50×10¹¹ m, which light crosses in about 499 seconds — the '8 light-minutes' of the first sub lesson. Planets' distances are quoted in AU, stars' in light-years; Mauritania's Grade 8 manual introduces both rulers in the same breath as the speed itself, so the learner sizes the universe with light from the start.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Light travels at a finite speed: c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s in vacuum
2. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth — we see distant things as they were
3. Distance = c × time turns light's travel time into a ruler
4. 1 light-year ≈ 9.46×10¹⁵ m — a distance, not a time
5. 1 AU ≈ 1.50×10¹¹ m, the Earth–Sun distance ≈ 8 light-minutes
