# Light at work — photosynthesis and photography

## Do it first
Before you read: a leaf and a strip of photographic film are both changed by light left in the sun. One grows food; the other keeps a face. What is light doing, chemically, in each?

## Sub lesson 1 — Light as a chemical agent

Light is not only for seeing. Its photons carry energy, E = hf, and where that energy lands in the right material it drives chemical change — reactions that will not run in the dark at the same temperature. The Grade 9 book gathers these as the chemical and biological action of light, with two great examples: photosynthesis, on which nearly all life feeds, and photography, on which a century of memory rests. Both are light writing chemistry: one writes sugar, the other writes an image.

## Sub lesson 2 — Photosynthesis — light stored as food

In the green leaf, chlorophyll catches light and spends it splitting water and joining carbon dioxide into sugar: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. The oxygen we breathe is its by-product; the sugar is stored sunlight, passed up every food chain — the wheat in bread is last summer's light, banked. Rate follows the physics: brighter light (up to saturation), more CO₂ and warmth speed it; darkness stops it dead. It is the largest chemical industry on Earth and it runs on photons.

## Sub lesson 3 — Photography — light written in silver

Film carries silver halide crystals in gelatine. Where photons strike, a few silver atoms form — an invisible latent image, stronger where the light was stronger. The developer amplifies those specks into black metallic silver grains: the negative, dark where the scene was bright. The fixer dissolves the unstruck crystals so the image stops changing, and printing reverses the negative into a positive picture. A photodiode and a phone sensor do the same job electronically — count photons place by place — but the film's chemistry is light's action in its purest classroom form.

## The key ideas

- Light drives chemical change where its photon energy lands — reactions that will not run in the dark
- Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ — stored sunlight in sugar, oxygen as by-product
- Photography: photons form a latent image in silver halide; developer amplifies it, fixer makes it permanent
- Both are light writing: the leaf writes food, the film writes a picture

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: light does chemical work: in photosynthesis it builds sugar from carbon dioxide and water, storing the Sun's energy in food; in photography it changes silver salts on a film, writing a lasting picture — the two great chemical actions of light the Grade 9 book teaches together. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the photosynthesis equation and label which side is light's raw material and which is its stored product.
2. A leaf in a dark cupboard and a leaf in sunlight are given equal water and air. Predict and explain their difference after a week.
3. Explain why an undeveloped film must be handled in darkness — what would room light do to it, in the language of latent images?
