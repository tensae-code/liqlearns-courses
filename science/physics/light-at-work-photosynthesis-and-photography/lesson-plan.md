## Sub lessons
1. **Light as a chemical agent** — Light is not only for seeing. Its photons carry energy, E = hf, and where that energy lands in the right material it drives chemical change — reactions that will not run in the dark at the same temperature. The Grade 9 book gathers these as the chemical and biological action of light, with two great examples: photosynthesis, on which nearly all life feeds, and photography, on which a century of memory rests. Both are light writing chemistry: one writes sugar, the other writes an image.
2. **Photosynthesis — light stored as food** — In the green leaf, chlorophyll catches light and spends it splitting water and joining carbon dioxide into sugar: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. The oxygen we breathe is its by-product; the sugar is stored sunlight, passed up every food chain — the wheat in bread is last summer's light, banked. Rate follows the physics: brighter light (up to saturation), more CO₂ and warmth speed it; darkness stops it dead. It is the largest chemical industry on Earth and it runs on photons.
3. **Photography — light written in silver** — Film carries silver halide crystals in gelatine. Where photons strike, a few silver atoms form — an invisible latent image, stronger where the light was stronger. The developer amplifies those specks into black metallic silver grains: the negative, dark where the scene was bright. The fixer dissolves the unstruck crystals so the image stops changing, and printing reverses the negative into a positive picture. A photodiode and a phone sensor do the same job electronically — count photons place by place — but the film's chemistry is light's action in its purest classroom form.
