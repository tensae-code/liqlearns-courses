# Wien's law and the Stefan–Boltzmann law — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A furnace peaks at 1450 nm. Find its temperature.
**Answer: T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ ÷ 1.45 × 10⁻⁶ = 2000 K.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a red-hot bar (1000 K) look dim beside a white-hot one (2000 K) — by roughly what factor per square metre?
**Answer: Stefan–Boltzmann: (2000/1000)⁴ = 16 — the white-hot bar radiates about sixteen times more per square metre (and its peak has also shifted bluer, per Wien).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Can Wien's law alone tell you a star's total radiated power? Say what else you need.
**Answer: No — Wien gives the temperature from the peak; the power per square metre then needs Stefan–Boltzmann (σT⁴), and a total power would also need the star's surface area.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must T be in kelvin in both laws?
**Answer: Both laws are built on absolute temperature — zero kelvin is the true zero of thermal motion; Celsius would shift every value and break the constant and the fourth power.**
