## Sub lessons
1. **Wien's law — hotter means bluer** — A black body radiates a spread of wavelengths, but the spread has a peak, and the peak slides as temperature changes: λmax × T = constant (2.9 × 10⁻³ m·K). At 3000 K the peak sits near 970 nm, in the infrared side of red heat; at the Sun's surface, about 5800 K, it sits near 500 nm, in visible green-yellow — which is why sunlight looks white-yellow. Read a glowing body's peak wavelength and Wien's law hands you its temperature without touching it.
2. **The Stefan–Boltzmann law — the fourth power** — Total it all up and the radiated power per square metre is P/A = σT⁴, σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴, T in kelvin. The fourth power is the drama: double the absolute temperature and the radiation multiplies by 16; triple it, by 81. A body at room temperature (300 K) radiates about 460 W/m² invisibly in the infrared; the Sun's surface at 5800 K radiates tens of millions of watts per square metre. Real surfaces radiate less than a black body — the black body is the ceiling.
3. **Using the two laws together** — Wien prices colour → temperature; Stefan–Boltzmann prices that temperature → total power. Worked chain: a star's spectrum peaks at 290 nm, so T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2.9 × 10⁻⁷ = 10,000 K; each square metre of its surface then radiates σT⁴ ≈ 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 10¹⁶ ≈ 5.7 × 10⁸ W. Furnaces, filament lamps, the Earth's own infrared glow to space and every star in the sky are all read with this same pair of laws.
