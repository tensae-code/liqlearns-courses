# Lesson plan — Conservative and nonconservative forces

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Conservative and nonconservative forces
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the energy strand — the distinction Jordan's Grade 11 conservation treatment rests on
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a force is conservative when the work it does between two points is the same on every path — and zero around any closed loop — so a potential energy can be stored against it; gravity and the spring force qualify. Friction is nonconservative: its work depends on the path's length, and mechanical energy paid to it does not come back. Jordan's Grade 11 book builds its whole conservation lesson on this distinction

## Sub lessons
1. **The path test — work that does not depend on the route** — Lift a mass from the floor to a shelf by any route — straight up, along a ramp, in stages — and gravity's work is the same: −mgh, fixed by the height alone. A force passing this test on every pair of points is conservative. The closed-loop form is sharper: carry the mass around any round trip and gravity's total work is zero — what it takes on the way up it returns on the way down.
2. **Potential energy — the conservative forces' account** — Only a conservative force can hold an account: because its work is path-independent, the work done against it is stored as a potential energy that depends only on position — gravitational potential energy mgh, elastic potential energy ½kx². Do work against gravity and the account grows; let gravity work and it pays out as kinetic energy. The account exists precisely because the path does not matter.
3. **Friction — the nonconservative force that spends** — Slide the mass across the floor and friction's work is −(friction force × distance travelled): take a longer path and more is spent — path-dependent, and around a closed loop never zero. Work done against friction is not stored anywhere recoverable; it leaves as heat and sound. That is why conservation of mechanical energy must be stated with the distinction built in: mechanical energy is conserved in systems where only conservative forces do work, and changes by exactly the nonconservative work otherwise.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A conservative force's work between two points is the same on every path, and zero around a closed loop
2. Gravity and the spring force are conservative
3. Only conservative forces can store potential energy — mgh and ½kx²
4. Friction is nonconservative: its work grows with the path's length and is not recoverable
5. Mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces work; otherwise it changes by the nonconservative work
