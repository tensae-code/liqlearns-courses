# Conservative and nonconservative forces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the two equivalent tests of a conservative force.
**Answer: Its work between any two points is independent of the path taken; equivalently, its work around any closed loop is zero.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why can friction hold no potential-energy account?
**Answer: An account needs the stored amount to depend only on position; friction's work depends on how far the body travelled, so the 'stored' amount would differ for the same position reached by different paths — no single account value exists.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A pendulum swings with a little air resistance. Describe its mechanical energy over many swings, using the distinction.
**Answer: Gravity (conservative) trades kinetic and potential energy back and forth unchanged, but air resistance (nonconservative) does negative work each swing, so the total mechanical energy falls swing by swing — by exactly the resistance's work.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A mass is lifted, carried horizontally 10 m at the same height, and set down where it started in height. What is gravity's total work, and what would friction's be on the same trip if it were dragged?
**Answer: Gravity's is zero (no net height change — closed in height). Friction's would be its force × the whole distance travelled, positive cost throughout — path, not endpoints, sets it.**
