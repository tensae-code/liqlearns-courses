# Steam and motion — heat engines and the industrial revolution — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the three parts of every heat engine's story, in order.
**Answer: It takes in heat from a hot side; it turns part of that heat into work; it throws the remaining heat away to a cold side.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Follow one full cycle of a steam engine in four steps.
**Answer: Water boils in the boiler and becomes steam; the steam expands in the cylinder and pushes the piston (the rod and flywheel turn the push into rotation); the steam is cooled and condensed back to water; the cycle begins again.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What did Watt's improvements do for the steam engine?
**Answer: They made it much stronger for the same coal — above all by condensing the steam in a separate vessel so the cylinder stayed hot — so the engine became thrifty enough to power factories anywhere, not only at mines.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two changes of the industrial revolution that steam power made possible.
**Answer: Any two of: factories rising in towns away from rivers, multiplying machines and leaping production of cloth and iron; railway locomotives; steamships — work and travel freed from muscle, wind and water.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Can a perfectly built heat engine turn all of its heat into work? Why or why not?
**Answer: No. Every heat engine works in a cycle and must throw part of its heat away to a cold side, so its efficiency is always below 100% — the waste heat is a law of the pattern, not a flaw of one design.**
