# Hands-on — Steam and motion — heat engines and the industrial revolution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Steam lifts
With care, watch a kettle or a lidded pot come to the boil (an adult's job if the water is very hot). Steam lifts and rattles the lid. Say aloud: expanding steam pushes — that push is the beginning of every steam engine.

## The paper spiral
Cut a paper spiral, hang it on a thread above (not in) rising steam or warm air, and watch it turn. Heat made motion with no engine at all — then say what a piston and cylinder add: they catch the push in turns and never let it rest.

## Trace an engine you know
Pick any engine you can see this week — a bus, a generator, a motorcycle. Find its hot side (the exhaust, the hot engine block) and its cold side (the air it throws heat into), and name the work it does. Write the three parts: heat in, work out, waste heat out.
