# Lesson plan — Steam and motion — heat engines and the industrial revolution

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Steam and motion — heat engines and the industrial revolution
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics — Thermodynamics neighbourhood, beside the heat lessons and after the zeroth law (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a heat engine turns part of a flow of heat into work — the steam engine did this with boiling water and a piston, and engines like it powered the industrial revolution — but no heat engine can turn all its heat into work; some heat is always thrown away to a cold side.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own world of boiling kettles, engines and trains — a learner can land here directly. Zeroth law of thermodynamics and temperature equilibrium and What heat is deepen it, but nothing must come first.

## Sub lessons
1. **Turning heat into motion — the heat engine** — every heat engine works the same way: take heat from something hot, turn part of it into work, and throw the rest away to something cold.
2. **The steam engine at work** — boiling water makes steam, the steam expands and pushes a piston, the steam cools and condenses, and the wheel turns; Watt's improvements made the engine strong and thrifty enough to leave the mine and enter the factory.
3. **Steam remakes the world — the industrial revolution** — engines freed work from muscle, wind and water: factories, railways and steamships followed — and the engine's limit stood through all of it: efficiency is always less than all of the heat.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **What heat is** and **Zeroth law of thermodynamics and temperature equilibrium**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe a heat engine in one line: heat in from a hot side, part turned into work, the rest thrown out to a cold side.
2. Follow one cycle of a steam engine: boil, expand and push, condense, repeat.
3. Say why the steam engine changed where factories could stand and how goods and people moved — the industrial revolution.
4. State the engine's limit: no heat engine converts all of its heat into work; efficiency is always below 100%.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: steam from a kettle lid lifts the lid. Something that lifts can push; something that pushes in turns can turn a wheel.
- Thinks the engine uses up heat like fuel → the fuel is coal; heat is what the engine works with — it takes heat from the hot side, keeps part as work, and must dump the rest on the cold side.
- Thinks a perfect engine is only a matter of better building → the waste heat is not a flaw of one design: every heat engine must throw heat away to a cold side, so efficiency is always below all of the heat.
