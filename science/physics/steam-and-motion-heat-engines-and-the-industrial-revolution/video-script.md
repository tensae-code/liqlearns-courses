Today: **Steam and motion — heat engines and the industrial revolution**.

Boil water. Steam pushes. That push can move the world.
A heat engine takes heat from a hot side, turns part of it into work — and throws the rest away to the cold side. Every engine. Always.
The steam engine put that idea in iron: boiler, piston, wheel. Then came the factories, the trains, the ships. Work no longer needed muscle or a river — only coal and fire.
But remember the limit: no engine turns all its heat into work. Some heat always escapes cold.
