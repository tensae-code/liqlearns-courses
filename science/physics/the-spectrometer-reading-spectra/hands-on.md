# Hands-on — The spectrometer — reading spectra

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Read a spectrum's angles
With a school spectrometer and a discharge tube (or a CD-grating viewer as the described alternative), set the telescope on the brightest lines left and right of the direct beam and record the angles; the mean deviation is the line's δ at minimum deviation. Say aloud the rule: the spectrometer measures angles, and geometry turns angles into wavelengths.

## Fingerprint an element
Compare the line patterns of two different discharge tubes (hydrogen and helium, or sodium and mercury lamps) sketched to scale from your readings. No two patterns coincide. Say aloud the rule: the set of wavelengths is the element's fingerprint — the instrument identifies without touching.
