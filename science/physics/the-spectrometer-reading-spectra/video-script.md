Today: **the spectrometer — reading spectra**. A collimator narrows light into a parallel beam; a prism on a graduated table spreads it; a telescope reads the angle of every colour. Set at minimum deviation, those angles become exact wavelengths. And because every element emits its own pattern of lines — its fingerprint — the spectrometer names substances it never touches: it found helium in the Sun before chemists found it on Earth.
