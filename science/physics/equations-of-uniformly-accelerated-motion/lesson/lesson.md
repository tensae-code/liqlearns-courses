# The equations of uniformly accelerated motion

## Do it first
Write the five letters v, u, a, s, t on paper. For one journey, fill in what you know and circle what you want — then notice only one equation fits the four.

## The equations of uniformly accelerated motion — the teaching
Four equations link v (final velocity), u (initial velocity), a (acceleration), s (displacement) and t (time) when acceleration is constant: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as; and s = ½(u + v)t. The skill is choosing: list what you know, name what you want, and pick the equation that contains exactly those four letters and not the fifth. Every worked example in the unit is that one choice, made well.

**Keep the formula:** v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as

## The key ideas

- v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²; v² = u² + 2as; s = ½(u + v)t.
- The equations hold only for constant acceleration.
- Choose the equation containing your three knowns and your one unknown.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the equations of uniformly accelerated motion to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
