## Ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel — the teaching
Meters must join the circuit the way the quantity flows. An ammeter measures current — the flow through a path — so it connects in series, and it is built with very low resistance so it hardly disturbs the flow it measures. A voltmeter measures potential difference between two points, so it connects in parallel across them, with very high resistance so it draws almost no current of its own. Swap them and the readings lie or the circuit breaks: a voltmeter in series nearly stops the current; an ammeter in parallel is almost a short circuit. Analog and digital meters follow the same two rules; a multimeter just changes its personality with its dial.

## The key ideas

- Ammeter in series, with low resistance, measures current.
- Voltmeter in parallel, with high resistance, measures voltage.
- Swapped meters lie, stop the circuit, or act as a short.
