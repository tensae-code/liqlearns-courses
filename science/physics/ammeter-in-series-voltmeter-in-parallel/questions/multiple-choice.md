# Ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** Where does an ammeter go, and why is its resistance low?
a) In series; low resistance so it hardly changes the current it measures.  b) In parallel, across the two points whose difference it reads.  c) Its high resistance nearly stops the current — the circuit barely works and readings are wrong.  d) Its low resistance is almost a short circuit.
**Answer: a) In series; low resistance so it hardly changes the current it measures.**

**2.** Where does a voltmeter go?
a) In series; low resistance so it hardly changes the current it measures.  b) In parallel, across the two points whose difference it reads.  c) Its high resistance nearly stops the current — the circuit barely works and readings are wrong.  d) Its low resistance is almost a short circuit.
**Answer: b) In parallel, across the two points whose difference it reads.**

**3.** A voltmeter is wired in series by mistake. Result?
a) In series; low resistance so it hardly changes the current it measures.  b) In parallel, across the two points whose difference it reads.  c) Its high resistance nearly stops the current — the circuit barely works and readings are wrong.  d) Its low resistance is almost a short circuit.
**Answer: c) Its high resistance nearly stops the current — the circuit barely works and readings are wrong.**

**4.** An ammeter across a battery (parallel) is dangerous because — ?
a) In series; low resistance so it hardly changes the current it measures.  b) In parallel, across the two points whose difference it reads.  c) Its high resistance nearly stops the current — the circuit barely works and readings are wrong.  d) Its low resistance is almost a short circuit.
**Answer: d) Its low resistance is almost a short circuit.**
