# Errors and error estimation in measurement — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A balance reads 0.2 g with nothing on it, and every mass measured on it comes out high. Classify the error and state the cure.
**Answer: A systematic error (a zero error). Cure: correct every reading by subtracting 0.2 g, or re-zero/recalibrate the balance — averaging repeats would not help, since every reading is biased the same way.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is a 0.1 s timing error worse in a 0.5 s measurement than in a 50 s one?
**Answer: The absolute error is the same but the relative error is 0.1/0.5 = 20% against 0.1/50 = 0.2% — relative (percentage) error is what judges how much an error matters.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A student reports a length as 12.734 mm from a caliper of least count 0.1 mm. What is wrong with the report?
**Answer: The digits claim a precision the instrument cannot give; with an error of about ±0.1 mm the honest report is 12.7 ± 0.1 mm — digits beyond the error are meaningless.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How is the error of a fluctuating quantity (say a flickering pointer) honestly estimated?
**Answer: Take repeated readings and use about half the spread (range) of the repeats as the error of the mean value, rather than the instrument's least count alone.**
