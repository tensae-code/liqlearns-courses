# States of matter at the particle level — intermolecular forces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a solid keep its own shape while a liquid takes the shape of its container?
**Answer: A solid's particles are locked in fixed places by strong pulls; a liquid's particles stay close but can slide past each other, so the liquid settles into the container's shape while keeping its volume.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Put solid, liquid and gas in order of intermolecular-force strength, strongest first.
**Answer: Solid strongest, then liquid, then gas weakest — the forces weaken as the particles gain freedom.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What changes at the particle level when ice melts, and what does the extra heat energy do?
**Answer: The particles vibrate harder as they are heated until the pulls can no longer hold the fixed pattern; the particles stay close but begin to slide — the heat energy goes into beating the pattern-holding part of the forces.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does mercury not climb a glass tube the way water does?
**Answer: Mercury's cohesion — its pull on itself — beats its adhesion to glass, so it pulls away from the glass instead of climbing it; water's adhesion to glass is strong enough to climb.**
