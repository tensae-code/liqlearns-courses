# Lesson plan — How this Physics course works — the Uganda competency-based way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** How this Physics course works — the Uganda competency-based way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the other "How this Physics course works" lessons — the Ethiopian, Zambian, Nigerian and other national ways: this is Uganda's framing lesson, from its Senior One teacher's guide
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Uganda's revised lower-secondary curriculum teaches Physics the competency-based way: the point is not covering content but building competences — generic skills (critical thinking, communication, problem solving, working with others) and values practised inside every topic — with lessons planned backwards from what the learner must be able to do, activity-first teaching, and assessment split into formative checks that steer learning while it happens and summative judgements (with sample rubrics) that certify it at the end

## Sub lessons
1. **The new curriculum — competences, generic skills and values** — the revised curriculum replaces content coverage with competence: every topic is a vehicle for generic skills — critical thinking and problem-solving, creativity, communication, co-operation and self-learning — and for values, practised in the activities themselves. The learner, not the teacher's talk, is at the centre: activities come first and the teacher guides, questions and consolidates
2. **Planning a lesson the competency way** — a competency-based lesson plan starts from the learning outcome — what the learner will be able to do — then chooses learner activities that build it, with locally available materials, and plans how evidence of learning will be gathered. Suggested responses to the learner activities are carried in the guide so the teacher can steer without simply giving answers
3. **Assessment — formative and summative, with rubrics** — formative assessment happens during learning — questions, observations and short tasks whose results change what the teacher does next; summative assessment happens at the end of a stretch and certifies achievement. Scoring is guided by rubrics: sample rubrics set out levels of performance so different markers judge the same work the same way, and learners know in advance what quality looks like
