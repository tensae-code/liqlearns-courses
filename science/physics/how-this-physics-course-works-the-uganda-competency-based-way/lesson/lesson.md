# How this Physics course works — the Uganda competency-based way

## Do it first
Before you read: think of two classes. In one, the teacher talks, you copy, and a test at the end tells you — too late — what you missed. In the other, you do an activity first, the teacher watches how you work, asks questions that fix misunderstandings on the spot, and you know before any test exactly what good work looks like. The second class is a design, not luck. This lesson builds that design: competences and values, planning backwards from outcomes, and formative and summative assessment with rubrics.

## Sub lesson 1 — The new curriculum — competences, generic skills and values

The revised curriculum replaces content coverage with competence: every topic is a vehicle for generic skills — critical thinking and problem-solving, creativity, communication, co-operation and self-learning — and for values, practised in the activities themselves. The learner, not the teacher's talk, is at the centre: activities come first and the teacher guides, questions and consolidates.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planning a lesson the competency way

A competency-based lesson plan starts from the learning outcome — what the learner will be able to do — then chooses learner activities that build it, with locally available materials, and plans how evidence of learning will be gathered. Suggested responses to the learner activities are carried in the guide so the teacher can steer without simply giving answers.

## Sub lesson 3 — Assessment — formative and summative, with rubrics

Formative assessment happens during learning — questions, observations and short tasks whose results change what the teacher does next; summative assessment happens at the end of a stretch and certifies achievement. Scoring is guided by rubrics: sample rubrics set out levels of performance so different markers judge the same work the same way, and learners know in advance what quality looks like.

## The key ideas

- A competency-based course aims at what learners can do — generic skills and values practised inside the topics — not at content covered.
- A competency-based lesson is planned backwards from the learning outcome to the activity to the evidence.
- Tell formative assessment (during learning, steering teaching) from summative assessment (at the end, certifying).

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole lesson in order, in your own words — start with the opening activity, then take each sub lesson in turn and finish with what it is used for. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write a learning outcome for a measurements lesson in the form "By the end, the learner can…", then name one learner activity that builds it.
2. A teacher gives a two-question check in the middle of a lesson and reteaches the missed point the next day. State whether this is formative or summative assessment and why.
3. Explain in two sentences what a rubric does for both the marker and the learner.
