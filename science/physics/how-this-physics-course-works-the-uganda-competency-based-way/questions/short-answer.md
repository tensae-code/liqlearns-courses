# How this Physics course works — the Uganda competency-based way — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name two generic skills and say how a single Physics activity could practise both.
**Answer: For example critical thinking and communication: learners predict whether a parcel of water will sink or float, test it, then explain their result to the class — the prediction and checking practise critical thinking, the explanation practises communication (any two named generic skills with a plausible activity earn the point).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the guide carry suggested responses to learner activities instead of leaving teachers to improvise every answer?
**Answer: So the teacher can steer learners toward the intended learning during the activity — prompting and correcting with a known destination in mind — without simply dictating answers; preparation makes guiding possible.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A teacher marks projects from two classes using the same sample rubric. What fairness does that buy?
**Answer: The same quality earns the same judgement in both classes, because the levels are described in advance and shared — marking depends on the described performance, not on which marker or class the work came from.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Give one formative and one summative assessment from a single topic and state the job of each.
**Answer: Formative: a mid-lesson question check whose misses are retaught next lesson — its job is to steer learning while it can still change. Summative: the end-of-topic test — its job is to certify what was learned.**
