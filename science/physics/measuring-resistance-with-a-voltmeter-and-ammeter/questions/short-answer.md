# Measuring resistance with a voltmeter and ammeter — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A voltmeter reads 4.5 V and the ammeter 0.30 A for an unknown resistor. Find R.
**Answer: R = 4.5 ÷ 0.30 = 15 Ω.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the voltmeter's own small current make one wiring arrangement read R slightly small?
**Answer: If the ammeter sits outside the voltmeter's branch it reads the resistor's current plus the voltmeter's sip; dividing V by that larger current gives a smaller R than the resistor's own.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Your three R values are 22.1, 21.8 and 24.9 Ω. What is the right move before averaging?
**Answer: Suspect the third reading: check the point on the V–I graph, retake that pair (loose contact or a misread scale are the usual causes) rather than averaging a bad reading in blindly.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the whole method in one sentence.
**Answer: Measure the current through the resistor with a series ammeter and the voltage across it with a parallel voltmeter, then divide — R = V/I — repeating at several voltages and averaging.**
