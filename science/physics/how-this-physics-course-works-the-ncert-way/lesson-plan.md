# Lesson plan — How this Physics course works — the NCERT way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** How this Physics course works — the NCERT way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md) — a new lesson built for the India placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how an NCERT Physics chapter is built and how to use it — numbered sections teach one idea at a time, worked examples sit inside the text at the moment the idea lands (not saved for the end), blue-box margin notes and "Points to Ponder" boxes stop the reader to think, and every chapter closes with exercises plus additional exercises: India's own way of learning physics, usable on every chapter that follows
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Numbered sections and worked examples inside the text** — An NCERT chapter is a staircase of numbered sections: each section states one idea, derives or shows it, and then — at once, inside the same text — works a full example of it, numbers and all, before the next idea arrives. The reader is never asked to hold a new law in the head through pages of theory and meet its first use at the chapter's end: use follows statement immediately, so the example teaches the method while the idea is fresh.
2. **Margin boxes and Points to Ponder** — Down the page run blue-box margin notes — a caution, a shortcut, a historical aside — and at the section's end a "Points to Ponder" box asks the questions the text did not answer outright: what if this quantity doubled, where does this law stop working, why did the old idea (Aristotle's fallacy, say) feel right for so long. These boxes are not decoration: they are where the chapter tests whether the reader understood, rather than merely followed.
3. **Exercises, then additional exercises** — Every chapter closes in two rings. The exercises practise exactly what the chapter taught, in the order it taught it. The additional exercises reach further — combined ideas, unfamiliar settings, estimation and reasoning — for the reader who finished the first ring and wants the harder climb. Working both rings, honestly and without skipping to answers, is the NCERT way of finishing a chapter.
