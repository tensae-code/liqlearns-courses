# Velocity and acceleration in harmonic oscillation — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the velocity zero at the extremes even though the acceleration is greatest there?
**Answer: At an extreme the oscillator must turn round, so its velocity passes through zero at that instant; the displacement — and so the restoring acceleration a = −ω²x — is at its greatest there, which is exactly what turns it round.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) An oscillator has A = 4.0 cm, ω = 10 rad/s. Find its speed at x = 0 and at x = 2.0 cm.
**Answer: At the centre, v = ωA = 10 × 0.040 = 0.40 m/s. At x = 2.0 cm, v = ω√(A² − x²) = 10 × √(0.040² − 0.020²) = 10 × 0.0346 ≈ 0.35 m/s.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A student claims a motion with a = −ω²x but with acceleration pointing away from the centre. What is wrong?
**Answer: The minus sign in a = −ω²x already fixes the direction: when x is positive (one side of the centre), a is negative (toward the centre); the acceleration cannot point away — a motion pulled away from its centre is not SHM.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How do the x, v and a graphs of one oscillator differ, and what stays the same across all three?
**Answer: All three are sine curves of the same period; v is shifted a quarter-cycle ahead of x, a is half a cycle ahead (antiphase with x). The period — and so ω — is the shared quantity; only the phase and the amplitude scale differ.**
