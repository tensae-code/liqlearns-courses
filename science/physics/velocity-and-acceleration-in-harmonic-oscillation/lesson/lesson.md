# Velocity and acceleration in harmonic oscillation

## Do it first
Before you read: watch a child on a swing (or a pendulum) for one full cycle and point at where it moves fastest and where it pauses. Fastest through the middle, paused at the tops — you already know the velocity's story. This lesson writes that story as equations, and adds the invisible one: where the pull back is strongest.

## Sub lesson 1 — Velocity — fastest at the centre, still at the extremes

Vietnam's Grade 11 book gives the kinematics of oscillation its own lesson (Bài 3), straight after the describing lesson: the motion is not fully known until its velocity and acceleration are known too, instant by instant.

## Sub lesson 2 — Acceleration — a = −ω²x, the signature of SHM

The size of the acceleration peaks at the extremes: **a_max = ω²A**, where the displacement is greatest and the velocity is zero. The oscillator's story at the extremes and the centre is exactly swapped between v and a: extremes — still but pulled hardest; centre — fastest but unpulled (a = 0 where x = 0).

## Sub lesson 3 — The three curves together

One more quantity the curves hand over: the **speed at any displacement**, v = ±ω√(A² − x²) — at x = 0 it returns ωA; at x = A, zero. Between the energy lesson (Bài 5) and this one, the oscillator is known completely: position, speed and pull at every point of its path.

## The key ideas

- Velocity in SHM: v = −ωA sin(ωt + φ) — maximum ωA at the centre, zero at the extremes, a quarter-cycle ahead of the displacement in phase
- Acceleration in SHM: a = −ω²x — proportional to the displacement and always opposite to it, directed back to the centre; maximum ω²A at the extremes
- A = −ω²x is the defining condition of simple harmonic motion, for any oscillator
- The speed at displacement x is v = ±ω√(A² − x²)

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: In harmonic oscillation the velocity and acceleration are oscillations too: v = −ωA sin(ωt + φ) — greatest at the centre, zero at the extremes — and a = −ω²x — always directed back toward the centre, greatest at the extremes, and the very signature of simple harmonic motion: acceleration proportional to displacement, and opposite to it. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. An oscillator has A = 0.10 m and ω = 5.0 rad/s. Find v_max and a_max.
2. At a displacement of +0.06 m an oscillator's acceleration is −3.0 m/s². Find ω² and ω, and state the acceleration when x = −0.04 m.
3. Sketch, for one period, x, v and a on the same axes for x = A cos(ωt). Mark which curve leads which, and by how much of a cycle.
