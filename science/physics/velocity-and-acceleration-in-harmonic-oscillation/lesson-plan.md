# Lesson plan — Velocity and acceleration in harmonic oscillation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Velocity and acceleration in harmonic oscillation
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), directly after Describing harmonic oscillation: what the describing equation implies for the oscillator's velocity and acceleration
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in harmonic oscillation the velocity and acceleration are oscillations too: v = −ωA sin(ωt + φ) — greatest at the centre, zero at the extremes — and a = −ω²x — always directed back toward the centre, greatest at the extremes, and the very signature of simple harmonic motion: acceleration proportional to displacement, and opposite to it

## Sub lessons
1. **Velocity — fastest at the centre, still at the extremes** — Follow a swinging mass through one cycle: it **stops** for an instant at each extreme (it must, to turn round), then dives through the centre at its **greatest speed**. The equation says the same: with x = A cos(ωt + φ), the velocity is **v = −ωA sin(ωt + φ)** — its maximum size is **v_max = ωA**, reached as cos is zero, at the centre; it falls to zero exactly where x = ±A. Velocity is a quarter-cycle **ahead** of displacement in phase: the oscillator's speed peaks a quarter period before its displacement does.
2. **Acceleration — a = −ω²x, the signature of SHM** — Differentiate again and the acceleration is **a = −ω²A cos(ωt + φ) = −ω²x**. Two facts live in that one line. First, the **minus sign**: the acceleration always points **opposite the displacement** — back toward the centre — which is the restoring character of every harmonic oscillator (the spring pulls back, gravity swings the pendulum back). Second, the **proportionality**: acceleration grows in direct proportion to displacement, with constant of proportionality ω². That pair — proportional to x, opposite to x — is the **definition** of simple harmonic motion: any motion obeying a = −ω²x is SHM, whatever is doing the moving.
3. **The three curves together** — Plot x, v and a against time and the family portrait is three sine curves of the same period, shifted: **v a quarter-cycle ahead of x, a half a cycle ahead of x** (a is in antiphase with x — the graph of a is the graph of x flipped). From the curves every qualitative question answers itself: slowing down while moving out (v and a opposed in effect), speeding up while falling back (a driving the return). Vietnam's lesson works these graphs as the lesson's main tool — the oscillator read three ways at once.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write v and a for an oscillator given x = A cos(ωt + φ), and state v_max and a_max.
2. Explain why the velocity is greatest at the centre while the acceleration is greatest at the extremes.
3. Recognise SHM from the condition a = −ω²x and find ω from it.
4. Use v = ±ω√(A² − x²) to find a speed at a given displacement.

## What you need
A pendulum or a mass on a spring and a phone with a slow-motion camera for the hands-on; paper for sketching the three curves.
