# Force on a moving charge in a magnetic field

## Do it first
Roll a ball straight across the floor, then roll it again while a partner gently fans air across its path. The sideways drift is the picture of qvB's direction — always across, never along.

[Picture 21918 — charge's path bending sideways as it enters a field region]

## Force on a moving charge in a magnetic field — the teaching
A charge moving through a magnetic field feels a force — but only when it moves across the field. In the clean case, velocity perpendicular to a uniform field, the force is F = qvB: proportional to the charge, its velocity and the field strength. Its direction is perpendicular to both the velocity and the field, so the charge's path bends sideways. Two zero cases matter: a charge at rest feels nothing, and a charge moving along the field feels nothing — crossing is everything.

**Keep the formula:** F = qvB

## The key ideas

- F = qvB when velocity is perpendicular to the field.
- The force is perpendicular to both velocity and field — paths bend sideways.
- No force on a charge at rest, or moving along the field.
