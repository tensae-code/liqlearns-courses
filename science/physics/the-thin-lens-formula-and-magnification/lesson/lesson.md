# The thin lens formula and magnification

## Do it first
Before you read: hold a magnifying glass (a convex lens) above this page and slowly lift it. At first the letters grow, upright. Lift further and suddenly the letters flip upside down and blur, then sharpen again, inverted. Nothing about the lens changed — only the distances did. This lesson is the two formulas that predict exactly where the flip happens, where the image sharpens, and how big it will be.

## Sub lesson 1 — The thin lens formula — one equation for every lens

For a **thin lens**, the object distance **u**, the image distance **v** and the focal length **f** always obey **1/f = 1/u + 1/v**. The formula holds for convex and concave lenses alike, provided the signs are kept by the **real-is-positive convention** (the convention used in this course): distances to **real** things are positive — a real object, a real image (formed where rays actually meet, catchable on a screen) and the focal point of a **convex** lens. A **virtual image** (where rays only appear to meet, on the same side as the object) takes a **negative v**, and a **concave** lens takes a **negative f**. From the formula: an object at infinity gives v = f (the image sits at the focus); an object at 2f gives v = 2f; an object inside f on a convex lens gives a negative v — the virtual, upright magnifier image of the Do It First.

## Sub lesson 2 — Magnification — how much bigger, and which way up

The **linear magnification** is **m = v/u** — and the same number is the ratio of heights, **m = image height ÷ object height**. m greater than 1: the image is enlarged. m less than 1: diminished. The formula's sign keeps the orientation: a real image from a single lens (positive v) is **inverted**; the virtual image of a magnifier (negative v, so negative m in the signed account) is **upright**. Read the two formulas together: as an object moves toward a convex lens, v grows — and m = v/u grows twice over, because v rises while u falls. That is the flip-and-grow you watched in the Do It First: past the focal point the image is real, inverted and, until u reaches 2f, enlarged.

## Sub lesson 3 — Working it — camera, projector and magnifier

Three worked cases carry the method. **Projector:** a convex lens of f = 10 cm, object (the slide) at u = 15 cm: 1/v = 1/10 − 1/15 = 1/30, so v = 30 cm, and m = 30/15 = 2 — a real, inverted, twice-size image, which is why projector slides go in upside down. **Camera:** f = 5 cm, object at u = 200 cm: 1/v = 1/5 − 1/200 = 39/200, so v ≈ 5.1 cm — the image sits just beyond the focus, and m ≈ 5.1/200 ≈ 0.026: a whole person shrunk to fit the sensor. **Concave lens:** f = −20 cm, object at u = 30 cm: 1/v = −1/20 − 1/30 = −1/12, so v = −12 cm — negative, so virtual, upright, on the object's side, with m = −12/30 in sign, 0.4 in size — always diminished, which is why a concave lens alone never projects. Method every time: write the signs first, use 1/f = 1/u + 1/v for the place, then m = v/u for the size.

## The key ideas

- The thin lens formula: 1/f = 1/u + 1/v, for convex and concave lenses alike
- Real-is-positive convention: real object, real image and a convex lens's f are positive; a virtual image takes negative v and a concave lens takes negative f
- Magnification m = v/u = image height ÷ object height: above 1 enlarged, below 1 diminished; a single lens's real image is inverted, its virtual image upright
- Object at infinity → image at f; object at 2f → image at 2f, same size; object inside f (convex) → virtual, upright, enlarged — the magnifier

## Do It Yourself
1. A convex lens has focal length 12 cm. An object is placed 20 cm from it. Find the image distance and the magnification, and describe the image (real or virtual, upright or inverted, enlarged or diminished).
2. A camera lens of focal length 4 cm photographs a building 50 m (5,000 cm) away. Find the image distance from the lens, and the magnification. Why do camera lenses need to move only a tiny distance to focus from a mountain to a portrait at 1 m?
3. A concave lens has focal length 15 cm (mind the sign). An object stands 10 cm from it. Find the image distance and magnification, and state why this lens can never form an image on a screen.
