## Sub lessons
1. **The thin lens formula — one equation for every lens** — For a **thin lens**, the object distance **u**, the image distance **v** and the focal length **f** always obey **1/f = 1/u + 1/v**. The formula holds for convex and concave lenses alike, provided the signs are kept by the **real-is-positive convention** (the convention used in this course): distances to **real** things are positive — a real object, a real image (formed where rays actually meet, catchable on a screen) and the focal point of a **convex** lens. A **virtual image** (where rays only appear to meet, on the same side as the object) takes a **negative v**, and a **concave** lens takes a **negative f**. From the formula: an object at infinity gives v = f (the image sits at the focus); an object at 2f gives v = 2f; an object inside f on a convex lens gives a negative v — the virtual, upright magnifier image of the Do It First.
2. **Magnification — how much bigger, and which way up** — The **linear magnification** is **m = v/u** — and the same number is the ratio of heights, **m = image height ÷ object height**. m greater than 1: the image is enlarged. m less than 1: diminished. The formula's sign keeps the orientation: a real image from a single lens (positive v) is **inverted**; the virtual image of a magnifier (negative v, so negative m in the signed account) is **upright**. Read the two formulas together: as an object moves toward a convex lens, v grows — and m = v/u grows twice over, because v rises while u falls. That is the flip-and-grow you watched in the Do It First: past the focal point the image is real, inverted and, until u reaches 2f, enlarged.
3. **Working it — camera, projector and magnifier** — Three worked cases carry the method. **Projector:** a convex lens of f = 10 cm, object (the slide) at u = 15 cm: 1/v = 1/10 − 1/15 = 1/30, so v = 30 cm, and m = 30/15 = 2 — a real, inverted, twice-size image, which is why projector slides go in upside down. **Camera:** f = 5 cm, object at u = 200 cm: 1/v = 1/5 − 1/200 = 39/200, so v ≈ 5.1 cm — the image sits just beyond the focus, and m ≈ 5.1/200 ≈ 0.026: a whole person shrunk to fit the sensor. **Concave lens:** f = −20 cm, object at u = 30 cm: 1/v = −1/20 − 1/30 = −1/12, so v = −12 cm — negative, so virtual, upright, on the object's side, with m = −12/30 in sign, 0.4 in size — always diminished, which is why a concave lens alone never projects. Method every time: write the signs first, use 1/f = 1/u + 1/v for the place, then m = v/u for the size.
