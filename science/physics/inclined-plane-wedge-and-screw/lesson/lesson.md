# Inclined plane, wedge and screw

## Do it first
Roll a stone up a plank instead of lifting it straight. Then tap a wedge-shaped piece of wood into a log's crack. Then turn a screw slowly and watch it advance — three forms of one slope.

[Picture 21844 — ramp, wedge splitting wood, and a screw unwrapped into its ramp spiral]

## Sub lesson 1 — Inclined plane
The inclined plane is a ramp: you trade a longer, gentler climb for a smaller effort. Its VR = slope length ÷ height — a ramp three times as long as it is high has VR 3.

**Keep the formulas:** Plane VR = length / height; Wedge VR = penetration / thickness; Screw MA = πd / p

## Sub lesson 2 — Wedge
The wedge is a moving inclined plane — knife, axe, nail, spear — that splits or separates. Its VR = penetration length ÷ thickness: a long thin wedge drives deep while opening a narrow split with multiplied force.

## Sub lesson 3 — Screw
The screw is an inclined plane wrapped into a helix around a cylinder. Turning it once advances it by one pitch, so MA = circumference ÷ pitch (πd/p). Nuts and bolts are screws working in pairs.

## The key ideas

- Inclined plane VR = slope length ÷ height.
- Wedge VR = penetration length ÷ thickness.
- Screw MA = circumference ÷ pitch = πd/p.

## Say it to your companion
Explain inclined plane, wedge and screw to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
