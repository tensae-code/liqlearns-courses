# The particle model — a body as a point — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A car's engine drives it forward, friction and air drag it back, and its weight pulls it down. Describe its particle diagram.
**Answer: One dot labelled with the car's mass; three arrows from the dot — driving force forward, friction/drag backward, weight mg downward (with the road's normal force upward balancing it on a level road).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a 10 m bus honestly become a point on a 120 km journey, but not when the question is whether it will tip on a bend?
**Answer: On the journey the bus only translates and the distance is thousands of times its length, so its extent changes nothing; tipping is decided by the body's width, height and where its weight acts — exactly what the point throws away.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A door opens easily when pushed at its edge and hardly moves when pushed near the hinge. What does this show about the particle model?
**Answer: Where a force acts changes the motion, and a point has no "where" — turning problems need the body's extent (and moments), so the particle model breaks.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Libya's Grade 12 book runs connected particles, strings and pulleys as systems of points. What is each point in such a system standing for?
**Answer: A whole body — a mass on a string, a load on a trolley — replaced by its mass at a point, so that F = ma can be written for each body and the string's tension links the equations.**
