# Measuring the speed of sound in air — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) A learner stands 170 m from a wall and times 50 clap-echo intervals at 50 s. Find the speed of sound.
**Answer: Each interval is 50 ÷ 50 = 1.0 s for the return trip of 2 × 170 = 340 m, so v = 340 ÷ 1.0 = 340 m/s.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the baseline be long in the direct method?
**Answer: The travel time must be large compared with reaction time; over hundreds of metres the sound takes seconds, so a tenth-of-a-second timing error is a small percentage.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A result comes out at 300 m/s. Name two checks before believing it.
**Answer: Check the distance measurement (the echo distance must be doubled) and whether the rhythm truly matched the echoes (timing drift), then repeat and average.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Does the speed of sound in air depend on the sound's loudness or pitch? What does it depend on?
**Answer: Practically no — in the same air all sounds travel together; the speed depends on the air itself, chiefly its temperature, rising about 0.6 m/s per degree Celsius.**
