# Renewable energy — evaluating energy resources — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) In one sentence each, say why sunlight is renewable and natural gas is not.
**Answer: Sunlight arrives as a flow whether we use it or not, refilled continuously by the sun; natural gas is a stock formed over millions of years, and every amount burned is gone for good on any human timescale.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A turbine rated at 2 MW averages 0.7 MW. Find its capacity factor and say what it means.
**Answer: 0.7 ÷ 2 = 0.35 = 35% — over the year the turbine delivers, on average, 35% of what it would deliver running at full rating all the time, because the wind varies.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the four tests for judging an energy resource.
**Answer: (1) Energy returned for energy invested over the plant's life; (2) steadiness/reliability, summed up by the capacity factor; (3) what it takes — land, materials, water; (4) what it leaves — emissions and waste.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must a good evaluation name the strongest point against its own claim?
**Answer: Because every resource fails some test somewhere — naming the strongest counter-point shows the weighing was honest, and it tells the reader exactly where the claim is weakest instead of hiding the trade-off.**
