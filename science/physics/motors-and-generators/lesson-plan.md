# Lesson plan — Motors and generators

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Motors and generators
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 12 · Unit 4 — Electromagnetism.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** applications — One family from one principle: magnetic relays switch big circuits with small ones, the electric bell's electromagnet pulls its hammer repeatedly, the DC motor turns…
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 12 Physics lessons before it in Unit 4 — Electromagnetism — and on the learner's own world: the examples are the learner's home, street, school and body.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Relays and the electric bell** — One family from one principle: magnetic relays switch big circuits with small ones.
2. **The DC motor** — The DC motor turns current-in-a-field into rotation.
3. **The AC generator** — And the AC generator turns rotation back into alternating current under Faraday's law.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

Paper and a pencil; a bar magnet, iron filings, wire, a cell and a coil for the activities where the class has them.
