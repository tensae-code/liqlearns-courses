# Motion on an inclined plane — the Lao Part III way — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the normal reaction on an incline smaller than the weight?
**Answer: Only the into-plane component mg cos θ presses the body into the surface; the reaction balances that component, and cos θ < 1, so N = mg cos θ < mg.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A 2 kg and a 10 kg block are released together on the same smooth slope. Which accelerates faster, and why?
**Answer: Neither — on a smooth slope a = g sin θ for both; the driving force and the mass grow together in F = ma, so the mass cancels.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State the routine the Lao Part III way uses for an incline problem.
**Answer: Draw the plane and its angle; split the weight into mg sin θ and mg cos θ; write N = mg cos θ; write the along-slope equation (adding friction if rough); solve for the acceleration, then use the motion equations for speeds, times and distances.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does friction change the condition for a body to start sliding?
**Answer: Friction can hold the body while mg sin θ ≤ μ_s N = μ_s mg cos θ, i.e. while tan θ ≤ μ_s; sliding starts only when the slope is steep enough that tan θ > μ_s.**
