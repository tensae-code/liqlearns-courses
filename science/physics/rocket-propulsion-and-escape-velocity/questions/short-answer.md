# Rocket propulsion and escape velocity — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a rocket not need air to push against?
**Answer: It pushes on its own exhaust: the backward momentum given to the gas is matched by forward momentum gained by the rocket, by conservation of momentum.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What does escape velocity mean, exactly?
**Answer: The launch speed at which a body's kinetic energy is just enough to climb completely out of the planet's gravity and not return — about 11.2 km/s from Earth's surface.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a satellite not fall straight down?
**Answer: Launched sideways fast enough, it falls toward Earth along a curve that matches the Earth's own curving away beneath it, so it keeps falling around the planet in orbit.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Would escape velocity be larger or smaller on a smaller, lighter planet? Why?
**Answer: Smaller — a lighter, smaller planet's gravity well is shallower, so less kinetic energy (less speed) is needed to climb out of it.**
