# The teacher-book pattern — chapter analysis, teaching suggestions and worked answers (China PEP)

## Do it first
Before you read: a student book tells its readers what to learn; a teacher's book tells its reader how the learning is meant to happen — and where it usually breaks. China's PEP guides follow one strict pattern for both jobs. This lesson lays the pattern out, from a real guide, and says plainly where the held copy stops.

## Sub lesson 1 — The chapter overview — the map before the march

Each chapter of the guide opens with a **chapter overview (本章教材概述)**: what the chapter is for, where it sits in the course's long argument, which ideas are its load-bearing walls and which are scenery, and where students are known to stumble. Read before teaching, it converts the student book from a sequence of pages into a campaign with objectives. In the held part of the Compulsory Book 2 guide, the overviews for the projectile-motion chapter (Ch.5, guide pp. 1–27) and the circular-motion chapter (Ch.6, pp. 28–60) show the form at work: goals stated, misconceptions named, time budgeted across sections.

## Sub lesson 2 — Section by section — analysis with teaching suggestions

The guide's core is its per-section **textbook analysis and teaching suggestions (教材分析与教学建议)**: for every section of the student book, what the section's experiment is really for, which demonstration to lead with, which question to open with, where the mathematics should be earned and where merely used, and how to handle the section's side columns. The Ch.6 treatment of centripetal force (guide printed p. 35) is the held part's clear specimen: the guide routes the teacher into the law through the conical-pendulum demonstration — the same experiment its reference materials then extend — so suggestion and apparatus speak with one voice.

## Sub lesson 3 — Worked answers and reference materials — the teacher's back room

Two closing layers complete the pattern. **Reference answers and hints** work the student book's “Review and Improve” problem sets, so the teacher marks from the book's own intentions, not from improvisation. And **reference materials (参考资料)** stock the bench with extensions beyond the student text: for Ch.6, build-it-yourself verification experiments — an electric conical pendulum for verifying the centripetal-force formula, and arch-bridge and concave-bridge pressure experiments with a spring balance fitted with a “memory pointer”. The pattern's honesty note rides with this lesson: the secured guide scan is **truncated at printed p. 60 of about 131** (Ch.5–6 guidance only) — the pattern is taught from what is held, and the missing Ch.7–8 guidance is named, never invented.

## The key ideas

- PEP teacher's teaching books follow one pattern: chapter overview, per-section analysis with teaching suggestions, reference answers, reference materials
- The overview (本章教材概述) names the chapter's goals, load-bearing ideas and known student stumbles before teaching begins
- Teaching suggestions (教材分析与教学建议) route each section — demonstration, opening question, where maths is earned vs used
- Reference materials extend the teacher's bench (e.g. the electric conical pendulum verifying the centripetal-force formula); the held Compulsory Book 2 guide is truncated at printed p. 60 of ~131 — Ch.7–8 guidance missing, named as library debt

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: for every PEP senior student volume, China publishes a **teacher's teaching book (教师教学用书)** built to one pattern: a chapter overview that analyses the chapter's place and pitfalls, section-by-section teaching suggestions that route the lesson, reference answers to the student book's own “Review and Improve” sets, and reference materials that extend the teacher's bench — this lesson teaches that pattern, from the Compulsory Book 2 guide, on the evidence of its held (truncated) part. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.
