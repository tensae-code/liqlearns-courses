# History of physics — the Central Asian scholars

## Do it first
Before you read: name the people you think invented physics. Most lists stop at Greece and Italy. Keep your list — by the end of this lesson you will be able to add four names from Central Asia and say what each one actually did.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Greek beginning — and the road east

Physics begins as the study of nature: Aristotle sorted motion and the heavens into a system, and Archimedes turned it into measurement — levers, floating bodies, the value of π. When Greek learning travelled east through translation, it did not stop: in the cities of Central Asia — Khwarizm, Bukhara, Samarkand — scholars read it, tested it, corrected it and pushed it further. The Grade 6 book tells the founding story with both halves present: the Greek founders and the Central Asian scholars standing beside them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Al-Khwarizmi and Beruni — measurement as a way of knowing

Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (c. 780–850), from Khwarizm, gave algebra its name and its method — a way of solving by rules that physics still uses every time it rearranges an equation. Abu Rayhan Beruni (973–1048) measured the Earth's radius by watching a mountain's horizon dip, weighed minerals and metals with unmatched care, and wrote physics as measurement: a claim is worth what you can measure. His exchange of letters with Ibn Sina argued real physics — whether the heavens could be otherwise, how heat and light behave.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ibn Sina and Omar Khayyam — motion, medicine and the calendar

Ibn Sina (Avicenna, 980–1037), from Bukhara, argued that a body thrown through empty space would keep moving unless stopped — an early statement of the idea Newton later made his first law — and his Canon carried physics into medicine. Omar Khayyam (1048–1131) weighed bodies in air and water to expose alloyed gold, and led the calendar reform that produced one of the most accurate calendars ever made. Galileo and Newton stand at the end of this road, not at its start: the lesson's point is the road itself — physics is a relay, and Central Asia ran some of its longest legs.

## The key ideas

- Physics has two founding homes in this course's story: the Greek world of Aristotle and Archimedes, and Central Asia
- Al-Khwarizmi founded algebra's method; Beruni measured the Earth and made measurement the test of a claim
- Ibn Sina argued a thrown body keeps moving unless stopped — an early form of the inertia idea; Khayyam weighed alloys and reformed the calendar
- Galileo and Newton complete the relay — they do not erase the runners before them

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: physics did not begin in one country: beside Aristotle, Archimedes, Galileo and Newton stand the Central Asian scholars — al-Khwarizmi, Beruni, Ibn Sina (Avicenna) and Omar Khayyam — whose measurements, instruments and arguments carried the science forward for centuries.
