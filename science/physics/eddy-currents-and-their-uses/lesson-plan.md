# Lesson plan — Eddy currents and their uses

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Eddy currents and their uses

## Sub lessons
1. **Eddy currents — loops swirling in solid metal** — Induce a changing flux through a coil and the induced current runs neatly round the wire. Induce the same changing flux through a solid plate or core and the metal offers the flux countless conducting paths at once: the induced currents circulate in closed swirls throughout the bulk of the metal, like eddies in a river — eddy currents. By Lenz's law their fields oppose the change that creates them.
2. **The waste — heating in cores, and lamination** — Wherever iron carries alternating flux — transformer and motor cores above all — eddy currents swirl inside the iron itself. They do no useful work: they heat the core, waste energy and can cook insulation. Two defences are standard: build the core from thin insulated laminations stacked so the sheets slice the eddy loops into narrow strips (raising the resistance of every loop and shrinking the currents), and use high-resistivity core iron, alloyed with silicon, so what current remains is small.
3. **The uses — brakes, damping, heating and finding metal** — Turn the same physics loose where braking or heating is wanted. Electromagnetic brakes: a metal disc on a train or fairground ride axle passes between electromagnets; the eddies induced oppose the rotation and brake it smoothly, with no contact and no wear — the stronger the field or the faster the disc, the harder the braking. Damping: the moving coil of a galvanometer is wound on an aluminium frame precisely so eddies in the frame brake its swings and let the pointer settle dead-beat instead of oscillating.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Eddy currents are induced currents circulating in closed swirls inside a bulk conductor wherever the flux through it changes; their fields oppose the change (Lenz).
2. Say, in their own words: They convert motion or electrical energy to heat in the metal — the falling magnet's slow drift and the swinging plate's dead stop are eddy braking.
3. Say, in their own words: In cores they are pure loss, fought by laminating the iron into insulated sheets and by high-resistivity or ferrite cores; the loss grows with frequency squared.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Pictures eddies flowing along the core like current in a wire → they circulate in closed loops inside the bulk metal, each loop around the changing flux; slicing the metal across the loops is what kills them.
- Thinks eddy braking breaks Lenz's law by 'creating' a force → the braking force is paid for in kinetic energy, which reappears as heat in the metal; the account balances exactly.
