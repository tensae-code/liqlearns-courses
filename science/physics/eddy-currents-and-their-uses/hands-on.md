# Hands-on — Eddy currents and their uses

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Brake a swinging plate
Hang a solid copper or aluminium plate as a pendulum and swing it between the poles of a strong magnet: it stops within a swing or two. Repeat with a slotted (comb-cut) plate of the same metal and watch it swing far longer. Say aloud the rule: the slots break the eddy loops into weak scraps — the same trick lamination plays inside a core.

## The falling magnet
Drop a small strong magnet and an identical-sized dummy of unmagnetised steel down a length of copper or aluminium tube. The dummy rattles straight down; the magnet floats down slowly. Say aloud the rule: the falling magnet induces eddies in the tube wall whose field opposes its fall — kinetic energy leaves as heat in the tube.
