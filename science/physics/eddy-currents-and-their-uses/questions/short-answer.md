# Eddy currents and their uses — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain why a solid copper plate swung between magnet poles stops so much faster than in free air.
**Answer: As the plate sweeps through the field, the flux through parts of it changes, inducing eddy loops whose fields oppose the motion (Lenz). The plate's kinetic energy is transferred to electrical energy in the loops and dissipated as heat in the copper, so the swing is braked hard — eddy braking.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Two identical transformers run at the same voltage, one at 50 Hz and one at 500 Hz, both with solid cores. Compare their eddy losses qualitatively and name the fix applied to the second.
**Answer: Eddy loss grows with the square of frequency, so the 500 Hz unit's eddy loss is about (500/50)² = 100 times larger per unit flux — unusable as a solid core. The fix: laminate finely or, at such frequencies, use a ferrite core whose resistivity is so high that eddy loops barely form.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the moving system of a sensitive galvanometer wound on an aluminium frame, when aluminium is not part of the electrical circuit?
**Answer: As the coil swings, the aluminium frame moves in the magnet's field and eddies are induced in it; their fields oppose the swing, providing electromagnetic damping. The pointer therefore settles quickly to its reading (dead-beat) instead of oscillating — the frame is a brake, not a conductor in the measuring circuit.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A metal detector's coil is passed over soil containing a coin. Outline how the coin announces itself.
**Answer: The coil's alternating field induces eddy currents in the coin; those eddies make their own field, which reacts back on the search coil and changes its current/inductance. The instrument detects that change and signals — the coin is found by the eddies it hosts, without being touched.**
