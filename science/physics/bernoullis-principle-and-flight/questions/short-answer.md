# Bernoulli's principle and flight — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A strip of paper held under your lip rises when you blow across its top. Explain why, naming both sides of the strip.
**Answer: The blown air above the strip moves fast, so by Bernoulli's principle its pressure is low; the air below the strip is still and keeps its full pressure. The stronger push from below lifts the strip into the fast air.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Air of density 1.2 kg/m³ moves at 55 m/s over a wing's top and 45 m/s under it. Find the pressure difference and the lift on a wing of 20 m².
**Answer: ΔP = ½ × 1.2 × (55² − 45²) = 0.6 × (3,025 − 2,025) = 0.6 × 1,000 = 600 Pa. Lift = 600 × 20 = 12,000 N.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Hurricane wind of 30 m/s races over a roof of 60 m² with still air inside. Find the upward force on the roof (air density 1.2 kg/m³).
**Answer: ΔP = ½ × 1.2 × 30² = 0.6 × 900 = 540 Pa. Force = 540 × 60 = 32,400 N upward — the weight of about 3,300 kg pushing the roof off from inside.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain the atomiser: why does liquid climb the tube when air is blown across its top?
**Answer: The blown air moves fast across the tube's mouth, so the pressure there falls. The air pressing on the liquid's surface in the bottle keeps its full pressure and pushes the liquid up the tube toward the low pressure, where it leaves as spray.**
