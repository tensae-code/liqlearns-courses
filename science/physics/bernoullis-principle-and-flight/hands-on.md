# Hands-on — Bernoulli's principle and flight

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The rising paper
Hold one end of a strip of paper just under your lower lip so the strip hangs down in a curve. Blow steadily *across the top* of the strip. The free end rises toward the moving air. Blow harder — it rises more. Say aloud: the fast air above the strip has the lower pressure, so the still air below pushes the strip up.

## Balls that come together
Hang two light balls (or two balloons) side by side with a hand's width of gap, and blow a steady stream of air *between* them. They swing together, not apart — the exact opposite of the first guess. Say aloud: between the balls the air is fast and its pressure low; the still air outside pushes them in.

## The straw atomiser
Stand a straw upright in a glass of water and blow hard across its top with a second straw (or pursed lips) — water climbs the standing straw and leaves its top as a fine spray. Say aloud: the fast air over the straw's mouth drops the pressure there, and the air pressing on the glass pushes the water up to meet it.
