# Simple machines at home

## Do it first
Open three kitchen drawers in your mind and hunt: find a wedge, a lever and a wheel. Name the load each one moves.

[Picture 21837 — kitchen scene with machines ringed — knife, scissors, bottle opener, broom]

## Simple machines at home — the teaching
Your home is full of simple machines hiding in plain sight. A knife is a wedge, scissors are two levers sharing a pivot — and a wedge each — a bottle opener is a lever, and a broom is a lever you swing. Naming the machine inside each tool tells you how it helps: where the effort goes in, where the load is, and which of the three purposes it serves.

## The key ideas

- A knife is a wedge; scissors combine levers and wedges.
- A bottle opener is a lever.
- A broom is a lever used as a speed multiplier.

## Say it to your companion
Explain simple machines at home to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw your kitchen and ring every simple machine, labelling its type.
2. Write the key ideas in your own words — one line each — without looking back. Then check them against the lesson and fix any line that drifted.
