# Processing measurement results — errors, graphs and reporting — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does repeating a measurement not protect against systematic error?
**Answer: Every repeat is pushed the same way by the same faulty instrument or method, so the mean of the repeats is pushed too; only checking, zeroing or calibrating the instrument, or changing the method, finds and removes it.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What is the gradient of a distance–time graph, and how is it read from the line of best fit?
**Answer: It is the speed. Read two far-apart points on the line, take the change in distance divided by the change in time (rise over run), with its unit, m/s.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A report gives a length as '24 cm' with no other information. Why is that only half a result?
**Answer: It carries no uncertainty — no spread, no instrument limit — so the reader cannot tell how far to trust the number or compare it fairly with another result.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name one thing done against random error and one against systematic error in a careful investigation.
**Answer: Against random error: repeat readings and take the mean. Against systematic error: zero or calibrate the instrument (or check the method against a known standard).**
