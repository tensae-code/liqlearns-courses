# How a refrigerator works — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the expansion valve's throat narrow, and what happens there?
**Answer: The narrow throat holds back the high-pressure liquid so that beyond it the pressure drops suddenly; the refrigerant expands and cools sharply — cold enough to absorb heat inside the cabinet.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain the open-fridge paradox: an open fridge in a sealed room warms the room.
**Answer: All the heat the loop removes from the cabinet air is shed back into the room at the condenser, together with the compressor's electrical work turned to heat — so the room gains exactly the work energy, and warms.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Which two latent-heat changes carry the heat round the loop?
**Answer: Evaporation inside (absorbing latent heat from the food's surroundings) and condensation outside (releasing that latent heat, plus the work's heat, to the room).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What is the coefficient of performance, in words?
**Answer: The heat the machine moves divided by the work it is paid — the buyer's measure of how much uphill heat-carrying each unit of electricity buys.**
