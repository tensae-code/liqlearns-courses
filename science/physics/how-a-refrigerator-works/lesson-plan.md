# Lesson plan — How a refrigerator works

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** How a refrigerator works
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the thermodynamics strand — Jordan's Grade 11 enrichment on applied thermodynamics
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a refrigerator is a heat pump run backwards: a refrigerant is compressed until it is hot, sheds that heat outside the cabinet, then expands suddenly until it is colder than the food, and soaks up heat inside — moving heat from cold to hot, which nature never does unaided, at the price of the electrical work the compressor is paid

## Sub lessons
1. **The cycle — four stations round a loop** — The refrigerant travels a sealed loop through four stations: the compressor squeezes it into a hot, high-pressure gas; the condenser coils on the back shed its heat to the kitchen; the expansion valve lets it through a narrow throat, where it expands and turns cold; the evaporator coils inside the cabinet let it absorb the food's heat and boil into a gas — and the loop begins again.
2. **Why work must be paid — heat does not flow uphill alone** — Heat flows from hot to cold by itself; the refrigerator's whole job is the forbidden direction, cold cabinet to warm kitchen. The second law's price is the compressor's work: the electrical energy is not wasted — it is exactly what allows heat to be carried uphill, and it too is finally shed as heat at the back, which is why a fridge warms the room it stands in by more than the heat it removed from the food.
3. **Jordan's enrichment — reading a real machine** — Jordan's Grade 11 book chooses the refrigerator as its thermodynamics enrichment because every idea of the unit is visible in it: evaporation cooling inside (the refrigerant boils by soaking up heat), condensation heating outside, latent heat carried round the loop, and efficiency judged as heat moved per unit of work paid — the coefficient of performance a buyer reads on the label.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. The refrigerant loops through compressor, condenser, expansion valve, evaporator
2. Compression heats the gas; expansion through the narrow valve cools it sharply
3. Inside, the cold refrigerant evaporates by absorbing the food's heat; outside, it condenses and sheds heat
4. Moving heat from cold to hot requires paid work — the compressor's electricity
5. The fridge warms its room by the removed heat plus the compressor's work
