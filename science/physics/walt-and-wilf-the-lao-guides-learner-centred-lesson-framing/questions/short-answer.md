# WALT and WILF — the Lao guide's learner-centred lesson framing — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the aim written on the board rather than kept in the teacher's plan?
**Answer: So learners can steer by it: with the aim visible, learners know what the lesson is for and can judge their own progress against it as the lesson runs — the heart of the guide's learner-centred method.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How do WALT and WILF differ?
**Answer: WALT states the destination — the learning the lesson aims at. WILF states the arrival signs — the observable things that show the learning has landed, which learners use to check themselves.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two more methods the same guide section names.
**Answer: Mind-mapping and higher-order-thinking questions, with the teacher as facilitator; the guide's routes to knowledge also include observation, survey, experiment, discussion and demonstration.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State this lesson's evidence footing exactly.
**Answer: It is built from the Grade 11 teacher guide's general introduction (teaching-methods section, guide printed pp. 5–9) and is cited as teacher-guide evidence only: Laos is named in the countries line and in no acceptance count.**
