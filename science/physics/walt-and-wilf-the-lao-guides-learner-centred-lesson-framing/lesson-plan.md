# Lesson plan — WALT and WILF — the Lao guide's learner-centred lesson framing

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** WALT and WILF — the Lao guide's learner-centred lesson framing
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Laos placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Lao Grade 11 teacher guide frames every lesson learner-centredly: WALT — 'We are learning to…' — states the aim on the board, WILF — 'What I am looking for…' — states the success signs, and the teacher works as facilitator with mind maps and higher-order thinking (teacher-guide evidence)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **WALT — We are learning to…** — The guide's teaching-methods section (general introduction, printed pp. 5–9) builds every lesson on a board-written aim beginning WALT: 'We are learning to…' The aim is stated for the learners, in their hearing, at the start — not kept in the teacher's plan. A WALT line names the learning, not the activity: 'we are learning to explain how a lever balances', not 'we are doing page 40'. Because the learners can read the aim, they can judge their own progress against it as the lesson runs.
2. **WILF — What I am looking for…** — Beside WALT stands WILF: 'What I am looking for…' — the signs that the learning has landed, listed where learners can check themselves: the key word used rightly, the worked example completed and checked, the idea said in the learner's own words. WILF turns marking criteria into a learner's mirror. Together WALT and WILF frame the lesson from both ends — where we are going, and how we will know we arrived.
3. **The facilitator teacher — mind maps and higher-order thinking** — The same methods section recasts the teacher as facilitator: learners build mind maps of the lesson's ideas, discussion and demonstration carry the content, and questions aim at higher-order thinking rather than recall alone. The guide's stated routes to knowledge are observation, survey, experiment, discussion, demonstration and gathering data from other sources (stocked in this course's science-skills lesson 'Doing science honestly'). WALT/WILF is the board-level frame that holds those methods together, lesson by lesson, across the Lao series.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first and do the opening activity with real objects before reading any explanation; the explanation is the story of that activity.
- Mixes the sub lessons up → re-read the key ideas in order; each key idea is one sub lesson compressed to a line.
