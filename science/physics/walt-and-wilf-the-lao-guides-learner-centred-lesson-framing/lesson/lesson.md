# WALT and WILF — the Lao guide's learner-centred lesson framing

## Do it first
Before you read: think of a lesson where you only found out what it was really about at the very end — or never. Now imagine the aim had been written at the top of the board before anything else, with a short list of what success would look like. That is the whole WALT/WILF device. This lesson explains where it comes from and how the Lao guide uses it.

## Sub lesson 1 — WALT — We are learning to…

The guide's teaching-methods section (general introduction, printed pp. 5–9) builds every lesson on a board-written aim beginning WALT: 'We are learning to…' The aim is stated for the learners, in their hearing, at the start — not kept in the teacher's plan. A WALT line names the learning, not the activity: 'we are learning to explain how a lever balances', not 'we are doing page 40'. Because the learners can read the aim, they can judge their own progress against it as the lesson runs.

## Sub lesson 2 — WILF — What I am looking for…

Beside WALT stands WILF: 'What I am looking for…' — the signs that the learning has landed, listed where learners can check themselves: the key word used rightly, the worked example completed and checked, the idea said in the learner's own words. WILF turns marking criteria into a learner's mirror. Together WALT and WILF frame the lesson from both ends — where we are going, and how we will know we arrived.

## Sub lesson 3 — The facilitator teacher — mind maps and higher-order thinking

The same methods section recasts the teacher as facilitator: learners build mind maps of the lesson's ideas, discussion and demonstration carry the content, and questions aim at higher-order thinking rather than recall alone. The guide's stated routes to knowledge are observation, survey, experiment, discussion, demonstration and gathering data from other sources (stocked in this course's science-skills lesson 'Doing science honestly'). WALT/WILF is the board-level frame that holds those methods together, lesson by lesson, across the Lao series.

## The key ideas

- WALT = 'We are learning to…' — the lesson's aim, written on the board for learners
- WILF = 'What I am looking for…' — the success signs learners can check themselves against
- The teacher works as facilitator; mind maps and higher-order thinking are the guide's named methods
- This lesson stands on the Grade 11 teacher guide as teacher-guide evidence only — Laos named, counted nowhere
