# Electric current in different media — gases, vacuum and semiconductors — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a gas become an insulator again when the field is removed?
**Answer: The ions and electrons recombine into neutral molecules, so the free carriers disappear and there is nothing left to carry a current.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What job did the grid between cathode and anode do in a vacuum tube, and why did it matter?
**Answer: A tiny voltage on the grid controlled the much larger flow of electrons from cathode to anode, so the tube could amplify — the working principle of early radio.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a semiconductor's resistance fall as it warms, unlike a metal's?
**Answer: Warming frees more electrons from their bonds, creating more electrons and holes to carry charge; the extra carriers outweigh everything else, so conductivity rises and resistance falls.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What is a hole, exactly?
**Answer: The empty place left in a bond when an electron breaks free; it moves as neighbouring electrons shuffle into it, and it carries charge in the direction opposite to the electrons' motion.**
