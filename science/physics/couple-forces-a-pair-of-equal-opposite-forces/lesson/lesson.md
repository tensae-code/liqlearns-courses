# Couple forces — a pair of equal opposite forces

## Do it first
Before you read: hold a ruler flat on the table. Push one end forward with one finger — the ruler slides and spins. Now push one end forward and the other end backward with two fingers at once, equally hard: the ruler spins in place and its middle stays put. Two pushes cancelled as pushes — yet the turning survived. What exactly survived, and how would you measure it? Keep the question while you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a couple is

Take two forces of equal size, opposite direction, acting along parallel lines that do not meet — a hand pushing up on one side of a steering wheel while the other hand pulls down on the other side. Added as vectors they cancel exactly, so a couple cannot move the body's centre of mass at all. But their turning effects do not cancel — they add. That pure-turning pair is a **couple**. Laos' Grade 10 book closes its statics part with a full lesson on couple forces (Part IV, Lesson 18), after moments and equilibrium — the couple is what is left when forces balance but turning does not.

## Sub lesson 2 — The moment of a couple

The moment of a couple is M = F × d, where d is the perpendicular distance between the two lines of action. Unlike a single force's moment, it does not depend on the point you measure from — take moments about any point and the answer is the same Fd, because the two forces' moments always combine to that one value. That freedom is the couple's signature: a couple is a pure turning effect with a size and a direction (clockwise or anticlockwise) and no point attached. Units are newton metres, as for any moment, but a couple's moment is not work — nothing travels along a force here.

## Sub lesson 3 — Couples at work

Couples are how hands turn the world: the two hands on a steering wheel or a bicycle's handlebar, fingers on opposite sides of a tap, a screwdriver driven by fingers on both sides of its handle, a key turned in a lock. In the Lao book's statics part the couple completes the equilibrium story: a body is in full equilibrium only when the resultant force is zero AND the resultant moment — including any couple acting on it — is zero. A single force can never balance a couple; only another couple, equal and opposite, can.

## The key ideas

- A couple is two equal, opposite, parallel forces — resultant force zero, turning effect not zero
- Moment of a couple M = F × d (d = perpendicular distance between the lines), the same about every point
- Only an equal and opposite couple can balance a couple — no single force can
- Laos' Grade 10 book gives couples a full lesson closing its statics part (Part IV, Lesson 18)

Tell your companion: a couple — two equal, opposite, parallel forces — has no resultant force at all, yet it turns the body: its turning effect (moment) is simply force × the perpendicular distance between the two forces' lines, the same about every point. ## Do It Yourself
1. Two hands apply a couple of 15 N each to a steering wheel whose two force lines are 0.38 m apart. Find the moment of the couple.
2. A couple of moment 24 N·m is made by two forces 0.6 m apart. Find the size of each force.
3. Explain why no single force, however placed, can balance a couple acting on a body.
