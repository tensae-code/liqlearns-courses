## Sub lessons
1. **What a couple is** — Take two forces of equal size, opposite direction, acting along parallel lines that do not meet — a hand pushing up on one side of a steering wheel while the other hand pulls down on the other side. Added as vectors they cancel exactly, so a couple cannot move the body's centre of mass at all. But their turning effects do not cancel — they add. That pure-turning pair is a **couple**. Laos' Grade 10 book closes its statics part with a full lesson on couple forces (Part IV, Lesson 18), after moments and equilibrium — the couple is what is left when forces balance but turning does not.
2. **The moment of a couple** — The moment of a couple is M = F × d, where d is the perpendicular distance between the two lines of action. Unlike a single force's moment, it does not depend on the point you measure from — take moments about any point and the answer is the same Fd, because the two forces' moments always combine to that one value. That freedom is the couple's signature: a couple is a pure turning effect with a size and a direction (clockwise or anticlockwise) and no point attached. Units are newton metres, as for any moment, but a couple's moment is not work — nothing travels along a force here.
3. **Couples at work** — Couples are how hands turn the world: the two hands on a steering wheel or a bicycle's handlebar, fingers on opposite sides of a tap, a screwdriver driven by fingers on both sides of its handle, a key turned in a lock. In the Lao book's statics part the couple completes the equilibrium story: a body is in full equilibrium only when the resultant force is zero AND the resultant moment — including any couple acting on it — is zero. A single force can never balance a couple; only another couple, equal and opposite, can.
