# Multipliers and direction changers

## Do it first
Mime the three effects with your arms: a slow strong lift, a fast sweeping broom, a downward pull that raises a bucket. Your body knows the three trades.

[Picture 21840 — three panels — big load small movement; small load big movement; effort down, load up]

## Multipliers and direction changers — the teaching
Machines can also be sorted by their effect. A force multiplier: the load is bigger than the effort, but the load moves less far than the effort — you trade distance for force. A speed multiplier: the load moves farther and faster than the effort, and the load force is smaller — you trade force for speed. A direction changer: the load moves a different way from the effort, like a fixed pulley where you pull down and the load rises. No machine gives both trades at once — that is the unit's honest bargain.

## The key ideas

- Force multiplier: load > effort, but the load moves less.
- Speed multiplier: load moves farther/faster than the effort.
- Direction changer: load moves a different way to the effort.

## Say it to your companion
Explain multipliers and direction changers to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
