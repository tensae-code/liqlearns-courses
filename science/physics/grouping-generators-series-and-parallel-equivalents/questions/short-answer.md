# Grouping generators — series and parallel equivalents — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Three cells (1.5 V, 0.4 Ω) in series: state the equivalent generator.
**Answer: E_eq = 4.5 V; r_eq = 1.2 Ω — one 4.5 V generator with 1.2 Ω inside.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) The same three cells in parallel: state the equivalent, and one advantage under load.
**Answer: E_eq = 1.5 V; r_eq = 0.4/3 ≈ 0.13 Ω. Advantage: the terminal voltage sags far less under load (small r_eq), and each cell carries a third of the current, so the team lasts longer.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A circuit needs 12 V from 1.5 V cells and as little sag as possible. Outline the team.
**Answer: Eight cells in series give 12 V; to cut the sag, build two such strings in parallel (16 cells) — the parallel pair halves the equivalent internal resistance while keeping 12 V.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does series grouping tax the circuit even as it adds voltage?
**Answer: The current must traverse every generator's interior, so each internal resistance drops part of the stacked EMF (rI per cell) and burns power as heat; r_eq = Σrᵢ is the price of ΣEᵢ.**
