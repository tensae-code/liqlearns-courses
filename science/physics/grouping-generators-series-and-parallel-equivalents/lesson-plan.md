# Lesson plan — Grouping generators — series and parallel equivalents

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Grouping generators — series and parallel equivalents
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 — Physics expansion) — a new lesson built for the Mauritania placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** generators can be teamed, and Mauritania's Grade 12 manuals replace a team by one equivalent generator: identical generators in series give one of EMF ΣEᵢ and internal resistance Σrᵢ, while in parallel they give one of the same EMF and internal resistance r/n — series buys voltage, parallel buys staying power

## Sub lessons
1. **Series — EMFs and resistances both add** — Connect generators nose-to-tail in series and their voltages stack: the team behaves as a single generator of EMF E_eq = ΣEᵢ — but the current must pass through every generator's inside, so the internal resistances stack too: r_eq = Σrᵢ. Four 1.5 V cells in series make a 6 V source — the flat 4.5 V battery of the collège books is three 1.5 V cells in series, opened and seen. The gain is voltage; the tax is that the whole team's r stands in the circuit.
2. **Parallel — same EMF, resistance divided** — Connect identical generators side by side (like poles together) and the team keeps one generator's EMF — E_eq = E — while the internal resistance is shared out: r_eq = r/n. The team cannot push a higher voltage, but it sags less under load and lasts longer: each generator carries only its share of the current. (The rule as the manuals state it is for identical generators; unequal EMFs in parallel fight each other in a circulating current, which is why the team must match.)
3. **Choosing the grouping** — The choice reads off the job: a load needing more voltage than one generator has → series. A load needing the same voltage but more current, or for longer → parallel. The operating point then follows with the equivalent generator exactly as for a single one: I = E_eq / (R + r_eq) — grouping is just a way of building the (E, r) pair the circuit needs.

3. A flat 4.5 V battery is three 1.5 V cells in series
4. Series for voltage; parallel for current capacity and endurance
5. The team is then one equivalent generator: I = E_eq/(R + r_eq)
