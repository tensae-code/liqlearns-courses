# Grouping generators — series and parallel equivalents

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Series — EMFs and resistances both add

Connect generators nose-to-tail in series and their voltages stack: the team behaves as a single generator of EMF E_eq = ΣEᵢ — but the current must pass through every generator's inside, so the internal resistances stack too: r_eq = Σrᵢ. Four 1.5 V cells in series make a 6 V source — the flat 4.5 V battery of the collège books is three 1.5 V cells in series, opened and seen. The gain is voltage; the tax is that the whole team's r stands in the circuit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Parallel — same EMF, resistance divided

Connect identical generators side by side (like poles together) and the team keeps one generator's EMF — E_eq = E — while the internal resistance is shared out: r_eq = r/n. The team cannot push a higher voltage, but it sags less under load and lasts longer: each generator carries only its share of the current. (The rule as the manuals state it is for identical generators; unequal EMFs in parallel fight each other in a circulating current, which is why the team must match.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing the grouping

The choice reads off the job: a load needing more voltage than one generator has → series. A load needing the same voltage but more current, or for longer → parallel. The operating point then follows with the equivalent generator exactly as for a single one: I = E_eq / (R + r_eq) — grouping is just a way of building the (E, r) pair the circuit needs.

## The key ideas

- Series grouping: E_eq = ΣEᵢ and r_eq = Σrᵢ — voltage bought at the price of added internal resistance
- Parallel grouping of identical generators: E_eq = E and r_eq = r/n
- A flat 4.5 V battery is three 1.5 V cells in series
- Series for voltage; parallel for current capacity and endurance
- The team is then one equivalent generator: I = E_eq/(R + r_eq)
