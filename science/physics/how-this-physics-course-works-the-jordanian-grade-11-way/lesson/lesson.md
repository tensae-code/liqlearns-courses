# How this physics course works — the Jordanian Grade 11 way

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two units per term — the senior shape

The academic track settles into two units a term: Term 1 carries Work and Energy, then Thermodynamics; Term 2 carries Static Electricity, then Oscillatory Motion and Waves. The shape is the same as Grades 9–10 — introductory experiment, general idea, lessons, unit review — at senior depth, with enrichment spreads chosen from real technology: wind farms for energy, the refrigerator for thermodynamics.

## Sub lesson 2 — The pattern holds — experiment, gloss, application

Nothing about the way changes: the work unit opens by calculating work with a spring balance, the thermodynamics unit by testing how mass changes a temperature rise. English terms stay glossed at first use, and the applications stay Jordanian and practical — the course's promise from Grade 9 is kept word for word in the academic track.

## Sub lesson 3 — The back-of-book tools — glossary and trig table

The senior book's back matter adds a trigonometric-ratios table to the glossary: a quiet admission that from Grade 11 the physics runs on resolved components, angled forces and wave phases, and the ratios must be at the learner's elbow. The tools at the back are part of the way — the course equips the mathematics it demands.

## The key ideas

- Grade 11 runs two units per term in the same NCCD pattern
- Introductory experiments open the senior units too — spring balance, calorimetry
- English terms remain glossed at first use in the academic track
- Enrichment spreads are real technology: wind energy, the refrigerator
- The back matter adds a trigonometric-ratios table beside the glossary
