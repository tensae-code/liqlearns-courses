# Resonance and pitch — why sound rings in some spaces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A bottle is blown across the mouth, then half-filled with water and blown again. The second note is higher. Why?
**Answer: Filling the bottle shrinks the air space inside it. A shorter body of air vibrates faster — its natural frequency is higher — so the resonant note it answers with is higher.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A tuning fork sounds faint alone, but loud when held over a tube of air of one particular length. Explain both facts.
**Answer: Alone, the fork moves little air. Over the right length of tube, the air column's natural frequency matches the fork's frequency, the air resonates, and the matched air body gives the large response that makes the sound loud. Other lengths do not match, so no build-up happens.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a drum have a hollow shell instead of being a solid block with a skin on top?
**Answer: The air enclosed in the shell has its own natural frequencies and resonates when the head is struck. That resonance gives the drum its boom and carrying power; a solid block would leave the head's vibration unreinforced and the sound thin.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A strong note is sung near a piano whose strings are free, and one string starts humming the same note untouched. What is this called, and why that string and not its neighbours?
**Answer: Sympathetic resonance. The sound waves push every string a little, but only the string whose natural frequency equals the note receives its pushes in step, so only its vibration builds up enough to hear.**
