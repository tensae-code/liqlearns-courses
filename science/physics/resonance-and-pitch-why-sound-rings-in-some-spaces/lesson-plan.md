# Lesson plan — Resonance and pitch — why sound rings in some spaces

## Sub lessons
1. **Pitch — high and low is frequency** — Pitch is the ear's reading of frequency: the faster the vibration, the higher the pitch. A short, tight, light string or a short tube of air vibrates fast and sounds high; a long, loose, heavy one vibrates slowly and sounds low. Loudness is a different knob entirely — hitting harder makes a sound louder, never higher.
2. **Resonance — an answer at the natural frequency** — Every object and every body of air has a natural frequency — the rate at which it prefers to vibrate. When pushes arrive at exactly that rate, each small push lands in step with the swing already going, so the vibration builds up large: that build-up is resonance. Pushes at any other rate fight the motion as often as they help it, and the answer stays small.
3. **Why some spaces ring — and how instruments use it** — The air in a bottle, a drum shell, a horn or a room has its own natural frequencies, set by its size and shape. A note that matches one of them rings and carries; a note that matches none dies quickly. Practice rooms, drums and horns are shaped to put useful notes on resonance — and a singer's strong note can make a nearby string or glass answer without being touched, by sympathetic resonance.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes pitch and loudness up → ask one question: did the note get higher, or only stronger? Higher is frequency; stronger is amplitude.
- Thinks resonance needs a big push → a swing answers a child-sized push if each push lands in step; timing, not size, is what builds it.
