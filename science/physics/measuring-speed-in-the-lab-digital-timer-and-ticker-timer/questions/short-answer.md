# Measuring speed in the lab — digital timer and ticker timer — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a card through one photogate give an instantaneous speed, while two gates give an average?
**Answer: The card's blocking time lasts only while it crosses the one gate, so width ÷ time is the speed at that gate; two gates time the whole run between them, so distance ÷ time averages the speed over that stretch.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A tape section of 20 gaps measures 10 cm. Find the speed and say what the same length with 40 gaps would mean.
**Answer: 20 gaps = 0.40 s, so speed = 0.10 ÷ 0.40 = 0.25 m/s. Forty gaps over the same 10 cm would take 0.80 s — half the speed, 0.125 m/s.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Give two traps that can spoil a ticker-tape measurement.
**Answer: The tape can drag in the guide or stretch as it is pulled, printing false spacings; and the first dots are often unclear or made while the object was still being pushed by hand, so they do not show the free motion.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Vietnam's practice lesson teaches the digital timer and the ticker timer side by side. What does each give that the other does not?
**Answer: The digital timer gives clean, immediate numbers with no reaction-time error; the ticker timer leaves a permanent paper record of the whole motion that can be re-measured, compared section by section, and kept.**
