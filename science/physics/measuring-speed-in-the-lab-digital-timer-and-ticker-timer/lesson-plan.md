# Lesson plan — Measuring speed in the lab — digital timer and ticker timer

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Measuring speed in the lab — digital timer and ticker timer
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the motion lessons — Average speed and instantaneous speed and Reading displacement–time graphs: the two school timing toolkits that turn motion into numbers
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** speed is measured, not guessed: a digital timer with light gates times a card passing through one gate (instantaneous speed) or between two gates, and a ticker timer stamps dots on a paper tape at a fixed rate — about 50 dots a second, 0.02 s apart — so the spacing of the dots on the tape is the motion itself, written down

## Sub lessons
1. **The digital timer and its light gates** — The school digital timer (Vietnam's Grade 10 practice lesson names the MC964) works with **photogates** — U-shaped gates whose light beam, when blocked, starts or stops the clock. Its modes are the whole toolkit: mode A times a card crossing one gate, B times a second crossing, A+B adds crossings, A↔B times the run **between** two gates, and T measures a period. A card of known width through one gate gives **instantaneous speed** = width ÷ blocking time; two gates give the **average speed** between them.
2. **The ticker timer and its paper tape** — A **ticker timer** is a vibrating needle driven by the mains at about **50 Hz**: it strikes a paper tape pulled through it by the moving object, printing one dot every **0.02 s**. The tape is the motion drawn by the object itself: where the object moved fast, the same 0.02 s carried it further, so the dots spread **wide**; where it crawled, the dots crowd **close**. Constant speed prints evenly spaced dots; speeding up prints dots that spread further apart dot by dot.
3. **From records to speeds — honestly** — Both toolkits obey the same honesty rules as every measurement: repeat the run, because one timing can be mistimed; state the speed **between stated points**, because a gate pair or a tape section measures an average, never the whole journey; and watch the traps — a photogate times the card, so the card must be vertical and fully block the beam, and a tape can stretch or drag, which prints false spacings. Vietnam's lesson pairs the two toolkits deliberately: the digital timer gives clean numbers fast, the ticker timer leaves a **permanent record** you can measure again, argue about, and keep.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: a photogate starts and stops the timer when its beam is blocked; mode A↔B times the run between two gates.
2. Work a one-gate timing: speed = card width ÷ blocking time, and a two-gate average speed.
3. Read a ticker tape: state the time between dots (0.02 s at 50 Hz) and find a speed from a measured run of gaps.
4. Say why wide dot spacing means fast motion and spreading spacing means acceleration.

## What you need
A trolley or toy car, a ramp or track, a ruler and a stopwatch for the hands-on comparison (the digital timer and ticker timer are the lab instruments this lesson describes).
