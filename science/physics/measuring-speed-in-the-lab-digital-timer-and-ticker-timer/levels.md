# Measuring speed in the lab — digital timer and ticker timer — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A digital timer with photogates (modes A, B, A+B, A↔B, T) times motion: a card through one gate gives instantaneous speed = card width ÷ blocking time; two gates give average speed between them; A ticker timer stamps dots at ~50 Hz — one dot every 0.02 s — on a tape pulled by the moving object: wide dot spacing means fast motion, crowded dots mean slow; Any run of tape gaps has a known time (gaps × 0.02 s), so distances measured off the tape give speeds directly, and spreading gaps show acceleration; Honest lab speeds name the method and the stretch they were measured over, and come from repeated runs — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
