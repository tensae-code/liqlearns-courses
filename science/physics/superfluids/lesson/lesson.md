# Superfluids

## Do it first
Superfluids need temperatures no school lab reaches, so work the comparison — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Zero viscosity — flow that never fades

Below about 2.2 K, liquid helium (helium II) loses all viscosity: set it swirling and it keeps swirling; push it through the finest tube and it flows without resistance or pressure loss. Every drag formula of the ordinary unit meets its exception — a fluid for which the retarding force is exactly zero.

## Sub lesson 2 — Creeping films — the liquid that climbs out

A superfluid coats surfaces with a thin film that creeps — up the inside of its beaker, over the rim, and down the outside, emptying an open vessel drop by drop. The film flows toward warmth and levels itself; a superfluid cannot simply be kept in an open cup the way water is.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why it matters — the two-fluid picture

Physicists picture helium II as two interpenetrating fluids — a normal part and a superfluid part — which explains its second marvel, heat conduction so effective that temperature differences are ironed out almost instantly. PECTAA names superfluidity as low-temperature physics' doorway: a macroscopic quantum behaviour you can watch in a beaker.
