# Experiment — measuring a metal's resistivity — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A wire 0.80 m long, diameter 0.50 mm, measures 0.64 Ω. Find ρ.
**Answer: A = π(5.0 × 10⁻⁴)²/4 ≈ 1.96 × 10⁻⁷ m². ρ = RA/L = 0.64 × 1.96 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 0.80 ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a 5% diameter error become roughly a 10% resistivity error?
**Answer: A = πd²/4 — the area carries d squared, so percentage errors in d double in A, and ρ = RA/L inherits the area's error whole.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Copper's ρ is 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ and nichrome's about 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω·m. Use those numbers to explain the two metals' jobs.
**Answer: For the same shape, nichrome resists ~65× more: hopeless for carrying current (it would waste it as heat), perfect for a heater element — while copper's tiny ρ is exactly what connecting wires are bought for.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the experiment's three measurements and the instrument for each.
**Answer: R — volt-ampere circuit (ammeter and voltmeter); L — a rule, between the voltmeter's contacts; d — a micrometer screw gauge, averaged over many readings.**
