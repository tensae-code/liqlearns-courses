# Hands-on — Experiment — measuring a metal's resistivity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Price a metal with a micrometer
Take a metre of resistance wire mounted on a board with terminals. (1) Micrometer work first: check the screw gauge's zero error, then measure the wire's diameter at six places, twice at each place at right angles; average all twelve. (2) Wire the volt-ampere circuit with the voltmeter clipped across exactly 1.00 m of the wire; run the smallest readable current for a few seconds at a time and take three (V, I) pairs; average R. (3) Compute A = πd²/4 and ρ = RA/L; compare with the table (your wire is likely nichrome or constantan). (4) Error account, in writing: which of R, L, d carries the biggest percentage uncertainty, and by how much does it move ρ? Finish by testing the prediction: halve the measured length and confirm R halves — R = ρL/A behaving as the law says.
