# Lesson plan — Thermodynamic systems and the heat engine

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Thermodynamic systems and the heat engine
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the heat and gas lessons — the full systems treatment of thermodynamics, companion to the Ecuador-stocked lesson Thermodynamics — the laws and heat engines (`physics/thermodynamics-laws-and-heat-engines`), which states the laws as a whole; this lesson builds the system, its internal energy and its transformations underneath them
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A **thermodynamic system** trades heat and work with its surroundings; its **internal energy** U is changed only by those two doors (the first law, ΔU = Q − W in the sign convention where W is work done by the system), its journeys between states are **transformations** (isothermal, adiabatic, isobaric, isochoric, cyclic), and a **heat engine** is a cyclic transformation that turns part of the absorbed heat into work — bounded forever by Carnot's efficiency and the second law, with **entropy** keeping the universe's account
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a system and its internal energy, and state the first law with its sign convention.
2. Name the five transformation kinds, what each holds constant, and read work as area on a p–V diagram.
3. Compute an engine's work and efficiency and a Carnot ceiling from two temperatures.
4. State the second law in engine form and say what entropy counts, macroscopically and microscopically.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
