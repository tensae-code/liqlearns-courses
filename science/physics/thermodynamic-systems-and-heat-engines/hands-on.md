# Hands-on — Thermodynamic systems and the heat engine

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Pump-work warming
Pump a bicycle pump with its outlet blocked by a thumb (briefly and safely, a few strokes only) and feel the barrel near the outlet warm. No heat was supplied — the work you did on the trapped air raised its internal energy. Say aloud: this is an adiabatic compression; ΔU = −W with W the work done *by* the gas, negative here.

## Draw the cycle
On paper, draw a rectangle on p–V axes: right along the top (expansion at high pressure), down, left along the bottom (compression at low pressure), up. Shade the enclosed area. Say aloud: the shading is the net work per cycle — expansion paid more than compression cost — and the machine that walks this loop is a heat engine.
