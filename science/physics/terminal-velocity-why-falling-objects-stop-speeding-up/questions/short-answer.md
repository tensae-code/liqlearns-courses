# Terminal velocity — why falling objects stop speeding up — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the two forces on a falling object and say which one changes during the fall.
**Answer: Weight (steady) and drag; drag changes — it grows with speed while weight stays the same.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a falling object stop accelerating even though gravity still pulls it?
**Answer: Because drag grows until it equals weight; with zero resultant force there is no acceleration, so the speed stays at the terminal velocity.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do raindrops not injure people, though they fall from kilometres up?
**Answer: They are small and light, so drag balances their weight at a modest terminal velocity; they arrive at that steady, gentle speed.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A heavier skydiver and a lighter one use identical canopies. Who has the higher terminal velocity under canopy, and why?
**Answer: The heavier skydiver — more weight needs more drag to balance it, and more drag only comes at a higher speed.**
