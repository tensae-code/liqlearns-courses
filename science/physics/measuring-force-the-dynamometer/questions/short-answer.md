# Measuring force — the dynamometer — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A book weighs 25 N. Say what that number means, in the unit's own terms.
**Answer: The Earth's pull on the book is 25 newtons — about the weight of a 2.5 kg mass, at roughly 1 N per 100 g.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the pointer of a dynamometer stop at one definite mark instead of wandering?
**Answer: The spring stretches until its elastic pull balances the applied force; since stretch is proportional to force, each force has exactly one stretch — one mark.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) List the three habits of a true dynamometer reading, in order.
**Answer: Check the zero unloaded; load smoothly and let the stretch settle; read the pointer at eye level, in newtons.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A pull is expected to be about 60 N. A 0–50 N dynamometer is at hand. What do you do, and why?
**Answer: Do not use it — get a dynamometer whose scale covers 60 N; pulling past the 50 N limit would spoil the spring and the reading would lie anyway.**
