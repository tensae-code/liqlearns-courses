## Sub lessons
1. **The unit of force — the newton** — §11 of the Grade 7 book fixes the unit before the instrument: force is measured in newtons (N). One newton is about the weight of a 100-gram mass — the pull of the Earth on a small apple — so a schoolbag's weight of 30 N and a finger-push of a few newtons have a size you can feel. Every force you will ever quote — push, pull, friction, weight — is a number of newtons.
2. **The dynamometer — a spring that reads force** — §12 builds the instrument on Hooke's law: inside the case a spring is stretched by the force applied to its hook; the stretch is proportional to the force, so equal extra newtons give equal extra stretches, and the scale can be evenly divided. A pointer fixed to the spring's moving end travels along that scale and stops at the force's value. Because the spring does the translating, the dynamometer reads any pull — and, hung vertically, any weight.
3. **Measuring with it — zero, load, read** — A measurement is three habits. First, check the zero: with nothing hanging, the pointer must sit on 0, and if it does not, note or adjust it before you start. Second, load smoothly — hang the body or attach the hook and let the stretch settle without jerks. Third, read at eye level with the pointer, in newtons, and never stretch the spring past the largest mark on its scale: beyond the limit the spring is spoiled and its readings lie.
