# Ecological culture — physics and responsibility for the environment — video script

**Opening (the hook):** The Kazakh course ends by handing the learner the bill: every joule physics delivers has an ecological price — exhaust, smog, warming, testing grounds — and an ecological culture is knowing the price, counting it honestly, and choosing how to pay less of it.

**Walk-through:** Take the sub lessons in order. For 'The price of energy — physics' footprint': Kazakhstan's Grade 9 books close on ecology, and their discussion ranges wide — from the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site and its poisoned steppe to Vernadsky's noosphere, the sphere of human thought now reshaping the planet. For 'Heat engines and their ecological problems': Kazakhstan's Grade 8 section names the charge sheet: exhaust gases from millions of heat engines — carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, unburned fuel — gather as smog over cities, and the carbon dioxide raises the greenhouse blanket, warming the climate the engines' owners live in. For 'Ecological culture — the response': The closing section's answer is a culture, not a gadget: know your own footprint, prefer the source with the smaller price, waste less of what is made (efficiency is an ecological act), and restore what was damaged. 
**The picture:** [Picture 36862] — a picture of a drawn diagram — a heat-engine exhaust plume rising on one side and a wind turbine and solar panel on the other, with a balance scale between them weighing energy against its ecological price. Walk the learner across it slowly, naming each part as the sub lessons name it.

**Close:** Say the one thing once more, plainly: the Kazakh course ends by handing the learner the bill: every joule physics delivers has an ecological price — exhaust, smog, warming, testing grounds — and an ecological culture is knowing the price, counting it honestly, and choosing how to pay less of it. Then send the learner to the Do It Yourself items.
