# Ecological culture — physics and responsibility for the environment

## Do it first
Name two gases from engine exhaust and one harm of each. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — The price of energy — physics' footprint

Kazakhstan's Grade 9 books close on ecology, and their discussion ranges wide — from the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site and its poisoned steppe to Vernadsky's noosphere, the sphere of human thought now reshaping the planet. The physics is plain: energy use on a civilisational scale moves matter and heat on a planetary scale — fuel burned becomes gas in the air, heat engines warm their surroundings, radiation outlives its makers. Physics as part of culture means physics' responsibilities come inside the subject, not after it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Heat engines and their ecological problems

Kazakhstan's Grade 8 section names the charge sheet: exhaust gases from millions of heat engines — carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, unburned fuel — gather as smog over cities, and the carbon dioxide raises the greenhouse blanket, warming the climate the engines' owners live in. The Astana Kitap books' "Ecology and energy" section widens it to every energy source: each has an ecological price, in land, water, air or waste, and comparing prices honestly — per joule delivered — is the physicist's part of the argument.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ecological culture — the response

The closing section's answer is a culture, not a gadget: know your own footprint, prefer the source with the smaller price, waste less of what is made (efficiency is an ecological act), and restore what was damaged. Kazakhstan's own renewable targets — wind on the steppe, sun in the south — appear in the English-stream Grade 9 book as the class's own research. The course that began by measuring the world ends by taking responsibility for it.
