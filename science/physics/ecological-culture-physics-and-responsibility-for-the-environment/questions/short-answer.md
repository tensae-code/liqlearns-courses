# Ecological culture — physics and responsibility for the environment — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a physics course end with ecology at all?
**Answer:** Because physics' own products — engines, power stations, nuclear technology — are the things that alter the environment at scale. The Kazakh books teach that understanding the price of using physics is part of physics, a matter of culture.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) List the ecological problems of heat machines as the Grade 8 section lays them out.
**Answer:** Exhaust gases (carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, unburned hydrocarbons) poisoning city air as smog; carbon dioxide strengthening the greenhouse effect and warming the climate; waste heat and noise around engines and stations.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What does an "ecological culture" ask of an ordinary learner?
**Answer:** To know the footprint of the energy they use, prefer lower-price sources, waste less (efficiency, switching off, maintaining engines), and support restoring damaged land and water — treating responsibility as a habit, not a slogan.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How do the Astana books suggest judging a new power station proposal?
**Answer:** Compare its ecological price per joule delivered — land, water, air emissions and waste — against the alternatives, including renewables and saving energy outright, and count long-lived harms (like radiation) at full weight.
