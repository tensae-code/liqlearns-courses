# Electrostatics at work — xerography and inkjet printers — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — In the photocopier a drum coated with a photoconductor is charged uniformly in the dark; light reflected from the document discharges it where the page is white, leaving a charged image where the page is dark; An inkjet breaks its ink into a stream of tiny droplets, gives them a controlled charge, and passes them between deflecting plates: the field bends each droplet's flight by an amount set by its charge, painting characters drop by drop — electrostatic force as a pen that never touches the paper; Both machines live on the chapter's core facts: charge can be placed, held and selectively removed; like charges repel, opposite attract; a charged body in a field feels a steering force — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
