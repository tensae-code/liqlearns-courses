# Lesson plan — Electrostatics at work — xerography and inkjet printers

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Electrostatics at work — xerography and inkjet printers
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the electrostatics strand — PTB Grade 12's application pair for static electricity
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the photocopier and the inkjet printer are electrostatics earning its keep: xerography paints a document in charged toner on a light-sensitive drum, and the inkjet steers charged droplets through deflecting fields onto the page

## Sub lessons
1. **Xerography — writing with charge and light** — In the photocopier a drum coated with a photoconductor is charged uniformly in the dark; light reflected from the document discharges it where the page is white, leaving a charged image where the page is dark. Charged toner sticks only to the charged image, is transferred to paper, and heat fuses it — a copy made by static, light and powder.
2. **The inkjet — droplets steered by fields** — An inkjet breaks its ink into a stream of tiny droplets, gives them a controlled charge, and passes them between deflecting plates: the field bends each droplet's flight by an amount set by its charge, painting characters drop by drop — electrostatic force as a pen that never touches the paper.
3. **One physics, two machines** — Both machines live on the chapter's core facts: charge can be placed, held and selectively removed; like charges repel, opposite attract; a charged body in a field feels a steering force. Punjab Grade 12 sets the pair as electrostatics' proof of usefulness — and its hazards lesson (sparks, dust explosions) is the same physics uninvited.
