# Electrostatics at work — xerography and inkjet printers

## Do it first
Meet the forces with safe statics — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Xerography — writing with charge and light

In the photocopier a drum coated with a photoconductor is charged uniformly in the dark; light reflected from the document discharges it where the page is white, leaving a charged image where the page is dark. Charged toner sticks only to the charged image, is transferred to paper, and heat fuses it — a copy made by static, light and powder.

## Sub lesson 2 — The inkjet — droplets steered by fields

An inkjet breaks its ink into a stream of tiny droplets, gives them a controlled charge, and passes them between deflecting plates: the field bends each droplet's flight by an amount set by its charge, painting characters drop by drop — electrostatic force as a pen that never touches the paper.

## Sub lesson 3 — One physics, two machines

Both machines live on the chapter's core facts: charge can be placed, held and selectively removed; like charges repel, opposite attract; a charged body in a field feels a steering force. Punjab Grade 12 sets the pair as electrostatics' proof of usefulness — and its hazards lesson (sparks, dust explosions) is the same physics uninvited.
