# Acceleration — velocity changing

## Do it first
Roll a ball down a gentle slope and watch the gaps it covers grow each second. That growing-gap pattern is acceleration you can see.

[Picture 21818 — cart with velocity arrows growing longer each second]

## Acceleration — velocity changing — the teaching
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity: how fast velocity itself is changing, each second. Speeding up is acceleration; slowing down is deceleration — acceleration in the opposite sense; and changing direction is also a change of velocity, so it is acceleration too. Acceleration = change of velocity ÷ time taken, and its unit is m/s² — metres per second, per second.

**Keep the formula:** Acceleration = change of velocity / time

## The key ideas

- Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
- Slowing down is deceleration; changing direction also accelerates a body.
- Acceleration's unit is m/s².

## Say it to your companion
Explain acceleration — velocity changing to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
