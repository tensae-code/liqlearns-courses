# How this Physics course works — the Iraqi Grade 12 way

## Do it first
Open any Iraqi physics book at a chapter start — do not read the teaching yet. Find the activity or the vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), read the bilingual terms aloud, then flip to the chapter's end and find its review and test. That opening and that closing are this lesson: the Iraqi way, in the Grade 12 book. Its nine chapters run from capacitors to nuclear physics.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book's shape — 9 chapters

The Grade 12 (Sixth Scientific) book runs in 9 chapters: capacitors; electromagnetic induction; alternating current; electromagnetic waves; physical optics; modern physics; solid-state electronics; atomic spectra and the laser; and nuclear physics. Its contents table is printed at the back of the book, on the last page.

## Sub lesson 2 — How every lesson is built — activity or vocabulary first, bilingual terms, worked examples

Each Grade 12 chapter opener lists its vocabulary (مفردات الفصل) with English titles alongside the Arabic, lessons open with activity or vocabulary, and worked examples — like the worked gold-nucleus example in the nuclear chapter — carry the teaching step by step.

## Sub lesson 3 — How every chapter closes — vocabulary review and a chapter test

The Grade 12 book closes each chapter with its vocabulary/concept review and a chapter test of short-answer and multiple-choice questions, chapter by chapter through to nuclear physics at the book's end.

## The key ideas

- The Iraqi Grade 12 book is organised as 9 chapters.
- Every Iraqi lesson opens with a hands-on activity or a chapter vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), and technical terms are printed in Arabic and English side by side.
- Worked examples carry the teaching, and every chapter closes with a vocabulary/concept review plus short-answer and multiple-choice tests.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole lesson in order, in your own words — start with the opening activity, then take each sub lesson in turn and finish with what it is used for. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
