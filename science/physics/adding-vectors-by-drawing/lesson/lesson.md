# Adding vectors by drawing — triangle and parallelogram

## Do it first
On paper, add a walk of 3 units east and 4 units north by both methods. Measure: the resultant should come out 5 units by the scale in both drawings.

## Adding vectors by drawing — triangle and parallelogram — the teaching
Vectors at angles are added by drawing to scale. The triangle method: draw the first vector, set the second vector's tail at the first's head (nose-to-tail), and the resultant runs from the first tail to the last head. The parallelogram method: draw both from one point, complete the parallelogram, and the diagonal from that point is the resultant. Measure its length for magnitude and its angle for direction. Subtraction joins in as adding the negative (reversed) vector.

## The key ideas

- Triangle method: nose-to-tail; resultant from first tail to last head.
- Parallelogram method: resultant is the diagonal from the common start.
- Measure the resultant's length (magnitude) and angle (direction).

## Say it to your companion
Explain adding vectors by drawing — triangle and parallelogram to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
