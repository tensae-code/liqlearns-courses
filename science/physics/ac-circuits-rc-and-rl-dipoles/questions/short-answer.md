# AC circuits — RC and RL dipoles — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is resistance alone not enough to describe a dipole on AC?
**Answer: On AC a capacitor or inductor opposes the current by a reactance that depends on the frequency, and it can also shift the current's timing against the voltage (the phase). A complete account needs the opposition and the phase, not resistance alone.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Find the reactance of a 10 µF capacitor at 50 Hz.
**Answer: X_C = 1/(2πfC) = 1/(2 × π × 50 × 10 × 10⁻⁶) ≈ 318 Ω.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A series RC dipole has R = 300 Ω and X_C = 400 Ω on a 10 V supply. Find the impedance and the current, and check the part-voltages by squares.
**Answer: Z = √(300² + 400²) = 500 Ω, so I = 10/500 = 0.02 A. V_R = IR = 6 V and V_C = IX_C = 8 V, and 6² + 8² = 100 = 10² — the squares close the account.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A coil of 0.1 H is connected to a 50 Hz supply. Find its reactance, and say what happens to it at 100 Hz.
**Answer: X_L = 2πfL = 2 × π × 50 × 0.1 ≈ 31.4 Ω. At 100 Hz the reactance doubles, to about 62.8 Ω — the inductor's opposition rises with frequency.**
