## Sub lessons
1. **The four laws in one list** — Maxwell gathered the field laws into four equations. (1) Gauss’s law for electricity: electric flux out of a closed surface counts the charge inside — charges are the sources of electric fields. (2) Gauss’s law for magnetism: magnetic flux out of any closed surface is always zero — magnetic field lines never end, so there are no magnetic monopoles; cut a magnet and you get two smaller magnets, never an isolated pole. (3) Faraday’s law: a changing magnetic field creates an electric field. (4) The Ampère–Maxwell law: a magnetic field is created by an electric current and by a changing electric field — Maxwell’s own added term.
2. **Magnetism of matter — a glimpse** — The same chapter looks at how matter answers a magnetic field: diamagnetic materials are weakly repelled, paramagnetic materials weakly attracted and aligned, and ferromagnetic materials — iron above all — strongly magnetised, keeping domains aligned even after the field is removed. That strand of the chapter is stocked in full in the lesson on the magnetic properties of substances; here it stands as the equations’ first look inside matter.
3. **The prediction — electromagnetic waves are light** — Put the four equations together in empty space and they demand a wave: a changing electric field makes a magnetic field, whose change makes an electric field, and so on — a self-carrying electromagnetic wave whose calculated speed is exactly the measured speed of light. Maxwell’s conclusion: light itself is an electromagnetic wave. Radio, microwaves, X-rays and visible light are one family, differing only in wavelength — the whole electromagnetic spectrum falls out of four lines of law.
