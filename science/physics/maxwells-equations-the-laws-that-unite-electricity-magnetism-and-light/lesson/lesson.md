# Maxwell’s equations — the laws that unite electricity, magnetism and light

## Do it first
Before you read: list everything you know that electricity can do and everything magnetism can do. The two lists used to live in separate chapters of physics. This lesson is the moment they became one subject — and light joined it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four laws in one list

Maxwell gathered the field laws into four equations. (1) Gauss’s law for electricity: electric flux out of a closed surface counts the charge inside — charges are the sources of electric fields. (2) Gauss’s law for magnetism: magnetic flux out of any closed surface is always zero — magnetic field lines never end, so there are no magnetic monopoles; cut a magnet and you get two smaller magnets, never an isolated pole. (3) Faraday’s law: a changing magnetic field creates an electric field. (4) The Ampère–Maxwell law: a magnetic field is created by an electric current and by a changing electric field — Maxwell’s own added term.

## Sub lesson 2 — Magnetism of matter — a glimpse

The same chapter looks at how matter answers a magnetic field: diamagnetic materials are weakly repelled, paramagnetic materials weakly attracted and aligned, and ferromagnetic materials — iron above all — strongly magnetised, keeping domains aligned even after the field is removed. That strand of the chapter is stocked in full in the lesson on the magnetic properties of substances; here it stands as the equations’ first look inside matter.

## Sub lesson 3 — The prediction — electromagnetic waves are light

Put the four equations together in empty space and they demand a wave: a changing electric field makes a magnetic field, whose change makes an electric field, and so on — a self-carrying electromagnetic wave whose calculated speed is exactly the measured speed of light. Maxwell’s conclusion: light itself is an electromagnetic wave. Radio, microwaves, X-rays and visible light are one family, differing only in wavelength — the whole electromagnetic spectrum falls out of four lines of law.

## The key ideas

- Gauss (electric): charges source electric fields; flux counts enclosed charge
- Gauss (magnetic): net magnetic flux is always zero — no magnetic monopoles exist
- Faraday: a changing magnetic field creates an electric field
- Ampère–Maxwell: currents and changing electric fields create magnetic fields — and together the four predict EM waves at light speed = light is an EM wave

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: four equations gather everything known about electric and magnetic fields: charges make electric fields, there are no magnetic monopoles, changing magnetic fields make electric fields, and currents — plus changing electric fields — make magnetic fields; together they predict electromagnetic waves travelling at the speed of light. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

1. Match each phenomenon to the Maxwell law that covers it: (a) a compass needle turning beside a wire; (b) a generator producing voltage as its coil spins; (c) the impossibility of isolating a magnet’s north pole; (d) field lines ending on a charged sphere.
