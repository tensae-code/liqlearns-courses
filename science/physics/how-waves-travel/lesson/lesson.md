# How waves travel — transverse and longitudinal

## Do it first
Flick a rope tied to a post: the bump travels, the rope stays. Then push a slinky or spring forward in pulses: crowds and gaps travel — two wave kinds with your hands.

[Picture 21857 — rope wave and a slinky compression wave side by side]

## How waves travel — transverse and longitudinal — the teaching
A wave is a disturbance that propagates through space or matter, carrying energy without carrying matter — the rope moves up and down, the wave moves forward. Waves sort two ways. Mechanical waves need matter (string, water, sound); electromagnetic waves do not. And by vibration direction: transverse waves vibrate perpendicular to the travel (string, water surface), while longitudinal waves vibrate parallel to it — sound's compressions and rarefactions travelling like crowds and gaps along a spring.

## The key ideas

- A wave carries energy without carrying matter.
- Mechanical waves need a medium; electromagnetic waves do not.
- Transverse: vibration perpendicular to travel. Longitudinal: vibration parallel — compressions and rarefactions.
