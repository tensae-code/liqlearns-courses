# Video script — How waves travel — transverse and longitudinal

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

A wave is a disturbance that propagates through space or matter, carrying energy without carrying matter — the rope moves up and down, the wave moves forward.
Waves sort two ways.
Mechanical waves need matter (string, water, sound); electromagnetic waves do not.
And by vibration direction: transverse waves vibrate perpendicular to the travel (string, water surface), while longitudinal waves vibrate parallel to it — sound's compressions and rarefactions travelling like crowds and gaps along a spring.

Remember: **A wave carries energy without carrying matter.**
Remember: **Mechanical waves need a medium; electromagnetic waves do not.**
Remember: **Transverse: vibration perpendicular to travel. Longitudinal: vibration parallel — compressions and rarefactions.**
