# The golden rule of mechanics — no mechanism gives a gain in work — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A pulley lifts a 600 N engine using a 150 N pull. How much rope is pulled for each metre the engine rises (ideal machine)?
**Answer: Force gain is 600/150 = 4, so by the golden rule the rope moves 4 m for each 1 m of lift — work in 150 × 4 = 600 J = work out.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why can no real mechanism reach 100% efficiency?
**Answer: Friction in axles, ropes and threads always turns some of the input work into heat, so useful work out is always less than work in.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Two machines lift the same load: one at 95%, one at 35%. Which do you choose for a quick lift, and which for holding a car during a wheel change — and why?
**Answer: The 95% machine for the lift — least wasted work. The 35% screw jack for holding — its thread friction locks the load in place when released, which is a safety feature, not a flaw.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) State the golden rule in the force–distance form for an inclined plane.
**Answer: The gentler the slope, the smaller the pushing force — but the barrel travels proportionally further along the slope, so F × s along the slope equals the work of lifting straight up (before friction).**
