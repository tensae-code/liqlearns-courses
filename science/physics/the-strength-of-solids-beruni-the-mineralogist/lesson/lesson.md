# The strength of solids — Beruni the mineralogist

## Do it first
Before you read: a bridge cable, a knife blade and a window pane are all solids, but each fails differently — one stretches and snaps, one holds an edge, one shatters. What properties separate them? A scholar who catalogued gemstones for kings needed exactly those answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beruni among the stones

Beruni's Book of Minerals priced and identified stones by measured properties: density by his overflow weighing, and hardness by scratching — which stone cuts which. A stone that scratches another is harder; a stone that powders is brittle; a metal that bends and stays bent is plastic, one that springs back is elastic. In Beruni's hands these were not adjectives but tests, repeated the same way on every sample. The Grade 9 book opens its lesson on the mechanical properties of solids with him because the lesson is his programme, formalised.

## Sub lesson 2 — Elasticity, plasticity, brittleness — the three answers to a force

Pull or press a solid and it answers in one of three ways. Elastic: it deforms and returns when the force lets go — a spring, a rubber band, a steel ruler within its limit (Hooke's law country: stretch proportional to force). Plastic: it deforms and stays — clay, warm wax, a bent wire. Brittle: it barely deforms, then breaks — glass, cast iron, a dry biscuit. Push any of them too far and all three meet the same end: the ultimate strength, the stress at which the body fails — the number a bridge designer may never cross.

## Sub lesson 3 — Strength as a number engineers live by

Modern form: stress = force/area, strain = stretch/original length, and their ratio in the elastic region is Young's modulus — stiffness as a property of the material itself. Safety factors divide the ultimate strength down to a working load, because real loads surprise. Beruni's scratch tests rank among the ancestors of every materials table in an engineer's handbook: properties measured the same way, on every sample, before anything is trusted with a load — or a price.

## The key ideas

- Beruni identified minerals by measured properties — density by overflow weighing, hardness by scratch tests
- A solid answers force three ways: elastic (returns), plastic (stays), brittle (breaks with little deformation)
- Every solid has an ultimate strength — the stress at which it fails; engineers work far below it with safety factors
- Stress, strain and Young's modulus are Beruni's programme in modern form: same tests, every sample, before trust
