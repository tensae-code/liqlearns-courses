# The strength of solids — Beruni the mineralogist — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What two measured properties anchor Beruni's mineral work in this lesson?
**Answer: Density (by overflow weighing) and hardness (by comparative scratching) — both measured the same way on every sample, so stones could be identified and priced by test, not opinion.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A spring stretches 4 cm under 10 N and returns fully. A clay strip stretches 4 cm under 10 N and stays. Classify each and name the property that differs.
**Answer: The spring is elastic — it recovers. The clay is plastic — it keeps its deformation. Same stretch under same force, different answer when the force is removed.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is ultimate strength alone not enough to design a bridge cable?
**Answer: Because real loads vary and surprise — gusts, crowds, corrosion, flaws. Designers apply a safety factor and keep working stress far below the ultimate strength.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Glass is strong in compression but fails suddenly in tension. Which of the three answers is glass giving, and what warning follows for its use?
**Answer: The brittle answer — little deformation, then sudden break. Warning: no visible stretch warns you before failure, so glass parts need protection from tension and shock loads.**
