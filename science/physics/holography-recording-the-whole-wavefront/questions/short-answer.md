# Holography — recording the whole wavefront — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in one chain, how interference lets a photographic plate store phase.
**Answer: The object's wave and the reference wave reach each point of the plate with a phase difference set by the object's shape and distances. Where they arrive in phase they reinforce (bright fringe); where in antiphase they cancel (dark). The resulting fringe pattern's positions and contrasts therefore encode the phase point by point — phase stored as intensity, which film can hold.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the reconstruction need the same reference-beam geometry as the recording?
**Answer: The hologram's fringes were written by that exact reference wave. Illuminating with an identical wave makes the fringe pattern diffract it into the object's wavefront (plus its conjugate) faithfully; a different angle or wavelength would rebuild a distorted, shifted wavefront, just as playing a record at the wrong speed distorts the music.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State two ways a hologram differs from a photograph of the same object, beyond 'it looks 3-D'.
**Answer: (Any two:) the plate itself shows no recognisable image, only fringes; the stored information is distributed — a fragment rebuilds the whole scene from a reduced window; the image has true parallax and can be viewed from different angles as if the object were present; the recording required coherent laser light; reconstruction rebuilds an actual wavefront an optical system could act on.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the scientific use of double-exposure holography described in the lesson and the size of effect it reads.
**Answer: Holographic interferometry: two holograms of an object (before and under stress) recorded on one plate rebuild two wavefronts whose interference fringes map the object's deformation — revealing displacements as small as a fraction of a wavelength of light.**
