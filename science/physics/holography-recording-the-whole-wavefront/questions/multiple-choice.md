# Holography — recording the whole wavefront — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) An ordinary photograph fails to store the scene's depth because it records…
a) Only red light  b) Intensity (amplitude squared) and discards the wave's phase, which carries the depth  c) The image upside down  d) Too few pixels
**Answer: b) Intensity (amplitude squared) and discards the wave's phase, which carries the depth**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Phase information is made recordable by…
a) A faster shutter  b) Interference with a reference beam, converting phase differences into brightness differences  c) Polarising the light  d) Using a larger lens
**Answer: b) Interference with a reference beam, converting phase differences into brightness differences**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) In recording a hologram, the object beam and reference beam must be…
a) From two different lamps  b) Split from one laser — coherent — so their interference pattern is stable  c) Of different colours  d) Switched on alternately
**Answer: b) Split from one laser — coherent — so their interference pattern is stable**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A small fragment of a hologram can still show the whole scene because…
a) The image is printed many times  b) Each part of the plate received light from every part of the object, so the scene is stored distributed across the plate  c) Fragments magnify  d) The emulsion copies itself
**Answer: b) Each part of the plate received light from every part of the object, so the scene is stored distributed across the plate**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) On reconstruction, the viewer sees a true 3-D image because…
a) The plate is curved  b) The hologram diffracts the reference beam back into a copy of the object's original wavefront, parallax and all  c) Two photographs are combined  d) The laser projects a film
**Answer: b) The hologram diffracts the reference beam back into a copy of the object's original wavefront, parallax and all**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) Security holograms on bank cards are hard to forge because…
a) They are very small  b) They encode a wavefront in a microscopic fringe structure, not a printable picture — copying the look does not copy the wave behaviour  c) They use rare inks  d) They change daily
**Answer: b) They encode a wavefront in a microscopic fringe structure, not a printable picture — copying the look does not copy the wave behaviour**
