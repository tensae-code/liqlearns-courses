# Hands-on — Holography — recording the whole wavefront

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## See a real hologram's parallax
Examine an embossed hologram (bank card or magazine cover) under a point light. Tilt and move it: the image shifts with true parallax, near features sliding against far ones — behaviour no photograph shows. Say aloud the rule: the plate stored a wavefront, not a picture, so viewing it replays the light the object itself would send.

## Fringes as stored phase
Look at a diffraction grating (or a CD's surface) under a lamp and note the fine line structure and colour play. A hologram's plate carries fringes a thousand to the millimetre, encoding phase as fringe position. Say aloud the rule: interference is the pen that writes phase into brightness — without it, film has nothing to hold the depth with.
