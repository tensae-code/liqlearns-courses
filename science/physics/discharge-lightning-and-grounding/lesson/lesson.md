# Discharge, lightning and grounding

## Do it first
Shuffle across a mat in socks and touch a metal handle: the tiny spark is a discharge you can feel. Lightning is that same event at sky scale.

[Picture 21899 — cloud-to-ground lightning path beside a building's lightning rod and earth wire]

## Discharge, lightning and grounding — the teaching
A charged object stays charged until it is given an escape path. A sudden, rapid transfer along that path is a discharge. Lightning is the giant case: charge builds up within clouds and between cloud and ground until the air itself gives way and the charge flashes down in a spark kilometres long. Grounding (earthing) is the controlled version: give charge a safe wire path to the earth — a building's lightning rod and earth wire — and it drains away without harm.

## The key ideas

- Discharge is rapid charge transfer along an escape path.
- Lightning is a giant discharge from charge built up in clouds.
- Grounding drains charge safely to earth — lightning rods use it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain discharge, lightning and grounding to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
