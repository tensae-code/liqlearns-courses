# Gravitational potential and gravitational potential energy

## Do it first
Before you read: a ball in a deep pit needs energy to get out — the deeper the pit, the more energy, and while it sits in the pit we can say it has 'less than nothing' compared with the flat ground outside. A planet's gravity is such a pit with no walls: this lesson draws the pit precisely, prices the climb out at every distance, and computes the speed of the one leap that never falls back.

The mgh of the lower school is still inside it: near the surface, moving from r to r + h changes E_p by almost exactly mgh — the deep formula flattens into the familiar one when h is tiny beside the planet's radius.

## Sub lesson 2 — Potential — the field's energy per kilogram

Potentials add as scalars — the potential at a point between two masses is simply V₁ + V₂, no directions to resolve — which is one reason the energy view so often wins in practice.

## Sub lesson 3 — Escape — when the account reaches zero

A mass escapes the field when its total energy reaches **zero**: kinetic + potential = 0, so ½mv² = GMm/r, giving the **escape velocity v = √(2GM/r)** — √2 times the circular-orbit speed at the same r. From Earth's surface that is **11.2 km/s**; from the Moon's, only 2.4 km/s — the shallow potential well is why the Moon holds no atmosphere: its gas molecules' fastest wanderers leak out of a well that shallow over geological time. Vietnam's lesson completes the topic's arc: field (Bài 1), its strength (Bài 2), its energy (Bài 3) — gravity known three ways, each way earning its keep.

## The key ideas

- Gravitational potential energy: E_p = −GMm/r — zero at infinity, negative everywhere finite, because the mass is bound and work is needed to free it
- Gravitational potential V = −GM/r (J/kg) is the field's energy-per-kilogram map; potentials from several masses add as scalars
- Field strength g is the slope of the potential — the force view and the energy view of one field
- Escape needs total energy zero: escape velocity v = √(2GM/r), 11.2 km/s from Earth's surface, 2.4 km/s from the Moon's

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: A gravitational field stores energy in any mass placed in it: the potential energy of a mass m at distance r from M is E_p = −GMm/r — negative because the zero is set at infinity and work must be done to haul the mass out of the field's grip — and the gravitational potential V = E_p/m = −GM/r (joules per kilogram) is the field's own energy-per-kilogram map, from which escape speed and orbital energies follow.
