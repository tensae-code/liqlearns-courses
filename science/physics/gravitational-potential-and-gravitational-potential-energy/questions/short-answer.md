# Gravitational potential and gravitational potential energy — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Reconcile E_p = −GMm/r with the school formula mgh.
**Answer: Near a planet's surface, over heights tiny compared with the radius, the deep formula's change between r and r + h works out to almost exactly mgh — mgh is the local, flat approximation of the universal curve, measured from the local ground instead of from infinity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A satellite in circular orbit has total energy −GMm/2r. What does the negative sign say, and what must be added to free it?
**Answer: It says the satellite is bound — its kinetic energy pays only half the well's depth GMm/r. Adding +GMm/2r of energy (raising its speed by the √2 factor to escape velocity) brings the total to zero and frees it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) At a point, g = 3.0 N/kg and V = −2.0×10⁷ J/kg. Which of these two numbers would change if a heavier satellite were placed there?
**Answer: Neither — both g and V are properties of the field at the point, defined per kilogram of visitor; the satellite's mass changes its force and its energy, not the field's per-kilogram maps.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do potentials add as scalars while field strengths add as vectors?
**Answer: Potential is an energy per kilogram — a plain amount with no direction; field strength is a force per kilogram, which pushes in a direction, so contributions from two masses must be combined with their directions.**
