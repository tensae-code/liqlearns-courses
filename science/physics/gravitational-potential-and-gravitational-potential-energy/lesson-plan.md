# Lesson plan — Gravitational potential and gravitational potential energy

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Gravitational potential and gravitational potential energy
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), after Gravitational field strength: the energy view of the same field — the closing lesson of Vietnam's gravitation topic
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a gravitational field stores energy in any mass placed in it: the potential energy of a mass m at distance r from M is E_p = −GMm/r — negative because the zero is set at infinity and work must be done to haul the mass out of the field's grip — and the gravitational potential V = E_p/m = −GM/r (joules per kilogram) is the field's own energy-per-kilogram map, from which escape speed and orbital energies follow

## Sub lessons
1. **Potential energy in a field that never lets go** — Near the ground, potential energy is mgh — but that formula is the flat-Earth approximation of a deeper truth. Far from a planet, lifting a mass means working against a weakening pull, and the total work to haul mass m from distance r **all the way to infinity** — out of the field entirely — is GMm/r. Setting the zero of energy at infinity (the only universal choice) gives the potential energy at r as **E_p = −GMm/r**: negative at every finite distance, because the mass is **bound** — you must add energy to free it. Vietnam's Grade 11 study-topics book closes its gravitation topic with this lesson (Bài 3): the energy account of the field that Bài 1–2 built.
2. **Potential — the field's energy per kilogram** — Divide out the visitor's mass, as was done for field strength, and the field gets its second map: the **gravitational potential** V = E_p/m = **−GM/r**, in **joules per kilogram**. Where g maps force-per-kilogram, V maps energy-per-kilogram: the work needed to bring 1 kg from infinity to that point (negative — the field does the work, you collect it) or to take 1 kg out (positive — you must pay it). Field strength is the potential's **slope**: g is how steeply V falls with distance — two descriptions, force and energy, of one field.
3. **Escape — when the account reaches zero** — A mass escapes the field when its total energy reaches **zero**: kinetic + potential = 0, so ½mv² = GMm/r, giving the **escape velocity v = √(2GM/r)** — √2 times the circular-orbit speed at the same r. From Earth's surface that is **11.2 km/s**; from the Moon's, only 2.4 km/s — the shallow potential well is why the Moon holds no atmosphere: its gas molecules' fastest wanderers leak out of a well that shallow over geological time. Vietnam's lesson completes the topic's arc: field (Bài 1), its strength (Bài 2), its energy (Bài 3) — gravity known three ways, each way earning its keep.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State E_p = −GMm/r and explain the negative sign and the zero at infinity.
2. Define gravitational potential, state its unit, and add two masses' potentials.
3. Relate potential to field strength as energy-map to force-map of one field.
4. Derive the escape condition and state Earth's and the Moon's escape velocities, with the Moon's missing atmosphere as a consequence.

## What you need
