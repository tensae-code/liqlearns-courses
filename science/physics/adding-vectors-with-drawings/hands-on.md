# Hands-on — Adding vectors with drawings

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Draw it to scale
On paper, choose a scale (1 cm = 10 units). Draw two arrows at an angle, join them head-to-tail, and measure the resultant with ruler and protractor. Check your drawing by components.

## See it and say it
Study this lesson's picture — two arrows joined head-to-tail with the resultant drawn across — and name every part of it out loud. Then close the picture and draw it from memory; check your drawing against the lesson.

## Teach it back
Explain this lesson to someone at home using one thing in the room as your example. Their questions show you which part to re-read.

## The farmer and the plough (teacher guide — evidence)
Watch a plough at work, or picture one clearly. List every force acting on it, draw each as an arrow, and say how the arrows add so the plough digs in instead of skating over the soil.

## Walk A to B two ways (teacher guide — evidence)
Choose two points in the school compound. Walk between them straight, then by a longer path. Pace both walks: the distances differ, the displacement arrow is one and the same.
