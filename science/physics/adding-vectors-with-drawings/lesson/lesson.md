## Graphical addition — scale drawing, triangle and parallelogram rules

Vectors are added head-to-tail (triangle rule), by the parallelogram rule for two vectors at a point, and by the polygon method for several; a scale diagram converts measured lengths and angles back into a resultant — the brainstorming questions drill why a scale is needed and how a vector differs from its negative.

## The teacher's demonstrations — seeing vectors add (teacher guide — evidence)

Your teacher's guide adds demonstrations that make graphical addition visible. Work through them as part of this lesson:

- **The class force table.** The class builds a chart — a force table — listing forces as vectors, to reinforce that a force is a vector: it has a size and a direction, and forces add like arrows.
- **The A-to-B walking demonstration.** Walk from point A to point B by two different paths — first straight, then by a longer way round. Both walks cover different distances, but the displacement — the straight arrow from A to B — is the same. That is why displacement (a vector), not distance (a scalar), describes the trip.
- **Three vectors can add to zero.** Draw three non-zero arrows head-to-tail so the last head lands exactly on the first tail, closing a triangle. Three non-zero vectors have added to zero — proof by drawing that a zero resultant does not need zero vectors.
- **The farmer and the plough.** In the field, a farmer ploughs his land: name the forces acting — the pull on the plough, the soil pushing back, weight and the ground's support — and see how they add so the plough digs, breaks and turns the soil. Vector addition is field work, not only paper work.
- **Words to own: orthogonal and non-coplanar.** Vectors at right angles to each other are **orthogonal**. Vectors that do not lie in one flat plane are **non-coplanar**. Both words belong to this unit's vector vocabulary.

The guide also shows the triangle law with arrows drawn to scale, head-to-tail, and the commutative law by reordering the same arrows — the resultant does not change when the order changes.
