## Sub lessons
1. **Electric flux — how much field passes through** — Flux counts how much electric field passes through a surface: for a flat area A in a uniform field E it is Φ = EA cos θ, where θ is the angle between the field and the surface’s normal — full value when the field strikes square-on, zero when it skims along the surface. For a closed surface, outward flux is counted positive and inward flux negative; field lines entering and leaving in equal numbers give zero net flux.
2. **Gauss’s law — flux prices the enclosed charge** — Gauss’s law states that the net outward flux through any closed surface is Φ = q_enc/ε₀, where q_enc is the total charge inside the surface and ε₀ is the permittivity of free space. Charges outside contribute lines that enter and leave again, cancelling out; only the enclosed charge survives the count. Double the enclosed charge and the flux doubles — enlarge or deform the surface and the flux is unchanged, because the same field lines must cross it somewhere.
3. **Using Gauss’s law — symmetry does the work** — Where the charge arrangement is symmetric, Gauss’s law finds the field itself. Around a point charge, choose a sphere: the field is the same all over it, so E × 4πr² = q/ε₀ gives E = q/(4πε₀r²) — Coulomb’s law recovered. The same move prices the field of a long charged wire and of a charged sheet, and proves the field inside a hollow charged conductor is zero: no enclosed charge, no flux, no field.
