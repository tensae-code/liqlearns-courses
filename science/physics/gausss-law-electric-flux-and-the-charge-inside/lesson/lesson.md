# Gauss’s law — electric flux and the charge inside

## Do it first
Before you read: imagine a lamp inside a closed cardboard box. The total light escaping through the box’s walls depends on the lamp — not on whether the box is big, small, round or square. Electric field lines behave the same way. Guess what “how much field passes out through a surface” might be called.

## Sub lesson 1 — Electric flux — how much field passes through

Flux counts how much electric field passes through a surface: for a flat area A in a uniform field E it is Φ = EA cos θ, where θ is the angle between the field and the surface’s normal — full value when the field strikes square-on, zero when it skims along the surface. For a closed surface, outward flux is counted positive and inward flux negative; field lines entering and leaving in equal numbers give zero net flux.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gauss’s law — flux prices the enclosed charge

Gauss’s law states that the net outward flux through any closed surface is Φ = q_enc/ε₀, where q_enc is the total charge inside the surface and ε₀ is the permittivity of free space. Charges outside contribute lines that enter and leave again, cancelling out; only the enclosed charge survives the count. Double the enclosed charge and the flux doubles — enlarge or deform the surface and the flux is unchanged, because the same field lines must cross it somewhere.

## Sub lesson 3 — Using Gauss’s law — symmetry does the work

Where the charge arrangement is symmetric, Gauss’s law finds the field itself. Around a point charge, choose a sphere: the field is the same all over it, so E × 4πr² = q/ε₀ gives E = q/(4πε₀r²) — Coulomb’s law recovered. The same move prices the field of a long charged wire and of a charged sheet, and proves the field inside a hollow charged conductor is zero: no enclosed charge, no flux, no field.

## The key ideas

- Flux through a flat surface: Φ = EA cos θ — square-on counts fully, skimming counts nothing
- Gauss’s law: net outward flux through a closed surface = q_enc/ε₀
- Only enclosed charge counts; outside charges’ lines enter and leave again
- With symmetry (sphere, cylinder, sheet) Gauss’s law gives E directly — e.g. E = q/(4πε₀r²) for a point charge

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: Gauss’s law: the total electric flux out of any closed surface equals the charge enclosed divided by ε₀ — count the flux leaving a surface and you have counted the charge inside it, whatever shape the surface takes. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A uniform field of 200 N/C strikes a 0.5 m² surface square-on, then at 60° to the normal. Calculate the flux both times.
2. A closed box encloses +3 μC and −1 μC. What is the net outward flux? A third charge of +5 μC sits outside the box — does it change your answer? Say why.
3. Use Gauss’s law with a spherical surface to show the field of a point charge falls as 1/r².
