# Relative velocity — motion seen by a moving observer

## Do it first
Walk toward a partner who walks toward you, both slowly. Your closing speed feels bigger than either walk — your bodies just added velocities.

[Picture 21886 — two cars passing, with the closing speed each driver sees]

## Relative velocity — motion seen by a moving observer — the teaching
Velocity is measured relative to an observer. Two vehicles moving the same way: each driver sees the other's speed as the difference of the two speeds. Two vehicles approaching: each sees the closing speed as the sum. The guide's two-observer framing is the key: a ground observer and a moving observer honestly report different velocities for the same motion, and relative velocity converts between their reports. Trains, boats and everyday passing cars all run on this rule.

## The key ideas

- Same direction: relative velocity = difference of speeds.
- Approaching: closing speed = sum of speeds.
- Velocity depends on the observer's own motion.

## Say it to your companion
Explain relative velocity — motion seen by a moving observer to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
