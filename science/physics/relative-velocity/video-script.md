# Video script — Relative velocity — motion seen by a moving observer

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Velocity is measured relative to an observer.
Two vehicles moving the same way: each driver sees the other's speed as the difference of the two speeds.
Two vehicles approaching: each sees the closing speed as the sum.
The guide's two-observer framing is the key: a ground observer and a moving observer honestly report different velocities for the same motion, and relative velocity converts between their reports.
Trains, boats and everyday passing cars all run on this rule.

Remember: **Same direction: relative velocity = difference of speeds.**
Remember: **Approaching: closing speed = sum of speeds.**
Remember: **Velocity depends on the observer's own motion.**
