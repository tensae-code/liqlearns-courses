# Hands-on — Charged particles circling in a uniform magnetic field

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Read a circle and price a particle
Work this lesson as a track-reader. (1) Given a photographed track: radius 0.25 m in a 1.2 T field, charge one e (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) — find the momentum mv = qBr = 4.8 × 10⁻²⁰ kg·m/s, and the speed if the particle is a proton (m = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg): v ≈ 2.9 × 10⁷ m/s. (2) Show the lap time for that proton, T = 2πm/qB ≈ 5.5 × 10⁻⁸ s, and check it against 2πr/v from the first part — the two routes must agree; if they do not, find the slip. (3) Design question: a detector's field is doubled. State, with the two formulas, exactly what happens to a track's radius and to its lap time, and say which change makes the particles easier to identify, and why (r measurable more precisely relative to the chamber, at unchanged timing).
