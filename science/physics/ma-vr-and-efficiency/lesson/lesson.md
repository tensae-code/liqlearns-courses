# MA, VR and efficiency — measuring a machine

## Do it first
Lift a book by hand (effort = its weight), then with a lever. You cannot feel MA as a number yet — but you can feel the trade the numbers describe.

## MA, VR and efficiency — measuring a machine — the teaching
Three numbers measure any machine. Mechanical advantage, MA = load ÷ effort: how many times the machine multiplies your force — in a real machine friction makes the actual MA smaller than the ideal. Velocity ratio, VR = distance moved by effort ÷ distance moved by load: fixed by the machine's geometry. Efficiency η = work output ÷ work input, and it also equals MA ÷ VR. All three are unitless ratios. An efficiency under 100% is not failure — it is friction's honest tax.

**Keep the formula:** MA = load / effort; VR = effort distance / load distance; η = MA / VR

## The key ideas

- MA = load ÷ effort; VR = effort distance ÷ load distance.
- Efficiency η = work output ÷ work input = MA ÷ VR.
- Friction makes actual MA less than ideal and efficiency less than 100%.

## Say it to your companion
Explain ma, vr and efficiency — measuring a machine to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
