# Brownian motion and the kinetic theory of matter — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does Brownian motion count as evidence for particles too small to see?
**Answer: The visible specks move randomly and ceaselessly in still fluid with nothing stirring them. The only explanation that fits is uneven blows from the fluid's own invisible particles in constant motion — we see the kicks' effect, so the kickers must be there.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the kinetic theory explain why a smell crosses a room?
**Answer: The scent's particles are in constant random motion among the air particles, colliding and wandering in every direction, so they spread through the whole room without any current — diffusion.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What would you expect to happen to Brownian motion if the water is warmed, and why?
**Answer: The specks jiggle more wildly. Warming raises the water particles' average kinetic energy — they move faster and strike harder and more often, so the kicks on each speck are stronger.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State the particle picture for a solid, a liquid and a gas in one sentence each.
**Answer: Solid: particles vibrate about fixed places in a regular arrangement. Liquid: particles stay close but slide past each other, taking the container's shape. Gas: particles fly freely in all directions, filling the space and colliding with the walls.**
