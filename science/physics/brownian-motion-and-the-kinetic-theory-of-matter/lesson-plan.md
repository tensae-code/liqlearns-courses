# Lesson plan — Brownian motion and the kinetic theory of matter

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Brownian motion and the kinetic theory of matter
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the heat lessons — Temperature, Specific heat capacity and the ideal gas law: the particle picture of matter that the heat and gas lessons stand on
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** all matter is made of tiny particles in constant random motion — the kinetic theory of matter — and Brownian motion, the ceaseless jiggling of visible specks in a fluid, is the visible evidence: the specks are knocked about by the invisible moving particles of the fluid itself
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Brownian motion — specks that never sit still** — The specks never sit still — they are being knocked about.
2. **The kinetic theory — matter made of moving particles** — Invisible moving particles kick the visible specks — that is the evidence.
3. **Temperature, diffusion and pressure in the particle picture** — One picture explains the dance, diffusion and pressure.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Brownian motion is the ceaseless random jiggling of tiny visible particles suspended in a fluid, caused by uneven blows from the fluid's own invisible moving particles.
2. Say, in their own words: The kinetic theory: all matter is made of particles in constant random motion — flying in gases, sliding in liquids, vibrating in solids.
3. Say, in their own words: Temperature measures the particles' average kinetic energy, so warming a fluid makes Brownian motion livelier.
4. Say, in their own words: Diffusion and gas pressure are the same picture at work: random motion mixes, and wall collisions press.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A clear glass of water, a drop of ink or milk, a bright light, and (if available) a microscope or phone camera zoom for the opening activity.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the specks move because the water is flowing → the activity uses still water in an untouched glass; the jiggle is random in every direction and never settles into a flow — uneven molecular kicks explain what no current can.
- Thinks Brownian motion is the molecules themselves moving → the specks we see are thousands of times bigger than molecules; we see the effect of the molecules' blows, never the molecules.
- Cannot start → return to Do it first. Hold that puzzle; the lesson is its solution.
