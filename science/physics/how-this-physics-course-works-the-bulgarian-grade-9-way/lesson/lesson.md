# How this Physics course works — the Bulgarian Grade 9 way

## Do it first
Before you read: a course arrives as two books, and the first one's cover carries a big numeral that disagrees with its own subtitle. Which do you trust — and what goes wrong if you trust the wrong one? This lesson settles the two parts' identity first, then walks through the design they share.

## Sub lesson 1 — One Grade 9 course in two printed parts

Bulgaria's Grade 9 Physics and Astronomy course for classes with intensive foreign-language study is printed as two books: Part 1 (filed in some records as "Grade 8" — its cover's own subtitle says Part 1 for Grade 9) carries mechanics and thermal phenomena; Part 2 carries electric current, oscillations and waves, and a laboratory experiments block. Together, and only together, they are the whole Grade 9 course: Part 2's entry test assumes Part 1, and its contents continue Part 1's numbering of ideas. Read the covers, not the file labels: the printed identity — Grade 9, Part 1 and Part 2 — is the true one.

## Sub lesson 2 — The same typed-lesson design, with laboratories inside the sequence

The design is the Bulgarian way already met in Grade 7, grown up: a numbered sequence of typed lessons — new knowledge, problem-solving, laboratory work, summary, and "I know and I can" self-checks — and every lesson carrying the same boxes: "I already know…" (Вече знам) recall, a worked example, "What I learned" (Какво научих), a "Curious" (Любопитно) box, and questions and tasks. What changes at Grade 9 is that laboratory works sit inside the numbered sequence itself — free fall measured in Lesson 5's laboratory, the second principle verified in a laboratory inside Lesson 7 — so measuring is a lesson type you meet on schedule, not an appendix.

## Sub lesson 3 — Entry and exit, glossary and answers

Each part opens with an entry test and the course closes with a year review, a project lesson, a glossary (Речник) and answers — Part 1 at printed pp. 151–160, Part 2's glossary at printed p. 125 and answers at p. 128. The working method is the same at both doors: test honestly, let every miss name the numbered lesson that teaches it, use the glossary the moment a word wobbles, and never check an answer before your own attempt is written down.

## The key ideas

- Grade 9 is one course in two printed parts: Part 1 (mechanics and thermal phenomena) and Part 2 (electric current, oscillations and waves, laboratory experiments) — cited under their printed identity
- The typed-lesson design continues from Grade 7, with laboratory works inside the numbered sequence — measuring is a scheduled lesson type, not an appendix
- Entry tests, a year review and project lesson, a glossary and answers close the course — every miss names the lesson that teaches it

## Do It Yourself
1. A learner holds only Part 2. List three things they cannot yet do properly, and what Part 1 lesson answers each.
2. Write the "I already know…" box (three lines) for Part 2's Lesson 8 on harmonic oscillation, using only Part 1 and early Part 2 learning.
3. Plan a self-check: pick one strand and write the five questions your own "I know and I can" lesson would ask.
