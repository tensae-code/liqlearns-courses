# How this Physics course works — the Iraqi Grade 11 way

## Do it first
Open any Iraqi physics book at a chapter start — do not read the teaching yet. Find the activity or the vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), read the bilingual terms aloud, then flip to the chapter's end and find its review and test. That opening and that closing are this lesson: the Iraqi way, in the Grade 11 book. Its ten chapters run from vectors to magnetism.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book's shape — 10 chapters

The Grade 11 (Fifth Scientific) book runs in 10 chapters: vectors; motion; laws of motion; torque and equilibrium; work, power, energy and momentum; thermodynamics; circular and rotational motion; wave and vibration motion and sound; electric current; and magnetism.

## Sub lesson 2 — How every lesson is built — activity or vocabulary first, bilingual terms, worked examples

In the Grade 11 book the bilingual presentation is at its strongest: Arabic text carries English chapter and section titles printed alongside throughout, so the learner reads every heading in both languages. Chapters open with their vocabulary (مفردات الفصل) or an opening experiment — Oersted's 1820 experiment opens the magnetism chapter — and worked examples carry the teaching.

## Sub lesson 3 — How every chapter closes — vocabulary review and a chapter test

The Grade 11 book closes each chapter with its vocabulary/concept review, exercises and a chapter test of short-answer and multiple-choice questions; the whole book's Chapter 10 exercises run to the last page.

## The key ideas

- The Iraqi Grade 11 book is organised as 10 chapters.
- Every Iraqi lesson opens with a hands-on activity or a chapter vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), and technical terms are printed in Arabic and English side by side.
- Worked examples carry the teaching, and every chapter closes with a vocabulary/concept review plus short-answer and multiple-choice tests.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole lesson in order, in your own words — start with the opening activity, then take each sub lesson in turn and finish with what it is used for. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
