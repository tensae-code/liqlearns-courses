# Molar heat capacities of gases — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Two moles of gas with Cv = 20 J/(mol K) warm by 15 K at constant volume. Find the heat absorbed and the change in internal energy.
**Answer: Q = n Cv ΔT = 2 × 20 × 15 = 600 J. At constant volume no work is done, so ΔU = Q = 600 J.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Derive Cp − Cv = R in two lines for one mole warmed by one kelvin.
**Answer: At constant pressure the heat pays for ΔU plus expansion work: Qp = ΔU + p ΔV. With ΔU = Cv × 1 (one mole, one kelvin) and p ΔV = R ΔT = R from the ideal-gas law, Cp = Cv + R, so Cp − Cv = R.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a diatomic gas have a larger Cv than a monatomic gas?
**Answer: Its molecules store energy in more ways: besides translation they rotate (and at higher temperatures vibrate). More stores to fill per kelvin means more heat per mole per kelvin — about 5⁄2 R against the monatomic 3⁄2 R.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A monatomic gas has Cv = 3⁄2 R. Give Cp and γ.
**Answer: Cp = Cv + R = 5⁄2 R ≈ 20.8 J/(mol K); γ = Cp/Cv = (5⁄2)/(3⁄2) = 5⁄3 ≈ 1.67.**
