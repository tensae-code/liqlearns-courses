# Lesson plan — Storing magnets and keepers

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Storing magnets and keepers
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), at the close of the Magnetism topic of South Sudan's Secondary 2 book — after Magnetisation and demagnetisation
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a magnet left carelessly loses its strength: stored in pairs with unlike poles together and soft-iron keepers across the ends — or, for a horseshoe magnet, one keeper across both poles — its magnetism is locked into a closed circuit and kept

## Sub lessons
1. **Why stored magnets weaken** — South Sudan's Secondary 2 Magnetism topic ends with a named sub-topic most courses skip: **methods of storing magnets** (Topic 10.9). A magnet is not permanent by magic — its strength leaks away when its domains are shaken out of line. Heat, hammering and dropping all demagnetise, and so does **time spent with its field open to the air**: a lone bar magnet's own field works against its domains day and night (its poles repel their own pattern back through the bar — self-demagnetisation), and a drawer of magnets thrown together any way they fall will weaken and scramble each other. Storage is therefore a physics problem with a designed answer, and the answer is to give the magnetism a closed iron path to run in.
2. **Bar magnets in pairs, with keepers** — Bar magnets are stored **in pairs, side by side, with unlike poles together** — the north pole of one beside the south pole of the other — and a piece of **soft iron called a keeper** is laid across each end, touching both magnets at once. The keepers complete a **closed magnetic circuit**: field leaves a north pole, runs through the keeper into the neighbouring south pole, through that magnet, out its far pole, through the second keeper and home — never travelling through air, never working against the domains. Soft iron is used because it magnetises easily in the field and loses its magnetism the moment it is removed — it is a path, never a rival magnet. Stored this way, a pair of bar magnets keeps its strength for years.
3. **Horseshoe magnets and everyday care** — A **horseshoe (U-shaped) magnet** needs only **one keeper**, laid straight across its two poles — the U already brings its poles close, and the single keeper closes the circuit the same way. The rest of Topic 10.9 is care that follows from the domain picture: store magnets **away from heat** (a hot magnet's domains shake loose), **do not drop or hammer them**, keep them **dry** against rust, and keep them away from things their fields would harm or that would scramble them — watches, compasses in store, and other magnets stored pole-to-like-pole. South Sudan examines this: a learner who can magnetise (Topic 10.7) is expected to be able to keep what was made.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why a carelessly stored magnet weakens (self-demagnetisation, heat, knocks).
2. Describe and draw the pair-and-keepers storage of bar magnets and say what the keeper does.
3. State the storage of a horseshoe magnet and the everyday care rules for all magnets.

## What you need
Two bar magnets, two soft-iron keepers, a horseshoe magnet if the kit has one, and paper clips for strength tests: the class stores the kit properly at the end and tests before and after.
