# Storing magnets and keepers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in domain or field terms, why a keeper keeps a magnet strong.
**Answer: The keeper closes the magnetic circuit: the field runs through iron from pole to pole instead of through air, so it no longer works back through the bar against its own domains — the domain pattern, and with it the strength, is held.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why soft iron and not steel for a keeper?
**Answer: Soft iron magnetises easily in the stored magnets' field, carrying it while stored, and loses its magnetism as soon as it is removed — it is a path for the field, never a rival magnet; steel would keep magnetism of its own.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State the storage rule for a horseshoe magnet and why one keeper is enough.
**Answer: One keeper straight across both poles: the U-shape already brings its poles close, so a single keeper closes the magnetic circuit the same way a pair of keepers does for two bar magnets.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) A kit box holds magnets thrown in any way, in a hot store room, and they are dropped often. Name the three faults and the damage each does.
**Answer: Any-way storage lets magnets weaken and scramble each other (like poles forced together, fields open to air); heat shakes domains out of line; dropping/hammering does the same mechanically — all three demagnetise.**
