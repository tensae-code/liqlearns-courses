# Storing magnets and keepers

## Do it first
Before you read: take two bar magnets from the school kit — if the kit has them — and try to guess, from how they pull, whether they are as strong as they should be. Then look at how they were lying in their box. This lesson is about that box: how magnets should be put away so that next term's class finds them still strong.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why stored magnets weaken

A magnet weakens in store when its domains are disturbed — by heat, knocks, and by its own field acting back through the bar when the field is left open to the air. Magnets thrown together carelessly weaken each other. Good storage is designed against all three: keep the field **closed inside iron**, keep unlike poles together so neighbouring magnets hold each other's pattern, and keep magnets away from heat and hard treatment. Topic 10.9 of South Sudan's book teaches storage as the direct partner of magnetisation: what one sub-topic makes, the other keeps.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bar magnets in pairs, with keepers

The keeper method in one picture: two bar magnets side by side, **unlike poles together**; a **soft-iron keeper across each end**, touching both magnets; the field running in a closed loop through magnets and keepers instead of through air. The closed circuit stops self-demagnetisation, the unlike-pole pairing makes each magnet hold the other's domains in line, and the soft iron is chosen because it carries the field while stored and lets go cleanly when the magnets are taken out for use.

## Sub lesson 3 — Horseshoe magnets and everyday care

One keeper across both poles stores a horseshoe magnet. Around that rule sits the everyday care the domain picture predicts: no heat, no hammering or dropping, no damp, and no like-pole-to-like-pole storage. Keeping is half of magnetism in South Sudan's course — magnetisation (Topic 10.7) makes the magnet, demagnetisation undoes it, and storage (Topic 10.9) is the deliberate holding of the line between them.

## The key ideas

- Magnets weaken in store by self-demagnetisation, heat, knocks and careless pole-to-pole storage — storage is a designed answer, not tidiness
- Bar magnets: store in pairs with unlike poles together and a soft-iron keeper across each end, closing the magnetic circuit
- A keeper is soft iron: it carries the field while stored and loses its magnetism when removed
- A horseshoe magnet takes one keeper across both poles; all magnets want cool, dry, knock-free storage

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: Magnets left open to the air weaken themselves. Store bar magnets in pairs, unlike poles together, with soft-iron keepers across both ends so the field runs in a closed circuit; give a horseshoe magnet one keeper across its poles; and keep every magnet cool, dry and free of knocks. That is how strength is kept. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.
