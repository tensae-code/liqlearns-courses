# Practical — find the refractive index with Snell's law — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) A ray meets glass at 50° and refracts at 31°. Find n (sin 50° ≈ 0.766, sin 31° ≈ 0.515).
**Answer: n = sin i / sin r ≈ 0.766 / 0.515 ≈ 1.49 — the glass value, within practical error of 1.5.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the pins be vertical and the ray line thin?
**Answer: A leaning pin or thick line shifts the marked ray path, so the measured angles — and with them n — are systematically off; vertical pins and a fine line keep the traced ray the true ray.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A learner gets n = 1.2 for glass. Give two likely causes.
**Answer: Angles measured from the surface instead of the normal (which swaps the geometry), or a misjoined ray inside the block / misread protractor — either spoils sin i / sin r.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the practical report a range rather than one exact n?
**Answer: Every angle is read to finite precision, so each computed n scatters; the honest result is the average with its spread, not a single pretend-exact figure.**
