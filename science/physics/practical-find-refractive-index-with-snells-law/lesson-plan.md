## Sub lessons
1. **Trace the ray — the block, the pins and the normal** — The glass block sits on paper and its outline is drawn. A ray is sent at the first face — fixed by two pins or a ray box — and the emergent ray is marked the same way. Joining the marks inside the block gives the refracted ray; the normal is drawn at the entry point, and the angles of incidence i and refraction r are measured from the normal, never from the surface.
2. **Compute n — and repeat for other angles** — Snell's law, n = sin i / sin r, turns each pair of angles into a value of the refractive index. One pair can be spoiled by one misread degree, so the practical repeats the measurement at several angles of incidence and averages the values of n — for glass the answer should land near 1.5. A graph of sin i against sin r makes the whole set speak at once: its slope is n.
3. **Judge the result — errors and the honest range** — The measured n is compared with the accepted value and the difference is explained, not hidden: pins not vertical, a thick ray line, angles read to the nearest degree. The practical's lesson is the method itself — a constant of the material recovered from angles any learner can measure, with a stated range instead of a pretend-exact number.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Trace the ray through the block and draw the normal at the entry point
2. Measure i and r from the normal, never from the surface
3. Snell's law gives the refractive index: n = sin i / sin r
4. Repeat at several angles and average — or take the slope of sin i against sin r
5. For glass, n should land near 1.5; state the range and the error sources
