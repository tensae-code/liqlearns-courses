# The electromagnetic spectrum — uses and dangers

## Do it first
Lay out seven objects or cards — radio, phone, stove heat, lamp, sun, clinic, the sky's most violent events — and order them by the spectrum before checking.

[Picture 21928 — spectrum as one long strip, wavelength shrinking left to right, uses and dangers under each band]

## The electromagnetic spectrum — uses and dangers — the teaching
One family, ordered by frequency, wavelength and energy: radio waves (longest wavelength), then microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays (shortest, most energetic). Each band has its work — radio carries broadcasts, microwaves cook and communicate, infrared is felt as heat, ultraviolet tans and sterilises, X-rays image bones, gamma rays treat and endanger. The rule of dangers: the higher the frequency, the deeper the harm it can do to living tissue.

## The key ideas

- Order by shrinking wavelength: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
- Higher frequency means higher energy and greater danger to tissue.
- Each band has named uses: broadcasts, heat, imaging, sterilising.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the electromagnetic spectrum — uses and dangers to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
