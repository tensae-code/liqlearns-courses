# Wheel and axle

## Do it first
Wind a string around a pencil (axle) using a bottle cap as the wheel and lift a small weight. Then build the same from local materials as the book directs.

[Picture 21846 — windlass drawing water from a well, wheel and axle labelled]

## Wheel and axle — the teaching
A wheel and axle is a large wheel fixed to a smaller axle, turning together. Put the effort on the wheel and the axle winds or lifts with multiplied force — screwdriver, windmill, doorknob, and the windlass that draws water from a well. Put the effort on the axle instead and the wheel's rim travels fast — the speed-multiplying arrangement in bicycles, car wheels and fans. The ratio of wheel radius to axle radius sets the trade.

## The key ideas

- Wheel and axle: a large wheel fixed to a smaller axle, turning together.
- Effort on the wheel multiplies force (windlass).
- Effort on the axle multiplies speed (bicycle wheel).

## Say it to your companion
Explain wheel and axle to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
