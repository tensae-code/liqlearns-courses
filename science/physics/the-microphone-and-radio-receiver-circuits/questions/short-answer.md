# The microphone and radio receiver circuits — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) In what sense is a loudspeaker a microphone in reverse?
**Answer: Both use a coil in a magnetic field joined to a diaphragm: in the microphone, sound moves the diaphragm and coil and induction makes a varying current; in the loudspeaker, a varying current drives the coil and diaphragm and makes sound — the same parts, with energy flowing the opposite way.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must selection come before detection in the receiver chain?
**Answer: If all arriving carriers were detected together, their audio patterns would mix into an unusable jumble; the resonance circuit first isolates one carrier in strength, so the detector has a single station's pattern to recover.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) After detection the signal is correct in pattern but very weak. What stage fixes this, and why is it placed after the detector rather than before?
**Answer: The amplifier. Amplifying after detection strengthens only the wanted audio; amplifying everything at the aerial would strengthen all stations and the noise with them, before any selection had been made.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A variable capacitor is set to a smaller capacitance. What happens to the circuit's resonant frequency and to the station heard?
**Answer: The resonant frequency rises (f depends on 1/√(LC)), so the tuning slides up the band and a station with a higher carrier frequency is selected instead.**
