# How this Physics course works — the Syrian Grade 8 way

## Do it first
Before you read: open any lesson of a course you know and ask — could you tell, before starting, exactly what you would be able to do at the end, and which words you would own? The Syrian books answer both questions on the first page of every lesson. This lesson teaches that shape, so the whole book becomes predictable: you always know where the objectives are, where the experiment is, and where you write your own conclusion.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book's shape — the units

The Grade 8 book is a combined Physics & Chemistry volume in four units, with physics and chemistry alternating by unit: **U1 is structural chemistry, then U2 Motion & forces, U3 Electricity and U4 Light are physics**. The design is the same as Grade 7's — objectives, key words, experiment-first lessons — now carrying the learner from forces through circuits to light in one year's journey.

## Sub lesson 2 — How every lesson is built — objectives, key words, "I try and I conclude"

Every lesson opens with printed **objectives** (الأهداف) — the exact things the learner will be able to do — and **key words** (الكلمات المفتاحية), the lesson's vocabulary named up front. The teaching itself is built around **"I try and I conclude"** (أجرّب وأستنتج): a bench experiment the learner runs with their own hands, followed by an explicit **"I conclude"** (أستنتج) box where the lesson's law is stated as the learner's own finding, not handed down. Worked applications (تطبيق محلول) then show the concluded idea solving a real problem step by step, and enrichment boxes (إثراء) stretch it for learners who finish early. Every lesson in the book opens the same way, and learning the shape once unlocks every lesson that follows.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit endings — questions and a project

Each unit closes with **unit questions** that test the whole unit together, and a **hands-on project** (مشروع) — something real to design and build that uses the unit's physics: the electricity unit closes with a physics project on the effect of resistance value on electrical-energy consumption — the unit's law tested against a real household cost. The project is not decoration: it is where the unit's separate conclusions have to work together in one made thing, and it is the book's proof that the physics was learned by doing.

## The key ideas

- Every Syrian lesson opens with printed objectives and key words — you know the target and the vocabulary before you start.
- The heart of every lesson is "I try and I conclude": run the bench experiment yourself, then state the law in the "I conclude" box as your own finding.
- Worked applications show the concluded idea solving a real problem; enrichment boxes stretch it further.
- Every unit closes with unit questions plus a hands-on project — a real thing built with the unit's physics.

## Do It Yourself
1. Pick any physics topic you know. Write its objectives list the Syrian way: three things a learner will be able to DO at the end.
2. For the same topic, name its key words — at most six — and say why each one earns its place.
3. Design the "I try and I conclude" experiment for that topic: what does the learner try, what do they watch, and what sentence lands in the "I conclude" box?
