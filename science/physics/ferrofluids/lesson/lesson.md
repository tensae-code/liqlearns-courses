# Ferrofluids

## Do it first
Ferrofluid itself is a bought material (handle per its safety sheet, keep off skin and clothes) — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — A liquid full of magnets

A ferrofluid is a stable suspension of nanoscale iron-oxide particles, each coated so they cannot clump, in a carrier oil or water. The particles are so small that thermal motion keeps them spread; the fluid flows like a liquid while every drop of it is magnetic.

## Sub lesson 2 — Spikes along the field

Bring a magnet close and the fluid is pulled toward the stronger field and heaps into spiked patterns that trace the field's shape — the normal-field instability: the surface breaks into a hedgehog of points aligned with the field lines. Remove the magnet and the spikes melt away; the magnetisation is temporary, like a soft magnetic material's.

## Sub lesson 3 — Uses — seals, bearings and speakers

Because a magnet can hold the fluid in place, ferrofluids seal rotating shafts against dust and vacuum (held in the gap by a magnet), centre loudspeaker voice coils while carrying their heat away, and serve in sensors and damping. PECTAA places them in electromagnetism as the chapter's living example: magnetism acting on a material that flows.
