# Ferrofluids — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A ferrofluid is a stable suspension of nanoscale iron-oxide particles, each coated so they cannot clump, in a carrier oil or water; Bring a magnet close and the fluid is pulled toward the stronger field and heaps into spiked patterns that trace the field's shape — the normal-field instability: the surface breaks into a hedgehog of points aligned with the field lines; Because a magnet can hold the fluid in place, ferrofluids seal rotating shafts against dust and vacuum (held in the gap by a magnet), centre loudspeaker voice coils while carrying their heat away, and serve in sensors and damping — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
