# Energy bands in solid crystals

## Do it first
Before you read: picture a staircase packed shoulder-to-shoulder with people, every step full. Nobody can move up a step — the crowd is frozen, however hard it is pushed. Now empty the top half of the stairs: everyone can shuffle up freely. A crystal's electrons live on such staircases — energy bands — and whether the top staircase is full or half-empty decides if the solid conducts. This lesson builds the staircases.

## Sub lesson 1 — From levels to bands — what closeness does

Two bands earn names: the highest band that is (nearly) full at low temperature is the **valence band**; the next band above it is the **conduction band**; between them may lie a **forbidden gap** — energies no electron in the crystal may have.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why full bands cannot conduct

So conductivity is decided by filling and gaps, not by the electrons' number alone: copper's conduction band is half-filled — a conductor; diamond's valence band is full with a wide forbidden gap above — a perfect insulator built of the same column's cousin chemistry.

## Sub lesson 3 — The gap sorts the solids — and semiconductors sit in the middle

That middle kingdom — gap small enough to engineer — is where diodes, transistors and solar cells live. Vietnam's capstone placement is the topic's point made whole: spectra gave the atom's levels, duality and the photoelectric effect gave light's particles, and the bands explain the material every modern circuit is carved from.

## The key ideas

- In a crystal, atomic energy levels split and merge into continuous energy bands; electrons fill them from the bottom
- A full band cannot conduct — no empty states for its electrons to move into; a partly filled band conducts freely
- The forbidden gap between valence and conduction bands sorts solids: conductor (no gap/partly filled), semiconductor (small gap, Si ≈ 1.1 eV), insulator (large gap)
- In a semiconductor, heat or light promotes electrons across the small gap, leaving holes; conductivity rises with temperature — the reverse of a metal

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: In a solid crystal, atoms sit so close that their sharp energy levels merge into continuous energy bands: a band that is full cannot carry current (its electrons have no empty state to move into), a partly filled band conducts freely, and the size of the forbidden gap between the valence band and the conduction band sorts every solid into conductor (no gap or overlapping bands), semiconductor (small gap, ~1 eV) or insulator (large gap) — the band picture behind all of electronics.
