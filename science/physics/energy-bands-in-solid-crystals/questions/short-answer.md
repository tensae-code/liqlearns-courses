# Energy bands in solid crystals — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the band picture replace 'free electrons' as the explanation of conduction?
**Answer: Because it also explains the non-conductors with the same machinery: conduction depends on whether electrons have empty states to move into — a filling-and-gap question — which sorts metals, semiconductors and insulators in one scheme, where 'free electrons' only describes the metals.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student says an insulator has no electrons in its conduction band 'because it has fewer electrons'. Correct the statement.
**Answer: Insulators can have plenty of electrons — their valence bands are full. The conduction band is empty because the forbidden gap is too wide for thermal energy to promote electrons across it; the count is not the issue, the gap is.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What is a hole, and why does it behave like a positive carrier?
**Answer: A hole is an empty state in the valence band left by a promoted electron. Neighbouring electrons step into it one by one, so the emptiness migrates opposite to their stepping — a moving positive charge in effect, and it contributes to the current.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the small-gap middle case the one electronics is built from?
**Answer: Because a small gap can be engineered: heat, light and doping move carriers across it in controllable numbers, so conductivity can be switched and modulated — a conductor cannot be switched off and an insulator cannot be switched on, but a semiconductor can be both.**
