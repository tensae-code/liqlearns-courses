# Lesson plan — Energy bands in solid crystals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Energy bands in solid crystals
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — the capstone of Vietnam's Grade 12 study-topics quantum topic: band theory, the solid-state sequel to Line spectra of atoms
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in a solid crystal, atoms sit so close that their sharp energy levels merge into continuous energy bands: a band that is full cannot carry current (its electrons have no empty state to move into), a partly filled band conducts freely, and the size of the forbidden gap between the valence band and the conduction band sorts every solid into conductor (no gap or overlapping bands), semiconductor (small gap, ~1 eV) or insulator (large gap) — the band picture behind all of electronics

## Sub lessons
1. **From levels to bands — what closeness does** — An isolated atom's electrons live on sharp **energy levels** — the ladder whose rungs the atomic-spectra lesson reads. Bring Avogadro-scale crowds of atoms into a crystal, each atom's levels perturbed by its neighbours, and every sharp level **splits into a crowd of closely spaced levels — a band**. The electrons fill the bands from the bottom up, two per state (Pauli), exactly as they fill shells in one atom — but now the useful question is not which rung an electron sits on, it is whether the band it tops is **full or partly filled**, and what lies above it. Vietnam's Grade 12 study-topics book ends its quantum topic here (Bài 12, Vùng năng lượng của tinh thể chất rắn — 'energy bands of solid crystals'): the atom's ladder, widened into the solid's landscape.
2. **Why full bands cannot conduct** — Current needs electrons that can **gain a little energy** from a field and drift — but an electron can only gain energy by moving into an **empty state** just above it. In a **completely full band** there are no empty states within reach: the field pushes, nobody can move up, and the band as a whole does not conduct — every electron's attempted drift is cancelled by the filled arrangement. In a **partly filled band**, empty states sit immediately above the filled ones: electrons step up freely, drift, and the solid conducts like a metal.
3. **The gap sorts the solids — and semiconductors sit in the middle** — Read the gap and you can sort the solid world. **Conductors**: bands overlap or the top band is partly filled — no gap to cross. **Insulators**: full valence band, **large gap** (several eV — diamond ≈ 5.5 eV): room-temperature thermal energy (~0.025 eV) can promote essentially nobody. **Semiconductors**: full valence band, **small gap** — silicon ≈ 1.1 eV, germanium ≈ 0.7 eV: heat or light promotes a few electrons into the conduction band, leaving **holes** in the valence band that also carry current — and because warmth promotes more, a semiconductor's conductivity **rises with temperature**, the opposite of a metal's, and doping can stock either band with carriers on purpose.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain how bands form from atomic levels in a crystal.
2. State why a full band cannot carry current.
3. Classify a solid from its band diagram and gap size, with silicon and diamond as examples.
4. Explain holes and why a semiconductor's conductivity rises with temperature.
