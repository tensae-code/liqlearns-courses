# Chemical cells — the simple cell and the dry cell — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Describe the simple cell — plates, electrolyte, terminals — in two sentences.
**Answer: Zinc and copper plates stand in dilute sulphuric acid (the electrolyte); chemical action makes the copper the positive terminal and the zinc the negative, giving about 1.1 V between them.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A simple cell's current fades, rests, and revives. Name the fault and explain both the fading and the revival.
**Answer: Polarisation: hydrogen bubbles blanket the copper plate and oppose the cell, so the current fades; at rest the bubbles disperse (or are stirred/brushed away), so the cell revives.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the dry cell called dry when it contains a paste?
**Answer: Its electrolyte is ammonium chloride as a paste, not a free liquid — nothing can spill, so the cell works in any position and can be carried.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can a dry cell never be recharged, and what finally ends its life?
**Answer: It is a primary cell: its chemistry runs one way, and its zinc case — the negative terminal — is eaten away by the cell's own action until the cell is spent (and may leak).**
