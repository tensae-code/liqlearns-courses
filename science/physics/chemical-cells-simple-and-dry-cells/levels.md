# Chemical cells — the simple cell and the dry cell — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A chemical cell turns chemical energy into electrical energy; a simple cell is zinc and copper plates in dilute sulphuric acid, giving about 1.1 V with copper positive; Polarisation: hydrogen bubbles blanket the copper plate and the current fades — cured by rest, stirring, or a depolariser; Local action: impurities in the zinc eat it away in tiny surface cells even when idle — cured by pure or amalgamated zinc; The dry cell: zinc case (negative), carbon rod (positive), ammonium chloride paste electrolyte, manganese dioxide depolariser — about 1.5 V, portable and primary — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
