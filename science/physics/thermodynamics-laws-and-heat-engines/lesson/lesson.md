# Thermodynamics — the laws and heat engines

## Do it first
Before you read: take a bicycle pump, seal its outlet with a finger, and pump hard twenty times. Now feel the barrel near the outlet. It is warm — and nothing burned, and no battery was near it. Write down — before reading on — where you think that warmth came from. Then read on: the answer is the first of the laws that run every engine, every fridge and every power station on Earth.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first law — energy is conserved, heat and work are its two doors

Inside any gas there is **internal energy** — the jiggling energy of its particles. That energy can change in exactly two ways. Heat can flow in or out (call it **Q**), and work can be done — by the gas pushing something out of its way, or on the gas squeezing it smaller (call it **W**). The **first law of thermodynamics** is the bookkeeping rule that joins them:

**Change in internal energy = heat added − work done by the system: ΔU = Q − W.**

That is all it says — and it says everything. Energy is never created and never destroyed; it only changes its account. Your pump barrel warmed because the work you did squeezing the air (work done *on* the gas, so W is negative in the formula's terms) raised the air's internal energy, and the warm air warmed the barrel. A gas that expands and pushes a piston gives up internal energy as work — unless heat flows in to replace it. Every joule is accounted for. There is no free energy, anywhere, ever: the first law is conservation of energy wearing its heat-and-work clothes.

## Sub lesson 2 — The second law — heat runs one way

The first law allows anything that balances the books — even heat flowing from a cold cup into a hot one, which would still conserve energy. Nature forbids it anyway. The **second law of thermodynamics**: **heat flows by itself from hot to cold, never the reverse** — and, in its wider form, the total **entropy** (disorder, spreading-out) of the universe never decreases. Processes run one way: ink spreads in water and never gathers back into the drop; a hot spoon cools and never reheats itself from the cold table.

The second law is also the law that humbles every engine: **no heat engine can turn all the heat it takes in into work** — some heat must always be rejected to a colder place. And it explains the refrigerator: a fridge moves heat from cold (inside) to hot (the kitchen), which never happens by itself, so work must be paid in — the plug, the motor, the electricity bill are the second law being obeyed, not beaten.

A third, quieter law completes the set: the **third law** — as a body is cooled toward **absolute zero** (−273.15 °C, 0 K), it can be brought closer and closer, but absolute zero itself can never quite be reached in any finite number of steps.

## Sub lesson 3 — Heat engines — turning heat into work

A **heat engine** is any machine that runs in a cycle between a **hot source** and a **cold sink**: it takes in heat from the hot side, turns **part** of it into work, and **rejects the rest** to the cold side. Steam engine, petrol engine, diesel, the turbines of a power station — all are this one shape. In the steam engine, burning fuel boils water (hot source); the steam expands and pushes the piston (work); the spent steam condenses in the cold condenser (heat rejected). In a car, the burning fuel-air mixture is the hot source, the pistons take the work, and the radiator and exhaust throw the rejected heat away — look at any car and you can point at its cold sink.

How good an engine is, is measured by its **efficiency: work out ÷ heat in**. Take in 100 J of heat, do 25 J of work, reject 75 J — the efficiency is 25%. The second law caps even a perfect, frictionless engine: the best possible efficiency is set only by the two temperatures (in kelvin), **efficiency max = 1 − T_cold/T_hot** — Carnot's limit. Hotter fire, colder sink, better cap; that is why power stations run their steam as hot as their metals can bear. No inventor, however clever, can pass that cap — the laws of thermodynamics are the one set of rules with no exceptions found anywhere in the universe.

## The key ideas

- **First law:** ΔU = Q − W — internal energy changes only by heat flowing and work being done; energy is conserved, always.
- **Second law:** heat flows hot to cold by itself; entropy never decreases overall; no engine can turn all its heat into work.
- **Third law:** absolute zero (0 K) can be approached but never reached.
- A **heat engine** cycles between a hot source and a cold sink: heat in, part becomes work, the rest is rejected.
- **Efficiency = work out ÷ heat in**, capped by Carnot's limit, 1 − T_cold/T_hot — hotter source and colder sink raise the cap.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the laws of thermodynamics to your companion in your own words: the first law as bookkeeping, the second law as the one-way rule, and then walk them around a real car or fridge, pointing at the hot source, the work, and the cold sink. If your companion can say why no engine can ever be 100% efficient, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A gas takes in 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work pushing a piston. What is the change in its internal energy? Then the same gas is squeezed: 150 J of work is done on it and no heat flows. What happens to its internal energy now?
2. A friend claims their new engine turns 100% of its fuel's heat into motion, wasting nothing. Which law says this is impossible, and what must every real engine have that theirs is missing?
3. Teach the picture: explain to someone who has not seen this lesson why a refrigerator must be plugged in — and why leaving its door open cannot cool the kitchen.
