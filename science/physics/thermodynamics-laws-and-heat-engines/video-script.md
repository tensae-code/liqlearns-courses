Today: **Thermodynamics — the laws and heat engines**.

Heat has laws, and nothing breaks them. First law: energy is never made and never lost — it only moves. Heat going in and work coming out must balance, always.
Second law: heat runs downhill, from hot to cold, all by itself — and because of that, no engine can turn all of its heat into work. Some heat must always be thrown away to the cold side. That is not bad engineering. That is the law.
And that is exactly what a heat engine is: it takes heat from something hot, turns part of it into work — a turning wheel, a moving car — and dumps the rest somewhere cold. Steam engine, car engine, power station: all the same shape.
