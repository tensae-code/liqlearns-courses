# Lesson plan — Thermodynamics — the laws and heat engines

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Thermodynamics — the laws and heat engines
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics — Heat neighbourhood, after Zeroth law of thermodynamics and temperature equilibrium and beside Steam and motion — heat engines and the Industrial Revolution (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** heat obeys laws as strict as any law of motion: energy is never created or destroyed, only moved (first law); heat flows from hot to cold and never wholly turns into work (second law); and every heat engine — steam, petrol, diesel — lives inside those two rules, taking heat from a hot place, turning part of it into work, and dumping the rest into a cold place.
**This lesson's own level:** it gathers the heat lessons into one statement of the laws — a learner can land here directly. The zeroth-law lesson and the steam-engine lesson are recommended first, never forced.

## Sub lessons
1. **The first law — energy is conserved, heat and work are its two doors** — a gas's internal energy changes only by heat flowing in or out and work done on or by it: ΔU = Q − W; nothing is created, nothing vanishes.
2. **The second law — heat runs one way** — heat flows hot to cold on its own, disorder (entropy) grows, and no engine can turn all its heat into work; the third law adds that absolute zero can be approached but never reached.
3. **Heat engines — turning heat into work** — an engine cycles between a hot source and a cold sink: it takes in heat, does work, and rejects the rest; its efficiency is work out ÷ heat in, capped by the temperatures of hot and cold.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the first law and use ΔU = Q − W on a simple case: given heat in and work out, find the change in internal energy.
2. State the second law two ways: heat flows hot to cold by itself; no heat engine can be 100% efficient.
3. Explain why a refrigerator needs a plug, in second-law terms.
4. Draw a heat engine as hot source → engine → cold sink with heat in, work out, heat rejected, and compute a simple efficiency.
5. Say what the third law claims about absolute zero.
6. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the pump barrel warmed because work done on the air became internal energy — that trade is the first law in the hand.
- Thinks an engine turns all its fuel heat into motion → the second law forbids it: some heat must be rejected to the cold side; the radiator and exhaust of every real car are that rejected heat leaving.
- Mixes up heat and temperature → heat is energy on the move; temperature is how hot a thing is. The laws of this lesson govern the moving energy.
