# Thermodynamics — the laws and heat engines — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Pumping a sealed bicycle pump warms its barrel. Explain with the first law.
**Answer: The work you do squeezing the air is work done on the gas, which raises its internal energy; the warmer air warms the barrel. No energy was created — your work changed its account, exactly as ΔU = Q − W requires.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A gas does 300 J of work while 300 J of heat flows in. What happens to its internal energy?
**Answer: Nothing — ΔU = 300 − 300 = 0. The heat flowing in exactly replaces the energy the gas spent doing work.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why can heat flowing from a cold drink into a warm hand never happen by itself, even though it would conserve energy?
**Answer: The first law would allow it, but the second law forbids it: heat flows by itself only from hot to cold; the reverse would decrease the universe's entropy.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the three parts of every heat engine's cycle and what travels between them.
**Answer: A hot source gives heat to the engine; the engine turns part of that heat into work; the rest of the heat is rejected to a cold sink. Steam engines, car engines and power-station turbines all have this shape.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) A power station raises its steam temperature while its cooling water stays the same. What happens to the best efficiency it could ever reach, and why?
**Answer: It rises. Carnot's limit, 1 − T_cold/T_hot, grows when T_hot grows (temperatures in kelvin), so a hotter source and colder sink raise the cap no engine can pass.**
