# Video script — Force on a current-carrying wire — the motor effect

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

A wire carries countless moving charges, so the field's force on them sums into a push on the whole wire — the motor effect.
A current-carrying conductor placed across a magnetic field is pushed sideways, the force growing with current, with the length of wire in the field, and with field strength (F = BIL in the perpendicular case), its direction set by the hand rule linking current, field and motion.
This push, harnessed, is every electric motor ever built.

Remember: **The motor effect: a current-carrying wire across a field is pushed.**
Remember: **Force grows with current, length in field and field strength — F = BIL (perpendicular).**
Remember: **Direction follows the hand rule for current, field and force.**
Keep the formula: **F = BIL**
