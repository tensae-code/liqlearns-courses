# Force on a current-carrying wire — the motor effect

## Do it first
If a battery, wire and magnet are available, rest the wire across the magnet's poles and close the circuit for an instant: the wire kicks. That kick is a motor clearing its throat.

[Picture 21920 — wire between magnet poles kicking sideways when current flows]

## Force on a current-carrying wire — the motor effect — the teaching
A wire carries countless moving charges, so the field's force on them sums into a push on the whole wire — the motor effect. A current-carrying conductor placed across a magnetic field is pushed sideways, the force growing with current, with the length of wire in the field, and with field strength (F = BIL in the perpendicular case), its direction set by the hand rule linking current, field and motion. This push, harnessed, is every electric motor ever built.

**Keep the formula:** F = BIL

## The key ideas

- The motor effect: a current-carrying wire across a field is pushed.
- Force grows with current, length in field and field strength — F = BIL (perpendicular).
- Direction follows the hand rule for current, field and force.

## Say it to your companion
Explain force on a current-carrying wire — the motor effect to your companion in your own words. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
