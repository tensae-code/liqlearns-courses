# Systems — internal and external forces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the boundary be held fixed while reasoning about a collision?
**Answer: Because the classification of every force — and therefore whether momentum is conserved — depends on it. Moving the boundary mid-argument silently moves forces between the internal and external ledgers and can turn a false claim into an apparent truth.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) An exploding shell's fragments fly apart with large kinetic energy, yet the total momentum equals the shell's momentum before the burst. Reconcile the two facts.
**Answer: The explosion's forces are internal to the shell system: they cancel in pairs in the momentum total, so total momentum is unchanged. But internal forces do work in separating the fragments, converting stored energy into kinetic energy — internal forces can change kinetic energy freely.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State the conservation of momentum with its system condition, as this lesson teaches it.
**Answer: For a chosen system, the total momentum remains constant if the net external impulse on the system is zero — in practice, if external forces are absent, balanced, or negligible during a brief event such as a collision.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) For the system 'a falling stone alone', is gravity internal or external, and what follows? Redraw the boundary to change the answer.
**Answer: External — the Earth is outside the boundary — so the stone's momentum grows under gravity's impulse. Include the Earth in the system and gravity becomes internal: stone-plus-Earth total momentum is conserved, the Earth recoiling imperceptibly.**
