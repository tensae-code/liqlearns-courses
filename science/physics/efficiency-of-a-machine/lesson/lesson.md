# Efficiency of a machine

## Do it first
Before you read: feed an engine a hundred joules of fuel and only about thirty turn the wheels; crank a stiff pulley system and half your work vanishes before the load moves. The missing energy is never destroyed — it is spent where you did not want it. Efficiency is the number that says how much of your input a machine actually delivers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Useful out of total in

A machine takes an **input** of work or energy and delivers a **useful output**; the rest is **wasted** — spent against friction, or carried away as heat, sound and vibration. The efficiency is the fraction that comes out useful: **efficiency = useful work (or energy) output ÷ total work (or energy) input**, quoted as a decimal or × 100 as a percentage. Run the machine steadily and the same ratio holds for power: efficiency = useful power out ÷ total power in. Conservation of energy is never broken — input = useful output + wasted output, always.

## Sub lesson 2 — Working with efficiency

The formula rearranges both ways: useful output = efficiency × input, and input needed = useful output ÷ efficiency. A motor of efficiency 0.75 fed with 400 J delivers 300 J of useful work; lifting a load needing 600 J of useful work with a system 60% efficient costs 600 ÷ 0.6 = 1,000 J of input. Percent and decimal forms must not be mixed inside one calculation — convert first. Efficiency also prices machines against each other: at the same job, the more efficient machine takes less input, less fuel and less money.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where the waste goes — and reducing it

In school machines the chief thief is **friction** — in bearings, ropes over pulleys, gears and pistons — turning useful work into heat; heat engines additionally must reject heat to a cold sink, which caps their efficiency well below 100% even with perfect workmanship. Waste is cut by lubrication, smoother and harder surfaces, rollers and bearings, and good alignment; it is never cut to zero. An efficiency above 100% is impossible — it would mean creating energy — so any calculation giving one has an error in it, and that check is part of using the idea honestly.

## The key ideas

- Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input (energy, work, or power over the same time) — a fraction, or × 100 as a percentage; always below 100%.
- Input = useful output + wasted output; useful output = efficiency × input; input needed = useful output ÷ efficiency.
- The waste is mostly friction turned to heat (plus heat rejected by engines); lubrication and bearings cut it, nothing removes it.

## Do It Yourself
1. A machine takes in 500 J and delivers 350 J of useful work. Find its efficiency as a percentage.
2. A hoist is 80% efficient. How much input work is needed to give a load 1,600 J of useful energy?
3. An engine's useful power out is 30 kW from a total power in of 120 kW. Find its efficiency, and the power wasted.
