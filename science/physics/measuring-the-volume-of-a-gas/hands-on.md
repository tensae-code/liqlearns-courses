# Hands-on — Measuring the volume of a gas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Blow up a balloon a little, tie it, and squeeze it gently: feel the air inside take the balloon's whole shape, and shrink when pressed. Then blow the balloon up more and feel it fill the bigger space — the same air, a new volume, because the container changed.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of measuring the volume of a gas — a gas has no volume of its own — it spreads out to fill the whole of whatever holds it, so the volume of a gas is the volume of its container. — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
