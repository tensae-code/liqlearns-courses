# Measuring the volume of a gas — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: a gas fills its container.
**Answer: Unlike a solid, which keeps its own shape and volume, and a liquid, which keeps its volume but takes its container's shape, a gas expands until it fills the whole space available to it. The volume of a gas sample is therefore the volume of the vessel that holds it — change the vessel and the gas's volume changes with it.**

**2.** Explain: you measure a gas by the space it fills.
**Answer: To find a gas's volume, trap it in a container of known volume — a gas syringe, a graduated vessel over water, or a bottle of known capacity — and read the volume of the space the gas occupies. That reading is the gas's volume, for that container, at that moment.**

**3.** Explain: pressing a gas changes its volume.
**Answer: Because gas particles are far apart, a gas can be compressed into a smaller volume and will spring back out when released. That is why the volume of a gas is always stated for the container and conditions it was measured in.**
