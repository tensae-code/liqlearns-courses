# Lesson plan — Signals — a message carried by sound, light or wire

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Signals — a message carried by sound, light or wire
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside Communication — radio, satellites and fiber optics: that lesson teaches the great systems; this one, at primary depth, teaches the thing they all carry — the signal itself
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a signal is a sign sent on purpose to carry a message from a sender (emitter) to a receiver: anything that can travel and be recognised can be a signal's carrier — sound (a whistle, a drum, a bell), light (a flare, a lighthouse, a traffic light) or electricity in a wire (a telephone, a doorbell) — and every signal chain has the same three parts: an emitter that sends, a carrier that travels, and a receiver that catches and understands
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Sound — vibration, volume and pitch is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **What a signal is — emitter, carrier, receiver** — a signal is a sign sent deliberately to carry a message across a distance: every signal has an emitter that produces it, a carrier that travels (sound through air, light through space, electricity through wire), and a receiver that picks it up and gets the message; if any of the three fails, no message arrives
2. **Signals with wires and without — sound, light, electricity** — signals ride three great carriers: sound (whistles, drums, bells, sirens — no wire, short range), light (flares, lighthouses, traffic lights, torches blinking code — no wire, as far as the light can be seen) and electricity along wires (telephone, doorbell, telegraph — the wire guides the signal exactly where it must go, over any distance, in any weather); distress signals — the flare, the whistle at sea, the SOS pattern — are signals designed to be unmissable when help is the message

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Sound — vibration, volume and pitch**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A signal is a sign sent on purpose to carry a message from an emitter to a receiver.
2. Say, in their own words: Every signal chain has three parts: emitter (sends), carrier (travels), receiver (catches and understands). Break one link and no message arrives.
3. Say, in their own words: Three great carriers: sound (whistle, drum, bell — hearing's range), light (flare, lighthouse, traffic light — sight's range), electricity in wires (telephone, doorbell — guided to one receiver, any distance).
4. Say, in their own words: Distress signals — flare, patterned whistle blasts, SOS — are built to be unmissable when the message is *help*.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A whistle or bell, a torch, two paper cups and a long string (a string telephone), and a friend at the other end of the playground.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the whistle stopped a whole game with no word and no touch. That travelling message is a signal; the lesson just names its parts.
- Thinks the message IS the sound → the sound is the carrier; the message is 'stop now'. The same message rides light in a flare and electricity in a doorbell.
- Thinks wires are always better → wires guide perfectly but must be laid; sound and light need no installation and a flare can be seen by every ship at once. Each carrier has its ground.
