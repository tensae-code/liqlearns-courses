# Signals — a message carried by sound, light or wire — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A doorbell rings in the kitchen when a visitor presses the button at the gate. Name the emitter, carrier and receiver.
**Answer: Emitter: the visitor pressing the button (the button/switch sends). Carrier: electricity travelling along the wire. Receiver: the bell in the kitchen, which turns the signal into sound the household understands as 'someone is at the gate'.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) The whistle sounds but a player with blocked ears does not stop. Which link of the chain failed, and what does that teach?
**Answer: The receiver link failed — the signal was emitted and carried, but never received. A signal chain only delivers its message when all three links work.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why were talking drums useful before telephones, and what limit did they have?
**Answer: Drums carry messages as sound over distances beyond shouting, village to village, with no wires to lay. Their limit is sound's own: they fade with distance and can be drowned by noise or blocked by terrain.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is a flare a better distress signal at sea at night than a whistle?
**Answer: Light travels as far as sight — kilometres on a dark sea — and a flare is unmissable against the night. A whistle's sound fades within hearing range and fights the noise of wind and waves.**
