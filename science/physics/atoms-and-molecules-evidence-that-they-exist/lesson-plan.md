# Lesson plan — Atoms and molecules — evidence that they exist

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Atoms and molecules — evidence that they exist
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the structure-of-matter strand — Armenia's Grade 7 §8, the phenomena that confirm atoms and molecules exist
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** atoms and molecules are far too small to see, yet the Grade 7 book does not ask for belief: it lines up the phenomena — diffusion, Brownian motion, dissolving, evaporation — that only moving invisible particles can explain, and the particles earn their place by explaining

## Sub lessons
1. **The atom idea — matter in tiny particles** — Break a substance finer and finer and the Armenian book stops the breaking at the atom: the smallest particle that still carries the substance's chemical identity. Atoms join into molecules — the smallest particle of a substance that keeps its ordinary properties. One molecule of water is still water; split it and you have gases, not water. Sizes are almost beyond picturing: a molecule is about a ten-millionth of a millimetre across.
2. **Molecules — atoms joined, substances built** — Atoms rarely travel alone: two or more joined make a molecule, and the molecule is the working particle of diffusion, pressure and temperature in this course. A substance's properties live at this level — whether its molecules are light or heavy, packed tight or flying free, is what later lessons on states of matter and temperature will keep using.
3. **The evidence — phenomena that confirm they exist** — §8's title carries its method: phenomena confirming their existence. A drop of ink spreads through still water by itself (diffusion); pollen grains in water jiggle without pause under the microscope (Brownian motion); sugar vanishes into tea yet its sweetness is everywhere (dissolving); a puddle dries though nobody lifts the water away (evaporation). No wind, no stirring, no hand — only ceaselessly moving particles explain all four. The particles are invisible; their doings are not.
