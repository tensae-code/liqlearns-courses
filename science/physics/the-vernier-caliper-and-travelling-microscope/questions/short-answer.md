# The vernier caliper and travelling microscope — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a vernier read finer than the main scale alone?
**Answer: Because its divisions differ from the main scale's by exactly the least count; sliding it makes exactly one division coincide, and that division's number counts the fraction of a main division that the eye could not judge directly.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student clamps the caliper hard onto a soft rubber ball and records the reading. Name the fault.
**Answer: The jaws compress the ball, so the reading is smaller than the ball's true diameter — a measurement (gross/systematic) error of technique; the jaws should just touch, without squeezing.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does zero error not matter when a travelling microscope measures a length?
**Answer: The length is the difference of two readings taken with the same instrument, so the same zero error sits inside both readings and cancels in the subtraction.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) State the three things a vernier caliper measures and the part used for each.
**Answer: Outside size with the large jaws, inside size with the small upper jaws, and depth with the thin rod that slides out of the end of the beam.**
