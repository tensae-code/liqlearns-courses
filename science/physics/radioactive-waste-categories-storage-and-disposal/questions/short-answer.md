# Radioactive waste — categories, storage and the long-term problem — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State the three categories of radioactive waste and one example of each.
**Answer: Low-activity (contaminated clothing, gloves, tools), medium/intermediate-activity (reactor parts, resins, sludges) and high-activity (spent reactor fuel and reprocessing liquids).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must high-activity waste be cooled when it is first removed from a reactor?
**Answer: Its radioactive decay still releases substantial heat; without cooling the fuel and its cladding would overheat and be damaged, so it cools in ponds or dry stores while its activity falls.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the layers of defence, in order, used for high-activity waste disposal.
**Answer: The waste form itself (glass or cement), the sealed corrosion-resistant canister, the engineered repository structures and backfill, and finally the deep stable rock — geology as the last barrier.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is Yucca Mountain mentioned in discussions of waste disposal?
**Answer: It is the deep geological repository site studied for decades in the United States — the standard example of burying high-activity waste in stable rock formations as the final barrier.**
