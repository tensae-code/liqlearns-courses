# Radioactive waste — categories, storage and the long-term problem — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Radioactive waste is sorted into categories mainly by…
**Answer: a) its activity — how strongly it radiates, and the heat it gives off  b) the country it comes from  c) the size of each piece  d) its colour**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Which of these is high-activity waste?
**Answer: a) spent reactor fuel  b) contaminated gloves  c) used medical syringes with tracer residue  d) workers' overalls**

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) Low-activity waste is usually managed by…
**Answer: a) compaction into drums and burial in shallow engineered sites, decaying to safe levels in years  b) cooling ponds for a century  c) vitrification in glass  d) deep burial in stable rock**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Vitrification means…
**Answer: a) setting high-activity waste into glass to make it a stable solid  b) cooling waste with water  c) burning waste at high temperature  d) diluting waste in the sea**

**5.** (Sub lesson 2) The agreed end point for high-activity waste is…
**Answer: a) deep geological disposal in stable rock, hundreds of metres down  b) storage in ordinary warehouses  c) launching it into the Sun  d) dumping in deep ocean trenches**

**6.** (Sub lesson 2) The long-term problem of radioactive waste is that…
**Answer: a) its danger lasts thousands of years, longer than any building, government or warning sign  b) it is too heavy to move  c) nobody can measure its activity  d) it becomes more radioactive over time**
