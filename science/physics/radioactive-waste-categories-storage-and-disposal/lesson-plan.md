# Lesson plan — Radioactive waste — categories, storage and the long-term problem

## Sub lessons
1. **The three categories — sorting waste by activity** — Waste is sorted by how radioactive it is. Low-activity waste — contaminated clothing, gloves, tools, medical items — is only mildly radioactive and decays to safe levels in years. Medium (intermediate) activity waste — reactor parts, resins, chemical sludges — needs shielding but gives off little heat. High-activity waste — above all spent reactor fuel and the liquids from reprocessing it — is fiercely radioactive and hot: it must be cooled, and it stays dangerous for thousands of years. North Macedonia's Grade 13 book (§5.8) teaches exactly these three categories.
2. **Storage and geological disposal — keeping it from the living world** — Because the danger outlives every building and every government, the method is layered barriers: waste is cooled in ponds, dried or set in glass (vitrification) or cement, sealed in corrosion-proof canisters, stored under watch — and for high-activity waste the agreed end point is deep geological disposal: a repository hundreds of metres down in stable rock, like the Yucca Mountain repository studied in the United States, where geology itself is the final barrier. The long-term problem is not engineering alone: the waste must be kept from water, from earthquakes and from future people who may dig without knowing what is there — for longer than any language or warning sign has ever lasted.
