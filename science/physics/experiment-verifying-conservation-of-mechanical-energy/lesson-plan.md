# Lesson plan — Experiment — verifying conservation of mechanical energy

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Experiment — verifying conservation of mechanical energy
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), in the energy strand of Compulsory Book 2 — the experiment that closes the conservation chapter
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the conservation law is put on trial in the lab: let a weight fall, driving a ticker timer, and check at several points of the fall whether the **kinetic energy gained** matches the **potential energy lost** (½mv² against mgh) — within the experiment's errors, it does, and accounting for the small shortfall is part of the verdict

## Sub lessons
1. **The apparatus — a falling weight and its paper tape** — A heavy weight hangs from a paper tape threaded through a ticker timer, held just above a cushion. Released, it falls, dragging the tape; the timer prints dots at equal time beats all the way down. The tape is the whole experiment in one strip: distances from the first dot give the heights fallen h at each marked point, and dot spacings around each point give the speed v there. Choose points well down the tape — the first dots crowd near the release, where the speeds are small and the spacings hardest to measure honestly — and measure the mass m once, carefully, since both energies carry it.
2. **The check — two columns that must agree** — For each chosen point compute the pair: potential energy lost since release, **mgh**, and kinetic energy gained, **½mv²** (the start being effectively from rest). Conservation predicts the columns match point by point — not only at the end of the fall but all the way down, which is the strong form of the test: energy is not merely book-kept overall, it is converted at the right rate throughout. Plot both against h on one graph if you want the verdict at a glance: two lines that lie on each other within the scatter of the measurements.
3. **The shortfall — errors named, not hidden** — In every real run the kinetic column sits a little **below** the potential column — a few percent missing. The experiment's honesty is in the accounting: the tape drags through the timer, the falling weight pushes air aside, the release may tremble — friction and air resistance convert a small share of the energy to heat, outside the mechanical account the law is about. So the result is stated as the Chinese book states it: **within the error the experiment allows, mechanical energy is conserved** — and the size of the shortfall is reported, not rounded away. A verification that hides its losses verifies nothing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A falling weight drags a ticker tape: dot positions give h fallen, dot spacings give v at each point
2. Conservation predicts ½mv² gained = mgh lost at every point of the fall, not only at the end
3. The kinetic column lands a few percent low: tape drag and air resistance convert a small share to heat
4. The honest verdict: within the experiment's error, mechanical energy is conserved — with the shortfall reported, not hidden
