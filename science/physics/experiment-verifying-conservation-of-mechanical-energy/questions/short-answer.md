# Experiment — verifying conservation of mechanical energy — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A 2.0 kg weight falls 0.80 m and reaches 3.8 m/s (g = 10). Run the check.
**Answer: mgh = 2 × 10 × 0.8 = 16 J. ½mv² = 1 × 3.8² = 14.4 J. Shortfall 1.6 J = 10% — the right size for tape drag in a school rig; conserved within error, with the loss named.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why test several points rather than only the bottom of the fall?
**Answer: One endpoint could agree by accident or by cancelling errors; point-by-point agreement shows the conversion happening at the right rate throughout the fall — the strong form of the law.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What single change most reduces the tape-drag share of the shortfall?
**Answer: Make the falling mass heavier relative to the drag (a heavier weight): the drag force stays similar while both energies scale with m, so the lost fraction shrinks. Accept also: align the timer so the tape runs freely.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State what happens to the “missing” energy — where does the shortfall actually go?
**Answer: It is not destroyed: friction in the timer and air resistance convert it to heat (and a whisper of sound) — outside the mechanical account, which is exactly why the mechanical account shows the small deficit.**
