# Hands-on — The electrification of Cuba — how a national grid was built, as physics

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

(1) Chain-tracing: draw the full chain from a power station to a lamp in a home — generator, step-up transformer, transmission line, substation, step-down transformer, service line — and label each link with the physics idea it uses (induction, AC, transformer turns ratio, I²R loss). (2) Loss arithmetic: a line of resistance 20 Ω carries 5 MW. Compute the current and the I²R loss at 25 kV and at 250 kV, and the percentage of the power lost each way. (3) Grid map: sketch your own region as the Cuban books sketch Cuba — mark where power is made, where the lines run, and where it is used; write one 'success' and one 'perspective' (a next improvement) for your map.
