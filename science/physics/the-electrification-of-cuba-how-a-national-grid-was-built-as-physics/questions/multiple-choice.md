# The electrification of Cuba — how a national grid was built, as physics — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Cuba's Grade 11 books place the electrification of Cuba…
**Answer: a) as the closing section of the alternating-current chapter  b) in the geography course only  c) as a footnote in the preface  d) in an appendix of tables**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) The physics idea that generates the grid's current is…
**Answer: a) electromagnetic induction  b) static electricity  c) the photoelectric effect  d) refraction**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Voltage is stepped up for long-distance transmission because…
**Answer: a) at the same power the current is smaller, and line losses grow as I²R  b) high voltage travels faster  c) wires are cheaper at high voltage  d) transformers only work one way**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) The three storeys of a national system are…
**Answer: a) generation, transmission, distribution  b) mining, refining, burning  c) daytime, evening, night  d) AC, DC, static**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Interconnection makes a grid more reliable because…
**Answer: a) every user draws on all stations, so one plant's failure is covered by the others  b) connected wires are thicker  c) electricity moves faster in a grid  d) stations stop needing maintenance**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) The 'perspectives' half of the older Cuban section is about…
**Answer: a) the forward look — losses to cut, sources to add, reach to extend  b) the history of the steam engine  c) foreign grids only  d) examination technique**
