# The electrification of Cuba — how a national grid was built, as physics — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can a national grid be taught inside a physics chapter? Give two load-bearing ideas.
**Answer: Induction generates its current and transformers (which need AC) set its voltages; Joule heating prices its losses — the grid is those ideas at national scale.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A line carries a fixed power. The voltage is multiplied by 10. What happens to the current and to the I²R loss?
**Answer: The current falls to a tenth; the loss, growing as I², falls to a hundredth.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two things an interconnected grid makes possible that isolated town generators do not.
**Answer: Any two of: users anywhere draw on all stations; maintenance rotates without cutting supply; a single plant failure is covered; new demand connects instead of building its own generator.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Where in the chain is voltage lowest, and why?
**Answer: At the user — homes receive the safe service voltage, stepped down in stages from the transmission voltage.**
