# Lesson plan — Bulk, intensive and extensive properties of matter

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Bulk, intensive and extensive properties of matter
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), as the opening frame of the Bulk Properties of Solids and Fluids topic that closes South Sudan's Secondary 3 book
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before a lump of matter can be studied it must be described: its bulk properties are the properties of the whole amount together, and they divide into extensive properties, which grow with the amount (mass, volume), and intensive properties, which do not (temperature, density, pressure) — the same however much you take

## Sub lessons
1. **Bulk properties — describing the whole amount** — South Sudan closes its Secondary 3 book with **bulk properties of solids and fluids** (Topic 8), and it opens the topic with definitions most courses assume: a **bulk property** is a property of a whole amount of matter taken together — the behaviour of the lump, the tankful, the room of air — as against the properties of its single particles. The topic that follows is built of them: the elasticity of a whole bar, the pressure at a depth in a whole fluid, flow in a whole stream. The frame is South Sudan's own, placed first so that solids and fluids can be studied with one vocabulary: take the amount as it comes, and ask which of its properties belong to the amount and which to the stuff.
2. **Extensive properties — they grow with the amount** — An **extensive property** scales with how much matter there is. Take twice the water and you take twice the **mass** and twice the **volume**; join two lumps and their masses, volumes and total internal energies simply add. Extensive properties are the bookkeeper's quantities: they measure the amount itself or ride along with it. Halve the sample and every extensive property halves with it — that halving test is the definition at work, and South Sudan's learners apply it property by property before the solids and fluids work begins.
3. **Intensive properties — the same however much you take** — An **intensive property** refuses the halving test: split the sample and it stands unchanged. A cup drawn from a tank of water has the tank's **temperature**, its **density**, its taste of salt; pressure at a point, refractive index, hardness and melting point likewise name the *stuff and its state*, not the size of the portion. Intensive properties are therefore the identifiers — the properties you measure on a spoonful to know the whole — and ratios of two extensive properties (density = mass ÷ volume) are intensive, which is why they appear in tables of materials. With the two families sorted, Topic 8 proceeds: elasticity and pressure are intensive-style descriptions of bulk behaviour, and the topic's solids and fluids are read through them.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a bulk property and say why Topic 8 opens with the frame.
2. Apply the splitting test to sort properties into extensive and intensive.
3. Explain why a ratio of extensive properties (density) is intensive and identifies a material.

## What you need
A container of warm water, thermometer, balance and measuring cylinders: the class halves a real amount and watches which numbers obey.
