# Bulk, intensive and extensive properties of matter

## Do it first
Before you read: picture a big tank of water at 40 °C. Now picture one cup drawn from it. The cup's water is 40 °C too — but it is certainly not the tank's mass. Some properties travelled with the cup unchanged and some shrank with the portion. This lesson sorts properties by exactly that test.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bulk properties — describing the whole amount

A **bulk property** describes the whole amount at once — the bar, the tank, the stream — not one particle of it. Topic 8 of South Sudan's Secondary 3 book is a bulk-properties topic from end to end (elastic solids, fluids under pressure, fluids in motion), and it teaches its title first: before asking how the bulk behaves, sort the words you will describe it with.

## Sub lesson 2 — Extensive properties — they grow with the amount

**Extensive**: doubles when the amount doubles, halves when it halves. Mass, volume, length, total internal energy, total charge. These properties belong to the *amount* — they are how the bulk is counted. If a property survives the halving test unchanged, it is not extensive — which is the bridge to the topic's second family.

## Sub lesson 3 — Intensive properties — the same however much you take

**Intensive**: unchanged by splitting — temperature, density, pressure, melting point. These belong to the *stuff*, not the portion, and they are how materials are identified and tabulated. Extensive ÷ extensive gives intensive (density is the type case). South Sudan's frame is now complete: bulk properties of the whole amount, sorted into those that count the amount and those that name the material — the vocabulary of the whole of Topic 8.

## The key ideas

- A bulk property describes a whole amount of matter taken together — the lump, the tankful, the stream
- Extensive properties grow with the amount and halve when it halves: mass, volume, length, total internal energy
- Intensive properties are unchanged by splitting: temperature, density, pressure, melting point
- A ratio of two extensive properties (density = mass/volume) is intensive — intensive properties identify the material

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: Bulk properties describe the whole amount of matter at once. Extensive ones — mass, volume — grow and shrink with the amount. Intensive ones — temperature, density, pressure — stay the same in a cup as in a tank, because they name the stuff, not the portion. Sort a property by the splitting test and you know which family it is in. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort into extensive and intensive, with the splitting test said aloud for each: mass, temperature, volume, density, pressure, melting point, total internal energy.
2. A block is cut into three equal pieces. For its mass, its density and its temperature, state each piece's share or value compared with the whole block.
3. Density is in the materials tables but mass is not. Explain, using the two families, why tables can list one and never the other.
