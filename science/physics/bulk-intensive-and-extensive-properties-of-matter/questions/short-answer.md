# Bulk, intensive and extensive properties of matter — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) Apply the splitting test: a bar's length, mass and hardness — sort each and say why.
**Answer: Length and mass are extensive — halving the bar halves both. Hardness is intensive — each half is exactly as hard as the whole bar; it names the material, not the amount.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can a small sample identify a material in a table of data, when a large one is no better?
**Answer: Tables list intensive properties (density, melting point), which are the same for any size of sample — a spoonful carries the material's identity as completely as a lorry-load.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) South Sudan's Topic 8 studies solids' elasticity and fluids' pressure as bulk properties. What does calling them bulk commit the study to?
**Answer: To describing the whole amount's behaviour — the bar's stretching, the fluid's pressure at depth — in bulk terms (stress, strain, pressure), not particle by particle.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Two extensive properties are divided to make density. Show with numbers why the result cannot be extensive.
**Answer: Double the amount: mass doubles (2m) and volume doubles (2V); the ratio 2m/2V = m/V is unchanged — a property unchanged by doubling is intensive, not extensive.**
