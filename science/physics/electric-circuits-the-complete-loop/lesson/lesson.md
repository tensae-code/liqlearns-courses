# Electric circuits — the complete loop

## Do it first
Build the smallest real loop: battery, wire, small bulb. Light it. Break one connection and watch a single gap command the whole circuit.

## Electric circuits — the complete loop — the teaching
An electric circuit is a complete loop: a source (battery) gives energy, conductors (wires) carry it, a load (a lamp) uses it, and a control (a switch) rules it. Circuits are drawn as diagrams with standard symbols, so anyone can rebuild the loop from the drawing. Break the loop anywhere — an open circuit — and the current stops everywhere; close it and current flows all the way round. One gap is enough to kill the whole circuit.

## The key ideas

- A circuit needs source, conductors, load and control in a complete loop.
- Open circuit: a break stops current everywhere. Closed: current flows.
- Circuit diagrams use standard symbols.

## Say it to your companion
Explain electric circuits — the complete loop to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
