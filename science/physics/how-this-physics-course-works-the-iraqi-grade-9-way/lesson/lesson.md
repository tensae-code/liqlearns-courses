# How this Physics course works — the Iraqi Grade 9 way

## Do it first
Open any Iraqi physics book at a chapter start — do not read the teaching yet. Find the activity or the vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), read the bilingual terms aloud, then flip to the chapter's end and find its review and test. That opening and that closing are this lesson: the Iraqi way, in the Grade 9 book. Its nine chapters run from electrostatics to the atmosphere and communication.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book's shape — 9 chapters

The Grade 9 book runs in 9 chapters: electrostatics; magnetism; electric current; the battery and electromotive force; electric energy and power; electricity and magnetism; the transformer; energy-sources technology; and physics of the atmosphere and communication. A maths refresher on powers of ten opens the book before Chapter 1.

## Sub lesson 2 — How every lesson is built — activity or vocabulary first, bilingual terms, worked examples

The Grade 9 book is the fullest statement of the Iraqi way in this set: every chapter prints its chapter vocabulary section list (مفردات الفصل), its behavioural objectives and a bilingual glossary. Lessons open with activity or vocabulary, terms stand in Arabic and English side by side, and worked examples carry the teaching.

## Sub lesson 3 — How every chapter closes — vocabulary review and a chapter test

The Grade 9 book closes each chapter with its vocabulary/concept review and a chapter test of short-answer and multiple-choice questions; the book itself closes with a full contents list and quantity/unit tables.

## The key ideas

- The Iraqi Grade 9 book is organised as 9 chapters.
- Every Iraqi lesson opens with a hands-on activity or a chapter vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), and technical terms are printed in Arabic and English side by side.
- Worked examples carry the teaching, and every chapter closes with a vocabulary/concept review plus short-answer and multiple-choice tests.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole lesson in order, in your own words — start with the opening activity, then take each sub lesson in turn and finish with what it is used for. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
