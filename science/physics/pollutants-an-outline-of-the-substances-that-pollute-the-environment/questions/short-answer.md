# Pollutants — an outline of the substances that pollute the environment — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does this lesson keep a 'source' column beside every pollutant's name?
**Answer: Because control acts on sources: nearly all air pollutants are combustion products, so naming the source (engines, factories, stoves) turns a catalogue of harms into a list of places where the harm can be reduced.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Ground-level ozone is a pollutant, yet ozone high in the atmosphere protects us. Resolve the puzzle in one or two sentences.
**Answer: It is the same substance in different places: high ozone absorbs harmful ultraviolet before it reaches the ground, while ozone at ground level — brewed from exhaust in sunlight — is breathed and damages lungs and plants. Place and amount define the pollutant.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A factory discharges warm cooling water into a small stream and the fish decline, though nothing toxic was added. Explain.
**Answer: The waste heat alone is the pollutant: warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen and speeds the stream life's metabolism, stressing the fish — pollution by energy in the wrong place, not by a substance.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do land pollutants accumulate over decades while river pollution can wash away in weeks?
**Answer: Rivers flow — their pollutants are carried off, diluted and partly broken down; land pollutants such as plastics, pesticide residues and heavy metals stay where they fell and have no cycle or current to remove them, so each year's addition stacks on the last.**
