# Lesson plan — Pollutants — an outline of the substances that pollute the environment

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Pollutants — an outline of the substances that pollute the environment
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside Vietnam's environment topic — after Physics and protecting the environment and before Renewable energy: the catalogue the framing lesson promised
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a pollutant is a substance (or an energy form, like noise and excess heat) released into air, water or land in amounts the environment cannot absorb, where it harms living things or the systems they depend on: air pollutants such as carbon monoxide, sulfur and nitrogen oxides, particulates and greenhouse gases; water pollutants such as fertiliser runoff, industrial chemicals, heavy metals and pathogens carried in waste; land pollutants such as persistent plastics, pesticides and heavy-metal residues — each named with its main source

## Sub lessons
1. **Pollutants of the air** — The air receives the exhaust of every combustion: **carbon monoxide** (CO, from incomplete burning — a poison that hijacks blood's oxygen carrying), **sulfur dioxide** and **nitrogen oxides** (from fossil fuels — the parents of acid rain), **particulates** — smoke, dust and soot fine enough to lodge deep in lungs — **unburned hydrocarbons** and ground-level **ozone** brewed from exhaust in sunlight, and the **greenhouse gases**, carbon dioxide and methane above all, which do not poison directly but thicken the atmosphere's heat blanket. Vietnam's Grade 10 study-topics book surveys the polluting substances as one outline lesson (Bài 9) — the catalogue organised by the medium they pollute.
2. **Pollutants of water and land** — Water collects what land sheds: **fertiliser and pesticide runoff** (nitrate and phosphate that overfeed algae — eutrophication — until decay strips the water of oxygen), **industrial chemicals and heavy metals** (lead, mercury, cadmium — toxic in traces and concentrated up food chains), **oil**, **untreated sewage** with its pathogens, and waste **heat** from power-station cooling, which alone can stress a river's life. Land takes the solids: **persistent plastics**, **pesticide residues**, heavy metals in contaminated soil, and buried industrial waste — materials with no cycle to reclaim them, measured in decades to millennia of persistence.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a pollutant, including the energy-form cases.
2. Name the main air pollutants with a source for each.
3. Explain eutrophication and heavy-metal concentration up a food chain.
4. Give the land pollutants' shared property — persistence.

## What you need
No equipment — the hands-on is a label-reading and source-hunting exercise at home.
