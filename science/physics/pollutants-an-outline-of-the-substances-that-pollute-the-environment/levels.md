# Pollutants — an outline of the substances that pollute the environment — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A pollutant is any substance — or energy form such as noise or waste heat — released beyond the environment's power to absorb it, where it does harm; Air: carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, particulates, ground-level ozone, and the greenhouse gases CO₂ and methane — mostly combustion's children; Water: fertiliser runoff (eutrophication), heavy metals, industrial chemicals, oil, sewage pathogens and waste heat; Land: persistent plastics, pesticide residues and heavy metals — materials no natural cycle reclaims — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 2 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
