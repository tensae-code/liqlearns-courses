# Strength of structures — beams, shear, struts and ties — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does an I-beam carry so much with so little material?
**Answer: Bending stress lives in the top and bottom faces; the I-section puts its material there (the flanges) and keeps only a thin web between to carry shear — depth without wasted weight.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A timber beam splits lengthwise along its grain near a support. Name the failure and why it happens there.
**Answer: Shear failure: adjacent layers slide along each other, the shear force is greatest near the supports, and timber's grain gives the sliding a ready path.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) In a roof truss, the rafters and the horizontal ceiling beam: which is the strut and which the tie, and what does each do?
**Answer: Rafters are struts — pushed along their length (compression) by the roof load. The horizontal beam is a tie — pulled (tension) — stopping the rafters' feet from spreading the walls apart.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do builders keep notches out of a beam's bottom face near mid-span?
**Answer: The bottom face at mid-span is the tension face at its most stretched; a notch there concentrates that tension at its root (the notch effect) and starts the crack that fails the beam.**
