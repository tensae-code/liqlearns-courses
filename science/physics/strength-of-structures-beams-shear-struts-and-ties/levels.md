# Strength of structures — beams, shear, struts and ties — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A loaded beam bends: top face in compression, bottom in tension, neutral layer between; depth makes a beam stiff; Shear force acts across and along the beam, greatest near the supports — sliding failure, as when timber splits along the grain; The notch effect: a nick, cut or hole concentrates stress at its root and starts cracks — a notched member fails at the notch; Struts work in compression, ties in tension; triangulated frames and strong shapes (triangles, tubes, I-sections, arches) turn loads into push and pull along members — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
