---

Today: **diffraction — waves bend into the shadow**. Send straight waves at a wall with a gap. They do not carry on as a straight beam: beyond the gap they spread into curves that reach the places a ruler-line says are in shadow. That spreading is diffraction. It is strongest when the gap is about as wide as one wavelength — then the gap acts like a new source and the waves fan into full semicircles. Widen the gap and the middle goes straight again, with spread only at the edges. That one rule — gap width against wavelength — explains harbours, hearing around corners, and why light needs a slit a hair wide before it will spread at all.
