# Saturated and unsaturated vapour, phase diagrams, triple point and critical state — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does saturated vapour pressure rise when the temperature rises?
**Answer:** A warmer liquid evaporates more strongly; equilibrium with condensation is reached only at a higher vapour density, and denser vapour exerts more pressure.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Sketch what a phase diagram shows and name its three regions and meeting point.
**Answer:** Axes pressure (vertical) and temperature (horizontal); three curves divide the plane into solid (low temperature), liquid and gas (vapour) regions. The three curves meet at the triple point, and the boiling curve stops at the critical point.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain the critical state in your own words.
**Answer:** At and beyond the critical temperature the difference between liquid and gas vanishes: the meniscus disappears, densities match, and the substance is a single fluid that no pressure can split into liquid plus vapour.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does a pressure cooker use the phase diagram?
**Answer:** Sealing the pan raises the pressure over the water, moving up the boiling curve, so the water (and steam) boil at well above 100 °C — the higher temperature cooks the food faster.
