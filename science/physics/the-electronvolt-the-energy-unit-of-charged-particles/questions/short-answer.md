# The electronvolt — the energy unit of charged particles — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Define the electronvolt and give its value in joules.
**Answer: The energy one elementary charge gains crossing a potential difference of one volt; 1 eV = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ J (≈ 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Convert 2.5 MeV to joules.
**Answer: 2.5 MeV = 2.5×10⁶ eV × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J/eV = 4.0×10⁻¹³ J.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do the manuals quote atomic energies in eV rather than joules?
**Answer: Single-particle energies are of order 10⁻¹⁹–10⁻¹³ J — unreadable in joules; in eV they are ones, tens and thousands, and they read straight off the accelerating voltages that produce them.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) An electron beam's electrons each carry 2 keV. What voltage accelerated them (from rest)?
**Answer: 2,000 V: an electron gains 1 eV per volt crossed, so 2 keV means a 2 kV accelerating voltage.**
