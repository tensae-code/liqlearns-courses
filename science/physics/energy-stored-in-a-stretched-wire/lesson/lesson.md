# Energy stored in a stretched wire

## Do it first
Before you read: pull a spring slowly and it fights you harder the further it goes. Every centimetre of that pull is work you did — and the spring is keeping all of it, ready to fire back. A stretched wire is the same store, in metal. This lesson counts exactly how much energy a stretched wire holds.

## Sub lesson 1 — Work done in stretching — the ½Fx rule

Within Hooke's law the stretching force grows in proportion to the extension, from 0 at the start to F at extension x. The work done is therefore not F × x (that would need the full force all the way) but the **average force, F/2, times x: W = ½Fx**. On a force–extension graph this is the **area under the line** — a triangle of base x and height F. That work is not lost: it is stored in the wire as **elastic strain energy**, and the wire returns it as it contracts, which is how springs drive clocks, catapults and weighing scales.

## Sub lesson 2 — Energy per unit volume — stress and strain

Divide by the wire's volume and the store takes its material form: energy per unit volume = **½ × stress × strain**. With stress = Y × strain (Young's modulus Y from the `youngs-modulus` lesson), it can also be written ½Y × (strain)², or (stress)² ÷ 2Y: for a given material, the store grows with the **square** of the strain — double the stretch (within the limit), four times the stored energy per volume. Total stored energy = ½ × stress × strain × volume (A × L).

## Sub lesson 3 — Getting it back — and the elastic limit

Release the load and a wire stretched **within its elastic limit** contracts fully, returning the stored energy as work — the ½Fx account balances. Stretch past the limit of proportionality and then the elastic limit, and part of the work is spent permanently rearranging the material: the wire keeps a permanent set, and the loading–unloading graphs enclose a loop whose area is energy lost as heat. The stored-energy formulas of this lesson are the elastic story only — they price the spring while it still behaves as one.

## The key ideas

- Work stored in stretching = ½Fx — the average force (F/2) times the extension, the triangular area under the force–extension graph.
- Per unit volume: ½ × stress × strain = ½Y(strain)² — the store grows with the square of the strain.
- Within the elastic limit the energy is fully returned on unloading; beyond it, part is lost as heat and the wire takes a permanent set.

## Do It Yourself
1. A force of 40 N stretches a wire by 2 mm (within Hooke's law). How much energy is stored?
2. A wire of volume 2 × 10⁻⁶ m³ is under a stress of 1.0 × 10⁸ Pa with a strain of 0.001. Find the stored energy using ½ × stress × strain × volume.
3. The strain in a wire is doubled (still elastic). By what factor does the stored energy per unit volume change?
