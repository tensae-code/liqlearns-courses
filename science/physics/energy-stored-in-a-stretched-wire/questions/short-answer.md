# Energy stored in a stretched wire — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the work ½Fx and not Fx?
**Answer: Because the force is not F throughout: within Hooke's law it grows steadily from 0 to F, so the average force over the stretch is F/2, and work = average force × extension = ½Fx.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A wire 2 m long and 1 mm² in cross-section (volume 2 × 10⁻⁶ m³) carries stress 2 × 10⁸ Pa at strain 0.002. Find the total stored energy.
**Answer: Energy per volume = ½ × 2 × 10⁸ × 0.002 = 2 × 10⁵ J/m³. Total = 2 × 10⁵ × 2 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.4 J.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What does the area of the loop between loading and unloading curves (past the elastic limit) represent?
**Answer: Energy lost as heat during the cycle — work done on the wire that is not recovered as stored elastic energy, spent in permanently rearranging the material.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) A spring extends 0.05 m under a final force of 10 N. How much energy is stored?
**Answer: W = ½Fx = ½ × 10 × 0.05 = 0.25 J.**
