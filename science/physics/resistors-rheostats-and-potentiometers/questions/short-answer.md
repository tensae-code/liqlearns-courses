# Resistors, rheostats and potentiometers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do resistors carry power ratings, not just ohm values?
**Answer: Because a resistor turns the electrical energy it takes into heat, P = I²R. Beyond its rating it overheats and burns — the ohms say what current it sets, the watts say what it can survive.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Describe the rheostat lab and the pattern it must show.
**Answer: Battery, lamp, ammeter and rheostat in series; slide the contact step by step. More wire in circuit → resistance up → ammeter reading down → lamp dimmer, smoothly and reversibly — current governed by slider position.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) One device, two jobs: state the wiring difference between rheostat and potentiometer use.
**Answer: Rheostat: two terminals (one coil end + slider), in series with the load, governing current. Potentiometer: three terminals, full voltage across the coil, slider tapping a chosen voltage share for the next stage.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A rheostat's coil is rated 50 Ω. At its midpoint the circuit sees about how much of it?
**Answer: About half — roughly 25 Ω — since the slider includes about half the coil's length of wire in the circuit.**
