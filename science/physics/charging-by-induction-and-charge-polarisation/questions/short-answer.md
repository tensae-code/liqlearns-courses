# Charging by induction and charge polarisation — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is a neutral piece of paper attracted to a charged rod, even though opposite and like charges are both present in the paper?
**Answer: Polarisation puts the opposite (near) charge closer to the rod than the like (far) charge, and the nearer force is stronger, so attraction wins overall.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why can induction not be done the same way on a wooden block?
**Answer: Wood is an insulator: its electrons cannot travel to one side or escape through an earth connection, so only polarisation occurs and no charge can be removed.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) List the four steps of charging by induction in order.
**Answer: Rod near (charge separates); earth the far side (repelled charge escapes); remove the earth connection; remove the rod — leaving the opposite-sign charge.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can the same rod charge many conductors by induction but only share its charge away by contact?
**Answer: Induction never transfers the rod's own charge — it only rearranges and removes the conductor's — so the rod is unchanged and reusable.**
