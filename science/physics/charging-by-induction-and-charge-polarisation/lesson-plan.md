# Lesson plan — Charging by induction and charge polarisation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Charging by induction and charge polarisation
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Kuwait placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a charged object can charge another without ever touching it: in a conductor, a nearby charge pulls the free electrons to one side, and if that side is earthed (or the far side let go) while the charge is still near, the conductor is left with a real, lasting charge of the opposite sign — charging by induction; in an insulator, where electrons cannot travel, the same nearby charge only stretches each atom's charge cloud — charge polarisation — which is why a charged comb picks up neutral paper

## Sub lessons
1. **Polarisation — charge shifting inside an insulator** — In an **insulator** the electrons are bound to their atoms and cannot travel through the material. A nearby charged rod still affects them: a negative rod pushes each atom's electron cloud slightly away and pulls its positive nucleus slightly toward, so every atom becomes a tiny stretched dipole — negative on the far side, positive on the near side. This is **charge polarisation**. The near side's opposite charge is closer to the rod than the far side's like charge, so attraction beats repulsion and the neutral insulator is pulled in — the paper jumping to the comb. Remove the rod and the clouds spring back: polarisation leaves **no lasting charge**.
2. **Induction in a conductor — separating free charge** — In a **conductor** the outer electrons are free to move through the whole object. Bring a negative rod near (not touching) a neutral metal sphere on an insulating stand: the free electrons are repelled to the far side, leaving the near side positive. The sphere is still neutral overall — its charge has only **separated**. Take the rod away now and the electrons simply spread back. Induction becomes charging only if, while the rod is still held near, one side's charge is given a way out — the next sub lesson's earth connection.
3. **Charging by induction — earth it, then remove the rod** — The full method, in order: (1) bring the charged rod near the conductor — charge separates; (2) **earth the far side** (touch it, or connect a wire to the ground) while the rod stays near — the repelled electrons escape to earth; (3) remove the earth connection first; (4) *then* take the rod away. The electrons that would have cancelled the near-side charge are gone, so the conductor keeps a lasting net charge — **opposite in sign to the rod's**. The rod itself loses nothing and never touched: it can charge conductor after conductor the same way, which no contact method can do.
