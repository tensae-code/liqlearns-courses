# Hands-on — Experiment: acceleration down a slope

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the practical
1. A group experiment: roll an object down an incline, record positions and times, plot the data (including in a spreadsheet such as Microsoft Excel), draw the best-fit line and take twice the slope as the acceleration, then write a report.

Work with a partner, write down what you measured or observed, and say what the result proves. If apparatus is missing, walk through the steps with drawings and say what you would expect to see.

## Time a falling object
Drop a small stone from a measured height, time the fall three times, and compare with the fall equation. Say why the times differ a little each drop.

## See it and say it
Study this lesson's picture — a ramp with a rolling ball and timing marks — and name every part of it out loud. Then close the picture and draw it from memory; check your drawing against the lesson.

## Teach it back
Explain this lesson to someone at home using one thing in the room as your example. Their questions show you which part to re-read.
