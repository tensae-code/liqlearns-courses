# Lesson plan — Dipole characteristics and the generator/receiver sign conventions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Dipole characteristics and the generator/receiver sign conventions
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 — Physics expansion) — a new lesson built for the Mauritania placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** any two-terminal component — a dipole — is known by its characteristic, the curve of voltage against current traced in both directions: a dipole is passive exactly when its curve passes through the origin (U = 0 when I = 0), and every reading of the curve depends on the arrow conventions — receiver arrows against the current, generator arrows with it

## Sub lessons
1. **Tracing a characteristic — both directions** — Connect the dipole to a variable supply with meters, and plot U against I point by point — then reverse the current and trace the other half. The full curve is the dipole's identity card: its shape says what the component is. Tracing in both directions tests symmetry: a curve that mirrors through the origin behaves the same whichever way the current runs.
2. **Passive or active — the origin test, the lamp, the Zener diode** — The Mauritanian test is experimental: a dipole is passive if and only if its characteristic passes through the origin — switch the current off and its voltage vanishes (U = 0 when I = 0); a source fails this test, keeping its voltage with no current. The lamp comes out passive but non-linear (its filament's resistance grows as it heats) and symmetric. The Zener diode is the asymmetric case: ordinary conduction one way, a sharp avalanche threshold in reverse — one curve, two different behaviours.
3. **The sign conventions — receiver and generator arrows** — A characteristic only reads correctly with its arrows declared. In the receiver convention the voltage arrow points against the current arrow (the dipole receives power: a resistor, a lamp). In the generator convention the voltage arrow points with the current (the dipole gives power out). The same component plotted under the other convention flips its curve — so the convention is part of the data, stated before any characteristic is read.
