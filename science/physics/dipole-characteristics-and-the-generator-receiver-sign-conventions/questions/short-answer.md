# Dipole characteristics and the generator/receiver sign conventions — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What two questions does tracing both directions of a characteristic answer?
**Answer: Whether the dipole is symmetric (same behaviour with current reversed) and — with the origin — whether it is passive; the pair of half-curves together are the component's identity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A student plots a battery's characteristic and finds U = 4.5 V at I = 0. Classify the dipole and justify it.
**Answer: Active: its voltage does not vanish when the current does, so its characteristic cannot pass through the origin — the origin test for passivity fails.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must the arrow convention be stated before a characteristic is read?
**Answer: The same component's curve flips when the convention changes: receiver arrows (U against I) and generator arrows (U with I) give mirror readings, so an undeclared convention makes the curve ambiguous.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the lamp non-linear even though it is passive?
**Answer: Its filament heats as current grows, and the hot filament's resistance is larger — so U grows faster than I and the characteristic bends; passivity only claims the origin, not straightness.**
