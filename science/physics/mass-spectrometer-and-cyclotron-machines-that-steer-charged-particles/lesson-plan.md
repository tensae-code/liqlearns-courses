# Lesson plan — Mass spectrometer and cyclotron — machines that steer charged particles

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Mass spectrometer and cyclotron — machines that steer charged particles

## Sub lessons
1. **The mass spectrometer — a balance that sorts by circling** — Ions are made (charge q, mass m), sped through a known voltage, and fired across a uniform magnetic field, where each circles with r = mv/qB. Lighter ions bend tighter, heavier ions wider — a photographic plate or detector array records **where each lands**, and position becomes mass: with v priced from the accelerating voltage, m falls out of the radius. Add a **velocity selector** first — crossed electric and magnetic fields tuned so only one speed passes straight (v = E/B) — and the reading is exact. This machine weighed the isotopes of the elements one by one, proving that atoms of one element can differ in mass; it still audits chemistry, geology's dates and medicine's samples today.
2. **The cyclotron — a merry-go-round with a kick** — Two hollow D-shaped electrodes sit in a magnetic field. A particle released near the centre circles inside them; each time it crosses the gap between the Ds, an alternating voltage — flipping in step with the circling — gives it a small push, a few thousand volts' worth. The push raises its speed, its circle widens (r = mv/qB), but its **lap time does not change**, so the same rhythm keeps meeting it at the gap, lap after lap — hundreds of laps — until it spirals out at the rim with the full energy. The arithmetic is the wonder: 200 crossings of a 10 kV gap leave the particle with the energy of a 2,000 kV supply nobody had to build or insulate.
3. **What limits them — and where they work** — The cyclotron's bargain breaks when the particle grows relativistic: near light speed its effective mass grows, the lap time stretches, the rhythm misses — so giant energies belong to synchrotrons, which adjust the rhythm and field as they go. The spectrometer's limit is resolution: field evenness and detector fineness decide how close two masses can stand and still be told apart. Both machines earn their section in the book the same way — they are the circling formulas with jobs: one **measures** the world particle by particle, the other **manufactures** fast particles for medicine (radioisotopes, therapy beams) and for probing the nucleus.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Mass spectrometer: ions circle at r = mv/qB in a known field — landing position reads out mass per charge; a velocity selector (v = E/B) makes it exact
2. This machine separated and weighed the isotopes — atoms of one element with different masses
3. Cyclotron: an alternating voltage kicks the particle at every gap crossing; lap time is speed-independent, so one rhythm works for hundreds of laps
4. Energy adds per crossing: hundreds of small kicks equal millions of volts; relativity sets the cyclotron's ceiling, where synchrotrons take over
