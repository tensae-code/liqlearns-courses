# Hands-on — Mass spectrometer and cyclotron — machines that steer charged particles

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Read a spectrogram, plan a cyclotron
(1) Spectrogram reading: singly-charged ions are accelerated through one voltage into one field, so radius grows as the square root of mass (r ∝ √m at fixed voltage). Strikes stand at 10.0 cm (carbon-12, given) and 11.5 cm: check the ratio — (11.5/10.0)² ≈ 1.32, and 12 × 1.32 ≈ 16 — the second strike is oxygen-16. A third strike at 12.6 cm gives (12.6/10.0)² ≈ 1.59, ×12 ≈ 19 — fluorine-19. (2) Cyclotron plan: B = 1.5 T, rim radius 0.50 m — the proton's exit speed from r = mv/qB is v = qBr/m ≈ 7.2 × 10⁷ m/s (compare it with c and note relativity knocking), and its lap frequency f = qB/2πm ≈ 2.3 × 10⁷ Hz is the rhythm the alternating voltage must keep. (3) Write two sentences: which machine a hospital's tracer unit uses, and which would weigh the atoms in a rock sample — and why.
