# Mass spectrometer and cyclotron — machines that steer charged particles — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Two isotopes of the same element, masses 20 and 22 units, enter a spectrometer identically charged and selected to the same speed. Compare their radii.
**Answer: r = mv/qB ∝ m at fixed v, q, B: the mass-22 ion's radius is 22/20 = 1.1× the other's — the plate separates them by that 10%.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the cyclotron not need a million-volt supply to make a million-volt particle?
**Answer: The particle crosses the modest gap voltage hundreds of times, gaining the gap's energy each crossing; the sum, not the supply, prices the exit energy — the field's circling keeps returning it to the same small kick.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A synchrotron succeeds the cyclotron at high energy by doing what differently?
**Answer: It adjusts — raising the magnetic field (and the kick rhythm) as the particle's energy and effective mass grow, holding the orbit's radius fixed instead of letting it spiral out.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name one thing each machine proved or produces that older tools could not.
**Answer: Spectrometer: that one element's atoms come in several masses (isotopes), weighed individually. Cyclotron: fast particles on demand — radioisotopes for medicine and beams for probing nuclei.**
