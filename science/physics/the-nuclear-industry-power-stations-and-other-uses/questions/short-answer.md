# The nuclear industry — power stations and other uses — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is a nuclear power station called 'a kettle with a nuclear fire'?
**Answer: Because everything after the heat source is the ordinary steam-station chain — steam, turbine, generator; only the fire differs: controlled fission instead of burning fuel.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give one medical, one industrial and one agricultural use of radioisotopes.
**Answer: Medical: tracer imaging of an organ (or PET scanning, or radiotherapy). Industrial: a gamma thickness gauge on a production line (or sterilising medical supplies). Agricultural: tracing fertiliser uptake in plants (or irradiating food to kill spoilage organisms).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does a tracer isotope need a short half-life?
**Answer: So that it decays away soon after the examination — long enough to be followed and imaged, short enough that the patient's dose stays small once the information is gathered.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Vietnam's lesson opens with the advantages/disadvantages debate rather than closing with it. What does a learner gain by meeting the judgement before the tour?
**Answer: The uses are then read critically from the start — each application is weighed as it is met (what property it exploits, what risk it carries), instead of the debate arriving as an afterthought bolted onto a list of wonders.**
