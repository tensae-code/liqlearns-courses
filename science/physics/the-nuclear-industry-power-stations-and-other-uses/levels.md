# The nuclear industry — power stations and other uses — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A nuclear power station is a steam station whose fire is controlled fission: reactor → turbine → generator; control rods hold the chain reaction at replacement rate; Uranium fuel is millions of times denser in energy than coal by mass — a year's fuel in trucks, not trains; The industry's other half is radioisotopes at work: medical tracers, PET/CT imaging, radiotherapy, sterilising, industrial gauges, agricultural tracers; The burdens are real and physical: long-lived radioactive waste, accident risk managed by layered safety and dose law, and weapons-proliferation controls — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
