# The sign rule for motion equations

## Do it first
Before you read: throw a ball (or a crumpled paper ball) straight up and catch it. On the way up it slows; at the top it stops for an instant; on the way down it speeds up — yet gravity pulled down the whole time, never switching. One pull, three behaviours: the puzzle is how to write that in numbers without the pull seeming to change. This lesson is the rule that solves it — choose a direction, sign everything by it, and let the signs do the talking.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choose a positive direction — then sign everything by it

Straight-line motion has only two directions, so one choice settles every sign. Pick the direction you will call **positive** — often the direction of the first motion, or upward for a thrown object — and write the choice down before any numbers. Then sign each vector quantity by it: a **displacement**, **velocity** or **acceleration** pointing along the chosen direction is **positive (+)**; one pointing against it is **negative (−)**. A car driving east at 20 m/s with east chosen positive has velocity +20 m/s; the same car returning west has velocity −20 m/s — the minus is not a smaller speed, it is the direction speaking. Gravity is signed the same way: with upward positive, the acceleration due to gravity is **−9.8 m/s²** for the whole flight of a thrown ball, up, at the top, and down — the sign rule is what lets one constant acceleration describe the whole journey.

## Sub lesson 2 — Signs belong to vectors — scalars never take them

The rule applies only to quantities that have a direction. **Distance, speed, time and mass are scalars**: they are never negative, and they take no sign from your choice — a journey's distance is +50 m worth of ground however you chose the positive direction, and time always runs forward. **Velocity is not speed**: −20 m/s tells you a speed of 20 m/s in the negative direction. **Acceleration's sign is not 'slowing down'**: an object slows when acceleration points against its velocity, whichever signs those are. A falling ball with upward positive has velocity −6 m/s and acceleration −9.8 m/s² — same signs, so it speeds up. A rising ball has velocity +6 m/s and acceleration −9.8 m/s² — opposite signs, so it slows. Read the pair of signs together, never one sign alone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Use the rule inside the motion equations

With every value signed, the familiar equations work unchanged: **v = u + at**, **s = ut + ½at²**, **v² = u² + 2as** — you simply feed them signed u, v, a and s and trust the signs in the answer. Worked pattern, upward positive: a ball is thrown up at 15 m/s, so u = +15, a = −9.8. After 1 s, v = 15 − 9.8 = **+5.2 m/s** (still rising). Its height then is s = 15×1 − 4.9×1² = **+10.1 m** (above the hand, as + requires). At the top v = 0, so 0 = 225 − 19.6 s gives the greatest height s ≈ **11.5 m**. On the way down the answers turn negative by themselves — a displacement of −5 m means 5 m below the starting point. The method is always the same four steps: state the positive direction, list the signed values, choose the equation, and read the answer's sign as a direction.

## The key ideas

- Choose one positive direction first and write it down; every displacement, velocity and acceleration along it is +, against it is −
- With upward positive, gravity is −9.8 m/s² for the whole flight — rising, at the top, and falling
- Scalars (distance, speed, time) never take signs; a negative velocity means direction, not a smaller speed
- An object slows when velocity and acceleration have opposite signs, and speeds up when they share a sign — and the motion equations are fed the signed values exactly as they stand

## Do It Yourself
1. Take east as positive. A cyclist rides east at 8 m/s, brakes with acceleration 2 m/s² westward, stops, then rolls west at 3 m/s. Write the signed velocity at the start, the signed acceleration while braking, and the signed velocity while rolling back — and say which single choice made all three signs follow.
2. A stone is dropped from a bridge with downward chosen positive. Write u, a and the displacement after 2 s (use g = 9.8 m/s²), then rewrite the same three values with upward chosen positive, and show the stone's position comes out the same place either way.
3. A ball thrown upward at 20 m/s (upward positive, g = 9.8 m/s²): find its velocity after 3 s and explain, from the sign of your answer, what the ball is doing at that moment.
