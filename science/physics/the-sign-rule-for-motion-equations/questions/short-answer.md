# The sign rule for motion equations — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does gravity keep the same sign while a thrown ball rises, pauses and falls?
**Answer: The sign of an acceleration is fixed by the chosen positive direction, not by the object's momentary motion: gravity pulls one way (down) the whole time, so with upward positive it is −9.8 m/s² at every stage; it is the velocity whose sign changes, at the top.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A car has velocity −12 m/s and acceleration −3 m/s². Is it speeding up or slowing down, and how do you know?
**Answer: It is speeding up: velocity and acceleration have the same sign (both negative), so the acceleration acts along the motion and the speed grows — direction signs must be read as a pair.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Using v = u + at with upward positive: a stone is thrown down from a cliff at 4 m/s. Write u and a with their signs, and find v after 2 s (g = 9.8).
**Answer: u = −4 m/s (thrown against the positive direction) and a = −9.8 m/s², so v = −4 − 19.6 = −23.6 m/s — the minus says it is moving downward, at a speed of 23.6 m/s.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A classmate says 'negative acceleration always means slowing down.' Give a counterexample from this lesson.
**Answer: A falling ball with upward positive has negative acceleration (−9.8 m/s²) and negative-and-growing velocity — it speeds up. Negative acceleration only means acceleration in the negative direction; slowing needs velocity and acceleration of opposite signs.**
