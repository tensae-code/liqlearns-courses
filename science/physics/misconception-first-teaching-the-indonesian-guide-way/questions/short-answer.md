# Misconception-first teaching — the Indonesian guide way — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why list this topic's own misconceptions rather than general warnings?
**Answer: Because each topic provokes its own specific wrong ideas; a general warning names none of them, while the chapter's own list gives teaching exact targets it can surface, confront and re-check.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Ammeters before and after a lamp read the same. Which misconception does this confront, and how?
**Answer: The idea that current is used up by the lamp. Learners predict a smaller reading after the lamp; equal readings are evidence that idea cannot explain, so the current-throughout-the-loop picture replaces it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A follow-up check must ask the trap “in new clothing”. Why not repeat the original question?
**Answer: Because learners may have memorised the original's right answer while still holding the misconception; a new surface with the same trap underneath shows whether the idea itself has moved.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What does the teacher's reflection record about misconceptions?
**Answer: Honestly, which misconceptions survived, in how many learners, and why the route taken did not dislodge them — the record the follow-up teaching is planned from.**
