# Alternating-current circuits — reactance, impedance and resonance — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a lamp on AC mains not flicker visibly at 50 Hz?
**Answer: The current reverses 100 times a second (two peaks per cycle), far faster than the eye resolves and faster than the filament can cool between peaks, so the light appears steady; the RMS value matches the steady DC brightness.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does reactance dissipate no energy on average, while resistance does?
**Answer: Reactance stores energy in a field (electric in the capacitor, magnetic in the inductor) for part of the cycle and returns it in the rest — the account balances to zero. Resistance converts electrical energy to heat irreversibly, every moment current flows.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Derive the resonance condition in words and give its frequency formula.
**Answer: Resonance is where inductive and capacitive reactance are equal, X_L = X_C, so they cancel in (X_L − X_C) and Z = R alone. Setting 2πfL = 1/(2πfC) gives f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Give one use and one danger of resonance.
**Answer: Use: frequency selection — a radio/TV tuner (or any filter) set so only the wanted station's frequency drives a large current. Danger: in power systems an unintended resonance can build currents and voltages far beyond design values, damaging equipment.**
