# Video script — Speed and road safety — braking, reaction and the dangers of speed

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **why a car cannot stop on the spot**. First, you. You see danger, you think, you move your foot — that takes about a second, and in that second the car rolls on at full speed. That is the reaction distance, and it grows in step with speed. Then the brakes bite — but the braking distance grows with the square of the speed. Twice as fast means four times as far to stop braking. Add the two together and you have the stopping distance: the clear road every driver must keep ahead. That is the whole danger of speed, in physics.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
