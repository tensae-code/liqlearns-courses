# Lesson plan — Speed and road safety — braking, reaction and the dangers of speed

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Speed and road safety — braking, reaction and the dangers of speed
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Morocco placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** speed turned into road safety: the **reaction distance** travelled while a driver reacts (proportional to speed), the **braking distance** travelled while the brakes shed the car's kinetic energy (proportional to the **square** of the speed), and the **stopping distance** — the two added together — that sets safe gaps and speed limits

## Sub lessons
1. **Reaction distance — the distance travelled while you think** — A driver who sees danger does not brake instantly: the brain must notice, decide and move the foot, and during that reaction time — about a second for an alert driver, longer when tired or distracted — the car travels at full speed. That thinking distance grows in direct proportion to speed: twice the speed, twice the reaction distance.
2. **Braking distance — the distance travelled while the brakes bite** — Once the brakes are on, the car sheds its kinetic energy through friction. Braking distance grows much faster than speed: it goes with the square of the speed, so twice the speed means four times the braking distance, because the car carries four times the kinetic energy. Wet roads, worn tyres and worn brakes stretch it further still.
3. **Stopping distance — the whole story, and why speed is dangerous** — The stopping distance is reaction distance plus braking distance together, and it is the gap a driver must keep from whatever lies ahead. Because one part grows in proportion and the other with the square, total stopping distance balloons as speed rises — the danger of speed is not only hitting harder, it is needing a road that may not be there.
