# Speed and road safety — braking, reaction and the dangers of speed — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can a better car not shorten the reaction distance?
**Answer: The reaction distance is covered before the brakes are touched, while the driver is still seeing, deciding and moving — it depends on the driver's reaction time and the speed, not on the car's brakes or tyres.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A car's braking distance at 20 m/s is 20 m. Predict its braking distance at 40 m/s.
**Answer: Doubling the speed multiplies braking distance by four: 20 m × 4 = 80 m.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A driver at 20 m/s reacts in 1 s and then needs 20 m to brake to a stop. What is the stopping distance, and what gap must the driver keep?
**Answer: Reaction distance = 20 m/s × 1 s = 20 m; stopping distance = 20 m + 20 m = 40 m — the driver needs at least 40 m of clear road ahead to stop safely.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) Name two things that lengthen a driver's reaction time.
**Answer: For example tiredness and distraction (such as looking at a phone) — also illness or anything that splits the driver's attention from the road.**
