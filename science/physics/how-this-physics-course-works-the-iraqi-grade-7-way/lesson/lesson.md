# How this Physics course works — the Iraqi Grade 7 way

## Do it first
Open any Iraqi physics book at a chapter start — do not read the teaching yet. Find the activity or the vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), read the bilingual terms aloud, then flip to the chapter's end and find its review and test. That opening and that closing are this lesson: the Iraqi way, in the Grade 7 book. Its five chapters run from states of matter to changes of state.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book's shape — 5 chapters / 12 lessons

The Grade 7 book runs in 5 chapters and 12 lessons: matter — its states, measuring its volume and its density; force and its kinds; pressure, liquid and gas pressure and Archimedes' principle; heat and how it travels; and heat's effects — expansion and changes of state. Every lesson is built on the 5-stage learning cycle stated in the preface: engage, explore, explain, evaluate, extend.

## Sub lesson 2 — How every lesson is built — activity or vocabulary first, bilingual terms, worked examples

In the Grade 7 book the cycle is explicit: a lesson engages the learner with an activity, lets them explore, then explains, evaluates and extends. Chapter vocabulary (مفردات الفصل) names the new terms, printed in Arabic and English side by side, and worked examples show each idea used on a real problem before the learner is asked to use it.

## Sub lesson 3 — How every chapter closes — vocabulary review and a chapter test

The Grade 7 book closes each chapter with a vocabulary/concept review and a chapter test of short-answer and multiple-choice questions — the evaluate stage of the cycle made visible at chapter scale; the whole book ends with the Chapter 5 chapter test.

## The key ideas

- The Iraqi Grade 7 book is organised as 5 chapters / 12 lessons.
- Every Iraqi lesson opens with a hands-on activity or a chapter vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), and technical terms are printed in Arabic and English side by side.
- Worked examples carry the teaching, and every chapter closes with a vocabulary/concept review plus short-answer and multiple-choice tests.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole lesson in order, in your own words — start with the opening activity, then take each sub lesson in turn and finish with what it is used for. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
