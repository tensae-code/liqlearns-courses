# Practice — synthesising forces experimentally — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the ring stay still under two pulls, and what must be noted before anything is moved?
**Answer: The ring stays still because the peg (or board) pushes back against the combined pull; before moving anything, the two balance readings and the directions of both cords must be recorded — they are the experiment's raw data.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Two forces of 6 N and 8 N act at right angles. What resultant does the parallelogram predict?
**Answer: The parallelogram is a rectangle, so the diagonal is √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 N, directed between the two forces, closer to the 8 N one.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A group's single balance reads noticeably more than their diagonal predicts. Suggest what to check first.
**Answer: Check the balances' zeros (an unzeroed balance inflates every reading), whether cords dragged on the board (friction adds to the needed pull), and whether the ring was held in exactly the same place as in the two-balance set-up.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What makes this a verification of the rule rather than a demonstration of it?
**Answer: The parallelogram prediction is made and written down before the single-balance check, and the check could have disagreed — the experiment tests the rule against the bench instead of merely illustrating it.**
