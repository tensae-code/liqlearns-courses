# The Doppler effect — pitch shifts when source or listener moves — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A source is stationary and you cycle toward it. Has the wavelength in the air changed? What has changed?
**Answer: No — the wavelength is unchanged; the rate at which you meet the wavefronts rises, so the frequency you hear rises.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the wavelength itself changed when the source moves, but not when only the listener moves?
**Answer: A moving source emits each front from a new position, physically crowding the fronts ahead of it; a moving listener only changes how fast she encounters fronts that are already spaced as emitted.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What is blueshift, and when is it observed?
**Answer: A shift of light to higher frequency (toward the blue end), observed when source and observer approach each other.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two practical uses of the Doppler effect beyond the passing siren.
**Answer: Any two of: police/traffic radar speed measurement, weather radar tracking storms, medical ultrasound measuring blood flow, astronomical measurement of star and galaxy motion.**
