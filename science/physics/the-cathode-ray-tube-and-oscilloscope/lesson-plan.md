# Lesson plan — The cathode ray tube and oscilloscope

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The cathode ray tube and oscilloscope
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the modern-physics and X-ray lessons — Seeing inside the body — X-ray, CT and MRI and Inside the nucleus: this is the lesson for the electron beam itself, the device that first showed electrons could be aimed, bent and made to draw
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** inside an evacuated glass tube, a heated cathode boils off electrons which a high voltage accelerates into a beam — cathode rays — that can be bent by electric and magnetic fields and makes a fluorescent screen glow where it lands; the cathode ray tube focuses and deflects that beam, and the cathode ray oscilloscope (CRO) turns it into a measuring instrument, drawing voltage against time as a trace whose height gives voltage and whose width gives time

## Sub lessons
1. **Cathode rays — an electron beam in a vacuum** — heat a metal cathode in an evacuated tube and electrons boil off its surface (thermionic emission); a high voltage between cathode and anode pulls them into a fast, narrow beam — cathode rays. The beam travels in straight lines, casts sharp shadows, carries energy and negative charge, and is deflected by both electric and magnetic fields — the behaviour that identified cathode rays as streams of electrons, identical whatever the cathode metal or the gas that was pumped out
2. **The cathode ray tube — gun, deflection and screen** — the tube has three working parts: the electron gun (heated cathode, control grid setting brightness, and anodes that accelerate and focus the beam), the deflection system (two pairs of plates — or coils — bending the beam sideways and up-and-down), and the fluorescent screen, coated inside so the spot glows where the beam lands. Set the two deflections working together and the spot can be placed anywhere, or swept in any pattern, across the screen
3. **The oscilloscope — measuring with the trace** — a CRO adds a time base: a steadily rising voltage sweeps the spot left to right at a known rate, then flies it back to start again. Connect a signal to the vertical plates and the spot draws the signal's graph — voltage up-and-down against time left-to-right. From the trace you read a voltage's size (height × the volts-per-division setting) and a wave's period (width of one cycle × the time-per-division setting, frequency = 1/period) — sound, heartbeat signals and a.c. waveforms all become pictures you can measure

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Cathode rays are beams of electrons boiled off a heated cathode in a vacuum and accelerated by a high voltage.
2. Name evidence that cathode rays are charged particles: straight-line travel and shadows, deflection by electric and magnetic fields.
3. Say, in their own words: A cathode ray tube is an electron gun, a deflection system and a fluorescent screen.
4. Read voltage and period/frequency from an oscilloscope trace using the per-division settings.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the oscilloscope trace is a picture of the wire or the sound itself → the trace is a graph the beam draws: the horizontal axis is time from the time base, the vertical axis is the signal voltage — it is a measurement, drawn live.
