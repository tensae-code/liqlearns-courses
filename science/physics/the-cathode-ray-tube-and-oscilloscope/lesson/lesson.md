# The cathode ray tube and oscilloscope

## Do it first
Before you read: an old television picture was not printed or projected — it was drawn, thousands of times a second, by a single spot of light racing across the screen faster than your eye can follow. The spot is the landing point of an electron beam, steered by voltages. This lesson builds that machine from the inside: cathode rays, the tube that aims them, and the oscilloscope that turns the beam into a measuring instrument.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cathode rays — an electron beam in a vacuum

Heat a metal cathode in an evacuated tube and electrons boil off its surface (thermionic emission); a high voltage between cathode and anode pulls them into a fast, narrow beam — cathode rays. The beam travels in straight lines, casts sharp shadows, carries energy and negative charge, and is deflected by both electric and magnetic fields — the behaviour that identified cathode rays as streams of electrons, identical whatever the cathode metal or the gas that was pumped out.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cathode ray tube — gun, deflection and screen

The tube has three working parts: the electron gun (heated cathode, control grid setting brightness, and anodes that accelerate and focus the beam), the deflection system (two pairs of plates — or coils — bending the beam sideways and up-and-down), and the fluorescent screen, coated inside so the spot glows where the beam lands. Set the two deflections working together and the spot can be placed anywhere, or swept in any pattern, across the screen.

## Sub lesson 3 — The oscilloscope — measuring with the trace

A CRO adds a time base: a steadily rising voltage sweeps the spot left to right at a known rate, then flies it back to start again. Connect a signal to the vertical plates and the spot draws the signal's graph — voltage up-and-down against time left-to-right. From the trace you read a voltage's size (height × the volts-per-division setting) and a wave's period (width of one cycle × the time-per-division setting, frequency = 1/period) — sound, heartbeat signals and a.c. waveforms all become pictures you can measure.

## The key ideas

- Cathode rays are beams of electrons boiled off a heated cathode in a vacuum and accelerated by a high voltage.
- Name evidence that cathode rays are charged particles: straight-line travel and shadows, deflection by electric and magnetic fields.
- A cathode ray tube is an electron gun, a deflection system and a fluorescent screen.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole lesson in order, in your own words — start with the opening activity, then take each sub lesson in turn and finish with what it is used for. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A CRO trace is 6 divisions peak-to-peak at 5 V per division, and one cycle spans 4 divisions at 2 ms per division. Find the peak-to-peak voltage, the period, and the frequency.
2. State two observations that show cathode rays are a stream of negatively charged particles, not light.
3. Name the three working parts of a cathode ray tube and the job of each in one phrase.
