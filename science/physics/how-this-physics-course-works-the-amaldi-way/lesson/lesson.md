# How this Physics course works — the Italian (Amaldi) way

## Do it first
Before you read: open any strong course book at a chapter and ask three questions — where is the theory, where do I watch a problem being solved, and where do I find out whether I am ready? Italy's standard scientific-high-school course, Amaldi's three blue volumes, answers those three questions the same way in every chapter of every volume. This lesson is the map of that design: the three volumes, the chapter machine inside them, and the examination the whole course is aimed at.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three volumes — the journey

The course runs the whole liceo scientifico arc in three volumes. **Volume 1, Mechanics and thermodynamics** (the 2020 new edition), opens with a synthesis of the first two years — velocity, acceleration, forces — then builds vectors, the principles of dynamics and their applications, work and energy, momentum and angular momentum, gravitation, fluids, temperature and gases, and closes with heat, the first law and the second law. **Volume 2, Waves, electric and magnetic field** (2012), carries waves and sound, light waves, the electric field and potential, electrostatics and capacitors, continuous current and current in metals, current in liquids and gases, and the magnetic field. **Volume 3, Induction and electromagnetic waves, relativity and quanta** (2012), is the capstone: induction and alternating current, Maxwell and the electromagnetic spectrum, special relativity, the crisis of classical physics, Bohr's atom, probability waves, fundamental particles, and a closing chapter on the frontiers of physics. Each volume is a complete year-spanning book, and the three together are one course, in one voice, by one author.

## Sub lesson 2 — The chapter machine — theory, model problems, stop-and-think

Every chapter is built from the same parts, in the same order. First the **theory pages**: the physics itself, stated and derived. Inside the theory sit **Fermati a pensare — “stop and think”** prompts: short questions planted at the exact point where a careless reader would slide past a difficulty, forcing a pause before the text moves on. Then come the **Problemi modello — model problems**: complete problems solved in full, step by step, before the student is asked to solve anything — the worked example is not an extra, it is the bridge between theory and exercise. Only after the model problems do the student's own **exercise pages** begin, graded sets that the chapter's index lists separately from the theory pages. Chapters close with a **formula map** — the chapter's equations gathered as one picture — a **test**, and **Sei pronto per la verifica? — “are you ready for the check?”** — a self-test the student takes privately before any teacher's test. Maths is never a separate prerequisite: boxes titled **La matematica per la fisica — the mathematics for the physics** introduce derivatives, integrals and probability exactly at the point where the physics first needs them.

## Sub lesson 3 — The examination frame — and the physics behind decisions

The course is aimed openly at its destination: the **esame di Stato**, Italy's state examination at the end of the liceo. Volume 1's front matter maps the whole course onto the examination framework, and the volume closes with a final chapter, **Verso l'esame — toward the exam**. The 2020 new edition of Volume 1 adds one more strand: **“the physics behind the decisions”** boxes tied to the UN's Agenda 2030 — places where a physics result (energy, climate, resources) is exactly the fact a public decision turns on. Volume 2 attaches **virtual experiments (Esperimento virtuale)** to its chapters, so a phenomenon that cannot be benched in a school lab can still be run, varied and watched. The design's promise, taken whole: no chapter is a surprise in shape, every solved problem is shown before it is set, every chapter tells the student privately whether they are ready, and the examination at the end is the same shape the student has practised for three volumes.

## The key ideas

- Italy's course is Amaldi's three volumes: Vol. 1 mechanics and thermodynamics, Vol. 2 waves and the electric and magnetic fields, Vol. 3 induction, relativity and quanta — one course for the licei scientifici (Zanichelli).
- Every chapter splits **theory pages** from **exercise pages**, and solves **model problems (Problemi modello)** in full before the student's own sets.
- **Fermati a pensare** (“stop and think”) prompts sit inside the theory; **La matematica per la fisica** boxes teach derivatives, integrals and probability exactly when the physics needs them.
- Chapters close with a formula map, a test and the **Sei pronto per la verifica?** self-test; the whole course is mapped onto the **esame di Stato**, and the 2020 Vol. 1 adds Agenda-2030 “physics behind the decisions” boxes.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole story in order: the three volumes and what each carries; the parts every chapter is built from, in order — theory, stop-and-think, model problems, exercises, formula map, self-test; where the maths comes from; and what examination the course is aimed at. If your companion can open an imaginary Amaldi chapter and predict its shape before seeing it, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Name the three Amaldi volumes in order and say what large territory each one carries.
2. A friend says a worked example in a textbook is “giving away the answers.” Answer them in two or three sentences, using the model-problem design.
3. Take any topic you have studied in this Physics title and write its chapter the Amaldi way: list, in order, the parts you would build — and write the one stop-and-think question you would plant in its theory.
