# How this Physics course works — the Italian (Amaldi) way — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the three Amaldi volumes in order, with the territory of each.
**Answer: Volume 1 — mechanics and thermodynamics; Volume 2 — waves, the electric field and the magnetic field; Volume 3 — induction and electromagnetic waves, relativity and quanta.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) In what order do a chapter's main parts come, and why do model problems come before the student's exercises?
**Answer: Theory pages (with stop-and-think prompts), then model problems solved in full, then the student's own exercise pages, closing with a formula map, a test and the ready-for-the-check self-test. Model problems come first so the student watches a complete solution's shape before being asked to produce one — the worked example is the bridge between theory and exercise.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What is “Sei pronto per la verifica?” and when does the student take it?
**Answer: “Are you ready for the check?” — the chapter's closing self-test, taken privately by the student before any teacher's test, so readiness is known to the student first.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What strand does the 2020 new edition of Volume 1 add, and what is it for?
**Answer: “The physics behind the decisions” boxes tied to Agenda 2030 — places where a physics result is exactly the fact a public decision (energy, climate, resources) turns on.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 1) What does Volume 2 attach to its chapters that Volume 1's design does not name?
**Answer: Virtual experiments (Esperimento virtuale) — runnable, variable simulations for phenomena that cannot be benched in a school lab.**
