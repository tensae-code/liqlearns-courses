# Lesson plan — Examination practice — sample papers inside the textbook

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Examination practice — sample papers inside the textbook
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), at the close of the Physics title: this is the course-way lesson for a textbook that carries its own mock examinations as its closing chapter — how to sit them, mark them and learn from them
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a course that closes with four full sample papers inside the textbook itself is finished not when the last topic is read but when the learner can perform across all of it under examination conditions — and each paper, marked strictly and logged by topic, becomes the map for the revision that follows it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. any of the Physics lessons it examines are recommended first, never forced — the papers themselves span the whole course

## Sub lessons
1. **Papers inside the book — the course's own way of closing** — The final year of this course is organised as examination topics, and it does not end with one more topic: it closes with four full sample papers printed inside the book itself, after the last chapter. A sample paper is a complete mock examination — the full spread of the course's topics, set out in examination form, to be sat in examination conditions. Carrying the papers inside the book is the course's own way of saying the work is not finished when the last topic is read: it is finished when the learner can sit down and perform across all of it, on paper, against the clock.
2. **Sitting a sample paper like the real thing** — A sample paper only teaches if it is sat honestly. Give it the full time the real examination allows and no more; sit in one stretch, with no book, no notes and no pausing the clock. Read the whole paper first, start with the questions you can do cleanly, show the working for every calculation — a method shown earns its marks even when the last number slips — and keep an eye on the time so the last question is attempted, not abandoned. What you are practising is not only the physics: it is pacing, choosing, and writing answers a marker can follow.
3. **Marking yourself and closing the gaps** — When the time is up, mark the paper strictly against the expected answers, question by question, and keep a written log: for every mark lost, name the topic it belonged to and the kind of loss — did not know it, knew it but slipped, or ran out of time. The log is the point of the whole exercise: it turns one sitting into a map of exactly which lessons to revisit. Revisit those lessons, do their questions again, and then sit the next sample paper the same honest way. Paper by paper — One, Two, Three, Four — the log should shrink, and the losses that remain should change kind: fewer 'did not know it', more small slips, until the papers stop surprising you.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A sample paper is a complete mock examination printed inside the book, and this course closes with four of them.
2. Say, in their own words: A sample paper teaches only if it is sat honestly — full time, one stretch, no book, no notes, working shown.
3. Mark a finished paper strictly and log every lost mark by topic and by kind of loss.
4. Turn the log into a revision list, revisit those lessons, and sit the next paper the same way.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
