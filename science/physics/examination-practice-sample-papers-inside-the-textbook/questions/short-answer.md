# Examination practice — sample papers inside the textbook — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does carrying the papers inside the book change what 'finishing the course' means?
**Answer: Because the book itself sets the standard of performance, not just coverage: the course is finished not when the last topic has been read but when the learner can sit the book's own complete papers, in examination form and against the clock, across all the topics at once.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A learner does a sample paper over three evenings, looking up two answers as they go, and scores very high. Why is the exercise wasted?
**Answer: The sitting broke every condition that makes a sample paper a measure: it was not one stretch, not against a real clock, and not closed-book, so the score says nothing about examination performance — and the gaps it should have exposed stayed hidden, so no honest log can be made from it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A log shows 10 marks lost on one topic as 'did not know it' and 2 marks lost across five topics as slips. Which loss do you act on first, and why?
**Answer: The 10-mark 'did not know it' loss first: it is the biggest single loss and the only kind that revisiting a lesson can fully remove. Slips are worth care and checking habits, but they are small, scattered and partly luck; the unknown topic is a whole lesson's worth of recoverable marks.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How should the log change from Paper One to Paper Four if the method is working?
**Answer: It should shrink overall, and its losses should change kind: fewer and smaller 'did not know it' entries as the named lessons are revisited, leaving mostly small slips and pacing losses — the papers stop surprising the learner.**
