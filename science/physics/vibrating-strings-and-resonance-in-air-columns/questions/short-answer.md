# Vibrating strings and resonance in air columns — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the three things that set a string's note.
**Answer: Its length, its tension, and its mass per unit length — shorten, tighten or lighten the string and the note rises.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does a closed column miss the even harmonics?
**Answer: Its pattern must have a node at the closed end and an antinode at the open end; only odd multiples of the quarter-wave fundamental fit that pair of conditions.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A closed and an open tube have the same length. Which sounds the lower fundamental, and why?
**Answer: The closed tube — its fundamental fits a quarter-wavelength in the length, twice the wavelength of the open tube's half-wave fit, so its frequency is half.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How does a player raise a wind instrument's note using this lesson?
**Answer: By shortening the effective air column (opening holes, valves or slides) — a shorter column resounds at higher frequencies.**
