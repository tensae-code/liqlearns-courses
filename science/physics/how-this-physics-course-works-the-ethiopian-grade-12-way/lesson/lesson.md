## The five-unit journey

This course runs in five units, in this order. First, **Unit 1 — Application of Physics in Other Fields**: physics at work in medicine, engineering, defense, agriculture and more — applications first, before any new machinery. Then **Unit 2 — Two-Dimensional Motion**: projectile motion — the motion Grade 11's identically titled unit never taught — plus rotation and gravitation. Then **Unit 3 — Fluid Mechanics**: pressure, flow and floating. Then **Unit 4 — Electromagnetism**: fields, induction, motors and transformers. Last, **Unit 5 — Basics of Electronics**: semiconductors, diodes, transistors and logic gates — electronics last, when you have the electricity to build on.

Applications first, then the three big machinery units — two-dimensional motion, fluids, electromagnetism — then electronics. A full Index closes the book, so any topic can be found again by name.

## How every unit speaks to you — "Dear students…"

Each unit opens with an Introduction and **Brainstorming questions addressed directly to you** — the book begins "Dear students…" and talks to the pupil, not over your head. It states its outcomes plainly: "At the end of this unit, you will be able to…". Then the numbered sections proceed with **Discussion questions in tinted boxes**, **Activities** — several of them bench experiments with materials lists — worked examples, Exercises, a summary in bullet points, and End-of-unit questions and problems.

So expect this rhythm in every Grade 12 lesson here: be spoken to directly, brainstorm first, discuss in the tinted boxes, do the bench activity, then work examples and exercises before the bullet summary checks you.

## Ethiopian anchors — look around you

The applications are anchored at home. The **Axum obelisks** and Lalibela's **Bete Giorgis** church and Addis Ababa's **Gotera interchange** illustrate civil engineering. The applications unit repeatedly ties physics to Ethiopian-relevant medicine, defense and agriculture. Physics in this book is not imported scenery — it is the country you live in, explained.

## The key ideas

- The Grade 12 course has five units, in order: Application of Physics in Other Fields; Two-Dimensional Motion; Fluid Mechanics; Electromagnetism; Basics of Electronics.
- Applications come first and electronics last; the three machinery units — motion, fluids, electromagnetism — sit between them.
- Every unit addresses the pupil directly ("Dear students…"), opens with Brainstorming, and uses Discussion questions in tinted boxes and bench Activities with materials lists.
- Ethiopian anchors: the Axum obelisks, Lalibela's Bete Giorgis church and Addis Ababa's Gotera interchange illustrate civil engineering.
