# Matter waves and the uncertainty principle

## Do it first
Before you read: light, the classic wave, was found behaving as particles — photons. In 1924 Louis de Broglie turned the symmetry around: if waves can be particles, cannot particles be waves? Within three years electrons were caught diffracting like waves. This lesson follows that inversion into the atom, and to the limit Heisenberg drew from it — the most misquoted, most consequential inequality in modern physics.

## Sub lesson 1 — De Broglie's hypothesis — every particle a wave

De Broglie assigned every moving particle a wavelength **λ = h/p** — Planck's constant over its momentum. For a thrown ball λ is absurdly small (h is tiny), which is why balls never diffract in daily life. For an **electron** accelerated through tens of volts, λ is the size of an atom's spacing — and in 1927 Davisson and Germer scattered electrons from a nickel crystal and found the **diffraction** pattern of a wave with exactly de Broglie's wavelength. The electron microscope is the hypothesis at work: electron waves, shorter than any light, image what light cannot resolve. Wave–particle duality is thereby universal: not light *or* matter waving, but everything doing both, with λ deciding which face shows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Standing waves in the atom — why Bohr's rungs exist

De Broglie's wave explains Bohr's mysterious postulate. An electron bound in an atom is a wave confined to a loop (later, to a region): only wavelengths that fit the confinement a whole number of times survive as **standing waves** — a loop of circumference holding exactly n wavelengths is precisely Bohr's condition mvr = nħ, with λ = h/mv substituted. Other “orbits” cancel themselves by interference. So the atom's allowed states are its standing-wave patterns — **probability waves** in the full theory: the wave's intensity at a place gives the **probability** of finding the particle there. The electron in an atom is not a tiny planet on a track; it is a pattern of probability filling a region — which is why it never spirals anywhere.

## Sub lesson 3 — Heisenberg's uncertainty principle — the floor on joint knowledge

A wave cannot be pinned to an exact place and an exact wavelength at once: a sharply localised wave is built from many wavelengths (a spread of momenta); a pure single wavelength stretches everywhere. Heisenberg turned that wave fact into the **uncertainty principle: Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π** — the product of position-uncertainty and momentum-uncertainty cannot go below a floor set by h. It is *not* about clumsy measurement disturbing a delicate particle (though measuring does disturb): even a perfect instrument faces it, because a particle with an exact momentum is a wave spread through space — its position is not a hidden exact number that we merely fail to read. Consequences run deep: a confined particle must move (zero-point motion — squeeze Δx and Δp, hence kinetic energy, grows), atoms have the sizes they do partly because of this floor, and the classical dream of tracking exact trajectories ends at the quantum scale.

## The key ideas

- **De Broglie**: every particle has λ = h/p; electron diffraction (Davisson–Germer, 1927) proved matter waves, and the electron microscope uses them.
- Bound electrons are **standing probability waves**: only whole-number fits survive — which is *why* Bohr's allowed states exist; the wave's intensity gives finding-probability.
- **Uncertainty principle**: Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π — a floor set by what a wave is, not by instrument clumsiness; confinement forces motion (zero-point energy).
- Duality is universal; λ's size decides whether the wave face or the particle face shows in a given experiment.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: de Broglie's formula and the 1927 proof; how a standing wave explains Bohr's rungs better than the postulate did; what quantity the wave's intensity measures; the uncertainty inequality in words, and why “our instruments are clumsy” is the wrong explanation of it. If your companion can say why a confined electron cannot simply sit still, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. An electron is accelerated from rest through 100 V, gaining kinetic energy 100 eV. Taking p = √(2mE) with m = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg and 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, estimate its de Broglie wavelength and compare it with an atom's size (≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m).
2. An electron's position is pinned to within an atom, Δx ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m. Estimate the least momentum uncertainty Δp from Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π, and the speed uncertainty that implies (Δv = Δp/m).
3. A dust grain of mass 10⁻⁹ kg moves at 1 mm/s. Compute its de Broglie wavelength and say, from its size, why no one will ever see the grain diffract.
