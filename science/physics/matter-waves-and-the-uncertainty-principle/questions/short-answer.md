# Matter waves and the uncertainty principle — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a thrown cricket ball never show diffraction, although de Broglie's formula applies to it?
**Answer: Its momentum is enormous beside h, so λ = h/p is fantastically small — many orders below any opening or obstacle spacing — so the wave face, while real, can never be observed; the particle face rules completely.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the standing-wave picture improve on Bohr's postulate 1?
**Answer: Bohr asserted allowed orbits without a reason. The wave picture derives them: a confined electron wave survives only when a whole number of wavelengths fits the confinement (for the circular case this gives exactly mvr = nħ); all other patterns interfere destructively and vanish.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A classmate says: “With better instruments we will beat the uncertainty principle.” Correct them.
**Answer: The principle is not an instrument limit. A state with a sharp momentum is literally a wave extended in space, so its position spread is large by construction, and vice versa; the inequality Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π describes what states exist, not what we can measure — perfect instruments face the same floor.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can a confined particle never sit perfectly still (zero-point motion)?
**Answer: Sitting still means Δp = 0 exactly; confined to a region, Δx is finite, so the product would be 0 — below the h/4π floor. Confinement therefore forces a momentum spread, i.e. irreducible motion and kinetic energy: the zero-point energy.**
