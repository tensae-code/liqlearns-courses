# Lesson plan — Matter waves and the uncertainty principle

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Matter waves and the uncertainty principle
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the quantum foundations and Bohr lessons — the deepening of the quantum picture that replaces orbits with waves
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Every particle carries a **wave** of wavelength **λ = h/p** (de Broglie): the electron's wave, not a planetary orbit, is what stands inside the atom — and nature sets a hard floor on joint knowledge, the **uncertainty principle Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π**, not from clumsy instruments but from what a wave *is*
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Compute a de Broglie wavelength and judge when wave behaviour can show.
2. Explain Bohr's allowed states as standing-wave fits, and probability as wave intensity.
3. State Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π, use it in estimates, and refute the “clumsy instruments” reading.
4. Explain zero-point motion from confinement.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
