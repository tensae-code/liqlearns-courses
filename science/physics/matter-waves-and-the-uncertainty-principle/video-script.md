Today: **particles are waves too**. De Broglie gave every moving thing a wavelength — Planck's constant divided by its momentum. For a ball it is far too small to matter; for an electron it is atom-sized, and electrons duly diffract like waves. Inside the atom the electron *is* a wave, standing in the only patterns that fit — which is the real reason Bohr's rungs exist — and its intensity is a probability: where the particle may be found. From that same waviness Heisenberg drew the limit: position times momentum can never both be sharp below h over 4π. Not clumsiness — nature, written as an inequality.
