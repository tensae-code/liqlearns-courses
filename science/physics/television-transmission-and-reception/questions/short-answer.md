# Television transmission and reception — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is the vision signal meaningless to a receiver that has lost synchronisation, even though all the brightness information is present?
**Answer: The brightness values only become a picture when each is placed at the right point of the right line; without the synchronising pulses the receiver's sweep drifts out of step with the camera's, so brightnesses land in the wrong places and the image rolls or tears.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Trace a goal scored in a match from the stadium camera to a home screen, naming each stage once.
**Answer: The camera scans the scene into 625 lines and makes a vision signal; the signal (plus sound on its own carrier) modulates carriers and is broadcast; the home aerial receives all channels; the tuner selects this channel; the receiver separates and detects picture and sound; amplifiers strengthen both; the screen repaints the lines in step with the synchronising pulses while the loudspeaker plays the sound.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A black-and-white scene has one completely black line across it. Describe the vision signal during that line and what the receiver screen does in response.
**Answer: During that line the vision signal stays at its weakest (dark) level from start to end of the sweep, so the screen's spot runs dark across that line, painting a black stripe exactly where the camera saw it.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How is television's use of carriers both the same as radio's and bigger than radio's?
**Answer: Same: signals are modulated onto carriers, stations/channels are separated by frequency and selected by resonance, and detectors recover the originals. Bigger: television sends a far richer signal — a whole picture's lines of brightness, refreshed many times a second, plus synchronising pulses and a separate sound signal — needing a much wider channel.**
