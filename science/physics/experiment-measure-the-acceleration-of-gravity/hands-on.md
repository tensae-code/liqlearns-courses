# Hands-on — Experiment: measure the acceleration of gravity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the practical
1. A free-fall experiment: drop an object through measured distances with a meter stick and timer (converting milliseconds to seconds), repeat for several heights, compute g from the fall equation for each, and graph height against time-squared.

Work with a partner, write down what you measured or observed, and say what the result proves. If apparatus is missing, walk through the steps with drawings and say what you would expect to see.

## Time a falling object
Drop a small stone from a measured height, time the fall three times, and compare with the fall equation. Say why the times differ a little each drop.

## See it and say it
Study this lesson's picture — a falling ball beside a ruler and stopwatch — and name every part of it out loud. Then close the picture and draw it from memory; check your drawing against the lesson.

## Teach it back
Explain this lesson to someone at home using one thing in the room as your example. Their questions show you which part to re-read.
