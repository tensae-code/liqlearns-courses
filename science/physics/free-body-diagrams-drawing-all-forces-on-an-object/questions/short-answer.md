# Free-body diagrams — drawing all the forces on an object — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A diagram of a sliding box includes an arrow for the force the box exerts on the ground. What is wrong?
**Answer: A free-body diagram shows only forces ON the chosen body; the box's force on the ground acts on the ground, so it belongs on the ground's diagram, not the box's.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) List the force cast to check, in order, when drawing a diagram.
**Answer: Weight (mg, down from the centre); normal reaction (perpendicular to the contact surface); friction (along the surface, opposing slip or its tendency); tension (along each rope, away from the body); any applied push or pull.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A body hangs still from two angled ropes. What does the diagram tell you before any numbers?
**Answer: The arrows must balance in both directions: the ropes' vertical components together carry the weight, and their horizontal components cancel each other.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why draw arrows to scale?
**Answer: So the diagram itself shows the physics — a glance reveals which forces dominate and whether balance is plausible before any calculation is trusted.**
