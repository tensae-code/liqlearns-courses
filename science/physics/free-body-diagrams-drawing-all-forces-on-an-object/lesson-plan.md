# Lesson plan — Free-body diagrams — drawing all the forces on an object

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Free-body diagrams — drawing all the forces on an object
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the forces strand — the working tool of the Newton's-laws lessons
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a free-body diagram strips an object out of its scene and draws it alone — a dot or a box — with an arrow for every force acting ON it, each arrow from the object, in the force's direction, with a length standing for its size. Jordan's Grade 10 book teaches the diagram as the first step of every force problem, for coplanar concurrent forces

## Sub lessons
1. **Isolate the body — one object, alone** — Choose the one object whose motion you want, and draw it by itself — a box or a dot — away from the ropes, surfaces and other bodies around it. Everything the surroundings do to the object must now be re-entered as an arrow; anything not drawn does not exist for the problem. Forces the object exerts on other things are NOT drawn — the diagram shows only forces on the chosen body.
2. **One arrow per force — from the object, sized and named** — Work through the cast of forces in a fixed order: weight (from the centre, straight down, mg), contact forces (normal reaction perpendicular to the surface, friction along it opposing slip), pulls (tension along each rope, away from the body), and any applied push or pull. Each arrow starts at the body, points the way the force pushes or pulls it, is drawn to a scale that respects the forces' sizes, and carries its name.
3. **Read the diagram — balance or accelerate** — With the arrows drawn, the physics is arithmetic: resolve any angled arrows into components, add each direction separately, and the resultant tells the story — zero in a direction means balance there (no acceleration), a leftover resultant means acceleration along it by F = ma. Jordan's unit review problems are done this way: diagram first, equations second, numbers last.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A free-body diagram draws ONE isolated object with every force acting ON it
2. Forces the object exerts on others are never drawn
3. The cast, in order: weight, normal reaction, friction, tension, applied forces
4. Arrows start at the body, point with the force, and are sized to scale
5. Resolve, add per direction: zero means balance, a resultant means F = ma
