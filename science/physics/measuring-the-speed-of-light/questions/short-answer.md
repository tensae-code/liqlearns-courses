# Measuring the speed of light — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must light's speed be measured over astronomical distances?
**Answer: Over earthly distances the flight time is far smaller than any timing error (a lantern signal crosses kilometres in microseconds, against tenths of a second of human reaction). Over hundreds of millions of kilometres the delay grows to minutes — measurable.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain Rømer's reasoning in three steps.
**Answer: Io eclipses on a steady 42½-hour schedule. Observed eclipses drift late as Earth recedes from Jupiter and catch up as it approaches. The drift is the light's changing travel time over the changing distance; delay divided into the orbit's diameter gives c.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Compute: light from the Sun takes 500 s and c = 300,000 km/s. How far is the Sun?
**Answer: Distance = 300,000 × 500 = 150,000,000 km — about 150 million km, one astronomical unit.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does c being a limit matter to the wave theory of light?
**Answer: A wave's speed ties its wavelength to its frequency (f = c/λ). With c known and λ measurable, light's frequency is a real number — light joins sound and water waves as a measurable wave, at the top speed there is.**
