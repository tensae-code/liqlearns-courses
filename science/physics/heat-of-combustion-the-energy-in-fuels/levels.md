# Heat of combustion — the energy in fuels — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Combustion is a chemical reaction with oxygen that turns the fuel's stored chemical energy into heat (and gases that carry some away); Different fuels release wildly different heat per kilogram, so the book defines the specific heat of combustion q: the heat given out when 1 kg of the fuel burns completely, measured in J/kg; The school measurement is calorimetry turned around: burn a small mass of fuel under a known mass of water, read the temperature rise, and divide the water's gained heat by the fuel's lost mass — an underestimate, since heat escapes around the water, and saying so is part of the physics — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
