# Lesson plan — Heat of combustion — the energy in fuels

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Heat of combustion — the energy in fuels
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the heat strand — Armenia's Grade 8 §54, fuel combustion priced per kilogram before the heat-engines paragraph
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a fuel is a store of energy that burning unlocks, and fuels are priced by one number — the specific heat of combustion, the heat released by burning one kilogram completely: Q = qm, the figure that lets a kilogram of petrol, gas, wood or coal be compared honestly

## Sub lessons
1. **Burning releases what the fuel stores** — Combustion is a chemical reaction with oxygen that turns the fuel's stored chemical energy into heat (and gases that carry some away). The Armenian Grade 8 book places it at the end of its heat chapter for a reason: after specific heat and melting and vaporisation have priced every way of absorbing heat, combustion prices the supply side — where the heat comes from in the first place.
2. **Specific heat of combustion — heat per kilogram** — Different fuels release wildly different heat per kilogram, so the book defines the specific heat of combustion q: the heat given out when 1 kg of the fuel burns completely, measured in J/kg. Petrol and natural gas sit near 4–5 × 10⁷ J/kg, dry wood near 1 × 10⁷ J/kg. Burn m kilograms and the heat is Q = qm — the multiplication every fuel bill, stove and engine quietly performs.
3. **Measuring it, and choosing fuels by it** — The school measurement is calorimetry turned around: burn a small mass of fuel under a known mass of water, read the temperature rise, and divide the water's gained heat by the fuel's lost mass — an underestimate, since heat escapes around the water, and saying so is part of the physics. With q in hand, choosing a fuel becomes a comparison: energy per kilogram against cost, smoke, storage and what the burning releases — the judgement every heating system and vehicle fleet is built on.
