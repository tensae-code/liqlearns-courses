# Heat of combustion — the energy in fuels — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) A stove burns 0.5 kg of gas (q = 4.6 × 10⁷ J/kg). How much heat is released?
**Answer:** Q = qm = 4.6 × 10⁷ × 0.5 = 2.3 × 10⁷ J.

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is q a fairer way to compare fuels than 'which flame feels hotter'?
**Answer:** It prices every fuel by the same measure — heat per kilogram completely burned — so the comparison ignores flame size, burning speed and the amount used, and compares the stores themselves.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) In the calorimeter experiment, list two places the missing heat goes.
**Answer:** Into the metal can itself, into the surrounding air (and the flame's hot gases carrying heat past the water), and into evaporating water — any two.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A house needs 10⁸ J of heat a day. With wood at q = 1 × 10⁷ J/kg, what mass must burn daily (ideal case)?
**Answer:** m = Q/q = 10⁸ ÷ 10⁷ = 10 kg a day — and more in practice, since no stove delivers all the heat to the house.
