# Lesson plan — Equilibrium of a solid under three non-parallel forces

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Equilibrium of a solid under three non-parallel forces
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 — Physics expansion) — a new lesson built for the Mauritania placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a solid held by three non-parallel forces rests only when the forces are concurrent — their lines of action meet at one point — and their vector sum is zero, which the Mauritanian lycée manuals solve two ways: by the force polygon (the 'dynamique des forces') and by projection on two axes
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Concurrency and the zero sum** — Add a third force and a new condition appears: the three lines of action must all pass through a single point — the forces must be concurrent — as well as cancelling as vectors, F₁ + F₂ + F₃ = 0. If the lines do not concur, the leftover turning effect rotates the body even when the arrows' sizes seem to balance. A person resting in a hammock is the picture: weight at the body's centre, two tensions along the ropes, all three lines meeting at the body's point of balance.
2. **Method one — the force polygon** — Draw the three force arrows head-to-tail at a chosen scale: equilibrium shows itself as a closed triangle — the 'dynamique des forces'. An unknown force is measured straight off the drawing, length and direction. The geometric method is fast and visible, and it checks the second method: any answer the axes give must close the same triangle.
3. **Method two — projection on axes, and the car on the incline** — Choose two axes and project: equilibrium demands ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0 separately, and the two equations price the unknowns algebraically. The Mauritanian worked case is a car held on an incline: the weight splits into mg·sin α along the slope (matched by friction) and mg·cos α into the slope (matched by the normal reaction). A plank leaning on a wall is solved the same way — three forces, two projections, no turning.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Three-force equilibrium needs concurrency: all three lines of action through one point
2. …and the vector sum zero: F₁ + F₂ + F₃ = 0
3. Geometric method: the three arrows drawn head-to-tail close into a force polygon (triangle)
4. Algebraic method: project on two axes — ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0
5. Car on an incline: friction = mg·sin α, normal reaction = mg·cos α
