# Equilibrium of a solid under three non-parallel forces — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is concurrency needed on top of the zero vector sum?
**Answer: If the three lines do not meet at one point, their turning effects do not cancel: the body can rotate even when the vector sum is zero. Concurrency makes the resultant a single force through one point, which the zero sum then kills.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A body's three forces are drawn to scale head-to-tail and the third arrow's tip misses the first arrow's tail by 1 cm (= 2 N). What do you conclude?
**Answer: The forces do not balance: there is a resultant of 2 N in the direction of the gap — the polygon must close exactly for equilibrium.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A 1,000 kg car (g = 10 N/kg) rests on a 30° slope. Find friction and normal reaction.
**Answer: Weight = 10,000 N. Friction = mg·sin α = 10,000 × 0.5 = 5,000 N up the slope. Normal reaction = mg·cos α = 10,000 × 0.87 ≈ 8,700 N.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the two solution methods the Mauritanian manuals teach for three-force equilibrium, and one strength of each.
**Answer: Geometric: the force polygon — fast, visible, measures unknowns off the drawing. Algebraic: projection on two axes (ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0) — exact numbers, works when the drawing is awkward.**
