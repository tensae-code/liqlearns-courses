# Measuring the size of small particles — the row method — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the row method more accurate than measuring one particle directly?
**Answer:** The row is long, so its reading error is a small fraction of the length; dividing by N shrinks that error N-fold as well, while a single particle's reading carries the full error on a tiny length.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) State the three conditions a row must satisfy for the division to be fair.
**Answer:** The particles must be equal in size, packed tight with no gaps or overlaps, and counted exactly — the row must also lie straight along the scale.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How is the Grade 11 oil-film lab the row method's relative?
**Answer:** Both replace one unmeasurable size with a measurable crowd measure and a division: length of N in a line ÷ N, or volume of a one-molecule film ÷ its area.

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A pupil's row of 30 beads reads 91 mm, but two beads overlapped. Is the answer too big or too small, and why?
**Answer:** Too small: the overlap makes the row shorter than 30 beads laid properly would be, so L ÷ 30 divides a too-short length — each computed size comes out below the true one. The row must be rebuilt with the beads just touching.
