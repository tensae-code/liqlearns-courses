# Video script — Measuring the size of small particles — the row method

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **when one particle is too small to measure, measure many in a row and divide: lay N equal particles tight in a line, read the row's whole length L, and one particle's size is L ÷ N — the row method, which Armenia's pupils meet at Grade 7 on grains and again at Grade 11 on molecules themselves**. A single millet grain, shot pellet or ball bearing defeats a millimetre ruler: the reading error is as big as the thing. Then the lesson builds its three parts in order — too small to measure one — line them up; the row method — length divided by number; the same idea at molecule scale — the grade 11 lab — each one first met in the hand, then said in words.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
