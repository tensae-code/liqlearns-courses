# How this Physics course works — the Yemeni Grade 10 way

## Do it first
Before you read: you open a new physics unit and, before any teaching, the first page tells you exactly what you will be able to do by the end of it — and gives the unit's title in English as well as Arabic. Why would a course put its objectives first, and what else is built into every unit? This lesson reads the Grade 10 course the Yemeni way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Objectives first, titles in two languages

Every Yemeni unit opens with its printed unit objectives (أهداف الوحدة) — a short list of what the learner will be able to do — before the teaching starts. The unit title and the key section titles are also printed in English, so the learner meets the English name of each idea beside its Arabic name from the start. Grade 10 runs in eight units: physics and measurement; motion in a straight line; properties of solids and fluids; work, power and energy; electrostatics; electric current; thermal measurements; and the effect of heat on bodies. Read the objectives first and the whole unit has a destination before it has a first step.

## Sub lesson 2 — Activities inside the lessons, an evaluation at the end

The teaching itself is not read-only: short hands-on activities (نشاط) are embedded inside the lessons, so a new idea is tried with the hands soon after it is met. And every unit closes the same way — with a unit evaluation (تقويم الوحدة), a set of questions that checks the unit's objectives one by one. The shape is a promise: objectives at the front, activities in the middle, evaluation at the back — a unit is finished when its evaluation can be answered, not when its last page is reached.

## Sub lesson 3 — The companion notebook — theory proved in the lab

Grade 10 physics in Yemen is a package of two books. Beside the student book sits a separate activities-and-experiments notebook (الأنشطة والتجارب العملية) with eleven named experiments, one per topic of the student book — Hooke's law, Archimedes' principle, Ohm's law, specific heat, thermal conductivity, linear expansion and the gas laws among them. Each experiment is laid out the same way: aim, theory, apparatus, steps, conclusion — and the apparatus deliberately uses materials from the local environment and the school. The design is the point: theory is proved in the lab notebook, not only stated in the textbook.

## The key ideas

- Every unit opens with printed objectives (أهداف الوحدة) and bilingual Arabic–English unit and key section titles
- Lessons embed short hands-on activities (نشاط), and every unit closes with a unit evaluation (تقويم الوحدة)
- Grade 10 is a two-book package: the student book plus a companion activities-and-experiments notebook of eleven experiments, laid out aim → theory → apparatus → steps → conclusion, using local materials

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: Every unit opens with printed objectives (أهداف الوحدة) and bilingual Arabic–English unit and key section titles; Lessons embed short hands-on activities (نشاط), and every unit closes with a unit evaluation (تقويم الوحدة); Grade 10 is a two-book package: the student book plus a companion activities-and-experiments notebook of eleven experiments, laid out aim → theory → apparatus → steps → conclusion, using local materials. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take any unit you are studying. Before reading it, write its objectives in your own words — three things you should be able to do when it ends.
2. Find one embedded activity (نشاط) in a lesson and do it, or picture it honestly step by step, naming the materials — all of them from your school or home.
3. Choose one experiment from the companion notebook's list (Hooke's law, Ohm's law, the gas laws…) and write its five parts in order: aim, theory, apparatus, steps, conclusion.
