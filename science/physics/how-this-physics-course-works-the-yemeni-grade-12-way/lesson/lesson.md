# How this Physics course works — the Yemeni Grade 12 way

## Do it first
Before you read: most prefaces say “this book will help you”. The Yemeni Grade 12 preface does something rarer — it walks you through every unit of the year, one by one, before you start. What does a student gain from that walk, and what is paired with the book this time? This lesson reads the Grade 12 course the Yemeni way.

## Sub lesson 1 — The preface walks the whole year

The Grade 12 book opens with a preface, spread over two printed pages, that summarises each unit in turn — what the student will meet in momentum and projectiles, in alternating current, in electronics and electronic devices, in atomic physics, radiation and matter, nuclear physics, solar energy and cosmic physics. It is an unusually explicit statement of the Yemeni way at this level: the whole year's map is handed to the student, in the student's own book, before Unit 1 begins.

## Sub lesson 2 — An applications strand in every unit

The preface makes a second promise: every unit carries its applications. The year's physics is not left abstract — alternating current meets the generator, electronics meets devices, radiation meets matter, the nucleus meets reactors, and the Sun meets the solar-energy unit. Reading the preface's walk, a student can already answer, for each unit, the question “what is this for?” — and the unit then has to keep that promise.

## Sub lesson 3 — The companion notebook for the hardest units

Grade 12 is also a two-book package. The activities-and-experiments notebook returns with ten experiments that deliberately track the student book's hardest units: obtaining an alternating current, AC through a capacitor, the resonance circuit, forward bias and the diode's characteristic curve, discharge through gases, the visible solar spectrum, collecting solar energy, and the Geiger counter at work on radioactivity. The pattern from Grade 10 holds to the end of school: the book states it, the notebook makes you measure it.

## The key ideas

- The Grade 12 preface summarises every one of the nine units, unit by unit, before the course begins
- Every unit carries an applications strand — the preface states what each unit's physics is for
- The Grade 12 companion notebook's ten experiments track the hardest units — AC, electronics, discharge, spectra, solar energy and radioactivity

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: The Grade 12 preface summarises every one of the nine units, unit by unit, before the course begins; Every unit carries an applications strand — the preface states what each unit's physics is for; The Grade 12 companion notebook's ten experiments track the hardest units — AC, electronics, discharge, spectra, solar energy and radioactivity. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the nine Grade 12 units in order. Beside each, write the application you would expect from its name — then compare with what the preface promises.
2. Choose the unit you most want to reach (cosmic physics is a popular pick) and write what you must be able to do in the earlier units to be ready for it.
3. Pick one companion-notebook experiment (the resonance circuit, the solar spectrum, the Geiger counter…) and state, in one sentence each, what it measures and which unit it proves.
