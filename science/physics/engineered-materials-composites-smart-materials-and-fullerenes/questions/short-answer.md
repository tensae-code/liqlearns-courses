# Engineered materials — composites, smart materials and fullerenes — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State what pores buy a material and what they cost it.
**Answer: Pores buy lightness, heat insulation and absorption of liquids and sound; they cost strength, because each pore is load-carrying material that is not there.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain the composite bargain in reinforced concrete.
**Answer: Concrete (the matrix) is strong in compression but cracks under tension; steel rods (the reinforcement) carry the tension. Together the beam carries bending loads neither material could carry as well alone.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Name two fullerene-family structures besides C60 and one property that makes the family remarkable.
**Answer: Nanotubes and graphene (sheets). Remarkable properties include extreme strength for their mass (nanotubes) and unusual electrical behaviour from the cage/tube/sheet carbon structures.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Give one everyday use of a photochromic material and say what changes.
**Answer: Spectacle lenses (or smart windows) that darken in bright sunlight and clear in shade: the material's light absorption changes reversibly with the light falling on it.**
