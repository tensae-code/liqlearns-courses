# Lesson plan — Engineered materials — composites, smart materials and fullerenes

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Engineered materials — composites, smart materials and fullerenes

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** some materials are engineered, not found: porous materials trade strength for lightness and insulation, composites combine a reinforcement and a matrix to gain properties neither part has alone, smart materials change a property on cue, and the fullerenes — C60 cages, nanotubes, graphene — are carbon built as architecture

## Sub lessons
1. **Porous and composite materials — building properties on purpose** — A porous material is full of holes on purpose or by nature — the Grade 13 book's §4.6 treats how pores form and grow, and why pores buy lightness, insulation and absorption at the price of strength. A composite goes further: two or more materials combined so the whole has properties neither part has alone — a strong reinforcement held in a binding matrix. Fibreglass (glass fibres in resin), reinforced concrete (steel in concrete) and carbon-fibre panels are the standard examples.
2. **Smart materials and fullerenes — matter that answers back** — Intelligent (smart) materials change a property on cue: shape-memory alloys return to a remembered shape when warmed, piezoelectric crystals trade squeeze for voltage both ways, photochromic glass darkens in sunlight. The fullerenes are carbon built as architecture: C60, the buckyball — 60 carbon atoms in a closed cage of pentagons and hexagons — and its family, nanotubes and graphene sheets, whose cage and tube structures give extreme strength and unusual electrical behaviour. The Grade 13 book's Theme 4 opener gathers unique, composite and intelligent materials and fullerenes as one family: materials designed, not found.
