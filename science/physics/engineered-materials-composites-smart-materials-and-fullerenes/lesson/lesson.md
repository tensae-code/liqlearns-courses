# Engineered materials — composites, smart materials and fullerenes

## Do it first
Before you read: a racing bicycle frame is not steel, not pure carbon, not plastic — it is carbon fibres set in resin, and it is lighter and stronger than any of its ingredients alone. A spectacle lens that darkens by itself in sunlight is not ordinary glass either. What has been done to matter in these objects that nature does not do by itself? Keep the question: which properties were designed in, and how?

## Sub lesson 1 — Porous and composite materials — building properties on purpose

A **porous material** is full of tiny holes — pores — whether nature put them there or a maker did; North Macedonia's Grade 13 book studies how pores form and grow (§4.6). Pores buy lightness, heat insulation and the power to soak up liquids and sound — and they cost strength, because every pore is material that is not there. A **composite** combines two or more materials so the whole beats its parts: a **reinforcement** that carries the load (glass fibres, steel rods, carbon fibres) held in a **matrix** that binds and shapes it (resin, concrete, plastic). Fibreglass, reinforced concrete and carbon-fibre panels are the standard family: the fibres carry the pull, the matrix carries the shape, and the combination is light where a single material would be heavy, strong where it would be weak.

## Sub lesson 2 — Smart materials and fullerenes — matter that answers back

**Smart (intelligent) materials** change one of their properties on cue and change back: **shape-memory alloys** return to a remembered shape when warmed; **piezoelectric** crystals turn a squeeze into a voltage and a voltage into a squeeze; **photochromic** glass darkens in bright light and clears in shade. The **fullerenes** are carbon as architecture: **C60**, the buckyball — 60 carbon atoms closing a cage of pentagons and hexagons, like a football — discovered as a new form of the element beside diamond and graphite; its relatives are **nanotubes**, cages stretched into tubes of extreme strength, and **graphene**, the single-atom sheet the tubes unroll into. Structure is destiny: the same carbon atom makes the softest pencil lead and the hardest diamond — and, arranged as a cage, a molecule that can carry other atoms inside it. The Grade 13 book's Theme 4 gathers unique materials, composites, intelligent materials and fullerenes as one story: properties are designed, not just found.

## The key ideas

- Porous materials trade strength for lightness, insulation and absorption — pores are designed absence of material
- A composite = reinforcement + matrix: the whole has properties neither part has alone (fibreglass, reinforced concrete, carbon fibre)
- Smart materials change a property on cue and change back — shape-memory, piezoelectric, photochromic
- Fullerenes are carbon built as cages and tubes: C60 the buckyball, nanotubes, graphene — structure decides properties
