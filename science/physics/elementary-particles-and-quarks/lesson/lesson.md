# Elementary particles and quarks — the fundamental interactions

## Do it first
Before you read: everything you have ever touched is made of two kinds of quark and one lepton — yet the particle family album holds dozens more, most of them unstable, many found only in cosmic rays and accelerators. This lesson is the family survey that closes the modern-physics arc: who the elementary particles are, how they are organised, and which interactions they answer to.

## Sub lesson 1 — From atoms to quarks — the layers of matter

The twentieth century peeled matter in layers: atoms, then nucleus and electrons, then protons and neutrons inside the nucleus — and then the protons and neutrons themselves opened. Scattering experiments found point-like constituents inside them: **quarks**. The proton is two up quarks and a down (uud), charge +2/3 + 2/3 − 1/3 = +1; the neutron is udd, charge 0. Quarks are never seen alone — the strong force binds them permanently in triplets (baryons: protons, neutrons) or quark–antiquark pairs (mesons). Particles built from quarks are the **hadrons**; the electron's family, the **leptons**, are elementary as far as any experiment can tell and feel no strong force.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three generations and antimatter

The elementary roster comes in **three generations** of rising mass. Quarks: up/down, charm/strange, top/bottom. Leptons: electron and its neutrino, muon and its neutrino, tau and its neutrino. Ordinary matter needs only the first generation; the heavier copies are unstable and decay down to it — they filled the early universe and are remade today in cosmic-ray collisions and accelerators. Every particle also has an **antiparticle** of opposite charge and otherwise mirroring properties — the electron's is the positron — and particle–antiparticle meetings annihilate into energy (the same pair-creation and annihilation the relativity lesson met from the other side, E = mc² spent and repaid). Charge, and several subtler accounts, are conserved through every reaction: the bookkeeping is strict, and particle physics is largely the art of reading it.

## Sub lesson 3 — The interactions and their carriers — and the Higgs

Each fundamental interaction acts through an **exchange particle**. Electromagnetism: the **photon**, acting on charge, infinite range. The strong interaction: **gluons**, acting on quarks' “colour” charge, binding nuclei's constituents — its leftover reach binds the nucleus itself. The weak interaction: the heavy **W and Z bosons**, acting on all quarks and leptons, responsible for beta decay and the Sun's fusion steps, range vanishingly short because its carriers are so massive. Gravitation stands apart in the Standard Model — universally attractive, described by general relativity, its expected carrier the graviton still undetected. (The companion lesson **The three fundamental interactions** treats the interactions' own story; this lesson's subject is the particle roster they act on.) Mass itself completes the survey: particles acquire it by interacting with the **Higgs field**, whose particle — the Higgs boson — was found at CERN in 2012, the Standard Model's last predicted piece.

## The key ideas

- Protons (uud) and neutrons (udd) are **quark** triplets; quarks are confined inside **hadrons**; **leptons** (electron, muon, tau, neutrinos) are elementary and feel no strong force.
- Three **generations** of quarks and leptons, rising in mass; ordinary matter uses only the first; heavier particles decay down to it.
- Every particle has an **antiparticle**; meetings annihilate to energy. Charges and the other accounts are conserved in all reactions.
- Forces ride on **exchange particles** — photon, gluons, W/Z — and mass comes from the **Higgs field**, whose boson CERN found in 2012.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: the layers from atom to quark, with the proton's and neutron's quark content and charges; the three generations and why daily matter uses one; what antimatter is and what happens when it meets matter; the carrier of each interaction, and what the Higgs particle's discovery completed. If your companion can say why no one has ever photographed a lone quark, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Compute the total charge of a baryon made of quarks u, u, s (charges: u +2/3, d −1/3, s −1/3, in units of the proton charge) and say whether it could be a proton.
2. A muon is called “a heavy electron.” From the generations picture, say what that means, why muons do not appear in ordinary matter, and what a muon eventually does.
3. An electron and a positron annihilate at rest. Using E = mc² with the electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, find the total energy released, and say in what form it leaves.
