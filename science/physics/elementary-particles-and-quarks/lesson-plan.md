# Lesson plan — Elementary particles and quarks — the fundamental interactions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Elementary particles and quarks — the fundamental interactions
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the nuclear and modern-physics lessons — the particle survey that completes the modern-physics arc; Brazil's The three fundamental interactions (`physics/the-three-fundamental-interactions`) teaches the interactions themselves and is cross-referenced, not duplicated
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Matter is built from **quarks and leptons** in three generations; the proton and neutron are quark triplets, forces are carried by **exchange particles** (photon, gluons, W and Z, and the graviton in the theory's expectation), antimatter mirrors every particle, and the **Higgs boson** completes the Standard Model's account of mass
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the quark content and charge of proton and neutron, and say why quarks are never isolated.
2. Lay out the three generations of quarks and leptons and antimatter's rule.
3. Match each interaction to its exchange particle and range character.
4. Say what the Higgs field and boson account for and when the boson was found.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
