# Elementary particles and quarks — the fundamental interactions — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Show, from quark charges, why the neutron is neutral and the proton is +1.
**Answer: Up = +2/3, down = −1/3 (proton-charge units). Proton uud: 2/3 + 2/3 − 1/3 = +1. Neutron udd: 2/3 − 1/3 − 1/3 = 0.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why are there no muon atoms in ordinary matter, although muons are made constantly in cosmic-ray collisions?
**Answer: The muon is unstable: it decays (to an electron and neutrinos) in about two microseconds. Ordinary matter is built only from the stable first-generation particles — up, down, electron — that survive indefinitely.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What happens when a particle meets its antiparticle, and which equation keeps the account?
**Answer: They annihilate: their mass-energy converts entirely to radiation (typically two gamma photons for electron–positron), and the reverse — pair creation — can spend radiant energy to make the pair. E = mc² keeps the account both ways.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the weak interaction's range so short when electromagnetism's is infinite?
**Answer: Range is set by the carrier's mass: the photon is massless, so its reach is infinite; the W and Z carriers are extremely massive (about 80–90 proton masses), so the weak interaction they mediate acts only over sub-nuclear distances.**
