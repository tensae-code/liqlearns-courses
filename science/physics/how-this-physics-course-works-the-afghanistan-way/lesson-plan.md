# Lesson plan — How this Physics course works — the Afghanistan way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** How this Physics course works — the Afghanistan way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md) — a new lesson built for the Afghanistan placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Afghanistan's physics course runs in the reverse order of most countries in this library: Grade 9 opens the subject with motion, waves and current electricity, Grade 10 is a full optics + electricity + magnetism year that opens with an explicit “What is physics?” chapter, and only Grade 11 turns to the mechanics year — equilibrium, motion, Newton's laws, energy, momentum and fluids — before Grade 12 closes with oscillations, waves, thermal properties, atomic physics and nuclear physics
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Grade 9 — the first physics book** — Afghanistan's physics begins in Grade 9 with one-dimensional motion (position, average velocity, acceleration — with Pythagoras used to find displacement), then oscillations, waves and sound in one chapter, then a long current-electricity chapter — current, potential difference, resistance, Ohm's law applied, joining resistors, the battery, and a full section on protecting against electrical hazards — closing with electromagnetism: the magnetic effect of a current, the galvanometer, induction, the electromagnet, and the bicycle dynamo and electric doorbell as worked applications.
2. **Grade 10 — light and electricity before mechanics** — Grade 10 opens with an explicit “What is physics?” chapter — introduction, brief history and the language of physics — then measurement, and then goes straight to light: properties of light, reflection and spherical mirrors, refraction, prisms and the rainbow, lenses with the eye, the camera and the microscope. The year continues with static electricity (charge, field, potential, capacitors), circuits (resistance, EMF, Kirchhoff's laws), magnetism, and electromagnetic induction with alternating current — RL, RC and LC circuits, transformers and generators. Mechanics is deliberately NOT in this book.
3. **Grade 11 — the mechanics year — and the Grade 12 finale** — Grade 11 is a full year of mechanics and fluids: mechanical equilibrium (force as a vector, torque, couples, the conditions of equilibrium, taught with levers, cranes and building machines), one- and two-dimensional motion, Newton's laws with friction, gravitation, lift and satellite orbits, work, energy and power, linear momentum and collisions, fluids at rest (pressure, the hydraulic press, Archimedes) and moving fluids (continuity, Bernoulli, the Venturi tube, viscosity). Grade 12 then closes the course with oscillations and simple harmonic motion, waves and their motion (including diffraction and polarisation of light), mechanical and thermal properties of matter (including Wien's law and the Stefan–Boltzmann law), a full atomic-physics chapter and a full nuclear chapter.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first and do the opening activity with real objects or numbers before reading any explanation; the explanation is the story of that activity.
- Mixes the sub lessons up → re-read the key ideas in order; each key idea is one sub lesson compressed to a line.
