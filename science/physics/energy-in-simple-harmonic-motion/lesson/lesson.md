# Energy in simple harmonic motion

## Do it first
Before you read: a pendulum bob is released from one side. It stops dead for an instant at each end of its swing, yet a moment later it is moving at its fastest. Where does the speed come from if at the ends it has none — and where does it go? Keep the question: what are the two stores, and what is traded between them?

## Sub lesson 1 — Two stores trading energy

An oscillator carries its energy in two stores. A pendulum raised to one side holds **gravitational potential energy** and no kinetic energy; falling through the centre it trades the lot for **kinetic energy**, moving fastest where the potential store is empty; rising on the other side it trades back. A mass on a spring does the same with **elastic potential energy** in the stretched or squeezed spring. Iran's Grade 12 books give this trading its own section, §3-3, right after the sections that define the motion itself (§§3-1–3-2).

## Sub lesson 2 — The total stays — and what sets it

With no friction, nothing is lost: the sum of kinetic and potential energy is the same at every point of the swing, and at the extremes it is all potential, at the centre all kinetic. The total is set by the **amplitude** — double the amplitude of a spring oscillator and its stored energy grows four times over (E = ½kA²), because both the stretch and the force grow. The trading happens twice per period for each store: kinetic energy peaks at both passes through the centre. Real oscillators leak energy to friction and air resistance, which is the bridge to the damping and resonance lessons.

## The key ideas

- An oscillator's energy lives in two stores: kinetic (motion) and potential (position or stretch)
- At the extremes: all potential, speed zero. At the centre: all kinetic, speed greatest
- With no friction the total is conserved; the amplitude fixes the total (for a spring, E = ½kA²)
- Each store fills and empties twice per period; friction is the leak that shrinks real oscillations

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: in simple harmonic motion the energy trades between potential and kinetic stores — all potential at the extremes, all kinetic at the centre — and the total stays constant, set by the amplitude. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.
