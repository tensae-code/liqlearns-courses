# Energy in simple harmonic motion — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Describe the energy trading of a pendulum over half a period, from one extreme to the other.
**Answer: It starts all potential and stationary; the potential store empties into kinetic energy as it falls to the centre, then the kinetic store empties back into potential as it climbs, ending all potential and stationary on the other side.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) At a point halfway between centre and extreme, what can be said about the two stores?
**Answer: Both hold part of the energy — the oscillator has some speed (kinetic) and is displaced from equilibrium (potential); together they make the constant total.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does E = ½kA² mean amplitude, not frequency, fixes a spring oscillator's energy?
**Answer: The total equals the potential energy at the extreme, which is ½k times the maximum stretch squared — the amplitude A; the frequency depends on k and m but does not appear in the energy total.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A real pendulum's extremes shrink over time. Account for the missing energy.
**Answer: Friction at the pivot and air resistance do negative work on the oscillator each swing, carrying the energy away as heat and air motion — the mechanical total is not conserved once the leak is counted.**
