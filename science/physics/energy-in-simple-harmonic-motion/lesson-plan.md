# Lesson plan — Energy in simple harmonic motion

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an oscillator's energy trades endlessly between a kinetic store and a potential store — all potential at the extremes, all kinetic at the centre — and with no friction the total never changes, fixed by the amplitude

## Sub lessons
1. **Two stores trading energy** — An oscillator carries its energy in two stores. A pendulum raised to one side holds gravitational potential energy and no kinetic energy; falling through the centre it trades the lot for kinetic energy, moving fastest where the potential store is empty; rising on the other side it trades back. A mass on a spring does the same with elastic potential energy in the stretched or squeezed spring. Iran's Grade 12 books give this trading its own section, §3-3, right after the sections that define the motion itself.
2. **The total stays — and what sets it** — With no friction, nothing is lost: the sum of kinetic and potential energy is the same at every point of the swing, E = ½mv² + potential, and at the extremes it is all potential, at the centre all kinetic. The total is set by the amplitude — double the amplitude of a spring oscillator and its stored energy grows four times over (E = ½kA²), because both the stretch and the force grow. The trading happens twice per period for each store: kinetic energy peaks at both passes through the centre. Real oscillators leak energy to friction and air resistance, which is the bridge to the damping and resonance lessons.
