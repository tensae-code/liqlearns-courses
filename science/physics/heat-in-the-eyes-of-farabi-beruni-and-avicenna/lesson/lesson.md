# Heat in the eyes of Farabi, Beruni and Avicenna

## Do it first
Before you read: you know heat is particles moving. But what did clever people think heat was 1,000 years ago, with no thermometer and no particle theory? Their answers were wrong in detail and sharp in method. Keep both halves in mind.

## Sub lesson 1 — Farabi — sorting heat into the sciences

Abu Nasr al-Farabi (c. 872–950), from Farab on the Syr Darya, wrote the great Classification of the Sciences, the map that told scholars where each kind of knowledge lives. Heat and cold sat inside natural philosophy: qualities bodies take on and lose, studied by observing how fire, sun, friction and mixing change them. Farabi's gift was order: heat was not magic or omen but a topic with causes, placed in a system where it could be argued about and taught — the first step toward it becoming physics.

## Sub lesson 2 — Beruni — measuring heat where he found it

Beruni treated heat the way he treated everything: measure it. He described and compared hot springs, noted how the Sun's heat varies with season and place, and asked how heat passes through bodies — questions he pressed on Avicenna in their correspondence, including whether heat could travel through empty space as light seems to. He had no thermometer, so his instrument was comparison: this spring against that, this season against that. The Grade 6 book places his heat work inside the thermal chapter because the habit — compare, measure, question — is the chapter's real foundation.

## Sub lesson 3 — Avicenna — heat in the body and in medicine

Ibn Sina (Avicenna) made heat a working tool of medicine: the Canon describes the body's own heat, fevers as its disorder, and heating and cooling as treatments to be dosed like drugs. He reasoned about heat as bound up with the motion and mixture of a body's parts — not the modern kinetic theory, but facing the same facts a doctor meets daily: friction warms, fever burns, a cold compress draws heat away. Read together, the three scholars show heat before the thermometer: classified by Farabi, measured by Beruni, used by Avicenna — the road the thermal chapter walks on.

## The key ideas

- Farabi classified heat and cold inside natural philosophy — a topic with causes, not magic
- Beruni measured heat by comparison — hot springs, seasons, places — and questioned how heat travels, even through empty space
- Avicenna built heat into medicine: the body's heat, fever as its disorder, heating and cooling as dosed treatments
- Their method — classify, measure, use — is the foundation under the modern kinetic account of heat

Tell your companion: before the thermometer, Central Asia's scholars reasoned about heat as a quality of bodies tied to motion and mixture: Farabi classified the sciences of heat and cold, Beruni measured hot springs and questioned how heat travels, and Avicenna built heat into his medicine — the historical root of the heat chapter.
