# Electrical energy consumed — the meter, E = P·t and the electricity bill — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is household energy counted in kilowatt-hours instead of joules?
**Answer: In joules the numbers are enormous and unwieldy; the kilowatt-hour — a 1 kW appliance running for one hour — is a size that fits household consumption and bills.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A meter reads 8,120 kWh at the start of a week and 8,155 kWh at the end. How much energy did the home consume?
**Answer: 8,155 − 8,120 = 35 kWh — thirty-five units in the week.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A home consumed 150 units and the price is 1.2 money units per kWh. Work out the energy charge.
**Answer: 150 × 1.2 = 180 money units, before any fixed charges and taxes the bill itself names.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) A kettle is rated 2 kW and a charger 0.01 kW. The charger runs 10 hours; how long would the kettle need to run to consume the same energy?
**Answer: The charger uses 0.01 × 10 = 0.1 kWh; the kettle uses 0.1 kWh in 0.1 ÷ 2 = 0.05 hours — three minutes. Power and time trade against each other.**
