## Sub lessons
1. **Electrical energy — power multiplied by time** — An appliance's power rating says how fast it uses energy; how much energy it actually consumes depends on how long it runs: E = P·t. In joules the numbers turn enormous, so household energy is counted in a bigger unit, the kilowatt-hour — the energy of a 1 kW appliance running for one hour.
2. **The meter — counting the units a home consumes** — The electricity meter at the entry of a home counts the electrical energy the home consumes, in kilowatt-hours — the “units” of the bill. Reading it at two dates, and subtracting, gives the consumption between them; every appliance in the house adds its own P·t share to that total.
3. **The electricity bill — from units to money, and where savings live** — A bill prices the counted units: cost = number of kilowatt-hours × price per unit, before any fixed charges and taxes the bill itself names. Working a real bill backwards shows which appliances cost most — the powerful ones and the long-running ones — and that is where saving energy actually saves money.
