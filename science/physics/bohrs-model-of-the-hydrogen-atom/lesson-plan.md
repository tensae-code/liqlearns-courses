# Lesson plan — Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom and energy levels

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom and energy levels
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the atomic and quantum lessons — the first quantum theory of the atom
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bohr's hydrogen atom lets the electron occupy only certain **allowed orbits** with definite energies E_n = −13.6/n² eV; it radiates nothing while in an orbit, and emits or absorbs a **photon of exactly the energy difference** when it jumps between orbits — which is why hydrogen's spectrum is lines, not a rainbow
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the two failures of the classical Rutherford atom.
2. Write Bohr's two postulates and the level formula E_n = −13.6/n² eV, and use both in jump calculations.
3. Place the Lyman, Balmer and Paschen series by the level landed on.
4. Name the model's limits honestly.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
