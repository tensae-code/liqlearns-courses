# Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom and energy levels

## Do it first
Before you read: pass hydrogen's light through a prism and you do not get a rainbow — you get a few bright lines at exact colours, like a barcode. Rutherford's atom could not explain the barcode, nor why its orbiting electron did not simply spiral into the nucleus, radiating all the while. In 1913 Niels Bohr repaired the atom with two daring rules. This lesson is those rules, the energy ladder they build, and the barcode they predict line by line.

## Sub lesson 1 — The problem — Rutherford's atom falls down

Rutherford's atom has a tiny, dense, positive nucleus with electrons circling it. Classical physics gives that picture two fatal wounds. First, an orbiting charge is an accelerating charge, and accelerating charges radiate: the electron should bleed energy continuously and spiral into the nucleus in a fraction of a second — atoms should collapse, and they do not. Second, a spiralling electron would radiate a continuous smear of frequencies — but hydrogen emits only **sharp spectral lines** at exact frequencies, grouped in series (Lyman in the ultraviolet, Balmer in the visible, Paschen in the infrared) with a tidy numerical pattern (Rydberg's formula). Any true model must forbid the collapse and produce the lines.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bohr's postulates — allowed orbits and jumps

Bohr kept the orbits and changed the rules. **Postulate 1 — allowed orbits:** the electron may occupy only certain orbits, those whose angular momentum is a whole multiple of ħ (mvr = nħ, n = 1, 2, 3, …). In an allowed orbit — a **stationary state** — the electron does *not* radiate, classical law notwithstanding. **Postulate 2 — jumps:** the electron changes orbits only in a sudden jump, and the energy difference is carried by a single photon: **hf = E_upper − E_lower** — emitted when it falls, absorbed when it climbs. The allowed energies for hydrogen form a ladder converging upward: **E_n = −13.6 eV / n²**. The ground state n = 1 sits at −13.6 eV; n = 2 at −3.4 eV; the rungs crowd together toward zero at n = ∞, where the electron is free — so **13.6 eV is hydrogen's ionisation energy**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The spectrum explained — and the model's limits

Every line of the barcode is now a jump: a fall from n = 3 to n = 2 releases 13.6·(1/4 − 1/9) = 1.89 eV — the red Balmer line, H-α; falls to n = 1 make the Lyman series, falls to n = 2 the visible Balmer series, falls to n = 3 Paschen's infrared. Absorption is the same ladder climbed: hydrogen gas absorbs exactly the photon energies it can emit, carving dark lines of the same pattern into a continuous spectrum — the pattern by which hydrogen is recognised in distant stars. The model's limits are honest ones: it fails for atoms with more than one electron, says nothing about line brightness, and its little planetary orbits will be replaced within a decade by the probability waves of the next lesson. As a first quantum theory — one integer n quantising the atom — it predicted hydrogen's spectrum from constants of nature, and that was enough to change physics.

## The key ideas

- Bohr's **allowed orbits** (mvr = nħ) are stationary states: the electron in one does not radiate, so the atom cannot collapse.
- Hydrogen's **energy levels**: E_n = −13.6/n² eV — ground state −13.6 eV, rungs crowding toward 0 at n = ∞; **ionisation energy 13.6 eV**.
- A **jump** between levels emits or absorbs one photon with **hf = E_upper − E_lower** — emission lines are falls (Lyman to n = 1, Balmer to n = 2, Paschen to n = 3), absorption the same ladder climbed.
- The model fails for multi-electron atoms and line intensities — the first quantum theory, later deepened by matter waves and the uncertainty principle.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: the two wounds in Rutherford's picture; Bohr's two postulates in your own words; the ladder's numbers for n = 1, 2 and ∞ and what 13.6 eV means; how a named line (the red H-α) is a named jump; and one honest limit of the model. If your companion can say why the spectrum has lines at all, the lesson has landed.
