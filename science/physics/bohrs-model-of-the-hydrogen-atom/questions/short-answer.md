# Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom and energy levels — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the two classical failures Bohr's model had to repair.
**Answer: Collapse: an orbiting (accelerating) charge should radiate, lose energy and spiral into the nucleus. Spectrum: hydrogen emits sharp lines in tidy series, not the continuous smear a spiralling electron would give.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Compute the photon energy for the fall n = 4 → n = 2 in hydrogen.
**Answer: E = 13.6·(1/2² − 1/4²) = 13.6·(0.25 − 0.0625) = 2.55 eV — a Balmer-series photon (blue-green region).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do the rungs — and each spectral series — crowd together as n grows?
**Answer: Because E_n = −13.6/n² eV: differences between neighbouring levels shrink like 1/n² as n rises, converging to 0 at n = ∞, so the photon energies of falls to any fixed lower level crowd toward a series limit.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) State two honest limits of Bohr's model.
**Answer: It fails quantitatively for atoms with more than one electron, and it cannot predict the relative brightness (intensity) of lines — and its fixed planetary orbits are superseded by the wave picture of the next lesson.**
