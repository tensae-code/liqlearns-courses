# Describing harmonic oscillation — amplitude, period, frequency, phase — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A bob swings between extremes 30 cm apart. What is the amplitude?
**Answer: 15 cm — the amplitude is measured from the centre to one extreme, half the full travel.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A heart beats 75 times a minute. Find the period and frequency of the oscillation.
**Answer: Frequency = 75 ÷ 60 = 1.25 Hz; period = 1 ÷ 1.25 = 0.80 s per beat.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A spring oscillator's mass is quadrupled. What happens to its period and to its amplitude?
**Answer: Its period grows (heavier mass, slower rhythm — T ∝ √m, so it doubles); the amplitude is unchanged by the mass — it is set only by how far the mass was displaced at the start.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) At t = 0 an oscillator described by x = A cos(ωt + φ) sits exactly at equilibrium, moving positive. What does that say about φ, in words?
**Answer: cos φ must be 0 at t = 0, so the phase constant is a quarter-cycle offset (φ = −π/2 in the convention where the oscillator moves positive first) — the phase constant records where in the cycle the clock was started.**
