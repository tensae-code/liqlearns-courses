# Describing harmonic oscillation — amplitude, period, frequency, phase

## Do it first
Before you read: set a pendulum swinging and clap once per full swing — out and back to your hand's side. However wide or narrow you start it (keep it gentle), your claps keep the same spacing. The swing's size changed; its rhythm did not. This lesson is the precise vocabulary for that observation — size, rhythm, and where in the cycle things are.

## Sub lesson 1 — Displacement and amplitude — how far from the centre

Amplitude is not the full travel: a pendulum swinging to A on each side travels from −A to +A — a total swing of 2A — but its amplitude is A, the displacement from centre to extreme. Nor does amplitude fade into the period: a bigger swing on a spring has the same rhythm as a small one (that sameness is what makes the motion *harmonic*) — amplitude says how big, never how fast.

## Sub lesson 2 — Period and frequency — how fast the cycle repeats

For a spring, T is set by the mass and the spring's stiffness; for a pendulum, by its length — never by the amplitude (for small swings) and never by how the oscillation was started. Change the mass or the length and T changes; push harder and only A changes. That separation — apparatus fixes the rhythm, the start fixes the size — is the heart of describing any oscillator.

## Sub lesson 3 — Phase — where in the cycle it is

Phase is what lets one equation carry the whole story: give A, ω and φ and the displacement at every instant follows, and — as the next lesson shows — the velocity and acceleration follow too, each with its own phase shift against the displacement. Vietnam's sequence is deliberate: describe completely first (this lesson), then differentiate the description (Bài 3).

## The key ideas

- Displacement x is the signed distance from equilibrium; the amplitude A is the greatest displacement — the size of the oscillation
- The period T is the time of one full cycle; the frequency f = 1/T is cycles per second, in hertz; the angular frequency is ω = 2πf = 2π/T
- Apparatus fixes the rhythm (mass and stiffness for a spring, length for a pendulum); the start fixes the amplitude — the two never mix
- Phase says where in the cycle the oscillator is: x = A cos(ωt + φ); oscillators half a cycle apart are in antiphase

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: A harmonic oscillation is fully described by four quantities: the amplitude A (the greatest displacement from the centre), the period T (the time of one full cycle), the frequency f = 1/T (cycles per second, in hertz), and the phase — where in its cycle the oscillation has got to — with the displacement written x = A cos(ωt + φ), ω = 2πf. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A swing repeats every 0.40 s. Find its frequency and its angular frequency.
2. An oscillator has A = 6.0 cm and φ = 0. Find its displacement at t = 0 and at a quarter period later, using x = A cos(ωt + φ).
3. Two identical pendulums swing with the same amplitude; one is at +A whenever the other is at −A. State their phase difference in cycles and in radians.
