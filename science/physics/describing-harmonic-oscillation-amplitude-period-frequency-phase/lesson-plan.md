# Lesson plan — Describing harmonic oscillation — amplitude, period, frequency, phase

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Describing harmonic oscillation — amplitude, period, frequency, phase
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the oscillations strand — directly after Pendulum and spring oscillations: the vocabulary and quantities that describe any harmonic oscillation
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a harmonic oscillation is fully described by four quantities: the amplitude A (the greatest displacement from the centre), the period T (the time of one full cycle), the frequency f = 1/T (cycles per second, in hertz), and the phase — where in its cycle the oscillation has got to — with the displacement written x = A cos(ωt + φ), ω = 2πf

## Sub lessons
1. **Displacement and amplitude — how far from the centre** — An oscillator — a mass on a spring, a pendulum bob — swings about its **equilibrium** position, the place it rests when undisturbed. Its **displacement x** is its signed distance from that centre at any instant: positive one side, negative the other, zero passing through. The greatest displacement it reaches is the **amplitude A** — the size of the oscillation, fixed by how far it was pulled or pushed at the start. Vietnam's Grade 11 book separates this describing work into its own lesson (Bài 2), after the oscillations themselves are met in Bài 1: first the phenomenon, then the precise words for it.
2. **Period and frequency — how fast the cycle repeats** — One **period T** is the time of one complete cycle — out, back, and ready to repeat identically, measured in seconds. The **frequency f** counts cycles per second: f = 1/T, measured in **hertz (Hz)**. A swing with T = 0.50 s repeats twice a second, f = 2.0 Hz. The pair are one fact in two dialects — rhythm as time-per-cycle or cycles-per-time — and the **angular frequency ω = 2πf = 2π/T** (radians per second) is the third dialect, the one the equations use: in one period the oscillation's phase advances by a full turn, 2π radians.
3. **Phase — where in the cycle it is** — Two identical pendulums can share A, T and f and still differ: one at its extreme while the other passes the centre. That difference is **phase** — the fraction of a cycle completed, counted as an angle: the displacement is x = A cos(ωt + φ), where (ωt + φ) is the phase and φ, the value at t = 0, is the **phase constant** (initial phase). Oscillators moving identically are **in phase** (phase difference 0 or a whole number of cycles); one at +A while the other is at −A are in **antiphase** — half a cycle, π radians, apart.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define amplitude, period and frequency and state their units.
2. Convert between T, f and ω for a given oscillator.
3. Say what sets the period of a spring oscillator and of a pendulum, and what sets only the amplitude.
4. Use x = A cos(ωt + φ) to find a displacement, and explain in phase and antiphase.

## What you need
A pendulum (string and a small weight) and a stopwatch or phone timer for the hands-on; a spring or elastic band and a mass if available.
