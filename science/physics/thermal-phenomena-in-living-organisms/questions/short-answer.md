# Thermal phenomena in living organisms — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Compare a thermos flask and a winter bird: name two shared insulation tricks.
**Answer:** Both trap a layer that barely conducts (vacuum/air gap in the flask, still air in feathers) to slow conduction, and both cut radiation loss (silvered flask walls; the feather coat's layered surfaces). The bird adds behaviour — fluffing — to thicken its layer.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why are burrows a good answer to both desert heat and steppe frost?
**Answer:** Soil a little way down keeps a near-steady temperature: cooler than the midday surface and warmer than the frosty night air. The burrow puts the animal in that steady layer in both seasons.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain how transpiration cools a plant, and its cost.
**Answer:** Water evaporates from the leaves (transpiration); the latent heat comes from the leaf, cooling it — the same physics as sweating. The cost is water itself, which is why desert plants add waxy coatings and small leaves to limit the loss.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A small mammal has short ears and a thick coat; a desert fox has huge ears and thin fur. Read both as heat physics.
**Answer:** The cold-climate animal minimises surface for heat loss (short ears) and maximises insulation (thick coat trapping air). The desert fox does the reverse: large ears are radiators that dump heat, and thin fur lets heat escape.
