# Thermal expansion — things grow when heated

## Do it first
Run hot water over a tight metal jar lid: the lid expands and loosens. Expansion just did a job for you.

## Thermal expansion — things grow when heated — the teaching
Solids expand on heating. For a bar, the length change follows ΔL = αL₀ΔT — the coefficient of linear expansion α times the original length times the temperature change. Liquids and gases expand more than solids. A heated ring's hole expands as if the hole were solid material. Engineers leave expansion gaps in concrete and rails — in the Afar heat a slab needs its gap (the guide's worked answer gives ≈ 0.00047 m for the book's slab) — and a bimetallic strip, two metals expanding unequally, curls as it warms.

**Keep the formula:** ΔL = αL₀ΔT

## The key ideas

- Linear expansion: ΔL = αL₀ΔT.
- Liquids and gases expand more than solids; a heated hole expands as if solid.
- Expansion gaps protect concrete and rails; bimetallic strips curl on heating.
