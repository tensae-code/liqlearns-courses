# Video script — Thermal expansion — things grow when heated

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Solids expand on heating.
For a bar, the length change follows ΔL = αL₀ΔT — the coefficient of linear expansion α times the original length times the temperature change.
Liquids and gases expand more than solids.
A heated ring's hole expands as if the hole were solid material.
Engineers leave expansion gaps in concrete and rails — in the Afar heat a slab needs its gap (the guide's worked answer gives ≈ 0.00047 m for the book's slab) — and a bimetallic strip, two metals expanding unequally, curls as it warms.

Remember: **Linear expansion: ΔL = αL₀ΔT.**

Remember: **Expansion gaps protect concrete and rails; bimetallic strips curl on heating.**
