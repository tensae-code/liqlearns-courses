# The triangular prism — deviation, minimum deviation and dispersion — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A ray enters a prism and the internal refraction angles are 28° and 32°. What is the apex angle, and why must those two angles always be related to it that way?
**Answer: A = 28° + 32° = 60°. The two internal angles and the geometry of the prism's triangle force r₁ + r₂ = A always — the internal ray and the two face normals form a quadrilateral whose angles fix the sum at the apex angle.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is the refractive index measured at minimum deviation rather than at any convenient incidence?
**Answer: At the minimum the path is symmetric, so i₁ = i₂ and r₁ = r₂ = A/2 — the unknowns collapse and n follows from A and δm alone by one exact formula. It is also the setting where δ changes slowest with incidence, so small aiming errors cost least accuracy.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A prism spectroscope shows sunlight as a band with dark lines. Where do the colours come from, and which end of the band lies nearest the prism's base direction?
**Answer: The colours were already mixed in the white sunlight; the prism separates them because n differs by colour, and the ray bends toward the base at both faces — so the whole spectrum lies on the base side of the original direction, violet (most deviated) furthest toward the base, red least.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Calculate n for a prism with A = 60° whose minimum deviation for a colour is 45°.
**Answer: n = sin((A + δm)/2) ÷ sin(A/2) = sin(52.5°) ÷ sin(30°) = 0.793 ÷ 0.500 ≈ 1.59.**
