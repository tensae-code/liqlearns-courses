## Sub lessons
1. **Bending through the prism — deviation** — A ray entering the prism meets the first face and refracts toward the normal, since glass is denser than air; crossing the prism it meets the second face and refracts away from the normal, since it leaves for air. Both bends turn the ray the same way — toward the prism's base, the thick end opposite the apex. The total turn between the ray's original direction and its emerging direction is the angle of deviation, δ.
2. **Minimum deviation and the refractive index** — At one exact incidence the deviation is least — the position of minimum deviation δm. There the ray crosses the prism symmetrically: incidence equals emergence (i₁ = i₂), the internal ray runs parallel to the base, and each refraction angle is exactly A/2. Symmetry collapses the general law into a measuring instrument: n = sin((A + δm)/2) ÷ sin(A/2).
3. **Dispersion — white light split into colours** — Repeat the minimum-deviation measurement colour by colour and n is not one number: it is larger for violet light than for red. So each colour is deviated by its own amount — violet most, red least — and white light entering the prism fans out into the spectrum: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet. That fanning is dispersion, and it belongs to the light, not the glass's mood: Newton recombined the spectrum with a second, inverted prism and recovered white light, proving white light is the mixture.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: A ray is bent at both faces of a prism, both times toward the base; the total bend is the deviation δ = (i₁ + i₂) − A.
2. Say, in their own words: At minimum deviation the ray crosses symmetrically (i₁ = i₂, internal angles A/2), and n = sin((A + δm)/2) ÷ sin(A/2) — the standard exact measure of refractive index.
3. Say, in their own words: A thin prism deviates by δ ≈ (n − 1)A.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Bends the second refraction the wrong way → entering glass bends toward the normal, leaving glass bends away from it; through a prism both turns rotate the ray toward the base.
- Expects n to be one number for all colours → n is a function of colour: violet highest, red lowest — that difference IS dispersion, not measurement error.
