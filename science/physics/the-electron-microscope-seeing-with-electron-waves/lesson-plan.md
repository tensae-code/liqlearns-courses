# Lesson plan — The electron microscope — seeing with electron waves

## Sub lessons
1. **Why light microscopes run out of detail** — Resolution — the fineness of detail an instrument can separate — is bounded by wavelength: two points closer than roughly half the wavelength of the illumination blur into one. Visible light's wavelengths run 400–700 nm, so even a flawless light microscope stops near 200 nm. Bacteria are visible; viruses (20–300 nm) mostly are not; the molecules of a cell never.
2. **Electron waves and magnetic lenses** — The instrument is built like its optical cousin, part for part. A heated cathode (electron gun) emits electrons, accelerated through a high voltage that sets their wavelength. Magnetic coils then play the lenses: a shaped magnetic field bends electron paths exactly as glass bends light — condenser lenses gather the beam onto the specimen, objective and projector lenses magnify the image onto a screen or camera.
3. **What the electron microscope shows** — The reward is magnification up to millions of times with resolution reaching toward the atomic scale. Viruses were first truly seen this way; the ribosomes, membranes and internal machinery of cells — invisible to light — stand out in transmission images; crystal lattices and the defects inside metals photograph directly; in the scanning instrument, whole insects and fracture surfaces appear in dramatic three-dimensional relief.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Resolution is limited by wavelength: light microscopes stop near 200 nm because visible light's wavelength is 400–700 nm.
2. Say, in their own words: Electrons accelerated through high voltages have de Broglie wavelengths of picometres — fine enough, in principle, to resolve atoms.
3. Say, in their own words: Magnetic coils act as lenses for electrons (condenser, objective, projector); the column must be vacuum and specimens thin (transmission) or scanned (scanning).
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Imagines sharper glass would fix the light microscope → the limit is set by the illuminating wavelength, not by lens quality; below ~λ/2 there is no detail to sharpen.
- Expects electron lenses to be glass → electrons are charged particles; shaped magnetic fields bend their paths and do the focusing, tuned by currents.
