# The electron microscope — seeing with electron waves — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) The finest detail a microscope can resolve is limited mainly by…
a) The quality of its glass  b) The wavelength of whatever illuminates the specimen  c) The size of the laboratory  d) The observer's eyesight
**Answer: b) The wavelength of whatever illuminates the specimen**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A light microscope's practical resolution limit is about…
a) 200 nm, set by visible light's wavelength  b) 2 nm  c) 200 µm, set by glass quality  d) There is no limit
**Answer: a) 200 nm, set by visible light's wavelength**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) In an electron microscope, the lenses are…
a) Ground quartz  b) Magnetic coils whose fields bend the electron beam  c) Mirrors  d) Plastic films
**Answer: b) Magnetic coils whose fields bend the electron beam**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) The electron microscope's column is kept in vacuum because…
a) Electrons would otherwise be scattered by air molecules within a very short distance  b) Vacuum speeds the electrons up  c) The cathode needs cold  d) Glass lenses require it
**Answer: a) Electrons would otherwise be scattered by air molecules within a very short distance**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) Which of these did the electron microscope first make truly visible?
a) Bacteria  b) Viruses and the internal machinery of cells  c) Whole insects with the naked-eye look  d) Planets
**Answer: b) Viruses and the internal machinery of cells**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) The transmission electron microscope differs from the scanning type in that…
a) It uses light  b) Its beam passes through an extremely thin specimen to form the image  c) It needs no vacuum  d) It magnifies less than a hand lens
**Answer: b) Its beam passes through an extremely thin specimen to form the image**
