Today: **the electron microscope — seeing with electron waves**. No microscope can separate detail much finer than the wavelength it looks with, and light's wavelength stops its microscopes at about two hundred nanometres. But an electron is a wave too — de Broglie's wave — and accelerated hard, its wavelength shrinks to picometres. Focus those electrons with magnetic coils instead of glass, in a vacuum, and you have an instrument that photographs viruses, the inner machinery of cells, and the atoms' own lattice.
