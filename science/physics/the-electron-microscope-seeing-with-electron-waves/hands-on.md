# Hands-on — The electron microscope — seeing with electron waves

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Compare the two microscopes' worlds
Find published micrographs of the same kind of specimen at light-microscope and electron-microscope scale (a textbook or the lesson's described picture slots serve). List three structures visible only in the electron images. Say aloud the rule: the finer the wavelength of the illumination, the finer the detail that can be resolved — resolution is bought with wavelength.

## Estimate an electron's wavelength
With λ = h/(mv) and the electron's speed after acceleration through 50,000 V (v ≈ 1.3 × 10⁸ m/s, from ½mv² = eV), estimate λ and compare it with green light's 550 nm. (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴, m = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹.) Say aloud the rule: at microscope voltages the electron's wavelength is some ten-thousand times smaller than light's — that is the whole case for the instrument.
