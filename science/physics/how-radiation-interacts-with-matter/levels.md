# How radiation interacts with matter — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A charged particle ploughing through matter ionises atom after atom, paying energy for every ionisation until it stops: alpha, heavy and doubly charged, spends itself in a few centimetres of air or a sheet of paper; beta, lighter and faster, ranges metres in air and millimetres in metal; A photon does not slow down; it survives untouched until a single event removes it: absorbed whole in the photoelectric effect at low energy, scattered with partial loss in the Compton effect at middle energies, or converted to an electron–positron pair above 1; With every layer removing the same fraction of the photons still present, intensity falls exponentially: I = I₀e^(−μx), with μ the absorption coefficient of the material for that radiation — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
