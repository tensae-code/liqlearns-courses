# Video script — Sinusoidal alternating current — period, frequency and rms values with the oscilloscope

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **the current that keeps turning round**. Battery current goes one way. Mains current does not — it swings forward, stops, swings back, fifty times every second, drawing a smooth wave. One whole wave is a cycle. The time of one cycle is its period; the cycles in one second are its frequency — and the two are locked together: frequency is one divided by period. An oscilloscope draws the wave on a screen so we can measure it. And the number on the socket, 230 volts, is not the top of the wave — it is the rms value, the steady value that would do the same work.
