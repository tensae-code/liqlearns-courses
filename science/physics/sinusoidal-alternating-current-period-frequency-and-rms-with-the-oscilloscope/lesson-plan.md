## Sub lessons
1. **Alternating current — a current that keeps reversing** — Direct current flows one way; the mains supply does the opposite — its voltage and current rise, fall back through zero and swing the other way, again and again, tracing a smooth sine wave. One complete swing — up, down and back to the start — is one cycle, and the wave's highest value is its peak (maximum) value, Umax for voltage and Imax for current.
2. **Period and frequency — how long one cycle takes, how many fit in a second** — The period T is the time of one complete cycle, in seconds. The frequency f is the number of cycles in one second, in hertz: f = 1/T. The two say the same thing from opposite ends — a short period means a high frequency — and the mains in Morocco runs at 50 Hz, so one cycle lasts 1/50 of a second, 0.02 s.
3. **The oscilloscope and rms values — reading the wave, and the value that does the work** — An oscilloscope draws the voltage wave on its screen, so period is read off the horizontal (time) scale and peak value off the vertical scale. A steady meter cannot follow the swings, so AC is priced by its rms (effective) value — the steady DC value that would do the same work: Urms = Umax/√2 ≈ 0.71 × Umax, and Irms = Imax/√2. The 230 V named for the mains is an rms value; the wave's peak is higher, about 325 V.
