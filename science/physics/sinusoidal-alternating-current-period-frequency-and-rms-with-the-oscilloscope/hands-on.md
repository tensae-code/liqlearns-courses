# Hands-on — Sinusoidal alternating current — period, frequency and rms values with the oscilloscope

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Trace the wave by hand
On squared paper, draw axes: time across, voltage up and down through a middle zero line. Mark equal steps along the time axis and plot a smooth wave that rises to +3 squares, returns through zero, dips to −3 squares and comes back to zero — one complete cycle spanning 8 time-squares. Now read your own drawing like an oscilloscope: write down the peak value (3 units), count the squares in one cycle (8), and if each time-square stands for 2 ms, work out the period (16 ms) and the frequency (about 63 Hz). Then work out the rms value of your wave (3 ÷ √2 ≈ 2.1 units) and draw a flat dashed line at that height — the steady value that would do the same work as your wave.
