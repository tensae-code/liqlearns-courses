# Lesson plan — The first law of thermodynamics — heat, work and internal energy in one account

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The first law of thermodynamics — heat, work and internal energy in one account
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Slovenia placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one account that ties a system's three energy quantities together — **heat** Q flowing in, **work** W done by the system, and the change in its **internal energy** ΔU — in the first law **ΔU = Q − W**: energy is never created or destroyed, only moved between the store inside the system and the world outside it

## Sub lessons
1. **Three quantities, one system** — Choose the system — say, a gas sealed in a cylinder with a piston. Its **internal energy** U is the total energy stored in its particles' motion and forces; for a gas, it is fixed by the temperature alone. Energy can cross the system's border in exactly two ways: as **heat** Q, flowing because of a temperature difference, and as **work** W, done when a force moves something — the gas pushing the piston out, or the piston squeezing the gas in.
2. **The first law — the account itself** — **ΔU = Q − W**: the change in internal energy equals the heat added to the system minus the work the system does on its surroundings. Signs follow the account: heat flowing in is +, heat flowing out is −; work done by the system is +, work done on the system is −. Nothing appears and nothing vanishes — every joule that enters as heat either stays, raising U, or leaves as work. The first law is the conservation of energy written as bookkeeping.
3. **Special cases — and why engines are this law at work** — Three cases unlock most problems. **Adiabatic** (Q = 0, insulated or too fast for heat to flow): ΔU = −W — compress a gas and its internal energy and temperature rise, which is why a bicycle pump warms. **Isothermal** (ΔU = 0 for a gas at steady temperature): Q = W — all the heat taken in is paid straight out as work. **Cyclic** (the system returns to its start, so ΔU = 0 over the cycle): net work out equals net heat in — a heat engine is exactly this, run round and round.
