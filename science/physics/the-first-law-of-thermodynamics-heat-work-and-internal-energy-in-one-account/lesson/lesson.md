# The first law of thermodynamics — heat, work and internal energy in one account

## Do it first
Before you read: pump a bicycle tyre quickly and feel the pump barrel — it warms up, though nothing hot touched it. Rub your palms together fast — they warm too. In both cases the warming was paid for by work, not by heat flowing in. This lesson is the single account that explains both — and every steam engine and refrigerator besides.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three quantities, one system

Choose the system — say, a gas sealed in a cylinder with a piston. Its **internal energy** U is the total energy stored in its particles' motion and forces; for a gas, it is fixed by the temperature alone. Energy can cross the system's border in exactly two ways: as **heat** Q, flowing because of a temperature difference, and as **work** W, done when a force moves something — the gas pushing the piston out, or the piston squeezing the gas in.

## Sub lesson 2 — The first law — the account itself

**ΔU = Q − W**: the change in internal energy equals the heat added to the system minus the work the system does on its surroundings. Signs follow the account: heat flowing in is +, heat flowing out is −; work done by the system is +, work done on the system is −. Nothing appears and nothing vanishes — every joule that enters as heat either stays, raising U, or leaves as work. The first law is the conservation of energy written as bookkeeping.

## Sub lesson 3 — Special cases — and why engines are this law at work

Three cases unlock most problems. **Adiabatic** (Q = 0, insulated or too fast for heat to flow): ΔU = −W — compress a gas and its internal energy and temperature rise, which is why a bicycle pump warms. **Isothermal** (ΔU = 0 for a gas at steady temperature): Q = W — all the heat taken in is paid straight out as work. **Cyclic** (the system returns to its start, so ΔU = 0 over the cycle): net work out equals net heat in — a heat engine is exactly this, run round and round.

## The key ideas

- A system's internal energy U can change by exactly two routes: heat Q crossing the border and work W done.
- The first law: ΔU = Q − W — heat added minus work done by the system; energy is conserved, never created or destroyed.
- Signs: heat in +, heat out −; work done by the system +, work done on the system −.
- Adiabatic (Q = 0): ΔU = −W — fast compression warms a gas. Isothermal (ΔU = 0): Q = W. Over a full cycle ΔU = 0, so a heat engine's net work out equals its net heat in.

## Do It Yourself
1. A gas absorbs 800 J of heat and expands, doing 350 J of work on its piston. Find ΔU, and state what happens to the gas's temperature.
2. A gas is compressed: 240 J of work is done on it, and it gives out 90 J of heat as it is squeezed. Find ΔU. (Careful with both signs.)
3. A kettle's heating element delivers 2,000 J to water in a sealed, rigid container that does no work. Find ΔU of the water, and name the special case (which term is zero, and why).
