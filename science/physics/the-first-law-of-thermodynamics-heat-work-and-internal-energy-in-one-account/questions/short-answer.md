# The first law of thermodynamics — heat, work and internal energy in one account — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the first law be stated for a named system, not for “the gas” in general?
**Answer: Heat and work are defined by what crosses that system's border: the same joule is work for the gas inside the cylinder and heat or internal-energy change for something else. Without a fixed border and system, Q and W have no definite signs and the account cannot be kept.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A gas gives out 120 J of heat while 250 J of work is done on it. Find ΔU and say whether the temperature rises or falls.
**Answer: Q = −120 J and W = −250 J, so ΔU = Q − W = −120 − (−250) = +130 J: the internal energy rises, so the gas's temperature rises.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) In an isothermal expansion of a gas, where does the energy for the work come from?
**Answer: From heat flowing in: ΔU = 0 at constant temperature, so Q = W — every joule of work the gas does is paid for by a joule of heat absorbed from the surroundings.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can no heat engine turn all its heat into work, cycle after cycle, with nothing else changing?
**Answer: Over a cycle ΔU = 0, so work out must equal the net heat in — heat taken from the hot source minus heat that must be dumped to the cold sink; a cycle that took in heat and returned to its start while dumping none would not close the account the first law keeps (the full statement of the limit is the second law's, carried in the heat-engines lesson).**
