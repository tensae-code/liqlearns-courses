# Centre of gravity of the human body — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Can a centre of gravity lie outside the body? Give an example.
**Answer: Yes — bending forward to touch the toes puts it in the air in front of the hips; the arched high jumper's passes under the bar. A balance point need not lie inside material.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is standing perfectly still tiring, if nothing is moving?
**Answer: The base is small and the body sways, so ankle and leg muscles must correct constantly to keep the line of gravity inside the base.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) How does a wrestler make himself harder to throw, in centre-of-gravity terms?
**Answer: He crouches to lower his centre of gravity and widens his stance to enlarge his base, so a much larger tilt is needed to carry his line of gravity outside it.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why do small children topple over more easily than adults when learning to walk?
**Answer: Their heads are proportionally large, placing their centre of gravity relatively higher, and their base (small feet, unsteady steps) is small — the stability margin is thinner.**
