# The electric motor and back EMF — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A 24 V motor of winding resistance 2 Ω runs with back EMF 20 V. Find the running current and the electrical power converted to mechanical work.
**Answer: I = (V − E′)/R = (24 − 20)/2 = 2 A. The power converted is E′ × I = 20 × 2 = 40 W (the remaining V·I − E′·I = 8 W is I²R heating in the windings).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain why the same motor in question 1 is in danger if its shaft jams.
**Answer: Jammed, the coil stops, so E′ falls to zero. The current becomes V/R = 24/2 = 12 A — six times the running current — and heating rises as I²R, thirty-six times the normal winding heat: the insulation burns unless protection cuts the supply.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A motor's load is suddenly doubled. Trace how the motor answers without any controller.
**Answer: Doubled load slows the rotor slightly. Slower rotation means slower flux change, so E′ dips. With (V − E′) larger, the current rises, the torque rises with the current, and the motor settles at a slightly lower speed delivering the new load — self-regulation through the back EMF.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the back EMF never quite reach the supply voltage in a motor doing work?
**Answer: If E′ equalled V, the current (V − E′)/R would be zero, so there would be no force on the coil sides and no torque to overcome friction and load. The motor settles where E′ sits just far enough below V to pass the current its load demands.**
