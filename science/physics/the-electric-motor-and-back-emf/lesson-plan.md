# Lesson plan — The electric motor and back EMF

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The electric motor and back EMF
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Egypt placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field is torqued round — that is the motor — but the spinning coil is simultaneously a generator, inducing a back EMF against the supply that grows with speed and throttles the current: the motor's own built-in governor, and the reason a stalled or starting motor draws a dangerous current

## Sub lessons
1. **The motor — a coil in a field turns** — Feed current to a coil free to rotate between magnet poles. Each side of the coil is a current-carrying conductor in a field, so each feels a force F = BIL — the two sides are pushed in opposite directions, forming a couple that torques the coil round. When the coil passes the vertical, a split-ring commutator reverses its current, so the torque always drives the same way round: continuous rotation from a steady supply. That is the DC motor — the dynamo run backwards, electricity in, motion out.
2. **Back EMF — the spinning motor becomes a generator** — But the coil is now a loop of wire rotating in a magnetic field — precisely the arrangement of the AC generator. Its flux changes continuously, so an EMF is induced in the coil while it motors. By Lenz's law that EMF opposes the supply driving it: the back EMF, E′. It grows in proportion to the speed of rotation, because a faster spin changes the flux faster.
3. **Starting current, running current and the starter** — At the instant of switch-on the motor is still: no rotation, no back EMF, so the starting current is I_start = V/R — with R a fraction of an ohm, that current can be ten or twenty times the running current, enough to burn the windings or blow the fuse if it lasted. Large motors are started through a starter resistance in series, cut out step by step as the motor gathers speed and builds its own back EMF to take over the limiting.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: The motor: opposite forces on the two sides of a current-carrying coil in a field give a turning couple; the commutator reverses the current each half-turn to keep the torque one-way.
2. Say, in their own words: A spinning motor coil is also a generator: it induces a back EMF E′ opposing the supply, proportional to its speed.
3. Say, in their own words: The motor current is I = (V − E′)/R — at full speed E′ nearly equals V and the current is small; the motor self-regulates its current to its load.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the motor current is fixed by V/R → R is only the winding resistance; the running current is set by (V − E′)/R, and E′ does the governing.
- Calls back EMF a fault or a loss → it is the mechanism that matches input to load: electrical power beyond I²R heating reappears as mechanical work exactly because E′·I is the power converted.
