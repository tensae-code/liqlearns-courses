# The structure of matter — Democritus, Razi, Beruni and Avicenna — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is Democritus' atom called a guess, however brilliant?
**Answer: Because no experiment supported it: for about two thousand years it rested on argument alone, with no way to test whether matter really has indivisible pieces.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Give one everyday fact Razi's atoms-and-void picture explains well.
**Answer: Compression (or mixing): a gas or sponge squeezes because particles can move into the empty spaces — packed matter with no void could not.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) What question did Beruni ask Avicenna, and what was Avicenna's answer?
**Answer: Beruni asked whether the atom can truly be indivisible, given how bodies grow, shrink and move. Avicenna answered that matter is divisible without end and the atom a fiction of the mind.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How did the story end when experiment arrived?
**Answer: Atoms proved real — but divisible: they are built of smaller parts (electrons, protons, neutrons), so both sides of the old argument held part of the truth.**
