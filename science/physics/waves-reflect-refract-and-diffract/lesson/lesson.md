# Waves reflect, refract and diffract

## Do it first
Before you read: stand at a harbour mouth and watch the sea come in: waves bounce off the wall, swing round as the bottom shallows, and spread into every corner behind the breakwater's end. Three behaviours in one glance. This lesson takes them one at a time, in a tank on a table.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reflection — the bounce that keeps the angles

Send straight water waves at a straight barrier in a ripple tank: they come back straight, and the angles are the light chapter's own — angle of reflection equals angle of incidence, measured from the normal. The reflected wave keeps its wavelength, frequency and speed; only its direction is new. Reflection is how an echo returns from a wall, how a harbour wall throws the sea back at itself, and how the fixed end of a rope flips a pulse upside down while a free end returns it upright — the rope cases are the tank's lesson carried into a line.

## Sub lesson 2 — Refraction — bending where the speed changes

Let the waves cross into shallower water — a glass plate lying in the tank. Over the plate the waves slow down; their wavelength shortens (the source's frequency cannot change), and a wavefront arriving at an angle **bends** toward the normal, exactly as light bends entering glass — one behaviour for all waves, because its cause is universal: a speed change at a border, one end of the front slowing before the other. Leave the shallow region and the wave bends back, wavelength restored. Refraction of water waves is why waves arriving at a beach swing round to meet the shore almost parallel, whatever their direction out at sea.

## Sub lesson 3 — Diffraction — spreading through gaps and around edges

Now give the waves a barrier with a gap. Through a wide gap they pass mostly straight, edges fraying; narrow the gap toward the wavelength and the emerging waves spread into **semicircles**, as if the gap itself were a new source — diffraction, strongest when gap and wavelength are comparable. Around an obstacle's edge the same spreading bends waves into the shadow. Sound through a doorway, sea waves spreading inside a harbour mouth, water waves behind a breakwater: each is this sub lesson outdoors. (The shared diffraction-of-waves lesson prices the gap rule in full; here the three behaviours stand together as the travelling wave's complete manners at a border — bounce, bend, spread.)

## The key ideas

- Reflection: angle of reflection = angle of incidence; wavelength, frequency and speed unchanged
- Refraction: where the wave's speed changes (shallower water), the wavelength changes and an angled front bends — frequency never changes at a border
- Diffraction: waves spread through gaps and around edges, strongest when the gap is about a wavelength wide
- One tray of water shows all three — the complete manners of a travelling wave at a border

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: a mechanical wave meeting the world does three things: it **reflects** off a boundary (angle in = angle out), it **refracts** where the medium's depth or nature changes its speed (bending at the border), and it **diffracts** — spreads — through gaps and around edges; the ripple tank shows all three in one tray of water. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Water waves of wavelength 4 cm meet a gap of 4 cm, and later a gap of 40 cm. Compare the spreading in the two cases.
2. Waves cross from deep to shallow water at an angle and slow down. What happens to their frequency, wavelength and direction?
3. A pulse travels down a rope to a fixed end and returns inverted; to a free end it returns upright. Which of the three behaviours is this, and at what kind of border?
