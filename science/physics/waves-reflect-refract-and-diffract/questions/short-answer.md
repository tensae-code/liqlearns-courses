# Waves reflect, refract and diffract — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a wavefront arriving at an angle bend at a speed border, while one arriving square-on does not?
**Answer: At an angle, one end of the front enters the slow region first and slows while the other end is still fast — the front slews round. Square-on, the whole front slows together: wavelength changes, direction cannot.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A reflected pulse returns from a fixed end inverted. What changes and what stays the same?
**Answer: Direction reverses and the pulse flips (sign changes); speed, frequency and wavelength — the medium's properties — stay the same.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain why you can hear a band around a corner but not see it, using this lesson's three behaviours.
**Answer: Sound's wavelength (decimetres to metres) is comparable to doors and corners, so it diffracts strongly into the shadow; light's wavelength is so tiny that its diffraction at such obstacles is negligible — it travels straight, so no seeing around corners.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the three behaviours and the border each one needs.
**Answer: Reflection — a boundary to bounce from; refraction — a border where speed changes; diffraction — a gap or an edge.**
