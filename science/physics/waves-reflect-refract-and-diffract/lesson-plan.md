# Lesson plan — Waves reflect, refract and diffract

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Waves reflect, refract and diffract
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), in the mechanical-waves strand of Selective Compulsory Book 1 — between describing waves and their interference
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a mechanical wave meeting the world does three things: it **reflects** off a boundary (angle in = angle out), it **refracts** where the medium's depth or nature changes its speed (bending at the border), and it **diffracts** — spreads — through gaps and around edges; the ripple tank shows all three in one tray of water

## Sub lessons
1. **Reflection — the bounce that keeps the angles** — Send straight water waves at a straight barrier in a ripple tank: they come back straight, and the angles are the light chapter's own — angle of reflection equals angle of incidence, measured from the normal. The reflected wave keeps its wavelength, frequency and speed; only its direction is new. Reflection is how an echo returns from a wall, how a harbour wall throws the sea back at itself, and how the fixed end of a rope flips a pulse upside down while a free end returns it upright — the rope cases are the tank's lesson carried into a line.
2. **Refraction — bending where the speed changes** — Let the waves cross into shallower water — a glass plate lying in the tank. Over the plate the waves slow down; their wavelength shortens (the source's frequency cannot change), and a wavefront arriving at an angle **bends** toward the normal, exactly as light bends entering glass — one behaviour for all waves, because its cause is universal: a speed change at a border, one end of the front slowing before the other. Leave the shallow region and the wave bends back, wavelength restored. Refraction of water waves is why waves arriving at a beach swing round to meet the shore almost parallel, whatever their direction out at sea.
3. **Diffraction — spreading through gaps and around edges** — Now give the waves a barrier with a gap. Through a wide gap they pass mostly straight, edges fraying; narrow the gap toward the wavelength and the emerging waves spread into **semicircles**, as if the gap itself were a new source — diffraction, strongest when gap and wavelength are comparable. Around an obstacle's edge the same spreading bends waves into the shadow. Sound through a doorway, sea waves spreading inside a harbour mouth, water waves behind a breakwater: each is this sub lesson outdoors. (The shared diffraction-of-waves lesson prices the gap rule in full; here the three behaviours stand together as the travelling wave's complete manners at a border — bounce, bend, spread.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Reflection: angle of reflection = angle of incidence; wavelength, frequency and speed unchanged
2. Refraction: where the wave's speed changes (shallower water), the wavelength changes and an angled front bends — frequency never changes at a border
3. Diffraction: waves spread through gaps and around edges, strongest when the gap is about a wavelength wide
4. One tray of water shows all three — the complete manners of a travelling wave at a border
