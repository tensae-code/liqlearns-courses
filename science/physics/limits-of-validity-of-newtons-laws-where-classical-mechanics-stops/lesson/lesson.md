# Limits of validity of Newton's laws — where classical mechanics stops working

## Do it first
Before you read: a car's speedometer, a falling ball and the Moon all obey Newton's laws so well that engineers land probes with them. But a GPS satellite's clock, left uncorrected, would drift out of step with Earth clocks every day, and no Newtonian rule explains why. The laws have a border — this lesson walks to it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where Newton's laws work — the everyday world

For balls, cars, bridges and planets in their stately orbits, Newton's three laws and F = ma predict motion superbly. The territory has edges, and they can be stated plainly: speeds small beside c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s, objects big beside atoms (~10⁻¹⁰ m), and gravitational fields weak beside a black hole's. Inside the territory the errors are too small to measure with ordinary instruments, which is why the laws ruled physics unchallenged for two centuries — and why every bridge and bicycle still gets designed with them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Too fast — near the speed of light

Push a body toward light speed and the Newtonian account breaks in checkable ways: time runs slower for the fast-moving clock, lengths shrink along the motion, and momentum and energy follow Einstein's rules. Fast particles in accelerators and the muons raining from the upper atmosphere live by relativity's bookkeeping — a muon created high above Earth reaches the ground only because its moving clock runs slow. Nothing massive reaches c at all: the energy needed grows without limit as the speed approaches it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Too small, too strong — atoms and strong gravity

Shrink to the atom and Newton's smooth trajectories fail differently: electrons do not orbit like planets; energy is exchanged in fixed quanta and only probabilities can be stated — quantum physics governs. Strengthen gravity far beyond Earth's and general relativity must be used: Mercury's orbit precesses by an extra 43 arc-seconds per century that Newton cannot explain, and light itself bends near the Sun. The deeper theories contain Newton's: at low speed relativity's formulas reduce to his, so the old laws remain the right tool wherever their territory holds.

## The key ideas

- Newton's laws are exact enough only inside a territory — speeds far below light speed, sizes far above atoms, gravity not extreme
- Near c, relativity governs: moving clocks run slow and nothing massive reaches c
- Inside atoms, quantum physics governs: energy comes in quanta, trajectories give way to probabilities
- Very strong gravity needs general relativity — Mercury's extra precession is the classic proof
- Newton's laws are the low-speed, everyday limit of the deeper theories — not wrong, but bounded

## Do It Yourself
1. A car moves at 30 m/s. As a fraction of c (3 × 10⁸ m/s), how fast is it, and which theory is the right tool for its motion?
2. A muon is created 10 km up and lives 2.2 µs on its own clock — too short, at nearly c, to reach the ground on Newton's account, yet muons do reach the ground. What does relativity say is happening?
3. State the three edges of Newton's territory and name the theory that takes over at each edge.
