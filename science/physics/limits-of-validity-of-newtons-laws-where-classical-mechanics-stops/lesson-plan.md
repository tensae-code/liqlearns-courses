# Lesson plan — Limits of validity of Newton's laws — where classical mechanics stops working

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Limits of validity of Newton's laws — where classical mechanics stops working
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the mechanics strand — directly after Newton's laws and their applications
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Newton's laws are not wrong, but they are not universal: they are exact enough only inside a territory — speeds far below the speed of light, sizes far above the atom, and gravity not too strong. Outside that territory two deeper theories take over — relativity for the very fast and the very strongly gravitating, quantum physics for the very small — and Newton's laws return as the limiting case of both at everyday speeds and sizes

## Sub lessons
1. **Where Newton's laws work — the everyday world** — For balls, cars, bridges, planets in their stately orbits and every machine ever built before the atom was split, Newton's three laws and F = ma predict motion superbly. The territory has edges, and they can be stated plainly: speeds small beside c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s, objects big beside atoms (~10⁻¹⁰ m), and gravitational fields weak beside a black hole's. Inside the territory the errors are too small to measure with ordinary instruments, which is why the laws ruled physics unchallenged for two centuries.
2. **Too fast — near the speed of light** — Push a body toward light speed and the Newtonian account breaks in checkable ways: time runs slower for the fast-moving clock, lengths shrink along the motion, and mass-energy follows Einstein's rules, not p = mv with a fixed mass. Fast particles in accelerators and the muons raining from the upper atmosphere live by relativity's bookkeeping — a muon created high above Earth reaches the ground only because its moving clock runs slow. Nothing massive reaches c at all: the energy needed grows without limit.
3. **Too small, too strong — atoms and strong gravity** — Shrink to the atom and Newton's smooth trajectories fail differently: electrons do not orbit like planets; energy is exchanged in fixed quanta and only probabilities of position can be stated — quantum physics governs. Strengthen gravity far beyond Earth's and relativity must be used in its general form: Mercury's orbit precesses by an extra 43 arc-seconds per century that Newton cannot explain, and light itself bends. The closing point of the Cuban section: the deeper theories contain Newton's — at low speed relativity's formulas reduce to his, so the old laws remain the right tool wherever their territory holds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Newton's laws hold inside a territory: speeds far below c, sizes far above atoms, gravity not extreme
2. Near light speed, relativity replaces the Newtonian account (moving clocks slow; nothing massive reaches c)
3. Inside atoms, quantum physics replaces smooth Newtonian trajectories
4. In very strong gravity, general relativity is needed (Mercury's extra precession)
5. Newton's laws are the limiting case of the deeper theories at everyday speeds — still the right tool there
