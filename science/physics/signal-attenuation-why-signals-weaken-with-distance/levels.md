# Signal attenuation — why signals weaken with distance — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — Attenuation is the weakening of a signal on its journey: spreading (inverse-square), absorption and scattering in the medium, and resistive heating in cables; Losses are counted in decibels: −3 dB halves the power, −10 dB leaves a tenth; a link budget adds up the journey's tolls; Directional aerials fight the spreading tax; optical fibre guides light with fractions of a dB lost per kilometre; The answers: amplify (noise and all), repeat (receive, regenerate, re-transmit — digital repeats best), and guide the signal so it cannot spread — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
