# Signal attenuation — why signals weaken with distance — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A torch's disc on a wall doubles in diameter when you double the distance. Why does its brightness fall to a quarter, not a half?
**Answer: The disc's area grows with the square of its diameter — four times the area painted by the same total light — so each patch receives a quarter as much.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A link loses 3 dB in the cable and 20 dB in free spreading. What fraction of the launched power arrives?
**Answer: Losses add in decibels: 23 dB total. 20 dB leaves 1/100 and another 3 dB halves it: about 1/200 of the launched power arrives.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can't one enormous amplifier at the end replace repeaters on a long link?
**Answer: By the end of a long link the signal has sunk near (or below) the noise picked up on the way; an amplifier boosts signal and accumulated noise together, so the message stays buried — repeaters regenerate the signal while it is still recognisable.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Vietnam's topic runs modulate → transmit → attenuate → receive. Why does attenuation deserve its own lesson in the chain?
**Answer: Because it is the stage that decides whether any of the others matter: modulation and transmission can be perfect and the link still fails if the surviving signal at the receiver is too weak against the noise — designing for the loss is half the engineering.**
