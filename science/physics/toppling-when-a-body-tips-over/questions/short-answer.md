# Toppling — when a body tips over — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does a body whose line of gravity falls outside its base topple with no push at all?
**Answer: Its weight then has an overturning moment about the nearest base edge and nothing restores it, so gravity itself rotates it over.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why is a tall, narrow filing cabinet more dangerous in an earthquake than a low, wide one?
**Answer: Its centre of gravity is high and its base narrow, so a small tilt carries its line of gravity outside the base; the low wide cabinet needs a much larger tilt.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Give the two design rules for stability and one example of each.
**Answer: Lower the centre of gravity (ballast / heavy base / racing-car floor) and widen the base (splayed legs / outriggers / feet apart).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Calculate the tipping angle for a body whose centre of gravity is 0.50 m above the ground and 0.30 m horizontally from the pivot edge.
**Answer: tan θ = 0.30/0.50 = 0.60, so θ ≈ 31°.**
