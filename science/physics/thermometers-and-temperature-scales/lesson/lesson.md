# Thermometers and temperature scales — including making your own

## Do it first
Hold a thermometer in cold water, then warm. Watch the thermometric property — the column — do the measuring for you.

[Picture 21867 — homemade liquid thermometer beside a laboratory one in the same water bath]

## Sub lesson 1 — Thermometers and thermometric properties
A thermometer measures temperature in degrees, using a thermometric property — something that changes steadily with heat, like the length of a liquid column. Give the property room to move, mark its positions, and you have an instrument.

## Sub lesson 2 — The named scales
The named scales fix two points and divide the gap: Celsius uses ice and steam points (0 °C and 100 °C), Fahrenheit sets them at 32 °F and 212 °F, and Kelvin starts at absolute zero. Every scale is an agreement about fixed points and step size.

## Sub lesson 3 — Make and calibrate your own scale
You can design a scale yourself: build a liquid thermometer from local materials, mark the column at the ice point and the steam point, divide the interval evenly, and calibrate it against a real thermometer. Your scale works the day its fixed points are honest.

## The key ideas

- A thermometer uses a thermometric property that changes with heat.
- Celsius fixed points: ice 0 °C, steam 100 °C; Fahrenheit: 32 °F and 212 °F.
- A homemade scale is made by marking two fixed points and dividing evenly, then calibrating.
