# Video script — Thermometers and temperature scales — including making your own

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

A thermometer measures temperature in degrees, using a thermometric property — something that changes steadily with heat, like the length of a liquid column.
Give the property room to move, mark its positions, and you have an instrument.
The named scales fix two points and divide the gap: Celsius uses ice and steam points (0 °C and 100 °C), Fahrenheit sets them at 32 °F and 212 °F, and Kelvin starts at absolute zero.
Every scale is an agreement about fixed points and step size.
You can design a scale yourself: build a liquid thermometer from local materials, mark the column at the ice point and the steam point, divide the interval evenly, and calibrate it against a real thermometer.
Your scale works the day its fixed points are honest.

Remember: **A thermometer uses a thermometric property that changes with heat.**
Remember: **Celsius fixed points: ice 0 °C, steam 100 °C; Fahrenheit: 32 °F and 212 °F.**
Remember: **A homemade scale is made by marking two fixed points and dividing evenly, then calibrating.**
