# Measuring mass and time

## Do it first
Balance two piles of stones by hand, swapping until your hands judge them equal — your hands are a rough balance. Then time a friend's walk across the yard in seconds.

[Picture 21809 — beam balance beside shop scales; sundial beside a stopwatch]

## Sub lesson 1 — Measuring mass
Mass is the amount of matter in a body. Its SI unit is the kilogram. Scientifically, mass is measured with a beam balance — the body on one side, known masses on the other, until the beam settles level. Shop scales do the same job in daily trade, but the beam balance is the laboratory standard.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring time
Time is the duration between an event's start and its end. Its SI unit is the second; minute, hour and day are familiar non-SI units. The sundial — reading time from a shadow — is the non-standard traditional device: clever, but it needs sunshine and gives only a rough reading.

## The key ideas

- Mass is the amount of matter in a body; its SI unit is the kilogram.
- Mass is measured scientifically with a beam balance.
- Time's SI unit is the second; the sundial is a non-standard traditional device.

## Say it to your companion
Explain measuring mass and time to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
