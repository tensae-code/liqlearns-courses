# How molecular speeds are distributed — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A gas's temperature is quadrupled. State what happens to the most probable speed and to the average molecular kinetic energy.
**Answer: Most probable speed doubles (√4 = 2). Average kinetic energy quadruples — it is directly proportional to T.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the mean speed sit above the most probable speed on the curve?
**Answer: The long fast tail pulls the average up: a minority of very fast molecules contributes disproportionately to the mean, while the peak only marks where the count is densest.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Use the distribution to explain why a puddle evaporates faster on a warm day, molecule by molecule.
**Answer: Warming fattens and extends the fast tail: at any moment a larger fraction of surface molecules carry enough energy to escape the liquid's pull, so more leave per second — evaporation is the tail, harvested.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) At the same temperature, why do helium atoms move faster on average than air molecules?
**Answer: Same average energy ½m·v² ̄, much smaller m — so v̄ must be larger by 1/√m; helium's rms speed is roughly √(29/4) ≈ 2.7× air's.**
