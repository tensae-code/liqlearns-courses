# Hands-on — How molecular speeds are distributed

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Build a distribution with dice and a staircase
You cannot track real molecules, but you can grow their curve's twin. (1) Galton-board simulation on paper: drop 200 “molecules” (tally marks) through a triangular peg board sketch — or simulate with dice sums: add two dice 200 times and histogram the totals 2–12. Note the shape: a rise, a peak, a fall — a distribution appearing from pure chance, with a most probable value (7) that is neither the mean of the extremes nor any single throw's story. (2) Now “warm the gas”: add three dice instead and histogram 200 totals — the range widens, the peak flattens and shifts (to 10–11): your curve has done what Maxwell's does with T. (3) Mark on both histograms the peak, the mean and the rms; verify the mean sits right of the peak when the tail is longer on the right. Keep the two histograms: they are the section's figure, grown by your own hands.
