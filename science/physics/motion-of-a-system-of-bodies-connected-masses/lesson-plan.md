# Lesson plan — Motion of a system of bodies — connected masses

## Sub lessons
1. **One system, one acceleration** — When a string joins two bodies — a mass on a table tied to a hanging mass, two trolleys tied together — the string's fixed length forces them to move together: same speed, same acceleration, always. So first treat the joined bodies as ONE body: the external forces on the whole system (the hanging weight, friction where surfaces rub) drive the total mass, F_net = (m₁ + m₂)a. Laos' Grade 10 book gives connected systems their own lesson (Part III, Lesson 13, Motion of the Matter System) in its type-by-type motion part.
2. **Tension — the internal pull** — The tension in the string never appears in the whole-system equation — it is internal, pulling equally both ways. To find it, isolate ONE body and apply F = ma to that body alone: for the mass on a smooth table, the tension is the only horizontal force, so T = m₁a; for the hanging mass, m₂g − T = m₂a. Both routes give the same T, and checking one against the other is the built-in test of the working. A string over a light, smooth pulley keeps the same tension throughout and only changes its direction.
3. **The standard cases the Lao way** — The book's worked-example shape runs the standard cases in order: both masses hanging (the Atwood arrangement — the heavier side falls, a = (m₂ − m₁)g/(m₁ + m₂)); one mass on a smooth table pulled by a hanging mass (a = m₂g/(m₁ + m₂)); then the table case with friction subtracting μm₁g from the drive. Each case is the same two moves — system first for a, one body second for T — and the exercises then vary masses, surfaces and starting pushes.
