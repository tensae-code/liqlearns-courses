# Motion of a system of bodies — connected masses

## Do it first
Before you read: tie two different-sized bottles together with string, hang the heavier one over the edge of a table and hold the lighter one on the table. Let go gently. Both move — but neither falls freely. The heavy bottle cannot fall at g, and the light one cannot stay still. What single number describes both their motions at once? Keep the question while you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — One system, one acceleration

When a string joins two bodies — a mass on a table tied to a hanging mass, two trolleys tied together — the string's fixed length forces them to move together: same speed, same acceleration, always. So first treat the joined bodies as ONE body: the external forces on the whole system (the hanging weight, friction where surfaces rub) drive the total mass, F_net = (m₁ + m₂)a. Laos' Grade 10 book gives connected systems their own lesson (Part III, Lesson 13, Motion of the Matter System) in its type-by-type motion part.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tension — the internal pull

The tension in the string never appears in the whole-system equation — it is internal, pulling equally both ways. To find it, isolate ONE body and apply F = ma to that body alone: for the mass on a smooth table, the tension is the only horizontal force, so T = m₁a; for the hanging mass, m₂g − T = m₂a. Both routes give the same T, and checking one against the other is the built-in test of the working. A string over a light, smooth pulley keeps the same tension throughout and only changes its direction.

## Sub lesson 3 — The standard cases the Lao way

The book's worked-example shape runs the standard cases in order: both masses hanging (the Atwood arrangement — the heavier side falls, a = (m₂ − m₁)g/(m₁ + m₂)); one mass on a smooth table pulled by a hanging mass (a = m₂g/(m₁ + m₂)); then the table case with friction subtracting μm₁g from the drive. Each case is the same two moves — system first for a, one body second for T — and the exercises then vary masses, surfaces and starting pushes.

## The key ideas

- Connected bodies share one acceleration — solve the whole system first: F_net = (total mass) × a
- Tension is internal to the system; find it by isolating one body with F = ma
- A light smooth pulley keeps one tension and only turns the string
- Laos' Grade 10 book teaches systems of bodies as their own lesson (Part III, Lesson 13)

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: bodies joined by a string move as one system — one acceleration for all of them — and the string's tension is an internal force found by isolating a single body after the system equation has given the acceleration. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A 3 kg mass on a smooth table is tied over a pulley to a hanging 2 kg mass. Find the acceleration and the tension (g = 9.8 m/s²).
2. Two masses, 5 kg and 3 kg, hang on opposite sides of a pulley (Atwood arrangement). Find the acceleration and say which side falls.
3. Explain why the tension in the string is less than the hanging mass's weight while the system accelerates.
