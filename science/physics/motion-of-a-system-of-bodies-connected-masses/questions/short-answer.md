# Motion of a system of bodies — connected masses — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must the system be solved before the tension can be found?
**Answer: The tension depends on the acceleration (for one body, T = m₁a), and the acceleration is a property of the whole system — it comes from the external forces on the total mass. System first gives a; isolating a body then gives T.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A hanging mass accelerates downward, yet the tension is less than its weight. Explain with F = ma.
**Answer: For the hanging mass, m₂g − T = m₂a with a > 0, so T = m₂(g − a) < m₂g: part of the weight is 'spent' accelerating the mass itself, leaving less for the string to carry.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A 4 kg table mass is pulled by a 4 kg hanging mass on a smooth table. Find a and T (g = 9.8).
**Answer: System: 4g = (4 + 4)a, so a = g/2 = 4.9 m/s². Isolate the table mass: T = 4 × 4.9 = 19.6 N (half the hanging weight, as the equal-mass case always gives).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What changes when the table in the table-and-hanging-mass case is rough?
**Answer: Kinetic friction μm₁g on the table mass subtracts from the driving force: a = (m₂g − μm₁g)/(m₁ + m₂), and the tension must be reworked from one body with the new a.**
