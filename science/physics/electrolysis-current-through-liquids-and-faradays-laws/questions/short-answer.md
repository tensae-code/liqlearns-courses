# Electrolysis — current through a liquid splits the substance — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Define an electrolyte and name the charge carriers in it.
**Answer: An electrolyte is a liquid (solution or melt) that conducts electricity by moving ions; its carriers are positive cations and negative anions, freed when an ionic substance dissolves or melts.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Describe what is seen at the cathode when copper sulphate solution is electrolysed with inert electrodes, and why.
**Answer: A pink-brown coat of copper grows on the cathode: Cu²⁺ cations drift there, take electrons (discharge) and become copper atoms, which plate onto the electrode surface.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Give two industrial uses of electrolysis.
**Answer: Extracting reactive metals such as aluminium from their molten compounds, refining impure copper, and electroplating objects with a protective or decorative metal coat (any two).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A current of 5 A passes for 965 s through a solution of singly charged ions of a metal with M = 108 g/mol. How much metal is deposited?
**Answer: Q = It = 5 × 965 = 4,825 C. Moles of electrons = 4,825 / 96,500 = 0.05 mol; with z = 1 the metal deposited is 0.05 mol × 108 g/mol = 5.4 g.**
