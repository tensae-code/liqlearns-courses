# Hands-on — Electrolysis — current through a liquid splits the substance

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Plate a coin with copper
Stand a beaker of copper sulphate solution (or vinegar-and-salt water with a piece of copper as the positive electrode) between two electrodes from a low-voltage battery pack: a copper strip as anode (+) and a clean coin as cathode (−). Run the current for some minutes and watch: the coin slowly takes a copper coat while the anode thins. Weigh or time it and reason with Faraday's first law: twice the charge passed — twice the time at the same current — plates twice the copper. School bench work, low voltage only, exactly as the Grade 8 and gymnasium books set current-through-liquids work.
