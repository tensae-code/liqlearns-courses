# Staying safe in extreme heat and cold

## Staying safe in extreme heat and cold — the teaching
Extreme heat harms in bands. Around 32–40 °C, cramps and exhaustion become possible. From 40–54 °C, exhaustion is likely — limit activity, rest and drink. Above about 54 °C, heat stroke threatens, which can kill. The defences are plain: hydrate before you feel desperate, dress for the environment, use sunscreen, and respect shade and rest. Extreme cold demands its own care — cover skin, keep dry, and keep moving warmly.

## The key ideas

- 32–40 °C: heat cramps and exhaustion possible.
- 40–54 °C: exhaustion likely — limit activity; above ~54 °C: heat stroke risk.
- Defences: hydrate, dress for the environment, sunscreen, rest in shade.

## Say it to your companion
Explain staying safe in extreme heat and cold to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
