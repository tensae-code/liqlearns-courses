# Dimensional formulas — checking equations by their dimensions — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Find the dimensional formula of pressure, starting from pressure = force ÷ area.
**Answer: Pressure = force ÷ area = (M L T⁻²) ÷ (L²) = M L⁻¹ T⁻². Each step replaces the quantity by the dimensions of its own defining equation.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Show by dimensions that the guess v = u + a·t² for a final speed must be wrong.
**Answer: [a·t²] = L T⁻² × T² = L, a length — but v and u are speeds, L T⁻¹. A length can never be added to a speed, so the guess fails the homogeneity check and is certainly wrong.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why can the dimensional method never deliver the 2π in T = 2π√(L/g)?
**Answer: Because 2π is a pure number with no dimensions. The method works only with the dimensional parts of a formula, so any dimensionless constant in a law is invisible to it and must come from derivation or measurement.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) A quantity Q is defined by Q = (change in speed) ÷ (time taken) × mass. Write its dimensional formula and name the quantity.
**Answer: (L T⁻¹ ÷ T) × M = M L T⁻² — the dimensions of force; Q is the rate-of-change-of-momentum form of force, F = m·a.**
