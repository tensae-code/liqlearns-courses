# Lesson plan — Dimensional formulas — checking equations by their dimensions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Dimensional formulas — checking equations by their dimensions
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Egypt placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every physical quantity can be written as a product of the base dimensions (mass M, length L, time T, and the rest), and a correct equation must balance dimensionally on its two sides — so dimensions give a fast, free check on any formula before a single number is trusted

## Sub lessons
1. **The dimensional formula of a quantity** — A quantity's dimensional formula says how it is built from the base quantities: write mass as M, length as L, time as T (current I, temperature Θ, amount mol and luminous intensity complete the seven). Speed is length ÷ time, so its formula is L T⁻¹; acceleration is speed ÷ time, L T⁻²; force, from F = ma, is M L T⁻². Area is L², volume L³, density M L⁻³, energy M L² T⁻² and pressure M L⁻¹ T⁻² — each one read straight off the quantity's own defining equation.
2. **Checking an equation by its dimensions** — An equation can only be true if every term in it has the same dimensional formula — quantities can only be added, subtracted or equated when they are the same kind of thing. Test v = u + at: [at] = L T⁻² × T = L T⁻¹, which is exactly [v] and [u], so the equation passes the check. Test s = ut + ½at² term by term: [ut] = L and [at²] = L T⁻² × T² = L, and the ½ is a pure number with no dimensions, so both terms are lengths and the equation is dimensionally sound.
3. **What dimensions can and cannot prove** — Passing the dimension check does not prove an equation right: s = ut + at² (without the ½) passes perfectly, yet it is wrong, because pure numbers — ½, 2, 2π — are invisible to the test. Dimensions also cannot tell apart different quantities that share a formula: work and torque are both M L² T⁻², and frequency and angular speed differ only by a hidden 2π.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Every quantity has a dimensional formula built from the base dimensions — force is M L T⁻², energy M L² T⁻², pressure M L⁻¹ T⁻².
2. Say, in their own words: An equation is dimensionally sound only when every added or equated term has the same dimensional formula.
3. Say, in their own words: The check kills wrong formulas for free (v = u + a/t fails, since a/t is L T⁻³, not a speed) and can even rebuild a law's shape, as √(L/g) for the pendulum's period.
4. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats a pass as a proof → s = ut + at² passes the check and is still wrong; dimensions never see the ½. A pass means "possibly right", a fail means "certainly wrong".
- Adds terms of different kinds → only like kinds can be added: a length and a time can never make a sum in any true equation, exactly as 3 metres + 2 seconds means nothing.
