# Batteries — primary, secondary and fuel cells — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Three 1.5 V cells are joined in series in a torch. What push does the battery give, and why?
**Answer: 4.5 V. In series each cell's electromotive force adds to the next, so the battery's total push is 1.5 + 1.5 + 1.5 = 4.5 V.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why should a primary dry cell not be put on a charger?
**Answer: Its chemistry runs one way only and cannot cleanly run backwards; forcing current backwards can make it leak or burst instead of storing charge again.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A phone battery and a hydrogen bus's fuel cell both power electric motors. State the difference in how each gets its energy.
**Answer: The phone's secondary battery stores chemical energy inside itself and must be recharged from a supply when spent. The fuel cell stores nothing for the journey: it makes electricity continuously from hydrogen and oxygen fed to it, and runs as long as the fuel lasts.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Sort these into primary, secondary and fuel: a remote-control dry cell, a car battery, a hydrogen cell powering a spacecraft.
**Answer: Primary: the remote-control dry cell. Secondary: the car battery (lead–acid, recharged in use). Fuel cell: the hydrogen cell, fed continuously from tanks.**
