# Newton's first law — inertia

## Do it first
Balance a coin on a card over a cup and flick the card away sideways. The coin drops into the cup — it stayed while the card left. That staying is inertia.

## Newton's first law — inertia — the teaching
Newton's first law: a body stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight line at constant velocity, unless an unbalanced force acts on it. Bodies resist changes to their state — that resistance is inertia, and mass is its measure: the bigger the mass, the bigger the inertia. You feel it in a car: brake suddenly and your body lurches forward, still trying to keep its old motion; pull away and you press back into the seat.

## The key ideas

- A body keeps its state unless an unbalanced force acts — Newton's first law.
- Inertia is resistance to change of state; mass measures it.
- A braking car throws passengers forward because their bodies keep the old motion.
