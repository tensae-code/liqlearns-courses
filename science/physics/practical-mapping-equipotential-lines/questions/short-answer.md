# Practical — mapping equipotential lines — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Describe the hunt for one equipotential line in three steps.
**Answer: Fix one voltmeter lead on the reference electrode; move the probe over the conductive paper until it reads the chosen voltage, marking each such point; join the marks — the curve through them is the equipotential line for that voltage.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must field lines cross equipotentials at right angles?
**Answer: The field points along the direction of fastest potential change; along an equipotential the potential does not change at all, so the field has no component along it — it must run perpendicular.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A learner's map shows lines bunching near a small electrode. Read the physics from the map.
**Answer: The potential changes rapidly with distance there — bunched equipotentials mean a strong field, as expected near a small, concentrated electrode.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What does this practical establish before any field formula is used?
**Answer: That potential is a measurable property of place with a mappable pattern, that moving along an equipotential costs no work, and that the field's direction and relative strength can be read from the potential's map.**
