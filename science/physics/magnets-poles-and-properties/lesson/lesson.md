# Magnets — poles and properties

## Do it first
If you have any magnet, hunt what it attracts around the house and sort the attracted from the ignored. No magnet? Two paper clips and a borrowed fridge magnet will teach the same list.

## Magnets — poles and properties — the teaching
Magnets began as lodestone, the natural magnetic stone known since ancient Greece. Today's magnets come shaped — bar, horseshoe, needle — and share strict properties: they attract iron and nickel materials; their strength crowds at two poles; like poles repel and unlike poles attract; and poles always come in pairs — saw a magnet in half and each half grows its own N and S. Permanent magnets keep their power; temporary magnets hold it only while influenced; and rough treatment, heat and time can rob a magnet of its strength.

## The key ideas

- Magnets attract iron and nickel materials; strength crowds at the poles.
- Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.
- Poles always pair — cutting a magnet makes two complete magnets.

## Say it to your companion
Explain magnets — poles and properties to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
