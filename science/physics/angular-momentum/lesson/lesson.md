# Angular momentum

## Do it first
Before you read: a skater spinning slowly pulls her arms in and suddenly whirls. No one pushed her. Something she carried — a quantity of turning — stayed the same while its shape changed, and the speed had to rise to keep the account. That quantity is this lesson: angular momentum.

## Sub lesson 1 — What angular momentum is

For a particle of mass m moving with speed v at perpendicular distance r from an axis, the angular momentum about that axis is **L = mvr** — mass × velocity × lever arm, in kg m²/s. It is the turning counterpart of ordinary momentum mv: where linear momentum measures quantity of straight motion, angular momentum measures quantity of turning motion about the chosen point or axis. For a rigid body turning about a fixed axis the particles' contributions gather into one product: **L = Iω**, with I the moment of inertia about that axis (the subject of the lesson `moment-of-inertia-rotations-mass`, which this lesson builds on) and ω the angular velocity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Torque changes angular momentum

Just as force is the rate of change of linear momentum, **torque is the rate of change of angular momentum: τ = dL/dt**. A torque applied for a time delivers an angular impulse, τ × t = the change in L, mirroring impulse = change in mv. With no torque, dL/dt = 0 — angular momentum does not change. This is Newton's second law for rotation stated in its deepest form, and it holds particle by particle and for whole systems, about any fixed point.

## Sub lesson 3 — Conservation of angular momentum

When the **resultant external torque is zero, L stays constant** — for a rigid body, Iω = constant. The skater pulls in her arms: I falls, so ω rises in the same proportion (halving I doubles ω). The diver tucks to somersault fast and straightens to stop the turning before entry; a planet sweeps its orbit with L fixed, moving faster where its radius is smaller. Note what is not conserved: rotational kinetic energy changes in the skater's spin-up (her muscles do the work of pulling in), while L — the quantity no internal effort can alter — holds exactly.

## The key ideas

- Angular momentum: L = mvr for a particle about an axis; L = Iω for a rigid body (I from `moment-of-inertia-rotations-mass`).
- Torque is its rate of change: τ = dL/dt — the rotational form of Newton's second law.
- Zero external torque ⇒ L conserved: Iω constant, so a body that shrinks its I must spin faster.

## Do It Yourself
1. A 2 kg mass on a 0.5 m string circles at 4 m/s. Find its angular momentum about the centre.
2. A skater with I = 6 kg m² spins at 1 rev/s. Pulling in to I = 2 kg m², what is her new rate of spin?
3. A turntable's I doubles when a load is set gently onto it (no external torque). What happens to its ω?
