# Angular momentum — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does angular momentum need the distance r as well as mv?
**Answer: Because turning effect about an axis depends on the lever arm: the same momentum carried further from the axis turns the system more. L = mvr counts both the motion and its distance from the centre of turning.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A diver leaves the board turning slowly with a straight body and tucks tightly. Explain the speeding somersault.
**Answer: The tuck moves mass closer to the axis, reducing I. With (effectively) no external torque in flight, L = Iω is conserved, so ω must increase as I falls — the somersault speeds up.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) State the condition for conservation of angular momentum, precisely.
**Answer: The resultant external torque about the chosen axis (or point) must be zero; internal torques within the system cannot change the system's total angular momentum.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A wheel has I = 4 kg m² and ω = 5 rad/s. A constant torque of 10 N m acts for 3 s in the spin's direction. Find the new L.
**Answer: Initial L = Iω = 20 kg m²/s. Angular impulse = τt = 30, so new L = 20 + 30 = 50 kg m²/s.**
