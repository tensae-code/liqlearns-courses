# The piston liquid pump — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) 'Pumps suck water up' is the common phrase. Correct it in one sentence.
**Answer:** Pumps do not suck: the piston lowers the pressure in the barrel and the atmosphere pushes the water up into the thinned space.

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) Trace one full stroke pair, naming every valve movement.
**Answer:** Up-stroke: piston valve shut, base valve open, water rises into the barrel under atmospheric pressure. Down-stroke: base valve shut, piston valve open, water passes above the piston — ready to be lifted out on the next up-stroke.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the 10.3 m ceiling a pressure fact and not an engineering failure?
**Answer:** The lift is paid for entirely by atmospheric pressure, which can balance at most a 10.3 m water column (p = ρgh); a perfect vacuum in the barrel cannot pay more than the atmosphere has.

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) What would happen to a piston pump's action if both valves were stuck open?
**Answer:** Nothing would be pumped: water would simply slosh up and down with the piston, the one-way traffic that makes strokes accumulate into a lift would be gone.
