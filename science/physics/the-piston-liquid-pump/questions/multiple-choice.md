# The piston liquid pump — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) In a piston pump, the water is actually pushed up the barrel by…
a) The piston's suction  b) Atmospheric pressure on the water outside  c) Magnetism  d) The valves' springs
**Answer: b) Atmospheric pressure on the water outside**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) During the up-stroke…
a) Both valves are shut  b) The base (inlet) valve opens and water rises into the barrel  c) The piston valve opens and water enters the spout  d) Air is pumped into the well
**Answer: b) The base (inlet) valve opens and water rises into the barrel**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) During the down-stroke…
a) The base valve opens  b) The piston's own valve opens and water passes above the piston  c) Both valves open  d) The piston dissolves
**Answer: b) The piston's own valve opens and water passes above the piston**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) The greatest height a suction pump can lift water is about…
a) 1 m  b) 10.3 m — one atmosphere's worth of water column  c) 100 m  d) Unlimited
**Answer: b) 10.3 m — one atmosphere's worth of water column**

**5.** (Sub lesson 2) A pump must be primed (filled with water) because…
a) Water lubricates the piston's colour  b) Air in the barrel would expand and compress without ever dropping the pressure enough — air leaks and air pockets defeat the pressure difference  c) The valves are shy  d) Tradition demands it
**Answer: b) Air in the barrel would expand and compress without ever dropping the pressure enough — air leaks and air pockets defeat the pressure difference**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) Deeper wells than ten metres are served by…
a) Longer handles  b) Pumps down the well that push the water up from below  c) Faster stroking  d) Wider barrels
**Answer: b) Pumps down the well that push the water up from below**
