# The three fundamental interactions

## Do it first
Before you read: sit still for a moment and list everything that is acting between you and the world right now — what holds you in the chair, what stops you falling through it, what holds your own body together. Write your list down before reading on. Then read on and check it: physics' closing claim is that everything on your list — and everything else that has ever pushed, pulled, shone or held — runs on just **three fundamental interactions**.

## The gravitational interaction — the weakest, ruling the largest

Every mass attracts every other mass. That is the **gravitational interaction**: it acts between masses, it only ever **attracts** (there is no negative mass to repel), and its reach has **no limit** — it weakens with distance but never reaches zero. Between small things it is astonishingly weak: two people standing a metre apart pull on each other with a force far too small to feel. But because it never cancels and never repels, it adds up without opposition wherever matter gathers — and so the weakest interaction rules the largest stage: falling bodies, the Moon's orbit, the planets, the stars, the shape of galaxies.

## The electromagnetic interaction — the ruler of daily life

Between electric charges acts the **electromagnetic interaction**: unlike charges attract, like charges repel, and it is enormously stronger than gravity. This is the interaction of the everyday world. It holds electrons around nuclei, so **atoms exist**; it binds atoms into molecules; and every contact force you have ever felt — the chair pushing back, friction, tension in a rope, the spring, the push of your hand — is atoms' electric forces at the surface, never truly touching, pushing back. A magnet's pull is the same interaction in its magnetic face. And light itself belongs here: light is this interaction travelling as a wave. If gravity rules the largest scale, electromagnetism rules the human one.

## The nuclear interaction — the strongest, across the smallest distance

Inside the nucleus sits a puzzle: protons, all positively charged, repel each other electrically with enormous force at such small distances — yet nuclei hold together. They are held by the third interaction, the **nuclear interaction**: the strongest of the three, attracting the particles of the nucleus to one another across a reach so short it ends at the nucleus's edge. Beyond an atom's tiny core it is nothing at all — which is why daily life never feels it directly, even though it decides which nuclei can exist, powers the Sun through fusion, and stores the energy released in fission. (Finer treatments split the nuclear story in two — a strong binding force and a weak one behind certain decays; the book's epilogue gathers them as the one nuclear interaction, the third of the three.)

## Three interactions, one world

Gather the course into one table of three rows. **Gravitational:** between masses; attracts only; unlimited reach; weakest — rules planets and stars. **Electromagnetic:** between charges; attracts and repels; far stronger — rules atoms, chemistry, contact forces and light. **Nuclear:** between the particles of the nucleus; strongest of all; reach of one nucleus only — rules what nuclei exist and the energy of stars.

That is why Brazil's book ends here, in an epilogue: movement, heat, electricity and light were never separate stories. They are three interactions, seen from different sides — and every force you will ever meet is one of the three, wearing the clothes of the scale it works at.

## The key ideas

- Everything bodies do to each other reduces to **three fundamental interactions**: gravitational, electromagnetic, nuclear.
- **Gravitational:** acts between masses, attracts only, unlimited reach, weakest of the three — but it never cancels, so it rules the planets and stars.
- **Electromagnetic:** acts between charges, attracts and repels, far stronger — holds atoms and molecules together, underlies every contact force (push, friction, tension), magnets, and light itself.
- **Nuclear:** acts between the particles inside the nucleus, strongest of all, with the shortest reach — one nucleus wide — holding nuclei together against electric repulsion.
- Everyday forces are not new forces: undress any of them and one of the three is underneath.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the three fundamental interactions to your companion in your own words. Name each one, what it acts between, and one thing it rules. Finish with the hardest point: why the weakest interaction rules the largest things in the universe. If your companion can undress one everyday force — the chair's push, a rope's pull — to the interaction underneath, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Undress these forces: (a) the friction stopping a sliding book; (b) your weight on the floor; (c) a magnet lifting a pin; (d) the force holding a helium nucleus together. Name the fundamental interaction underneath each.
2. Gravity between two books on a table is far too weak to notice, yet gravity holds the Moon. In two sentences, explain how both can be true, using the words *attracts only* and *never cancels*.
3. Teach the picture: explain to someone who has not seen this lesson why a course that began with falling bodies ends with three interactions — and why three is enough.
