# Lesson plan — The three fundamental interactions

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The three fundamental interactions
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics — closing lesson of the course's arc, in the modern-physics neighbourhood of CURRICULUM.md Unit 18 (the book places it as its Epílogo).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** everything one body does to another — falling, pushing, shining, holding together — reduces to three fundamental interactions: the gravitational, the electromagnetic, and the nuclear; every force met in daily life is one of these three at work underneath.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole course's forces, fields and light — a learner can land here directly; it gathers the course rather than standing on any one lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (the three interactions are a gathering of equals, not steps that must be proved in order; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Universal gravitation**, **Electric fields and field lines** and **The strong force — the nucleus's glue**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three fundamental interactions: gravitational, electromagnetic, nuclear.
2. Say what each one acts between, its reach, and what it holds together or rules.
3. Undress an everyday force — friction, a push, tension in a rope, a magnet's pull — down to the interaction underneath it.
4. Explain why gravity, the weakest of the three, rules the planets: it only attracts, it never cancels, and its reach is unlimited.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → go back to Do it first: what is acting on you right now? Gravity holds you down — one interaction; the chair pushes back through electric repulsion between atoms — a second; your atoms hold together — the third is in the nuclei inside them.
- Thinks friction and pushing are their own fundamental forces → they are the electromagnetic interaction in disguise: surfaces touch atom to atom, and the forces between atoms are electric.
- Thinks gravity must be strongest because it rules the universe → it is the weakest of the three; it rules because it never repels and never cancels, and its reach has no end.
