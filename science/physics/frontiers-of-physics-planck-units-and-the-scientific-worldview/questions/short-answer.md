# Frontiers of physics — Planck units and the scientific worldview — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the three constants of the course's three great theories and what each governs.
**Answer: c — relativity: the speed limit and space–time exchange rate. G — gravitation: the strength of gravity between masses. h — quantum theory: the grain of action and of energy exchange (E = hf).**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What makes the Planck units “nature's own” rather than human conventions?
**Answer: They are built solely from c, G and h — constants of nature, not artefacts or body parts; their combination is unique, so any civilisation in the universe combining the same constants gets the same length, time, mass and energy.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is there no accepted physics below the Planck length?
**Answer: There both quantum effects on spacetime and gravitational effects are unavoidable at once: spacetime itself should fluctuate quantum-mechanically, and general relativity and quantum theory give contradictory accounts; the needed merged theory (quantum gravity) does not yet exist in accepted form.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Give the essay's one-test rule for models, with a course example.
**Answer: A model is judged by whether its predictions meet the world; failure directs its replacement. Example: Rutherford's atom failed (collapse, spectral smear), Bohr's stationary-state model repaired those failures, and the wave picture later repaired Bohr's.**
