# Lesson plan — Teaching the Lao way — lesson concept inventories and worked-example lessons

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Teaching the Lao way — lesson concept inventories and worked-example lessons
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18) — a new lesson built for the Laos placement named in lesson/sources.md
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Lao Grade 10 teacher guide teaches through two devices — a per-part concept inventory that names every concept a part must land, and worked-example lessons that teach problem-solving itself — with teaching notes and an answer key for every one of the 28 lessons (teacher-guide evidence)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Earlier lessons make a good preparation and are recommended, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The per-part concept inventory** — Before the lesson guidance, the guide inventories each part's concepts. The Part II (Dynamics) inventory names the force types, inertia, the kinds of friction, Hooke's Law, centre-of-gravity and pressure terms — the part's whole conceptual cargo on one list. A teacher planning from the inventory can check a lesson against it: every named concept must appear, in its lesson, in the book's concept → worked examples → exercises shape. The inventory is the guide's contract for what the part teaches, stated before any single lesson begins.
2. **Teaching notes and answer keys, lesson by lesson** — The guide mirrors the student book exactly — 7 parts, 28 lessons, guide printed pagination running from Lesson 1 guidance at p. 12 to Lesson 28 at p. 251 — and for each lesson it adds teaching notes and the answers to the book's work. Nothing in the student book is left without its guide counterpart, so the worked examples a learner studies and the answers a teacher checks are the same mathematics seen from the two sides of the desk.
3. **The worked-example lesson as a teaching device** — The guide's apparatus supports the student book's boldest device: Lesson 5, a whole lesson of worked examples of solving motion problems. The guide treats that lesson as teaching, not testing — the worked solutions are the content. Read together, inventory + worked-example lesson + answer keys form one system: name every concept up front, show the solving whole, then check every exercise against a key. That system is Laos' own teaching way, and this lesson stands on the guide as evidence of it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first and do the opening activity with real objects before reading any explanation; the explanation is the story of that activity.
- Mixes the sub lessons up → re-read the key ideas in order; each key idea is one sub lesson compressed to a line.
