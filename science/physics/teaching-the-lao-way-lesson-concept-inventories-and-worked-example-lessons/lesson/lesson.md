# Teaching the Lao way — lesson concept inventories and worked-example lessons

## Do it first
Before you read: imagine you must teach a lesson tomorrow from a book you have never seen. What would you want its teacher guide to hand you before you start — a list of the ideas the chapter must land? Worked answers to every exercise? Laos' Grade 10 guide hands the teacher both, in a fixed system. This lesson lays that system out.

## Sub lesson 1 — The per-part concept inventory

Before the lesson guidance, the guide inventories each part's concepts. The Part II (Dynamics) inventory names the force types, inertia, the kinds of friction, Hooke's Law, centre-of-gravity and pressure terms — the part's whole conceptual cargo on one list. A teacher planning from the inventory can check a lesson against it: every named concept must appear, in its lesson, in the book's concept → worked examples → exercises shape. The inventory is the guide's contract for what the part teaches, stated before any single lesson begins.

## Sub lesson 2 — Teaching notes and answer keys, lesson by lesson

The guide mirrors the student book exactly — 7 parts, 28 lessons, guide printed pagination running from Lesson 1 guidance at p. 12 to Lesson 28 at p. 251 — and for each lesson it adds teaching notes and the answers to the book's work. Nothing in the student book is left without its guide counterpart, so the worked examples a learner studies and the answers a teacher checks are the same mathematics seen from the two sides of the desk.

## Sub lesson 3 — The worked-example lesson as a teaching device

The guide's apparatus supports the student book's boldest device: Lesson 5, a whole lesson of worked examples of solving motion problems. The guide treats that lesson as teaching, not testing — the worked solutions are the content. Read together, inventory + worked-example lesson + answer keys form one system: name every concept up front, show the solving whole, then check every exercise against a key. That system is Laos' own teaching way, and this lesson stands on the guide as evidence of it.

## The key ideas

- The guide inventories each part's concepts before the lessons (Part II: force types, inertia, friction kinds, Hooke's Law, centre of gravity, pressure)
- Guide and student book mirror each other lesson for lesson — 28 lessons, notes and answer keys throughout
- The worked-example lesson (student Lesson 5) is taught as content, and the guide's keys complete the system
- This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only — the guide is cited as evidence of the teaching way, counted nowhere
