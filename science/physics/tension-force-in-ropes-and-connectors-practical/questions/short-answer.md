# Tension force in ropes and connectors — practical — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A cut rope's two ends pull each other. With what force, and why does it matter?
**Answer: Each end pulls the other with the tension T — the pair of equal, opposite pulls is what 'the rope carries a pull' means, and it is why tension acts away from a body at its end of the rope.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What does the multi-balance measurement prove, and what is the idealisation?
**Answer: It proves tension is uniform along the rope used; the idealisation is a light (massless), inextensible rope over a smooth support — real ropes with mass or rough running depart from it measurably.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A 3 kg mass hangs still from a rope (g = 10 N/kg). State the tension and where a balance reads it.
**Answer: T = mg = 30 N, read by a balance anywhere in series with the light rope — at the top, middle or just above the mass.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is 'one rope, one tension' the first line of a connected-bodies solution?
**Answer: Because both bodies' free-body diagrams then share a single unknown T, so Newton's second law for the two bodies can be solved together instead of chasing two different pulls.**
