# Lesson plan — Current as moving charge — I = nAvq

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Current as moving charge — I = nAvq
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the current-electricity strand — PECTAA Grade 11's carrier-level account of current
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a current is charge on the move, and its size is set by how many carriers pass: I = nAvq — n carriers per unit volume, cross-section A, drift speed v, charge q each — the equation that connects the ammeter to the electrons

## Sub lessons
1. **Counting charge past a point** — In time Δt, every carrier within a length vΔt of a cross-section drifts past it: volume AvΔt, carriers nAvΔt, charge nAvqΔt. Divide by the time and the current stands revealed: I = nAvq. Current is not a substance but a rate — this equation is its census.
2. **Why drift is slow yet light is instant** — In a metal wire n is enormous (about 10²⁸–10²⁹ per cubic metre), so even amperes need drift speeds of fractions of a millimetre per second. The lamp lights instantly not because electrons race from the switch but because the electric field is established along the whole wire at near light speed, setting all carriers drifting together.
3. **Reading circuits with the carrier equation** — The equation explains the wire menu: thicker wire (larger A) carries more current at the same drift speed; materials differ through n and q. It also powers later instruments — the Hall effect and the velocity selector trade on carriers moving at v through fields, and PECTAA places I = nAvq as the bridge between electrostatics' charges and circuits' currents.
