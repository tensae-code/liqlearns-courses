# Energy efficiency — useful out over total in

## Do it first
Audit one device at home with its label — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ratio every conversion is judged by

Efficiency compares what a conversion was for with what it cost: η = useful output / total input. A filament bulb turning 100 J of electrical energy into 5 J of light has η = 0.05, or 5%. The ratio has no unit, can never exceed 1, and — Punjab's point — belongs to the conversion, not to the device’s name: the same engine is more or less efficient at different loads.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where the rest goes — wasted energy

The input that is not useful output is wasted, and in most machines it leaves as heat (with sound and vibration taking a share). The filament bulb wastes 95 J as heat; a car engine wastes most of its fuel's energy the same way. Naming the waste is the first step to cutting it — a Sankey picture of a conversion shows the useful arrow and the waste arrows side by side.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chains multiply their losses

Convert in steps and the efficiencies multiply: generate at 35%, transmit at 90%, and the lamp at 5% and the original fuel's energy arrives as light at 0.35 × 0.90 × 0.05 — under 2%. Punjab teaches efficiency at the level of the single conversion so the learner can later audit whole chains — and see why saving at the point of use beats generating more.

## The key ideas

- Efficiency compares what a conversion was for with what it cost: η = useful output / total input.
- The input that is not useful output is wasted, and in most machines it leaves as heat (with sound and vibration taking a share).
- Convert in steps and the efficiencies multiply: generate at 35%, transmit at 90%, and the lamp at 5% and the original fuel's energy arrives as light at 0.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: no conversion is perfect: efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input, a fraction (or percentage) that Punjab's Grade 9 book applies to every machine and conversion chain — the rest of the input is wasted, mostly as heat. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A motor takes in 500 J and lifts a load gaining 350 J of potential energy. Find its efficiency and the wasted energy.
2. Why can efficiency never exceed 100%?
3. A chain has efficiencies 0.4, 0.9 and 0.25. What fraction of the original energy ends up useful?
