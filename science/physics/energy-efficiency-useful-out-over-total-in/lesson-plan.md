# Lesson plan — Energy efficiency — useful out over total in

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Energy efficiency — useful out over total in
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the energy strand — Punjab Grade 9's own section on the efficiency of energy conversion
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** no conversion is perfect: efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input, a fraction (or percentage) that Punjab's Grade 9 book applies to every machine and conversion chain — the rest of the input is wasted, mostly as heat

## Sub lessons
1. **The ratio every conversion is judged by** — Efficiency compares what a conversion was for with what it cost: η = useful output / total input. A filament bulb turning 100 J of electrical energy into 5 J of light has η = 0.05, or 5%. The ratio has no unit, can never exceed 1, and — Punjab's point — belongs to the conversion, not to the device’s name: the same engine is more or less efficient at different loads.
2. **Where the rest goes — wasted energy** — The input that is not useful output is wasted, and in most machines it leaves as heat (with sound and vibration taking a share). The filament bulb wastes 95 J as heat; a car engine wastes most of its fuel's energy the same way. Naming the waste is the first step to cutting it — a Sankey picture of a conversion shows the useful arrow and the waste arrows side by side.
3. **Chains multiply their losses** — Convert in steps and the efficiencies multiply: generate at 35%, transmit at 90%, and the lamp at 5% and the original fuel's energy arrives as light at 0.35 × 0.90 × 0.05 — under 2%. Punjab teaches efficiency at the level of the single conversion so the learner can later audit whole chains — and see why saving at the point of use beats generating more.
