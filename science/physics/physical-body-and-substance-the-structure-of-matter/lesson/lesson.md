# Physical body and substance — the structure of matter

## Do it first
Lay out five objects (a cup, a coin, a wooden ruler, a rubber ball, a spoon). For each, say two sentences: 'The body is…' and 'The substance is…'. Then find two bodies made of the same substance and one body-shape made of two different substances. The sorting is the lesson: body and substance are different questions. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Physical bodies — the things you can point to

A physical body is any single object with a place in space: a cup, a ruler, a stone, a drop of water, the Moon. Bodies can be big or small, solid, liquid or gas, made by people or found in nature — what makes each one a body is that it is one thing, separate from the things around it. Armenia's book names the body first, before any property is measured on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Substance — what a body is made of

Ask what the body is made of and you name a substance: the cup is plastic, the ruler wood, the ring gold. One substance makes countless bodies (iron — nails, ships, bridges), and the same body-shape comes in many substances (a cup of glass, of clay, of steel). Body says which thing; substance says what it is of. The properties of the substance — density, melting point — are what later lessons measure.

## Sub lesson 3 — Structure of matter — the step underneath

The chapter's real destination: substances are not solid through and through — they are built of tiny particles, atoms and molecules, with spaces between them. That single picture, opened here in §7 and proved in §8, is what will explain diffusion, temperature, pressure and every change of state later in the book. Body and substance are the visible rungs; structure is the rung underneath that does the explaining.

## The key ideas

- A physical body is any single object with a place in space: a cup, a ruler, a stone, a drop of water, the Moon.
- Ask what the body is made of and you name a substance: the cup is plastic, the ruler wood, the ring gold.
- The chapter's real destination: substances are not solid through and through — they are built of tiny particles, atoms and molecules, with spaces between them.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: every thing you can point to is a physical body, every body is made of some substance, and substances themselves are built of tiny particles — the three-step ladder (body, substance, structure) on which the whole Armenian matter chapter stands. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.
