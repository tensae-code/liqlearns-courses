# The greenhouse effect — trapping heat, the Greek experiment way — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A classmate says the covered flask warms more 'because glass magnifies the lamp'. Correct the claim in one or two sentences.
**Answer: The cover makes no extra energy. Both flasks receive the same inflow; the covered one loses heat more slowly (heat radiation passes glass poorly and the warm air cannot rise away), so its temperature climbs higher before inflow and outflow balance again.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the two escape routes the cover throttles.
**Answer: Heat (infrared) radiation passing out through the glass, and convection — the warmed air inside rising and being replaced by cooler air. The cover slows both.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain why adding greenhouse gases warms the Earth using the flask model.
**Answer: The gases act as a thicker cover: sunlight still gets in at nearly the same rate, but the surface's heat radiation escapes to space more slowly. The balance is restored only at a higher surface temperature — the same reason the covered flask settles warmer.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Is the greenhouse effect itself harmful? Answer precisely.
**Answer: No — the natural effect keeps the Earth warm enough for life (without it the average would be far below freezing). The problem is its strengthening by extra greenhouse gases, which moves the balance temperature upward.**
