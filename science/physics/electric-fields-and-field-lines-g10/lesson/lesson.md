## Electric fields and field lines — the teaching
Around every charge is an electric field — the region where another charge would feel a force. Field strength is defined through a small test charge: E = F/q, the force per unit charge, in newtons per coulomb. Field lines draw the field: they radiate outward from a positive charge, inward to a negative one, and between a pair they curve from + to − as the dipole pattern. Where lines crowd, the field is strong.

**Keep the formula:** E = F / q

## The key ideas

- Field strength E = F/q — force per unit charge (N/C).
- Field lines leave positive charges and enter negative ones.
- Crowded field lines mean a strong field.
