# Video script — Electric fields and field lines

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Around every charge is an electric field — the region where another charge would feel a force.
Field strength is defined through a small test charge: E = F/q, the force per unit charge, in newtons per coulomb.
Field lines draw the field: they radiate outward from a positive charge, inward to a negative one, and between a pair they curve from + to − as the dipole pattern.
Where lines crowd, the field is strong.

Remember: **Field strength E = F/q — force per unit charge (N/C).**
Remember: **Field lines leave positive charges and enter negative ones.**
Remember: **Crowded field lines mean a strong field.**
Keep the formula: **E = F / q**
