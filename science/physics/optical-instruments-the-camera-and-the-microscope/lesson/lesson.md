# Optical instruments — the camera and the microscope

## Do it first
Before you read: a phone camera makes a whole landscape fit on a tiny sensor, while a microscope makes a speck of pond water fill your eye. Both are boxes and tubes of lenses. Which instrument shrinks its image and which enlarges it? Which uses one lens stage and which uses two? Sort the two instruments here.

## Sub lesson 1 — The camera — one lens, a real image

A camera's converging lens forms a real, inverted, diminished image of the scene on the film or sensor at the back of a light-tight box. Focusing moves the lens: nearer objects need the lens moved farther from the sensor. The aperture (hole size) and the shutter (open time) together dose the light — wide aperture or long shutter for dim scenes, the reverse for bright ones. One lens, one stage, a permanent record.

## Sub lesson 2 — The microscope — two stages of magnification

The compound microscope magnifies twice. The objective — a short-focus convex lens close to the specimen — forms a real, enlarged, inverted image inside the tube. The eyepiece then acts as a magnifying glass on that image, giving a final virtual, still more enlarged image for the eye. Total magnification = objective magnification × eyepiece magnification: a ×10 objective with a ×10 eyepiece gives ×100 in all.

## Sub lesson 3 — Comparing the two instruments

Set them side by side: the camera's image is real (it lands on a surface), diminished and single-stage; the microscope's final image is virtual, enlarged and two-stage. Both obey the same lens rules — the difference is what each is for: the camera records the big world small, the microscope reveals the small world big. The eye itself is the camera's cousin — one lens, a real inverted image on a screen (the retina).

## The key ideas

- Camera: one converging lens, real inverted diminished image on film/sensor; focusing moves the lens
- Microscope: objective makes a real enlarged image, eyepiece magnifies it again — total magnification is the product
- Camera records the big world small; the microscope reveals the small world big

## Do It Yourself
1. A microscope has a ×15 eyepiece and a ×40 objective. What is the total magnification?
2. A camera photographs a nearby flower, then a distant mountain, without moving. Which shot needs the lens farther from the sensor? Explain with image distance.
3. Say which instrument — camera or microscope — matches each description: (a) final image virtual and enlarged; (b) image caught on a sensor; (c) magnification found by multiplying two numbers.
