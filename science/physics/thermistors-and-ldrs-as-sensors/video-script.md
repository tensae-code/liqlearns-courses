# Video script — Thermistors and LDRs as sensors

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **a thermistor's resistance falls steeply as it warms (negative temperature coefficient) and an LDR's resistance falls as light on it brightens; placed in a potential divider, each turns temperature or light into a changing voltage a circuit can act on**. A thermistor is a semiconductor resistor built to be temperature-sensitive: heat frees more charge carriers, so its resistance drops sharply as temperature rises — a negative temperature coefficient (NTC). Then the lesson builds its three parts in order — the thermistor — warmth lowers resistance; the ldr — light lowers resistance; sensors in dividers — resistance becomes signal — each one first met in the hand, then said in words.
