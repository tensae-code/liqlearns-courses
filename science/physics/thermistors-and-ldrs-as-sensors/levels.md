# Thermistors and LDRs as sensors — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can do all of these — A thermistor is a semiconductor resistor built to be temperature-sensitive: heat frees more charge carriers, so its resistance drops sharply as temperature rises — a negative temperature coefficient (NTC); A light-dependent resistor does the same trade with light: photons free carriers in its semiconductor track, so the brighter the light, the lower its resistance — megohms in the dark, hundreds of ohms in bright light; Alone, a changing resistance does nothing; in series with a fixed resistor across a supply, it forms a potential divider whose output voltage swings with the sensor's value — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — restating each part in the learner's own examples, working its numbers, and checking new cases against it until the teaching is the learner's own.
