# Thermistors and LDRs as sensors

## Do it first
Build both dividers on a breadboard with a multimeter on the output — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — The thermistor — warmth lowers resistance

A thermistor is a semiconductor resistor built to be temperature-sensitive: heat frees more charge carriers, so its resistance drops sharply as temperature rises — a negative temperature coefficient (NTC). Its resistance–temperature curve is steep and curved, the opposite behaviour to a metal's gentle rise.

## Sub lesson 2 — The LDR — light lowers resistance

A light-dependent resistor does the same trade with light: photons free carriers in its semiconductor track, so the brighter the light, the lower its resistance — megohms in the dark, hundreds of ohms in bright light. No junctions, no power of its own: light alone sets the value.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sensors in dividers — resistance becomes signal

Alone, a changing resistance does nothing; in series with a fixed resistor across a supply, it forms a potential divider whose output voltage swings with the sensor's value. That voltage can switch a transistor, trigger a relay or feed a meter — a thermostat's probe and a street light's dusk switch are this sub lesson at work, in both Punjab books.
