# Armenian physicists — physics in Armenia's own history

## Do it first
Take a map of Armenia (or sketch one). Mark a high mountain area and the capital. Cosmic-ray detectors and observatory domes are put high up, away from city air and lights — find where you would put them and say why. Then make a 'physicist card' for one Armenian physicist: name, what they studied, and the instrument or observatory their work used. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — A small country, its own scientists

The Grade 7 book's §2, right after saying what physics studies, presents famous Armenian physicists. The order is the teaching: before any law or formula, the pupil learns that physics is done by people — and that some of those people are Armenian. A science that might look foreign arrives as something one's own country already does.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stars and cosmic rays — the work Armenia is known for

Armenia's best-known physics looks upward. Viktor Ambartsumian founded the Byurakan Observatory and reshaped how astronomers think about young stars and active galaxies; the Alikhanian brothers built cosmic-ray research on Mount Aragats, where detectors still catch particles arriving from space. Mountains, clear skies and patient instruments turned a small country into a name in astrophysics.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the book starts here

A course-way choice: opening with national science history gives every later topic a home. The pupil who has met Armenian physicists first reads Newton's laws and atomic physics as a continuing story they could join — the observatory and the laboratory are presented as places Armenians work, not places far away. That is the function of §2: belonging before content.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: the first physics book an Armenian pupil opens begins not with a formula but with the physicists of Armenia itself — the science belongs to every nation, and Armenia's own scientists, observatories and cosmic-ray stations are part of its story. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.
