# Armenian physicists — physics in Armenia's own history — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 3) In your own words, what is a 'course-way' choice in this lesson?
**Answer:** The book's own way of organising the teaching — here, opening the whole physics course with Armenia's own physicists so the subject starts from belonging, before any law or formula.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name two Armenian physics sites and what each is for.
**Answer:** The Byurakan Observatory — telescopes studying stars and galaxies; the Aragats cosmic-ray station — detectors catching high-energy particles from space.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do cosmic rays have to be studied from mountains or with detectors that look at the showers they make in the air?
**Answer:** Most cosmic rays never reach the ground intact — they collide with air nuclei high up and arrive as showers of secondary particles; the higher the station, the less air above it and the cleaner the signal.

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) What does §2 teach a pupil before it teaches any physics content?
**Answer:** That physics is a human activity their own country takes part in — its scientists, observatories and stations are real, named and near.
