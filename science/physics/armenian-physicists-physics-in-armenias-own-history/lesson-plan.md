# Lesson plan — Armenian physicists — physics in Armenia's own history

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Armenian physicists — physics in Armenia's own history
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the opening strand — Armenia's Grade 7 book opens the whole course with the physicists of its own country (Ch. I §2)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the first physics book an Armenian pupil opens begins not with a formula but with the physicists of Armenia itself — the science belongs to every nation, and Armenia's own scientists, observatories and cosmic-ray stations are part of its story
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **A small country, its own scientists** — The Grade 7 book's §2, right after saying what physics studies, presents famous Armenian physicists. The order is the teaching: before any law or formula, the pupil learns that physics is done by people — and that some of those people are Armenian. A science that might look foreign arrives as something one's own country already does.
2. **Stars and cosmic rays — the work Armenia is known for** — Armenia's best-known physics looks upward. Viktor Ambartsumian founded the Byurakan Observatory and reshaped how astronomers think about young stars and active galaxies; the Alikhanian brothers built cosmic-ray research on Mount Aragats, where detectors still catch particles arriving from space. Mountains, clear skies and patient instruments turned a small country into a name in astrophysics.
3. **Why the book starts here** — A course-way choice: opening with national science history gives every later topic a home. The pupil who has met Armenian physicists first reads Newton's laws and atomic physics as a continuing story they could join — the observatory and the laboratory are presented as places Armenians work, not places far away. That is the function of §2: belonging before content.
