# How this Physics course works — the Iraqi Grade 8 way

## Do it first
Open any Iraqi physics book at a chapter start — do not read the teaching yet. Find the activity or the vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), read the bilingual terms aloud, then flip to the chapter's end and find its review and test. That opening and that closing are this lesson: the Iraqi way, in the Grade 8 book. Its three units run from motion and force to sound and light.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book's shape — 3 units / 6 chapters / 13 lessons

The Grade 8 book runs in 3 units, 6 chapters and 13 lessons: Unit 1 Motion & Force (measurement, motion, Newton's laws, gravity), Unit 2 Force, Work & Energy (work and power, energy, levers and the simple machines), and Unit 3 Sound & Light (waves, sound, and light — reflection and refraction).

## Sub lesson 2 — How every lesson is built — activity or vocabulary first, bilingual terms, worked examples

Each unit of the Grade 8 book opens with a starter activity of the ball-kick/stopwatch kind — a real timed or measured event before any law is stated. Lessons then open with their activity or chapter vocabulary (مفردات الفصل), terms printed in Arabic and English side by side, and worked examples carry each idea before questions use it.

## Sub lesson 3 — How every chapter closes — vocabulary review and a chapter test

The Grade 8 book closes its chapters with vocabulary/concept reviews and chapter tests in short-answer and multiple-choice form; the whole book closes with the Chapter 6 review questions.

## The key ideas

- The Iraqi Grade 8 book is organised as 3 units / 6 chapters / 13 lessons.
- Every Iraqi lesson opens with a hands-on activity or a chapter vocabulary list (مفردات الفصل), and technical terms are printed in Arabic and English side by side.
- Worked examples carry the teaching, and every chapter closes with a vocabulary/concept review plus short-answer and multiple-choice tests.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the whole lesson in order, in your own words — start with the opening activity, then take each sub lesson in turn and finish with what it is used for. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

2. Open a chapter (real or imagined) the Iraqi way: name its opening activity or vocabulary list, one bilingual term pair, and its closing test kinds.
3. A friend says Iraqi books are just translated copies of other countries' books. Give two features from this lesson that are the Iraqi book's own way.
