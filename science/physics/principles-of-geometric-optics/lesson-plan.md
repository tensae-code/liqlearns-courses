# Lesson plan — Principles of geometric optics — independence and reversibility of light rays

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Principles of geometric optics — independence and reversibility of light rays
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the geometric-optics strand — directly after Light travels in straight lines and before Shadow and penumbra and the reflection lessons: the two ray principles every later construction quietly uses
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** light rays obey two working principles: **independence** — rays that cross pass through each other and continue exactly as if the other were not there, so each ray in a diagram can be traced on its own — and **reversibility** — a ray sent back along its path retraces that path exactly in reverse, so any light path can be travelled both ways

## Sub lessons
1. **Independence — rays cross without disturbing each other** — Two beams cross in a dusty room and emerge on the far side unbent and unmixed: each ray keeps its own direction through the crossing, as if it travelled alone. That is the **principle of independence of light rays**: rays neither push nor turn one another. It is the licence for every ray diagram — a construction with five rays is five single-ray problems drawn on one page. Mozambique's PESD I Módulo 4 states the principles as laws in their own right (Lição 14) after rectilinear propagation (Lição 13) and before shadow and penumbra (Lição 15): first light travels straight, then the two rules its rays obey, then everything built from them.
2. **Reversibility — the path retraces itself backwards** — Send a ray from a lamp to a mirror and on to a screen; now place the lamp where the screen was, and the ray returns along the very same lines to the lamp's first place. That is the **principle of reversibility**: every light path can be travelled in either direction, with the same angles at every surface. It is why your eyes meet in a mirror — the path from their eyes to you is your path to them reversed — and why a construction drawn for an object can be read backwards for its image.
3. **The principles at work — tracing real paths both ways** — Together the two principles turn ray tracing into bookkeeping: split a scene into independent rays (independence), trace each one straight, bouncing by the reflection law and bending by refraction, and know the whole diagram also works in reverse (reversibility). The periscope, the mirror check, the eclipse geometry of the next lessons in the module — each is these two principles plus straight lines. A ray you cannot send back along its own path is a ray drawn wrong: reversibility is the self-check of geometric optics.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the principles of independence and reversibility in one line each.
2. Explain why a multi-ray diagram can be traced one ray at a time.
3. Use reversibility to predict the return path of a ray and to check a drawn construction.

## What you need
Two torches, a dusty or smoky space (or a tank of slightly milky water), a plane mirror and a card screen: the crossing and the return are both things to see, not only to be told.
