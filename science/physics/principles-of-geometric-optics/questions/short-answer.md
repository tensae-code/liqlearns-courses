# Principles of geometric optics — independence and reversibility of light rays — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Two torch beams cross in a dusty room. State what happens to each beam at the crossing and name the principle.
**Answer: Nothing happens to either — each continues in its own direction with its own brightness, exactly as if it travelled alone. That is the principle of independence of light rays.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A ray leaves a lamp, reflects off a mirror at 35° to the normal, and hits a screen. The lamp is moved to the screen's spot and aimed back along the ray. Where does the light land, and at what angle does it meet the mirror?
**Answer: It lands exactly where the lamp first stood, meeting the mirror again at 35° to the normal — reversibility retraces the same path with the same angles, in reverse.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does reversibility make a useful check on a mirror construction drawn on paper?
**Answer: Because every true light path can be travelled both ways: tracing the drawn path backwards from image to object must land exactly on the object — if it misses, the drawing contains an error.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 1) A diagram shows twelve rays from one object. How many separate tracing problems is it, and which principle says so?
**Answer: Twelve — independence means each ray can be traced from start to finish on its own, ignoring the other eleven.**
