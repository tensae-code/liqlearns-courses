# Three ways to charge a body

## Do it first
Charge a ruler by rubbing, then pick up paper bits (friction at work). Touch the ruler to a metal tap and feel its charge leave — conduction and grounding in one gesture.

[Picture 21895 — three panels — rubbing, contact, induction — with electron arrows]

## Three ways to charge a body — the teaching
Bodies are charged three ways. By friction: rub a comb through hair and electrons transfer from one surface to the other — the receiver goes negative, the giver positive. By conduction: touch a charged body to a neutral one and charge flows across the contact until they share it. By induction: bring a charged body near (never touching) a neutral one, let the near charge drive electrons to one side, ground the far side so charge escapes or enters, then remove the ground and the charged body — the neutral body is left charged, oppositely to the inducer.

## The key ideas

- Friction charging transfers electrons between rubbed surfaces.
- Conduction charging shares charge by contact.
- Induction charges without touching, using a nearby body and grounding.

## Say it to your companion
Explain three ways to charge a body to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
