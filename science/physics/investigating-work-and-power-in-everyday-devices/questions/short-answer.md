# Investigating work and power in everyday devices — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Design the kettle test: what do you measure, and what do you keep the same?
**Answer:** Measure: mass of water, start and end temperature, time to boil, the kettle's rating. Keep the same: the amount of water and start temperature for every run; repeat timings. Compare energy given to the water (from its heating) per second with the rating.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why might measured power come out below the kettle's rating?
**Answer:** Not all the electrical energy reaches the water: the kettle body, the air and evaporation carry some away, and the element may run slightly under rating. The gap measures those losses.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Two cars reach 100 km/h in 6 s and 12 s. What does the investigation conclude, and why is it a power statement?
**Answer:** The 6-second car is the more powerful in practice: reaching the same speed means gaining the same kinetic energy, and doing it in half the time means delivering work at twice the rate — power is the rate of doing work.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Write the conclusion this investigation teaches about the 60 W lamp and the 2,200 W kettle.
**Answer:** The kettle is far more powerful, but energy bills count power × time: three minutes of kettle ≈ 0.11 kWh, while the lamp left on 10 hours uses 0.6 kWh. Duration can outweigh power.
