# Investigating work and power in everyday devices — video script

**Opening (the hook):** Power stops being a formula when you go and look: read the ratings of the devices heating and lighting your home, time what they do, and compare vehicles by the time they take to reach 100 km/h — the investigation the NIS book sets as its own section.

**Walk-through:** Take the sub lessons in order. For 'Plan — what "rated power" promises': Every electrical device carries a rating: the power it is designed to draw — a kettle near 2,200 W, a heater 1,500 W, a lamp tens of watts. For 'Measure and compare': Collect the evidence the NIS way: read rating labels, time the kettle boiling a measured litre, time a car's 0–100 km/h from published tests, and set the numbers side by side. For 'Report — from numbers to judgement': The investigation ends by answering its question in the open: a table of devices by measured and rated power, a ranking of vehicles by acceleration time, and the conclusion the evidence supports — including the surprises (a heater left on all evening outspends the mighty kettle's three minutes; energy = power × time, and time is the silent multiplier). 
**The picture:** [Picture 36863] — a picture of a drawn diagram — a kettle, a heater and a lamp each with its rating label (2200 W, 1500 W, 60 W) and a stopwatch beside a car's speedometer climbing to 100 km/h, the NIS research-task bench. Walk the learner across it slowly, naming each part as the sub lessons name it.

**Close:** Say the one thing once more, plainly: power stops being a formula when you go and look: read the ratings of the devices heating and lighting your home, time what they do, and compare vehicles by the time they take to reach 100 km/h — the investigation the NIS book sets as its own section. Then send the learner to the Do It Yourself items.
