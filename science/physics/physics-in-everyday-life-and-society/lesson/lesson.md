# Physics in everyday life and society

## Do it first
Before any explaining: think of one place in your own day where why physics matters to society shows itself. Say what you see out loud, in one sentence. Then read on, and check your idea against the lesson — keep what matches, fix what does not.

## Why physics matters to society

Physics underlies transport, electronics, health care, energy and everyday technology; the opening section walks the influence of physics on transport, health, the economy and technology and asks pupils to explain that influence in their own setting.

The guide's answer to the Unit 1 applications activity expands the student book's list into thirteen named fields with examples: lighting systems (house and road lights); health and medical (X-ray, MRI, ultrasound); communications (mobile phones, radio, satellite); transport (airplanes, cars, ships); information technology (computers, internet); remote sensing (weather prediction, environmental protection); mineral exploration (gold, aluminium, diamond); petroleum exploration (oil and gas); groundwater exploration (drinking-water sources); geothermal exploration; nuclear energy; wind energy; and the entertainment industry (television, films, videos).

This list is content the course should carry, not only pedagogy.
