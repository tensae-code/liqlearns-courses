# General properties of matter

## Do it first
Before you read: fill a glass with water right to the brim. Now lower a stone in, slowly. Water spills — exactly the water that was sitting where the stone now sits. The stone and the water could not share the place, so the water had to leave. That one act shows a property every body has. This lesson names it — and the five others that go with it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mass and volume — how much matter, how much space

Every body has **mass**: how much matter it holds, weighed on a balance and measured in kilograms. And every body has **volume**: how much space it takes up — length × width × height for a regular block, the rise in a measuring cylinder for a stone or a liquid. Keep the two apart: a big sack of feathers and a small sack of stones can have the same mass and very different volumes. Mass measures the matter; volume measures the room it needs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Impenetrability and divisibility — one place, one body; and matter cuts smaller

**Impenetrability** is the property your spilling glass just proved: two bodies can never occupy the same place at the same time. It is why a boat displaces water, why you cannot walk through a wall, why the air in a diving bell stays trapped. **Divisibility** runs the other way: matter can be cut into smaller and smaller parts — a loaf into slices, a slice into crumbs — and every part, however small, is still matter with mass and volume of its own. Exclusive room, endlessly shareable by cutting: both are true of every body.

## Sub lesson 3 — Compressibility and elasticity — squeezed smaller, and springing back

Push on matter and it answers two ways. **Compressibility**: its volume can be squeezed smaller — a gas in a sealed syringe gives a lot, water barely gives, a stone gives almost nothing, because their particles have different room to close up. **Elasticity**: deformed within its limit, a body returns to its first shape and volume when the force stops — the spring, the rubber band, the sponge all come back. Yield a little under force, and push back toward the first form: that pair completes the six general properties, the properties Mozambique's PESD I course teaches as one set (Módulo 1, Lição 2) before it goes on to the states of matter.

## The key ideas

- Mass is how much matter a body holds; volume is the space it takes up — two different facts about the same body
- Impenetrability: two bodies can never occupy the same place at the same time
- Divisibility: matter can be divided into smaller and smaller parts, each still matter
- Compressibility: volume can be squeezed smaller — gases most, liquids barely, solids least
- Elasticity: deformed within its limit, a body returns to its first shape and volume

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: Every body has six general properties. It has mass and volume. No other body can share its place — that is impenetrability — yet it can be cut smaller and smaller. It can be squeezed a little, gases most of all, and what is bent or stretched within its limit springs back. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. A stone is lowered into a measuring cylinder and the level rises by 40 cm³. Which property did the rise measure, and which property explains why the water had to move aside?
2. Sort by compressibility, most to least: the air in a bicycle pump, the water in a bottle, the steel of the bottle's cap. Say why the order goes that way.
3. A rubber ball is pressed flat and returns round; a lump of clay is pressed flat and stays flat. Which property does the ball show that the clay, past its limit, does not?
