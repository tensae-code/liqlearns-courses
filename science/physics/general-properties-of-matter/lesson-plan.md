# Lesson plan — General properties of matter

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** General properties of matter
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the opening matter strand — directly after What physics is and beside States of matter: the properties every piece of matter has, whichever state it is in
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every body of matter, solid, liquid or gas, shares the same six general properties: it has **mass** (how much matter), it has **volume** (it takes up space), it is **impenetrable** (two bodies can never occupy the same place at the same time), it is **divisible** (it can be cut into smaller and smaller parts), it is **compressible** (it can be squeezed into less space — gases most, solids least), and it is **elastic** (deformed, it tends to return to its first shape and size)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Mass and volume — how much matter, how much space** — A body is a portion of matter, and the first two general properties measure it two different ways. **Mass** says how much matter the body holds — weighed on a balance, in kilograms, the same on Earth or on the Moon. **Volume** says how much space the body takes up — read off a measuring cylinder for a liquid, length × width × height for a regular solid. The two are not the same fact: a sack of feathers and a sack of stones can share a volume and differ wildly in mass. Mozambique's PESD I Módulo 1 teaches the whole general-properties set as one lesson (Lição 2) straight after the body/matter distinction of Lição 1: first what a body is, then the properties every body has.
2. **Impenetrability and divisibility — one place, one body; and matter cuts smaller** — **Impenetrability**: two bodies can never occupy the same space at the same time — that is why a stone dropped into a full glass pushes the water out: the water must leave the place the stone takes. **Divisibility**: matter can be divided into smaller and smaller parts — a block cut in two, each half cut again — and each part is still matter with all six properties, however small the cutting goes in school practice. The pair pull in opposite directions and both hold: matter takes exclusive room, and its room can be shared out into pieces, never overlapped.
3. **Compressibility and elasticity — squeezed smaller, and springing back** — **Compressibility**: pushed hard enough, every body gives a little — a gas in a syringe squeezes a lot (its particles have room to close up), a liquid barely, a solid least of all. **Elasticity**: a body deformed within its limit returns to its original shape and volume when the force stops — the spring stretches and comes back, the rubber ball dents and rounds out. Together they describe how matter answers force: it yields in volume, and it pushes back toward its first form. With the six named, the module moves on to the states of matter — the properties stay the same, only their sizes change.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six general properties of matter and define each in one line.
2. Distinguish mass from volume with an example where one is shared and the other differs.
3. Use impenetrability to explain displacement, and order solid, liquid and gas by compressibility.

## What you need
A balance, a measuring cylinder, a full glass of water and a stone, a sponge or syringe of air, and a spring or rubber band: each property is first met in the hand, not on the page.
