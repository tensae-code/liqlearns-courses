# Viscous flow and Stokes' law — video script

**Opening (the hook):** A viscous liquid resists flowing — its layers drag on each other, fastest at a pipe's centre and slowest at its walls — and a small sphere falling through it is held back by Stokes' drag, 6πηrv, until it sinks at a steady terminal speed the Kazakh laboratory measures.

**Walk-through:** Take the sub lessons in order. For 'Viscosity — a liquid's inner friction': Viscosity is friction inside a liquid: layer slides over layer, and the layers drag. For 'Stokes' law — drag on a falling sphere': A small sphere moving slowly through a viscous liquid feels a drag force F = 6πηrv — Stokes' formula (η the viscosity, r the sphere's radius, v its speed). For 'The falling-ball investigation (Kazakhstan Lab 5)': Kazakhstan's laboratory work 5 turns Stokes' law into a measurement: time small balls of different radii falling a fixed distance through a viscous liquid, and investigate how the speed depends on the radius — then swap liquids (the humanities track's Lab 1 compares liquids of different viscosity directly).
