# Viscous flow and Stokes' law — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is a real liquid's speed leaving a tank hole less than Torricelli's √(2gh) predicts?
**Answer:** Torricelli's formula is for an ideal, viscosity-free liquid. A real liquid's layers drag on each other and on the walls, wasting energy, so the jet emerges slower.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A steel ball and a same-sized plastic ball fall through oil. Which reaches the higher terminal velocity, and why?
**Answer:** The steel ball. Its weight is greater for the same upthrust and size, so drag (6πηrv) must grow larger to balance it, which needs a higher speed v.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Describe Kazakhstan's Lab 5 investigation in three steps.
**Answer:** (1) Drop a small ball in a tall vessel of viscous liquid and confirm it reaches a steady speed (equal distances in equal times). (2) Time balls of different radii over the same fixed falling distance and record their speeds. (3) Compare: terminal speed rises with radius, as Stokes' law predicts — or repeat in different liquids to compare their viscosities.

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does warming a viscous liquid usually make balls fall faster through it?
**Answer:** Viscosity η falls as the liquid warms; Stokes' drag 6πηrv is then smaller at any speed, so the ball must fall faster before drag balances its weight.
