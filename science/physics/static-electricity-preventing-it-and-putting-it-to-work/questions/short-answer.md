# Static electricity — preventing it and putting it to work — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do electronics workers wear earthed wrist straps even though the “spark” from a fingertip is too small to feel?
**Answer: A discharge too small to feel can still carry enough voltage to puncture a chip's tiny structures; the strap drains the worker's charge continuously so no fingertip spark can happen at all.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A factory wants less paint wasted. Explain, with the field idea, why charging the spray helps.
**Answer: Uncharged spray drifts ballistically — much misses or overshoots. Charged droplets are pulled toward the earthed work by the field, bending even around edges, so more paint lands on target and less in the air.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Lightning protection and tanker earthing look different but share one principle. State it.
**Answer: Give charge a chosen, safe path to earth before it can take an unchosen, harmful one — drain continuously (strap) or invite the strike to a prepared route (rod).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the three earlier sections of the chapter whose physics this section applies.
**Answer: Charge and its conservation/transfer (§1), Coulomb's law (§2), and the electric field (§3) — prevention and use are those three, engineered.**
