# Hands-on — Static electricity — preventing it and putting it to work

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Map static's two faces in your school
(1) Danger hunt: walk the building and list three places static could bite — synthetic carpets by computer rooms, fuel or gas stores, dry-season door handles — and for each, write the prevention the physics prescribes (earthing, conductive path, humidity, a rod's chosen route). (2) Use hunt: find one working use within reach — the school photocopier is the classic; open its story in your notes: drum charged, light discharging the white parts, toner seeking the charged image, paper taking the print. (3) Bench test: charge a balloon by rubbing and use it first as a hazard (bring it near a classmate's hair — chaos) then as a tool (use the same attraction to pick up paper scraps and sort them from a mixed pile — a desktop precipitator). Record both faces of your balloon in two sentences each.
