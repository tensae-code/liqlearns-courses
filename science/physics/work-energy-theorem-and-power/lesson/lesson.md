## Sub lesson 1 — The work–energy theorem

Net work equals the change in kinetic energy.

## Sub lesson 2 — Power — how fast work is done

Power is the rate of doing work (watts), applied to machines and people in worked examples.


## The teacher's demonstrations — power you can time (teacher guide — evidence)

Your teacher's guide prescribes two demonstrations for power. Do them, or work them through carefully:

- **The timed upstairs race.** Two pupils of nearly equal mass race upstairs — or lift equal masses — against the clock. Both do nearly the same work (same weight lifted through the same height), but the faster one does it in less time, and so has the greater power. Time the runs and estimate each pupil's power: P = W/t with real bodies, real stairs, real seconds.
- **The waterfall and the dam.** A waterfall is nature's power demonstration: water falling through a height delivers work continuously, and a dam and power plant turn that fall into electrical output. The plant's power is the rate at which the falling water's potential energy is delivered — the same P = W/t, at the scale of a river.
