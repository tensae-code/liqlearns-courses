# Work–energy theorem; power — short answer

Answer in a few words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Give one key fact this lesson teaches about the work–energy theorem and power.**
**Answer: Net work equals the change in kinetic energy.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2) Give one key fact this lesson teaches about the work–energy theorem and power.**
**Answer: Power is the rate of doing work (watts), applied to machines and people in worked examples.**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) State one thing this lesson teaches about the work–energy theorem and power.**
**Answer: Net work equals the change in kinetic energy.**

**4. (Sub lesson 2) State one thing this lesson teaches about the work–energy theorem and power.**
**Answer: Power is the rate of doing work (watts), applied to machines and people in worked examples.**

**5.** In the timed upstairs race, why must the two pupils be of nearly equal mass for the comparison to be fair?
**Answer: So both do nearly the same work lifting their weight through the same height — then the only difference left is the time, and power P = W/t can be compared honestly.**

**6.** State the waterfall example as a power statement, using P = W/t.
**Answer: The falling water does work W as its potential energy is delivered; the plant's power is that work divided by the time it takes — the rate of delivery, not just the total fall.**
