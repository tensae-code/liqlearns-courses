# Special relativity — basic principles — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) State both postulates of special relativity.
**Answer: (1) The laws of physics are the same in every inertial frame. (2) The speed of light in vacuum, c, is the same for every observer, whatever the motion of the source or the observer.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Fast-moving unstable particles created high in the atmosphere reach the ground even though their rest lifetime seems too short. Explain.
**Answer: By Earth's clocks the particles' moving clocks run slow — time dilation stretches their lifetime as measured from Earth — so many survive the trip; equivalently, in the particles' frame the atmosphere is length-contracted.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) What does general relativity add to special relativity, in one or two sentences?
**Answer: It extends the theory to gravity: mass and energy curve spacetime itself, and gravity is objects following the straightest possible paths through that curved spacetime.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) A reaction releases energy and its products are found to weigh slightly less than the starting materials. Where did the released energy come from, and which equation keeps the account?
**Answer: From the missing mass itself — mass and energy are equivalent, and E = mc² converts the small mass loss into the large energy released.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 4) What do the Lorentz transformations do, and which three effects of Sub lesson 2 fall out of them?
**Answer: They translate an event's time and position from one inertial frame to another moving at v, using γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²). Time dilation, length contraction and relative simultaneity are all contained in them — each falls out as arithmetic when the transformations are applied to a clock, a rod, or two distant events.**
