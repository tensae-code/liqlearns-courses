# How this Physics course works — the Indonesian Kurikulum Merdeka way

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the last science topic you learned. Did you start by reading definitions, or by doing something — measuring, dropping, shining, pushing — and only afterwards getting the words for what you had seen? Write down which way it was, and whether the words stuck. This lesson is about a course that always starts the second way: do first, name afterwards.

## Sub lesson 1 — Activity first — every chapter opens by doing

In the Kurikulum Merdeka way, a physics chapter opens with **apperception**: a short bridge from daily life to the new topic — a city map before vectors-in-motion, a radar gun before acceleration, a sprint race before graphs. Next comes a **misconception check**: the teacher names the wrong idea learners commonly bring — for example, that a heavier object must fall faster — and the learners commit to an answer before any teaching, so the wrong idea is out in the open where the chapter can work on it. Only then does the building start, and it starts with the learners' own hands: a numbered **Aktivitas** — an investigation with apparatus, steps and questions — comes before the formal concept, so every definition that follows is a name for something the learner has already seen happen.

## Sub lesson 2 — The shape of a chapter — check, build, check again

The same rhythm repeats through the whole chapter. Concepts grow through the Aktivitas investigations in order, each one feeding the next: measure, vary one thing, record, conclude. After each stretch of work the chapter stops for **“Ayo, Cek Pemahaman”** — “come on, check your understanding” — a short set of questions that must be answered from what has just been built, not from memory of a text. The chapter then closes with an **Asesmen**, a full assessment of the whole chapter. The shape matters: check, build, check again means a learner who is lost is found early, while the step that lost them is still the current step, and the final assessment holds no surprises — it asks, in bigger form, exactly what the small checks have been rehearsing.

## Sub lesson 3 — The learner in the chapter — character and projects

A chapter in this way carries more than physics. Each one names its **Profil Pelajar Pancasila** dimension — the learner-character profile the work is meant to grow: critical reasoning when an investigation's result argues back, independence when an Aktivitas is carried out without being led, cooperation when a group must agree on a conclusion. Chapters also offer **project options** — longer work that takes the physics out of the page, like building and testing a real circuit for a real purpose. So the course's promise is double: learn the physics, and become, chapter by chapter, the kind of learner the profile describes — one who checks, investigates and concludes for themselves.

## The key ideas

- Every chapter opens with apperception and a misconception check, then builds through numbered Aktivitas investigations the learners carry out themselves.
- “Ayo, Cek Pemahaman” checks close each stretch of work, and a chapter Asesmen closes the chapter — check, build, check again.
- Each chapter carries a Profil Pelajar Pancasila character dimension and project options, so the course grows the learner as well as the learning.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take any physics topic you know — falling objects, circuits, lenses. Write the apperception for it: one daily-life scene that leads straight into the topic, in two sentences.
2. For the same topic, write one misconception check: the wrong idea many learners bring, stated as a question with two or three answers to choose between, learners committing before any teaching.
3. Sketch the chapter shape for that topic as a flow of five boxes: apperception → misconception check → Aktivitas investigations → Ayo, Cek Pemahaman → Asesmen — and under the sketch, name the Profil Pelajar Pancasila dimension your Aktivitas would grow, and why.
