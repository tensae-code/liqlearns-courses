# Internal energy and how it changes

## Do it first
Before you read: rub your palms together fast for ten seconds — they warm, though nothing hot touched them. Now hold a cold spoon under warm water — it warms too, though nothing rubbed it. Two warmings, two utterly different causes. What single thing grew inside the palms and inside the spoon both times? Name it here, and the two ways it can change.

## Sub lesson 1 — What internal energy is

§2 of the Grade 8 book names the store: the particles of every body move without stopping and pull on each other, so every body holds internal energy — the sum of all its particles' kinetic energy of random motion and potential energy of interaction.

Heat the body and the particles move faster: internal energy rises with temperature. More substance means more particles and a bigger store — and melting or boiling pours energy into the potential part, loosening the particles' grip, even while the temperature stands still.

## Sub lesson 2 — Changing it by work

§3 gives the first way: do work on a body and its internal energy grows — rub your palms and they warm, hammer a nail and it heats, squeeze air quickly in a bicycle pump and the air warms. Let a body do work and the store falls: gas pushing a piston outwards cools as it expands.

Work changes internal energy with no hotter body anywhere in sight — the energy arrives as organised motion of the whole and ends as the particles' disordered motion.

## Sub lesson 3 — Changing it by heat transfer — and why the two ways are one account

§3's second way needs no force moving anything: set a cold spoon in hot tea and energy passes from tea to spoon until they level — heat transfer, carried by conduction, convection and radiation, the next paragraphs' business.

The account closes: a body's internal energy changes by work and by heat transfer together. The same final state can be reached by either road, or a mix of both, because internal energy belongs to the body's state, not to the road taken to reach it.

## The key ideas

- Internal energy = the sum of all particles' kinetic energy of random motion + potential energy of interaction
- It rises with temperature and with the amount of substance; at melting/boiling the added energy goes into the potential part at steady temperature
- Work done ON a body raises its internal energy; work done BY it (expanding gas) lowers it
- Heat transfer — energy passing hotter → colder with no work — is the second way; the total change counts both ways, and internal energy belongs to the state, not the road

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: every body stores internal energy in its particles' motion and mutual pulls; the store grows when work is done on the body or when heat passes into it from something hotter, and shrinks when the body does work or passes heat out — two roads into one account. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Classify each as work or heat transfer changing internal energy: (a) a drill bit warming as it bores; (b) soup warming on a stove; (c) bicycle-pump air warming as you pump fast; (d) hands warming at a fire.
2. A gas in a cylinder is compressed quickly and warms; the same gas, left in a hot room with the piston free, also warms and expands. In which case did work change the internal energy, in which heat transfer — and what is the same in both endings?
3. Ice at 0 °C is given energy and melts to water at 0 °C. The temperature never moved — say which part of the internal energy grew, and what the energy did to the particles' grip on each other.
