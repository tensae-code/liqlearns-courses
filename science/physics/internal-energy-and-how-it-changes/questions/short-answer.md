# Internal energy and how it changes — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) At melting, energy enters ice at a steady 0 °C. Where does it go?
**Answer: Into the potential part of the internal energy — loosening the particles' grip on each other as the solid becomes liquid; the kinetic part, and so the temperature, stands still.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A cyclist pumps a tyre fast and the pump barrel warms. Explain it in internal-energy words.
**Answer: Compressing the air quickly is work done on the gas; the work raises the air's internal energy, so its temperature rises — and warms the barrel with it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Name the two ways a body's internal energy can change, with one example of each.
**Answer: Work (hammering a nail warms it) and heat transfer (a spoon warming in hot tea).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Hot water in a thermos slowly cools though no work is done on or by it. Which road is at work?
**Answer: Heat transfer — energy passes from the hotter water to its colder surroundings (slowly, through the thermos wall) until they level.**
