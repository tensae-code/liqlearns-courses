# Power of a lens and combining lenses

## Do it first
Before you read: hold a magnifying glass at arm's length from a window, then close to this page. The strong lens — the one that bends light most — has the shortest focal length. Which should carry the bigger number, the strong lens or the weak one? And if you stack two magnifying glasses together, do you get a stronger or a weaker lens? Decide both here.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lens power — the dioptre

Power prices a lens by how strongly it bends light. Power P = 1/f, with f the focal length in metres; the unit is the dioptre (D). A convex (converging) lens has positive power, a concave (diverging) lens negative power. Short focal length means strong bending means high power: f = 0.25 m gives P = +4 D, while f = 2 m gives only +0.5 D. Opticians prescribe glasses by power, not by focal length, because power is the number that adds up — as the next part shows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Combining lenses — powers add

Put two thin lenses in contact and the pair bends light twice in immediate succession, so their powers simply add: P = P1 + P2, equivalently 1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2 for the combined focal length F. A +4 D lens against a +1 D lens works as a single +5 D lens (F = 0.20 m). A diverging lens subtracts: +4 D with −1 D gives +3 D. This is why a lens set can be built up — and checked — one power at a time.

## Sub lesson 3 — Using combined power

Work in powers, convert at the end. Example: a convex lens of f = 50 cm (P = +2 D) in contact with a concave lens of f = 100 cm (P = −1 D) gives P = +1 D, so F = 1 m — still converging, but weaker than the convex lens alone. Check the sign first (is the pair converging or diverging?), then the size (a small combined power means a long focal length). Spectacle prescriptions, camera lens groups and the eye's own lens-plus-glasses pairing all run on this adding rule.

## The key ideas

- Lens power P = 1/f (f in metres), in dioptres — positive for convex, negative for concave
- Short focal length = strong lens = high power
- Lenses in contact add their powers: P = P1 + P2, so 1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2

## Do It Yourself
1. A lens has focal length 20 cm. Find its power, and say whether it is convex or concave if the power comes out positive. (Convert cm to m first.)
2. Two lenses in contact have powers +3 D and −1.5 D. Find the combined power and the combined focal length, and say whether the pair converges or diverges light.
3. An optician needs a combination of +5 D but has only a +8 D lens in the tray. What power must the second lens in contact be?
