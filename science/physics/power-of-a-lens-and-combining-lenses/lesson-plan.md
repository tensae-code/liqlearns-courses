## Sub lessons
1. **Lens power — the dioptre** — Power prices a lens by how strongly it bends light. Power P = 1/f, with f the focal length in metres; the unit is the dioptre (D). A convex (converging) lens has positive power, a concave (diverging) lens negative power. Short focal length means strong bending means high power: f = 0.25 m gives P = +4 D, while f = 2 m gives only +0.5 D. Opticians prescribe glasses by power, not by focal length, because power is the number that adds up — as the next part shows.
2. **Combining lenses — powers add** — Put two thin lenses in contact and the pair bends light twice in immediate succession, so their powers simply add: P = P1 + P2, equivalently 1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2 for the combined focal length F. A +4 D lens against a +1 D lens works as a single +5 D lens (F = 0.20 m). A diverging lens subtracts: +4 D with −1 D gives +3 D. This is why a lens set can be built up — and checked — one power at a time.
3. **Using combined power** — Work in powers, convert at the end. Example: a convex lens of f = 50 cm (P = +2 D) in contact with a concave lens of f = 100 cm (P = −1 D) gives P = +1 D, so F = 1 m — still converging, but weaker than the convex lens alone. Check the sign first (is the pair converging or diverging?), then the size (a small combined power means a long focal length). Spectacle prescriptions, camera lens groups and the eye's own lens-plus-glasses pairing all run on this adding rule.
