# Power of a lens and combining lenses — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do opticians prescribe power rather than focal length?
**Answer: Because powers add when lenses are combined (and the power states the bending strength directly); focal lengths do not add.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A +6 D lens is placed in contact with a −6 D lens. What is the pair's power and focal length?
**Answer: Power 0 D — the pair neither converges nor diverges overall, so its focal length is infinite (it acts like a parallel plate).**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in bending words, why powers add for lenses in contact.
**Answer: Each lens bends the ray in turn, one immediately after the other, so the total bending is the sum of the two bendings — and power is exactly the bending measure.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Which is stronger: a lens of f = 40 cm or one of f = 80 cm? Give both powers.
**Answer: The 40 cm lens: P = 1/0.40 = +2.5 D, against 1/0.80 = +1.25 D — the shorter focal length is the stronger lens.**
