# How this Physics course works — the Lao Grade 10 (ມ.5) way

## Do it first
Before you read: think of how your own schoolbooks were arranged. Did any of them stop, mid-course, and spend a whole lesson just showing you how to solve problems — not teaching a new idea, but teaching the solving itself? Laos' Grade 10 Physics book does exactly that. This lesson is the map of that book: its parts, its lesson shape, and its most unusual lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Seven parts, twenty-eight lessons

The course is organised in numbered ພາກ (parts) of numbered ບົດ (lessons): Part I Kinematics (Lessons 1–5), Part II Dynamics (6–9), Part III Different Types of Motion (10–15), Part IV Statics (16–18), Part V Work and Energy (19–21), Part VI Momentum (22–23) and Part VII Change of Matter due to Heat (24–28). Two choices stand out: kinematics gets a whole extra part (III) that re-teaches motion type by type — horizontal, vertical, inclined-plane, systems of bodies, throwing, circular — and statics is a full part of its own, ending with couple forces. Position and reference frames come before any formula: the book opens by defining where a thing is.

## Sub lesson 2 — The shape of every lesson

Each lesson is built the same way: the concept, then worked examples, then exercises. The teacher guide mirrors the student book lesson for lesson and adds a per-part concept inventory — the dynamics inventory names force types, inertia, friction kinds, Hooke's Law, centre-of-gravity and pressure terms — plus teaching notes and per-lesson answer keys. The guide's concept lists are the course's own checklist of what each part must land, and the worked examples in the student book carry the standard methods in full view before learners attempt the exercises.

## Sub lesson 3 — The worked-problems lesson

Part I does not end with exercises — it ends with Lesson 5, a whole lesson of worked examples of solving motion problems (printed p. 35). Problem-solving is taught as a lesson, not set as homework: the method is shown whole, on the page, in teaching time. That is Laos' own way of teaching made visible — the same worked-example spine that runs inside every lesson is gathered, once, into a lesson of its own, so learners see the solving itself as content to be learned, not a test of what was learned.

## The key ideas

- The Lao Grade 10 course: 7 parts / 28 lessons — Kinematics, Dynamics, Types of Motion, Statics, Work and Energy, Momentum, Heat
- Every lesson is built concept → worked examples → exercises
- Lesson 5 is a whole lesson of worked problem-solving — solving taught as content
- The teacher guide mirrors the book with per-part concept inventories, teaching notes and answer keys (evidence)
