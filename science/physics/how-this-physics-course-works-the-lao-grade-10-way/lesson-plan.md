## Sub lessons
1. **Seven parts, twenty-eight lessons** — The course is organised in numbered ພາກ (parts) of numbered ບົດ (lessons): Part I Kinematics (Lessons 1–5), Part II Dynamics (6–9), Part III Different Types of Motion (10–15), Part IV Statics (16–18), Part V Work and Energy (19–21), Part VI Momentum (22–23) and Part VII Change of Matter due to Heat (24–28). Two choices stand out: kinematics gets a whole extra part (III) that re-teaches motion type by type — horizontal, vertical, inclined-plane, systems of bodies, throwing, circular — and statics is a full part of its own, ending with couple forces. Position and reference frames come before any formula: the book opens by defining where a thing is.
2. **The shape of every lesson** — Each lesson is built the same way: the concept, then worked examples, then exercises. The teacher guide mirrors the student book lesson for lesson and adds a per-part concept inventory — the dynamics inventory names force types, inertia, friction kinds, Hooke's Law, centre-of-gravity and pressure terms — plus teaching notes and per-lesson answer keys. The guide's concept lists are the course's own checklist of what each part must land, and the worked examples in the student book carry the standard methods in full view before learners attempt the exercises.
3. **The worked-problems lesson** — Part I does not end with exercises — it ends with Lesson 5, a whole lesson of worked examples of solving motion problems (printed p. 35). Problem-solving is taught as a lesson, not set as homework: the method is shown whole, on the page, in teaching time. That is Laos' own way of teaching made visible — the same worked-example spine that runs inside every lesson is gathered, once, into a lesson of its own, so learners see the solving itself as content to be learned, not a test of what was learned.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
