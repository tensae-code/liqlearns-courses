# Lesson plan — Teaching to the standard — expected outcomes and cross-cutting concepts, the Armenian guide way

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Teaching to the standard — expected outcomes and cross-cutting concepts, the Armenian guide way
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the teaching-way strand — the shared opening chapter of every Armenian methodological guide (Grades 8–12): the state standard, the grade's expected outcomes, and the cross-cutting concepts, named before any topic (teacher-guide evidence)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every Armenian teacher guide opens the same way, before a single topic: Chapter I states the standard — what the state standard asks of physics teaching, the expected outcomes for that grade named as abilities, and the cross-cutting concepts that run through the whole course — so the year's teaching is planned from outcomes backwards (teacher-guide evidence only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The standard first — the guide's opening move** — The shared architecture of the Armenian guides is itself the content: Chapter I is never a topic. It sets out the conceptual provisions for teaching physics under the new state standard — the principles on which the course's content is built. A teacher who starts here knows what the course is FOR before knowing what it contains; the guides repeat the chapter at every grade because each grade's work must be re-anchored to the standard, not merely continued from last year's book.
2. **Expected outcomes — abilities named before topics** — For its own grade, each guide names the expected outcomes: what the learner will be able to do — explain, measure, solve, investigate — by the year's end. Outcomes are written as abilities, not as chapter titles, and they are printed before the thematic planning that serves them. That order is the method: plan from the promised abilities backwards to lessons, and every topic must show which outcome it pays into.
3. **Cross-cutting concepts — the threads through every topic** — The guides name cross-cutting concepts: the big ideas that reappear inside every topic — cause and effect, systems, energy, patterns, change — so that kinematics, heat and electricity are met as one connected subject wearing different clothes. The teacher is charged with making the threads visible: the same concept met in five topics is five lessons for the price of one, and the guides' first chapter exists to hand the teacher the thread-map before the topics begin.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
