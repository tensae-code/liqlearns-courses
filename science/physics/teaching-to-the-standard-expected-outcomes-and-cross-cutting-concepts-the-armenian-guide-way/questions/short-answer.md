# Teaching to the standard — expected outcomes and cross-cutting concepts, the Armenian guide way — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 2) Rewrite the topic 'Newton's second law' as an expected outcome in the guides' style.
**Answer:** Example: 'the learner will be able to predict how a body's motion changes under a known resultant force, and to test the prediction by measurement' — an ability with a performance in it, not a title.

**2.** (Sub lesson 3) What does a cross-cutting concept buy a teacher, practically?
**Answer:** Transfer: an idea met in several topics (energy in mechanics, heat and electricity) is recognised by pupils as one tool, so each new topic starts partly learned; the teacher plans those meetings deliberately instead of by accident.

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do the guides put content-construction principles in the same first chapter as the standard?
**Answer:** Because the principles are the standard made concrete: they tell the teacher how the course's content was chosen and ordered, so daily decisions (what to stress, what to connect) follow the same logic as the book's design.

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A guide's outcomes for a grade include laboratory abilities. Why does that matter for planning?
**Answer:** Laboratory outcomes force the year's plan to reserve time, apparatus and practice for investigation — if outcomes were knowledge-only, labs would be the first thing squeezed out.
