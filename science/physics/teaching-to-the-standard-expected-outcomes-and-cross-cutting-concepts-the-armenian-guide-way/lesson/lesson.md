# Teaching to the standard — expected outcomes and cross-cutting concepts, the Armenian guide way

## Do it first
Take any topic you teach (or are learning) and write its outcome the Armenian way: not 'Chapter 4: Friction' but 'the learner will be able to… measure friction, predict its effect and argue from evidence'. Then list which cross-cutting concepts the topic carries (cause and effect? energy? systems?). If the outcome cannot be written as an ability, the guides' first chapter says the planning has not started yet. — begin with the hands-on opening in hands-on.md before reading on. This lesson is the story of that activity.

## Sub lesson 1 — The standard first — the guide's opening move

The shared architecture of the Armenian guides is itself the content: Chapter I is never a topic. It sets out the conceptual provisions for teaching physics under the new state standard — the principles on which the course's content is built. A teacher who starts here knows what the course is FOR before knowing what it contains; the guides repeat the chapter at every grade because each grade's work must be re-anchored to the standard, not merely continued from last year's book.

## Sub lesson 2 — Expected outcomes — abilities named before topics

For its own grade, each guide names the expected outcomes: what the learner will be able to do — explain, measure, solve, investigate — by the year's end. Outcomes are written as abilities, not as chapter titles, and they are printed before the thematic planning that serves them. That order is the method: plan from the promised abilities backwards to lessons, and every topic must show which outcome it pays into.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cross-cutting concepts — the threads through every topic

The guides name cross-cutting concepts: the big ideas that reappear inside every topic — cause and effect, systems, energy, patterns, change — so that kinematics, heat and electricity are met as one connected subject wearing different clothes. The teacher is charged with making the threads visible: the same concept met in five topics is five lessons for the price of one, and the guides' first chapter exists to hand the teacher the thread-map before the topics begin.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the lesson's one thing in your own words: every Armenian teacher guide opens the same way, before a single topic: Chapter I states the standard — what the state standard asks of physics teaching, the expected outcomes for that grade named as abilities, and the cross-cutting concepts that run through the whole course — so the year's teaching is planned from outcomes backwards (teacher-guide evidence only). If your companion can work the Do It Yourself items without looking back, the lesson has landed.
