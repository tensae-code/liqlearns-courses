# Entropy and the second law of thermodynamics

## Do it first
Before you read: a hot mug of tea always cools; cooled tea never warms itself back up by gathering heat from the room. Both directions would conserve energy — so why does nature allow only one? Name another one-way process you know. The lesson gives the law behind all of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — One-way processes and entropy

Many processes conserve energy in both directions yet run only one way: heat flows hot → cold, gases expand to fill a space and never gather back, friction turns motion into heat and never the reverse. The second law prices the direction with a quantity called entropy: in every real (irreversible) process the total entropy of the universe increases; in an ideal reversible process it stays the same; it never decreases. Entropy measures how spread out and disordered the energy and matter have become.

## Sub lesson 2 — Heat engines — work from a flow of heat

A heat engine (a car engine, a steam turbine) takes heat from a hot source, turns part of it into work, and must dump the rest into a colder sink. The second law sets its ceiling: the efficiency can never reach 100%, because some heat must be rejected — the best possible (Carnot) efficiency depends only on the two temperatures. An engine trying to keep all its heat would be a one-reservoir machine, and the second law forbids it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Refrigerators — paying work to run heat backwards

A refrigerator moves heat from cold to hot — the forbidden direction — and can do it only because work is paid in: a motor drives the cycle, and the heat dumped at the back is the heat removed from inside plus the work’s energy. Left with its door open, a fridge warms a kitchen rather than cooling it. Engines, refrigerators and the cooling mug are one law seen three ways: entropy of the whole never falls.

## The key ideas

- Entropy measures spread-out disorder; in every real process the universe’s total entropy increases
- Heat flows by itself only from hotter to colder — the second law’s simplest statement
- A heat engine must reject some heat to a cold sink; no engine is 100% efficient
- A refrigerator moves heat cold → hot only by paying work in

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: the second law of thermodynamics: heat flows by itself only from hotter to colder, no heat engine can turn all its heat into work, and the universe’s entropy — its spread-out disorder — never decreases in any real process. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Classify each as allowed or forbidden by the second law: (a) a mug of tea cooling in a room; (b) a room’s heat gathering itself into a cold mug, warming it; (c) an engine turning 30% of its heat into work and rejecting 70%; (d) an engine turning 100% of its heat into work with no cold sink.
2. A power station takes heat at high temperature and rejects it to a river. Why must the river warm at all — why can the station not use every joule for electricity?
3. A fridge is left running with its door open in a sealed kitchen. Predict, with the second law, whether the kitchen ends warmer or colder, and why.
