# The three effects of an electric current — thermal, magnetic, chemical

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The thermal effect — current makes heat

Pass a current through a wire and the wire warms: electrical energy becomes heat, the Joule effect. It is the working principle of the toaster, the iron and the radiator — a resistance chosen to glow or warm — and of the fuse, the safety device that sacrifices itself by this same effect, melting when the current runs too large. Mauritania's chapter order is experience first: feel the warm wire, then name the effect, then list where it is used and where it is guarded against.

## Sub lesson 2 — The magnetic effect — Oersted's compass

Lay a wire beside a compass needle and switch on: the needle swings. In 1820 Oersted's observation — a current creates a magnetic field around its wire — founded electromagnetism. Strengthen the field by coiling the wire and adding an iron core and you have the electromagnet: lifted scrap in yards, the bell, and inside every motor, where the field's push on a current-carrying coil is turned into rotation.

## Sub lesson 3 — The chemical effect — electrolysis

Send a current through a liquid compound and the compound splits: electrolysis. Water divides into hydrogen and oxygen; metals plate onto objects dipped in the right bath; batteries are charged by driving the effect backwards. The Mauritanian chapter closes the three effects with their shared shape — one current, three signatures: heat in a wire, magnetism around it, chemistry through a liquid — each read by its own experiment.

## The key ideas

- One current, three effects: thermal, magnetic, chemical
- Thermal (Joule) effect: current heats a resistance — toaster, iron, and the fuse that melts to protect
- Magnetic effect: Oersted 1820 — a current's field swings a compass needle; coiled with an iron core it is an electromagnet
- Chemical effect: electrolysis splits compounds — water into hydrogen and oxygen, metal plating, battery charging
- Each effect is met by its experiment first: warm wire, swinging needle, gas bubbles
