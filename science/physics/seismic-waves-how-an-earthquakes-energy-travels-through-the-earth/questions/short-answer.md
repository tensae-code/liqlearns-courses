# Seismic waves — how an earthquake's energy travels through the Earth — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) In Moldova's Grade 10 book, where does an earthquake's energy come from?
**Answer: From the movement of tectonic plates: stress builds as plates press and creep against each other until the rock slips suddenly at the focus, releasing the stored energy as seismic waves.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A distant station receives P waves from an earthquake but no S waves. What does the missing S tell us, and about which layer?
**Answer: The S waves were stopped by liquid on their path — S waves are transverse and travel through solids only — which is how we know the Earth's outer core is liquid.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Put the three wave families in arrival order at a distant station and say why that is the order.
**Answer: P first (fastest, longitudinal body wave), then S (slower, transverse body wave), then the surface waves (slowest, travelling round the surface).**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How do three seismograph stations fix an epicentre between them?
**Answer: Each station uses its P–S gap to estimate its distance from the earthquake and draws a circle of that radius on the map; the epicentre is where the three circles meet.**
