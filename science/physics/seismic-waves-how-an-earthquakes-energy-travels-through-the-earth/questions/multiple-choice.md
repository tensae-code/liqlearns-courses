# Seismic waves — how an earthquake's energy travels through the Earth — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) The focus (hypocentre) of an earthquake is…
**Answer: a) the underground point where the rock slips and the earthquake starts  b) the place on the surface that reports the earthquake  c) the instrument that records ground motion  d) the edge of a tectonic plate**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) The epicentre is…
**Answer: a) the point on the surface directly above the focus  b) the deepest point of the slip  c) the last place the waves reach  d) the centre of the Earth**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) P waves are…
**Answer: a) longitudinal, the fastest seismic waves, travelling through solids and liquids  b) transverse and the slowest  c) surface waves that roll along the ground  d) waves that travel through solids only**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) S waves cannot pass through liquids because they are…
**Answer: a) transverse — a liquid cannot spring back from a sideways shake  b) too fast  c) longitudinal  d) reflected by water**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) The waves that do most of the damage to buildings are…
**Answer: a) the surface waves — they move the ground at the surface, where buildings stand, and arrive last but shake longest  b) the P waves  c) the S waves  d) the waves in the core**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) A station farther from an earthquake records a bigger P–S arrival gap because…
**Answer: a) P and S travel at different speeds, so the faster P pulls further ahead the longer they travel  b) the S waves get tired  c) the focus moves  d) the seismograph slows down with distance**
