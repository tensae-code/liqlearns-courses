# Lesson plan — Seismic waves — how an earthquake's energy travels through the Earth

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Seismic waves — how an earthquake's energy travels through the Earth
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the wave lessons — How waves travel, Wave words and When waves meet: the wave family turned on the Earth itself

## Sub lessons
1. **Where the shaking starts — plates, focus and epicentre** — The Earth's outer shell is made of tectonic plates that creep against each other; stress builds until the rock slips suddenly at one point underground — the **focus** (hypocentre). The point on the surface directly above it is the **epicentre**, the place that reports the earthquake and usually feels the strongest shaking. Moldova's Grade 10 book closes its waves chapter with seismic waves as their own optional section (§5.13), starting from exactly this plate origin.
2. **Body waves — P and S through the Earth** — Two wave kinds travel through the Earth's body. **P waves** (primary) are longitudinal — the rock squeezes and stretches along the direction of travel, like sound in air; they are the fastest seismic waves and pass through solids and liquids alike. **S waves** (secondary) are transverse — the rock shakes side to side, at right angles to the travel; they are slower, and because a liquid has no stiffness against sideways shaking they cannot pass through liquids at all — the S-wave shadow on the far side of the Earth is how we know the outer core is liquid.
3. **Surface waves — the rolling that does the damage** — The third family never dives: **surface waves** travel round the surface from the epicentre, slowest of the three, and their rolling, side-to-side motion shakes buildings at the surface — they carry the destruction. A seismograph records all three families in arrival order — P first, then S, then the big surface waves — and the gap between the P and S arrivals grows with distance, which is how one station estimates how far away the earthquake was, and three stations fix the epicentre.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: an earthquake's focus is the underground point where the rock slips; the epicentre is the surface point directly above it.
2. Say, in their own words: P waves are longitudinal, fastest, and travel through solids and liquids; S waves are transverse, slower, and travel through solids only.
3. Say, in their own words: surface waves travel along the surface, arrive last, and do most of the damage.
4. Say, in their own words: the P–S arrival gap at a seismograph grows with distance, so stations can locate the epicentre.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.
