# Simple machines at work

## Do it first
List the tools used in one real job near you — digging, building, fetching water — and name the machine inside each tool.

[Picture 21838 — farm and workshop scene with machines ringed — hoe, axe, crowbar, wheelbarrow]

## Simple machines at work — the teaching
Workplaces run on the same family. On farms and building sites: the hoe and the axe (wedge and lever together), the crowbar (a lever for prising), and the wheelbarrow (a lever on a wheel — load between fulcrum and effort). Local work is the test bench of this unit: each tool earns its place by multiplying force, multiplying speed, or changing direction for a real job.

## The key ideas

- A crowbar is a lever for prising loads loose.
- A wheelbarrow is a lever on a wheel, load in the middle.
- An axe combines a lever handle with a wedge head.

## Say it to your companion
Explain simple machines at work to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Visit (or picture) a workshop and catalogue its simple machines by purpose.
2. Write the key ideas in your own words — one line each — without looking back. Then check them against the lesson and fix any line that drifted.
