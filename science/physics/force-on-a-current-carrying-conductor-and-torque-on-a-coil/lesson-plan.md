## Sub lessons
1. **Force on a straight conductor — F = BIL sinθ** — A current is moving charge, and a magnetic field pushes moving charge sideways — so a current-carrying conductor in a field feels a force. Its size is F = BIL sinθ: stronger field B, bigger current I and longer conductor L in the field all raise it, and θ — the angle between conductor and field — scales it: full force across the field (θ = 90°), no force along it (θ = 0°). Fleming's left-hand rule gives the direction: First finger Field, seCond finger Current, thuMb Motion (the force).
2. **Reading the direction — and the zero cases** — Interchange current direction or field direction and the force reverses; reverse both and it stays. A conductor lying along the field lines feels nothing, however large the current — sinθ = 0 does the silencing. Two parallel current-carrying wires use the same law twice: each sits in the other's field, so currents the same way attract and opposite ways repel. Every direction question in this lesson is the left-hand rule plus the sinθ size check.
3. **Torque on a coil — a couple that turns** — Wind the conductor into a rectangular coil in the field and the two long sides carry current in opposite directions, so they feel opposite forces — a couple. The couple's moment (torque) is τ = NIBA sinθ for N turns of area A, greatest when the coil's plane lies along the field and zero when the plane stands across it. Fit a commutator to swap the current each half-turn and the torque never reverses: the coil spins continuously. That is the electric motor; without the commutator, the same coil settles pointing across the field — the moving-coil galvanometer.
