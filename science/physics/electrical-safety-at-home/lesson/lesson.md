## Electrical safety at home — the teaching
Electric shocks harm according to the current through the body, which grows when resistance falls — and wet skin has far lower resistance than dry. That is why wet hands at a switch and damaged, frayed insulation are so dangerous. Fuses and proper earthing protect homes: a fuse melts and breaks the circuit when current runs too high; earthing gives fault current a safe path. The lesson gathers safety measures for Ethiopian homes and local wiring practice: dry hands, sound insulation, no overloaded sockets, respect for every wire.

## The key ideas

- Wet skin lowers resistance, so wet hands make shocks worse.
- A fuse breaks the circuit when current grows too high.
- Earthing gives fault current a safe path to ground.
