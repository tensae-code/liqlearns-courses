# Video script — Electrical safety at home

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

Electric shocks harm according to the current through the body, which grows when resistance falls — and wet skin has far lower resistance than dry.
That is why wet hands at a switch and damaged, frayed insulation are so dangerous.
Fuses and proper earthing protect homes: a fuse melts and breaks the circuit when current runs too high; earthing gives fault current a safe path.
The lesson gathers safety measures for Ethiopian homes and local wiring practice: dry hands, sound insulation, no overloaded sockets, respect for every wire.

Remember: **Wet skin lowers resistance, so wet hands make shocks worse.**
Remember: **A fuse breaks the circuit when current grows too high.**
Remember: **Earthing gives fault current a safe path to ground.**
