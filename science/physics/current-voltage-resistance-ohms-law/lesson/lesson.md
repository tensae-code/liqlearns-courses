# Current, voltage, resistance — Ohm's law

## Do it first
Wire a battery and bulb; add a longer, thinner wire in series and watch the bulb dim. Resistance just announced itself.

## Current, voltage, resistance — Ohm's law — the teaching
Three quantities run every circuit. Current is the rate of flow of charge, measured in amperes (A). Voltage (potential difference) is the energy given to each unit charge, measured in volts (V). Resistance opposes the flow, measured in ohms (Ω) — and a conductor's resistance depends on its material, length and thickness. Ohm's law binds them: V = IR. The book's puzzle: birds sit unharmed on one transmission wire because both feet stand at the same potential — no potential difference across the bird, so no current through it.

**Keep the formula:** V = IR

## The key ideas

- Current = rate of charge flow (A); voltage = energy per charge (V); resistance opposes flow (Ω).
- Ohm's law: V = IR.
- Resistance depends on material, length and cross-section (thickness).

## Say it to your companion
Explain current, voltage, resistance — ohm's law to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
