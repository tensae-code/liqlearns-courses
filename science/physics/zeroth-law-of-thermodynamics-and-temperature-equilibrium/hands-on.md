# Hands-on — Zeroth law of thermodynamics and temperature equilibrium

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The spoon test
Put a metal spoon in a cup of hot water (take care — hot, not boiling). Hold the top of the spoon. Feel the heat arrive, then feel it stop changing. Say aloud: heat flowed from the hotter to the colder — and when my fingers stop feeling change, spoon and water are near thermal equilibrium.

## Wait for the settle
If a thermometer is at hand: put it in cold water, then in warm water. Watch the reading move — and keep watching until it stops. Time how long settling takes each time. The lesson in your hands: a reading taken before the settle is the thermometer's own journey, not the water's temperature.

## Three bodies, one check
Choose three things in the room that have sat together a long time — a book, a table, a cup left standing. They have been in thermal contact through the air and the table. Predict: are they in equilibrium with each other? Use the zeroth law to say why they must share one temperature without your measuring all three pairs.
