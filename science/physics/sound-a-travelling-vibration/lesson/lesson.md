# Sound — a travelling vibration

## Do it first
Press your ear to a table while a partner taps the far end softly. The tap arrives clearer through the wood than through the air — sound loves a medium.

[Picture 21859 — vibrating kirar string with compressions and rarefactions spreading from it]

## Sound — a travelling vibration — the teaching
Sound is produced by vibrating sources — a kirar string, a voice, a drum skin. It travels as a longitudinal wave and it needs a medium: in a vacuum there is nothing to compress, so there is no sound. Its speed differs by material — faster in solids and liquids than in air — and changes with the air's temperature. Estimating echoes and timing thunder are everyday ways of measuring the speed of sound in air.

## The key ideas

- Sound is made by vibrating sources and travels as a longitudinal wave.
- Sound needs a medium — no sound in a vacuum.
- Sound travels faster in solids and liquids than in air.

## Say it to your companion
Explain sound — a travelling vibration to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
