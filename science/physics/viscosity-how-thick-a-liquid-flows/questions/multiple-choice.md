# Viscosity — how thick a liquid flows — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Viscosity is best described as…
a) A liquid's density  b) The internal friction between a liquid's layers as they slide over each other  c) The pressure a liquid exerts on walls  d) A liquid's surface tension
**Answer: b) The internal friction between a liquid's layers as they slide over each other**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) In liquid flowing through a pipe, the fastest layer is…
a) At the wall  b) Just under the surface skin  c) At the centre, with layers slowing toward the wall  d) All layers move together at one speed
**Answer: c) At the centre, with layers slowing toward the wall**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) The SI unit of the coefficient of viscosity η is…
a) The pascal  b) Pa·s (pascal-second)  c) N/m  d) kg/m³
**Answer: b) Pa·s (pascal-second)**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) When a liquid is warmed, its viscosity…
a) Always rises  b) Falls — the grip between its molecules weakens  c) Stays exactly constant  d) First rises, then falls to zero
**Answer: b) Falls — the grip between its molecules weakens**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) A ball falling in oil reaches a terminal speed when…
a) Its weight becomes zero  b) Weight = upthrust + viscous drag, so the resultant force is zero  c) The oil's viscosity becomes zero  d) It reaches the centre of the liquid
**Answer: b) Weight = upthrust + viscous drag, so the resultant force is zero**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) An engine oil must be viscous enough to…
a) Stop the engine overheating by itself  b) Keep a separating film between moving metal surfaces, yet thin enough to be pumped on a cold start  c) Make the fuel burn slower  d) Raise the fuel's octane rating
**Answer: b) Keep a separating film between moving metal surfaces, yet thin enough to be pumped on a cold start**
