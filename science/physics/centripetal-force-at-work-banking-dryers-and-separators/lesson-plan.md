# Lesson plan — Centripetal force at work — banking, dryers and separators

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Centripetal force at work — banking, dryers and separators

## Sub lessons
1. **Banked roads — the slope lends a hand** — On a flat curve, friction alone must pull the car toward the centre. Bank the road — raise its outer edge — and the normal reaction tilts inward, so a component of it points to the centre and helps supply mv²/r. At the banking's design speed the car can take the bend with no friction at all; the road itself does part of the centre-pulling.
2. **The dryer — what cannot turn, leaves** — In a washing-machine dryer the drum spins fast and its wall pushes the clothes inward, supplying their centripetal force. The water drops in the clothes need the same inward pull — but where a hole in the drum faces them, no wall pushes, so they fly out straight along the tangent. Drying is centripetal force failing, on purpose, for the water only.
3. **The cream separator — turning sorts by mass** — Spin milk in a separator and every part is pulled toward the centre with the force its circular path demands. The heavier skim milk can be held to the wider, outer path; the lighter cream gathers nearer the axis. Turning has sorted the mixture by density — the same mv²/r bookkeeping, used as a sieve.
