# Resistors in series and parallel

## Do it first
Wire two lamps in series and feel their dimness with your eyes; then in parallel and see them brighten. The arithmetic of this lesson has a visible face.

[Picture 21906 — two lamps in series vs parallel, brightness compared]

## Sub lesson 1 — Resistors in series
In series, resistors sit on one path. The same current flows through each; the voltages across them add up to the supply; and the resistances simply add: R = R₁ + R₂ + … Check case: 4 Ω + 8 Ω + 12 Ω in series totals 24 Ω.

**Keep the formulas:** Series: R = R₁ + R₂; Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂

## Sub lesson 2 — Resistors in parallel
In parallel, resistors sit on branching paths. The same voltage stands across each branch; the currents divide between the branches and add; and the reciprocals add: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ — giving an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest branch.

## Sub lesson 3 — Combination circuits
Combination circuits are reduced step by step: collapse one clear series or parallel group into its equivalent, redraw, and repeat until one equivalent resistance remains; then work currents and voltages back through the redrawings.

## The key ideas

- Series: resistances add, current the same, voltage divides.
- Parallel: reciprocals add, voltage the same, current divides.
- Combinations are reduced one group at a time and redrawn.
