# Video script — Resistors in series and parallel

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.
---

In series, resistors sit on one path.
The same current flows through each; the voltages across them add up to the supply; and the resistances simply add: R = R₁ + R₂ + … Check case: 4 Ω + 8 Ω + 12 Ω in series totals 24 Ω.
In parallel, resistors sit on branching paths.
The same voltage stands across each branch; the currents divide between the branches and add; and the reciprocals add: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ — giving an equivalent resistance smaller than the smallest branch.
Combination circuits are reduced step by step: collapse one clear series or parallel group into its equivalent, redraw, and repeat until one equivalent resistance remains; then work currents and voltages back through the redrawings.

Remember: **Series: resistances add, current the same, voltage divides.**
Remember: **Parallel: reciprocals add, voltage the same, current divides.**
Remember: **Combinations are reduced one group at a time and redrawn.**
Keep the formula: **Series: R = R₁ + R₂; Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂**
