# Two wires pull or push each other

## Do it first
Hold two pencils as model wires and act the story: draw field circles around one, push the other with the motor rule, and check the verdict matches the lesson.

[Picture 21922 — two hanging wires bowing toward (same current) and away (opposite)]

## Two wires pull or push each other — the teaching
Two parallel wires, each carrying current, meet through their fields. Wire one's field circles it; wire two, sitting in that field, feels the motor-effect push. Chain the reasoning and the verdict is: currents in the same direction attract; currents in opposite directions repel. Hang the wires loosely and switch on — same-way currents bow the wires toward each other; reverse one and they bow apart.

## The key ideas

- Each wire's field acts on the other wire's current.
- Same-direction currents attract.
- Opposite-direction currents repel.

## Say it to your companion
Explain two wires pull or push each other to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
