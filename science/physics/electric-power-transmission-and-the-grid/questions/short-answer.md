# Electric power transmission and the grid — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A heater is rated 2,400 W at 240 V. Find its current and the energy it uses in 3 hours, in kWh.
**Answer: I = P/V = 2,400/240 = 10 A. Energy = P × t = 2.4 kW × 3 h = 7.2 kWh.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Show, with numbers, why a 100-fold rise in voltage beats any practical thickening of the wire for cutting loss.
**Answer: At fixed power the current falls 100 times, so I²R falls 10,000 times. Cutting resistance by thickening could at best divide R by a small factor for a huge cost in metal — the voltage lever is squared, the resistance lever is not.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) List the grid journey in order from power station to socket.
**Answer: Generation → step-up transformer → high-voltage transmission lines → step-down substation(s) → distribution lines → final step-down to supply voltage → home meter and circuits → socket.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why must the grid balance supply and demand moment by moment rather than once a day?
**Answer: Electricity in the grid is used as it is generated; if demand exceeds supply the voltage sags and blackouts follow, and surplus generation has nowhere to go — so generation is matched to demand continuously.**
