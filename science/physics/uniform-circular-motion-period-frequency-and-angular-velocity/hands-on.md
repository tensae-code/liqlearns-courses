# Hands-on — Uniform circular motion — period, frequency and angular velocity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Count the turns
Put a tape dot on the rim of a bicycle wheel (or on a round lid). Spin it steadily and count how many turns the dot makes in 10 seconds. Divide by 10: that is the frequency in turns per second. Flip it, 1 ÷ f: that is the period, the seconds for one turn. Write both down — you have measured T and f with nothing but a count.

## Two points, one turning
Put a second dot halfway between the centre and the rim. Spin the wheel again at the same steady rate. Both dots share the same period, the same frequency and the same angular velocity — but the rim dot travels a circle twice as wide, so its linear velocity is twice the inner dot's. Say aloud: same ω, double r, double v — that is v = ωr in your hands.

## Swing the circle
Tie a small ball to a string and swing it in a steady horizontal circle above your head, clear of people. Have a partner time 10 full turns. Work out T, f, and — with the string's length as the radius — the ball's linear velocity v = 2πr/T and angular velocity ω = 2π/T. Change the rate and measure again: every quantity in the lesson, from your own arm.
