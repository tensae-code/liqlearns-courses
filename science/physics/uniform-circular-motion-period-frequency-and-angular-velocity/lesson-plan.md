# Lesson plan — Uniform circular motion — period, frequency and angular velocity

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Uniform circular motion — period, frequency and angular velocity
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the circular-motion lessons — Going round in circles — angular speed, Centripetal acceleration — always toward the centre and Centripetal force — what pulls you in: this is the uniform-circular-motion lesson itself, the one that defines the quantities those lessons use
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a body in uniform circular motion rounds a circle at constant speed, and three quantities describe the whole motion — the period T (the time for one full turn), the frequency f (the turns made each second, f = 1/T), and the two speeds the turning carries: the linear (tangential) velocity v = 2πr/T = 2πrf along the circle, and the angular velocity ω = 2π/T = 2πf, the angle swept each second, joined by v = ωr
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Uniform motion is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Round and round at a steady pace — the period and the frequency** — uniform circular motion: a circular path travelled at constant speed; the period T is the time one full turn takes, and the frequency f is how many turns fit into one second — the two are each other's flip, f = 1/T, with f measured in hertz.
2. **Linear velocity — how fast along the circle** — in one period the body travels the whole circumference 2πr, so its linear (tangential) velocity is v = 2πr/T, the same written with frequency as v = 2πrf; it points along the tangent, so its direction changes at every instant even though its size never does.
3. **Angular velocity — how fast the angle sweeps** — the radius sweeps one full turn's angle, 2π radians, in one period: the angular velocity ω = 2π/T = 2πf, in radians per second; the two speeds are joined by v = ωr — farther from the centre, same turning, faster travel.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Uniform motion** and **Scalars and vectors**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what uniform circular motion is, and define the period and the frequency, flipping between them with f = 1/T.
2. Work out the linear (tangential) velocity of a point on a turning circle from its radius and period or frequency: v = 2πr/T = 2πrf.
3. Work out the angular velocity ω = 2π/T = 2πf and use v = ωr to pass between the two speeds.
4. Say why the speed can be constant while the velocity keeps changing — the direction turns with the body.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A bicycle wheel (or a round lid or plate), a piece of tape or chalk for a marker dot, a phone or clock that counts seconds, string and a small ball for swinging in a horizontal circle, paper and a pencil for the turns table.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the counted turns. Turns per second is the frequency; seconds per turn is the period; the whole lesson is built on those two counts.
- Mixes up period and frequency → they are flips of each other: many turns per second (big f) means little time per turn (small T). Check with the flip: f = 1/T.
- Thinks constant speed means no change at all → the speed's size is constant, but the velocity points along the tangent, and the tangent turns: the direction changes at every instant.
