# Force between two parallel current-carrying wires — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) The force on wire 2 exists because…
a) Wire 2 is charged  b) Wire 2's current flows through the magnetic field made by wire 1  c) The wires are warm  d) Gravity acts sideways on currents
**Answer: b) Wire 2's current flows through the magnetic field made by wire 1**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) By Newton's third law, the force on wire 1 compared with the force on wire 2 is…
a) Twice as large  b) Half as large  c) Equal in size and opposite in direction  d) Zero — only wire 2 is pushed
**Answer: c) Equal in size and opposite in direction**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Two parallel wires carrying currents in OPPOSITE directions…
a) Attract  b) Repel  c) Feel no force  d) Rotate until parallel to the field
**Answer: b) Repel**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A coil's turns squeeze together along its axis when it carries a current because…
a) The turns are parallel currents in the same direction, attracting each other  b) The coil heats and contracts  c) The insulation shrinks  d) Earth's field pulls them
**Answer: a) The turns are parallel currents in the same direction, attracting each other**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) The force per unit length between two wires is F/L = μ₀I₁I₂/(2πd). Doubling BOTH currents multiplies the force by…
a) 2  b) 4  c) 8  d) It stays the same
**Answer: b) 4**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) The ampere was historically defined as the current producing, between wires 1 m apart, a force per metre of…
a) 1 N  b) 2 × 10⁻⁵ N  c) 2 × 10⁻⁷ N  d) 9.8 N
**Answer: c) 2 × 10⁻⁷ N**
