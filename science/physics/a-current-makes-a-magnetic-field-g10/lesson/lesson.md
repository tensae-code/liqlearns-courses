# A current makes a magnetic field

## Do it first
Grip your right fist with thumb up: that hand is now the rule. Point the thumb along any imagined current and read the field off your fingers.

[Picture 21916 — wire through card, compasses circling it; solenoid with bar-magnet-like poles]

## A current makes a magnetic field — the teaching
Pass a current through a straight wire and a magnetic field appears in concentric circles around the wire. The right-hand grip rule gives its direction: thumb along the current, fingers curl the way the field runs. Wind the wire into a coil — a solenoid — and the circles add up into a field like a bar magnet's, with poles at its ends, strength growing with current and with turns. Electricity has just manufactured magnetism: the bridge this whole unit walks on.

## The key ideas

- A current's field forms concentric circles around the wire.
- Right-hand grip rule: thumb = current, curled fingers = field direction.
- A solenoid's field resembles a bar magnet's, with poles.

## Say it to your companion
Explain a current makes a magnetic field to your companion in your own words. Use the key ideas above, give one example from your own home or street, and finish with the hardest point — the one you would have got wrong before this lesson. If your companion can repeat the main idea back to you, the lesson has landed.
