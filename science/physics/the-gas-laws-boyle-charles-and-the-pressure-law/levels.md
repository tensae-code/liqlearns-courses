# The gas laws — Boyle, Charles and the pressure law — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner states the lesson's one thing in their own words — a fixed mass of gas obeys three simple laws when one quantity is held steady: Boyle's law — at constant temperature, pressure × volume is constant (squeeze it smaller, it presses harder); Charles's law — at constant pressure, volume grows in proportion to the Kelvin temperature; and the pressure law — at constant volume, pressure grows in proportion to the Kelvin temperature — and the three combine into one rule, PV/T = constant — and proves it with the lesson's own activities, answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has 3 sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — doing the lesson's own experiments and sorts by hand, naming each idea aloud, and reasoning about new examples until the teaching is the learner's own.
