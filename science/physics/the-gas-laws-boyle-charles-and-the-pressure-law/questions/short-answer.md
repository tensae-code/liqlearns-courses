# The gas laws — Boyle, Charles and the pressure law — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A gas at 200 kPa occupies 4 litres. At the same temperature it expands to 8 litres. Find the new pressure.
**Answer: By Boyle's law P₁V₁ = P₂V₂, so P₂ = 200 × 4 ÷ 8 = 100 kPa. Doubling the volume halves the pressure.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do the gas laws fail if temperatures are left in Celsius?
**Answer: The laws are proportions counting from absolute zero, where particle motion's energy scale begins. Celsius sets its zero at freezing water, so ratios of Celsius temperatures are meaningless — 20 °C is not twice as hot as 10 °C, but 293 K versus 283 K states the true ratio.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A sealed rigid can of air at 300 K has pressure 120 kPa. It is heated to 450 K. Find the new pressure.
**Answer: By the pressure law P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂, so P₂ = 120 × 450 ÷ 300 = 180 kPa. The volume cannot change, so the faster particles strike harder and more often.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Show that Boyle's law is inside the combined law.
**Answer: Set T₁ = T₂ in P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂. The temperatures cancel, leaving P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ — Boyle's law. Holding a quantity steady is what picks each simple law out of the combined one.**
