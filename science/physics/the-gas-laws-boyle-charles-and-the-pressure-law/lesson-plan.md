# Lesson plan — The gas laws — Boyle, Charles and the pressure law

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** The gas laws — Boyle, Charles and the pressure law
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), beside the heat lessons and the ideal gas law: the three simple laws that the ideal gas law gathers into one equation
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a fixed mass of gas obeys three simple laws when one quantity is held steady: Boyle's law — at constant temperature, pressure × volume is constant (squeeze it smaller, it presses harder); Charles's law — at constant pressure, volume grows in proportion to the Kelvin temperature; and the pressure law — at constant volume, pressure grows in proportion to the Kelvin temperature — and the three combine into one rule, PV/T = constant
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Any earlier lesson named below is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Boyle's law — squeeze it and it presses back** — At fixed temperature, P × V never changes.
2. **Charles's law and the pressure law — heat it and something must give** — Kelvin or the laws break: V/T at fixed pressure, P/T at fixed volume.
3. **The combined law — all three at once** — One equation, three laws inside it.

## Before this lesson
- This lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly; anything that would help is explained inside it as it is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Boyle's law: at constant temperature, P × V = constant — halve the volume, double the pressure.
2. Say, in their own words: Charles's law: at constant pressure, V/T = constant, with T in kelvin.
3. Say, in their own words: The pressure law: at constant volume, P/T = constant, with T in kelvin.
4. Say, in their own words: The combined law P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ handles changes where nothing is held steady, and each simple law falls out of it.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A sealed plastic syringe (no needle) whose tip can be blocked, a kettle of warm water, and a bowl of cold water for the activities.

## If the learner is stuck
- Doubles the Celsius temperature in Charles's law → the proportions count from absolute zero — convert to kelvin first (add 273), then take the ratio.
- Applies Boyle's law while the gas is also warming → Boyle needs temperature held steady; if temperature changes too, use the combined law.
- Cannot start → return to Do it first. Hold that puzzle; the lesson is its solution.
