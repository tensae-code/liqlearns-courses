# Practice — finding momentum before and after a collision

## Do it first
Before you read: roll a ball into an identical resting ball, head-on, on a smooth floor. The first ball stops dead and the second rolls away at about the first ball's speed — something was handed over whole. Now roll it into a lump of soft clay instead: they stick and crawl away together, slower. This lesson measures exactly what was handed over in each case.

## Sub lesson 1 — Momentum before — weigh, then time

Vietnam's Grade 10 book sets this as Bài 30, a practice lesson in its own right: the law of conservation of momentum is not just stated in Bài 29, it is **measured** in Bài 30 — before totals and after totals, computed from the student's own weighings and timings.

## Sub lesson 2 — Collide — stick or bounce — and measure after

The after-total is the sum of the signed momenta: m₁v₁′ + m₂v₂′ for a bounce, (m₁ + m₂)v′ for a stick. Both come from measurements taken the same way as the before-values — same gates, same tape — so the comparison is fair.

## Sub lesson 3 — Compare the totals — and account for the difference

When the totals agree within the measured errors, the class has done more than use the law: it has **checked** it, with its own trolleys, and can say how well it held. That is the standing of every conservation law in physics — believed because benches keep agreeing with it.

## The key ideas

- Momentum before: weigh each trolley and measure its incoming velocity, then p = mv for each, with a chosen positive direction and signs written down
- After a sticking collision the pair moves as one mass at one velocity; after a bouncing collision each trolley has its own signed velocity
- The total momentum after is the sum of the signed momenta — a rebound counts negative
- Equal before/after totals (within error) verify conservation; friction and a sloping track explain the small shortfall

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: Momentum is put to the test by measuring it: weigh each trolley, measure its velocity before the collision and both velocities after it (gates or tape), compute mv for each, and compare the total momentum before with the total after — equal, within honest error, for a collision that sticks and for one that bounces. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Trolley A (0.40 kg) moves at 0.80 m/s into resting trolley B (0.40 kg) and they stick. Find the momentum before, and the pair's velocity after if momentum is conserved.
2. Trolley A (0.50 kg) at 1.2 m/s bounces off resting trolley B (0.50 kg); afterwards A rebounds at 0.20 m/s. Use conservation to find B's velocity after, watching the signs.
3. A group finds total momentum 0.60 kg·m/s before and 0.54 kg·m/s after. Give two bench reasons for the shortfall and say whether the law has failed.
