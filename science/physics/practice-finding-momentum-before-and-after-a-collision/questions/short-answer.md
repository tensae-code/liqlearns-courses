# Practice — finding momentum before and after a collision — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A 0.60 kg trolley moves at 0.50 m/s. What is its momentum, and what else must be stated for the value to be complete?
**Answer: p = mv = 0.60 × 0.50 = 0.30 kg·m/s — and its direction (the chosen positive direction along the track) must be stated, because momentum is a vector.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Two 0.40 kg trolleys stick after one hits the other at 0.90 m/s. What is the pair's speed if momentum is conserved?
**Answer: Before: 0.40 × 0.90 = 0.36 kg·m/s. After: (0.40 + 0.40) × v = 0.36, so v = 0.45 m/s — half the incoming speed, as equal masses sharing the momentum must.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Why is the same timing method used before and after the collision?
**Answer: So the comparison is fair: any small error of the method then affects both totals in the same way, instead of a difference of methods masquerading as a change in momentum.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Sound and heat are produced in the collision. Do they carry away momentum? What do they carry away?
**Answer: No — the sound and heat carry away kinetic energy, which is why a sticking collision loses kinetic energy; momentum is carried by the moving masses, and its small measured loss is friction's work, not the sound's.**
