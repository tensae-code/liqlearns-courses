# How this physics course works — the Jordanian Grade 9 way

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The introductory experiment comes first

Every Jordanian unit opens with a تجربة استهلالية — an introductory experiment the learner does before any theory. Unit 1 opens by measuring, Unit 2 by pushing and watching motion, Unit 3 by calculating work with real objects. The experiment is not an illustration of the lesson; it is the lesson's first page, and the theory that follows is the story of what the hands just did.

## Sub lesson 2 — The general idea and the glossed terms

After the experiment comes a general-idea page that states the unit's big idea in plain words, and throughout the book every English physics term is glossed beside the Arabic at its first use — measurement, force, work and power each arrive with their English names attached, so the learner builds the Arabic understanding and the international vocabulary together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Review, unit review and Physics and life

Each lesson ends with review questions, each unit closes with a full unit review, and a الفيزياء والحياة — 'Physics and life' — spread applies the unit to daily life: the centre of mass met by balancing a metre rule, pressure met in the body, lenses met in the eye. The pattern is the course's promise: try it, name it, check it, use it.

## The key ideas

- Every Jordanian unit opens with an introductory experiment done before the theory
- A general-idea page states each unit's big idea in plain words
- English physics terms are glossed beside the Arabic at first use
- Lessons end with review and units close with a unit review
- A 'Physics and life' spread applies each unit to daily life

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion, in your own words: Jordan's Grade 9 physics book runs every unit the same way: an introductory experiment first, a general-idea page, every English physics term glossed in Arabic at first use, lessons ending in review, and the unit closing with a unit review and a 'Physics and life' spread that puts the unit to work in daily life. If your companion can work the Do It Yourself tasks without looking back, the lesson has landed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Open any Jordanian Grade 9 unit and list its parts in order. Which part comes before all theory?
2. Find one English physics term glossed in Arabic in the book and write both names. Why does the book give both?
3. Take the 'Physics and life' spread of one unit and state, in one sentence, which lesson idea it applies and to what.
