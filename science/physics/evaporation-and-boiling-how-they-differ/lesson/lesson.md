# Evaporation and boiling — how they differ

## Do it first
Before you read: work the opening task of the first sub lesson with real objects, real numbers or a real observation — the explanation that follows is the story of what you just did. Keep your result beside you as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Evaporation — escape from the surface, at any temperature

In a liquid the particles' energies spread widely; the fastest at the surface can escape into the air at any temperature. That is evaporation: a surface-only change, faster with heat, wind, a larger surface and drier air. Because the escapees are the highest-energy particles, those left behind average less energy — evaporation cools the liquid, which is why sweat and a porous water jar work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Boiling — bubbles throughout, at one temperature

Heat a liquid and vapour bubbles form inside it; a bubble survives and grows only when the vapour pressure inside it can push back the outside pressure — which happens at exactly one temperature for a given pressure: the boiling point. Then boiling runs throughout the liquid, temperature pinned at the boiling point while the supplied heat becomes latent heat of vaporisation, parting particles instead of warming them.

## Sub lesson 3 — The comparison — Jordan's table in words

Place: surface only against throughout. Temperature: any against fixed at the boiling point. Speed: slow against vigorous. Temperature of the liquid: falls (cooling) against stays constant. Bubbles: none against throughout the liquid. One change, two regimes — and confusing them misreads everyday life, from drying clothes to why a pressure cooker cooks faster (a raised boiling point, not hotter steam magic).

## The key ideas

- Evaporation: surface only, at any temperature, slow, and it cools the liquid
- Boiling: throughout the liquid, only at the boiling point, vigorous, temperature steady
- Evaporation's escapees are the fastest surface particles — their leaving lowers the average energy
- Boiling begins when vapour pressure inside bubbles matches the outside pressure
- Evaporation speeds up with heat, wind, larger surface and drier air
