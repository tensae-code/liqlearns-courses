# Lesson plan — Evaporation and boiling — how they differ

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Physics · **Lesson:** Evaporation and boiling — how they differ
**Place in the curriculum:** Physics (CURRICULUM.md Unit 18), inside the heat strand — Jordan's Grade 11 direct comparison of the two vaporisations
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** evaporation and boiling are both liquid turning to vapour, and Jordan's Grade 11 book sets them side by side because they differ in everything else: evaporation happens only at the surface, at any temperature, slowly, and cools the liquid; boiling happens throughout the liquid, only at the boiling point, vigorously, with the temperature held steady while it lasts

## Sub lessons
1. **Evaporation — escape from the surface, at any temperature** — In a liquid the particles' energies spread widely; the fastest at the surface can escape into the air at any temperature. That is evaporation: a surface-only change, faster with heat, wind, a larger surface and drier air. Because the escapees are the highest-energy particles, those left behind average less energy — evaporation cools the liquid, which is why sweat and a porous water jar work.
2. **Boiling — bubbles throughout, at one temperature** — Heat a liquid and vapour bubbles form inside it; a bubble survives and grows only when the vapour pressure inside it can push back the outside pressure — which happens at exactly one temperature for a given pressure: the boiling point. Then boiling runs throughout the liquid, temperature pinned at the boiling point while the supplied heat becomes latent heat of vaporisation, parting particles instead of warming them.
3. **The comparison — Jordan's table in words** — Place: surface only against throughout. Temperature: any against fixed at the boiling point. Speed: slow against vigorous. Temperature of the liquid: falls (cooling) against stays constant. Bubbles: none against throughout the liquid. One change, two regimes — and confusing them misreads everyday life, from drying clothes to why a pressure cooker cooks faster (a raised boiling point, not hotter steam magic).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Evaporation: surface only, at any temperature, slow, and it cools the liquid
2. Boiling: throughout the liquid, only at the boiling point, vigorous, temperature steady
3. Evaporation's escapees are the fastest surface particles — their leaving lowers the average energy
4. Boiling begins when vapour pressure inside bubbles matches the outside pressure
5. Evaporation speeds up with heat, wind, larger surface and drier air
