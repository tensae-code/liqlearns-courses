# Density — the Beruni and Khazin way

## Do it first
Before you read: a gold ring and a silver ring can look alike and cost very differently. How could you tell which is which without cutting or scratching either? A scholar in Central Asia solved exactly this problem a thousand years ago. Keep the puzzle.

## Sub lesson 1 — Density — mass in each unit of volume

Density prices how tightly matter is packed: ρ = m/V, mass divided by volume, in kilograms per cubic metre or grams per cubic centimetre. Every pure substance has its own density — gold about 19.3 g/cm³, silver about 10.5 g/cm³ — so density is a fingerprint: measure a body's mass and its volume and the number names the material. Mass is easy (a balance). Volume is easy only for regular shapes; a ring, a stone, a crown defeat the ruler.

## Sub lesson 2 — Beruni's overflow vessel — volume by displaced water

Beruni's answer, described in his book on minerals and metals: fill a vessel brim-full through a spout, set the body gently into it, and catch every drop of water that runs out. The overflow's volume is exactly the body's volume — the body pushes aside its own volume of water. Weigh the body, catch and measure the overflow, divide: ρ = m/V. Beruni used it to build density tables for metals and gemstones accurate enough to expose fakes and price stones — the ring puzzle solved without a scratch.

## Sub lesson 3 — Khazin's vacuum weighing — squeezing out the last error

A century later, al-Khazin refined the art in his 'Book of the Balance of Wisdom'. He saw that air itself buoys a body a little — weighing in air slightly understates true mass — so the finest weighings compare the body in air and in a vacuum (or correct for the air). Between Beruni's vessel and Khazin's balance, Central Asian workshops held the best density values in the world; the modern school method — balance plus measuring cylinder of water — is their method, unchanged in idea.

## The key ideas

- Density ρ = m/V is a fingerprint of a pure substance — gold ≈ 19.3 g/cm³, silver ≈ 10.5 g/cm³
- Beruni's overflow vessel gives an irregular body's volume as the volume of water it displaces
- Khazin's refinement: air buoys a body slightly, so the finest weighing corrects for air, or weighs in vacuum
- The school measuring-cylinder method is Beruni's method in modern glass

1. A stone has mass 52 g and pushes 20 cm³ of water out of an overflow vessel. Find its density, and say whether it could be pure gold (19.3 g/cm³).
