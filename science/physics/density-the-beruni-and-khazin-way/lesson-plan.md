## Sub lessons
1. **Density — mass in each unit of volume** — Density prices how tightly matter is packed: ρ = m/V, mass divided by volume, in kilograms per cubic metre or grams per cubic centimetre. Every pure substance has its own density — gold about 19.3 g/cm³, silver about 10.5 g/cm³ — so density is a fingerprint: measure a body's mass and its volume and the number names the material. Mass is easy (a balance). Volume is easy only for regular shapes; a ring, a stone, a crown defeat the ruler.
2. **Beruni's overflow vessel — volume by displaced water** — Beruni's answer, described in his book on minerals and metals: fill a vessel brim-full through a spout, set the body gently into it, and catch every drop of water that runs out. The overflow's volume is exactly the body's volume — the body pushes aside its own volume of water. Weigh the body, catch and measure the overflow, divide: ρ = m/V. Beruni used it to build density tables for metals and gemstones accurate enough to expose fakes and price stones — the ring puzzle solved without a scratch.
3. **Khazin's vacuum weighing — squeezing out the last error** — A century later, al-Khazin refined the art in his 'Book of the Balance of Wisdom'. He saw that air itself buoys a body a little — weighing in air slightly understates true mass — so the finest weighings compare the body in air and in a vacuum (or correct for the air). Between Beruni's vessel and Khazin's balance, Central Asian workshops held the best density values in the world; the modern school method — balance plus measuring cylinder of water — is their method, unchanged in idea.
