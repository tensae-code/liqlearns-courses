# Selling and Customer Care

## Do it first

Think of the last thing you bought from a person, not a machine. Walk through what the seller did: how they greeted you, showed you the goods, answered your doubts, agreed the price and the terms, and how they treated you after you paid. That whole path is selling and customer care. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Selling

**Selling.** Personal (direct) selling is done by a salesperson, face to face with the buyer. Successful salespeople share qualities: empathy, focus, responsibility, persistence, enthusiasm and communication. The personal selling process has five steps: preparation, prospecting (finding likely buyers), presenting the goods, handling rejections and objections, and closing the sale — with follow-up after it. Merchandising techniques — how goods are displayed and presented in the shop — support the selling.

**Selling.** The art of selling (arte de vender) (Mozambique's book.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Terms of sale

**Terms of sale.** Every sale is made on terms, and each term favours buyer or seller differently: cash (paid at once), credit (goods now, payment later), hire purchase (instalments, ownership at the last payment), layaway (the seller holds the goods while the buyer pays), consignment (goods left with a seller who pays only for what is sold), and cash and trade discounts between businesses. A good seller states the terms plainly before the sale, so both sides know what they have agreed.

**Terms-of-sale.** Terms and conditions for selling goods and services (Mozambique's book.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Customer service

**Customer service.** Customer service is caring for the customer's needs before, during and after the purchase. Its marks are personalisation, politeness, prompt response, professionalism and awareness of special needs. It takes forms such as warranties, after-sales service, feedback, online chat, toll-free numbers and call centres, suggestion boxes and surveys. A business gathers information from its customers to improve the service — because excellent service keeps customers and wins new ones by word of mouth.

**The selling process.** The elevator pitch ("discurso del elevador"): planning a pitch of at most about 60 seconds — first define its objective (sell, convince, obtain financing, get a job), then the target market and the benefits and advantages for the listener (Ecuador's book.)

**Customer care.** Customer relationships (part 4): knowing the customer personally is vital — a business that cannot gather its customers' important characteristics cannot win their satisfaction; the first step of customer orientation is identifying the customer's needs and expectations by going to the customer and hearing their views (Iran's book.)

**Selling and customer care.** Customer care as the hinge of failure and revival: businesses fail through poor customer care by owners or employees — customers not handled well go to businesses where they are treated better; and the first revival measure the book names is ensuring good customer care to existing and prospective customers (Uganda's book.)
**Service part.** Providing services as an objective: the services a business offers customers to draw them to buy, including the advantages of after-sales service (Malaysia's book.)

**Customer-service part.** The sales and customer-service functional departments: customer service defined as the organisation's ability to deliver products that meet and exceed what customers expect, continuously and consistently (Malaysia's book.)

**Selling.** Strand 3 Unit 2: the sales process in seven steps — prospecting, preparation, approach, presentation, handling objections, closing, follow-up (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)


**Sales-management part.** Sales management is essential: if sales do not cover all costs and create profit, the business cannot succeed. (Laos's book.)

**Customer-care part.** Customer standards: the prospective customer is the most important person for the business; the customer does not depend on the business — the business depends on the customer; the customer is the business's purpose, not an interruption of it; serving the customer is not a favour. (Laos's book.)

**Content-summary part.** The guide's selling teaching: a seller must be able to solve the problems that block customers from buying, have the art of selling, make customers satisfied, and keep up their selling image wherever they are in the community, because they sell goods and services to the public; communication is of the first importance in selling. (Laos's teacher guide.)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: selling is a process with steps — prepare, find the buyer, present the goods, handle objections, close the sale — done on terms both sides understand, and it is finished only by customer care: looking after the buyer before, during and after the purchase so that they come back. Give one real example from your own home, market or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: selling, terms of sale, customer service. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
