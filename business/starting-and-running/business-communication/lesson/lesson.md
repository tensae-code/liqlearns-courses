# Business Communication

## Do it first

Follow one message through a business you know: a manager's instruction going down to the workers, a worker's report going up, and two departments talking across to each other. Name the medium of each — spoken, written, electronic, gesture. That is this lesson.

## Communication inside the firm

**Communication.** Communication inside the firm: written, oral, electronic and gesture media, and downward, upward and lateral flows between and within departments.

## Communication in the organisation

Internal and external communication: the communication process (sender, channel, receiver, feedback, noise), upward/downward/horizontal and formal/informal channels, verbal/written/visual methods and their means, the barriers to effective communication (language, culture, emotion, overload, perception, wrong channel) and strategies for communicating effectively

**Negotiation part.** Negotiation as an entrepreneurial skill: integrative negotiation where both sides gain (win-win), preparing by assessing the other party's background, and the principles of negotiating and interacting (responsive, courteous, mutual help) (Malaysia's book.)

**Communication part.** The communication process (sender, message formation, delivery, interpretation, reception, feedback, understanding) and the oral and non-oral techniques (telephone, letters and memos, meetings, reports, seminars), with the steps for planning effective communication (Malaysia's book.)

**Interaction part.** How the functional departments relate and interact: the flow of information between departments (sender creates the message, receivers act on it), the flow of goods and services between businesses, orders received by the sales unit, and supplier invoices received and paid by the finance department (Malaysia's book.)

**Communication part.** The concepts of business communication and the principles and skills of effective business communication: effective communication fosters rapport within the organisation and with stakeholders and raises productivity (Bhutan's book.)

**Correspondence part.** Business correspondences: the written instruments of business, including minutes of meeting (the written resolutions agreed on the agendas, documented for compliance and later action) and the quotation (the supplier's correspondence listing specifications, prices, quantities and modes of payment) (Bhutan's book.)

**Negotiation part.** Etiquette in business communication, and negotiation: the concepts of negotiation, the negotiation process, and effective cross-cultural negotiation (patience named as a negotiator's need) (Bhutan's book.)

**Communication.** Storytelling as a business communication tool: carrying information to an audience through a story with real or invented characters (Armstrong's use of it to train newcomers and raise company performance), and its working principles — content first (the information pyramid: statement, argumentation, restatement), research the topic deeply, build the visual part, and work the headline (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Storytelling.** Storytelling: a told story is understood and remembered better than read information; the story's structure (introduction, main part, conclusion); end with a call to action and motivate the listener; storytelling as a teaching method and as a digital-marketing tool balancing message and action (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Communicating in business.** Unit 9.3: the principles and importance of good communication and the barriers to it; business letters (block and semi-block layout, the letter of enquiry), memorandums, reports and meeting minutes; office equipment (fax, computer, photocopier, scanner, telephone); telephone etiquette; organising and conducting meetings (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Application writing.** Unit 10.4: the job application letter in block and semi-block layout (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Communicating in business.** Strand 1 Unit 1: communication defined; the principles of good communication (clear, concise, correct, courteous); the communication process (sender encodes, channel, receiver decodes, feedback); verbal and non-verbal communication; the forms of business communication; internal against external communication; channels (top-down, upward, bottom-up); the barriers to communication (people, language, listening, equipment, defective message) (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Communication technology.** Strand 1 Unit 1: communication technology — its types and their uses in business (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Keeping business information safe.** Strand 1 Unit 1: security and confidentiality of communication technology — confidentiality controls and encryption, and why businesses keep information from competitors (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)


**Skills part.** Listening skills teaching: when nobody listens to us we dislike it; listening is an essential skill, and to become an outstanding businessperson you must learn to listen and listen to others as well as possible; a good talker who is also the best listener; with advice for developing good listening (give full attention to the speaker, as when speaking on the telephone; give as much time to listening as to speaking; real communication cannot run both ways at once). (Laos's teacher guide.)

**Communication-in-leading part.** How effective communication makes the leading process succeed: systematic communication flowing through the organisation structure (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)
