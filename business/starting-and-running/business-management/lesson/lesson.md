# Business Management

## Do it first

Think of one group you belong to — a team, a class, a club. Name who plans what it does, who organises the work, who leads on the day, and who checks it was done well. Those four jobs are the functions of management. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What management is — and why it matters

**Opening.** What management is.

**Importance.** Why management is important to a business.

**Skills.** The skills a manager needs and why each matters.

**Concepts.** Effectiveness and efficiency as the two tests of management functions.

**Time-management.** Time management and the time-management plan: managing one's time as a business resource (the book's two closing lessons taught as one sequence) (Mozambique's book.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Levels of management — and the skills each level needs

**Levels.** The three levels of management.

**Skills-to-levels.** How management skills map onto the management levels.

**Organisation structures.** The three levels of management (board/top, tactical/middle, operational), who sits at each, position titles, and what an organisation chart shows and hides.

## Sub lesson 3 — The functions of management — and the objectives they serve

**Functions.** The management functions one by one (planning, organising, leading/directing and controlling), including work-procedure manuals as the organising tool.

**Management.** The objectives a business pursues (survival, profit, growth, market share, innovation, social responsibility), how objectives conflict, and management by objectives as the reconciliation.

**Functions and principles.** The functions of management: planning, coordination and organisation, motivation and control; administration as one part of management.

**Functions of management.** Management defined, and its functions worked through one by one: planning (with SMART goals), organising, directing, controlling, coordinating, delegating, motivating and staffing; plus the personal qualities a manager needs

**Business growth.** Business growth: internal growth (more machinery, premises, outlets, labour, financed from savings/profits/loans/shares) and external growth/integration — joint ventures, mergers, takeovers/acquisitions, and horizontal, vertical and lateral integration, each with examples; the benefits of growth including economies of scale

## Sub lesson 4 — Joining the functions together

**Coordination.** How the business functions are coordinated with one another.

**Linking.** How the management functions connect with the business functions.

**Organisation-structure.** Creating an organisation structure for a business.

**Organisation-chart.** Building an organisation chart for a chosen form of business ownership.

## Sub lesson 5 — Organisation structures — how the business is arranged

**Organisation structures.** What an organisation is and bureaucracy in its technical sense: hierarchy, specialisation, appointment on competence, written rules and records.

**Organisation structures.** Centralisation versus decentralisation, run through cost centres and profit centres, with the gains and costs of spreading authority.

**Leadership and decision-making.** Delegation (authority passes down, responsibility stays), kinds of authority (traditional, charismatic, positional), the five sources of power, and responsibility.

**Organisation structures.** Span of control: what it is, the case for and against a wide span, and the factors that fix its size.

**Organisation structures.** Organisation levels: flat versus tall structures and what each does to communication and control.

**Organisation structures.** Departmentalisation and structure types — functional, product-based and geographically-based — read off organisation charts (Nigeria's own way: worked charts for a manufacturing company).

**Organisation structures.** Line, staff and functional authority, the scalar chain and unity of command.

**Organisation structures.** What the major departments of a company actually do: production, marketing, finance, human resources and administration, corporate planning and management services, research and development, and the company secretary.

**Organisation structures.** Management structures: centralised control in sole traders and partnerships (flat structure, owner/partners decide everything) versus decentralised, delegated control in companies (shareholders → directors → general manager → managers/supervisors), with specialisation as the gain and loss of owner control as the cost.

**Functional areas.** The functional areas of a business and what each does: production, marketing, finance and accounts, personnel/human resources, research and development, and information technology

**Stakeholders.** Stakeholders: internal (owners/shareholders, board of directors, managers, employees) and external (government, customers, suppliers, lending institutions, competitors, the community), with the role each group plays and why their interests can conflict

**Management responsibilities.** Management's responsibilities to each stakeholder group: to owners (profit, efficient resource use, accurate reports, legality), employees (fair pay, safe conditions, training, union rights), government (taxes, statutory deductions, compliance), customers (quality, after-sales, information) and the wider community (environment, a CSR plan)

**Organisational structure.** Organisational structure and charts: hierarchy, chain of command and span of control; line, staff and functional relationships; charts for small and for growing businesses; tall vs flat structures and the factors that affect span of control (manager/employee competence, task complexity)

**Leadership.** Leadership: what makes a good leader (honesty, integrity, competence, inspiration, charismatic and transformational qualities) and the three leadership styles — autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire — each with characteristics, advantages and disadvantages and when to use it

**Managing people and work.** What a team is: a limited number of people with complementary skills, formed for a common goal with mutual responsibility to one another; the stages of forming and developing a team, and the key points of teamwork — mutual communication, at least two people, a shared goal (Iran's book.)

**Managing people and work.** Barriers to effective teamwork — distrust between members, no commitment to plans or to responsibilities, lack of proper relations, inattention to the team's goals — and that strengthening teamwork means identifying these barriers and removing them, since together they make teams fail (Iran's book.)

**Managing people and work.** Factors in a team's success: setting shared goals that are clear, understandable and achievable; a team guide who steers the team and resolves its conflicts; choosing capable people with commitment, expertise, skill and responsibility; mutual trust so members share ideas freely; and hard work and perseverance (Iran's book.)

**Leading a team.** The team guide (leader): the qualities a team's guide needs — foresight about the team's results, high scientific and skill competence, strong communication and interaction, high moral standing that builds trust, and the authority to stop the team falling apart; the synergy idea that a team achieves more than the sum of its members (2 + 2 > 4) (Iran's book.)

**Deciding and solving.** Problem solving starts with defining the problem correctly: a problem exists when a gap opens between the existing state and the desired state; examine the existing state and focus on the real problem, not its symptoms, because a wrongly defined problem ruins the whole solution process (Iran's book.)

**Deciding and solving.** The Six Thinking Hats method for team problem solving: six coloured roles — blue organises the team's thinking, white stays neutral and gathers information, red speaks feelings, and the remaining hats cover criticism, optimism and new ideas in turn (Iran's book.)

**Deciding and solving.** Decision making: a process through which a definite solution is chosen for a problem; important life decisions with long-term effects (field of study, career path) must be made by the rational decision-making method, thinking things through (Iran's book.)

**Deciding and solving.** Goal setting: goals are the most important factor in moving and creating the future; the features of effective goals — specific and clear (answering what, when, where, by whom), measurable, achievable within one's limits (Iran's book.)

**Deciding and solving.** The "Five Whys" technique: asking why five times in a chain to root-cause a problem and reach its main causes (Iran's book.)

**Running a business.** Functions of business: the production function, financing function, marketing function, organising function, employment function and research-and-development function (identifying consumer tastes, finding market demand, studying new technology, watching competitors' strategies) (Nepal's book.)

**Running an organisation.** Company meetings: the shareholders' annual general meeting, the special/extraordinary general meeting (called for urgent matters such as mergers, bonus shares, buy-back of shares, or converting private to public), and meetings of the board of directors (approving financial statements, recommending dividends, plans and delegation of authority, with dissent recorded in the minutes) (Nepal's book.)

**Running an organisation.** Indexing: arranging a guide so files and documents can be found fast; its objectives (saving staff time, preventing repeated work from lost documents, keeping old records available for memory and reference), and its types — book index, loose-leaf index, card index (including album, visible and rotary card indexes) and the electronic index searched by name, title, date or code (Nepal's book.)

**Running an organisation.** Filing: keeping letters and records arranged so they can be used easily when needed as reference; its objectives, and its types — traditional filing (wire filing, pigeonhole filing, baggage/bundle filing, misil filing, box filing) against modern filing (open-shelf, horizontal, vertical, suspension and lateral filing, and electronic filing) (Nepal's book.)

**Running an organisation.** Information management: the information cycle — collection, classification, storage (guarded against theft, loss and unauthorised use), distribution and protection — and the principles of information management: accuracy, relevancy, timeliness, completeness, accessibility, security, efficiency, uniformity, usability and legal compliance (Nepal's book.)

**Running an organisation.** Record management: records as the formal information an institution will need again (correspondence, registers, vouchers, files, HR, accounts and legal documents), and the importance of managing their creation, keeping and destruction — protection of documents, easy access to information, help in decision making, fulfilling legal compliance, preservation of institutional memory, accountability and transparency, and coordination and communication (Nepal's book.)

**Running an organisation.** The importance of the office and office system development: the office as information centre, evidence of existence, medium of communication, centre for planning and policy, and the place of coordination of work, direction and control, memory and service; developing the office's own work system so work becomes quick, uniform and effective (Nepal's book.)

**Running an organisation.** The office and its functions: the traditional view (a place) against the modern view (a process that also implements the plans and policies it makes); office functions split into regular/basic functions — information acquisition and collection, recording of information (inward and outward/dispatch books), analysis of information, storage and distribution — and executive/managerial functions, including the human-resource, purchase-and-control, public-relation and property-protection functions (Nepal's book.)

**Organising the business.** Organising the venture, alone or in a group: one person alone must assume every responsibility, while a group works best with trusted partners or family with the right capacities; (Nicaragua's book.)

**Organising the business.** Organisation as the base of the venture: without organisation optimal results are impossible; its pillar is a well-structured business plan that defines the productive process and who is assigned to each process, because the plan is how decisions are anticipated — and what is only in your head does not count until it is documented in writing (Nicaragua's book.)

**Managing the business.** Two principles of administering the venture: order and discipline — each person in their post is responsible for doing their function as well as possible and guiding their team; and unity of direction — every member works from the same mission and vision, supporting and respecting each other so the venture grows (Nicaragua's book.)

**Managing / deciding.** Entrepreneurial decision-making: decision-making is the whole process — identifying the need/problem, gathering relevant data, setting out the alternatives, evaluating them, choosing one and following it up; (Palestine's book.)

**Managing / deciding.** What sound entrepreneurial decision-making requires, and why decisions go wrong: (Palestine's book.)

**Managing / deciding together.** Brainstorming as the group's decision method: involving the work team in decision-making generates more and better solution options and wins the team's commitment to the decision they helped make; (Palestine's book.)
**Business management.** Industrial work culture — the 5R/5S method: Ringkas, Rapi, Resik, Rawat, Rajin (sort, set in order, shine, standardise, sustain) as a simple method for arranging and cleaning the workplace that raises efficiency, applied step by step in the accounting office (Indonesia's book.)

**Direction-setting part.** Vision, mission and objectives defined as the core of strategic planning that sets an organisation's direction, with objectives written the SMART way (specific, measurable and the rest) (Malaysia's book.)

**SWOT part.** SWOT analysis applied to the prospective entrepreneur himself: own strengths and weaknesses against external opportunities and threats, and building TOWS strategies for self-development from the four combinations (Malaysia's book.)

**Self-development part.** The prospective entrepreneur's self-development plan built from the SWOT/TOWS analysis: developing mind and emotions, character, and technical skills (Malaysia's book.)

**Skills part.** The basic entrepreneurial skills introduced, starting with thinking skills and their techniques (brainstorming, forced analogy and staged idea generation) (Malaysia's book.)

**Decision-making part.** The decision-making and problem-solving process step by step: set specific goals, identify the problem, form and evaluate alternatives, choose and act (Malaysia's book.)

**Time-management part.** Time management for the entrepreneur: identify the important things, divide time by task priority, plan a work schedule, note things in a diary and evaluate continuously (Malaysia's book.)

**Networking part.** Networking in business: what a network is, its benefits (confidence before investing, cutting bureaucracy, sharing knowledge, improving image) and how to keep a network alive (Malaysia's book.)

**Leadership part.** Leadership as influencing a group toward the organisation's goals, and the entrepreneur's leadership styles — autocratic (all authority centred on the manager) against democratic (authority shared, subordinates join decisions) (Malaysia's book.)

**Strategy part.** Entrepreneurial strategy: what a strategy is (an action plan to reach profit goals), its types including market-penetration and retention strategies, and its importance — reducing failure risk, setting the business's direction and mobilising resources effectively (Malaysia's book.)

**Life-cycle part.** The Business Life Cycle (Kitaran Hayat Perniagaan) in five phases — idea generation (seed), start-up, growth, maturity and establishment — and matching the right strategy to each phase (Malaysia's book.)

**Change-factors part.** Analysing the factors that bring change: management styles read through the managerial grid up to the democratic (9,9) style, and human-resource change through recruiting from internal and external sources with their costs and benefits (Malaysia's book.)

**SWOT part.** Using the SWOT matrix: matching internal strengths and weaknesses against external opportunities and threats (with PESTEL named as the external analysis) to turn outside opportunities into the business's advantage (Malaysia's book.)

**Meanings part.** Vision, mission and objectives: what each means, and how their statements differ between a profit organisation, a non-profit organisation (OBBU) and a voluntary association (Malaysia's book.)

**Objectives part.** The characteristics of a business's objectives: short-term against long-term objectives, and objectives written SMART (specific first), with the Gantt chart as the planning tool that sets activities against time (Malaysia's book.)

**Purposes part.** Why vision, mission and objectives are set: the vision as the long-term (over five years) goal inside strategic management, the mission formed after the vision to direct current activity, objectives tied to SMART (Malaysia's book.)

**Change-factors part.** The factors that force vision, mission and objectives to change: internal factors (the finance function, quality control) and external factors (the economic cycle, socioculture, technology) (Malaysia's book.)

**Achievement part.** Vision, mission and objectives achieved in real chosen businesses: worked case readings (a national motorcycle maker raising market share from 10% to 15%, a food multinational's objectives, a bank's vision) (Malaysia's book.)

**Departments part.** The functional departments of a business organisation: what a functional department is, the management levels inside it, and the organisation chart (chief executive over marketing, production and finance managers over supervisors) (Malaysia's book.)

**Small-vs-large part.** The same functional departments interacting in a small business against a large one: in the small business the sales department deals with customers directly and departments combine functions (Malaysia's book.)

**Stakeholders part.** Managing stakeholders: who the stakeholders are, intermediaries (distribution firms, marketing agencies, resellers, financial intermediaries) among them, and the roles each stakeholder plays (Bhutan's book.)

**Management part.** The concept of management and its importance: achievement of goals, optimum utilisation of resources, optimising cost, segregation of roles and responsibilities, meeting market demands and serving stakeholders' expectations (Bhutan's book.)

**Org plan part.** The organisational and management plan as a business-plan component: it describes the legal form of the business and depicts the organisational structure (Bhutan's book.)

**Support services.** Consulting as a service to business: what consulting is (advising managers on financial, commercial, legal, technical questions so the management system reaches its goals), its specialisations, and the three stages by which Kazakhstan's own consulting market formed after privatisation (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Analysis.** Analysis and synthesis as the manager's paired method: analysis breaks the object into parts and improves each, synthesis agrees the parts' characteristics and rejoins them into a whole; applied as business analysis — a standing system for finding the business's problems by comparing key stakeholders' requirements with the firm's actual economic, social and ecological performance (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Management theory.** Strand 2 Unit 1: Scientific Management Theory (Taylor, 1911) — its principles (replace rule of thumb with science, match workers to jobs and train them, monitor performance, divide the work between managers and workers), its strengths, weaknesses and limitations, and Henry Ford's application (division of labour, specialisation, pay as motivation, the conveyor belt) and its bad side (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Levels of management.** Strand 2 Unit 3: people management — the management functions and the three levels of management (low/front-line, middle, top) and their roles (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Goal setting.** Strand 4 Unit 1: SMART goals — setting goals that are specific, measurable, achievable, realistic and time-bound (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Running the company.** Strand 1: the board of directors and the corporate officers, and the role of each (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Officers' duties.** Strand 1: officers' personal liability — the company as an independent entity, limited liability and its limits (lying to government, stealing, embezzlement), and fiduciary duties (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Growing the company.** Strand 1: corporate expansion — conglomerates (Steamships and WR Carpenters as PNG examples, with advantages and disadvantages), mergers (the Air Niugini/Airlines PNG aborted merger), consolidation, and the four integration types (horizontal, vertical, conglomerate, lateral) (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Takeovers.** Strand 1: stock acquisition against asset acquisition, with BSP's takeovers as the example (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Layers of planning.** Strand 4: supporting the strategic plan with tactical and operational plans — how the layers of strategy fit; tactical planning (middle managers) and operational planning (the 'how'); operational risks (fraud, lawsuits, environmental and fire risks from failed systems, processes and people) (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**SWOT.** FOFA (SWOT) analysis: strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats as the Mozambican-named SWOT (Mozambique's book.)


**Business management.** How each enterprise form is organised and managed under the law: the multi-member LLC is run by its Members' Council (the highest decision body, all members or their authorised representatives), its chair and its Director or General Director who runs daily business (a state LLC must also have a Supervisory Board); the one-member LLC's owner holds the highest decision power, and an organisation-owned one-member LLC works under one of two models (company president + director, or Members' Council of 3–7 owner-appointed members + director); the joint-stock company works under one of two models built on the General Meeting of Shareholders (highest body: sets development orientation, share types, dividends, elects the Board and supervisors, amends the charter), the Board of Directors (manages the company, decides strategy, the medium-term and annual business plans, share sales and capital raising, appoints and pays the Director) and the Director who runs daily business; the partnership is run by a Members' Council of all the general partners, who are the company's legal representatives and organise its daily business. (Vietnam's book.)


**Manager-qualities part.** Qualities for managing people: adaptability, flexibility, intelligence, readiness, human-relations skill, sensitivity to others, leadership, helpfulness, listening and teachability; appraise employees on their performance, not personal feelings, and consider consequences before deciding. (Laos's book.)

**Time-management part.** Time and resource management: every businessperson must be a good manager of time (computers and mobile phones as aids); techniques — self-motivation, setting deadlines, asking what/where/when/who/why before starting work, choosing the most beneficial activities, daily specific goals, a detailed plan for tomorrow, keeping records, not trying to do everything yourself (delegation), and reviewing a weekly timetable to improve use of time. (Laos's book.)

**Information-sources part.** Sources of information and help, by type: marketing information; technical information (product/service design, tools and equipment, materials, technology choices, site advice); financial information (funding sources, economic conditions and trends, financial management and control, accounting systems large and small, audit requirements, taxes and duties, credit and savings cooperatives, equipment-leasing companies); legal information (law firms, state organisations, libraries, the internet, business-development services, business and workers' associations) — and how to use them (join associations, read their journals, attend meetings, subscribe to trade publications, interview customers, suppliers, competitors and former employees). (Laos's book.)

**Leadership part.** Authority and responsibility: the businessperson as a leader who builds a team. (Laos's book.)

**Worked-example part.** Time-use teaching with a worked example: building a timetable of your activities shows which activities are necessary and which are not; worked example — a secretary's day timetable (date, time, goal, activity, result: travel to work 7:00, prepare meeting documents 7:30, attend the meeting 8:00…). (Laos's teacher guide.)

**Definition part.** What management is: the classic definitions (Follett, Stoner & Freeman, Kreitner) built into one working definition — planning, organising, leading and controlling resources efficiently and effectively in a dynamic business environment — and why management is necessary (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Managerial-roles part.** The roles of a manager (interpersonal, informational and decisional roles and the functions inside them) (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Levels-and-skills part.** The three management levels (top, middle, first-line) — what each level decides and supervises — and the management skills each level needs (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Process part.** The management process: the four basic functions — planning, organising, leading, controlling — examined as one process (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Decision-making part.** Formal decision making and problem solving: identifying and analysing the problem, forming alternatives, evaluating them (acceptability, affordability, practicability, legality, morality), selecting the best, planning its implementation, evaluation and feedback (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Planning part.** Planning to achieve goals: definitions of planning, the planning process (aims, objectives and strategies, environment analysis incl. SWOT, resource analysis, performance gap, strategic decisions, implementation, measuring success) (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Organising part.** Organising: allocating work, authority and resources; the steps of organising (recognising tasks, division of work, departmentalisation, delegation, work standards, resource allocation, co-ordination) and job specialisation (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Organising-concepts part.** The concepts behind successful organising: chain of command, unity of command, span of control (narrow/wide), authority and the sources of power (legitimate, expert, reward, coercive, charismatic) (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Leadership part.** Leadership: the characteristics of leaders and the bases/styles of leadership for directing the people who implement plans (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Motivation part.** Motivation as part of leading: financial and non-financial methods of motivating employees, with examples of each (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Controlling part.** Controlling: comparing actual performance with what was expected, and the types of control (feedforward, concurrent, feedback) that confirm whether goals are achieved (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Introduction part.** Management introduced: what management is (Sri Lanka's book.)

**Functions part.** The functions of management (Sri Lanka's book.)
