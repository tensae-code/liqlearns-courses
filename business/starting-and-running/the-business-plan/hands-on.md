# Hands-on — The Business Plan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take the business you imagined in Starting a Business — or invent one now. Write five headings on a page: The business. The market. The people. The making. The money. Put one true sentence under each. You have just drafted the skeleton of a business plan. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a business plan is the written plan of the whole business — its purpose, market, organisation, production, marketing and financial plans — prepared before starting so the entrepreneur, and the financiers, suppliers and customers who read it, can judge whether the business can work; a feasibility study tests the idea first. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
