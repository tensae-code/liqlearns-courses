# The Business Plan

## Do it first

Take the business you imagined in Starting a Business — or invent one now. Write five headings on a page: The business. The market. The people. The making. The money. Put one true sentence under each. You have just drafted the skeleton of a business plan. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a business plan is — and why it matters

**Opening.** What a business plan (Rancangan Perniagaan) is.

**Importance.** Why preparing a business plan matters.

**Opening.** What a business plan is.

**Purposes.** The purposes and importance of preparing a business plan, for the entrepreneur and for financiers, suppliers and customers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Feasibility first — can this idea work?

**The feasibility-study.** Sets a group task to prepare an economic feasibility study (دراسة جدوى اقتصادية) for a small production project: pupils design and produce useful fretwork models for it — the book's only feasibility-study teaching, carried as project work at the end of the fretwork lesson.

## Sub lesson 3 — The parts of the plan

**Format.** The format of a business plan: its sections from introduction through the organisation, production, marketing and financial plans.

**Components.** The components of a business plan: executive summary, background, market analysis, organisation plan, production/operations plan, marketing plan, financial plan and appendix.

**Financial-plan.** The financial plan component in detail: project cost, sources of financing, the cash-budget statement and the financial statements.

**Feasibility study and market research.** The feasibility study before starting: market research from primary and secondary sources, qualitative vs quantitative research, and how the study's findings (viability, costs, sources of finance) feed into the business plan; planning over short, medium and long-term goals

## Sub lesson 4 — Worked plans — the plan made whole

**Worked-example.** Preparing one complete business plan, worked through section by section including its financial plan.

**Worked-models.** Preparing a complete business plan: full worked model plans for different types of business, each following the book's format.

**Writing a business plan.** The full SBEC business-plan outline a village-store couple work through: statement of purpose (operating guide or financing proposal), the business, the market, competition (name the five nearest), location, management, personnel, and the financial data (sources and application of funding, capital equipment, projected position statements, income statements and cash-flow projections for three years).

**The business plan.** The business plan: reasons for preparing one (test feasibility, attract investors, raise finance, guide decisions, ensure legal compliance) and its six elements — Introduction, Executive Summary, the Business Opportunity, Operational Plan, Marketing Plan and Financial Forecast — each section's contents set out, including the financial forecast's projected profit and loss, cash-flow projections, break-even analysis and return on investment

**Opening / what a business plan is.** An investment project (proyecto de inversión) defined as the document that plans the future venture — market research, how it will work, marketing plan, financial projections and their evaluation — and its benefits: it describes the venture, fixes the resources needed, shows the risks, raises the chance of success and is usually required to obtain financing (Ecuador's book.)

**Structure of the plan.** The contents of an investment project: executive summary, description of the venture, operational plan (organisation structure, staff functions, production/service processes, technology, investment, costs), communication plan, financial plan (projected income and outgoings, contribution margin, break-even point) and project evaluation (Ecuador's book.)

**Writing the plan.** Describing the chosen venture inside the project: statement of the problem it solves, the activity and type of venture, its general objective, its beneficiaries and the needs it satisfies (Ecuador's book.)

**Writing the plan.** The vision: the future position the venture wants, stated as realistic, reachable goals with an inspiring character — built by answering what we want to achieve, where we want to be (the book asks the entrepreneur to picture the venture in 10 or 15 years), how to make sure of getting there, how long it will take and who else we want to reach (Nicaragua's book.)

**Writing the plan.** The mission: every business needs one so its general objectives and working principles are clear; in the plan it is stated for the short term from the questions — what do we do, what is our reason for being, who is our target public, what is our geographic field, what is our competitive advantage and what differentiates us from competitors (Nicaragua's book.)

**Writing the plan.** From objectives to a plan of activities: the actions to carry out the venture are fixed in an activity plan, which is what makes the schedule (cronograma), the budget and the investment calendar possible (Nicaragua's book.)

**Writing the plan.** Objectives inside the plan: an objective states precisely the future situation to be reached, with time and quantity parameters, and must be observable and measurable; every venture needs one general objective (what the project is for) and specific objectives (the simpler, clearer activities that lead to it) (Nicaragua's book.)

**Writing the plan.** Writing the plan, part 1: the plan opens with the business's title — its name and general goal stated briefly, with its scale (a language school for 1,000 learners a year; mushroom growing at 100 tons a year) — and the names of the plan's writers (Iran's book.)

**The plan on one page.** The Business Model Canvas (TIC page): the venture mapped in nine blocks — target market, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue flows, key resources, key activities, key partners, cost structure (Ecuador's book.)

**The plan on one page.** The business model as one map (part 1 opening): the model's parts — customer identification, value proposition, distribution channels, customer relationships, revenue, resources, key activities, partners, costs — each answering its own question about the business (Iran's book.)

**Closing the plan.** Conclusions of the project: after the quantitative and qualitative evaluations and the risk analysis, the conclusions state whether implementation is feasible or not, beginning with the satisfaction of needs (Ecuador's book.)

**What a business plan is.** The business plan: everyone entering business must have one — a written, organised document stating how a business's activities will be carried out over a set period; a map that answers three questions: where are you, where do you want to go, and how will you reach the destination (Iran's book.)

**What a business plan is.** What a business plan is: the fundamental piece that makes a firm work well; before writing or updating it, analyse the venture's present situation with tools the book names — the FODA (SWOT) matrix, PESTEL analysis, the Canvas canvas, Design Thinking and Lean Startup; the plan describes the business idea, the objectives to reach and the strategies to reach them (Nicaragua's book.)

**Purpose of the plan.** What the plan is for: it lets the entrepreneur examine the idea in detail — its technical, economic and financial viability — sets out the procedures and strategies to turn the idea into a real business, reduces risks, anticipates problems, gives better control, and shows potential investors the idea is serious and well supported so financing can be obtained (Nicaragua's book.)

**Writing the plan / using the money.** The investment plan: it details how the financing will be used — what the money from a credit will be invested in — listing everything the venture needs to start up: equipment and tools, furniture, raw material, infrastructure and work clothing (Nicaragua's book.)

**Writing the plan / goals.** Entrepreneurial goals: a goal is what the entrepreneur seeks to achieve through his project; setting goals is a core leadership task, the cornerstone for measuring management's effectiveness, the first step of sound planning (each goal needs its own detailed execution plan), a motivator that directs workers' efforts, and a standard for follow-up, control and evaluation; (Palestine's book.)

**Writing the plan / goals.** The SMART characteristics of an entrepreneurial goal, in the book's own five: (Palestine's book.)

**The plan / judging the idea first.** The feasibility study and cost–benefit thinking: the investor seeks to maximise his wealth, which happens only when a project's return exceeds its cost — economists' cost–benefit analysis; (Palestine's book.)

**The plan / judging the idea first.** The stages of the investment decision, and preliminary against detailed feasibility: opportunity exists → the idea crystallises → preliminary (initial) feasibility study → detailed feasibility study → evaluation of the investment project → the investment decision → start of implementation; (Palestine's book.)

**The plan / the studies around the project.** The legal and administrative studies that complete the feasibility study: (Palestine's book.)
**Project-plan.** Planning the chosen project: the plan states the project description answering "what are we going to do?" (the example: produce regional artisan breads to sell to the school's neighbours and on market days), the goals answering "what do we want to achieve?", what the team needs to learn and how it will learn it, and the operative plan — a table of activities by stage with dates, the person responsible, the resources and the product of each — all gathered in the team's project portfolio (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Picturing-the-business.** Visioning the chosen business: sketch the one selected idea in detail before planning it — the field of the business (exactly what kind: clothing manufacture, vehicle repair, food production…), the style of the business (home-based, opening a shop, a training centre, selling by internet), the product or service it will produce (down to which parts of it), and its clients described by their characteristics (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Worked-model part.** One complete worked project paper profiling a real small sole-trader business (Rimbun Sejadi Enterprise): its vision, mission and objectives, business profile, organisation chart and functional departments written out in the set format (Malaysia's book.)

**Plan and users part.** What a business plan is and who uses it: the entrepreneurs themselves are the foremost users (pitching their ideas to win support from stakeholders), and market analysis inside the plan shows the gap between existing supply and demand on which projected sales rest (Bhutan's book.)

**Projections part.** The projected statements inside the financial plan: templates for the projected statement of financial position and the projected statement of cash flows with operating, investing and financing activities segregated year by year (Bhutan's book.)

**Pitching part.** Business plan pitching: developing a comprehensive plan matters, but the entrepreneur's ability to pitch it matters even more for garnering support; the pitch deck and using the competitive analysis inside the pitch to show the business understands its competition (Bhutan's book.)

## Say it to your companion
