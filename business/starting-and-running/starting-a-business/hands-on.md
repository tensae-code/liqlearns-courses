# Hands-on — Starting a Business

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Choose a business you might one day start. Write down: where you would put it, and why there; two reasons you want to start it; and three things you must check before you make or buy anything — who will buy, where your materials come from, and where the money comes from. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: starting well means choosing where to locate, weighing the reasons and the factors before starting, and investigating the market, supplies, finances and process before producing anything — groundwork first, because businesses fail quickly without it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
