# Starting a Business

## Do it first

Choose a business you might one day start. Write down: where you would put it, and why there; two reasons you want to start it; and three things you must check before you make or buy anything — who will buy, where your materials come from, and where the money comes from. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing where to locate

**Location.** Choosing where to locate a firm: availability of raw materials (heavy, bulky or perishable inputs pull the firm to their source), nearness to the market (bulky, perishable or fragile products), availability of labour, transport and communication network, power and water supply, government policy and incentives, plus auxiliary services, room to expand, security, terrain and climate.

**Location.** Localisation (similar firms concentrated in one area: its causes, advantages such as a labour pool, support services and shared infrastructure, and disadvantages such as pollution, regional imbalance and congestion) versus delocalisation (spreading firms across the country, often government-encouraged: its advantages and disadvantages).

**Business location.** Choosing a business location: the factors weighed — geographical setting and market pull, availability of raw materials/supplies, roads/ports and infrastructure, power and water, telecommunications, supply of suitable labour, access to health facilities, and government assistance (grants/tax incentives) vs planning restrictions

## Sub lesson 2 — Why people start — and what to weigh first

**Push-factors.** The factors that push a person to start a business.

**Factors.** The factors to consider before starting a business, each explained.

**Reasons for starting a business.** Why people start businesses: to generate their own income / escape unemployment, self-fulfilment, using their skills and talents, increasing their income, and taking control of their working life

## Sub lesson 3 — The start-up investigation — and where help is

**The start-up investigation.** The pre-production investigation checklist: the market (want and price), supplies/suppliers (reliability, backup suppliers, terms, quantities), distribution, finances (paying for materials, bank transfer and lending services, insurance in transit), the production process (factor mix, quality) and storage (perishability, cost, fashion risk); freight inwards and outwards costed carefully.

**Starting a business; sources of help.** Why small businesses fail quickly without groundwork, and where a Samoan starter gets help: the Small Business Enterprise Centre (SBEC) and Women in Small Business advise and run workshops for prospective owners.

**Steps to start a business.** The essential steps in establishing a business, in order: conceptualisation of the idea, research (market probe), identification of resources, preparing the business plan, acquisition of funds, and operation of the business

**What a new business needs.** Resource sourcing (part 6): every business needs a set of key resources to create its value proposition, reach the market, keep customer relationships and earn revenue; resources are classed as physical (buildings, vehicles, equipment), human, financial and social (networks, cooperation, trust) (Iran's book.)

**What a new business needs.** Resource mobilization defined: the process by which a business mobilises the financial, physical, human and information resources it needs to reach its goals; these resources are the backbone of the business, and people come first — a plan needs planners, managers, production experts, staff and workers (Nepal's book.)

**Setting up.** Partner sourcing (part 8): partnerships are the cornerstone of many businesses; owners take partners for three motives — optimising the business, reducing risk in an uncertain competitive environment, and acquiring resources (Iran's book.)

**Starting / help available.** Nicaragua's own support platforms for ventures at every stage: (Nicaragua's book.)

**Starting / help available.** Incubator services and Palestine's admission criteria: (Palestine's book.)

**Starting / help available.** The three kinds of incubator: general incubators develop all kinds of business project without a set specialisation, concentrating on renewal and innovation; specialised incubators restructure a region's economy by attracting a particular kind of investment or industry, or creating jobs for wanted specialisations or groups of job-seekers; (Palestine's book.)

**Starting / help available.** Business incubators: institutions with their own legal entity (state, private or mixed) that give small investors starting small projects — especially creative ones — a bundle of services and facilities (expertise, place, financing) to carry them over the burdens and risks of the launch and establishment stage, and may market the projects' products; (Palestine's book.)

**Starting a business.** Factors that enable an individual to start up a business: personal interest in the business; technical skills for the business; government support in tax incentives and subsidies; presence of a market for the product; inspiration from successful business role models; a high degree of creativity and innovation; presence of raw materials/inputs; (Uganda's book.)

**Starting a business.** The ten steps in starting a business: 1) identify potential business opportunities by scanning the environment; 2) select a suitable business opportunity; 3) carry out a market survey for it — checking it can become a profitable business with competitive advantage; (Uganda's book.)
**Starting-a-small-project-with-a-partner.** The book's only project brief, "Invent your project" (ابتكر مشروعك), closing the cheese lesson: pupils set up a cheese-making project together with a classmate as a partner in the project (Egypt's book.)

**Registering-the-business.** Declaring the start of business: a partnership files its start-of-activity declaration at the tax office within 30 days of its lawful formation, bringing the published articles, the lease or title of the business premises, the members' insurance certificates, their tax-clearance papers, the chamber-of-commerce registration and the start-of-trade stamp duty, on forms M3 (the legal person), M7 (the manager's relations) and M8 (the members); the tax office then issues the company's tax number (Α.Φ.Μ.), used in all its transactions (Greece's book.)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: starting well means choosing where to locate, weighing the reasons and the factors before starting, and investigating the market, supplies, finances and process before producing anything — groundwork first, because businesses fail quickly without it. Give one real example from your own home, market or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: location, location, push-factors, factors. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
