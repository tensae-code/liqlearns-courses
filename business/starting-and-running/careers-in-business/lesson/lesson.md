# Careers in Business

## Do it first

Name one person you know and list the different work they have done over their life. One item on the list was a job; the whole list together is their career. Then ask: what did they get better at by staying with one kind of work — and what did staying cost them? That is this lesson.

## Job and career

A job is a single post — the work you hold at one place and time. A career is longer: the whole path of work a person builds over a working life, post by post, skill by skill. The two words are not the same: a person can change jobs several times inside one career, and can also change careers. Seeing your work as a career — a path, not a single post — is what lets you plan it: each job becomes a step chosen for where the path is going.

## Specialising in an occupation

Most careers are built by specialising: settling into one occupation and going deep in it. Specialisation brings real benefits — expertise (you become genuinely good), security (experts are harder to replace) and earnings (deep skill is paid more). It also has drawbacks: the work can grow monotonous, and when a specialism becomes obsolete — when the world stops needing it — the specialist finds change harder than the generalist does. A wise career holds the depth of a specialism and keeps some breadth beside it.

## Career options in business

Business study opens a wide range of career options: the book surveys the paths business opens — working in the functional areas of firms (production, marketing, finance and accounts, human resources), in banking and insurance, in selling and customer care, in managing and running businesses, and in founding a business of your own as an entrepreneur. The point of the survey is choice: the same business knowledge stands behind all of them, and a learner can aim at the path that fits their strengths.

**Knowing yourself for work.** Self-knowledge through personality types: the four-type model — dominant, influential, stable, conscientious — each with its own traits, abilities and skills, learned so the student can choose a fitting study and career path (Iran's book.)

**Planning your own career.** The personal development plan: a continuous process that assesses a person's educational and career needs and plans to meet them — stating what you want to learn and what success you aim for, and beginning by assessing your current state and strengths (Iran's book.)

**Planning your own career.** Identifying skills, talents and interests: skills are the building blocks of your future job; a career-readiness plan sorts them into basic skills (reading, writing, speaking, listening, calculating), thinking skills (creative thinking, problem solving/decision making), interpersonal skills (negotiation, serving customers, effective communication) and information-and-technology skills, each prioritised (Iran's book.)

**Planning your own career.** Identifying your values and needs as the second step of career design: a values framework in which each value (creativity, independence, and others) is defined and prioritised from 1 to 5 (Iran's book.)
**Careers in business.** The accounting profession and its jobs, Indonesia's own ladder: what a profession is, the accounting professions and their professional associations and titles (CA, CPA, CAT and the technician certifications), the national qualification levels (operator, technician/analyst, expert), and the job openings for accounting graduates in private firms, tax, village-owned enterprises (BUMDes) and… (Indonesia's book.)

**Careers in business.** The guide's own framing of the profession chapter: the phase-E outcome is that learners can explain the SMK graduate's profession as Junior Accounting Technician at KKNI Level 2 rising to Young Accounting Technician at Level 4, and be inspired toward the accounting jobs and business opportunities the field offers (Indonesia's teacher guide.)

**Looking-for-work.** Inside the practical-application field, the Introduction names job-seeking skills (مهارات البحث عن عمل) first, alongside initiative and facing challenges (Egypt's book.)

**Jobs-in-trade.** The people of trade: the trader (έμπορος) defined by commercial law — by the substantive criterion of habitually performing commercial acts and by the formal criterion of persons the law names (SA and Ltd companies, cooperatives, stock-exchange brokers, state-controlled companies); the main persons of trade — wholesaler, retailer, the banker as "trader of money", shipowner, carrier — and the auxiliary persons who serve them: commercial agents, brokers, commission agents, warehouse and sales staff, and the travelling salesman (πλασιέ) who presents a new product from samples (Greece's book.)

**Profession part.** Accounting as a career and profession: the accountant as a professional (expertise, commitment), the leadership traits an accountant needs (accountability, integrity, credibility), the IFAC Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants (competence, confidentiality, integrity, objectivity), and Malaysia's accounting bodies — MASB, the standard-setting body established under the Financial Reporting Act 1997 (issues and reviews accounting standards, advises accountants, sets minimum financial-reporting requirements), and MICPA (established 1958, advancing the profession through training and examinations) (Malaysia's book.)

**Career paths.** Three career paths compared: employee in a small firm, a career inside a large company (start small, salary capped however high it climbs), and the independent business path, which removes the "salary ceiling" — you earn as much as your business earns — but brings responsibility with the freedom (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Getting a job.** Unit 10.4: preparing for the workplace — the job application portfolio and record of achievement (CV/resume, references, certificates, school reports), job search research, and formal and informal interview skills and preparation (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Careers.** Careers in accounting: what accountants do, chartered accountants across audit, taxation and management in public practice, private sector and government, and the Fiji Institute of Accountants pathway (Fiji's book.)

**Careers.** Career opportunities in commercial studies in Fiji (Fiji's book.)

**Occupations.** Occupation: its meaning and the types of occupation across the industrial, commercial and direct-services sectors (Nigeria's book.)

**Career-choice.** The factors that determine a person's choice of, and eligibility for, different types of employment and occupation (Nigeria's book.)

**Careers.** Career opportunities: a review of occupations and the career opportunities open to commerce graduates in productive occupations (Nigeria's book.)
