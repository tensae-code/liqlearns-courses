# Human Resources in Business

## Do it first

Count the workers in one business you know. For each, name the job they do and one skill it needs. Then ask: who decided that business needed those workers, who found them, and who works out their pay? That whole job is human resource management. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What human resource management is — and its roles

**Opening.** What human resource management means in a business.

**Roles.** The roles HRM plays in a business (planning manpower, recruiting, developing and keeping people).

**Business-functions.** The human resource function of a business.

**Dispute resolution and trade unions.** Resolving disputes: grievance procedures, and the third-party routes — mediation (facilitates, does not decide), arbitration (both sides accept the arbiter's binding ruling in advance) and conciliation (peacemaker who drafts a non-binding settlement); trade unions, collective bargaining and what makes a union's bargaining position strong

**Staff relations.** Building good management–staff relations: lead by example, two-way communication and open-door policies, good working conditions, rewarding effort, clear grievance procedures and encouraging teamwork

## Sub lesson 2 — Managing people so the business grows

**Application.** Applying HRM so the business develops sustainably.

**Managing-people.** The aspects of managing people that support business development.

**Employability.** How workers' employability skills connect to a business's success.

**Motivation and teamwork.** Motivating employees: financial methods (overtime pay, piece rate, bonuses, commission, profit-sharing, allowances) and non-financial methods (training, promotion opportunities, awards/recognition, team-oriented culture); teamwork — how to build a team (task, skills mix, personalities) and its advantages/disadvantages

**Training support.** Government-supported training and human resource development: recruitment and training are costly and businesses under-invest because trained workers may leave, so governments assist with training; the benefits — improved worker productivity and performance, closing the skills gap, consistent working to correct procedures, and increased job satisfaction

## Sub lesson 3 — Pay — how wages and salaries are worked out

**Pay.** Payroll systems: how an organisation works out and pays wages and salaries.

**Industrial conflict.** Conflict at work: sources of conflict, industrial relations, and the strategies each side uses — employees (work to rule, go-slow, overtime ban, sick-out, sit-in, strike, picketing) and employers (lock-outs, temporary/scab labour, refusing to negotiate, scare tactics)

**Looking after people at work.** Occupational safety and health: the WHO sense of it — the activities that control and prevent anything that can harm workers' health or safety, in public or private firms alike (Ecuador's book.)

**Looking after people at work.** Human resources in the business: recruitment — seeking out likely employees and encouraging them to apply, done internally (promotion, transfer, internal advertisement) or externally — then selection (application forms, selection tests and examinations, deep interview, background study, medical examination, placement), and retention: creating the environment that keeps high-performing employees in the organisation (Nepal's book.)

**Organising people.** The human-resources layout chart (inside plan part 1): the chart of how people are arranged in the business — management team, human resources and their duties — fixes each member's limits and authority, drawn as manager, deputy, finance manager, sales manager and human-resources manager (Iran's book.)
## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human resource management is the business's work with its people — planning how many are needed, recruiting them, developing and keeping them, and paying them — because a business grows and lasts through the skills and employability of its workers. Give one real example from your own home, market or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: opening, roles, application, managing-people. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
