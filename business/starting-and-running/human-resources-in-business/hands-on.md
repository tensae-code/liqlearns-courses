# Hands-on — Human Resources in Business

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Count the workers in one business you know. For each, name the job they do and one skill it needs. Then ask: who decided that business needed those workers, who found them, and who works out their pay? That whole job is human resource management. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: human resource management is the business's work with its people — planning how many are needed, recruiting them, developing and keeping them, and paying them — because a business grows and lasts through the skills and employability of its workers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
