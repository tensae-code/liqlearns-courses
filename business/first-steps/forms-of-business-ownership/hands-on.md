# Hands-on — Forms of Business Ownership

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take three businesses you know: one run by a single person, one run by partners or a family, and one big company. For each, answer: who owns it? If it owed money it could not pay, who would carry the loss? Write the three answers down. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: every business has an ownership form — sole trader, partnership, company, or a government or non-profit form — and the form fixes who owns the business, who carries its risks and debts, how it is formed and registered, and how large it can grow. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
