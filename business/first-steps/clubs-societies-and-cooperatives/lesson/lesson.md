# Clubs, Societies and Co-operatives

## Do it first

Name one club, church group or society you know. Say what shared interest brings its people together, what need it meets that no shop and no government office meets, and what money passes through its hands in a year. That is this lesson.

## Non-profit organisations as producers

**Non-profit organisations as producers.** Non-profit organisations — clubs, churches, societies and welfare groups — formed voluntarily around a shared interest to meet needs private enterprise and government do not provide, yet handling large sums of money (Samoan examples from sports clubs to the Red Cross and PTAs).

## Non-profit organisations

Not-for-profit private organisations — charities, local sports and social clubs and social enterprises — exist to improve social, animal or environmental welfare rather than maximise profit

**Co-operative and community forms.** Youth entrepreneurship inside Ecuador's popular and solidarity economy: Constitution art. 283 names the economic system's forms — public, private, mixed, popular and solidarity — including the cooperative, associative and community sectors; state policies link young researchers and youth employment to that economy (Ecuador's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** The cooperative organisation: founded on the philosophy "one for all and all for one" for members' common needs; its features — voluntary association, open membership, democratic management, limited liability, distribution of profit/surplus according to the transactions members made (not capital alone), service to members who are also its customers, social responsibility and separate legal status; the International Cooperative Alliance named (Nepal's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** Nepal's own way — registering a cooperative under the Cooperative Act, 2074 BS: at least 30 Nepali citizens for a subject-based or multipurpose cooperative (15 for a labour/skill-based one); preliminary meetings that form ad-hoc and bylaw-drafting committees and study business feasibility, passing the draft bylaws clause by clause, then application with the bylaws, and registration once the registrar is satisfied the society follows cooperative values and is community-based and member-controlled (Nepal's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** The cooperative's role in Nepal's economic development: poverty reduction (the poor pooling labour, skill and capital into trade and enterprise, creating jobs and self-employment), commercialisation of agriculture, financial access, promotion of entrepreneurship, and public awareness and empowerment (Nepal's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** Common forms of cooperative: producer cooperatives (Nepal's dairy cooperatives named as leaders — milk collection, distribution, livestock services), consumer cooperatives (daily goods at concessional prices), financial (savings and credit) cooperatives, labour cooperatives, multipurpose cooperatives, marketing cooperatives (small farmers marketing together, cutting out middlemen), housing cooperatives and transport cooperatives (Nepal's book.)
**Cooperatives part.** Cooperatives in Malaysia: registered under the supervision of the Suruhanjaya Koperasi Malaysia (SKM) and set up to serve members in finance/banking, housing, consumer, transport, farming, industry, construction and service functions (Malaysia's book.)

**Cooperatives part.** The cooperative as a form of business ownership: formed voluntarily by a group with shared interests to protect its members, owned jointly ("milik bersama"), its board (ALK) elected by vote at the annual general meeting, membership voluntary and open (Malaysia's book.)

**Opening part.** Community organisations introduced through clubs and societies: the unit's aim that students describe the role clubs and societies play, from the study of producers' decisions and the diversity and goals of producers (Samoa's book.)

**Structure part.** The structure of a typical club/society drawn as a hierarchy — Patron, President/Chairman, Deputy President, Secretary, Accountant/Treasurer and Committee — worked through a Samoan rugby club case (55 members, meeting every Thursday, dissatisfied with how their needs and wants are met) (Samoa's book.)

**Samoan clubs part.** Clubs and societies identified in Samoa — Rugby Union, Weight Watchers, Scouts and Girl Guides, Racing Club, Young Farmers, Athletics, Golf Club, Aumaga, Play Centre, RSA, Church, Parent Teacher Association, Aero Club, Samoa Kiriketi, Suicide Awareness among them — and the needs they satisfy: the clubs provide for cultural/religious needs, societies work for cultural/welfare needs and wants (Samoa's book.)

**Meetings part.** Running the club's meetings: the Notice of Meeting and Agenda (who the notice is for, who prepares it, when — with a fundraising discussion as the worked example) (Samoa's book.)

**Roles and procedure part.** The roles and procedures of the office-holders: the Patron, a nominated head of the society who attends meetings when the secretary asks and sponsors and supports the club; the President/Chairman, who chairs the meetings, is the official spokesperson, is elected by all the members and gives good leadership; the Secretary beside them — and the procedure words of a motion: amendment, closure, right of reply, and a motion voted on, carried (action taken as voted) or lost (things stay as they are) (Samoa's book.)

**Forming an association.** Appendix: the steps to incorporating an association (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Cooperatives.** Cooperative societies: their meaning and nature, formation, and the history and principles of the cooperative movement (Nigeria's book.)

**Organisation.** The characteristics, financing, internal management and types of cooperative societies, and their primary, secondary and apex bodies (Nigeria's book.)

**Benefits.** The benefits and problems of cooperative societies (Nigeria's book.)


**Clubs, societies and co-operatives.** The co-operative and union-of-co-operatives model: a collectively owned economic organisation with legal personality, founded voluntarily by at least 5 official members who help each other in production and business on the principles of self-reliance, equal responsibility, equality and democratic management, each member having an equal vote; a union is formed by at least 3 member co-operatives on the same principles. (Vietnam's book.)


**Group-benefits part.** Benefits of doing business as a group/cooperative: joint bulk purchasing of raw materials brings lower prices and better delivery terms; joint marketing (sharing transport responsibility lowers prices; the group can agree deals with big customers an individual cannot); and easier access to services and training, because providers prefer groups. (Laos's book.)

**Co-operatives part.** The co-operative business sector: what a co-operative is, its significance and modern trends (teacher guide) (Sri Lanka's teacher guide.)

**Co-operatives part.** Co-operative companies: their start, registration and working (Sri Lanka's book.)
