# Clubs, Societies and Co-operatives

## Do it first

Name one club, church group or society you know. Say what shared interest brings its people together, what need it meets that no shop and no government office meets, and what money passes through its hands in a year. That is this lesson.

## Non-profit organisations as producers

**Non-profit organisations as producers.** Non-profit organisations — clubs, churches, societies and welfare groups — formed voluntarily around a shared interest to meet needs private enterprise and government do not provide, yet handling large sums of money (Samoan examples from sports clubs to the Red Cross and PTAs).

## Non-profit organisations

Not-for-profit private organisations — charities, local sports and social clubs and social enterprises — exist to improve social, animal or environmental welfare rather than maximise profit

**Co-operative and community forms.** Youth entrepreneurship inside Ecuador's popular and solidarity economy: Constitution art. 283 names the economic system's forms — public, private, mixed, popular and solidarity — including the cooperative, associative and community sectors; state policies link young researchers and youth employment to that economy (Ecuador's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** The cooperative organisation: founded on the philosophy "one for all and all for one" for members' common needs; its features — voluntary association, open membership, democratic management, limited liability, distribution of profit/surplus according to the transactions members made (not capital alone), service to members who are also its customers, social responsibility and separate legal status; the International Cooperative Alliance named (Nepal's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** Nepal's own way — registering a cooperative under the Cooperative Act, 2074 BS: at least 30 Nepali citizens for a subject-based or multipurpose cooperative (15 for a labour/skill-based one); preliminary meetings that form ad-hoc and bylaw-drafting committees and study business feasibility, passing the draft bylaws clause by clause, then application with the bylaws, and registration once the registrar is satisfied the society follows cooperative values and is community-based and member-controlled (Nepal's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** The cooperative's role in Nepal's economic development: poverty reduction (the poor pooling labour, skill and capital into trade and enterprise, creating jobs and self-employment), commercialisation of agriculture, financial access, promotion of entrepreneurship, and public awareness and empowerment (Nepal's book.)

**Co-operative forms.** Common forms of cooperative: producer cooperatives (Nepal's dairy cooperatives named as leaders — milk collection, distribution, livestock services), consumer cooperatives (daily goods at concessional prices), financial (savings and credit) cooperatives, labour cooperatives, multipurpose cooperatives, marketing cooperatives (small farmers marketing together, cutting out middlemen), housing cooperatives and transport cooperatives (Nepal's book.)
