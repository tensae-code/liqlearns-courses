# Entrepreneurship

## Do it first

Picture one person in your area who started something of their own — a stall, a workshop, a service. Name what they organised: what land or place, whose work, what money or tools. Then name one risk they took and one plan they had to make. That is this lesson.

**The what-entrepreneurship-is / definition.** Defines entrepreneurship in the book's own way: a person's ability to turn ideas into action, bringing together creativity, innovation and willingness to take risks, together with the ability to plan and manage projects so as to reach set goals (info box inside the natural-woods lesson).

## The entrepreneur at work — organising land, labour and capital

**The entrepreneur and what entrepreneurship is.** Entrepreneurship defined as the organisation of land, labour and capital in order to make goods or services for sale at a profit, and the entrepreneur pictured as someone in business who buys land, builds a factory and hires workers to make goods or provide services.

## Entrepreneurship in the Caribbean

Entrepreneurship in the Caribbean: defined as identifying opportunities, allocating resources and creating value through business ownership; unemployment and fear of redundancy as drivers, government support and the EPIC regional business-incubator programme, and why Caribbean start-ups fail (costs, lack of market information, technology, capital, training, quality awareness)

## The entrepreneur

The entrepreneur: functions performed (conceptualising, researching, planning, accessing finance, organising, operating, evaluating performance, risk bearing, making profit/managing losses), the characteristics of successful entrepreneurs (creative, innovative, flexible, goal-oriented, persistent, calculated risk-takers, motivated, organised)

## Entrepreneurs and economic development

The entrepreneur as the central decision-maker through every start-up step, and how entrepreneurs support economic development: collaborating, providing goods/services that satisfy citizens, creating jobs, and contributing to nation building (incomes, less poverty, training, taxes funding infrastructure, foreign earnings, innovation)

**The entrepreneur.** The stages of a venture and the entrepreneur's profile: gestation (motivation, building personal capacities, researching and selecting the idea), start-up (writing the project and launching), and strengthening (consolidating by trial and error so the venture survives its first years); leadership as the capacity to influence others toward a goal (Ecuador's book.)

**The entrepreneur.** The benefits of a successful business to the entrepreneur: self-reliance and fulfilment (doing things for oneself, self-confidence, independent decisions, meeting one's basic needs); increased income and further investment (part of the growing profits for personal use, part to expand the business); recognition in the community (respected owners attract still more customers); (Uganda's book.)

**The entrepreneur.** The entrepreneur and the personal entrepreneurial characteristics (PECs): an entrepreneur produces new or better goods or services to sell to customers for profit, committing resources — money, time, land, machines, labour — to start and operate the venture; (Uganda's book.)

**The entrepreneur.** The entrepreneurial character: (Palestine's book.)

**The entrepreneur.** False beliefs about entrepreneurship, corrected: it is not only about creating jobs (it must create new value), the entrepreneur is not necessarily a good manager, its aim is not simply getting rich (it improves society's economy), and it does not only require financial capital (Iran's book.)

**The entrepreneur.** What entrepreneurship is: a way of working that uses opportunities and ideas to create a profitable, value-creating business; the driving engine of a country's economy and a lifestyle of growth, creativity, innovation and moving forward; its types — independent entrepreneurship (small businesses), organisational entrepreneurship, social entrepreneurship and cultural entrepreneurship (Iran's book.)

**The entrepreneur's mind.** How thinking works: the left brain receives, analyses and evaluates information while the right brain offers ideas intuitively; convergent thinking accepts things as they are and reaches for the first correct (usually old) solution, and over-reliance on it blocks creativity (Iran's book.)

**What entrepreneurship is.** What entrepreneurship is: in general, an organised approach of identifying ideas or opportunities, planning their implementation, gathering the needed resources and carrying them out effectively and responsibly toward society for a return or reward; (Palestine's book.)

**What entrepreneurship is.** What entrepreneurship is: the spirit of taking a risk to set up a business or enterprise to produce and sell goods or services, meeting customers' needs with the aim of earning profits; it involves doing new things, or doing the things already being done but in a new and better way; studying it builds a positive attitude towards work and business (Uganda's book.)

**What encourages entrepreneurs.** The factors that encourage entrepreneurship: social culture and values (family, community and state support, material or moral — among the most important); the possibilities of the environment (where a group's unmet need appears, the entrepreneur turns the idea of meeting it into an income-earning project); (Palestine's book.)

**Entrepreneurship.** The entrepreneur's role in development, and what makes a project entrepreneurial: entrepreneurial work creates new jobs, raises productivity and diversifies products through the entrepreneurs' varied talents, opens new markets for a good or service, transfers and localises technology, and restructures investment projects for new products and ways of serving; (Palestine's book.)

**Why learn entrepreneurship.** The purpose of entrepreneurship education: it is the process of imparting knowledge, skills and values so learners can identify opportunities around them and undertake business activities for profit; (Uganda's book.)
**What-entrepreneurship-includes.** The Introduction's field map: the subject melts its craft skills into entrepreneurship "with its different fields and skills", and sorts them into three fields — practical application, ideas and opportunities, and resources — the book's own map of what entrepreneurship covers (Egypt's book.)

**What-undertaking-is.** What undertaking means at its root: when we try to find the solution to a problem we call it emprendimiento; everyone has an entrepreneurial spirit, it only has to be cultivated, and learners are agents of change in their own life and society (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Entrepreneurial-spirit.** The entrepreneurial spirit (espíritu emprendedor) in its three ideas: the entrepreneur — a person with a positive attitude who anticipates events, dares to invent and create new activities or projects, is innovative, creative, dynamic and persevering, identifies opportunities and takes advantage of them, makes mistakes and learns from them; to undertake — to do, start, create, try, innovate, dream, propose; and the undertaking — an initiative with objectives and goals founded on a dream, carrying a challenge, the result of an idea and of perseverance; the book widens undertaking beyond the economic/productive kind to social, cultural, sporting, artistic and ecological initiatives, and insists on an ethics of entrepreneurship — respect for others' rights, human rights, care of the environment (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Personal-entrepreneurial-competencies.** The Personal Entrepreneurial Competencies (Competencias Emprendedoras Personales, CEP), first five: initiative and seeking opportunities — opportunities do not arrive by themselves, they must be gone out to meet, and preparation and opportunity go together; persistence — keeping the firm decision to reach a goal, repeating or changing actions to pass an obstacle; fulfilling commitments — keeping promises wins the trust of bosses, colleagues, clients and family, and successful people keep clients satisfied valuing the long-term relation over short-term benefit; demanding efficiency and quality — quality is the capacity to satisfy others and must be observed through the whole process, from buying the raw material to the final packaging, not only in the finished product; and taking calculated risks — every risk needs a purpose, the potential reward and the potential risk must both be evaluated, and the risk taken should be moderate, calculable and predictable (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Personal-entrepreneurial-competencies.** The CEP, second five: setting goals — goals with personal meaning and a challenge in them, measurable objectives at short, medium and long term, beginning with a clear end in mind; seeking information — deciding well needs data, background and details, and information has become a good of extreme value for personal and business development, so seeking it is an investment; systematic planning and control — planning defines the path to the objectives: what tasks, how long, what resources; persuasion and creating support networks — convincing others to act together and building cooperation networks, since nobody is wholly self-sufficient; and self-confidence and independence — the inner force that animates or stops action, shown in assuming tasks with responsibility and decision and finishing them on time and well done (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Life-plan.** The life plan (plan de vida) as the entrepreneur's own plan: to reach one's dreams one must first answer who am I, what are my strengths and weaknesses, what have I achieved, what is my mission, where do I want to go and how far; dreams are reached at short, medium and long term by advancing in small steps, taking every opportunity and persisting; the learner writes life objectives across the personal, family and community spheres and traces his route of life — the force that motivates him, his objectives and goals, what he must do to reach them, the support he needs, the obstacles he may meet and how he will beat them (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Knowing-yourself-first.** Self-knowledge as the seed of business culture: knowing yourself is the key that makes a businessperson succeed — before starting a venture, identify what activities you like, what kind of person you are, your satisfactions and interests, your values and talents, your strengths and weaknesses, and how you will use those characteristics for the new business; then self-evaluate and make a continuous-improvement plan, so as to face the difficulties on the road to success (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Organising-together.** Community organisation as the vehicle of collective ventures: an organisation is the structure a group of people gives itself to function according to a method and a common objective — people organise because they share interests or problems that demand union to face them, and every organised group's work must hold a common objective and the same interests; organisation's advantages — it lets the group use its collective human, material and economic resources better, facilitates negotiating and managing with local and central governments and other organisations to reach its objectives, improves results by improving the use and distribution of resources, and develops leaders; its process — members meet voluntarily and decide to participate, plan the necessary tasks and order them, execute them, and evaluate them all — and its qualities: common interests of the majority, free and voluntary decision, majority decision-taking, joint planning of all actions, benefiting the greatest number possible, solidarity and shared responsibility (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Social-entrepreneur.** Social entrepreneurship: a social entrepreneur is an individual who sets up an enterprise destined to solve a problem and generate a self-sustainable solution through the product or service it provides; his goal is to create an impact through a business model — an impact that does not sustain itself on external financing or on the traditional sources of international development; the deep transformation of a community's problems comes from the experts in them, those who live the problem day by day, and social entrepreneurs implement their solutions in a practical and rentable (viable, income-covering) way, which makes them the right candidates to identify, execute and maintain the solutions for many years (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Kinds-of-venture.** The kinds of venture this year studies, learned from real Guatemalan cases: social ventures (a youth project educating 300+ children in Antigua; a restaurant in Xela whose profits fund the owning NGO's social projects), environmental ventures (an NGO that builds pedal-powered machines — bicimáquinas — from recycled bicycles and sells them cheaply to rural families, developing their production, promotion and commercialisation so that both it and the beneficiary groups are self-sustainable), cultural ventures (a youth orchestra in Santa Cruz Balanyá whose instruments were earned through agriculture), and cooperative ventures (an agricultural cooperative whose young members' farm projects pay for their studies, and the cooperative sector — fishing, crafts, transport, savings, credit and housing cooperatives — working to bring young people in to innovate its practices) (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Opening part.** The history and development of entrepreneurship in Malaysia taught as the country's own story: barter in primitive society, the pedagang of the Melaka Malay kingdom, then development before, during and after the Dasar Ekonomi Baru (DEB) and under the Dasar Pembangunan Negara (DPN) (Malaysia's book.)

**Development-efforts part.** The government's DEB-era efforts to grow entrepreneurs: new agencies (UDA, FAMA, BPIMB), expanding MARA and PERNAS, technical and vocational training, interest-free and low-interest loans through MARA and the CGC credit-guarantee scheme, rural industry and business premises/infrastructure (Malaysia's book.)

**Definitions part.** Entrepreneur and trader defined and distinguished side by side: the entrepreneur founds, manages, bears risk and keeps seeking new potential business, the trader simply buys and sells for profit; goals and innovation compared in the book's own table (Malaysia's book.)

**Concept part.** The concept of entrepreneurship as combining entrepreneurial resources to produce goods or services for human needs, with the scholars' definitions (Gartner's creation of an organisation, Timmons' creative act) (Malaysia's book.)

**Perspectives part.** Entrepreneurship from the Islamic perspective, Malaysia's own way: enterprise as a form of general worship (ibadah umum) and its principles — Falah (success that solves the community's problems), Amanah (trust), Tawakal (reliance after effort) and Istiqamah (steadfastness) (Malaysia's book.)

**Perspectives part.** Entrepreneurship from the conventional perspectives: the psychological, economic and sociological approaches to who becomes an entrepreneur and why (Malaysia's book.)

**Elements part.** The elements inside the entrepreneurship concept: continuous initiative, daring to take risks, being able to gain returns, and mobilising entrepreneurial resources (Malaysia's book.)

**Roles part.** The roles of entrepreneurship in developing the self, the family, society, the nation and religion (Malaysia's book.)

**Career part.** The advantages of entrepreneurship as a career: unlimited opportunities, owning one's own business/organisation, a source of income and rewards, improving one's own and society's status, and a career that can be inherited (Malaysia's book.)

**Traits part.** The characteristics of an entrepreneur: creative, innovative, quick to seize opportunities, visionary, internally driven, never giving up, daring to take risks, able to lead and competitive (Malaysia's book.)

**Profile part.** What a successful entrepreneur's profile contains: background, push factors, challenges and obstacles, the ways of facing them, success achieved and life principles (Malaysia's book.)

**Profiles-in-practice part.** Malaysia's own worked role-model profiles of national entrepreneur figures (a diversified corporate figure, the founder of a low-cost airline, and an internet-business figure) showing background, push factors and success in full (Malaysia's book.)

**Competencies part.** The Personal Entrepreneurial Competencies (PECs) an entrepreneur is rated on: opportunity seeking, persistence, commitment to the work contract, demand for quality and efficiency, risk taking, goal setting, information seeking and the rest of the profile (Bhutan's book.)

**What entrepreneurship is.** Entrepreneurship defined as a citizen's independent, initiative activity carried on in their own name under their own property risk to gain profit or personal income; the individual entrepreneur as its subject, and the starting concepts of need and good (a good as what satisfies everyday needs) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Entrepreneur part of the lesson (Kazakhstan's own way: the two figures taught as stages of one path).** Entrepreneur versus businessman: entrepreneurship as the first step into business (the owner works in the firm himself, often alone or with family, and the income stops when he stops), against business as the already-built system of processes and a team whose income no longer depends on the owner's presence; the entrepreneur's constant search (for clients, materials, premises, people) and the risk he carries (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Purpose.** The subject, aim and tasks of entrepreneurship: its aim is to satisfy consumers' needs, earn profit and grow the activity up to the level of a business; its tasks are to raise income, build capitalisation, and invest in modernising and expanding production (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Views of entrepreneurship.** How economists have seen the entrepreneur, compared in one table: Cantillon (readiness for risk), Marx, and Schumpeter (the drive to innovate) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Entrepreneur.** Schumpeter's entrepreneur as the engine of economic growth: growth comes from the entrepreneur's "new combinations" of the factors of production — innovation — and economic growth itself measured as the rise in real GDP, in total or per head (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Entrepreneurial mindset.** The entrepreneurial approach as a competence: initiative, the continuous drive to develop and innovate, thinking ahead to future needs, imagination and creative work as the maker's mindset, counted among the skills of the 21st century (Kazakhstan's book.)

**What entrepreneurship is.** Entrepreneurship as a phenomenon (the web of political, economic, social, organisational and personal relations aimed at organising a business) and as a process (the entrepreneur's unbroken chain of actions from the appearance of an idea to its practical realisation in a project) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Functions.** The functions of entrepreneurship (general-economic, creative-search/innovative, resource — named the most important, social, organising) and its principles: independence and free choice, free hiring, free disposal of profit, aiming at success, risk, personal responsibility, flexibility (Kazakhstan's book.)

**The entrepreneur's mindset and staged tasks.** The entrepreneurial mindset: an entrepreneur is active, responsible and results-driven; in law there is no concept "businessman", only the individual entrepreneur; an entrepreneur creates something new or improves what exists at high risk; a business grows through staged tasks — survival, then liquidity, then market leadership; entrepreneurship carries ethical and social responsibility (Kazakhstan's book.)

**What entrepreneurship does and how the state supports it.** Entrepreneurship defined through its results: producing and redistributing goods and creating jobs; how governments support it (tax reductions, cheap loans for small and medium business, information and finance support); the Atameken chamber defends entrepreneurs' interests; entrepreneurship is incompatible with corruption (Kazakhstan's book.)

**The entrepreneurial approach.** The entrepreneurial approach: entrepreneurship as self-realisation; the qualities it needs (initiative, perseverance, independence, restraint, reliability, energy, adequate self-esteem, inventiveness, flexibility, balance, calculated risk); entrepreneurial competence defined; a business idea must be tested against who will be interested in it and what demand exists (Kazakhstan's book.)

**The entrepreneur.** Strand 1 Unit 2: entrepreneurship defined (identify an opportunity, create an organisation); the characteristics of the entrepreneur (vision, passion, product knowledge, networking, self-confidence, risk management) (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**What entrepreneurs do.** Strand 1 Unit 2: the two entrepreneurial functions — risk taking and innovation (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Encouraging enterprise.** Strand 1: ways the economy encourages entrepreneurship — entrepreneurship programmes and awareness, business schools, National Development Bank funds and SME incubation centres, protection of private ownership, technology, contract law, a tradeable currency, reducing corruption, stability, and infant-industry protection (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Opening.** Myths and convictions about the nature of work: what a myth is and the false beliefs people hold about work, examined and corrected (Mozambique's book.)

**Work-and-society.** The social status of work: how work gives a person standing in society (Mozambique's book.)

**Business-myths.** Myths and convictions about business itself, and how to overcome false judgements about business (the book's two-lesson sequence taught as one correction arc) (Mozambique's book.)

**Competencies.** Personal entrepreneurial competencies (CEPs) of power and planning: the competencies an entrepreneur must build in himself (Mozambique's book.)

**Self-assessment-of-traits.** Assessing one's own risk-taking capacity, goal-setting and self-confidence (Mozambique's book.)

**Traits.** The achievement syndrome and imagination: assessing one's drive to achieve and to imagine (Mozambique's book.)

**Development-plan.** The personal entrepreneurial competency development plan: planning how to grow one's own competencies (Mozambique's book.)

**Goals.** My goals, my dreams: telling goals from dreams — goals are specific, timed, achievable and planned, dreams are unplanned wishes — and setting one's own goals (Mozambique's book.)

**Work-values.** The dignity of work (dignidade de trabalho) (Mozambique's book.)


**Entrepreneurship.** The competencies a businessperson needs: leadership competence (setting strategy, dynamism, creativity, seizing business opportunities), management competence (organising and building relationships in business), professional competence (knowledge of the trade one is in), and the competence to keep learning and accumulating knowledge and skills. (Vietnam's book.)


**Rewards-and-effort part.** The rewards of being a businessperson set against the effort it demands. (Laos's book.)

**Mindset part.** The business mindset: the traits, enthusiasm and psychological motivation of a businessperson. (Laos's book.)

**Example/case part.** Worked case: Somsak, a 40-year-old Vientiane tailor who trained in tailoring, employs five full-time and two part-time staff and moved to larger rented premises near his customers — used to show jobs created, staff living standards, competition, foreign currency and contribution to GDP. (Laos's book.)

**Self-employment part.** Self-employment weighed: advantages (personal satisfaction, independence and freedom, profit, career and status security, flexibility, being your own boss) against disadvantages (investment money may be lost, uncertain or low income, long hours, routine daily work). (Laos's book.)

**Reasons part.** Reasons people enter business: family unity, and personal reasons of economic, psychological and social kinds. (Laos's book.)

**Decision part.** The decision to become a businessperson: triggers such as job loss, family crisis, relocation and role models; entrepreneurial capacity; and the need for a supportive business environment. (Laos's book.)

**Attitude part.** Keeping a business attitude: the traits list (do what you are capable of, positive attitude and self-competition, discipline, motivating and leading others, planning, persistence, appropriate dress, good health and enthusiasm); and goal-setting — when performance drifts from goals, adjust the goals to reality; goal adjustment is a regular business process. (Laos's book.)

**Mindset part.** Having a business mindset: its meaning and scope at the individual level (an idea turned into a planned, organised business) and at the business level; its benefits — becoming valuable to family, community and society, and being able to handle any situation. (Laos's book.)

**Characteristics part.** Characteristics of the mindset, including empathy — understanding others' feelings and putting yourself in their place; information-gathering feeds creative thinking; intuition and deep understanding. (Laos's book.)

**Components part.** Goals must be specific and measurable, and set for the short and the long term; the mindset has five components (idea/concept, objective-setting, imagination, thinking, plan). (Laos's book.)

**Potential part.** Evaluating a businessperson's potential: the knowledge areas a successful small-business builder needs — business opportunities, the market, customers, competitors, the production process, technical problems, business administration, and sources of help; knowledge alone is insufficient. (Laos's book.)

**Distinctions part.** Knowledge vs skills vs traits: traits are personal qualities in thought and action, and are slower to develop. (Laos's book.)

**Comparison part.** Self-employment vs salaried employment in parallel tables: self-employment (control the working atmosphere, give orders, keep learning — but limited time, an uncertain future, hard-to-find replacement staff, much paperwork, dependence on employees' actions) vs salaried work (limited specific responsibility, but following orders). (Laos's book.)

**Advantages/disadvantages part.** Advantages of your own business: it buys goods and services from other local businesses, borrows from local banks and pays taxes; independence — not under others' control, using your own knowledge and skills as you see fit; you cannot be dismissed or forced to retire at a set age; status in society and customer acceptance — and its disadvantages: family savings accumulated over years are at stake, and income is uncertain and low (profit income is not as regular as a monthly salary, especially in the first six months to a year). (Laos's book.)

**Reasons part.** Push and pull reasons for entering business: forced circumstances (unhappy with boss or colleagues, no job after school or training) and practical reasons (economic — build a livelihood, become rich, prepare for retirement, earn extra income; psychological; social). (Laos's book.)

**Answer-key part.** Model answers for the Somsak tailoring case: how he created jobs for local people; how he raised his employees' living standards; how he contributed to competition in the garment industry (buying cloth and employing four skilled tailors, displaying goods in his shop); how he helped the government earn foreign currency; and how he provided cheap vocational training by learning on the job. **Content-summary part.** The teacher's summary teaching: doing business is a process of activities including production, trade and services; and the characteristics of having a business mindset, including skills, independence, networking and goal-orientation. (Laos's teacher guide.)

**Answer-key part.** Answer material: the traits of a successful businessperson — initiative, sees and seizes opportunities, persistence, seeks information personally, concern for quality, commitment to contracts, efficiency orientation, solves problems from first principles, self-confidence, systematic planning, persuasion.
