# What Is a Business — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about what a business is?
**Answer:** A firm as one unit of business organisation under one management bringing factors of production together (it may run several plants/branches), set against an industry as all the firms making the same product for one market, with Kenyan examples (Bata, Kenya Commercial Bank, the petroleum and motor industries).

**2.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about what a business is?
**Answer:** What "business" means, the business as an open system turning inputs into outputs, and business resources: people, money, materials and opportunities.

**3.** Explain the basic concepts part of this lesson in your own words.
**Answer:** The basics the book builds on: needs vs wants, goods vs services, tangible vs intangible products, and subsistence production before specialisation created surpluses to exchange

**4.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about opening / kinds of business?
**Answer:** Classifying businesses: by type of activity (production, services, trade), by production line, by size (small, medium, large) and by place and ownership, with everyday trades sorted into the classes

**5.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about opening / features of a business?
**Answer:** The characteristics of business: it is a human economic activity (set up, run and managed for economic benefit, using labour, capital and other factors), and it creates utilities, deals in production, distribution and exchange, involves regular transactions, the profit motive, capital investment, consumer satisfaction, organisation and risk

**6.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about why businesses matter?
**Answer:** Why small projects matter: they supply most of the goods and services consumed daily and employ most of the workforce — creating jobs, raising individuals' incomes and social welfare, and through their profits and taxes raising government spending on public services, national income and GDP;
