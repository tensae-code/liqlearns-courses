# What Is a Business

## Do it first

Look along your street or around your village and pick one business. Name what it makes or does, who runs it, and what it uses — its people, its money, its materials. Then name a second business that makes the same kind of thing: the two together stand inside one industry. That is this lesson.

## A firm, and the industry it stands in

**What a business is.** A firm as one unit of business organisation under one management bringing factors of production together (it may run several plants/branches), set against an industry as all the firms making the same product for one market, with Kenyan examples (Bata, Kenya Commercial Bank, the petroleum and motor industries).

**What a business is.** What a business is: any legal economic activity done by an individual or a group of persons with the aim of making profits — buying and selling agricultural produce, running a retail shop, operating a restaurant, services like salons, growing vegetables; (Uganda's book.)

**What a business is.** The investment project as a system: a set of activities using material and human resources to produce goods or services that meet a need, for profit — if profit is not the aim but social goals are, it is a charitable/social project, not an investment project; (Palestine's book.)

## Business as a system — inputs in, outputs out

**What a business is.** What "business" means, the business as an open system turning inputs into outputs, and business resources: people, money, materials and opportunities.

## Three kinds of organisation — private, government, non-profit

**What a business is / kinds of organisation.** Production is organised through business organisations that combine the four factors of production; organisations are sorted by goal and ownership into private enterprise (profit), government (citizens' welfare) and non-profit (members' needs).

## The three sectors in Samoa's economy

**Business activities and sectors.** The three sectors of the economy as business activity: primary (extracting/harvesting natural resources), secondary (manufacturing/processing raw materials into finished or intermediate goods) and tertiary (services to consumers plus service industries supporting production — transport, finance, communication, marketing, accounting), set in Samoa's economy of agriculture, copra/coconut exports, remittances and tourism.

## Producers in three sectors

**The sectors of business activity.** Producers sorted into primary (obtaining and collecting raw materials — farming, fishing, forestry), secondary (using raw materials to produce other things, in factories) and tertiary (providing services), with an industry defined as a group of producers who produce a similar product.

## Opening / what a business is

Business defined as all the activities concerned with producing and distributing goods and services to satisfy consumers' needs and wants; a business as an organisation supplying goods/services for money, needing capital and customers, earning revenue and profit, though some pursue social/environmental aims too

## Basic concepts

The basics the book builds on: needs vs wants, goods vs services, tangible vs intangible products, and subsistence production before specialisation created surpluses to exchange

**Opening / what a business is.** What a business is: all production, service and buying-and-selling activities carried on for profit; its features — selling and transferring goods and services for value, repeated transactions, the profit motive as the main driver, and activity joined with risk, always facing an uncertain future (Iran's book.)

**Opening / what a business is.** Meaning and definitions of business: a human economic activity in which a person regularly engages in economic activity with the aim of profit; the traditional concept (profit-centred, ignoring the consumer) set against the modern, consumer-oriented concept (satisfying consumers through regular production, distribution and exchange while reaching business goals), with scholars' definitions (Nepal's book.)

**Opening / kinds of business.** Classifying businesses: by type of activity (production, services, trade), by production line, by size (small, medium, large) and by place and ownership, with everyday trades sorted into the classes (Iran's book.)

**Opening / features of a business.** The characteristics of business: it is a human economic activity (set up, run and managed for economic benefit, using labour, capital and other factors), and it creates utilities, deals in production, distribution and exchange, involves regular transactions, the profit motive, capital investment, consumer satisfaction, organisation and risk (Nepal's book.)

**Kinds of business.** Service enterprises: businesses that earn by giving intangible services — knowledge, skill, experience — in kinds including education, health, hospitality and tourism, technology and IT, personal and professional services, transportation and logistics, and maintenance and repair services (Nepal's book.)

**Kinds of business.** Uganda's four types of business by activity: agro-businesses (produce or sell agricultural products for profit), manufacturing businesses (process raw materials to produce different products), trading businesses (buy and sell goods, in retail or wholesale) and service businesses (provide services to customers using talents or skills) (Uganda's book.)

**Kinds of business.** Classifying projects by economic activity into four sectors: primary (extracting raw materials from nature — quarries, minerals, farming, animal raising, dams and irrigation, fish farming); industrial (converting raw materials into finished or intermediate products — dairies, iron, leather tanning, soap); (Palestine's book.)

**Kinds of business.** Classifying projects by size: the measures used are annual sales volume, number of workers, capital size, asset value and where products are marketed; Palestine's own bands — small: no more than about 9 workers and capital up to 50,000 dinars, products marketed locally; medium: roughly 10–19 workers, capital up to half a million dinars, sometimes exporting; large: (Palestine's book.)

**Why a business exists.** Objectives of business in three families: economic objectives (earning profit, creating markets, producing to consumers' tastes, research and development), social objectives (providing quality goods, creating employment, protecting the environment, social responsibility) and human objectives (employee welfare, satisfaction of shareholders/investors, consumer satisfaction) (Nepal's book.)

**Why businesses matter.** Why small projects matter: they supply most of the goods and services consumed daily and employ most of the workforce — creating jobs, raising individuals' incomes and social welfare, and through their profits and taxes raising government spending on public services, national income and GDP; (Palestine's book.)

**Why businesses matter.** The importance of doing business: people do business to get money to meet personal needs, and business also benefits the family, the community/village and the government — the book's own four-way sorting of the benefits (owner/personal, family, community members, government) (Uganda's book.)
**What a business is / kinds of business.** What a business is and its three kinds by activity: a business creates added value by producing goods or services society needs in order to earn a profit; a service business sells intangible services, a trading business acts as intermediary buying and reselling goods, and a manufacturing business processes raw materials into finished goods (Indonesia's book.)

**What-an-economic-organisation-is.** Economic organisations (οικονομικοί οργανισμοί): organised entities that combine the factors of production — nature, labour and capital — to produce economic goods under the economic principle (greatest result from the fewest factors, without sacrificing quality); they are distinguished by their owner (private, public, municipal/community, mixed) and by purpose (non-profit public-benefit units vs profit-seeking units) (Greece's book.)

**What-a-business-is.** The business (επιχείρηση) defined by its basic characteristics: it has its own property, independent from its owner's; it produces economic goods and disposes of them on the market; it applies the economic principle aiming at its owner's profit (Greece's book.)

**Kinds-of-business-by-sector.** Businesses distinguished by their field of activity: primary production (farming, mining, livestock, fishing, forestry), secondary production (manufacturing and crafts), and tertiary production (trade, tourism, transport, banks, insurance and other services) (Greece's book.)

**What-a-business-and-an-entrepreneur-are.** What an economic enterprise is: an activity that offers goods and/or services in exchange for money or other goods or services — with the kinds of business activity named (construction, communication, manufacturing/production including making parts for other firms' final products, supply and distribution, repair, services such as tourism and lodging, textiles, produce collection centres); and what an entrepreneur (empresario) is: a person who creates or administers an enterprise and assumes the responsibility, offering goods or services to individuals or firms in exchange for payment — together with the personal qualities that help him reflect, analyse, solve problems and decide: curiosity and creativity, learning from one's own and others' errors, motivation and self-confidence, facing obstacles, taking risks, capacity for innovation and direction, identifying outlets for the product, and a cooperative spirit — qualities that can be acquired and developed (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Sectors-of-the-economy.** Economic activities and the sectors of the economy: an economic activity is one carrying effort and projection to produce, distribute, commercialise and intermediate products and services within a society, with the object of generating utility; activities are grouped by sector — agricultural, livestock, industry/manufacturing, commerce and services — and classified by the international CIIU (Clasificación Industrial Internacional Uniforme, from 1948) and, in Guatemala, by the NAEG nomenclature used by the INE, the Banco de Guatemala, the SAT and the IGSS, which counts some 375 economic activities (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**What a business is.** The concept of a business: buying and selling goods or services to meet human needs and wants for profit, with goods (physical products) distinguished from services (work done for others) (Malaysia's book.)

**Opening part.** What a business is: an activity of buying and selling goods or services between seller and buyer to meet consumer needs and wants, involving an exchange of value (money for product) from which the seller gains profit; goods and services together are the product (Malaysia's book.)

**Purpose part.** The purpose of business: offering goods and services, the importance of profit to a business (measuring performance, funding growth), and the non-profit organisation (OBBU) that offers goods/services at cost or free for society's benefit (Malaysia's book.)

**Size-classification part.** Classifying business by size, Malaysia's SME way: micro, small, medium and large set by annual sales and full-time employees for manufacturing and for services (e.g. small manufacturing RM300,000–<RM15 million, 5–<75 workers), with the strengths and weaknesses of each size (Malaysia's book.)

**Sector-classification part.** Classifying business by sector: the primary sector, the secondary sector (manufacturing and processing, engineering, energy supply, construction) and the tertiary sector, and how the three sectors need one another (Malaysia's book.)

**Interrelation part.** How the four ideas interlock: the goods/services offered, the form of ownership, the classification and the scale of a business are chosen together and shape one another (Malaysia's book.)
