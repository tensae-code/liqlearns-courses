## E-commerce

E-commerce defined as business activity/transactions over a computer-mediated network (internet, intranet, extranet), with its advantages (global 24-hour reach, choice, lower overheads, quick customer response) and disadvantages (no personal contact or inspection, delivery problems, security/fraud risk, technology cost)

## IT in logistics

Information technology in logistics: GPS (route planning, tracking trucks, fuel and security) and GIS (route modelling, navigation, fleet management), Portnet port portals, telemarketing and B2B/B2C e-commerce in the supply chain; and the global logistics providers (FedEx, DHL, Amazon Logistics) and logistics hubs, with Jamaica's hub development as the Caribbean case

## Business technology and ICT

Business technology and ICT: technology as methods, systems and devices developed by applying knowledge and science; business technology created for business goals, with businesses as both producers and consumers of it; earlier technology (hammers, sewing machines, eyeglasses) vs modern technology (CT scanners, CAD, CAM, inventory systems); ICT defined — devices, networking, applications and systems for using and communicating information digitally — and its main roles in business: storage, retrieval, editing, transmission and receipt of information, including its visible impact on banking

## ICT tools

ICT tools by business function: management tools (management information systems; alarm/security systems); accounting tools (accounting software, spreadsheets, databases); production tools (computerised stock control, CAD, CAM); marketing tools (online surveys, graphics software, websites, social media advertising, online coupons, e-commerce); and human resource tools (word processing, presentation software, email, training simulations and online courses, online job advertisements and applications)

## CAD and CAM

Computer aided design and manufacturing: CAD uses computer systems to create and modify 2D and 3D designs (machinery, buildings, vehicles, animations), with simulation of how a product will work — better visualisation, faster design, improved quality and accuracy, lower development cost, against expensive software, training and processing power; CAM uses computer-controlled machinery to automate manufacturing processes such as cutting, grinding, bending and gluing — faster production, less waste, precision and consistent quality, against set-up cost, technology failure and job losses

## Management information systems

Management information systems (MIS): an organisational system that uses ICT to obtain, store, process and share the information management needs for decision making; its types — process control systems, sales and marketing systems, inventory control systems (with automatic reordering), office automation systems, and accounting and finance systems; benefits (performance reports, cost control, comparing actual with expected performance, coordinating departments) and limitations (only as good as its design and data, quantitative focus, needs trained users)

## E-commerce and e-business

Digital communications, e-commerce and e-business: digital communication technologies — the internet and mobile technologies — let businesses communicate instantly and cheaply with any stakeholder; e-commerce is buying and selling goods and services online, using electronic data interchange (EDI) between business partners; e-business is wider — e-commerce plus ICT running internal processes (production, inventory, logistics with GPS/GIS, product development, risk management, knowledge management, human resources); Amazon as the e-business case study

## Impact of ICT

The impact of ICT on business operations: improvements in speed and time savings; automation of repetitive and hazardous tasks; reduced physical storage requirements and costs; improved sharing of information (email, messaging, tele/videoconferencing, websites, social media); reaching more customers through e-commerce and online marketing; developing customer relationships and databases; better customer service; streamlining operations and reducing travel costs; maximising profits; devoting talent to core business instead of overheads; and supporting better relationships with suppliers and distributors

## Ethics of ICT

Ethical implications of using ICT in business: security — cybercrime in its forms (unauthorised access and corporate espionage, sabotage, stealing credit card numbers and banking fraud, skimming, phishing) and its costs in losses and customer confidence; privacy — cookies, camera systems, hacked social media profiles and employee monitoring; intellectual property — how easily online work, music, video and trade secrets are copied without payment; and the human impact — jobs lost to automation vs new ICT jobs created, continuous training, working from home, fake news and cyber bullying, health effects of prolonged computer use, and distraction

**Where-e-commerce-came-from.** The birth of e-commerce: banks' electronic funds transfer systems of the early 1970s, electronic data interchange (EDI) over private value-added networks (VANs), and the move to transactions between two or more parties through computers and networks (Greece's book.)

**What-e-commerce-is-made-of.** The components of e-commerce: the partner categories that transact electronically — business-to-business orders, purchases and sales, business-to-consumer, and public-sector-to-citizen dealings such as tax obligations and certificates — the types of products traded, and the business processes a network can carry, classified from a network of information up to a network of transactions (Greece's book.)

**Uses-of-e-commerce.** The fields where e-commerce is applied: information exchange, customer support — including after-sales contact groups where customers talk to the company and to each other — virtual (online) companies, electronic distribution, joint business processes between firms, and online banking (Greece's book.)

**Effects-of-e-commerce.** The effects of e-commerce: for businesses, access to a worldwide market and more choice of customers and suppliers whatever their capital; better service quality through electronic communication; new business and organisational forms; the effects on work and employment — the computer and new-technology skills now demanded of staff — and the open questions of taxation and payment security (Greece's book.)

**ICT-department part.** The ICT functional department: information technology as computing plus telecommunications in the business, speeding transactions between trader and buyer and opening e-marketing (Malaysia's book.)

**IT-use part.** The use of information technology across the business: its purposes (smoother operations, better decisions, widening the market to global level without borders) and e-banking as online banking that makes paying bills, investing and checking faster and cheaper (Malaysia's book.)

**Trends part.** Other trends in business: e-business (conducting business by electronic gadgets and internet; the e-commerce models B2B, B2C and C2B, with a Bhutanese e-commerce firm as the B2C example, and Amazon/Alibaba integrating machine learning and AI) and business process outsourcing (BPO/ITES, with Bhutan's BIABPO as its promotional body) (Bhutan's book.)

**Online marketing.** The tools of digital marketing, each weighed: SEO (search optimisation), contextual advertising (keyword-matched, paid per click, with remarketing), display/media advertising (banners, teasers, video), SMM, viral marketing, e-mail mailings and content marketing — advantages and drawbacks of each (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Online marketing.** The firm's own website as a commercial tool (orders taken automatically, consumers found across the whole country, managers freed from routine questions) and the plan for carrying value to the end consumer through digital marketing and SMM (internet-strategy tasks, channels, community building, reputation management, personal branding) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Online marketing.** Online marketing: targeting an offer to users whose characteristics fit it; retargeting/remarketing to people who already visited; contextual advertising matched to search queries; SEO — search engines reward original content and demote copied pages; landing pages that collect enquiries; native advertising fitted to its platform; the advantages of internet marketing: interactivity, measurability, flexibility and low cost (Kazakhstan's book.)

**SMM.** Social-media marketing in practice: learn when your audience is online and time posts to the top of their feed; write content that invites discussion — comments show the post is on a topic the audience cares about; run contests and answer critical comments (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Internet.** The structure of the internet in one diagram: the "super highway" linking supercomputer centres, the Internet Society and the Internet Registry, Network Information Centres, Internet Service Providers, on-line services and regional networks (Kenya's book.)
