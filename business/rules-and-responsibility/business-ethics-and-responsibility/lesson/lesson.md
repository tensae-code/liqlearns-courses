# Business Ethics and Responsibility

## Do it first

Name one business near you. List the people and groups it touches: its workers, its customers, its suppliers, the government, and the neighbours who breathe its air and drink its water. Beside each, write one thing the business owes them. That is this lesson.

## Production and the harm it can do

**Environmental responsibility.** How production harms the environment and community health: air pollution (acid rain, CFCs and the ozone layer, greenhouse gases, breathing harm), water pollution, noise pollution, destruction of the environment (deforestation, quarries) and solid waste pollution.

## The entrepreneur's ethics in digital business

**Ethics.** Entrepreneurial ethics connected to digital-business law.

## Responsibility to everyone the business touches

**Responsibility.** Corporate social responsibility owed in turn to society at large, suppliers, lenders, shareholders, employees, customers and government, with Nigerian examples.

**Responsibility.** Areas of social responsibility: responsibility towards employees (fair remuneration, safe working environment, career development), towards investors, towards the environment (pollution control, wise use of raw materials, sustainable production), towards consumers, towards the community and towards the government, together with ethical and legal responsibility (Nepal's book.)

**Responsibility.** Ethical principles for business: integrity (the foundation stone), fairness, transparency (clear, true, understandable information to employees, customers and stakeholders; never deceiving customers), responsibility, respect towards stakeholders, environmental responsibility, and compliance with local, national and international laws and regulations (Nepal's book.)

**Responsibility.** Business ethics and corporate social responsibility introduced together: ethics is directly joined to CSR — the ethical business reduces environmental harm through sustainable practice, joins community development and keeps ethics through its supply chain — and ethics is treated as an indispensable element of long-term success in the modern business environment (Nepal's book.)

**Responsibility.** The benefit to society: the qualitative evaluation of the project weighs its social and community benefits — increased community production, employment generated and needs covered — alongside the financial numbers (Ecuador's book.)

**Responsibility.** Ventures with social interest: community and associative ventures that use nature's gifts to cover a community's needs with a social and environmental component, guided by three innovation questions (why is this done, what if it were not done, what if it were done differently) (Ecuador's book.)

## Corporate social responsibility

A firm's corporate social responsibility in action: the book's supermarket-chain case shows scholarships and community support presented as part of the business's CSR agenda — "good community relations means good business"

## Ethics and the law in business

Legal vs ethical issues in setting up and running a business (registration, licences, taxes, waste disposal, truthful advertising, money laundering, product safety), the principles to adopt (legitimate firm and capital, code of ethics, environmental and data-handling policies) and the consequences table for unethical/illegal practice (fines, closure, lawsuits, imprisonment, lost reputation)

## Environmental protection

Business and the environment: laws exist to stop businesses polluting water and air, contaminating land with hazardous chemicals and dumping waste to cut costs; Environment Agencies implement the policy (Jamaica's NEPA — managing air pollution, monitoring endangered species, environmental impact assessments, permits and licences); international agreements and the UN Sustainable Development Goals; and why complying benefits a business — lower energy, materials and water costs, recycling income, avoided clean-up costs, a stronger brand, easier investment and access to government contracts

## Green technology and waste

Green technology and sustainable business practice: green technology and sustainability defined; how businesses protect the environment — alternatives to fossil fuels, energy efficiency, recyclable products, responsible waste management; government tools — environmentally preferred purchasing, green building policy, tax incentives for green business (Barbados), reforestation; waste management in the Caribbean (Site Waste Management Plans and their cost savings, Barbados's sanitary landfill and recycling, medical waste treatment, re-refining used oil in Antigua); and zoning laws that separate commercial, residential, industrial and agricultural land use

**Responsibility / judging a venture's social worth.** Social evaluation of a venture: a venture is judged not only on profit and payback but on social goals and impact indicators (education level, employment, unemployment in the community), with key questions the entrepreneur must ask about the problems it eases (Ecuador's book.)

**Responsibility / the venture's principles.** The venture's values: the ethical principles its work rests on, drawn from the questions what do we want to achieve, how are we, what do we believe in and how do we want to be remembered; the book lists the entrepreneur's own traits as the seed of them — creative, honest, transparent, prudent, bold, risk-taking (Nicaragua's book.)

**Responsibility / the environment.** The environmental feasibility study: production activity can harm the environment people live in (factory smoke, waste, polluted water, noise, dust), so the project needs a study of the mutual effect between project and environment; (Palestine's book.)
**Business ethics.** Professional ethics for accountants: what professional ethics and a professional code of ethics are, and the Indonesian Institute of Accountants (IAI) code's basic principles — integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality and professional behaviour — taught through the case of an accountant refusing a client's bribe (Indonesia's book.)

**Culture-and-ethics-of-a-business.** Business culture (cultura empresarial): the individual or collective actions and works undertaken to procure economic and social wellbeing; when a business is created, the founder's own culture — customs, attitudes, values, ways of thinking and doing — marks its decisions and projections, and the business spirit turns that purpose, foundation and values into the rules and norms that will govern the firm; inside business culture sits ethics — acting with decency and honesty, a fair labour and environmental policy, care for the wellbeing of workers, their families and the communities, good relations with clients and suppliers, and meeting tax, labour and environmental obligations; a firm's main end is to produce profits and be rentable, but that does not conflict with its social end, which grows with its size, and even a small venture's actions, however simple, impact its clients, community and family (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Social-ends.** What marks a social venture: social problems are its objective — where private entrepreneurs create firms from zero and guarantee their sustainability with intelligent solutions, social entrepreneurs focus on society's problems, using management techniques, innovation and creativity to maximise a community's social capital, transforming people's lives and adding value to the community; they become recruiters, seeking society's support to potentiate projects and spread the improvements as widely as possible; the key factor that distinguishes a social-entrepreneurship project is the existence of social ends — whether unique, principal, important or simply secondary — with the target social group both benefiting and being implicated and committed in the process; and its benefits are of two types, direct (services, scholarships, infrastructure) and indirect (employment, social integration) (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Ethics part.** Entrepreneurial ethics defined, and ethics toward customers: be customer-oriented, produce the best goods/services, keep quality, never cheat in buying and selling, give correct product information and charge a fair price (Malaysia's book.)

**Ethics part.** Ethics toward the other parties: suppliers (a trusted supplier as the business's key asset), the environment (preservation and the 5R concept), workers (fair pay), society, religion and the nation (Malaysia's book.)

**Ethics part.** Ethical business practices and social responsibilities: ethics as society's accepted moral values and conduct, ethical behaviour improving public image, consumer satisfaction and stakeholder trust, and the business's social responsibility to meet society's claims and expectations (Bhutan's book.)

**Ethics in the project.** Unit 9.4: ethical practice in the small business project — honest records, honest money management, and respect for customary taboos (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**What ethics means.** Strand 1 Unit 2: ethics defined, alongside the comparison of ownership forms (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Ethics.** Strand 2 Unit 4: business ethics — its definition, its principles (honesty, integrity, promise-keeping, loyalty, fairness, respect), ethical against unethical behaviour, ethics against law, and the role of ethics (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Values.** Strand 2 Unit 4: business attitudes and values — developing values into principles and into behaviours (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Marketing ethics.** Strand 3: the ethical issues in marketing — unfair or deceptive practices, offensive materials, ethical product and distribution practices, and the special issues in marketing to children, to minorities, and in the portrayal of women (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Ethics in practice.** Strand 4: facilitating ethical behaviour in business — ethical behaviour and the loyal customer; ethics programmes, codes and policies; managers as role models; the main responsibilities to stakeholders; change management; social justice; ecological sustainability; compliance with the law; codes of practice (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Ethics online.** Strand 5: strategies for facilitating ethical business behaviour in e-business — ethical behaviour defined; business ethics at three levels (personal, professional, organisational) and how they link; establishing a code of ethics and training employees in ethical decisions; the ethical categories that can harm (fraud, sustainability, diversity, exploitation) (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Internet fraud.** Strand 5: fraudulent behaviour and practices on the internet — false or fraudulent data (biased sponsorships and small sample sizes, data seized in leaks and manipulated) and investment scams such as 'pump and dump' (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Values.** The value of work: work as dignified and worthwhile in itself (Mozambique's book.)

**Environment.** The environment and global problems as the producer's responsibility (Mozambique's book.)

**Effluent.** Effluent from production processes: what production throws out and its harm (Mozambique's book.)

**Cleaner-production.** Cleaner production (produção mais limpa): producing with less waste and harm (Mozambique's book.)

**Sustainability.** Sustainable exploitation of resources: using resources without destroying them (Mozambique's book.)


**Business ethics and responsibility.** What business ethics is and how it shows: the set of basic moral principles and standards expressed in each field of business, which adjust, judge and guide the behaviour of those in business; the Vietnam Chamber of Commerce and Industry's six ethical rules for Vietnamese entrepreneurs (create value for society; obey the law; be transparent, fair and upright; create, co-operate and develop together; respect nature and protect the environment; love the country and be responsible to society and family); and its expressions in the trader's qualities — responsibility, honesty and keeping one's word, principle (following the unit's rules and keeping secrets), respect for people (staff, customers and competitors alike), and joining the enterprise's interest with the interests of customers and society. (Vietnam's book.)

**Business ethics and responsibility.** The role of business ethics: practised well, it turns traders' behaviour in a positive direction, raises the enterprise's quality and reputation, satisfies customers and builds a strong economy — taught through the áo-dài shopkeeper whose after-hours care of one foreign customer made her shop a trusted brand, and the industrial firms whose COVID-19 support for workers bound them to the firm for the recovery. (Vietnam's book.)

**Business ethics and responsibility.** Corporate social responsibility and its four forms: CSR is the enterprise's commitment to contribute to sustainable development by raising the quality of life of its workers and their families, the community and society, in a way that benefits both the enterprise and society's general development (the World Bank definition the book quotes); it is carried out as economic responsibility (efficient business, jobs and incomes, quality and safe products), legal responsibility (obeying the law on tax, environment and consumer protection), ethical responsibility (doing business ethically, treating workers fairly, harming neither society nor the environment) and humanitarian responsibility (charity, humanitarian and public-benefit activities for the community). (Vietnam's book.)

**Business ethics and responsibility.** Why carrying out CSR matters, and the citizen-manager's own duties: for society it supports sustainable national development, helps solve community difficulties and protects the environment; for the enterprise it raises quality, brand value and reputation, builds the trust of the public and its workers and strengthens competitiveness; and the citizen who helps run an enterprise must obey the law, protect workers' safety and lawful interests, guarantee product quality and consumers' interests, and build the firm's community-responsibility policies to fit its conditions. (Vietnam's book.)
