# Business Law and Regulation

## Do it first

Watch one sale today — or make a small one. Ask: what was offered, what was accepted, what was paid (the consideration)? You have just seen a contract form. Then name the law standing behind that sale if the goods turned out faulty. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What business law is

**Opening.** What business law is and the fields it covers (contract, agency, employment, sale of goods, hire purchase, government regulation, and more).

## Sub lesson 2 — Contracts — made, kept valid, and ended

**Contract.** How a contract is formed: invitation to treat, offer and acceptance; express and implied terms; conditions versus warranties; formalities — deeds, contracts that must be in writing, contracts needing written evidence.

**Validity.** Consideration, who has capacity to contract (minors, drunkards, the mentally disordered), and the invalid contracts: unenforceable, voidable, void and illegal, with mistake, misrepresentation, duress and undue influence.

**Contract.** Ending a contract (agreement, performance, breach, frustration, lapse of time) and the remedies for breach: damages, refusal of further performance, rescission, specific performance, injunction, quantum meruit.

**Contracts in business.** Contracts: a voluntary, legally binding agreement (written, verbal or implied) between parties — suppliers, employees, customers, government; the book works through the contracts a business makes daily and what supplier/employment/customer contracts must set out

**Elements of a contract.** The essential elements of a valid contract taught in full: offer (certain, communicated, express or implied; how an offer is terminated — rejection, death, insanity, destruction, counter-offer, expiry, revocation), acceptance (must be certain and communicated; the postal rule; a counter-offer is not acceptance), consideration (present/future, of some value, never past), invitation to treat distinguished from an offer (catalogues, displays, advertisements, tenders, auctions)

**Intention and capacity.** Intention to create legal relations (vs social agreements) and competence/capacity: minors (bound only for necessaries), persons intoxicated or of unsound mind, and persons under duress lack full capacity

**Types of contract.** Simple contracts vs specialty contracts (deeds: signed, sealed and delivered, enforceable without consideration; used for land, mortgages, insurance), contracts of record, void and voidable contracts (illegality, misrepresentation, duress, undue influence)

**Discharge and remedies.** How contracts end: discharge by performance, breach, agreement, impossibility/frustration, lapse of time and death; and the remedies for breach — damages, specific performance, injunction, restitution

## Sub lesson 3 — Agency, employment, sale of goods and hire purchase

**Agency.** Agency: how it is created (express agreement, implication, necessity, ratification, estoppel), actual versus ostensible authority, special/general/universal agents, the duties and rights of agent and principal, and how agency ends.

**Employment.** Contract of service versus contract for service, and the two-way duties of employer and employee (pay, safety, indemnity; care, obedience, loyalty) including vicarious liability.

**Sale of goods.** Sale of goods law: implied terms, when ownership passes from seller to buyer, sale by non-owners and the nemo dat rule with its exceptions, the unpaid seller's rights and the buyer's remedies.

**Hire purchase.** Hire purchase in law: its three-part nature (bailment, option, sale), the finance company's place in the deal, the hirer's right to cancel and how the agreement terminates.

**Employment law.** Employment law: it covers everything from employment contracts to dismissal — a healthy and safe working environment, the right to belong to a trade union, pay statements, maternity/adoption/paternity rights and pay, and protection against discrimination (race, age, disability, religion, sex, marital status and more), with employers also liable for discrimination by their staff or visitors; job security and severance law — dismissal needs a valid reason, a full investigation, consistency, and the contractual or statutory notice period, with summary dismissal only for gross misconduct

## Sub lesson 4 — Registering, complying — and deregulation

**Formation-law.** The Acts under which a business entity is formed in Malaysia (business registration, partnership and companies legislation).

**Compliance.** The responsibilities an entrepreneur must keep meeting after registering (licences, renewals and legal obligations).

**Registration.** Registering a business in Nigeria: business-name registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission, partnership and cooperative-society registration, and incorporating a limited liability company (memorandum, articles, certificate of incorporation).

**Regulation.** Deregulation: the scope of economic regulation (price ceilings and floors, quotas, licensing), why it was imposed, why it is later undone, and the advantages and disadvantages of undoing it.

**Regulation.** Nigeria's own case study: regulation of petroleum products (PPPRA licensing, the Petroleum Support Fund and Equalisation Fund, uniform pump prices) and the case for deregulating the sector.

**Government regulation of business.** Government regulation of the establishment and conduct of businesses: the areas regulated (licences, advertising, product safety and labelling, health and safety, taxes, accounts, waste, employment contracts, planning), the agencies involved (Fair Trading Commission, Bureau of Standards, Consumer Affairs Commission) and the consequences of non-compliance (reputation damage, compensation claims, licence withdrawn, imprisonment)

**Regulation and deregulation.** Regulation of business activity: government regulations affect every aspect of running a business — health and safety, premises, working conditions, exporting, accounting and the environment — with compliance costs (new technology, training, altered premises) and penalties for breaking them (fines, clean-up costs, reputational damage); the rules a start-up must follow (business structure, registration, licences and permits, insurance, premises, storing personal information, employing people, paying taxes); the balance between fair regulation and bureaucracy that inhibits growth; and deregulation — removing unnecessary rules to cut costs and drive competition, with the risk of going too far

## Sub lesson 5 — Ideas, data and digital business in law

**Intellectual-property.** Procedures for managing technology resources, taught the Malaysian way through protecting patents and copyright as business assets.

**Intellectual property.** Government protection of ideas under Nigerian law: patents (what can be patented, rights, infringement, duration as printed), industrial designs, trademarks and copyright.

**Digital-law.** The Acts that govern digital-technology business in Malaysia (electronic commerce and related legislation).

**Personal-data.** What personal data is, with examples.

**Protection.** Why personal data must be protected.

**Intellectual property.** Intellectual property rights: what intellectual property is and the four protections — patents (inventions, usually 20 years), copyright (expression of an idea), trademarks and industrial design rights — and why businesses protect them (differentiation, licensing revenue, collateral, encouraging innovation)

**The legal rules.** Workplace safety as law: Ecuador's Labour Code arts. 410–411 oblige employers to give workers conditions that do not endanger health or life, and workers to follow the prevention, safety and hygiene measures (Ecuador's book.)

**The legal rules.** Business tax in Nepal and the PAN: corporate/business tax works by self-assessment; the Permanent Account Number (PAN) is the unique and permanent nine-digit code identifying each taxpayer, issued by the Inland Revenue Department and kept for life whatever changes in the business; the PAN registration process — filling the application form, verification of documents and of information (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** Indirect taxes: charged on goods and services and borne by the consumer inside the price, so the payer and the bearer differ — value added tax (charged at every stage from production to distribution on the value added; Nepal's rate 13%), customs duty and excise duty — with direct and indirect tax compared on shifting, the feeling of burden, rates and examples (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** Direct taxes: paid straight to the government by the person taxed and impossible to shift to another — income tax (charged at progressive rates on the year's earnings, deducted in advance from salary payments), house and land tax, property/wealth tax, corporate tax, interest tax, vehicle tax, rent tax and windfall gain tax (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** What tax is: a compulsory payment to the state from which the taxpayer can expect no direct benefit; its features — compulsory payment, indirect benefit, the government's important source of revenue, law-based (levied by act and rules without discrimination), progressive in nature (more income, property and profit pays at a higher rate), redistribution of wealth, and variety (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** Winding up a company: voluntary winding-up, in which a liquidator (with an auditor) takes control of the company's assets, accounts and records, files income-and-expenditure accounts every six months, pays creditors, distributes the remainder to shareholders and reports to the Registrar until the name is struck off; and court-ordered winding-up under the Insolvency Act, 2063 BS (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** The company registration process in Nepal: choose private or public, choose a distinct name on the Office of the Company Registrar's online portal, prepare the Memorandum, Articles, application and founders' citizenship copies, pay the fee scaled to authorised capital, receive the certificate of incorporation (within seven days), and, for a public company, the certificate of commencement before trading (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** The company's founding documents: the Memorandum of Association — the company's main legal document, like its birth certificate, stating name, registered office address, objectives and scope, powers and the capital structure (Companies Act, 2063 BS); the Articles of Association — the internal rules (directors, meetings, records); and the Prospectus — the document a public company must publish before selling shares or securities to the public, which a private company does not need (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** Registering and renewing a partnership firm in Nepal: registration with the concerned department within six months of formation under the Partnership Act, 2020 BS, fees scaled to capital, and renewal within 35 days of the end of each fiscal year with tax-clearance certificate and audit report (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** Rights and duties of partners: rights — to share profit, to express an opinion, to participate in the business, to be indemnified, to use the firm's property; duties — to share losses, to act faithfully and transparently, to present true accounts to any partner, and to compensate the firm for loss caused by one's own fraud (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** The partnership deed (कबुलियतनामा) and its contents: name and address of the firm, names and addresses of the partners, capital contributions, sharing of profit and loss (equally, by capital ratio, or as agreed), remuneration of working partners, accounts and audit, and other provisions (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** Nepal's own way — registering a sole-trader (private) firm under the Private Firm Registration Act, 2014 BS: application to the Department of Commerce, Supplies and Consumer Protection (trade firms) or the Department of Industry (industry firms), registration fee scaled to capital (with a 50% concession for women, Dalit and deprived-class owners), checking of documents and issue of the certificate; yearly renewal, and the consequences of not renewing (fines, no bank account or loans, tax-evasion exposure) (Nepal's book.)

**The legal rules.** Pre-operational expenses: the set-up costs of complying with Ecuadorian law to create a company — notary payments, the municipal patent (operating permit), and fees to the lawyers who constitute the venture (Ecuador's book.)

**The legal rules / formalising.** Why formalise: a formalised small or medium business gains access to all the programmes, rights and incentives of the MIPYME law (Ley N° 645, Ley de Promoción, Fomento y Desarrollo de la Micro, Pequeña y Mediana Empresa); (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / taxes.** The fiscal benefits of being formal: tax benefits from the official list; exports of nationally produced goods and services rendered abroad carry a 0% VAT (IVA) rate; a tax credit of 1.5% of the FOB value of exports can be applied against advance or annual income tax with the tax administration's approval; (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / where to register.** The Ventanilla Única de Inversiones (VUI): the one-stop business formalisation centre where a business registers under the general regime as a natural or a legal person; it gives free orientation, advice and technical assistance, manages the applications (requirements, costs, fiscal and legal aspects, additional permits) and hands over the documents needed to operate (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / registering step by step.** The formalisation route and its times through the VUI: step 1 at the VUI itself (24 hours for expedited procedures, 5 days ordinary), step 2 the Dirección General de Ingresos (DGI) issuing the RUC (8 days for legal persons, half a day with the DUR for natural persons), step 3 the municipal mayor's office (about 5 days) — all of it also doable online through the VUI (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / registering step by step.** The registration procedures themselves (the book's "for information only" section): (Palestine's book.)

**The legal rules / tax registration.** Getting the RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyente) as a natural person on the fixed-quota regime: be 18, present the citizen identity document (with the rules the book gives for nationals, nationals living abroad and foreigners), prove the fiscal domicile with a basic-services bill or lease contract, and if a third party files, a simple written authorisation (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / tax registration.** RUC registration on the general regime and for legal persons: the same identity and fiscal-domicile proof (a notarial declaration when the bills are not in the applicant's name), professional title or graduation letter for professional services, a notarised special power when a third party files; (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / local licence.** The municipal licence (matrícula): anyone, natural or legal, who carries on an economic activity must notify their municipality and request the licence, which first requires the RUC; (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / health licence.** The sanitary licence (MINSA): food, drink, pharmaceutical and cosmetics ventures apply at their nearest health centre, receive the application form and an inspection of the premises (within about 20 days), then file the mayor's licence, fire inspection certificate, fumigation certificate, health certificates and fee; (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / registering a product.** Sanitary registration of a product: (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / sector licences.** Tourism-sector licences (INTUR): (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / sector licences.** Fishing-sector formalisation (INPESCA): (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / environmental permits.** Environmental permits under Decreto 20-2017 (the environmental evaluation system for permits and authorisations for sustainable use of natural resources): (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / protecting the brand.** Registering a trademark: a trademark is the symbol that identifies your product or service in the market and makes it different from the rest; (Nicaragua's book.)

**The legal rules / registering.** Palestine's own registration map: before starting, the investor must know every body whose approval the project needs, or legal gaps can lose him the project; the bodies and what each gives — the local authority (municipality/village council) for the crafts licence (رخصة الحرف) covering trades touching public health, safety and veterinary supervision; (Palestine's book.)
**The rules a business works under.** Work guidelines, procedures and work rules in the financial-services industry: what work guidelines, work procedures (SOP) and work systems are and why a financial institution works by them (Indonesia's book.)

**The rules a business works under.** The financial-services industry's own marks and its work rules, in the guide's essential-material summary: a financial-service company buys and resells no merchandise, produces no goods, holds no raw-material stock, spends mostly on staff operations, and holds cash, receivables and fixed assets; work guidelines function to order that work, a work procedure (tata kerja) is the efficient way of… (Indonesia's teacher guide.)

**Publishing-the-articles.** Publication of the articles: an unpublished articles document is formally non-existent toward third parties yet binds absolutely the partners who signed it; the same holds for every amendment; after signing, the articles go to the competent tax office (Δ.Ο.Υ.) with the capital-concentration tax declaration (Greece's book.)

**Who-may-trade.** The limitations on practising trade: objective limits — activities that need a special operating licence (banking, insurance, stockbroking, pharmacy, commercial agency, broking, travel offices, exporting, industry) — and subjective limits — who has the legal capacity to trade and which professions are incompatible with trader status; multi-level marketing noted (Greece's book.)

**Legislation part.** Business legislation: what an Act is (law drafted, passed and gazetted by Parliament) and the purposes business law serves in keeping enterprise activity lawful and protecting customers, suppliers and workers (Malaysia's book.)

**Production-law part.** The production-side Acts: the Environmental Quality Act 1974 (Amendment 2012) with its EIA machinery, and the Copyright Act 1987 — intellectual property (copyright, trademarks, designs) taught as an intangible business asset (Malaysia's book.)

**Sales-law part.** The sales-side Acts: the Sale of Goods Act 1957 governing contracts for the sale of goods wholesale and retail, the Sales Tax Act 2018 (single-stage tax) and the Service Tax Act 2018 (Malaysia's book.)

**Data-protection part.** The Personal Data Protection Act 2010: the law protecting personal and sensitive personal data processed for commercial transactions (Malaysia's book.)

**Licensing part.** The licences, permits and certificates a business must hold: licence (12-month permission, renewed yearly), permit (temporary, up to six months) and certificate (recognition of practice, e.g. GMP, HACCP and the JAKIM Halal certificate) defined and listed, with why each matters (Malaysia's book.)

**Government-and-law part.** The government's role on the legal side: Parliament makes the business laws so trade runs smoothly, consumer-protection laws stop traders misleading buyers on quality and price, and tax reliefs (pioneer status, investment tax allowance, deductions and exemptions) encourage people into chosen businesses (Malaysia's book.)

**Law-effects part.** How business laws press on the environment: health-and-safety law (fire rules, worker food protection), company law and advertising law that must keep pace with promotion practice (Malaysia's book.)
