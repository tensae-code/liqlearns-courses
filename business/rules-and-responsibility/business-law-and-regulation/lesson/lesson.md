# Business Law and Regulation

## Do it first

Watch one sale today — or make a small one. Ask: what was offered, what was accepted, what was paid (the consideration)? You have just seen a contract form. Then name the law standing behind that sale if the goods turned out faulty. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What business law is

**Opening.** What business law is and the fields it covers (contract, agency, employment, sale of goods, hire purchase, government regulation, and more).

## Sub lesson 2 — Contracts — made, kept valid, and ended

**Contract.** How a contract is formed: invitation to treat, offer and acceptance; express and implied terms; conditions versus warranties; formalities — deeds, contracts that must be in writing, contracts needing written evidence.

**Validity.** Consideration, who has capacity to contract (minors, drunkards, the mentally disordered), and the invalid contracts: unenforceable, voidable, void and illegal, with mistake, misrepresentation, duress and undue influence.

**Contract.** Ending a contract (agreement, performance, breach, frustration, lapse of time) and the remedies for breach: damages, refusal of further performance, rescission, specific performance, injunction, quantum meruit.

**Contracts in business.** Contracts: a voluntary, legally binding agreement (written, verbal or implied) between parties — suppliers, employees, customers, government; the book works through the contracts a business makes daily and what supplier/employment/customer contracts must set out

**Elements of a contract.** The essential elements of a valid contract taught in full: offer (certain, communicated, express or implied; how an offer is terminated — rejection, death, insanity, destruction, counter-offer, expiry, revocation), acceptance (must be certain and communicated; the postal rule; a counter-offer is not acceptance), consideration (present/future, of some value, never past), invitation to treat distinguished from an offer (catalogues, displays, advertisements, tenders, auctions)

**Intention and capacity.** Intention to create legal relations (vs social agreements) and competence/capacity: minors (bound only for necessaries), persons intoxicated or of unsound mind, and persons under duress lack full capacity

**Types of contract.** Simple contracts vs specialty contracts (deeds: signed, sealed and delivered, enforceable without consideration; used for land, mortgages, insurance), contracts of record, void and voidable contracts (illegality, misrepresentation, duress, undue influence)

**Discharge and remedies.** How contracts end: discharge by performance, breach, agreement, impossibility/frustration, lapse of time and death; and the remedies for breach — damages, specific performance, injunction, restitution

## Sub lesson 3 — Agency, employment, sale of goods and hire purchase

**Agency.** Agency: how it is created (express agreement, implication, necessity, ratification, estoppel), actual versus ostensible authority, special/general/universal agents, the duties and rights of agent and principal, and how agency ends.

**Employment.** Contract of service versus contract for service, and the two-way duties of employer and employee (pay, safety, indemnity; care, obedience, loyalty) including vicarious liability.

**Sale of goods.** Sale of goods law: implied terms, when ownership passes from seller to buyer, sale by non-owners and the nemo dat rule with its exceptions, the unpaid seller's rights and the buyer's remedies.

**Hire purchase.** Hire purchase in law: its three-part nature (bailment, option, sale), the finance company's place in the deal, the hirer's right to cancel and how the agreement terminates.

**Employment law.** Employment law: it covers everything from employment contracts to dismissal — a healthy and safe working environment, the right to belong to a trade union, pay statements, maternity/adoption/paternity rights and pay, and protection against discrimination (race, age, disability, religion, sex, marital status and more), with employers also liable for discrimination by their staff or visitors; job security and severance law — dismissal needs a valid reason, a full investigation, consistency, and the contractual or statutory notice period, with summary dismissal only for gross misconduct

## Sub lesson 4 — Registering, complying — and deregulation

**Formation-law.** The Acts under which a business entity is formed in Malaysia (business registration, partnership and companies legislation).

**Compliance.** The responsibilities an entrepreneur must keep meeting after registering (licences, renewals and legal obligations).

**Registration.** Registering a business in Nigeria: business-name registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission, partnership and cooperative-society registration, and incorporating a limited liability company (memorandum, articles, certificate of incorporation).

**Regulation.** Deregulation: the scope of economic regulation (price ceilings and floors, quotas, licensing), why it was imposed, why it is later undone, and the advantages and disadvantages of undoing it.

**Regulation.** Nigeria's own case study: regulation of petroleum products (PPPRA licensing, the Petroleum Support Fund and Equalisation Fund, uniform pump prices) and the case for deregulating the sector.

**Government regulation of business.** Government regulation of the establishment and conduct of businesses: the areas regulated (licences, advertising, product safety and labelling, health and safety, taxes, accounts, waste, employment contracts, planning), the agencies involved (Fair Trading Commission, Bureau of Standards, Consumer Affairs Commission) and the consequences of non-compliance (reputation damage, compensation claims, licence withdrawn, imprisonment)

**Regulation and deregulation.** Regulation of business activity: government regulations affect every aspect of running a business — health and safety, premises, working conditions, exporting, accounting and the environment — with compliance costs (new technology, training, altered premises) and penalties for breaking them (fines, clean-up costs, reputational damage); the rules a start-up must follow (business structure, registration, licences and permits, insurance, premises, storing personal information, employing people, paying taxes); the balance between fair regulation and bureaucracy that inhibits growth; and deregulation — removing unnecessary rules to cut costs and drive competition, with the risk of going too far

## Sub lesson 5 — Ideas, data and digital business in law

**Intellectual-property.** Procedures for managing technology resources, taught the Malaysian way through protecting patents and copyright as business assets.

**Intellectual property.** Government protection of ideas under Nigerian law: patents (what can be patented, rights, infringement, duration as printed), industrial designs, trademarks and copyright.

**Digital-law.** The Acts that govern digital-technology business in Malaysia (electronic commerce and related legislation).

**Personal-data.** What personal data is, with examples.

**Protection.** Why personal data must be protected.

**Intellectual property.** Intellectual property rights: what intellectual property is and the four protections — patents (inventions, usually 20 years), copyright (expression of an idea), trademarks and industrial design rights — and why businesses protect them (differentiation, licensing revenue, collateral, encouraging innovation)
