# The Business Environment

## Do it first

Stand outside one business and look at its surroundings. Name one economic pressure on it (prices, customers' money), one technological, one social, and one political or legal. Then look at the ground and air around it: is its own environment clean and safe? That is this lesson.

## Keeping the business environment healthy

**Environment.** Keeping a healthy business environment: avoiding or reducing each kind of pollution (clean energy, treating/recycling effluent, proper waste disposal and recycling, silencers and ear defenders, protective goggles), ensuring security inside and outside the business, keeping required resources available, and maintaining good interpersonal relations with employees, government, customers, suppliers and the public.

## The four kinds of business environment

**Environment.** The business environment in four kinds — economic, technological, social and political/legal — and the elements of each that press on business decisions.

## The role of government

The government's role and its impact on business: government is consumer, producer, employer, law maker and regulator, and its taxes, spending and laws change the risks and opportunities businesses face (Caribbean examples — port infrastructure spending raising demand in Trinidad; St Lucia land legislation restricting where businesses can build); its responsibilities include security of the state, protection and general welfare of citizens (healthcare, education, transport, water, power), and a safe environment for business

## Government and investors

How government creates a safe environment for investors: the central bank can lower interest rates to stimulate lending and expansion; tax incentives boost fledgling markets and encourage start-ups (e.g. incentives for solar equipment); trade policies — lower tariffs and quotas and free-trade zones — cut business costs; government contracting puts public work out to private companies; grants, subsidies, loans and disbursements steer activity; and political stability, transparency and tackling corruption make a country trusted by investors

## Social services and infrastructure

Government-provided services and infrastructure as business conditions: governments fund socially and economically desirable services the private sector will not supply at affordable prices — healthcare (a healthy population is more productive and takes fewer days off), education (a skilled workforce and better employment prospects), National Insurance contributions funding pensions and benefits, and roads and transport networks, whose direct benefits to business are shorter delivery times, lower costs, and access to larger pools of labour, materials, markets, ports and airports

**The world the business sits in.** The venture and care of the environment: Ecuador's great biodiversity must be protected from profit-only interests, and the Constitution's articles on the rights of nature frame what a venture may do (Ecuador's book.)

**The world the business sits in.** The business environment and its components: the internal environment (organisational objectives and policies, structure, culture, resources) and the external environment, split into the task environment (customers, suppliers, competitors, government, financial institutions, pressure groups) and the general / macro environment (political and legal, economic, social-cultural, technological, natural and global environments) (Nepal's book.)

**The world the business sits in / analysing the venture.** The FODA (SWOT) analysis: its aim is to build up strengths, seize opportunities, reduce or eliminate weaknesses and neutralise threats; (Nicaragua's book.)

**The world the business sits in / green ventures.** MARENA's three national strategies aimed at entrepreneurs: registering and certifying wildlife-breeding farms (zoocriaderos) as nature-based income that reduces extraction of wild fauna; private wildlife reserves, where the entrepreneur conserves natural and water resources and diversifies the farm; (Nicaragua's book.)
**Market-forms.** Competition (ανταγωνισμός) defined as rivalry between traders pursuing the same aim in a market governed by supply and demand, and the four market forms it produces: perfect competition, monopoly (one seller, e.g. public utilities), oligopoly (a few sellers of like products, e.g. car industries) and monopolistic competition (many sellers of slightly differentiated products) (Greece's book.)

**Sustainability-of-a-business.** Sustainability applied to an enterprise: sustainable development reconciles three pillars — economic, social and environmental — and an activity counts as economically sustainable when, moving towards environmental and social sustainability, it is financially possible and profitable; for a company the social pillar covers its workers (working conditions, wage level), its suppliers, its customers, the local communities and society in general (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Internal-environment part.** The internal environment elements an entrepreneur controls: human resources, organisation structure (the hierarchy/chain of command), finance (capital needs, control, budgets) and research and development (Malaysia's book.)

**External-environment part.** The external environment elements, Malaysia's own list: politics and law, economy, socioculture, technology, the natural environment, and the market actors — buyers, suppliers and competitors (Malaysia's book.)

**Analysis part.** Analysing the environment: scanning, monitoring and forecasting change; the effects of internal and external changes on the business set out in the book's tables, and finding solutions to those effects (Malaysia's book.)

**Sector-development part.** The development of Malaysia's business sector toward the 21st century: the rising contribution of the third (services) sector as the country moves toward developed status (Malaysia's book.)

**Trends part.** Business-environment trends framed as internal factors the organisation controls (management style, human resources, marketing, production, finance and accounting, information technology, R&D) against external factors, and the market defined as where seller and buyer transact at an agreed price and time, now also online (Malaysia's book.)

**Growth-trends part.** The trends of employment, income and economic growth: how growth raises business profit and tax, household income and government spending in a cycle, shown through the economic cycle diagram (Malaysia's book.)

**Effects part.** The effects of environment change on the business organisation: employment effects, new laws that protect business from fraud and from foreign dumping (selling below cost to drive small businesses out) (Malaysia's book.)

**Opening part.** Economics introduced: economics is about people and the choices they make; it is based on the activities of individuals and institutions producing, exchanging and consuming goods and services, and it deals with the major problem of scarcity — limited resources against unlimited wants — which drives us to make decisions (Samoa's book.)

**Systems part.** The four types of economic system compared by how they answer who makes the decisions, who owns the resources, what is produced, how it is produced and whether there is specialisation; the spectrum drawn from free market economy to planned economy (consumer influence against government influence); the features chart worked out in full — Free Enterprise (e.g. USA, Japan: private ownership of factors, little government intervention, profit motive, freedom of choice, consumer has greater influence), Planned (e.g. China: government ownership, almost total intervention, very little freedom of choice, consumer has only a small say), Mixed (e.g. Canada, NZ: government and private ownership, some intervention, some freedom of choice, consumer has a large say), Traditional (e.g. tribal Papua New Guinea: simple society, independent and isolated, decisions made by a chief) — and Samoa's own features summarised (government and private ownership, some intervention, some freedom of choice, consumer has a large say), Samoa and New Zealand both mixed economies, and Samoa's change from a traditional economy to a mixed economy (Samoa's book.)

**Producers part.** Local producers and service organisations (people who produce goods and provide services within their community — the farmers at Aleipata, a Women's Committee, Youth Clubs, Rugby Clubs) against national producers (people, groups or organisations who produce goods and services for the wider public) (Samoa's book.)

**Ecosystem part.** The business ecosystem concept (Moore): an economic community supported by a foundation of interacting organisations and individuals, producing goods and services of value to customers (Bhutan's book.)

**Ecosystem-in-Bhutan part.** Why a vibrant business ecosystem is needed, and the entrepreneurship ecosystem in Bhutan described as the country's own environment for new ventures (Bhutan's book.)

**Selection factors part.** The factors weighed when selecting an opportunity, including the economic factors: the purchasing power of the target market determines the demand for the products and services (Bhutan's book.)

**Business environment.** The external and internal factors that decide whether entrepreneurship can be realised: external — natural-demographic, socio-cultural, technological, economic, institutional, legal and political; internal — property relations and their guarantees, the forms of ownership, and the firm's own organisation and management (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Location part of the lesson (Kazakhstan's own examples).** Geography and region decide what business will work: climate, distance from water and borders, ethnic and cultural makeup, soil, population size and age, religion, average wages and big employers form the demand in a place (air-conditioners sell in Shymkent, not Pavlodar; a window plant belongs by fast-growing Nur-Sultan, with the workshop-in-the-region versus in-the-city choice settled by a feasibility study of rent against delivery cost) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Environment.** The business and its environments in one diagram: at the centre the business, ringed by its internal environment (business structure, owners, business resources, business culture), and outside it the external environments it must reckon with — demographic, physical, economic, competitive, social-cultural, legal-political and technological (Kenya's book.)

**The environment.** Strand 1 Unit 2: the business environment — internal (structure, management, policies, processes, planning, staff), operating (customers, suppliers, competitors, interest groups) and macro (social, political/legal, economic — interest rates, taxes/GST, inflation, employment; technological) (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Development-context.** Human development indicators and the relation between population growth and development: the development setting a business grows in (the book's two lessons taught as one context sequence) (Mozambique's book.)
