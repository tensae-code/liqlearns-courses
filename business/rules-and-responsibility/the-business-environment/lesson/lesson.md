# The Business Environment

## Do it first

Stand outside one business and look at its surroundings. Name one economic pressure on it (prices, customers' money), one technological, one social, and one political or legal. Then look at the ground and air around it: is its own environment clean and safe? That is this lesson.

## Keeping the business environment healthy

**Environment.** Keeping a healthy business environment: avoiding or reducing each kind of pollution (clean energy, treating/recycling effluent, proper waste disposal and recycling, silencers and ear defenders, protective goggles), ensuring security inside and outside the business, keeping required resources available, and maintaining good interpersonal relations with employees, government, customers, suppliers and the public.

## The four kinds of business environment

**Environment.** The business environment in four kinds — economic, technological, social and political/legal — and the elements of each that press on business decisions.

## The role of government

The government's role and its impact on business: government is consumer, producer, employer, law maker and regulator, and its taxes, spending and laws change the risks and opportunities businesses face (Caribbean examples — port infrastructure spending raising demand in Trinidad; St Lucia land legislation restricting where businesses can build); its responsibilities include security of the state, protection and general welfare of citizens (healthcare, education, transport, water, power), and a safe environment for business

## Government and investors

How government creates a safe environment for investors: the central bank can lower interest rates to stimulate lending and expansion; tax incentives boost fledgling markets and encourage start-ups (e.g. incentives for solar equipment); trade policies — lower tariffs and quotas and free-trade zones — cut business costs; government contracting puts public work out to private companies; grants, subsidies, loans and disbursements steer activity; and political stability, transparency and tackling corruption make a country trusted by investors

## Social services and infrastructure

Government-provided services and infrastructure as business conditions: governments fund socially and economically desirable services the private sector will not supply at affordable prices — healthcare (a healthy population is more productive and takes fewer days off), education (a skilled workforce and better employment prospects), National Insurance contributions funding pensions and benefits, and roads and transport networks, whose direct benefits to business are shorter delivery times, lower costs, and access to larger pools of labour, materials, markets, ports and airports

**The world the business sits in.** The venture and care of the environment: Ecuador's great biodiversity must be protected from profit-only interests, and the Constitution's articles on the rights of nature frame what a venture may do (Ecuador's book.)

**The world the business sits in.** The business environment and its components: the internal environment (organisational objectives and policies, structure, culture, resources) and the external environment, split into the task environment (customers, suppliers, competitors, government, financial institutions, pressure groups) and the general / macro environment (political and legal, economic, social-cultural, technological, natural and global environments) (Nepal's book.)

**The world the business sits in / analysing the venture.** The FODA (SWOT) analysis: its aim is to build up strengths, seize opportunities, reduce or eliminate weaknesses and neutralise threats; (Nicaragua's book.)

**The world the business sits in / green ventures.** MARENA's three national strategies aimed at entrepreneurs: registering and certifying wildlife-breeding farms (zoocriaderos) as nature-based income that reduces extraction of wild fauna; private wildlife reserves, where the entrepreneur conserves natural and water resources and diversifies the farm; (Nicaragua's book.)
**Market-forms.** Competition (ανταγωνισμός) defined as rivalry between traders pursuing the same aim in a market governed by supply and demand, and the four market forms it produces: perfect competition, monopoly (one seller, e.g. public utilities), oligopoly (a few sellers of like products, e.g. car industries) and monopolistic competition (many sellers of slightly differentiated products) (Greece's book.)
