# Business Risk and Insurance — depth

This lesson is divided into sub lessons, each proved before the next opens. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — insurance is the pooling of risk: many people pay a premium into one pool, and the pool pays the one who suffers the loss — it holds together only under its principles (utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity and the rest), and a business chooses the policy that matches each risk it faces. — in their own words, give one real example from their own life, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new home, market or street situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
