# Business Risk and Insurance

## Do it first

Imagine ten shopkeepers each put a small amount into a common pot every month. When fire destroys one shop, the pot pays for that loss. No shopkeeper could carry the loss alone; together they carried it easily. That pot is insurance. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Principles of insurance

**Principles of insurance.** Insurance works by pooling risk: the insured pays a premium into a pool, and the pool compensates whoever suffers the loss. Seven principles hold the pool together, taught one by one: utmost good faith (both sides tell the whole truth), insurable interest (you may insure only what you stand to lose by), indemnity (you are put back where you were, never better off — with over-insurance and under-insurance worked through), contribution (several insurers share one loss), subrogation (the insurer steps into your shoes to recover from whoever caused the loss), loss minimisation (you must act as if uninsured to limit the loss) and proximate cause (the loss must come from the nearest, insured cause).

## Sub lesson 2 — Types of business insurance

**Types of business insurance.** Insurance (against events that may happen) is distinguished from assurance (against events that must happen, like death); life insurance comes as whole life and endowment policies. The business policies cover the risks a firm actually faces: public/general liability, property, auto (third-party vs comprehensive), workers' compensation, professional liability, product liability, health, accident and sickness, business interruption and event insurance. Insurance facilitates business and trade itself: by transferring risk, it lets firms operate at all where one uninsured loss would end them.

**Risk in one's own business.** Risk analysis: listing the venture's potential risks in detail (worked example: community opposition to cutting trees for sports courts), then prioritising them by probability of occurrence and impact, and designing mitigation strategies so the entrepreneur is prepared (Ecuador's book.)

**Risk in one's own business.** The role of insurance in business: risk management (theft, fire, natural disaster, legal cases), business continuity, compliance with insurance law, financial stability (the insurer shares the burden of unexpected losses), protection of employees, building reputation and trust, and help with investment and expansion (Nepal's book.)

**Risk in one's own business.** The concept of business risk: the possibility of loss from unexpected events arising in the course of running a business; estimating such risk in advance and insuring against it is one method of risk management (Nepal's book.)

**Business risk.** The risks of entrepreneurial work and small projects: income is not guaranteed — the owner may not earn large revenues at first and may even lose; the entrepreneur may lose all or part of his investment; high stress from the fear of failure, lost deals and fierce competition; long, exhausting working hours that follow him home; (Palestine's book.)

**Business risk.** Managing risk in business: business is all about risk taking and management — do not fear the risk, assess and weigh it; risks are minimised by risk avoidance (measures that prevent the risk occurring, e.g. a driver not drinking and driving), risk reduction (measures that minimise the likely loss or the chances of it happening, e.g. (Uganda's book.)

**Business risk.** Assessing risks: the outcome of a risk is not known but must be evaluated — the greater the possible loss, the greater the risk; (Uganda's book.)

**Business risk.** What risk is, and its types: a risk is a situation where one must choose between two or more alternatives which may result in differing rewards for success or penalties for failure; business risk is the factors that may negatively affect a business's profitability or success; (Uganda's book.)
**Protection part.** Protection as a part of the financial plan: insurance or takaful bought to protect the entrepreneur, family and property against death, accident, theft and fire, and to keep the business running without fear of the risks (Malaysia's book.)

**Risk concepts part.** The concepts of risk management: identifying and handling the risks a business faces as a discipline of its own (Bhutan's book.)

**Risk process part.** The process of risk management drawn as the full loop: communication and consultation running alongside scope/context/criteria, risk identification, risk analysis, risk evaluation, risk treatment, monitoring and review, and recording (Bhutan's book.)

**Internal control part.** The internal control system: its three primary objectives — efficiency and effectiveness in operations (minimising wastage, misutilisation and theft) and the reliability and compliance objectives beside them (Bhutan's book.)
