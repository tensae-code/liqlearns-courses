# Hands-on — Business Risk and Insurance

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine ten shopkeepers each put a small amount into a common pot every month. When fire destroys one shop, the pot pays for that loss. No shopkeeper could carry the loss alone; together they carried it easily. That pot is insurance. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: insurance is the pooling of risk: many people pay a premium into one pool, and the pool pays the one who suffers the loss — it holds together only under its principles (utmost good faith, insurable interest, indemnity and the rest), and a business chooses the policy that matches each risk it faces. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
