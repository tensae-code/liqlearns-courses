# Hands-on — Capital Markets and the Stock Exchange

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine a company that needs money for a factory it will use for twenty years. A bank loan must be repaid soon; instead the company can sell shares to the public. Write down who gives the money first (the primary market) and who can buy those shares afterwards from the first buyers (the secondary market). That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: on the capital market a company raises long-term money in the primary market — by offer for subscription, offer for sale, rights issue, private placement or introduction — and investors then trade those securities in the secondary market, through brokers, under the exchange's discipline. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
