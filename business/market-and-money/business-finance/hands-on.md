# Hands-on — Business Finance

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three things a new business must pay for before it earns anything. Beside each, write where the money could come from: the owner's own money, profit kept back, a bank loan, a supplier's credit, or investors. You have just mapped the sources of finance. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: every business needs finance at each of its stages — from its own capital and retained profit, from bank credit, suppliers and customers, and for the long term from the capital market — and the finance skill is knowing the sources and choosing the one that fits the need. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
