# Business Finance

## Do it first

List three things a new business must pay for before it earns anything. Beside each, write where the money could come from: the owner's own money, profit kept back, a bank loan, a supplier's credit, or investors. You have just mapped the sources of finance. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why a business needs finance — and what profit is for

**Needs.** Why a business needs financing at each stage.

**Why businesses need finance.** What profit is for: a reward that keeps entrepreneurs going, retained (ploughed-back) profit as a permanent internal source of finance, long-run survival, and the tax the government takes from it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The sources of finance

**Opening.** The sources of finance available to a business, in overview.

**Types.** Internal versus external finance: the types in each family (own capital and retained profit against loans, hire purchase, shares and the rest).

**Business-functions.** The finance function of a business.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing a source — and obtaining it

**Comparison.** Strengths and weaknesses of each type of financing.

**Procedure.** The procedure for obtaining a source of financing.

**Choosing.** Justifying which type of financing suits starting a particular business.

## Sub lesson 4 — Credit and other ways of getting the use of money

**Sources of finance.** The map of business credit and its bank sources: loans (and the "triple C" of collateral, creditworthiness, character), overdrafts, bill discounting, debt factoring and acceptance credits.

**Sources of finance.** Credit from specialised government institutions (development corporations, loans boards), insurance-company policy and other loans, and cooperative society loans.

**Sources of finance.** Trade credit from suppliers (interest-free but with hidden cost and supply risk) and finance raised from customers through advance/progress payments.

**Sources of finance.** Getting the use of an asset without borrowing to buy it: ordinary leasing, sale and lease-back, and venture capital funds investing in small risky firms.

**Sources of finance.** Instalment credit arrangements compared: hire purchase (ownership passes only at the end), credit sale (ownership passes at once) and conditional sale.

## Sub lesson 5 — Long-term finance — the capital market as a source

**Sources of long-term finance.** The history of Nigeria's capital market told through the stock exchange: first securities 1946, Lagos Stock Exchange 1960, renaming as NSE 1977, All Share Index 1984, Second-tier Market 1985, CSCS clearing 1997, automated trading 1999, plus the Abuja exchange and the OTC market.

**Sources of finance.** How the market's referee evolved: Capital Issues Committee (1962), Capital Issues Commission (1973), Securities and Exchange Commission (1979), the Investment and Securities Act 1999 and the Investment and Securities Tribunal.

**Sources of finance.** The forces that grew the Nigerian capital market (indigenisation decrees, privatisation, bank consolidation, globalisation) and the challenges still holding it back (weak regulation and contract enforcement, low awareness).

**Sources of finance.** The capital market itself: what it is (long-term securities, against the money and foreign-exchange markets) and its structure of exchanges, regulators, arbitration, operators and transparency agencies.

**Sources of finance.** The Securities and Exchange Commission as apex regulator: its investor-protection functions (registration, investigation, enforcement, approving issues, mergers and unit trusts) and its market-development work.

**Sources of finance.** The people who work the market: issuing houses, underwriters, registrars, investment advisers, reporting accountants, solicitors, trustees, fund managers, receiving agents and banks, stockbrokers and dealer/market makers, and the CSCS clearing house.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: every business needs finance at each of its stages — from its own capital and retained profit, from bank credit, suppliers and customers, and for the long term from the capital market — and the finance skill is knowing the sources and choosing the one that fits the need. Give one real example from your own home, market or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: opening, types, needs, comparison. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
