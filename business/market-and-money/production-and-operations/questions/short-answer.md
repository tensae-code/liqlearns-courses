# Production and Operations — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about scale? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Telling the size of a firm: by number of employees, volume of output, floor area of premises, capital invested in assets, production methods/technology, size of market served and sales volume — and the weakness of each measure.

**2.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about scale? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Economies of scale as a firm grows: internal economies (marketing, financial, risk-bearing, managerial, technical, research and welfare) and external economies from the growth of the whole industry.

**3.** Explain the productivity part of this lesson in your own words. (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Production vs productivity: rate of production, productivity as the output/input ratio, labour productivity and average revenue productivity worked with numbers, why productivity matters (lower unit costs, competitiveness, profit, wages) and the ways to raise it — skilled labour and human-resource development, motivating employees, better organisation/management, lean production processes, better land use and better capital equipment

**4.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about quality in production? (Sub lesson 4)
**Answer:** The final product or service must keep the characteristics the customer values — quality, size, durability and other attributes — because a quality service wins contented customers who come back

**5.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about planning production? (Sub lesson 4)
**Answer:** Writing the plan, part 3 — the production programme: detailing how the product is made or the service delivered from the first stage to the final product — the resources needed, design of the work method, product design and development, location, quality, and the layout of offices and equipment

**6.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about kinds of production? (Sub lesson 4)
**Answer:** Types of industry: genetic industry (agriculture, animal husbandry, fisheries, plants), extractive industry (taking minerals and forest produce from nature), construction industry, and manufacturing industry in four kinds — analytical (one raw material split into many products, e.g. crude oil into petrol, diesel, aviation fuel), processing, synthetic, and assembling (joining parts made by others into watches, TVs, vehicles)

**7.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about what the business makes or does? (Sub lesson 4)
**Answer:** Product or service: a product is a physical good that has passed through stages to become what is sold (bread-making is the example), a service is the activity of doing something for another person (hairdressing), and a venture can do both — the custom-furniture maker whose product is the furniture and whose service is making and installing it for the particular client

**8.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about how production works / siting? (Sub lesson 4)
**Answer:** Choosing the project's location: the deciding factor differs by project — nearness to raw materials where transport of materials is costly (quarries) or the product perishes fast (milk, vegetables), nearness to centres of cheap labour for labour-intensive projects, nearness to the road network and transport, availability of land, of a suitable sewage network, or of cheap power;

**9.** In your own words, what does this lesson say about how production works / siting? (Sub lesson 4)
**Answer:** Location of a business: setting up in a particular area is one of the factors affecting business success, and the entrepreneur should choose an appropriate site;
