# Personal Finance and Budgeting

## Do it first

Write down everything you, or your family, received last month and everything it was spent on. Was anything left over? Where did the biggest spending sit? Making that list on purpose, before the month starts, and making the two sides balance, is a budget. That is this lesson.

## Budgeting and financial planning

A budget is a spending plan: it balances income against expenditure before the money is spent. Outgoings divide into fixed, regular ones (rent, school fees, loan payments) and flexible spending that can move (food beyond the basics, entertainment, extras). Income itself may vary — seasonal work pays in some months and not others. Budget analysis works the plan: find the peaks (heavy months) and troughs, work out the year's surplus, deficit or balance, prepare a budget forecast for the time ahead, cut spending to match income where the plan shows a deficit, and set savings targets the plan must protect.

## Ways of saving

Saving is putting money aside — for a rainy day, and for set goals. The book's ways: interest-bearing deposit accounts (your money earns interest; withdrawal limits and minimum balances apply); short-term fixed deposits (higher interest, but the money is locked until the term ends); and the sou-sou — an informal rotating savings club where members contribute equally and take the whole pool in turn. The sou-sou is unregulated and built entirely on trust: it works because every member knows every other member will keep paying in after their own turn has come.

**A person's own budgeting system.** The family budget (Educación financiera page): a monthly model budget listing income (wages, overtime, micro-business income) against outgoings (food, rent, education, debts, and more), ending in a monthly surplus or deficit, and sorting spending into essential, medium-importance and unforeseen (Ecuador's book.)

**A person's own budgeting system.** Forms of saving and the saving plan: savings are held by opening bank accounts, buying property (animals, houses), investing in business, buying land and buying shares in other businesses; (Uganda's book.)

**A person's own budgeting system.** Improving the family budget (Educación financiera page): the budget's parts — income against basic monthly spending, secondary spending, non-monthly outgoings, unforeseen payments, debt payments, investments and savings — with surplus as the goal and debt as the sign of deficit (Ecuador's book.)

**Borrowing wisely.** Optimising family debts (Educación financiera page): good or convenient debts — those that buy something that grows the family's wealth or earns future income, such as a mortgage, business inventory or children's education, "debts that pay themselves" — set against bad debts that only consume (Ecuador's book.)

**Borrowing wisely.** Using a credit card responsibly (Financia tu emprendimiento page): how a card purchase works (automatic authorisation, signed voucher), paying "corriente" (all next month) versus "diferido" (in monthly instalments), and the advice for using cards in purchases and in financing a new project (Ecuador's book.)

**A person's own money.** Saving and investment: saving is reserving one's earnings for future use — reducing current consumption to reserve a balance for future consumption or investment; investment is using savings productively — starting one's own business, buying productive resources (land, houses), buying shares in other businesses, buying treasury bills, depositing money in banks to earn interest; (Uganda's book.)

**A person's own money.** What determines saving and investment, and why people do them: savings are determined by availability of financial institutions, knowledge of the saving culture, clear goals for saving and high motivation to save; investment by availability of investment opportunities, favourable government policies and determination to work hard; (Uganda's book.)
**Personal-budget.** The personal budget as the exercise before budgeting a project: first identify the month's habitual income (salary, family contributions) and expenses (transport, food, telephone, internet); the balance is income minus expenses, and a positive balance can be destined to a savings account for unforeseen expenses or an investment; a personal budget lets you control your money so as not to fall into unnecessary debts (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Financial-management part.** The basics of the entrepreneur's financial management: what financial management is, why it matters, and what weak financial management does — bankruptcy risk, liabilities exceeding assets, being unable to borrow and losing the trust of partners, workers, customers and suppliers (Malaysia's book.)

**Credit-and-planning part.** Credit and debt management (asset financing by loan, personal loans, credit cards) and the financial planning plan: what the plan covers and why having one matters (Malaysia's book.)

**Plan-preparation part.** Preparing the financial plan in full: income split into active, passive and investment income; savings (including saving up the start-up capital itself); and the worked personal budget table of monthly and yearly outgoings (Malaysia's book.)

**Investment part.** The investment side of the plan: types of investment open to the entrepreneur (bank investment, cooperative investment for members, PNB products such as ASB and ASN) with their risk levels, and allowing for income tax and zakat inside the plan (Malaysia's book.)
