# Hands-on — Personal Finance and Budgeting

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down everything you, or your family, received last month and everything it was spent on. Was anything left over? Where did the biggest spending sit? Making that list on purpose, before the month starts, and making the two sides balance, is a budget. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a person controls their own money with a budget — a spending plan that balances income against expenditure, fixed outgoings first and flexible spending after — watched for its peaks and troughs, and with saving done on purpose toward set goals, in ways that run from a bank deposit account to the sou-sou. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
