# Hands-on — Marketing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one product sold near you. Answer four questions about it: What exactly is it (product)? What does it cost (price)? Where is it sold (place)? How do buyers hear of it (promotion)? Those four answers are its marketing mix. That is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: marketing is directing the flow of goods from producer to consumer — knowing the market through research first, then mixing product, price, place and promotion so purchasing power becomes effective demand. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at the market, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
