# Marketing

## Do it first

Take one product sold near you. Answer four questions about it: What exactly is it (product)? What does it cost (price)? Where is it sold (place)? How do buyers hear of it (promotion)? Those four answers are its marketing mix. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What marketing is

**What marketing is.** What marketing is (converting purchasing power into effective demand), its functions in reaching business goals, and its importance to the whole economy.

**Marketing concept.** The four orientations a business can take to customers — production, product, sales and market orientation — and the marketing concept in depth, including where it does not apply.

**Business-functions.** The marketing function of a business.

**Marketing-plan.** The marketing plan: drawing the parts of marketing into one plan (Mozambique's book.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Knowing the market — research first

**Market research.** Market versus marketing; market research versus the wider marketing research; push and pull promotion strategies.

**Market research.** Presenting the survey: after gathering the information, analyse it and prepare the findings in a written report; the information gathered guides the entrepreneur in preparing and writing a business plan if it shows the business will be profitable (Uganda's book.)

**Market research.** Conducting a market survey: it is collecting and using market information to determine the market for the products and identify its problems, gathering information personally from customers, suppliers and competitors and seeking experts' advice, with the findings prepared in a written report; (Uganda's book.)

**Market research.** Market and market survey: a market is all the buyers (people, institutions, businesses) who need the business's products and are willing and able to buy or pay for them; (Uganda's book.)

**Market research.** The market study: the market for the project's products is the basic factor in its success or failure; (Palestine's book.)

**Market research.** Market research as a feature of good management: before producing, establish that buyers exist, that the price still allows acceptable profit, that the market will last, how competition will be handled, that materials supply can be maintained, and that the location suits customers, delivery and visibility.

**Market research.** Market research as the first marketing activity: systematically collecting and analysing information on consumer preferences and market segmentation (dividing the market into groups by age, gender, income etc. to find the target market), consumer behaviour and spending patterns, and competition

**Branding.** Branding: what a brand is, what good branding does (legal protection, brand awareness, segmentation, brand image, brand loyalty), how a brand is created from research into the target audience, and social media as a brand-awareness tool

**Branding.** Designing the brand's logo: the best logos are simple; the design must be consistent with the message the business wants to send, memorable so consumers remember that you exist and why, and striking enough to stand out from competitors; (Nicaragua's book.)

**Branding.** Choosing the brand name: a brand is a name or word, often with a symbol, that identifies a venture's goods or services and differentiates them from the competition; the name must be chosen with care because once it is fixed in people's minds changing it is very hard and costly (Nicaragua's book.)

**Branding.** Choosing the business's name and trade mark (inside plan part 1): the name must fit the business's activity and goal, and famous companies' trade marks show the role a mark plays in a company's success (Iran's book.)

**Branding.** The brand and its parts: a brand as the intangible set of characteristics and impressions in consumers' heads; its verbal elements (name, slogan, brand legend), visual elements (trademark, logo, corporate colours, corporate font — with the tests a good element must pass: meaningful, memorable, legally protected, transferable), and ideological element (the brand idea); the brand platform and the branding complex that carries one idea through research, strategy, verbal and visual identity to implementation (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Market-study.** Notions of market study (estudo de mercado): studying the market before starting (Mozambique's book.)

## Sub lesson 3 — The marketing mix

**Marketing mix.** The marketing mix as a combination of product, price, place and promotion offered at a point in time, and market segmentation: dividing the market and matching a mix to each segment, with its criteria.

**The marketing mix in the book's four-part form.** Marketing as directing the flow of goods from producer to consumer through product, price, advertising and distribution; non-price factors (customer service, packaging, after-sales service, warranty) can matter more than undercutting competitors.

**Consumer behaviour.** Consumer behaviour: price factors (own price, prices of substitutes — close/perfect vs imperfect substitutes) and non-price factors (product quality in its four dimensions — features, durability, conformance, serviceability — taste, tradition/culture, income, spending patterns including demographics, seasonality and consumer confidence, and brand loyalty)

**Packaging.** Packaging and presentation: what good packaging does (brand recognition, easy distribution, customer information, protection of product and of the end user), the packaging materials compared (plastics, metal, Tetra Pak, cardboard, glass), and labelling regulation — the details the law requires on a label

**Marketing's environment.** Strand 3: how economic conditions, stakeholders' decisions, culture and technology affect marketing — inflation and purchasing power, disposable income, recession, interest rates and credit purchases, ecological forces, and technology shaping buying behaviour (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

## Sub lesson 4 — Product and price

**Product.** The product side of the mix: product mix and product lines, quality, branding, labelling, packaging, after-sales service, warranties, technical advice and home delivery.

**Price.** The price side of the mix: pricing objectives and the factors that move price; penetration and skimming on entry; full-cost, target-return and marginal-cost pricing, loss leaders, price discrimination and resale price maintenance.

**Product/price.** The product life cycle (introduction, growth, maturity, saturation, decline) and how costs, sales, profit and price behave at each stage.

**The marketing mix and product life cycle.** The marketing mix: product, price, place and promotion (the 4Ps, extended to 7Ps — people, processes, physical evidence — for services), each element supporting the others; and the product life cycle (introduction, growth, maturity, decline) and how marketing responds at each stage

**Pricing strategies.** Pricing: the factors in a pricing decision (cost, demand, competition, substitutes, life-cycle stage) and the strategies set out one by one — cost-plus, penetration, price skimming, psychological pricing, loss leader, competition-based, going rate and price discrimination

**Pricing.** Pricing policy and pricing strategies: the policy as the firm's measures for forming its prices, in two directions (towards buyers of its product, towards suppliers of its resources), framed by the state's own price policy and by elasticity of demand, rivals' reactions, the product's life-cycle stage, quality, consumers' incomes and the firm's image; strategies classed as differentiated, competitive and assortment pricing (Tellis) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Supply, demand and price.** Strand 3: the influence of supply and demand on pricing — the law of supply and demand and how prices move toward the equilibrium price (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Internet pricing.** Strand 5: pricing goods and services on the internet in normal markets — price regulation defined (governments dictating prices, most common with monopolies and utilities); the ICCC as principal economic regulator and consumer watchdog; and the internal and external factors influencing pricing (teacher guide) (Papua New Guinea's teacher guide.)

**Place (distribution).** Place: channels of distribution from direct selling to the full producer–wholesaler–retailer channel, and physical distribution (transport, inventory, warehousing, communication).

**Knowing the customer.** Analysing the customers: customers are the people, ventures or organisations who will buy; study their needs, tastes and habits, where they live, work and study, whether they come grouped in institutions, what income they have and whether the market is growing — summed up as needs, tastes, customs, buying habits, age, income level, preferences and cultural patterns (Nicaragua's book.)

**Knowing the customer.** Customer identification (business model part 1): customer segments — for which people do we create value, and what relationships do the different customers expect (Iran's book.)

**The marketing mix.** The market variables (marketing mix): product, price, place (plaza) and promotion — the 4 Ps — plus a fifth variable for new ventures, personalisation of the sale (Ecuador's book.)

**The marketing mix / offering.** Designing the value proposition (part 2): the value proposition is the reason customers prefer one business over others — a chosen bundle of products or services meeting a particular segment's needs — and the ways of developing one, such as novelty and brand/status (Iran's book.)

**Place in the marketing mix.** Determining distribution channels (part 3): communication, distribution and sales channels are the interface and touchpoints between a company and its customers and shape the customer experience; channels are classed as direct or indirect, and as the business's own or a partner's, each with its functions (Iran's book.)

**Knowing the market.** Writing the plan, part 2 — the market and marketing programme: the most important part of the plan; the market study examines the two factors of customers (identifying all of a product's customers) and competitors (their number, abilities and weaknesses), plus the opportunities and barriers to entering the business, and from that information the venture's sales are estimated (Iran's book.)

**Knowing the competition.** Studying the competition: competitors are ventures offering, or able to offer, the same product or service — and a competitor is not an enemy but a source of learning; find out where they are, who their customers are and why they buy, their strengths and weaknesses, their marketing strategies, their suppliers, their distribution channels, their quality and their prices (Nicaragua's book.)

**Market research / selling into the market.** Winning a market share, and the marketing plan: market share is the portion of total demand the project's good or service will take, and fixing it fixes expected sales and the production capacity needed; a project wins share from existing producers through a competitive advantage — distinguishing its product by price (e.g. (Palestine's book.)

**Marketing.** Markets and marketing functions: the kinds of market (consumer, reseller — wholesalers and retailers who buy finished goods and resell them unchanged for profit — and organisational markets) and market structures, then the functions a marketing department performs for the organisation (Tanzania's book.)

**Marketing.** The marketing mix: the elements a company uses to satisfy its market, taught as seven — product, price (set on the bases of cost, demand and competition, by named pricing methods), placement/distribution (the channel of distribution moving products from producers to consumers through its institutions), promotion, people, process and physical evidence (Tanzania's book.)

**Marketing-the-product-you-made.** Activity brief closing the yoghurt lesson: pupils work together on a project to produce yoghurt and market it in the school canteen (تسويقه في مقصف المدرسة) (Egypt's book.)

**Marketing-the-product-you-made.** Activity brief in the Greek-yoghurt lesson repeating the same project pattern: produce Greek yoghurt and market it in the school canteen — the book's own way of teaching marketing as part of every dairy production project (Egypt's book.)

**Pricing-and-selling.** Inside the same practical-application field, the Introduction names pricing, marketing and selling products (تسعير وتسويق وبيع المنتجات) as entrepreneurship skills (Egypt's book.)

**Selling-the-project's-product.** Marketing and commercialising the project's product as a named phase of execution: identify the buyers and sell the product (the example sells the bread) (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**What-a-market-is.** The market defined: the relations of buying and selling products or services; "our market" is all the clients who are interested in and acquire what we offer, plus all the people who could be potential buyers; and the elements that compose a market — products, competition, clients, suppliers, services, and the buy-and-sell transactions (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Marketing-mix.** Marketing (mercadeo): the activities that bring products and services to the clients so they are satisfied and the firm gains profits; marketing researches and analyses the market, plans strategies to position itself, and evaluates constantly — and builds its strategies on the 4Ps: product (what the client really buys is the satisfaction — phone companies sell security and closeness, appliance shops sell small instalments and payment facilities; brand, packaging, service and guarantee all count), price (the common error of pricing from production cost plus a percentage; price must also weigh what the product is worth to the client — supply and demand — and price policies must be set: wholesale discounts, shipping charges, commissions), place (where we sell — a good point of sale can mean success or a higher price, a bad one the firm's failure), and promotion (informing people the product exists — nobody buys what they do not know — through advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, publicity and public relations) (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Market-niche.** The market niche: a well-defined portion of a market segment whose individuals have similar characteristics and needs not wholly covered by the market's general offer; a niche's marks — specific, somewhat complex needs its members will pay extra to satisfy, the will and the economic capacity to buy, the need for specialised suppliers, and very few or no existing provider firms; examples by sector (organic foods, clothing for older women, executive-class air travel…); serving a niche can make a firm leader of a smaller market and highly profitable, since it knows the niche well and can charge a little more for the added value (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Niche-and-competition-analysis.** Analysing your own niche before launching: the analysis format works through the sector and sub-sector, your product or service (its price against the competition, quality control of raw materials and of the finished product, speed of answering client claims, home delivery), your clients (who they are, where they are, what challenges they have, their preferences, when and how much they buy and why), the competition (its geographic area, how many and how big the firms are, how many clients they hold, what they offer, its quality and its prices) — and ends by stating "the difference": what your product or service will have that the competition does not offer (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Market-research.** Doing market research with a questionnaire: before drafting one, be clear about the research objective, the information needed and who the questions address; the rules of a good questionnaire (an introductory greeting, clear questions in accessible language, no questions suggesting their answer, no intimate questions, logical progressive order, a clean attractive appearance) and the question types — introductory, open (rich information but laborious to record and analyse), closed (quick, categorical, easy to tabulate, but rigid), multiple-response, evaluation and weighted/ranking questions — each with its advantages and disadvantages; the team then interviews at least three potential clients per member in the community and tabulates the results (teacher guide) (Guatemala's teacher guide.)

**Market-scale part.** The scale of a business's market: domestic scale in three levels (local, state, national) and international scale (zone and global), the global market now reachable through the Internet (Malaysia's book.)

**Marketing-mix part.** The marketing mix set out element by element (product: quality, design, brand, packaging, size, guarantee; distribution: channels, coverage, inventory, transport, location; promotion: advertising methods and sales approach; price) together with marketing research (Malaysia's book.)

**Market-share part.** Market share and sales as business objectives: the sales-promotion strategies a seller uses — cheap sales in festive seasons, price discounts, samples and contests (Malaysia's book.)

**Marketing-department part.** The marketing functional department: making sure goods and services reach consumers quickly, creating demand for them, identifying the target market and building relations (Malaysia's book.)

**Market research part.** Market research taught inside Unit 1's "Selling a Product" activity, in its Important Points to Remember: businesses produce goods and services for a profit; businesses need to know which goods and services consumers want so they can produce them; businesses do this by conducting market research on consumer wants; market research needs to be carried out to ensure the consumer actually wants the good or service and is prepared to pay the price charged for it; and a business might produce a good consumers want, but if it is too expensive consumers will not buy it — with the research steps worked (decide the product and the price it could be produced at, list what you need to know about consumers, design a questionnaire, judge from the answers whether the product is worthwhile) (Samoa's book.)

**Marketing part.** The market defined — a situation or place where buyers and sellers meet and exchange goods or services for money — and marketing studied through a producer interview: the company's marketing strategy, the five factors a producer looks at to make his products attractive, and consumer satisfaction and the result of creating greater consumer satisfaction (Samoa's book.)

**Marketing part.** The concepts of marketing and the marketing mix (Bhutan's book.)

**Creative brief part.** The creative brief: the document that sets out what a promotion must say and achieve (Bhutan's book.)

**Modern marketing part.** Traditional against modern marketing concepts: modern marketing as marketing goods and services using the internet and disruptive technologies, social media marketing among its techniques (Bhutan's book.)

**Market analysis part.** Market analysis defined: analysing an industry or market for its attractiveness and the needs of its customers, as the first half of the market analysis and marketing plan (Bhutan's book.)

**Target market part.** The target market segment: the customer group the business plans to sell to, toward which its marketing efforts and campaigns are directed (Bhutan's book.)

**Marketing plan part.** Competitor and product strategy analysis (what special features make competitors' products sellable), and setting the marketing goals and objectives (awareness, sales, market share) with the strategies to achieve them (Bhutan's book.)

**Competition.** Competitive struggle: its minimum aim (keep your buyers) and maximum aim (take the main rivals' buyers), its stages (fix the target audience and main competitors, find and strengthen a competitive advantage, settle the industry strategy, work out tactics), and its three rules — do not harm the market, do not harm yourself, work within the law (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Competition.** The strategies of competitive struggle: offensive strategies (frontal attack, flanking attack on a leader's weak side, encirclement, concentrating forces on segments the leaders ignore, and bypass — avoiding competition with non-competing goods) against passive strategies (peaceful coexistence in small free niches, profit before share), including the copycat "false mushroom" strategy (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Competition.** Price and non-price competition: price competition (lowering prices, price discrimination) against non-price competition (quality, technology, innovation, branding, and competition by conditions of sale — better service); and perfect competition (many independent producers and consumers, homogeneous product, free information) against imperfect competition (market divided among a few large firms or dominated by one) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Pricing.** Pricing: what forms the final price (production cost, the balance of demand and supply, competitors' prices, the product's advantages, marketing, storage and logistics costs, and the firm's profit), and what depends on the price level — the firm's profit, its competitiveness and its financial stability (Kazakhstan's book.)

**What marketing is.** What marketing is: the chain need → want → demand (a want backed by purchasing power) → product → exchange/transaction → market, marketing as the activity of satisfying needs through exchange, its functions, and its four maximisation goals (consumption, choice, consumer satisfaction, quality of life) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Strategy.** Marketing strategy (the firm's long-term system of goals and measures for the competitive struggle) and the kinds of marketing set by the state of demand: conversion marketing for negative demand, stimulating marketing for no demand, developmental marketing for latent demand, synchromarketing for irregular demand, supportive marketing for full demand, demarketing for excessive demand (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Marketing mix.** The marketing mix: the 4P model and Lauterborn's 4C answer to it (customer needs and wants, cost to the customer, convenience, communication), with the five-step routine for applying the mix — choose the P set for the kind of business, describe the ideal state of each, describe the actual state, derive corrective actions, set priorities and distribute the budget (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Market research.** Marketing research: its five stages (define the problem and goals, choose information sources, collect, analyse, present results) and the desk/field split — desk research working secondary information (cheap, fast, several sources, but general and possibly stale) against field research gathering primary information for the exact task (controlled and precise, but costly) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Market research.** The methods of desk and field research: desk — analysing the firm's own potential, its competitors, and the micro- and macro-environment (the micro-environment's groups named: suppliers, marketing intermediaries, financial institutions, state bodies, civic groups); field — survey/interview, observation, experiment, panel and expert estimates, each characterised (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Analysis.** SWOT analysis: strengths and weaknesses as the firm's internal, controllable traits against opportunities and threats from the uncontrollable market environment; the five rules of a good SWOT (focus it on one segment, keep the internal/external line, count only what buyers perceive, rank by importance, ground it in objective data) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Analysis.** From SWOT to strategy: the matrix of strategic measures — SO (use strengths to take opportunities), WO (overcome weaknesses by using opportunities), ST (use strengths to remove threats), WT (minimise weaknesses to escape threats) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Segmentation.** Market segmentation and the target audience: dividing consumers into narrow homogeneous groups by geographic, demographic, socio-economic, national-cultural, personal and behavioural criteria, each group possibly needing its own product or marketing mix; and the customer avatar — a partly fictional portrait of a client type built from demography plus the entrepreneur's understanding of motives and needs (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Promotion.** Advertising against PR: advertising as the paid, tactical instrument that presents the product's concept to consumers from the producer's side, PR as the strategic instrument that works through public opinion to harmonise the company and society; the ATL (above-the-line) and BTL (below-the-line) split of marketing communications, and the classic kinds of advertising by aim (informative, persuasive, reminding) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Market research.** Product testing in marketing research: of six novelties only one succeeds, so new and existing products are tested before and during their market life; the tasks of a test (judge a new product's prospects, choose between prepared variants, check packaging/design changes) and its set-up (choose the target-audience buyers, the place — shop, studio or home — and the methods) (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Competition.** Competition and its kinds: perfect and imperfect competition, monopoly and natural monopoly, monopolistic competition; price and non-price competition (non-price includes advertising — most owners credit marketing for their results); direct and indirect competitors and substitute goods (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Competitive strategies.** The focus strategy: concentrate on one or a few market segments or geographic areas and build high loyalty, or a unique value offer, inside that narrow segment instead of serving the whole market (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Competitive strategies.** "blue ocean" against "red ocean": a blue ocean is a new market space with no direct competitors, created from an unfilled niche where real demand already exists (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Introduction to marketing.** What marketing is and is not: not advertising and not pushing unwanted goods; its aim is not selling but meeting real consumer needs, so it begins by identifying those needs; the marketing cycle (research → strategy → product development and tactics → organising sales → communication); consumer insight defined; services differ from goods because their quality also depends on the client (Kazakhstan's book.)

**The marketing mix.** The marketing mix: Borden coined the mix (1953) and McCarthy grouped it as the 4P (1960); the full 4P decision table — the decisions taken under Product, Price, Place and Promotion; the extensions to 5P and 7P adding Process, People and Physical Evidence, and modern additions (Positioning, Packaging, Profit, Purchase); the 4C as the same mix seen from the customer's side (Kazakhstan's book.)

**SWOT.** SWOT analysis as a working method: run after internal and external research; internal strengths and weaknesses against external opportunities and threats; inside, examine marketing, the sales system, production, finance and management; outside, the social, economic, technological, political, ecological and competitive factor groups; the four boxes are then crossed to derive actions (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Segmentation.** Market segmentation as both a strategy and a method, its goal to be the best supplier inside your segment; the descriptor groups (by decision role, demography, purchase place, geography, behaviour, and benefits/needs); need distinguished from want; undifferentiated, differentiated and concentrated approaches; the criteria for choosing a segment, including compatibility and safety (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Brand and positioning.** Brand and positioning: "products are made in factories, brands are made in minds" (Landor); a loved brand is forgiven price, service and quality slips; positioning is built from research and segmentation around the one quality competitors cannot offer; the five levels of positioning (socio-demographic, rational, functional, emotional, values); the perception map as the tool for choosing a positioning direction (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Communications planning.** The marketing communications plan in five stages: set communication goals and the target audience (from research and segmentation); build the plan, choose channels and calculate the budget (commonly as a percentage of turnover — high for perfumery and cosmetics, low for food); create the creative, the key message and the unique selling proposition; run it; then evaluate effectiveness using audience surveys, awareness before/during/after, reach data and promotion participation (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Sales promotion.** Sales promotion tools: seasonal and other discounts, discount cards, coupons, gifts for purchase, sampling and tastings, code and label-collection promotions, and special offers such as 1+1 (Kazakhstan's book.)

**BTL.** BTL communications: direct marketing (promoters offering tastings and samples while explaining the product), POS materials that guide the buyer at the point of sale, sms and email direct channels, and incentives aimed at sellers and distributors (Kazakhstan's book.)

**Definition part.** The market defined three ways: generally, a place of buying, selling and exchange; economically, the group of buyers and sellers — producers and consumers — who negotiate exchange; in marketing, all customers who have a need or want for a product and the purchasing power to buy it. (Laos's book.)

**Marketing-mix part.** The marketing mix: product and services (focus narrowly; develop the product/service), place/distribution (use existing distributors or manufacturer's agents; retailers weigh cost and transport), price (pricing policy, including credit policy, is a main factor reflecting income), promotion. (Laos's book.)

**Evaluation part.** Evaluating marketing decisions: judge each marketing decision against standards and past performance. (Laos's book.)

**Life-cycle part.** The product life cycle: introduction, growth, maturity, decline; the cycle shapes marketing strategy and mix; at decline the options are to improve or add quality, cut production costs and price and keep selling, or stop production. (Laos's book.)


**Customer-knowledge part.** Knowing your customers: who they are, where they are, when they buy (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, seasonally — which sets opening hours, advertising timing and stock levels) and how they pay. (Laos's book.)

**Research part.** Small-scale market research: basic questionnaires to current and future customers reveal problems and satisfaction, and research should identify the factors that affect sales. (Laos's book.)

**Framework part.** The 5 Ws of the market: who / what / when / where / why questions, with the factors behind each and their information sources (formal market survey vs informal observation). (Laos's book.)

**Competitor part.** Competitor analysis: estimate competitors' income, sales and prices; and learn from a business that failed — which factors made it fail or succeed. (Laos's book.)

**Survey part.** Conducting a market survey, and information sources: primary sources (direct contact, e.g. furniture makers and sellers for a furniture survey) vs secondary, already-existing sources. (Laos's book.)

**Forecasting part.** Sales forecasting: build monthly sales up over 12 months; keep separate plans per product or service, and separate plans for wholesale and for bulk retail. (Laos's book.)
