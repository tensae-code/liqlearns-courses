# Business Calculations

## Do it first

Price one loaf of bread in your head, in steps: what the bread cost to produce, what it cost to bring it to the shop and show it, and the shopkeeper's profit. Add the three steps: that total is the shop price. That is this lesson.

## Costing a small job

**The working-out-what-a-job-costs.** Costing a small job: a plumbing practical activity is built around working out the cost of changing the candles (cartridges) of a home water filter, and its assessment extends the same costing to fitting basins, taps and mixers.

## The priced estimate — costings, then the profit added

**The pricing-and-profit-margin.** A worked priced estimate (مقايسة) for supplying and fitting a 3-stage water filter: every item is listed with its unit, quantity and unit price, the line prices are totalled, and a 20% profit is then added to reach the final amount — the book's own way of teaching how a tradesperson prices a job.

## The landed cost of an imported television

**Project/import costing at business-studies level.** A full landed-cost build-up for an imported television: production cost + delivery = landed cost, + tariffs/taxes = importer's cost, + importer mark-up, + retailer mark-up, + VAGST = the customer's price — Samoa's worked example of how each stage's margin builds the final price.

## What a shop price is made of

**The build-up of a selling price.** What a shop price is made of: the price paid for bread consists of the cost of producing the bread, the cost of marketing the bread and the profit taken by the shopkeeper.

## Price, demand and revenue calculations

How a price change affects total revenue, worked through with numbers: a 10% price cut on bananas raises weekly revenue ($400 → $432) because demand rises 20%, while the same cut on sea salt lowers revenue ($300 → $283.50) because demand rises only 5%

**The numbers behind choosing.** Weighting and scoring the matrix: each variable is given a percentage weight (community ventures weight qualitative variables more, private productive ventures weight quantitative ones more), each venture is scored 1–5 per variable, and the weighted totals pick the winner — worked example in which "plant a different crop" beats a collection centre, a cargo vehicle and machinery (Ecuador's book.)

**Costing.** Fixed and variable costs: production costs (raw materials, direct labour, indirect costs such as packaging, maintenance and workshop electricity) grouped into costs that stay fixed and costs that vary with output; total cost = fixed + variable (Ecuador's book.)

**Costing.** Operating expenses split into administration expenses (head-office salaries, office rent, services) and selling expenses (sales salaries, commissions, social benefits) (Ecuador's book.)

**Costing and pricing numbers.** Total cost and unit cost of a product or service: the unit cost is what one unit costs to make, its components differ by type of business, and unit costs fall as production rises, so the venture should find its optimal production level; contribution margin introduced (Ecuador's book.)
