# Business Calculations

## Do it first

Price one loaf of bread in your head, in steps: what the bread cost to produce, what it cost to bring it to the shop and show it, and the shopkeeper's profit. Add the three steps: that total is the shop price. That is this lesson.

## Costing a small job

**The working-out-what-a-job-costs.** Costing a small job: a plumbing practical activity is built around working out the cost of changing the candles (cartridges) of a home water filter, and its assessment extends the same costing to fitting basins, taps and mixers.

## The priced estimate — costings, then the profit added

**The pricing-and-profit-margin.** A worked priced estimate (مقايسة) for supplying and fitting a 3-stage water filter: every item is listed with its unit, quantity and unit price, the line prices are totalled, and a 20% profit is then added to reach the final amount — the book's own way of teaching how a tradesperson prices a job.

## The landed cost of an imported television

**Project/import costing at business-studies level.** A full landed-cost build-up for an imported television: production cost + delivery = landed cost, + tariffs/taxes = importer's cost, + importer mark-up, + retailer mark-up, + VAGST = the customer's price — Samoa's worked example of how each stage's margin builds the final price.

## What a shop price is made of

**The build-up of a selling price.** What a shop price is made of: the price paid for bread consists of the cost of producing the bread, the cost of marketing the bread and the profit taken by the shopkeeper.

## Price, demand and revenue calculations

How a price change affects total revenue, worked through with numbers: a 10% price cut on bananas raises weekly revenue ($400 → $432) because demand rises 20%, while the same cut on sea salt lowers revenue ($300 → $283.50) because demand rises only 5%

**The numbers behind choosing.** Weighting and scoring the matrix: each variable is given a percentage weight (community ventures weight qualitative variables more, private productive ventures weight quantitative ones more), each venture is scored 1–5 per variable, and the weighted totals pick the winner — worked example in which "plant a different crop" beats a collection centre, a cargo vehicle and machinery (Ecuador's book.)

**Costing.** Fixed and variable costs: production costs (raw materials, direct labour, indirect costs such as packaging, maintenance and workshop electricity) grouped into costs that stay fixed and costs that vary with output; total cost = fixed + variable (Ecuador's book.)

**Costing.** Fixed and variable costs: costs are all the money outflows needed to produce and sell; a product's total cost is made of fixed costs, which do not vary (or vary very little) when output and sales rise or fall and which the venture needs simply to function, and variable costs, which change with the units produced, services given and sales (Nicaragua's book.)

**Costing.** Worked example of a plan's money: a start-up's fixed costs (land, machinery and equipment, installations, vehicle, training) totalled separately from its variable costs (raw materials, wages, fuel and energy) to give the plan's total investment and its production costs (Iran's book.)

**Costing.** Cost estimation (part 9): the cost structure names the most important costs of running the model — creating and delivering value, keeping customer relationships and generating revenue all cost money, and costs should be reduced as far as possible; cost-driven models set against value-driven ones (Iran's book.)

**Costing.** Operating expenses split into administration expenses (head-office salaries, office rent, services) and selling expenses (sales salaries, commissions, social benefits) (Ecuador's book.)

**Costing and pricing numbers.** Total cost and unit cost of a product or service: the unit cost is what one unit costs to make, its components differ by type of business, and unit costs fall as production rises, so the venture should find its optimal production level; contribution margin introduced (Ecuador's book.)

**Pricing.** Setting the price: price is the money value of a good or service; to fix it correctly, weigh the objective the price is meant to achieve, how demand behaves when prices change, and the competition's prices — and stay alert to changes in any of these so the price can be adjusted quickly (Nicaragua's book.)

**The numbers behind the market.** Estimating total demand by the per-capita consumption method: total consumption of the good = local production + (imports − exports) + change in stock (closing minus opening stock); average per-capita consumption = total consumption ÷ population in the same year; (Palestine's book.)

**Costing / sizing the need.** Estimating the financing need realistically, and opportunity cost: the financial need arises from the value of the assets the project needs; (Palestine's book.)
**Business calculations.** Interest and its factors: interest is built from three factors — the principal, the rate and the time — and because the time must often be found between two dates, the book teaches computing the period in years, in years and months, and in exact days between a deposit/loan date and a withdrawal/repayment/maturity date (Sudan's book.)

**Business calculations.** Simple interest: interest on the principal alone for periods stated in years, months or days, including commercial interest computed on the 360-day commercial year against ordinary interest on the exact days (Sudan's book.)

**Business calculations.** The true rate of interest: lenders can load a loan with commissions, administrative charges, interest deducted in advance, or counting both the borrowing and repayment months in full, so the rate the borrower really pays is higher than the stated rate; the true rate is worked out from what the borrower actually receives and actually pays (Sudan's book.)

**Business calculations.** Compound interest and its laws: interest is added to the principal at the end of each period so the next period's interest is earned on a growing amount — always more than simple interest beyond one year; the amount law (amount = principal × the amount of one pound for the period), the tables built for it, and finding any variable by computer (Sudan's book.)

**Costing-and-expected-profit.** The same brief requires working out the cost of the project's raw materials and the profits the project is expected to make (Egypt's book.)

**Costing-and-profit.** The same brief requires recording, in a table, the calculation of the project's costs and profits (حساب التكاليف والأرباح) and presenting the project's results to the class (Egypt's book.)

**Costing-and-profit.** The same Greek-yoghurt brief again requires the costs-and-profits calculation table (جدول حساب التكاليف والأرباح) and presenting the project's results (Egypt's book.)
