# Lesson plan — Poultry — the four production systems

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Poultry — the
four production systems
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four systems run from loosest to tightest: free-
range extensive, backyard extensive, semi-intensive, and intensive — and chickens' advantages
are a short generation, high productivity, easy transport and sale, low unit value, few
religious taboos, and fit with crop–livestock farming.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the poultry lesson before it — chickens matter; now
the four ways of keeping them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written
as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books;
on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as
the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its
source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,
each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The loose systems — free-range and backyard** — (1) Free-range extensive: birds
unconfined, scavenging widely, nesting in the bush, shelters optional.
2. **The tight systems — semi-intensive and intensive** — (3) Semi-intensive: birds confined to
a fenced area with shelter, mixing both systems, common in urban, semi-urban and rural areas.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four systems in order and define each by its confinement and feeding.
2. Say where semi-intensive is common: urban, semi-urban and rural areas.
3. Say the traditional system's defence: it makes best use of local resources.
4. List chickens' advantages.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes backyard and free-range → the night house and the grain handful separate them: backyard
birds come home.
- Thinks intensive is 'best' by definition → it costs more capital and makes birds wholly
dependent; systems fit resources, not ranks.
