# Poultry — the four production systems

## Do it first

Keeper A: birds wander the whole valley. Keeper B: birds sleep in a house, roam by day. Keeper
C: birds live inside a fence. Keeper D: birds never leave their house. Put them in order,
freest first. The book's four systems are exactly that order.

## Sub lesson 1 — The loose systems — free-range and backyard

(1) Free-range extensive: birds unconfined, scavenging widely, nesting in the bush, shelters
optional. (2) Backyard extensive: housed at night, free-ranging by day, given a handful of
grain morning and evening. The traditional system is defended as making best use of local
resources.

- Free-range: unconfined, scavenging, bush nests, shelter optional.
- Backyard: night housing, day ranging, a handful of grain morning and evening.
- Traditional system's defence: best use of local resources.

## Sub lesson 2 — The tight systems — semi-intensive and intensive

(3) Semi-intensive: birds confined to a fenced area with shelter, mixing both systems, common
in urban, semi-urban and rural areas. (4) Intensive: fully confined in houses or cages by
medium-to-large commercial enterprises (and some households), costing more capital, with birds
wholly dependent on the owner. Chickens' advantages across all systems: short generation
interval, high productivity, easy transport and sale, low unit value, few religious taboos, and
fit with crop–livestock farming.

- Semi-intensive: fenced area + shelter; mixes both; common everywhere.
- Intensive: fully confined, houses or cages; more capital; birds wholly dependent on owner.
- Advantages: short generation, high productivity, easy transport/sale, low unit value, few
taboos, fits crop–livestock farming.
