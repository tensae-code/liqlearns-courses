# Dairy — the breeding and lactation cycle

## Do it first

Quick question, honestly answered: a young cow who has never calved — does she give milk? And a
cow milked for many months after calving — does her milk stay at its first level forever? This
lesson is the cycle behind both answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — The cycle — calving starts the milk

A dairy cow's life is a cycle of pregnancy and lactation beginning at puberty. She gives no
milk until she calves, so timing the first and every later breeding sets the farm's milk
output. Female calves are raised as replacements for unproductive older cows — the herd renews
itself from its own calves.

- No calving, no milk — breeding timing sets output.
- The cycle begins at puberty: pregnancy, calving, lactation.
- Female calves = replacements for unproductive older cows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lactation's curve — and waste that is not waste

Lactation — milk secretion from the mammary glands — starts at calving, and production steadily
declines while milking continues; farmers time the next breeding against this lactation curve.
And properly managed dairy waste is not waste: its nutrients make excellent fertiliser that
builds soil organic matter and fertility.

- Lactation starts at calving; production steadily declines.
- Next breeding is timed against the lactation curve.
- Managed waste → fertiliser → soil organic matter and fertility.
