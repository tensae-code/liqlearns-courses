# Dairy — milking by hand

## Do it first

Cup one hand as if it held a cow's teat. Show, without words, how your fingers would make the
milk come out. Hold your idea. This lesson teaches the book's two real hand methods — compare
yours with them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Milk and milking

Milk is the nutrient-rich food made in the mammary glands. Milking is removing it from the
udder, by hand or by machine. Locally it is done by hand, indoors, twice a day — morning and
evening — with the cow tied or held in a stanchion in the barn.

- Milk: nutrient-rich food made in the mammary glands.
- Milking: removing milk from the udder, by hand or machine.
- Local way: by hand, indoors, twice daily, cow tied or in a stanchion.

## Sub lesson 2 — The two hand methods

Method one — pinch and squeeze: pinch the top of the teat shut between fingers and thumb, to
trap milk in its lower part; then squeeze with the other fingers, so milk squirts out of the
tip into the bucket. Method two — pinch and slide: pinch the top of the teat shut, then slide
the fingers down the teat, pushing the milk down and out. Both begin by trapping the milk —
that is the shared secret.

- Both methods start: pinch the top of the teat shut to trap milk.
- Squeeze: the other fingers press, milk squirts from the tip.
- Slide: fingers slide down the teat, pushing milk down and out.
- The bucket catches the milk below the udder.

## Say it to your companion

Teach your companion the two methods as if their hands were on the teat: "First trap the milk —
pinch the top shut. Then squeeze… or slide…" Your companion repeats the words while miming.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the two methods as numbered steps, from memory.
2. Draw a teat and mark where the pinch traps the milk.
3. Say why milking is twice a day, morning and evening, in the book's local picture.
4. List what must be washed before milking, from the clean-milk lesson's rule: hands,
containers and teats.
