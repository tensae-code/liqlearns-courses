# Starting a small business — and knowing your profit

## Do it first

A jar of honey stands before you. Bees were kept, jars were bought, transport was paid. If you
sell the jar, how do you know whether you gained or lost? Do the sum in your head first. This
lesson gives the seven ideas of a small business — and the one formula that judges them all.

## Sub lesson 1 — The product, the place, the resources

(1) Offer a product or service customers actually want, and build good customer relationships.
(2) Choose a location accessible to buyers. (3) Know your resources — physical, human and
financial: employees, hives, clippers, seeds, land, a sewing machine, cash.

- Wanted product/service + good customer relationships.
- Location accessible to buyers.
- Resources: physical, human, financial — know yours.

## Sub lesson 2 — Distribution, activities, price

(4) Plan distribution — deliver yourself or through others by local transport. (5) Know the
production and marketing activities your trade involves. (6) Set a reasonable price covering
costs plus profit.

- Distribution: self-delivered or through others, local transport.
- Know your production and marketing activities.
- Price = costs + profit, set reasonably.

## Sub lesson 3 — The formula — profit = income − cost

(7) Record income and costs and compare them: profit = income − cost (P = I − C). Income below
cost is a loss; above it, a profit. The ledger is not paperwork for its own sake — it is how a
trader knows the truth about the business.

- Record income and costs; compare them.
- P = I − C.
- Income < cost: loss. Income > cost: profit.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion through the seven ideas using one trade you know, then do a sum aloud:
"Income 500, costs 320 — profit is…" Let your companion answer: 180, by P = I − C.

## Do It Yourself

1. Open a ledger page: two columns, income and costs, for a real or imagined week of one trade.
2. Cost one real item end-to-end (a jar of honey, a tray of eggs) and set its reasonable price.
3. Write your resources list under the three headings.
4. Do three P = I − C sums, one of them a loss, and label each.
