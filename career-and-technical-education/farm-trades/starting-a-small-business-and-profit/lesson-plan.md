# Lesson plan — Starting a small business — and knowing your profit

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Starting a
small business — and knowing your profit
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a trade becomes a business in seven ideas — a product
customers want, good customer relationships, an accessible location, known resources, a
distribution plan, the production and marketing activities, and a reasonable price — and the
books are kept by one formula: profit = income − cost (P = I − C).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the Farm Trades lessons — a trade skill is
learned; now turning it into a business. The same closing is pointed back to at the end of the
salon unit too.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written
as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books;
on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as
the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its
source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,

1. **The product, the place, the resources** — (1) Offer a product or service customers

2. **Distribution, activities, price** — (4) Plan distribution — deliver yourself or through
others by local transport.
3. **The formula — profit = income − cost** — (7) Record income and costs and compare them:
profit = income − cost (P = I − C).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: any one Farm Trades skill lesson — gardening, dairy, fattening, poultry or
beekeeping. Recommended, never forced.
- The other Farm Trades lessons stand next door in this same title.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Recite the seven business ideas.
2. Name the three resource kinds: physical, human and financial — with the book's examples
(employees, hives, clippers, seeds, land, a sewing machine, cash).
3. Work the formula: profit = income − cost; income below cost is a loss, above it a profit.
4. Set a reasonable price: covering costs plus profit.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and a notebook that could become a real ledger.

## If the learner is stuck
- Prices by copying a rival → price from your own costs plus profit; a rival's costs are not
yours.
- Sees the ledger as schoolwork → without records, 'profit' is a feeling; P = I − C makes it a
fact.
