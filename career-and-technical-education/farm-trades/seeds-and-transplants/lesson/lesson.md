# Gardening — seeds or seedlings? Starting your plants

## Do it first

You want tomatoes this season. The shop offers a packet of seed and a tray of young tomato
plants. The seed is cheaper and there are more kinds. The young plants are already growing.
Which do you take, and why? This lesson holds both answers — and the careful way to move a
young plant into the ground.

## Sub lesson 1 — Seed or transplant — the choice

Seed is the small hard part a new plant grows from. More varieties exist as seed than as
transplants, and seed costs less; some seed is sown directly, by the directions on it.
Transplanting moves seedlings from containers into the garden, so the harvest comes sooner —
essential for slow plants that could not mature from a direct sowing in one season.

- Seed: more varieties, lower cost, some sown directly.
- Transplanting: seedlings moved from container to garden.
- Transplants bring the harvest sooner — vital for slow plants.

## Sub lesson 2 — Moving a seedling — the two rules

Rule one: harden the seedlings off before moving them — give them longer periods in full sun
each day for about a week, so the move does not shock them. Rule two: plant at the same depth
the seedling grew in its container. Tomatoes are the exception — plant them slightly deeper.
And know the limit: root crops such as carrots, radishes and beets do not transplant well.

- Harden off: longer full-sun periods each day, about a week.
- Plant at the same depth as in the container.
- Exception: tomatoes go slightly deeper.
- Carrots, radishes and beets do not transplant well.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two transplanting rules and the tomato exception. Your companion tries
to catch you out with a carrot: "Shall we transplant it?" — answer with the book's limit.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a one-week hardening-off calendar for a tray of seedlings: a little more full sun each
day.
2. Practise the depth rule with a drawn seedling: mark the soil line from its container, then
the line in the ground — the same, except a tomato's.
3. Sort a list of plants: sow direct, transplant, or do-not-transplant (carrots, radishes,
beets).
4. Say in one sentence when transplanting is essential: for slow plants that cannot mature from
a direct sowing in one season.
