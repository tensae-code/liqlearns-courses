# Lesson plan — Gardening — seeds or seedlings? Starting your plants

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Gardening —
seeds or seedlings? Starting your plants
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** seed costs less and offers more varieties, transplanting
brings the harvest sooner — and transplanting has two rules: harden seedlings off for about a
week, and plant at the same depth (tomatoes slightly deeper).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the gardening lessons before it — the learner has a
plot and tools; now the plants themselves.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written
as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books;
on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as
the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its
source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,
each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Seed or transplant — the choice** — Seed is the small hard part a new plant grows from.
2. **Moving a seedling — the two rules** — Rule one: harden the seedlings off before moving
them — give them longer periods in full sun each day for about a week, so the move does not
shock them.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a seed is and two advantages of seed: more varieties, lower cost.
2. Say what transplanting is and why it brings the harvest sooner.
3. Harden seedlings off correctly: longer periods in full sun each day for about a week.
4. Plant a transplant at the same depth it grew in its container — and name the exception,
tomatoes, planted slightly deeper.
5. Name root crops that do not transplant well: carrots, radishes, beets.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A seedling in a container for the hands-on, if one can be had.

## If the learner is stuck
- Plants everything deeper 'to be safe' → depth is a rule, not a guess: same depth, tomatoes
only slightly deeper.
- Wants to transplant carrots → root crops resent moving; sow them where they will grow.
