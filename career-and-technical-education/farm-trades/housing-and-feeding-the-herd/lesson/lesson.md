# Dairy — housing and feeding the herd

## Do it first

this lesson's agenda.

## Sub lesson 1 — Housing — grouped, shaded, watered

Modern farms group animals into management units by age, nutritional needs, reproductive status
and milk production, managing the lactating herd most intensively. Housing must give access to
feed and water and protection from heat and cold. Heat stress cuts fertility and milk yield, so
shade — and fans in barns — matters; and housing must stay warm enough not to kill in cold.

- Groups: by age, needs, reproductive status, production.
- Lactating herd: managed most intensively.
- Housing gives feed/water access; protection from heat and cold.
- Heat stress cuts fertility and yield — shade and fans answer it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Feeding — the largest cost

Feeding is the largest cost on the dairy farm. Pasture-based producers maintain grazing land
and rotate grazing. Diets are formulated per animal's growth, production and reproductive state
— large operations may hire a nutritionist — and each cow must eat its full ration: a ration
planned but not eaten feeds no milk.

- Feeding = the largest cost.
- Pasture producers: maintain grazing land, rotate grazing.
- Diets formulated per animal's state; big operations may hire a nutritionist.
- Each cow must eat its full ration.
