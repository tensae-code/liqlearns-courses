# Animal fattening — feeding animals for meat and profit

## Do it first

Two bulls: one pulls a plough all season, one stands in a pen and eats. Which one is being
fattened, and why does the difference matter? This lesson defines fattening — and maps
Ethiopia's three ways of doing it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What fattening is — and why it pays

Fattening is feeding animals continuously for a set period, kept in one place with no farm work
and little movement, so they yield the greatest quantity of high-quality meat. Humans need
meat's protein; demand rises yearly and supply lags — so fattening is presented as a
profitable, fast-return career.

- Continuous feeding, set period, one place.
- No farm work, little movement — energy goes to meat.
- Greatest quantity of high-quality meat is the goal.
- Protein need + rising demand + lagging supply = profit opening.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's three systems

The traditional system: oxen sold after the ploughing season, in poor condition, fed grass and
some cereals. The by-product-based system: cattle raised on maize, soy and other feeds,
including feedlots. And the Hararghe fattening system, Ethiopia's own named third way.

- Traditional: after ploughing, poor condition, grass and some cereals.
- By-product-based: maize, soy and other feeds; feedlots included.
- Hararghe: the third named system.
