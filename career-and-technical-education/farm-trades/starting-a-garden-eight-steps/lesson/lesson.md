# Gardening — starting your garden in eight steps

## Do it first

Someone hands you an empty plot and says: "Make a garden." Write the jobs in the order you
would do them — first, second, third — as far as you can. Then compare your order with the
book's eight steps in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The eight steps

The start-up sequence: (1) decide what to grow, (2) choose the location, (3) plan the garden
beds, (4) arrange the right tools, (5) choose the right seeds or transplants, (6) plant with
care, (7) nurture the garden, (8) enjoy and sell the harvest.

- Steps 1–3: decide, locate, plan the beds.
- Steps 4–5: arrange tools; choose seeds or transplants.
- Steps 6–8: plant with care, nurture, enjoy and sell.

## Sub lesson 2 — Care — the most important duty

Garden care is named the gardener's most important duty, because the yield depends on it. Care
includes preparing for seasons when rain does not come as expected — the garden that is only
planted is not yet a garden that yields.

- Care, not planting, decides the yield.
- Plan ahead for rains that fail or come late.
- Nurture is step 7 — and it never really ends before harvest.

## Say it to your companion

Recite the eight steps to your companion in order. Your companion shuffles three step-cards;
you put them back in order and say what goes wrong if two steps swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight steps as a path across a drawn plot, from empty ground to a harvest stall.
2. For your own real or imagined plot, fill in each step with your actual choices.
3. Write your rain-failure plan in two sentences.
4. Pin the path where you will see it while the garden grows.
