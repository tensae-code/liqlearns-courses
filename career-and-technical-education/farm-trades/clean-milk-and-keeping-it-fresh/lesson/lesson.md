# Dairy — clean milk and keeping it fresh

## Do it first

Fresh milk leaves the udder clean. Between the udder and the cup, many things can dirty it —
hands, containers, hair, dirt, air, time, heat. List all you can think of. This lesson is the
defence, in the book's words.

## Sub lesson 1 — Clean milk — the hygiene rule

Hygiene guards both hand and machine milking: the milker washes hands, containers and teats,
and keeps hair and dirt out of the milk. The reason is not tidiness — contaminated milk carries
diseases, some of them deadly. Clean milk is safe milk.

- Wash: hands, containers, teats.
- Keep out: hair and dirt.
- Why: contaminated milk carries diseases, some deadly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keeping milk fresh — cooling

Preservation is mainly cooling: cooling extends milk's storage life until use or transport to
market. Before electricity, milk was cooled with continuously pumped cold underground water in
a cooling barrel. Today the refrigerator does the work — cold slows bacterial growth, so the
milk keeps longer.

- Main preservation: cooling.
- Old way: cold underground water pumped through a cooling barrel.
- Today: the refrigerator — cold slows bacterial growth.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the hygiene rule as a command list — wash these three, keep out these two —
then the reason in one sentence: contaminated milk carries diseases, some deadly.
