# Beekeeping — the colony: queen, drones and workers

## Do it first

A hive hums with tens of thousands of bees, yet it runs in perfect order. Who lays the eggs?
Who does the work? Who is thrown out when food runs short? Guess the three castes before you
read — then meet the queen, the drones and the workers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beekeeping and its products

Beekeeping (apiculture) is managing honeybees in hives for pollination and for honey and other
products: wax, royal jelly, propolis, pollen, and live bees and queens. Bees are social — the
colony works together — which is why one hive can be managed as a single living unit.

- Apiculture = managing honeybees in hives.
- For pollination, and products: honey, wax, royal jelly, propolis, pollen, live bees and
queens.
- Bees are social: the colony works together.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three sorts of bee

The queen: one per colony, the mother, laying up to 1,000 eggs a day at peak season. The
drones: males, about 500 when food is plentiful, whose job is mating with a young queen —
driven out in the dry season. The workers: females who cannot lay eggs and do all the hive's
work, up to 40,000 in the main honey season.

- Queen: 1; mother; up to 1,000 eggs/day at peak.
- Drones: males, ~500 in plenty; mate; driven out in dry season.
- Workers: females, cannot lay; all the work; up to 40,000 in honey season.

## Say it to your companion

Present the colony to your companion as a household: "One mother, laying a thousand eggs a day.
Five hundred sons, who mate and are thrown out in the dry season. Forty thousand daughters, who
do everything."
