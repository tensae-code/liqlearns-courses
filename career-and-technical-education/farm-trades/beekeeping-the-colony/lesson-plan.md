# Lesson plan — Beekeeping — the colony: queen, drones and workers

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Beekeeping —
the colony: queen, drones and workers
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** beekeeping (apiculture) is managing honeybees in hives
for pollination and products — honey, wax, royal jelly, propolis, pollen, live bees and queens
— and the social colony has three sorts: one queen laying up to 1,000 eggs a day, about 500
drones, and up to 40,000 workers who do all the work.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own locality — bees, honey and hives
are familiar; now the colony's order.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define beekeeping/apiculture and name its products.
2. Give the three sorts with numbers and jobs: queen (one; mother; up to 1,000 eggs a day at
peak), drones (males, about 500 when food is plentiful; mating; driven out in the dry season),
workers (females who cannot lay; all the hive's work; up to 40,000 in the main honey season).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the queen rules like a king → her job is laying; the workers run the hive's work.
- Confuses drones and workers → drones are male and seasonal; workers are female and do all the
work.
