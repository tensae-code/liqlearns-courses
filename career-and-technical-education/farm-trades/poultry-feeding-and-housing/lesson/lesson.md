# Poultry — feeding and housing your flock

## Do it first

Watch chickens around a home for one imagined day: the yard, the kitchen door, the grain store,
the shrubs. What do they actually eat? And at night, where do they sleep — and what could reach
them there? Feed and housing: this lesson's two halves.

## Sub lesson 1 — Feeds — seven sources

Egg production tracks feed and nutrient intake — feed a hen poorly and the eggs answer for it.
Chicken feeds come from household cooking waste, cereals and cereal by-products, roots and
tubers, oilseeds, trees and shrubs, animal proteins, and commercially prepared feed. Every
locality has its own mix of these — the lesson's work is to list yours.

- Eggs follow feed and nutrients.
- Sources: cooking waste; cereals and by-products; roots and tubers; oilseeds; trees and
shrubs; animal proteins; commercial feed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Housing — four things always

Housing ranges from inside the family house — even under a bed, against theft — to purpose-
built shelter. Whatever the house, it must always provide four things: space, ventilation,
light, and protection from weather and predators. A house missing one of the four is not
finished, however fine it looks.

- Range: inside the family house to purpose-built shelter.
- Always: space, ventilation, light, protection from weather and predators.

## Say it to your companion

Name the seven feed sources for your companion, then housing's four: "Space, ventilation,
light, protection." Your companion names a house; you say which of the four it lacks.

## Do It Yourself

1. List your locality's chicken feeds under the seven source headings.
2. Audit a real chicken house against the four requirements.
3. Draw a small poultry house and label the four into it.
4. Write one sentence linking feed to eggs.
