# Dairy — starting your own dairy farm

## Do it first

You have decided: a small dairy farm. Before the first animal sets foot on your land, what must
already be arranged? Write your list — then check it against the book's six requirements.

## Sub lesson 1 — The six requirements — place, animals, feed

Before establishing a dairy farm, arrange: (1) space and shelter for the animals, (2) improved
dairy breeds — local breeds can also be used — and (3) fodder management. Place first, then
animals, then what they will eat.

- 1. Space and shelter.
- 2. Improved dairy breeds (local breeds also usable).
- 3. Fodder management.

## Sub lesson 2 — The six requirements — management, health, breeding

Then: (4) daily farm management, (5) disease control and management, and (6)
reproduction/breeding management. A farm is not its shed; it is the daily running, the disease
defence and the breeding plan that keep milk flowing.

- 4. Daily farm management.
- 5. Disease control and management.
- 6. Reproduction/breeding management.

## Say it to your companion

Recite the six to your companion as a gate list: "No cow arrives until…" and name all six. Your
companion names a gap; you say which requirement it breaks.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six as a checklist board for a named site.
2. Score a real or imagined farm against each of the six: ready, partly, missing.
3. For the weakest requirement, write the first action that would fix it.
4. Say why fodder is arranged before animals arrive.
