# Beekeeping — starting out and choosing the site

## Do it first

Pick a spot near your home for a beehive. Now defend it: will the bees thrive there? Will
people be safe? What grows near it? Your defence, tested against the book's start-up steps, is
this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four start-up steps

Four start-up steps: (1) establish the beekeeping site, (2) obtain colonies of honeybees, (3)
prepare the basic equipment, materials and tools, and (4) clear and fence the apiary site. Site
first, bees second, kit third, ground work fourth.

- 1. Establish the beekeeping site.
- 2. Obtain colonies of honeybees.
- 3. Prepare equipment, materials and tools.
- 4. Clear and fence the apiary site.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the site

Site selection decides success: productive beekeeping needs a suitable area located and
evaluated — balancing the bees' needs against the sites available — to save time, energy and
money. At the site, traditional and modern hives stand side by side in the book's picture: the
keeper's old and new tools for the same bees.

- A suitable area is located and evaluated.
- Balance: the bees' needs vs the sites available.
- A good site saves time, energy and money.
- Traditional and modern hives both house colonies.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the four steps in order, then your own chosen site and its defence in three
points.

## Do It Yourself

1. Walk (in fact or on paper) three possible apiary spots and score each: bees' needs, people's
safety, access.
2. Write the four steps as a start-up gate list.
3. Draw a traditional hive and a modern frame hive side by side.
4. Say what a bad site costs, in the book's three currencies: time, energy, money.
