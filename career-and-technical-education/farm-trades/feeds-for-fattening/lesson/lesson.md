# Fattening — feeds: concentrates and roughages

## Do it first

Spread before you: barley grain, dry hay, green silage, pasture grass, maize. Some are rich and
building; some are bulky and fibrous. Sort them into two piles in your mind. This lesson names
the piles: concentrates and roughages.

## Sub lesson 1 — Concentrates — the rich feeds

Concentrates are high in protein or carbohydrate. Barley is named the best lot-feeding grain,
with wheat, triticale, sorghum, maize and oats also used — oats alone is imperfect, so it is
mixed with other grains. Home-mixed rations need vitamin and mineral supplements, and by-
product feeds from processing traditional ingredients can be economical nutrient sources.

- Concentrates: high protein or carbohydrate.
- Grains: barley (best), wheat, triticale, sorghum, maize, oats.
- Oats alone is imperfect — mix it.
- Home-mixed rations need vitamin and mineral supplements.

## Sub lesson 2 — Roughages — the fibrous feeds

Roughages are high in fibre: pasture, hay and silage. Hay is mowed, dried forage — the main
winter nutrient source. Silage is high-moisture herbage preserved by controlled fermentation,
made from forage or grain crops.

- Roughages: high in fibre — pasture, hay, silage.
- Hay: mowed, dried forage; main winter nutrient source.
- Silage: high-moisture herbage, preserved by controlled fermentation.

## Say it to your companion

Sort ten feed names for your companion into the two piles, saying each definition once:
"Concentrates — high in protein or carbohydrate. Roughages — high in fibre." Finish with
barley's crown: the best lot-feeding grain.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a two-column feed chart for your locality: concentrates left, roughages right.
2. Write the hay and silage definitions from memory, then check them.
3. Plan a mixed ration on paper: a concentrate, a roughage, and the supplements the book
requires.
4. Say why a by-product feed can be the economical choice.
