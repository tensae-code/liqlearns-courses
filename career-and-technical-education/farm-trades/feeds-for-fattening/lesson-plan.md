# Lesson plan — Fattening — feeds: concentrates and roughages

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Fattening —
feeds: concentrates and roughages
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** feed divides into concentrates (high in protein or
carbohydrate) and roughages (high in fibre — pasture, hay, silage); barley is the best lot-
feeding grain, hay is mowed dried forage, and silage is high-moisture herbage preserved by
controlled fermentation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the site-and-animal lesson — the animal is chosen;
now what goes into it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort any feed: concentrate or roughage, by its definition.
2. Name the lot-feeding grains, with barley best; say why oats alone is imperfect and is mixed.
3. Define hay and silage exactly.
4. Say what home-mixed rations need (vitamin and mineral supplements) and why by-product feeds
can be economical.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Samples of local feeds help, handled as food for animals, not tasted.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts by green/dry instead of content → the definitions rule: protein/carbohydrate against
fibre. Silage is wet and still a roughage.
- Calls hay and silage the same → hay is dried; silage is fermented and moist. The preserving
verb separates them.
