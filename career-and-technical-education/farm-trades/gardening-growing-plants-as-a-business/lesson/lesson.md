# Gardening — growing plants as a business

## Do it first

Before you read anything, name the plants growing near your home, your school or your street.
For each one say: is it eaten, is it looked at, is it used as medicine, or does it give colour?
Keep your list — this lesson grows out of it. First, collect in your mind the medicinal herbs
people in your own locality actually use.

## Sub lesson 1 — What horticulture is

Horticulture is the art and science of growing flowers, fruits, vegetables and shrubs in
gardens and orchards. It is the branch of agriculture that deals with plants only. Agriculture
as a whole is bigger: it covers crops and animals too.

- Horticulture = plants only: flowers, fruits, vegetables, shrubs.
- Agriculture as a whole = plants AND animals.
- Horticulture is practised in gardens and orchards.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gardening — the practice

Gardening is the practice of horticulture: laying out and caring for a plot of land to grow
plants. Plants are grown for their flowers, leaves and appearance, for food, and for dyes,
medicine and cosmetics.

- Gardening = laying out + caring for a plot.
- Grown for: appearance, food, dyes, medicine, cosmetics.
- A garden is a business plot, not only a decoration.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "Horticulture deals with plants only; agriculture covers animals too.
Gardening is laying out and caring for a plot." Your companion names one local plant for food
and one for medicine.

## Do It Yourself

1. Walk your home or school grounds and list ten plants.
2. Beside each plant write its use: food, appearance, dye, medicine or cosmetics.
3. Circle the plants that could be sold. Those are a gardening business waiting to start.
4. Write one sentence: "Horticulture is …" in your own words.
