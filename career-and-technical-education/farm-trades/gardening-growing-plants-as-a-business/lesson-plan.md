# Lesson plan — Gardening — growing plants as a business

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Gardening —
growing plants as a business
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** horticulture is the art and science of growing flowers,
fruits, vegetables and shrubs, and gardening is its practice — laying out and caring for a
plot.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own home and locality — the plants,
herbs and gardens they already see every day. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written
as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books;
on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as
the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its
source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,

2. **Gardening — the practice** — Gardening is the practice of horticulture: laying out and
caring for a plot of land to grow plants.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what horticulture is, and how it differs from agriculture as a whole.
2. Say what gardening is: laying out and caring for a plot to grow plants.
3. Name four reasons plants are grown: flowers and appearance, food, dyes, and medicine and
cosmetics.
4. Name medicinal herbs used in your own locality.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A walk past a real garden helps.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes horticulture with all agriculture → hold the line: horticulture stops at plants; the
moment animals enter, you are speaking of agriculture as a whole.
- Thinks a garden is only decoration → ask what is eaten, sold, dyed or healed from it.
