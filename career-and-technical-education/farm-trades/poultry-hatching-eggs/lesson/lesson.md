# Poultry — hatching eggs: the broody hen and the incubator

## Do it first

A hen sits on eggs for weeks. If she must wander far for food and water, what happens to the
eggs under her? And in a dry place, what does the air steal from the nest? Hold both questions
— the book answers them in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Natural incubation — the broody hen

Under natural incubation a large, healthy, preferably vaccinated broody hen with a good
hatching and mothering record covers the eggs. Eggs become fertile about four days after the
rooster joins the hens. Feed and water are placed near the hen so she stays in condition and
the eggs do not cool, and in dry regions slightly damp soil under the nest helps hold humidity
at 60–80%.

- Hen: large, healthy, preferably vaccinated, broody, proven record.
- Fertility: about four days after the rooster joins.
- Feed and water near the hen — eggs must not cool.
- Dry regions: damp soil under the nest, humidity 60–80%.

## Sub lesson 2 — Artificial incubation — the incubator

Artificial incubators are commercial machines of varying capacity, heated by electricity, gas
or kerosene. A thermostat holds their temperature constant within one degree — the machine
does, steadily, what the hen's body does by instinct.

- Machines of varying capacity.
- Heated by electricity, gas or kerosene.
- Thermostat: temperature constant within one degree.

## Say it to your companion

Compare the two ways for your companion: "The hen gives warmth and turning by instinct; the
machine gives warmth held within one degree by a thermostat." Include the four-day fertility
line.
