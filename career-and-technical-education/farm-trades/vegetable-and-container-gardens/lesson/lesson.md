# Gardening — vegetable gardens and container gardens

## Do it first

Imagine you have no field. Look at the space you do have — a yard, a balcony, a wall side, a
roof edge. Could a bottle, a pot or a barrel stand there? Hold that picture. This lesson is
about growing food in the ground and growing it in containers.

## Sub lesson 1 — The vegetable garden — the full chain

A vegetable garden runs the full chain: choose the site, plan, prepare the soil, choose seeds
and plants, plant, and nurture the plants to harvest. It yields cabbage, carrots and potatoes
to eat, share or sell. As a home garden it supplements a family's food, income and health care
on little money, space or talent — in the ground or in a raised bed.

- Chain: site → plan → soil → seeds/plants → plant → nurture → harvest.
- Yields cabbage, carrots and potatoes — to eat, share or sell.
- A home garden supplements food, income and health care, on little money, space or talent.

## Sub lesson 2 — The container garden

A container garden grows deep-rooted vegetables in bottles, pots, barrels or bought planters.
The book's container list is long: beans, beets, carrots, cucumbers, garlic, lettuce, peas,
peppers, potatoes, spinach, squash and tomatoes. Bigger containers succeed more easily — but
containers need more frequent watering, ideally drip irrigation on a timer.

- Containers: bottles, pots, barrels, bought planters.
- Bigger containers succeed more easily.
- Containers dry out faster — water more often; drip irrigation on a timer is the book's ideal.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the vegetable-garden chain in order, without looking. Then name three
vegetables you would grow in a barrel, and say why the barrel needs watering more often than a
ground bed.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose a real site for a vegetable garden and write the chain for it, step by step.
2. Fill one bottle or pot with soil as your first container.
3. Choose one deep-rooted vegetable from the book's list for it and say why it fits.
4. Write a watering plan: how often, and how you will remember.
