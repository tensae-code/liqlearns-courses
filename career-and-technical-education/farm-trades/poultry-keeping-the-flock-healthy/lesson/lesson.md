# Poultry — keeping the flock healthy

## Do it first

A flock is healthy today. Three weeks later, half are sneezing. Walk the sickness backwards:
what arrived, who was bought, what was carried in? This lesson blocks the roads disease travels
— including the four routes the teacher guide's key names.

## Sub lesson 1 — Keeping disease out

Infectious disease enters through contaminated materials and sick birds, so never buy from
live-bird markets or unknown sources — especially during outbreaks — and quarantine new or
gifted birds for two weeks, observing them before they join the flock. (Teacher guide —
evidence) The guide's answer key names the routes diseases travel: contact, droplet, airborne
and vector-borne — every defence in this lesson blocks one of those roads.

- Entry: contaminated materials and sick birds.
- Never buy from live-bird markets or unknown sources — above all in outbreaks.
- Quarantine: two weeks apart, observed, before joining.
- (Teacher guide — evidence) Transmission: contact, droplet, airborne, vector-borne.

## Sub lesson 2 — Air, disease kinds and control

Poor ventilation lets ammonia from droppings build up and causes respiratory disorders —
sneezing, running eyes, discharges — which good ventilation prevents. Common diseases divide
into infectious (viral, bacterial, parasitic) and non-infectious (feed deficiencies, toxicity).
Control in family flocks rests on improved management and nutrition — hygiene, housing, flock
structure, young-chick care and feeding — plus vaccination, deworming and external-parasite
treatment.

- Ammonia from droppings + poor ventilation = respiratory disorders.
- Infectious: viral, bacterial, parasitic. Non-infectious: feed deficiencies, toxicity.
- Control: management + nutrition, vaccination, deworming, parasite treatment.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the three gates: "Never buy from markets or unknown sources. Quarantine two
weeks. Ventilate." Then name the four transmission routes and one control for each family of
disease.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the four transmission routes as roads into a flock, with the defence that blocks each.
2. Write a quarantine plan for a gifted hen: where, how long, what you watch for.
3. Audit a flock's ventilation by the ammonia smell test a keeper uses, and record the finding.
4. Sort six described illnesses into infectious / non-infectious.
