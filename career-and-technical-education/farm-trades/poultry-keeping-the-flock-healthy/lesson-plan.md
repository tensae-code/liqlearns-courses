# Lesson plan — Poultry — keeping the flock healthy

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Poultry —
keeping the flock healthy
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** disease enters through contaminated materials and sick
birds — never buy from live-bird markets or unknown sources, quarantine new birds two weeks —
ventilation prevents the ammonia respiratory disorders, and control rests on management,
nutrition, vaccination, deworming and parasite treatment; disease transmission runs by contact,
droplet, airborne and vector-borne routes (teacher guide — evidence).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the poultry lessons — a flock exists and is fed
and housed; now defending its health.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,
each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Keeping disease out** — Infectious disease enters through contaminated materials and sick
birds, so never buy from live-bird markets or unknown sources — especially during outbreaks —
and quarantine new or gifted birds for two weeks, observing them before they join the flock.
2. **Air, disease kinds and control** — Poor ventilation lets ammonia from droppings build up
and causes respiratory disorders — sneezing, running eyes, discharges — which good ventilation
prevents.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the buying rule: never buy from live-bird markets or unknown sources, especially
during outbreaks.
2. Run quarantine: new or gifted birds kept apart two weeks and observed before joining the
flock.
3. Explain the ammonia line: poor ventilation lets ammonia from droppings build up, causing
respiratory disorders (sneezing, running eyes, discharges) — good ventilation prevents them.
4. Sort diseases: infectious (viral, bacterial, parasitic) and non-infectious (feed
deficiencies, toxicity).
5. Name the control set: improved management and nutrition — hygiene, housing, flock structure,
young-chick care, feeding — plus vaccination, deworming and external-parasite treatment.
6. Name the transmission routes (teacher guide — evidence): contact, droplet, airborne, vector-
borne.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats quarantine as distrust → it is two weeks of observation that protects the whole flock,
gift birds included.
- Blames 'bad air' vaguely → name it: ammonia from droppings, under poor ventilation.
