# Lesson plan — Gardening — keeping plants healthy: pests, disease and safe spraying

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Gardening —
keeping plants healthy: pests, disease and safe spraying
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** healthy plants resist attack — sun, water, fertility,
beneficial-insect flowers and resistant seed come first; pesticides are the chemical last
resort, sprayed only with gloves, face mask and eye protection.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the starting-a-garden lesson — the garden exists; now
keeping it alive and safe.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,
each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Prevention — the first defences** — Healthy plants resist attack, so the first defences
are not chemicals: adequate sun, water and fertility; flowering plants that attract beneficial
insects; choosing disease- and pest-resistant seed; and clean, healthy transplants.
2. **Pesticides — the last resort, sprayed safely** — Pesticides are the chemical last resort,
sprayed on soil or plants when prevention is not enough.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so
the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the first defences: adequate sun, water and fertility; flowering plants that attract
beneficial insects; disease- and pest-resistant seed; clean, healthy transplants.
2. Say why pesticides are the last resort, not the first.
3. State the spraying safety rule exactly: gloves, face mask and eye protection, because
pesticides harm human health.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any real spraying, the protective gear named in the lesson — and an
adult in charge.
