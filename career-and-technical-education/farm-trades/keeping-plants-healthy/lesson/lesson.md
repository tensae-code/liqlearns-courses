# Gardening — keeping plants healthy: pests, disease and safe spraying

## Do it first

A gardener finds insects eating the cabbage. What should happen first — reach for a chemical
spray, or ask what made the plants weak? Write your instinct down. This lesson gives the book's
order: strength first, chemicals last — and a strict safety rule when chemicals are used.

## Sub lesson 1 — Prevention — the first defences

Healthy plants resist attack, so the first defences are not chemicals: adequate sun, water and
fertility; flowering plants that attract beneficial insects; choosing disease- and pest-
resistant seed; and clean, healthy transplants. A strong garden is attacked less and survives
attack better.

- Adequate sun, water and fertility keep plants strong.
- Flowering plants attract beneficial insects.
- Choose resistant seed and clean, healthy transplants.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pesticides — the last resort, sprayed safely

Pesticides are the chemical last resort, sprayed on soil or plants when prevention is not
enough. Spraying carries a safety rule that is never skipped: gloves, face mask and eye
protection — because pesticides harm human health. That is the book's rule, taught exactly as
the book teaches it.

- Pesticides = chemical last resort, on soil or plants.
- Safety rule: gloves + face mask + eye protection.
- Reason: pesticides harm human health.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the first defences in order, then the safety rule word for word: "Gloves,
face mask and eye protection — because pesticides harm human health."

## Do It Yourself

1. Inspect a real garden and score its first defences: sun? water? fertility? flowers for
beneficial insects?
2. Write the spraying safety rule on a card and place it where spraying chemicals are stored —
with an adult's agreement.
3. List the seed you would choose next season with resistance in mind.
4. Say in one sentence why spray is last, not first.
