# Dairy farming — milk as a business

## Do it first

Think of the milk in a cup at home. Where did it come from — which animal, which farm, whose
hands, what road? Follow it backwards as far as you can. This lesson is that journey, run as a
business.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a dairy is

A dairy is the business of harvesting and processing animal milk for human consumption — mostly
cows, also goats, sheep and camels. It runs on a dedicated dairy farm or as a section of a
mixed farm. A dairy farm produces the milk; a dairy factory processes it into products such as
yogurt, cheese and butter.

- Animals: cows mostly; also goats, sheep, camels.
- Place: a dairy farm, or a section of a mixed farm.
- Farm produces milk; factory processes it into yogurt, cheese, butter.

## Sub lesson 2 — When dairying pays

Large-scale dairying only pays where durable products are made or a paying market exists — milk
spoils, so scale without a market is loss. At the small end, farmers earn side income taking
morning milk to town: a daily, modest, reliable earning alongside the farm's other work.

- Large scale pays only with durable products or a paying market.
- Small farmers: morning milk taken to town = side income.
- (Teacher guide — evidence) Cattle by production purpose: beef-only, dairy-only and dual-
purpose — the guide's answer key; the student book's review asks it but its body never teaches
it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion: farm produces, factory processes. Then give the three cattle types
by purpose and say which one a milk seller keeps.
