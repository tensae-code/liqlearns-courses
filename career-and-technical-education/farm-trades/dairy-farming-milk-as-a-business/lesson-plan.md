# Lesson plan — Dairy farming — milk as a business

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Dairy farming
— milk as a business
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a dairy is the business of harvesting and processing
animal milk — mostly cows, also goats, sheep and camels — on a dairy farm or a section of a
mixed farm; the farm produces milk, the factory processes it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own locality — milk, milk cans and
morning trips to town are familiar sights.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written
as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books;
on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as
the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its
source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,
each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What a dairy is** — A dairy is the business of harvesting and processing animal milk for
human consumption — mostly cows, also goats, sheep and camels.
2. **When dairying pays** — Large-scale dairying only pays where durable products are made or a
paying market exists — milk spoils, so scale without a market is loss.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so
the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a dairy is and which animals give its milk.
2. Tell a dairy farm from a dairy factory: the farm produces, the factory processes — yogurt,
cheese, butter.
3. Say when large-scale dairying pays: where durable products are made or a paying market
exists.
4. Say how small farmers earn side income from milk.
5. Name the cattle types by production purpose (teacher guide — evidence): beef-only, dairy-
only, dual-purpose.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses farm and factory → one verb each: produce (farm), process (factory).
- Thinks bigger is always better → milk spoils; without durable products or a paying market,
scale loses money.
