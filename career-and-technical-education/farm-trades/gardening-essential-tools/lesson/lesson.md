# Gardening — the essential tools

## Do it first

Here are three jobs: dig a hole for a seedling, cut back a thick branch, clear a patch of rough
land. For each job, name the tool you would pick up first. Keep your choices — the lesson's
tool table will judge them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The pictured tool set

Before planting, the gardener prepares the pictured tool set of Table 3.1: gloves, pruning
shears (secateurs), loppers, garden fork, hand trowel, spade, rake, hoe — and the traditional
hand hoe. Gloves protect the hands; shears and loppers cut growth back; the fork, trowel, spade
and hoes work the soil; the rake levels and clears it.

- Gloves — protect the hands.
- Pruning shears (secateurs) and loppers — cut and prune growth.
- Garden fork, hand trowel, spade, hoe, traditional hand hoe — work the soil.
- Rake — levels and clears the ground.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional tools of the locality

The hoe for digging and the sickle for clearing land are named as the traditional tools of
rural gardens. Every locality has its own traditional tools — the lesson's own work is to list
the ones used where you live, with each one's use beside it.

- Hoe — digging (traditional tool).
- Sickle — clearing land (traditional tool).
- Your locality's list: tool name + its use, for each.

## Say it to your companion

Lay the nine tool names before your companion like cards. Your companion names a job — digging,
pruning, levelling — and you pick the tool and say why.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the nine tools in a grid and label each one.
2. Beside each label write its job in three words or fewer.
3. Add your locality's traditional tools to the grid, with their uses.
4. Check your grid against a real gardener's tools if you can.
