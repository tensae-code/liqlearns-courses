# Poultry — chickens for eggs, meat and income

## Do it first

One hen. Follow her gifts across a year: eggs on many mornings, chicks some seasons, and
herself or her sons sellable when money is suddenly needed. Add it up in your mind. This lesson
is why the smallest livestock carries so much weight.

## Sub lesson 1 — The smallest livestock

Chickens are almost universal in rural households — the smallest livestock investment a village
family can make. They supply protein and income together: eggs give small regular income, and
live birds give flexible cash when a family needs money at a particular moment.

- Almost universal in rural households.
- Smallest livestock investment a village family can make.
- Eggs: small regular income. Live birds: flexible cash when needed.

## Sub lesson 2 — What poultry keeping supports

Small-scale production meets a family's animal-nutrition demand. Across rural, urban and semi-
urban Ethiopia, poultry keeping supports poverty alleviation, nutrition and food security — a
small bird doing national-scale work, household by household.

- Meets the family's animal-nutrition demand.
- Supports poverty alleviation, nutrition and food security.
- Practised in rural, urban and semi-urban Ethiopia.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two incomes and which is which: "Eggs — little and often. A live bird —
cash when the need comes." Then name the three big things poultry keeping supports.

## Do It Yourself

1. Survey your street: who keeps chickens, how many, and what do they get from them?
2. Write the protein path: hen → eggs/meat → family's nutrition.
3. List what a first small flock would need, from the lessons ahead.
4. Say in one sentence why the smallest livestock matters to food security.
