# Lesson plan — Poultry — chickens for eggs, meat and income

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Poultry —
chickens for eggs, meat and income
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** chickens are almost universal in rural households — the
smallest livestock investment a village family can make — supplying protein and income: eggs
give small regular income, live birds give flexible cash when needed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own household — chickens around the
home are the entry point.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written
as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books;
on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as
the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its
source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why chickens are called the family's smallest livestock investment.
2. Tell the two incomes apart: eggs — small and regular; live birds — flexible cash when
needed.
3. Say what small-scale production meets (a family's animal-nutrition demand) and what poultry
keeping supports across rural, urban and semi-urban Ethiopia (poverty alleviation, nutrition,
food security).

## If the learner is stuck
- Sees chickens as too small to matter → add the year up: regular eggs plus flexible bird sales
plus nutrition.
- Confuses the two incomes → eggs repeat; a bird sells once, at the moment of need.
