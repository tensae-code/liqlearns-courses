# Lesson plan — Poultry — choosing the best birds and eggs

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Poultry —
choosing the best birds and eggs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** six selection criteria: hatching ability, mothering,
growth rate, egg selection for hatching, day-old chick quality, and disease resistance.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the systems lesson — the keeping way is chosen; now
choosing the birds themselves.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,
each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Choosing the birds** — Three criteria judge the birds themselves: good hatching ability
(hens that incubate well); good mothering (protecting chicks and escaping predators); and high
growth rate — keep fast growers, and cull poor growers, dwarfs, late maturers and surplus cocks
at slaughter age.
2. **Choosing eggs and chicks** — Egg selection for hatching: average size, sound shell with no
deformity or cracks, broader bottom with a sharper tip.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so
the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Recite the six criteria.
2. Apply them: keep fast growers; cull poor growers, dwarfs, late maturers and surplus cocks at
slaughter age.
3. Choose a hatching egg: average size, sound shell, no deformity or cracks, broader bottom
with a sharper tip.
4. Judge a day-old chick: good body condition, no leg or eye deformity, keen to eat and drink.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Real eggs to inspect help — handled gently, not for eating after handling
practice unless a keeper says so.
