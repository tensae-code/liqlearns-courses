# Poultry — choosing the best birds and eggs

## Do it first

A tray of eggs, all promised for hatching. Some will never hatch well. By eye alone — size,
shell, shape — which do you choose, and which do you reject? This lesson's six criteria turn
guessing into judging.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing the birds

Three criteria judge the birds themselves: good hatching ability (hens that incubate well);
good mothering (protecting chicks and escaping predators); and high growth rate — keep fast
growers, and cull poor growers, dwarfs, late maturers and surplus cocks at slaughter age.
Disease resistance completes the bird side: breed from birds that survive outbreaks.

- Hatching ability: hens that incubate well.
- Mothering: protects chicks, escapes predators.
- Growth: keep fast growers; cull poor growers, dwarfs, late maturers, surplus cocks.
- Disease resistance: breed from outbreak survivors.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing eggs and chicks

Egg selection for hatching: average size, sound shell with no deformity or cracks, broader
bottom with a sharper tip. Day-old chick quality: good body condition, no leg or eye deformity,
and keen to eat and drink — a chick that will not eat or drink is failing its first test.

- Hatching egg: average size, sound shell, no cracks/deformity, broad bottom, sharp tip.
- Day-old chick: good condition, straight legs and eyes, keen to eat and drink.

## Say it to your companion

Recite the six criteria to your companion. Then judge aloud: "This egg — average, sound, broad
bottom, sharp tip: hatch it. This one — cracked: reject."

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six criteria as a judging card.
2. Inspect real eggs against the egg rule and sort them: hatch / not for hatching.
3. Describe a day-old chick that passes, point by point.
4. Say which birds in a described flock you would keep, cull, and breed from.
