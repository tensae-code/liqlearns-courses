# Lesson plan — Beekeeping — the beekeeper's kit

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Beekeeping —
the beekeeper's kit
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the beekeeper's pictured kit (Table 3.2): veil,
protective clothing, smoker, sprayer, hive tool, queen excluder, honey extractor, honey
presser, wax extractor, honey sieve and uncapping fork — protection prevents most stings, smoke
gives control, and the hive tool works the hives.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the starting-out lesson — the apiary is established;
now the kit the keeper carries into it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written
as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**.
The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books;
on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as
the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its
source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the eleven tools of Table 3.2.
2. Match kit to purpose: protection (veil, protective clothing), control (smoker, sprayer, hive
tool), and the honey/wax working tools.
3. State the text's reasoning: protective clothing prevents most stings; smoke gives some
control over the bees.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Real kit shown by a beekeeper helps — handled only as the keeper directs.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sees the smoker as harm → the book's word is control: smoke calms and gives the keeper
control; it is not a weapon.
- Mixes worn kit with hive kit → veil and clothing are worn; excluder lives in the hive;
extractor works at harvest.
