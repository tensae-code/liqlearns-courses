# Beekeeping — the beekeeper's kit

## Do it first

You are about to open a hive of forty thousand bees. What is on your body, what is in your
hands, what waits back at the shed for harvest day? Sort the beekeeper's eleven tools into
those three piles — protection, working, harvest — as this lesson names them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Protection and control tools

Beyond the hive, the beekeeper needs protection and control tools, pictured in Table 3.2: veil,
protective clothing, smoker, sprayer and hive tool head the list. The text's reasoning is
plain: protective clothing prevents most stings, smoke gives some control over the bees, and
the hive tool works the hives.

- Veil and protective clothing — prevent most stings.
- Smoker — smoke gives some control over the bees.
- Sprayer; hive tool — works the hives.

## Sub lesson 2 — The honey and wax tools

The rest of the table serves the harvest and the products: queen excluder, honey extractor,
honey presser, wax extractor, honey sieve and uncapping fork. Each belongs to a product of the
colony lesson's list — honey and wax above all. Beekeepers in your own area use their own
version of this kit — listing it is the lesson's local work.

- Queen excluder — keeps the queen from the honey section.
- Honey extractor, honey presser, honey sieve, uncapping fork — the honey tools.
- Wax extractor — the wax tool.

## Say it to your companion

Name the eleven for your companion, family by family: protection, control, harvest. Your
companion drops one name; you restore it and its job.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the kit in a labelled grid, veil and smoker first.
2. Sort the eleven into protection / control / harvest columns.
3. List the equipment beekeepers in your own area actually use — the lesson's own task.
4. Say the text's reasoning in one line: clothing, smoke, hive tool.
