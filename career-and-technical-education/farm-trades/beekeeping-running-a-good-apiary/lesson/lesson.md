# Beekeeping — running a good apiary

## Do it first

Picture an apiary done badly: hives beside a busy road, under noon sun all day, a long walk
from any water, no fence, neighbours annoyed. List every fault you can see. This lesson's site
rules will grade your list.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where hives stand — people, sun, water

Good management starts with where hives hang or stand: a poor site gets people and animals
stung and honey stolen. Rules: the site must be easy to reach for regular checks; fence or
hedge it off from people and animals and keep it away from dwellings, roads and public areas;
shade it in the hot part of the day but give morning sun; shelter it from strong wind while
keeping air moving; and keep it near fresh water — river, pond, even a dripping tap.

- Easy to reach for regular checks.
- Fenced/hedged off; away from dwellings, roads, public areas.
- Midday shade + morning sun; wind shelter + moving air.
- Near fresh water: river, pond, dripping tap.

## Sub lesson 2 — Protection — and the wider weighing

A lockable bee house is an anti-theft option, if wall holes let bees come and go; keep bees
away from smoke, fire and unfriendly neighbours. Site choice also weighs sunrise, wind
direction, freedom from pollution and bee disease, dry open ground, natural or artificial
windbreaks, and rich bee flora nearby.

- Lockable bee house (with bee holes) against theft.
- Keep away: smoke, fire, unfriendly neighbours.
- Weigh also: sunrise, wind direction, no pollution/disease, dry open ground, windbreaks, rich
flora.

## Say it to your companion

Recite the site rules to your companion as an inspection: "Reach it? Fence it? Away from roads?
Shade at noon, sun in the morning? Water near?" — six questions, all answerable yes or no.
