# Lesson plan — Beekeeping — running a good apiary

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Farm Trades · **Lesson:** Beekeeping —
running a good apiary
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Farm Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a good apiary site is fenced from people and animals and
away from dwellings, roads and public areas, shaded at midday with morning sun, sheltered from
strong wind with air moving, near fresh water, and safe from theft, smoke, fire and unfriendly
neighbours.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the starting-out and kit lessons — the apiary exists
and the keeper is equipped; now running the site well, day after day.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,
the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the site rules: easy access for regular checks; fenced/hedged from people and animals;
away from dwellings, roads and public areas.
2. State the sun/wind/water rules: shade in the hot part of the day, morning sun; shelter from
strong wind while air keeps moving; near fresh water (river, pond, even a dripping tap).
3. Name the protection options and enemies: a lockable bee house against theft (with wall holes
for the bees); keep bees away from smoke, fire and unfriendly neighbours.
4. List the wider site-choice factors: sunrise, wind direction, freedom from pollution and bee
disease, dry open ground, windbreaks, rich bee flora nearby.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts hives near home 'to watch them' → the rule is distance from dwellings and roads;
watching is done by regular checks on an easy-to-reach site.
- Gives total shade → bees want morning sun; it is the hot part of the day that needs shade.
