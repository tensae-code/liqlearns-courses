# What CTE Is — Learning Joined to Doing

## Do it first

Think of one skilled worker you know — a builder, a tailor, a farmer, a hairdresser. Answer two questions about them:

1. What do they know from learning — reading, numbers, facts?
2. What can their hands do?

If your two answers are both full, you have already found this lesson.

## Two kinds of education, joined

**Career and Technical Education — CTE** — combines two kinds of education:

- **Academic education** — education whose purpose is *learning*.
- **Technical education** — education in *practical skills for performing a task*.

Joined together, CTE teaches **specific, technology-based, career-oriented skills**. It introduces **workplace competencies — knowledge, skills and attitudes** — in a **real-world, applied context**: you learn the thing where the thing is really done, in the way it is really done.

[Picture 11804 — learning joined to doing]

And it points in two directions at once: CTE **prepares learners for further education, and for employment across economic sectors**. It is not a dead end. It is a door that opens both ways.

## Say it to your companion

Explain CTE to your companion without using the words "career" or "technical": say what is joined, what three competencies it builds, and what two futures it prepares you for. Then let your companion try.

## Do It Yourself

1. Pick one trade from your locality list.
2. Write its "book hand": two things a learner must learn (facts, numbers, reading).
3. Write its "tool hand": two things a learner's hands must practise.
4. Write one sentence: where would this trade's CTE learning happen — classroom, workshop, field, or all three?
