# Women's hair — the basic styling techniques

## Do it first

Think of the women's styles you see in a week: hair blown smooth, hair straightened, curls,
plaits raised up. Sort them into families in your mind. This lesson names the book's six
technique families — and the one definition and three preparations that stand before them all.

## Sub lesson 1 — The definition and the three preparations

Women's styling is defined as washing with shampoo, applying styling lotion, setting the hair
on rollers and drying it gently before shaping a soft curl style. And every styling begins with
three preparations: prepare yourself, prepare the client, prepare the work area.

- Definition chain: shampoo → lotion → rollers → gentle drying → soft curl style.
- Three preparations: yourself, the client, the work area.

## Sub lesson 2 — The six techniques

The basic techniques: blow drying (flat, root lift, movement), straightening, smoothing,
curling (waves and curls), finger drying (natural look, spiky effect) and hair up (plait,
pleat, rolls, curls). Pupils discuss Ethiopian and local women's styles in groups — the
techniques are universal, the styles are the locality's own.

- Blow drying: flat, root lift, movement.
- Straightening; smoothing; curling: waves and curls.
- Finger drying: natural look, spiky effect.
- Hair up: plait, pleat, rolls, curls.

## Say it to your companion

Recite the definition chain and the three preparations to your companion, then the six
techniques with one look from each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the definition chain as a five-link diagram.
2. Make a six-tile technique chart with the looks under each.
3. Discuss (in a group or with family) Ethiopian and local women's styles — the book's own
activity — and assign each style its technique.
4. Say the three preparations before any styling you plan.
