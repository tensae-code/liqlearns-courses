# Hair styling — reading the client before you cut

## Do it first

A client sits down and says, "Make me look good." Before any tool is picked up, what should a
wise stylist find out — about the hair, the head, the face, and the person's life? Write your
questions. The book's check-card in this lesson is longer than most learners expect.

## Sub lesson 1 — The six factors first

Before styling, the stylist weighs: previous chemical services, percentage of grey, the
client's requirements, tools and equipment, added hair, and how the style will be maintained.
Six questions, asked before the work — because each one can change what the right style even
is.

- Previous chemical services.
- Percentage of grey.
- The client's requirements.
- Tools and equipment.
- Added hair.
- How the style will be maintained.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading hair and head in detail

Then the stylist reads the client in detail: hair condition (dry, greasy, normal, virgin,
chemically treated; also elasticity and porosity), the cut/style (uniform layer, one length,
short or long graduation), temperature, texture (fine, medium, coarse), length, density, growth
patterns (cowlick, widow's peak, nape whorl, double crown, male-pattern baldness), skin tone,
face shape (oval, round, square, oblong, heart, pear), head size, existing curl, and the
client's lifestyle.

- Condition: dry/greasy/normal/virgin/treated; elasticity; porosity.
- Texture: fine, medium, coarse. Growth: cowlick, widow's peak, nape whorl, double crown,
baldness patterns.
- Face shapes: oval, round, square, oblong, heart, pear.
- Plus length, density, temperature, head size, curl, lifestyle.

## Say it to your companion

Run the six factors as questions to your companion, playing a new client. Your companion
answers in character — a grey percentage, a past chemical service — and you say how each answer
changes the styling plan.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make the stylist's check-card: six factor boxes on top, the detail list below.
2. Practise reading (visually, no touching without consent) texture and face shape on
volunteers or pictures.
3. Write three growth patterns and how each warns the stylist.
4. Say why 'how the style will be maintained' is a before-cut question.
