# Men's hair — safety, hygiene and hair damage

## Do it first

A client leaves a salon with hair that snaps like dry grass and a scalp that itches for weeks.
What was done to them? List the dangers you can imagine. This lesson teaches the book's five
safety topics — exactly as the book teaches them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Washing and colouring

(1) Washing: shampoo with water removes sweat, oil and product; conditioner afterwards smooths
the roughened cuticle and protects the hair. (2) Colouring: adding or removing pigment —
colouring against bleaching — requires opening the cuticle, and the chemicals can leave hair
dry, weak and breakage-prone.

- Shampoo removes sweat, oil, product; conditioner smooths and protects the cuticle.
- Colouring/bleaching opens the cuticle to change pigment.
- Chemical cost: dry, weak, breakage-prone hair.

## Sub lesson 2 — Perms, damage and hair loss

(3) Perms and chemical straightening — relaxing or thermal reconditioning — alter the hair's
internal structure; permed hair is weaker and must be handled gently. (4) Breakage and damage
come from chemical exposure and prolonged or repeated heat; oil harms rough hair and dry scalp;
the scalp can suffer mites, lice, follicle infections, fungus and allergic reactions, with a
weeks-long odour signalling a scalp health problem. (5) General hair loss: by choice (shaving
the head) or through illness and its treatment.

- Perms/straightening alter internal structure; permed hair is weaker — handle gently.
- Damage: chemicals, prolonged/repeated heat, oil on rough hair/dry scalp.
- Scalp troubles: mites, lice, follicle infections, fungus, allergy; weeks-long odour = health
signal.
- Hair loss: by choice, or illness and its treatment.

## Say it to your companion

Teach your companion the five topics as warnings with reasons: "Colour opens the cuticle — so
hair can come out dry and weak. Permed hair is weaker — so handle it gently."

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five topics as a salon safety wall: one panel each, with its rule.
2. Sort ten described salon events into safe / damaging, citing the topic.
3. Ask a local barber about safety and hygienic styling — the book's own activity — and record
the answers.
4. Write what a weeks-long scalp odour signals, and what a stylist should not ignore.
