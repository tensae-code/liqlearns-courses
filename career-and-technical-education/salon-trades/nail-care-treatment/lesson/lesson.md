# Nail care — trimming, filing and setting up a treatment

## Do it first

Look at your own fingernails. Which need trimming? Which edges are rough? Now — before anyone
touches them — what must be prepared: whose hands, what space, which tools? This lesson gives
the reasons nail care is health care, and the four preparations of a treatment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why nails are cared for

Keeping nails trimmed and filed reduces fungus in the nails and relieves the pressure pain
thickened nails cause. Nail care is not vanity first — it is health first, appearance
following. Nail-care tools and equipment are the durable, hand-held articles of the service —
things made to be held, used, cleaned and used again.

- Trimmed and filed: less nail fungus.
- Trimmed nails relieve the pressure pain of thickened nails.
- Tools/equipment: the durable, hand-held articles of the service.

## Sub lesson 2 — Setting up — the four preparations

A treatment is set up in four preparations: (1) prepare the beautician/worker, (2) prepare the
model (client), (3) prepare the work area, and (4) prepare the products, tools and equipment.
The same three-preparation rhythm from women's styling, with the kit itself added as the fourth
— because here the whole service runs through the hand-held tools.

- 1. Prepare the beautician/worker.
- 2. Prepare the model (client).
- 3. Prepare the work area.
- 4. Prepare the products, tools and equipment.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion nail care's two health reasons, then the four preparations in order —
worker, client, area, kit.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four preparations as a treatment set-up card.
2. Inspect your own nails against the lesson: length, edges, thickness — record, do not treat
in this step.
3. List the durable hand-held articles a nail service needs, from the lesson's definition.
4. Say why 'durable' matters: the tools are cleaned and used again — which points to the
hygiene lesson.
