# Women's hair — blow-drying well

## Do it first

A dryer screams, a brush tangles, hair frizzes instead of smoothing. Most blow-dry failures
were decided before the switch — wrong product, unjudged hair, surprises unprepared for. List
what you would check first. The book's three preparation points are this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Before the dryer switches on

Blow-drying skill starts before the dryer switches on: (1) identify the condition and thickness
of the hair in hand, (2) choose a product that will support the blow-dry on that hair, and (3)
know what hair problems may occur during drying and how to deal with them. Preparation is not
delay — it is the first half of the skill.

- Identify: condition and thickness of this hair.
- Choose: a product that supports the blow-dry on this hair.
- Know: the problems that may occur, and their handling.

## Sub lesson 2 — The moving skill

With preparation done, the blow-dry itself is a two-handed moving skill: the brush rolls under
a section of hair while the dryer follows it, and the hair smooths as the pair passes — the
motion the GIF in this lesson shows, section by section, over the whole head.

- Brush rolls under the section; dryer follows.
- Hair smooths as the two pass together.
- Section by section over the whole head.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the three befores as a checklist — "Condition and thickness? Product
chosen? Problems known?" — then describe the brush-and-dryer motion in one sentence.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a blow-dry preparation card for three hair types: fine, thick, chemically treated
(from the safety lesson).
2. Practise the rolling-brush motion in the air, dryer hand following, until both hands move
together.
3. List problems that may occur during drying and a dealing for each, from what the styling and
safety lessons teach.
4. Time a full preparation: under two minutes, nothing skipped.
