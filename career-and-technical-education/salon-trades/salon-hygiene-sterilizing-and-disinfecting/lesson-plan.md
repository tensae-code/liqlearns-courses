# Lesson plan — Salon hygiene — sterilizing and disinfecting tools

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Salon Trades · **Lesson:** Salon
hygiene — sterilizing and disinfecting tools
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Salon Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sterilization is the strongest form of cleaning — it
removes all bacteria, fungi and viruses — while disinfection kills most (not all) on tool
surfaces and cannot destroy resistant spores or some viruses; between every client, tools are
washed in hot soapy water, rinsed, dried, disinfected and stored clean.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands across the whole salon title — every tool lesson before
it ends here, at the hygiene line that protects the next client.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below,

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead,
each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Sterilization — the strongest cleaning** — Sterilization is the strongest form of
cleaning: it removes all bacteria, fungi and viruses, and matters most for tools that have been
used.
2. **Disinfection — strong, with limits, and the routine** — Disinfection kills most — not all
— bacteria and fungi on tool surfaces, and cannot destroy resistant bacterial spores or some
viruses.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define sterilization exactly and name its kit: alcohol-based hand rub, liquid soap,
disposable hand towels, disposable gloves, labelled rigid airtight washable containers for used
equipment.
2. Define disinfection by its limits: kills most — not all — bacteria and fungi; cannot destroy
resistant bacterial spores or some viruses.
3. Run the between-clients routine in order: wash in hot soapy water, rinse thoroughly, dry,
disinfect, store clean.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For practice: the hygiene kit items, used under a supervising practitioner.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses the two words as synonyms → anchor on the quantifiers: ALL against MOST. That one word
is the whole lesson.
- Skips the wash before disinfecting → the routine's order is fixed: wash, rinse, dry,
disinfect, store.
