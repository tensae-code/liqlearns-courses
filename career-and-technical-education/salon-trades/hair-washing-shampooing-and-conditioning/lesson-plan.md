# Lesson plan — Hair washing — how shampooing and conditioning work

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Salon Trades · **Lesson:** Hair washing
— how shampooing and conditioning work
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Salon Trades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** shampoo and conditioner are cosmetic products built on
surfactants that clean the hair; water alone cannot mix with fatty dirt, so a dispersing agent
(detergent) dissolves it; shampoo is massaged into wet hair — the massaging mechanically
increases washing effectiveness — and warm/hot water is recommended.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the men's safety lesson, where washing came first —
now the science of the wash itself.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what shampoo and conditioner are: cosmetic products (liquid, cream, solid or powder)
built on surfactants.
2. Explain why water alone fails: it cannot mix with fatty dirt, so a dispersing agent
(detergent) is needed to dissolve the dirt of hair and scalp.
3. State the technique: shampoo massaged into wet hair; the massaging mechanically increases
washing effectiveness.
4. State the water rule: warm/hot water is recommended — noting heat dilates pores and
stimulates the scalp.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For practice: a backwash basin or bowl, shampoo, and a model or volunteer,
under a practitioner.
