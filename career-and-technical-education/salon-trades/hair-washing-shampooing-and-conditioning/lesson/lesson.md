# Hair washing — how shampooing and conditioning work

## Do it first

Grease and plain water refuse each other — you have felt it at a sink. Hair carries fatty dirt
of the same kind. So what does shampoo actually add, and why do hairdressers massage instead of
just pouring? This lesson answers both, from the book.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why shampoo works

Shampoo and conditioner are cosmetic products — liquid, cream, solid or powder — built on
surfactants that clean the hair and can also treat it. Water alone cannot mix with fatty dirt,
so a dispersing agent (detergent) is needed to dissolve the dirt of hair and scalp: the
surfactant is the bridge between water and grease.

- Shampoo/conditioner: cosmetics in liquid, cream, solid or powder form.
- Built on surfactants that clean (and can treat) the hair.
- Water alone cannot mix with fatty dirt — detergent disperses and dissolves it.

## Sub lesson 2 — How the wash is done

Shampoo is massaged into wet hair — and the massaging mechanically increases washing
effectiveness: fingers are part of the chemistry's success. Warm/hot water is recommended for
the wash, while noting that heat dilates pores and stimulates the scalp. Pupils visit a
hairdresser and list the techniques actually used.

- Massage shampoo into wet hair.
- Massaging mechanically increases effectiveness.
- Warm/hot water recommended; heat dilates pores, stimulates the scalp.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the grease problem to your companion: "Water and fatty dirt will not mix — so the
detergent in shampoo disperses the dirt, and my fingers' massage makes the washing work
harder."
