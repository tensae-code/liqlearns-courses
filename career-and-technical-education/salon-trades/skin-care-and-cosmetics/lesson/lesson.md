# Skin care — looking after skin, and what cosmetics do

## Do it first

Your skin works all week in sun, dust and sweat. What does it need from you — and what can a
salon add? Make your list before reading. This lesson closes the salon unit with the book's
skin care: its daily practices, its facial treatments, and what cosmetics actually are.

## Sub lesson 1 — Skin care — the practices

Skin care is the range of practices that support skin integrity, enhance appearance and relieve
skin conditions: nutrition, avoiding excessive sun, and appropriate emollients, plus cosmetics
for appearance. It reaches further than beauty: wound healing, radiation therapy and medication
contexts, care of skin that is too dry or too moist, dermatitis prevention and injury
prevention.

- Supports skin integrity, enhances appearance, relieves conditions.
- Base: nutrition, avoiding excessive sun, appropriate emollients.
- Reach: wound healing, therapy/medication contexts, dry/moist skin, dermatitis and injury
prevention.

## Sub lesson 2 — Facials and cosmetics

Facial skin care is the named treatment family — steam, exfoliation (physical and chemical),
extraction, creams, lotions, masks, peels and massage — normally performed in beauty salons for
general skin health and specific conditions. Cosmetics are mixtures of chemical compounds from
natural or synthetic sources, used to cleanse or protect the body and skin, to conceal
blemishes, enhance features, add colour and fragrance, or change a face's appearance entirely.

- Facial family: steam, exfoliation, extraction, creams, lotions, masks, peels, massage.
- Exfoliation: physical and chemical.
- Cosmetics: chemical-compound mixtures, natural or synthetic; cleanse/protect, conceal,
enhance, colour/fragrance, transform.
