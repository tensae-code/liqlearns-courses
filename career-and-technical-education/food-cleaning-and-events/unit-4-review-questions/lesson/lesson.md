## The study sheet — what the book's review tests

**Hospitality and food.** Hospitality is the guest–host relationship received with goodwill; its events (weddings, meetings, promotions, conferences, fundraisers) are gathered under the hospitality-events teaching of this title. Food provision at sites and venues is the food preparation (catering) business; counter service orders at the counter, table service at the table. The knife is the kitchen's cutting and scoring tool. A balanced diet carries vitamins, minerals, antioxidants, carbohydrates, protein and healthy fats, from fruits, vegetables, grains, dairy and protein foods. Liquid detergent is a cleaning agent in liquid form — one of the seven families' everyday members.

**Cleaning.** A cleaner's skills: interpersonal and communication skills, physical abilities, numeracy and literacy, attention to detail — and cleaning skills (the review's own multiple-choice corner) are what a cleaner is hired for beyond any certificate, since no specific qualifications are needed to start. Event managers carry health-and-safety responsibility for their events (the review's true/false corner — organisers must keep guests safe, as the risk-audit teaching shows). PPE is worn to minimise exposure to hazards — used properly, the right items for the job's hazards, as Cleaning Safely teaches. Cleaning equipment: vacuum with attachments, mop and bucket, dusters, dustpan and broom, paper towels, trash cans and liners.

**Fresh food cooked properly.** The review asks after cooking fresh food and cleaners' duties and dress: fresh foods are served raw where the book lists them raw, and cooked into snacks (roasted chickpeas, snack mixes) and the cultural dishes exactly as their lessons teach; a cleaner's dress for office cleaning follows the appearance rules — clean, pressed, fitting clothing, closed shoes, hair neat (and tied back near equipment and food). Duties of a cleaner follow the scope: routine internal cleaning, deep cleans, and the work-area standard of the four benefits.
