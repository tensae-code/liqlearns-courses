# Cleaning Safely — PPE and How a Cleaner Presents

## Do it first

A cleaner works a hotel shift: chemicals splash, floors are wet, a vacuum hums, rubbish holds surprises. List everything that could hurt or sicken them in one shift. Then read how the book armours the cleaner — and how it dresses them.

## Sub lesson 1 — PPE — equipment that minimises exposure

**Personal protective equipment (PPE) is equipment worn to minimise exposure to hazards causing serious injury and illness.** The hazard families: **chemical, physical, electrical and mechanical**.

The kit the book lists: **gloves, safety glasses and shoes, earplugs or muffs, hard hats, respirators, coveralls, vests and full body suits**. As in the carpentry shop, the list is a system — each item answers a different hazard, and the job in front of you decides which items you wear.

## Sub lesson 2 — Appearance — how a cleaner presents

**Clothing: clean, pressed, in good condition and fitting appropriately. Socks or tights are worn with shoes and match the pants/shoes. No open-toe shoes; high heels not recommended.**

**Hair, sideburns, moustache and beard: neat and groomed, with no extreme artificial colours. Long hair is tied back and away from the face when operating equipment or working in food and beverage.** And the closing clause that ties dress to safety: **clothing must not interfere with operating equipment.** Appearance here is not vanity — it is presentation and safety in one list.

## Say it to your companion

Define PPE for your companion, list the kit, then give the appearance rules — ending with the two equipment clauses.
