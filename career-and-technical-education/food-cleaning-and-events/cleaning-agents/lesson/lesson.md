# Cleaning Agents — the Seven Families

## Do it first

List every cleaning substance in your home — under the sink, by the bucket, in the bathroom. Beside each, write what dirt it fights. The book sorts all of them into seven families; your list will land inside it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What cleaning agents are

**Cleaning agents (hard-surface cleaners) are the substances — usually liquids, powders, sprays or granules — used to remove dirt, dust, stains, bad smells and clutter from surfaces.**

They are the chemistry leg of the cleaning blend, bottled: each agent is a chemical answer to a kind of unwanted substance. The next sub lesson is the book's classification — the seven families every agent belongs to.

## Sub lesson 2 — The seven families

The classification runs: **water, detergents, abrasives, degreasers, acid cleaners, organic solvents, and other cleaning agents** — seven families.

Learn them as a shelf in order: water itself is family one — the base agent every cleaner uses; detergents, the everyday washers; abrasives, which scour by friction; degreasers, aimed at grease; acid cleaners, for the deposits acids dissolve; organic solvents, dissolving what water cannot; and the honest seventh family — everything else the trade uses. Choosing an agent is choosing a family first.

## Say it to your companion

Define cleaning agents for your companion, then list the seven families in order — water to 'others' — with one dirt each family answers.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven families from memory and check the order.
2. Classify five home products by label into the families.
3. Write one sentence: why does the classification end with an 'other' family?
