# Hand Washing — the Ten Steps

## Do it first

Go to a sink now and wash your hands your usual way. How many distinct rubs did you make? Did you touch anything with wet hands afterwards? Return and read the book's ten steps — a class drill, practised step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — What hand washing is — and why drying counts

**Hand washing (hand hygiene) is cleaning the hands with soap and water to remove viruses, bacteria and microorganisms, dirt, grease and other harmful substances.**

And **drying is part of the process, because wet hands re-contaminate easily**. A wash that ends with wet hands touching the next surface has spent its work — the dry finish is a step, not an afterthought.

## Sub lesson 2 — The ten steps

**(1) Wet hands with water. (2) Apply enough liquid soap for a good lather. (3) Rub palms together. (4) Rub the backs of the hands, interchanging hands. (5) Interlink the fingers. (6) Cup the fingers. (7) Clean the thumbs. (8) Rub palms with the fingers. (9) Rinse with water until clean. (10) Dry the hands before touching any object.**

Notice the design: the rubs (3–8) visit every surface of the hand — palms, backs, between fingers, cupped fingers, thumbs, fingertips in palms. Whatever a rub skips, the microbes keep. The book practises the sequence **step by step, as a class drill** — and this lesson's hands-on is that drill.

## Say it to your companion

Recite the ten steps to your companion, numbered — and mime the six rubs as you name them. End with the reason drying counts.

## Do It Yourself

1. Run the drill twice more today, at real sinks, before food.
2. Write the ten steps from memory; check and correct.
3. Write one sentence: which two steps protect the work of all the others? (The lather's amount — and the dry.)
