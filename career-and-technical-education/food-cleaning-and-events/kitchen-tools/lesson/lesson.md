# Kitchen Tools — Names, Jobs, and the Essential Kit

## Do it first

Picture a working kitchen counter. Name every tool on it and beside it, and write each one's job in three words. How many can you name? The book's essential kit has eleven entries — compare your list with Table 4.1 as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a kitchen utensil is

A **kitchen utensil is a small hand-held tool for food preparation** — its tasks run: **cutting to size, heating, baking, grinding, mixing, blending and measuring**.

Utensils are **general-purpose** — a chef's knife serves many foods — or **specialised** — an egg separator, an apple corer does one job only. And the kit **varies with cooking style**: there is no single kit for every kitchen, which is why this lesson teaches the book's essential list, not every tool that exists.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Table 4.1 kit

**Knives** — cutting and scoring, with a tough serrated bread knife for slicing. **Cutting boards** — for hygiene and easier cleaning. **Pasta strainer** and **mesh strainer** — the mesh giving stocks and sauces a velvet texture. **Spice grinder** — coffee beans and whole spices. **Kitchen tongs** — turning food in the pan and reaching into the oven.

**Coffee maker. Blenders** — sauces, juices, soups. **Mixer** — dough and batter without the labour. **Electric grill.** And the **oven**, with its floor coil and roof broiler coil. Learn the kit as pairs of name-and-job: in a kitchen you reach for the job, and the hand must know the tool.
