# Hospitality Events — Weddings, Meetings, Promotions, Conferences, Fundraisers

## Do it first

List every organised gathering you have ever been to: a wedding, a meeting, a dinner for a cause, a sports day that drew a crowd. For each, write: who organised it, and what was it FOR? The book's event types are in your list already.

## Sub lesson 1 — The event types

The events are **weddings, business meetings, celebration dinners and fundraisings** — run by **event planners, hotel owners, banquet-room managers or restaurant owners**, who all work to make the event succeed for **client, guests and host alike**.

**Promotional events** exist to **promote a sport or recreation activity to a target market and raise participation** — with the sponsor promoted second. **Conferences** gather participants to **plan, review progress, discuss issues, circulate information, select committee personnel and impart new knowledge**. **Fundraising events** run from dinner functions and special entertainment to bingo and card nights, charity auctions and awards evenings.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planning an event — and auditing its risks (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide adds a concept the student book's types section does not name: the **risk audit** — **identifying and assessing all risks so a plan exists for any undesirable occurrence that could harm people or damage the organisation**.

The guide's assessment puts it to work: groups choose a hospitality event actually run locally and **plan it — with tasks, deadlines and possible health-safety points** — observed and presented. An event type is only learned when you have planned one.

## From the teacher guide (evidence)

The risk audit and the local event-planning assessment are teacher-guide evidence (Grade 8 guide, Unit 4, printed pp. 53–54, section level — the guide is an image-only scan). The guide also has pupils compare each event type's operations and challenges from the viewpoints of client, guests and organiser — the three viewpoints the student book names as the parties an event must succeed for.
