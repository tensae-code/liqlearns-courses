# Ethiopian Foods — Shiro, Atkilt, Gonfo, Kitfo

## Do it first

Four dishes, four clues: a chickpea-powder stew; cabbage and potato simmered together; a porridge so stiff it holds a hole full of sauce; raw minced beef in chili butter. If you know any of their names, write them. This lesson gives all four, exactly as the book describes them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shiro and atkiltwot — the pulse stew and the vegetable stew

**Shiro** is a **chickpea-powder dish** — sometimes with lentils and beans — **slow-cooked with the spicy red pepper sauce**, ranging from the **soupy shiro wot** to thicker kinds. The powder is the point: pulses ground fine, cooked slow, in the red sauce the wat lesson built.

**Atkiltwot** is **cabbage, carrots and potatoes simmered together in a light sauce** — the vegetable plate of the set, gentle where shiro is spiced.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gonfo and kitfo — the stiff porridge and the marinated beef

**Gonfo** is a **stiff barley- or wheat-flour porridge, stirred continuously with a wooden spoon**, then **formed round with a hole in the middle** for its dipping sauce of **butter and red pepper, or pulses**. The stirring never stops — that is the technique the GIF shows.

**Kitfo** is **minced raw beef marinated in mitmita** — a chili-powder spice blend — **and herb-and-spice-infused butter**, served with **injera and/or kocho**, the thick enset flatbread. Raw service is the book's description, carried as printed: this is the dish as Ethiopia's kitchen defines it.
