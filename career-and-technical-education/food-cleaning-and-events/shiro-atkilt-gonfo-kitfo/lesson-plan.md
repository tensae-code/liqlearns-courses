# Lesson plan — Ethiopian Foods — Shiro, Atkilt, Gonfo, Kitfo

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Food, Cleaning and Events · **Lesson:** Ethiopian Foods — Shiro, Atkilt, Gonfo, Kitfo
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8 — Food, Cleaning and Events (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** four more cultural dishes: shiro (chickpea-powder stew), atkiltwot (cabbage, carrot, potato), gonfo (stiff porridge with its sauce-filled hole) and kitfo (mitmita-marinated minced beef).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Injera and Wat: the foundation dishes are built; now the four dishes that complete the book's cultural-foods set.

## Sub lessons
1. **Shiro and atkiltwot — the pulse stew and the vegetable stew** — chickpea powder slow-cooked; cabbage, carrots and potatoes simmered
2. **Gonfo and kitfo — the stiff porridge and the marinated beef** — continuous stirring and the sauce hole; mitmita and infused butter

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Ethiopian Foods — Injera and Wat, and How Wat Is Built. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe each dish's base and making.
2. Give gonfo's technique: continuous stirring, the hole for the sauce.
3. Give kitfo's marinade and its servings: injera and kocho.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; tasting or cooking only with an adult.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes shiro with wat generally → shiro's identity is its base: chickpea POWDER (with lentils/beans at times), slow-cooked in the red sauce. Name the powder first.
- Forgets gonfo's hole → the hole is functional: it holds the dipping sauce. Form round, hole in the middle, sauce in the hole.
