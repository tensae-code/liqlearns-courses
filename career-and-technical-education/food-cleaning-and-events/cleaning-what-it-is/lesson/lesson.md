# Cleaning — Removing Dirt, and Chemistry Plus Mechanics

## Do it first

Think of one hard cleaning you have done — a greasy pot, a muddy floor. Two things happened: something LOOSENED the dirt, and something LIFTED it away. Write both halves of your memory. The book teaches cleaning as exactly that blend.

## Sub lesson 1 — What cleaning is — and where it happens

**Cleaning is the process of removing unwanted substances — dirt, infectious agents and other impurities — from an object or environment.**

It happens in four contexts that **differ in scale and requirements**: **commercial** (office, hotel), **domestic** (kitchen, toilet, house), **personal**, and **environmental** cleaning. A hotel corridor and a kitchen sink are the same process at different scales — with different requirements, which is why cleaning is a trade and not a chore.

## Sub lesson 2 — How cleaning works — chemistry plus mechanics

Cleaning is taught as a **blend of chemistry and mechanics**: the **cleaning chemical product** — detergents, solvents, acids, alkalis, polishes, waxes — **interacting with the mechanical action of the equipment on the surface**, under the **conditions at the time**.

All three decide the result: the right chemical on the wrong dirt fails; the right action with no chemical grinds dirt around; and conditions — time, temperature, the surface itself — bend both. The lessons after this one are that blend, job by job: floors, hands, work areas, and the agents themselves.
