# Making Drinks — Hot Drinks and Soft Drinks

## Do it first

A guest arrives in ten minutes. What drinks could you actually make and serve — hot and cold? Write your real list. The book's hot-drinks table has four entries; see how many of yours match.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hot drinks — Table 4.2

Four hot drinks are named as needing **preparation skill** (Table 4.2): **fresh ginger tea, fruit tea, coffee and hot lemon**.

They are called skills, not afterthoughts: each has its making — strength, timing, serving temperature — and a food business is judged by them as surely as by its food. Coffee among them carries the ceremony of the course's hospitality lessons: poured well, it is welcome made visible.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cold drinks — soft drinks and how they are made

**Cold and soft drinks are made by mixing dry or fresh ingredients with water.** They come **factory-made or home-made**.

The book's three makings: **mixing syrup or dry ingredients with carbonated water**, and drinks made **by lacto-fermentation** — alongside the plain mixing of ingredients with water. A small business can start here: soft drinks ask for ingredients and care, not machinery.

## Say it to your companion

Name the four hot drinks for your companion, then the three ways a soft drink is made, ending with lacto-fermentation.
