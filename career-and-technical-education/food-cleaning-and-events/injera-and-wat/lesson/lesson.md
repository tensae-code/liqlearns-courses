# Ethiopian Foods — Injera and Wat, and How Wat Is Built

## Do it first

A wat pot is being started. On the counter: chopped onion, butter, red pepper, turmeric, and beef. What goes in FIRST — and why would a cook start there? Write your order for all the ingredients. The book's order is exact, and every wat cook knows it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Injera — the bread underneath everything

**Injera is the sour, spongy round bread made from teff flour.** Teff is grown across the country, and the injera's colour follows the teff variety: **white, red or mixed**.

Injera is eaten **with wat** — the bread is both plate and partner: spread flat, it carries the wat in its centre and is torn to scoop it. Every dish in the next lesson arrives on or beside it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wat — built in order, and the trade rules

Wat is built in order. **First: a large amount of chopped onion, simmered soft in a pot. Then butter or vegetable oil is added. Then colour and heat: red pepper for a spicy red wat — turmeric instead for a milder yellow one — both omitted for vegetable stews.**

**Then the main ingredient:** beef, chicken, fish, goat or lamb; legumes such as split peas or lentils; or vegetables such as potatoes, carrots and chard.

And the trade rules for prepared food, which the book sets beside the recipes: prepared food must be **free of bad substances, fairly priced, and inside the regulations that govern food sellers**. The recipe makes the food; the rules make it a business.
