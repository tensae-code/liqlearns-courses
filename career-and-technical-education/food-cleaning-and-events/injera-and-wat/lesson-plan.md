# Lesson plan — Ethiopian Foods — Injera and Wat, and How Wat Is Built

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Food, Cleaning and Events · **Lesson:** Ethiopian Foods — Injera and Wat, and How Wat Is Built
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8 — Food, Cleaning and Events (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's cultural foods at their foundation: injera — the sour, spongy teff bread — and wat built in its order (onion first, then fat, then pepper or turmeric, then the main ingredient), plus the trade rules for prepared food.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Fresh Foods and Balanced Meals: now the cooked heart of Ethiopian food service.

## Sub lessons
1. **Injera — the bread underneath everything** — teff flour, sour and spongy, its colours from the teff
2. **Wat — built in order, and the trade rules** — onion first, then fat, then colour, then the main ingredient

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Fresh Foods — Raw Foods and Healthy Snacks, and Balanced Meals. Recommended, never forced.
- All cooking practice is with an adult; the mitad is hot equipment.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe injera: teff, sour, spongy, round — and its colour rule.
2. Build a wat in order: onion, fat, pepper/turmeric, main ingredient.
3. State the trade rules: free of bad substances, fairly priced, inside the regulations.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; cooking practice only with an adult at a real kitchen.

## If the learner is stuck
- Starts wat with the meat → the book's order is onion FIRST, simmered soft, before fat, colour or main ingredient. The onion is the foundation, not a garnish.
- Confuses the colours → red pepper = spicy red wat; turmeric = milder yellow; vegetable stews take neither. Colour is a choice made at step three.
