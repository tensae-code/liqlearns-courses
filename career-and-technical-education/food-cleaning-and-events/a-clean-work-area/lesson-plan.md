# Lesson plan — A Clean Work Area — the Four Business Benefits

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Food, Cleaning and Events · **Lesson:** A Clean Work Area — the Four Business Benefits
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8 — Food, Cleaning and Events (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** why keeping the work area clean pays a business: staff stay safe and healthy, productivity rises, the professional image improves, and it proves cost-effective in the long run.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the cleaning lessons before it: the learner can clean; now the business case for keeping the work area clean always.

## Sub lessons
1. **More than a sparkling building** — what 'clean workplace' really means
2. **The four benefits** — safety and health, productivity, image, long-run cost

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Cleaning — Removing Dirt, and Chemistry Plus Mechanics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a clean workplace is beyond 'a sparkling building'.
2. List the four benefits in the book's order.
3. Argue the case to a sceptical owner using all four.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Recites 'it looks professional' as the whole case → image is benefit 3 of 4; safety/health and productivity come before it in the book's order, and cost closes the argument.
- Treats clean as a one-time event → the book's phrase is KEEPING the area neat and orderly — a habit whose long-run cost-effectiveness (4) only exists because it is kept.
