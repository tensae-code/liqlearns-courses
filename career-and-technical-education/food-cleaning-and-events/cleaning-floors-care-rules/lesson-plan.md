# Lesson plan — Cleaning Floors — Detergents, Water, and How Often

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Food, Cleaning and Events · **Lesson:** Cleaning Floors — Detergents, Water, and How Often
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8 — Food, Cleaning and Events (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** floor care by type: what never to use (soap-based detergents, wax polish, excess water), the vinegar solution for marks, damp mopping defined, and the cleaning rhythm for high-traffic floors.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Cleaning — Removing Dirt, and Chemistry Plus Mechanics: the blend applied to the largest surface in any workplace.

## Sub lessons
1. **Floor care rules — methods and nevers** — vacuum/dust/damp-mop; no soap detergents, no over-wetting, no wax, no water on wood
2. **Damp mopping and the rhythm** — what damp mopping is, the weekly deep clean, and high-traffic frequency

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Cleaning — Removing Dirt, and Chemistry Plus Mechanics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the nevers: no soap-based detergents, no over-wetting, no wax polish, no water on wood.
2. Define damp mopping and its weekly deep-clean place.
3. Give the high-traffic rhythm: sweep/vacuum every 1–3 days, mop weekly — and why.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Floor practice itself is the next lesson (the eleven steps).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads 'mop' as 'soak' → the book's mop is DAMP: slightly moistened and wrung out. Over-wetting is on the never list.
- Applies one routine to all floors → the first words of the section are 'by type': wood takes NO water; high-traffic floors take the 1–3-day rhythm. Type and traffic set the plan.
