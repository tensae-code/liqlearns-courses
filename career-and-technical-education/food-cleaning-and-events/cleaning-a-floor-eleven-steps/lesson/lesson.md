# Cleaning a Floor — the Eleven Steps

## Do it first

Six actions, shuffled: mop the floor; sweep it; fill buckets; wring the mop; rinse the floor; let it dry. Order them as you would actually work. Then read the book's eleven steps and see what your six were missing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Steps 1–5 — prepare: choose, sweep, fill, wring

**Step 1 — Choose a mop and bucket matched to the floor type** (a string/strip mop for textured ceramic tile). **Step 2 — Choose a cleaner designed for the flooring.**

**Step 3 — Sweep or vacuum first, thoroughly, before the mop touches the floor.** **Step 4 — Fill the buckets with hot water — hotter cleans better and quicker — with detergent in the wash bucket.** **Step 5 — Dip and wring the mop until damp, not sopping.** Preparation is five of the eleven steps: the mopping itself only works because these came first.

## Sub lesson 2 — Steps 6–11 — mop backward, rinse, finish dry

**Step 6 — Begin mopping from one end to the other, moving backward so you always stand on the un-mopped area** (straight lines with a sponge mop). **Step 7 — Stop for stubborn spots: rub back and forth rapidly with downward pressure.**

**Step 8 — Rinse the mop in the rinse bucket, dunking up and down, then wring out the dirty water. Step 9 — Continue mopping section by section, watching both waters.**

**Step 10 — Final rinse: mop the whole floor once more with fresh hot rinse water only. Step 11 — Let everything dry: rinse mop and bucket, store only when dry, and let the floor dry completely before walking on it.** The floor is finished when it is dry — not when the mopping stops.

## Say it to your companion

Recite the eleven steps to your companion, numbered, in order — prepare (1–5), mop (6–9), finish (10–11) — with the backward rule and the dry rule said aloud.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eleven steps from memory; check and correct in another colour.
2. Clean one floor at home by the steps and record your time and your near-misses.
3. Write one sentence: why does the cleaner walk backward?
