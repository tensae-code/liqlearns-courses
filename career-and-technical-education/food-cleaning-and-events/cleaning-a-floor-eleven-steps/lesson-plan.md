# Lesson plan — Cleaning a Floor — the Eleven Steps

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Food, Cleaning and Events · **Lesson:** Cleaning a Floor — the Eleven Steps
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8 — Food, Cleaning and Events (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the book's eleven steps of cleaning a floor, in order — from choosing the mop and sweeping first, through the damp-mop cycle worked backward, to the final rinse and complete drying.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Cleaning Floors — Detergents, Water, and How Often: the rules are known; now the full procedure, step by step.

## Sub lessons
1. **Steps 1–5 — prepare: choose, sweep, fill, wring** — matching tools to the floor, sweeping first, and the damp mop
2. **Steps 6–11 — mop backward, rinse, finish dry** — the working cycle and the closing discipline

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Cleaning Floors — Detergents, Water, and How Often. Recommended, never forced.
- PPE and appearance for this work are taught in Cleaning Safely — PPE and How a Cleaner Presents.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the eleven steps in order.
2. Work the core cycle: dip, wring to damp, mop backward, rinse-dunk, wring.
3. State the closing discipline: fresh-water final rinse, everything dried and stored dry.

## What you need
For hands-on: a mop, two buckets, a floor that may be cleaned, and an adult's permission for the space. Otherwise paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mops before sweeping → step 3 exists because a mop on unswept dirt makes mud and spreads it. Sweep first, thoroughly — it is a numbered step, not advice.
- Walks on the finished floor → step 11: the floor dries COMPLETELY before walking, and tools are stored only when dry. The job ends at dry, not at rinsed.
