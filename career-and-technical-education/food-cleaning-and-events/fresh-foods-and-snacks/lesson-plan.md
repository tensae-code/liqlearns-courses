# Lesson plan — Fresh Foods — Raw Foods and Healthy Snacks

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Food, Cleaning and Events · **Lesson:** Fresh Foods — Raw Foods and Healthy Snacks
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8 — Food, Cleaning and Events (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fresh foods in two halves: those eaten raw — fruits, dried and canned fruit, raw vegetables — and those made into cooked snacks: nut-and-fruit mix, roasted chickpeas, apples with peanut butter.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Balanced Meals and Making Drinks: the fresh side of the food business, before the cooked cultural foods.

## Sub lessons
1. **Fresh foods eaten raw** — fruits, dried and canned fruit, raw vegetables and salads
2. **Fresh foods made into cooked snacks** — nut-and-fruit mix, roasted chickpeas, apples with peanut butter

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Balanced Meals — Nutrients and the Five Food Groups. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the raw fresh foods: fruits, dried fruit, canned fruit, raw vegetables, salads.
2. List the cooked-snack makings.
3. Say the snack mix's business virtue: healthy and non-perishable.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; hands-on snack making is done with an adult, with hand washing first (see Hand Washing — the Ten Steps).

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts canned goods outside 'fresh foods' → the book includes canned fruit and lower-sodium canned vegetables in this section's lists. Carry them as printed.
- Misses why non-perishable matters → a snack that keeps is stock that survives unsold days; a raw plate is same-day goods. The contrast is the business lesson.
