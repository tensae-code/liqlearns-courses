# Fresh Foods — Raw Foods and Healthy Snacks

## Do it first

A market basket holds: apples, raisins, carrots, avocado, chickpeas, nuts, a tin of fruit, a bag of washed lettuce. Sort it into two piles — eat as it comes, and cook or prepare first. Your two piles are this lesson's two halves.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fresh foods eaten raw

**Fresh foods that cannot be cooked** — eaten as they are: **fruits (apples, oranges, bananas), dried fruit such as raisins, and canned fruit**.

**Vegetables eaten raw: carrots, peppers, avocado, small cucumbers, tomatoes** — plus **lower-sodium canned vegetables** and **bagged washed lettuce or salads**. Raw service is still food service: the freshness IS the preparation, and washed, ready produce is a product a business can sell.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fresh foods made into cooked snacks

**Nuts and dried fruit** make **a healthy non-perishable snack mix** — it keeps without spoiling, which makes it stock a seller can hold.

**Roasted chickpeas** turn a staple pulse into a snack. **Apples with peanut butter** pair the raw half with a made accompaniment. Three makings, all from ordinary market goods — the snack shelf of a fresh-food business.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion both halves: the full raw list, then the three snack makings — and the word non-perishable with its meaning.

## Do It Yourself

1. Design a raw plate of five items from the book's list.
2. Write a snack-mix label: contents, and why it keeps.
3. Write one sentence: which half would you sell at a school gate, and why?
