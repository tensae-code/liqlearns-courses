# The Cleaner's Skills — People, Body, Numbers, Detail

## Do it first

Write the advert: 'Cleaner wanted.' What will you ask for? Most adverts ask for experience — read what the book actually asks for, and notice what it does NOT demand at the door.

## Sub lesson 1 — Open entry — no qualifications demanded

**No specific qualifications or experience are needed to start as a cleaner.** The door to this trade is open — a fact that matters in a course built for school leavers choosing sellable work.

But open entry is not the same as 'anyone is good at it'. The book immediately names what a GOOD cleaner possesses — four possessions, and they are the difference between hired-once and hired-always.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four possessions of a good cleaner

A good cleaner possesses: **good interpersonal and communication skills** — clients and guests are dealt with face to face; **physical abilities** — the work is bodily, all shift long; **good numeracy and literacy** — measures, labels, lists and records are read and written; and **a high level of attention to detail** — the missed corner is the job's verdict.

Read the four as a day: speaking with the client (people), carrying the work (body), diluting and recording (numbers and letters), and catching the last uncleaned edge (detail).

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the entry fact, then the four possessions — people, body, numbers and letters, detail — with a day-moment for each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four possessions from memory.
2. Complete the self-audit and set one practice target.
3. Write one sentence: which possession protects a client relationship most directly?
