# Concrete-Work Safety — Protecting Skin, Head, Eyes, Ears, Feet and Back

## Do it first

Picture a worker mixing and pouring concrete all morning. Go through the body: skin, head, eyes, ears, feet, back. For each one, write what could hurt it on that site. Six dangers — find them before the lesson names the protections.

## Sub lesson 1 — Skin, head and eyes — the concrete dangers

**Skin.** Wet concrete, mortar and cement mixtures cause irritation and chemical burns — up to third-degree burns — and serious eye damage. And here is the trap: cement burns hurt only hours later. Because the pain comes late, the rule is strict: **wash any contact off at once** — do not wait to feel it.

**Head.** Always wear an **approved hard hat** on site, and care for it: never drill it, never store it on a car's rear shelf (sun and heat weaken it), never wear it backward or damaged — and never wear a steel one, because steel conducts electricity.

**Eyes.** Wear shatterproof safety protection at all times on the job. And wear **no contact lenses** on the job — the book's rule is plain.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ears, feet and back — hearing, boots and the safe lift

**Ears.** There is no cure for noise-induced hearing loss — once it is gone, it is gone. So wear **self-fitting earplugs** where the work is loud.

**Feet.** Wear **steel-toed safety boots** against falling objects, crushing and punctures. For standing in wet concrete, wear **waterproof rubber boots**.

**Back.** Do not lift too much. The safe lift, step by step: **back straight, knees bent, load held close, lift with the legs** — and to turn, **pivot the feet; never twist** the back.

## Say it to your companion

Go body part by body part for your companion — skin, head, eyes, ears, feet, back — and give each one's protection. Finish by talking through the safe lift as you mime it with empty hands.

## Do It Yourself

1. Check one workspace at home against the six protections: what is missing?
2. Practise the safe lift three more times with the empty box, feet pivoting.
3. Write the hard-hat rules as a five-line poster from memory.
