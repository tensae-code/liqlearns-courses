# Lesson plan — Concrete-Work Safety — Protecting Skin, Head, Eyes, Ears, Feet and Back

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Building and Retail · **Lesson:** Concrete-Work Safety — Protecting Skin, Head, Eyes, Ears, Feet and Back
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Building and Retail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how to protect six parts of the body when working with concrete (the book's Table 3.2) — skin, head, eyes, ears, feet and back — taught exactly as the book teaches it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Making Concrete and Brickwork Tools: the learner knows the material and the tools; this lesson is the safety gate before any real site work.

## Sub lessons
1. **Skin, head and eyes — the concrete dangers** — wet concrete burns, the hard-hat rules, and eye protection
2. **Ears, feet and back — hearing, boots and the safe lift** — earplugs, safety boots, and lifting with the legs

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Making Concrete — Materials and the 1:2:3 Mix, and Brickwork Tools. Recommended, never forced.
- Read this lesson before any hands-on work near real concrete.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the concrete danger to skin and eyes, and the wash-at-once rule.
2. Give the hard-hat rules: approved, cared for, never drilled, never on a car's rear shelf, never steel.
3. Protect ears, feet and back the book's way — including the safe lift.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. The safe-lift practice in hands-on uses a light, empty box only.

## If the learner is stuck
- Waits to wash cement off until it hurts → the lesson's trap: cement burns hurt only hours later. The wash rule is 'at once', precisely because feeling is a late messenger.
- Twists while lifting → stop and reset the feet. The turn lives in the feet (pivot), never in the back.
