# Health and Safety at Work — the Law's List and Your Own Duties

## Do it first

You are asked to write the safety law for one small workshop. What would your law cover — the air, the floor, the hours, the toilets, the tools? Write your list. Then compare it, item by item, with the book's.

## Sub lesson 1 — The law's list — what safety law covers

**Workplace safety and health is the set of laws made to protect people's health and safety at work** — and how strict those laws are varies by country.

The book's list of what the law covers: **workplace temperature; floor trip hazards and clothing snags; smoking; pollution and fire hazards; toilets per person; safety equipment such as hard hats; the rights of disabled people; and daily working hours.** Read it slowly — a safe workplace is not one rule but a whole list, from the air you breathe to the hours you work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Your own part — the worker's duties

The law is only half of it. **The worker's own part:** use the **proper safety equipment for the task** — appropriate clothing and shoes; know **where the fire extinguishers and first aid kits are**; wear a **hard hat where objects may fall**; wear **gloves for toxic substances or sharp objects**; wear **eye protection where eyes are at risk**; wear **safe shoes on slippery surfaces and for lifting**; and use **seat belts and all protective equipment intended for the task**.

Notice the pattern: every duty matches a risk of the actual task in front of you. Safety is not a poster — it is the equipment of THIS job, worn for THIS job.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the law's list (eight areas), then your own duties (seven items). Finish with the pattern: equipment matched to the task.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write both lists from memory and check them.
2. Find the extinguisher and first aid kit in one building you use; write where they are.
3. Write one sentence: which duty protects a worker lifting crates on a wet floor?
