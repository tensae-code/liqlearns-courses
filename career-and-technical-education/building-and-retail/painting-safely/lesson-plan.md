# Lesson plan — Painting Safely — Warnings, Masks, and the Lead Rule

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Building and Retail · **Lesson:** Painting Safely — Warnings, Masks, and the Lead Rule
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Building and Retail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the occupational health and safety rules of painting work: warn others and barrier the area, dust masks when sanding, eye protection at all times, never sand lead materials, no grinding near painting, and read every paint label.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Painting Tools: the learner knows the kit; this lesson is the safety gate before using it.

## Sub lessons
1. **Warn, mask, protect the eyes** — barriers and signs, dust masks when sanding, eye protection at all times
2. **The absolute rules — lead, sparks, labels** — never sand lead; never grind near painting; read every label

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Painting Tools — Applicators and Abrasives. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Set up the warning: fence, barrier or signs before coating work.
2. State the mask and eye rules.
3. State the two absolute rules: never sand lead materials; never grind where painting is in progress — and why.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats the lead rule as caution rather than ban → the book's word is NEVER, with the reason named: lead can cause cancer. Unknown old paint is not sanded.
- Forgets the warning set-up → paint work endangers bystanders too: fence, barrier or signs come before the first coat, not after a complaint.
