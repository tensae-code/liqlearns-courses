# Painting Safely — Warnings, Masks, and the Lead Rule

## Do it first

A painter is sanding an old wall inside a house where children play, about to paint, while someone nearby grinds metal. Write every danger you can see in that scene. The book's rules answer each one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Warn, mask, protect the eyes

**Warn others about the risk.** Before and during coating work, set up a **fence or barrier**, or use **signs** — the people around the work must know it is happening and keep clear.

**Wear dust masks when sanding.** Sanding fills the air with dust; the mask is the rule whenever the sandpaper is moving.

**Wear eye protection at all times** during painting work — the same standing rule as the carpentry shop: at all times, not only for the messy moments.

## Sub lesson 2 — The absolute rules — lead, sparks, labels

**NEVER sand materials containing lead — it can cause cancer.** This is the book's strongest rule in the unit, and it is absolute: old paint of unknown make-up is not sanded.

**Never grind where painting is in progress** — grinding throws sparks, and sparks near paint work are a fire hazard.

**Read and obey the label on every type of paint.** Each paint carries its own instructions and warnings on its label; the label is part of the job, read before the tin is opened.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the six rules in order — warn, mask, eyes, lead, grinding, labels — and say the reason inside the lead rule and the grinding rule.
