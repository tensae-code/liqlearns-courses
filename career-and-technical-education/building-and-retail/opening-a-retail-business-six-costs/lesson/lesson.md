# Opening a Retail Business — the Six Costs to Budget

## Do it first

You will open a small shop next month. Money is ready for stock. What else will take money before the first customer walks in — and in the first months after? Write every cost you can think of. The book's list has six.

## Sub lesson 1 — Place costs — location, rent, utilities

Retail trade **buys from wholesalers and manufacturers and sells to final consumers for profit**. A new retail budget must face six main costs — the first three are the costs of place.

**(a) Location:** foot traffic and regular customers cost money up front — a **deposit plus remodel/customising funds**. (Online, the same cost appears as website production and hosting.) **(b) Rent:** paid **monthly, by square footage and area value** — and it is highest exactly where foot traffic is popular. **(c) Utilities:** electricity and gas, priced by **area prices, space size and climate**.

## Sub lesson 2 — People and protection costs — insurance, employees, marketing

**(d) Insurance:** coverage for **business property, income, liability and crime**. **(e) Employee costs:** **wages, salary and benefits — plus training, payroll and timecards**, the costs around the wage itself.

**(f) Marketing:** the **logo, business cards, website and opening promotions** — named by the book as **the cost people most often overlook**. A shop nobody hears about has paid the other five costs for nothing.

## Say it to your companion

List the six costs for your companion in the book's order — location, rent, utilities, insurance, employees, marketing — with one line each, and end with the most-overlooked one.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six costs from memory and check the order.
2. For one imagined shop, write one line per cost: what exactly would you pay for?
3. Write one sentence: why is marketing the dangerous cost to forget?
