# Carpentry Tools — Hand Tools and Power Tools

## Do it first

A carpenter's kit is spread on a bench: saws, a hammer, a plane, chisels — and drills, sanders and saws with cords. Two families hide in this kit. Sort them by one question: where does the power come from? Write your two lists before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hand tools — power from the hand

A **hand tool** is powered by hand, not a motor. It is used for cutting and modifying wooden materials — the worker's own arm supplies every stroke.

The book pictures the hand family together: **saws, a hammer, a plane and chisels**. Each one moves only when the hand moves it — stop your arm, and the tool stops.

## Sub lesson 2 — Power tools — power from a source

**Power tools** are machine tools needing an **electric source**. The book pictures them opposite the hand family: **drills, sanders and powered saws** — each with its cord, each able to keep moving when your hand only guides.

The sort is one question: **where does the power come from?** Hand or motor? Saw with no cord — hand tool. Saw with a cord — power tool. The same work (cutting wood) can live in both families; the power source, not the job, decides the family — and the next lesson's safety rules follow from exactly that.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the two definitions, then sort this kit aloud: hand saw, drill, hammer, sander, chisel, powered saw. One question decides each one — say the question too.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the one sorting question at the top of a page and sort eight tools you know under it.
2. Find one job that tools from both families can do, and name both tools.
3. Write one sentence: why will the safety lesson care which family a tool is in?
