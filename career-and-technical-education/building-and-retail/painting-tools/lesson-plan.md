# Lesson plan — Painting Tools — Applicators and Abrasives

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Building and Retail · **Lesson:** Painting Tools — Applicators and Abrasives
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Building and Retail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the painter's two tool families (the book's Table 3.3): the applicators — brush, hammer, roller, dusting brush, tray, putty knife — and the abrasives — sandpaper, sanding blocks, wire brush, scraper — plus how tools are protected and cleaned.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Painting and Decorating — a Trade That Protects and Beautifies: the learner knows the trade; now its tools.

## Sub lessons
1. **Applicators — the tools that put paint on** — brush, hammer, roller, dusting brush, tray and putty knife
2. **Abrasives — the tools that prepare the surface** — sandpaper, sanding blocks, wire brush, scraper — and protecting and cleaning

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Painting and Decorating — a Trade That Protects and Beautifies. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Match each applicator to its job: brush, hammer, roller, dusting brush, tray, putty knife.
2. Match each abrasive to its job: sandpaper, sanding block, wire brush, scraper.
3. Name the protection (drop sheets, gloves, dust masks) and the cleaning fluids (turpentine, kerosene).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; practice is drawing and matching — real painting practice belongs with an adult and Painting Safely read first.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the dusting brush among applicators of paint → it applies nothing: it removes dust BEFORE painting. The family line is 'puts paint on' vs 'prepares'.
- Confuses wire brush and scraper → wire brush attacks rust on metal; the scraper lifts old paint. Different enemies, different tools.
