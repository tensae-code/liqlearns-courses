# Bricks and Blocks — Concrete Block, Concrete Brick, Clay Brick

## Do it first

Three building units stand in a row. One is big and hollow. One is small and solid, grey. One is small, solid and red-brown. Which is which — and which two are cousins? Guess now; the lesson sorts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Concrete blocks — the hollow units

A **concrete block** is a hollow precast building unit — also called a cement block, or a **concrete masonry unit**, and locally a blocket. It is used mainly for walls.

Look at its shape: it carries **one or more hollow cavities**, and its sides are cast smooth or with a design. The cavities are part of the unit — a block is not a solid lump.

Its smaller cousin, the **concrete brick**, is made of cement and aggregate — usually sand — formed in moulds and cured until hard. Mineral colours can be added to make a concrete brick resemble a clay brick, so colour alone does not tell you which family a brick belongs to.

## Sub lesson 2 — Clay bricks — the second family

**Clay bricks** are the second family of brick construction. Where concrete units are cast or moulded from cement mixes, clay bricks are the older, earth-based kind, pictured in the book side by side with both concrete kinds.

To tell the families apart, look and think: a big hollow unit is a concrete block. A small solid unit of cement and sand is a concrete brick — even if it is coloured to look like clay. A clay brick is the clay family's own unit.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three units in order — concrete block, concrete brick, clay brick — and for each one say: hollow or solid, and what it is made of.
