# Carpentry — the Trade and Its Basic Skills

## Do it first

Look around one room — any room. List everything in it that is made of wood or fitted into place: frame, door, window, shelves. Who made and fitted them? Write your guess at the worker's full skill list before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — What carpentry is

**Carpentry is the skilled trade and craft of cutting, shaping and installing building materials.** Its work appears in buildings, ships, timber bridges and concrete formwork — wherever timber (or its substitutes) must be shaped and put in place.

Note the three verbs at the trade's heart: **cutting, shaping, installing**. The carpenter does not only make things in a workshop — the carpenter fits them into the building itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — The basic skill set

The book's basic skill set runs: **general framing** — walls, floors, doorframes; **building staircases**; **installing windows, doors and siding**; **installing roofing systems**; and **fitting and installing trim, doors, stairs and hardware**.

Under all of it sits the foundation skill: **measuring, cutting and joining wood or wood substitutes**. Every framed wall and fitted door begins with a measurement, a cut, and a joint that holds.

## From the teacher guide (evidence)

The teacher guide (evidence) assesses carpentry at a real workplace briefing, adding working safely, communicating effectively and productively with colleagues, and responding to requests and seeking help. It also names the trade's numeracy load: whole numbers, decimals, fractions, measurement and metric measurement, and setting up problems that need more than one calculation — the measure-twice habit in the hands-on work is that numeracy in action.
