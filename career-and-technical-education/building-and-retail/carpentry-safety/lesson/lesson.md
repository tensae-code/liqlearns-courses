# Carpentry Safety — Glasses, Hearing Protection and Protective Gear

## Do it first

A saw is cutting, a plane is shaving, a sander is humming. Chips fly. Dust hangs. Noise fills the room. Before you read the rules: what protection would you want on your eyes, your ears, your hands and your face? Write your list — then compare it with the book's.

## Sub lesson 1 — Eyes and ears — the two standing rules

**Eyes:** always wear **safety glasses or goggles, or a face shield** in the carpentry shop. The word is *always* — not only for the loud machines. Wood work throws chips and dust at the eyes in every task.

**Ears:** wear **hearing protection matched to the noise level and frequency** of the woodworking shop. Protection is chosen for the actual noise — a quiet hand-tool bench and a powered-saw room are not the same noise, and the protection must fit the one you are in.

## Sub lesson 2 — The full protective set

Beyond eyes and ears, the book lists the carpenter's full protective set: **gloves, gowns or coats, face shields or masks, eye protection, resuscitation masks, hats and booties** — pictured in the book as the construction worker's protective materials.

Read the list as a system, not a menu: each item guards a different exposure — hands, body, face, eyes, breathing, head, feet. In class, pupils list the tools and present the safety rules — knowing the set well enough to teach it is the standard this lesson sets.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the eye rule, the hearing rule, and the full protective set — all seven items, counted on your fingers.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven-item gear list from memory, then check it against the lesson.
2. Write one line per item: what exposure does it guard?
3. Write the two standing rules (eyes, ears) as poster headlines.
