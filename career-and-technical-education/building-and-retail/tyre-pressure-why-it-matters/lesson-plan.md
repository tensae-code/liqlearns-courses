# Lesson plan — Tyre Pressure — Why the Right Air Keeps Tyres Alive and People Safe

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Building and Retail · **Lesson:** Tyre Pressure — Why the Right Air Keeps Tyres Alive and People Safe
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Building and Retail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** why tyre pressure matters: correct inflation gives vehicles long life and passengers safety, while under-inflated tyres wear out early and overheat toward failure.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the title's opening key words, where tyre pressure was named: now the reason behind the measurement.

## Sub lessons
1. **The promise of correct pressure** — long life for the vehicle, safety for the passengers
2. **The danger of under-inflation** — wear, excessive heat, and failure at the end of the chain

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Construction and Carpentry — the Building Trades and Their Key Words (tyre pressure is defined there). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define tyre pressure in use: the air measurement that must be kept correct.
2. State what correct inflation buys: long tyre life and passenger safety.
3. State what under-inflation does: accelerated wear, excessive heat, possible failure.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. No vehicle work in this lesson — the checking procedure is the next lesson.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner predicts what a soft tyre does on a long hot road before the book's reasons are shown (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the two promises of correct pressure, then the under-inflation chain: wear, heat, failure (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 11957]: a picture of two tyres side by side — one correctly inflated, rolling evenly; one under-inflated, bulging and heating — the student say which tyre is which, and what each one's next month looks like.

4. **Questions** — from questions/: served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks pressure is about comfort → the book's stakes are life and safety: tyre life (wear) and passenger safety (heat to failure). Comfort is never mentioned.
- Misses the heat step → heat is the bridge between wear and failure: under-inflation generates excessively high heat, and the heat weakens the tyre.
