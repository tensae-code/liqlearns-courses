# Ways Customers Can Pay — Cash, Card, Mobile, Transfer

## Do it first

Think of the last five times you saw anyone pay for anything. How did the money move each time — notes, a card, a phone, a bank? Write the ways. The book's table sorts four of them and gives each one its advantage.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cash and cards

**Cash** is the **most common and easiest** form of payment — and many customers expect you to accept it. A shop that refuses cash refuses the payment most people carry.

**Credit cards** lead customers to make **more frequent or larger purchases**, and let them make **large purchases safely**. The card grows the sale itself, not only the way of paying.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mobile and transfer — and the owner's choice

**Mobile payments** are described in the book's table in the same terms as cards: they too lead customers toward more frequent or larger purchases, made safely. (The book's table gives mobile payments the identical description to credit cards — this lesson carries both as the book prints them.)

**Electronic bank transfers** receive **large payments without paying fees**, and are **quicker and more convenient than cash or checks**.

The table ends in a decision: **the small-business owner must decide which types to accept — every method has merits and demerits.** There is no single right mix; there is only the mix chosen on purpose.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the four options with the book's advantage for each, then finish with the owner's duty: decide which to accept — every method has merits and demerits.
