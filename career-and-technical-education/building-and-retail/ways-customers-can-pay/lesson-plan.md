# Lesson plan — Ways Customers Can Pay — Cash, Card, Mobile, Transfer

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Building and Retail · **Lesson:** Ways Customers Can Pay — Cash, Card, Mobile, Transfer
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Building and Retail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the payment options of the book's Table 3.4 — cash, credit cards, mobile payments and electronic bank transfers — with the advantage the book gives each, and the owner's duty to choose.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Retail Payments — Why Collecting Payment Goes Wrong: the troubles are named; now the options a shop can offer.

## Sub lessons
1. **Cash and cards** — the most common way, and the way that grows purchases
2. **Mobile and transfer — and the owner's choice** — mobile payments, electronic transfers, and deciding what to accept

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Retail Payments — Why Collecting Payment Goes Wrong. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the four payment options of Table 3.4.
2. State each option's book-given advantage.
3. State the owner's duty: decide which types to accept — every method has merits and demerits.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

1. **Do it first** — the learner lists every way they have ever seen someone pay before the table is shown (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Table 3.4 option by option, then the owner's choice (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 11960]: a picture of four till icons in a row — cash notes, a credit card, a phone, a bank transfer slip — with each one's advantage beneath — the student cover the advantages and give each option's from memory.

4. **Questions** — from questions/: served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Assumes cash is simply best → the table gives every option an advantage cash lacks somewhere: cards grow purchases; transfers take large sums without fees.
- Misses that mobile's line duplicates the card line → that duplication is IN THE BOOK's table; the lesson names it openly rather than silently inventing a different line.
