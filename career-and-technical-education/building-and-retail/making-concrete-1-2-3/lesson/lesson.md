# Making Concrete — Materials and the 1:2:3 Mix

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — The four materials of concrete

Concrete for blocks is made of four materials: **powdered Portland cement**, **water**, **sand** and **gravel** (stone), mixed in proper proportion.

Each material has its job in the batch: the cement is the powder that binds, the sand and stone give the body, and the water wakes the cement up and lets the whole mix be worked and poured. Leave one out and you do not have concrete.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1:2:3 mix — and the quality rule

The standard brick mix ratio is **1:2:3** — **one part cement, two parts sand, three parts stone**, with adequate water.

Count it in buckets: one bucket of cement, two buckets of sand, three buckets of stone. The same measure every time — that sameness is the whole point of a ratio.

Making bricks and blocks is easy — the book stresses this. What decides profit is not the making but the standard: blocks of **standard size** and **consistent quality**, batch after batch. A buyer trusts the maker whose blocks never change.
