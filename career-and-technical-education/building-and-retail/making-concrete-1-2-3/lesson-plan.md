# Lesson plan — Making Concrete — Materials and the 1:2:3 Mix

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Building and Retail · **Lesson:** Making Concrete — Materials and the 1:2:3 Mix
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Building and Retail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four materials of concrete and the standard 1:2:3 mix — one part cement, two parts sand, three parts stone, with adequate water.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Bricks and Blocks: the learner knows the units; now they learn what the concrete in them is made of. Safety for wet concrete is taught in full in Concrete-Work Safety — wash skin at once.

## Sub lessons
1. **The four materials of concrete** — cement, water, sand and gravel — and what concrete for blocks is
2. **The 1:2:3 mix — and the quality rule** — the standard ratio counted out, and what makes blocks sell

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Bricks and Blocks — Concrete Block, Concrete Brick, Clay Brick. Recommended, never forced.
- Concrete-Work Safety teaches how to protect your skin, eyes and back around wet concrete — read it before any real mixing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four materials of concrete.
2. State the 1:2:3 ratio and what each number counts.
3. Say what decides profit in block making: standard size and consistent quality.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; three cups or buckets and dry sand/gravel for the hands-on ratio practice (dry materials only — no cement, no mixing real concrete).

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner guesses the recipe of a block before the ratio is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the four materials, then the 1:2:3 ratio counted out in buckets, then the quality rule (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reverses the ratio (3 cement, 1 stone) → the smallest number belongs to the costly binder: cement 1, sand 2, stone 3 — count it on buckets again.
- Thinks more water is always better → the book says adequate water: enough to work the mix. The ratio is the recipe; the water serves it.
