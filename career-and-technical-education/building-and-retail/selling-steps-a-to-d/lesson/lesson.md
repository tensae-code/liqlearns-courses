# Selling — the Retail Process and Selling Steps A–D

## Do it first

A good shopkeeper sells a phone to a stranger who walked in 'just looking'. Reconstruct the invisible steps: what did the seller do before the meeting, at the meeting, and in the showing? Write your version — the book names seven steps; this lesson covers the first four.

## Sub lesson 1 — The retail process — the whole inside which selling sits

The **retail process** overall runs: **developing value propositions** (what makes your offer worth choosing), **learning customer preferences**, **establishing retail networks and supply chains**, **getting customers for the merchandise**, and **setting up stores and filling them with merchandise**.

Inside that whole process sits the **selling process** — the seven-step path one sale walks, from finding a possible customer to following up after the order. This lesson climbs its first four steps.

## Sub lesson 2 — Selling steps A–D

**A. Prospecting** — identifying potential customers. Before anyone is sold to, someone must be found who might buy.

**B. Pre-approach** — collecting information about them and understanding them **before the sales call**. The good seller arrives already knowing.

**C. Approach** — the salesperson **meets the customer for the first time**. **D. Presentation** — showing **how the product will satisfy the customer's needs and add value to his or her life**. Note the presentation's test: not the product's features, but the customer's needs met and life improved.

## From the teacher guide (evidence)

The teacher guide (evidence) assesses retail selling through group answers on the selling process's key steps, the key skills and qualities of successful sales staff, the features and benefits of a selected product that attract customers, and how to demonstrate that product — observed during discussion and presentation. The scripting activity follows that pattern.
