# The Trouble with Cash — Six Problems

## Do it first

Cash is the most common and easiest payment — the last lesson said so. Now argue the other side: you run a busy shop paid only in cash for a year. Where does the cash itself hurt you? Write every problem you can find. The book lists six.

## Sub lesson 1 — Time and customers — the slow, eating payment

**Problem 1 — slowness:** payment in cash can take **up to a week**, leaving customers dissatisfied. The easiest payment at the counter can be the slowest to settle.

**Problem 2 — collection time:** collecting and dealing with cash **eats time that should serve end users** — the customers themselves. **Problem 5 — processing time:** checking and processing cash takes **too much time**. Two of the six counts are the same thief in different clothes: cash eats the shop's hours.

## Sub lesson 2 — Security and the notes themselves

**Problem 3 — risk:** carrying cash is risky — **cashiers loaded with cash are robbery targets**.

**Problem 4 — leakage:** large cash handling leaks — **some goes missing, stolen, or used for bribes**. Money that cannot be traced quietly disappears.

**Problem 6 — the notes themselves:** paper money has a **short lifetime and is costly to print**. The tool itself wears out fast and costs the country to replace.

## From the teacher guide (evidence)

The teacher guide (evidence) sets groups to identify how errors arise when accepting cash payments and how they turn into losses, then present to the class — the six-count audit in this lesson follows that pattern, with the student book's charge-sheet as the audit list.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the six problems in the book's order — week's delay, collection time, robbery risk, leakage, processing time, the notes' short costly life — counted on your fingers.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six problems from memory and check the count.
2. Write which two counts are about time, which two about security, one about customers, one about the notes.
3. Write one sentence: which count would worry a lone cashier most, and why?
