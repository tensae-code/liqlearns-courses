# Checking Tyre Pressure — Placard, Gauge, Inflate, Cap

## Do it first

Six actions, shuffled: read a reading; open a small cap; push a gauge on; check a door placard; add air; close the cap. Put them in the order you would actually do them at a real car. Then compare with the book's six steps.

## Sub lesson 1 — The six steps — placard to cap

**Step 1 — Read the correct pressures** from the **tyre placard** (usually inside a front door opening) or the **owner's manual**. Front and rear pressures often differ — read both.

**Step 2 — Unscrew the valve cap. Step 3 — Push the gauge firmly onto the valve. Step 4 — Read the pressure.**

**Step 5 — If the pressure is low, attach the air hose and inflate to the correct level, rechecking with the gauge.** **Step 6 — Screw the cap back on.** Placard, cap, gauge, read, inflate, cap: six steps, and the cap bookends the job — off at step 2, on at step 6.

## Sub lesson 2 — The gauge — the recommended tool and its signals

The gauge the book recommends buying is the **pen-shaped pneumatic/mechanical gauge**, for accurate readings; a typical mechanical gauge is precise to **±3 psi (21 kPa)**.

The gauge also speaks: a **hissing sound means the gauge is not seated tight, or its angle needs adjusting**. Silence and a steady reading mean a square seat. The teacher guide adds the practical frame: service-station air hoses exist, but the standard recommendation is to buy the simple pen gauge and record the readings — your own gauge, your own record.

## From the teacher guide (evidence)

The teacher guide (evidence) frames this lesson's practice: the standard recommendation is to buy a simple pen-shaped pneumatic/mechanical gauge for accurate readings, and readings are recorded; pair work is organised with gender balance in the group; the tyre-workplace visit is arranged considering students with disabilities when moving between places; and assessment takes learners to a tyre workplace (or shows pictures/video) for a briefing on the tyre pressure chart, the pressure gauge and the tread gauge. Support materials include internet/YouTube videos downloaded if possible.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion through the six steps in order — placard, cap, gauge, read, inflate-and-recheck, cap — then add the gauge facts: pen-shaped, ±3 psi, and what a hiss means.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six steps from memory and check them against the lesson.
2. With an adult, find a family or neighbour vehicle's placard and write its front and rear values (look only).
3. Write one sentence: why does the cap appear twice in the six steps?
