# Your CV — Writing Your Achievements for an Employer

## Do it first

On paper, list everything about you that an employer might want to know: your name and how to reach you, your schooling, things you can do, work you have done, things you are proud of. Do not organise it yet — just empty your pockets onto the page.

## Sub lesson 1 — The CV and the sample CV

A **curriculum vitae (CV)** is the detailed document that highlights a person's **professional and academic achievements** for an employer considering them for employment. It carries:

- **personal information** — name, age, sex, address, email, phone;
- **qualifications and education**;
- **work experience and other achievements**.

The book's printed sample CV, for a primary-school student, shows the shape at this level: name, an Addis Ababa address, telephone and email lines; **skill highlights** (gardening, poultry, family support activities at home); **education** (elementary school, Grade 7); **hobbies** (football, morning exercise, tennis, pool); and a **reference** — the home-room teacher, with a mobile number.

Your experience counts even before any paid job: skills practised at home and in the community belong in the skill highlights, honestly stated.

## Sub lesson 2 — Deeper: stay current, and fit your CV to the job

Two of the Grade 8 book's hiring tips belong to the CV itself.

**Tip 1 — be up to date.** Beyond qualifications, employers look for people who can create a path to the future of the business: innovative ideas, creativity, motivation, attitude and strategic time management, kept current in communication and in the qualities diverse tasks demand. A CV should show a person who is still growing.

**Tip 2 — adjust your CV to the job description.** A CV is the comprehensive listing of the significant achievements of a career plus the personal basics (age, sex, marital status, hobbies). Employers post the requirements of each position — so the applicant **produces the CV in line with those requirements**: the same true achievements, ordered and worded so the match with this job is easy to see. Tailoring never means inventing; it means choosing and foregrounding what is true and relevant.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the one thing this lesson teaches, in your own words: a CV is the detailed document of your achievements that an employer reads — and, deeper, a strong CV is tailored: rewritten to match the requirements of the one job you are applying for. Your companion checks you against the lesson, then swaps roles.

## Do It Yourself

1. Copy the five sections of the sample CV as headings.
2. Fill each heading with your own true information.
3. Read one job advertisement and underline its requirements; then star the lines of your CV that match them.
