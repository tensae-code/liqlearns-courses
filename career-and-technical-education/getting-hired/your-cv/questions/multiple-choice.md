# Your CV — Writing Your Achievements for an Employer — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A CV is —
a) a letter asking for money  b) the detailed document highlighting a person's professional and academic achievements for an employer  c) a school report card
**Answer: b) the detailed document highlighting a person's professional and academic achievements for an employer** — that is the book's definition.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Which belongs in a CV's personal information?
a) Name, address, email and phone  b) The employer's salary  c) Your friend's hobbies
**Answer: a) Name, address, email and phone** — personal information is who you are and how to reach you.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** In the book's sample CV, the reference is —
a) a famous athlete  b) the home-room teacher, with a mobile number  c) left blank
**Answer: b) the home-room teacher, with a mobile number** — the sample names the home-room teacher as reference.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Tip 2 — adjust your CV to the job description — means —
a) invent achievements the job wants  b) produce the CV in line with the posted requirements, foregrounding your true matching achievements  c) send the identical CV to every job
**Answer: b) produce the CV in line with the posted requirements, foregrounding your true matching achievements** — tailoring chooses and foregrounds; it never invents.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** 'Be up to date' (Tip 1) shows an employer a person who —
a) has stopped learning  b) keeps current — ideas, creativity, motivation and communication  c) only collects certificates
**Answer: b) keeps current — ideas, creativity, motivation and communication** — employers look for people who can create a path to the business's future.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** A tailored CV is produced —
a) once, for all jobs  b) in line with the requirements of the position applied for  c) by the employer
**Answer: b) in line with the requirements of the position applied for** — employers post requirements; the applicant matches the CV to them.
