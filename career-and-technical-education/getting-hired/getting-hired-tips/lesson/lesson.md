# Getting Hired — Soft Skills, Contacts, and Thinking Like the Employer

## Do it first

List every way you have ever heard of a job being available — a notice, a friend, a phone post, walking past a shop. Star the ways that came through a person who knew you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Soft skills and your network (Tips 3–4)

**Tip 3 — emphasise soft skills.** Businesses are rapidly transforming toward soft skills — **teamwork and time management** named as more critical than ever — so employers emphasise them. A pupil who lacks them develops them by **engaging in volunteering and community projects**.

**Tip 4 — use your contacts.** Professional networks develop a career and help it reach its goals. Relationships with people who might be your next employers take a career in the right direction: **introduce yourself** to individuals and organisations, and interact with experts from different industries who can guide you with their knowledge and inputs.

## Sub lesson 2 — The employer's view, social media, smart preparation (Tips 5–7)

**Tip 5 — think like the employer.** Put yourself in an employer's shoes. Understand the difference between **benefits** (what the employer gains by hiring you) and **features** (your strengths that back your nomination) — and know your core strengths.

**Tip 6 — use social media wisely.** Job announcements and hiring have moved onto **Facebook, Twitter (X), LinkedIn, Google+, WhatsApp and Telegram**, which have become a source of vacancies.

**Tip 7 — prepare smartly for interview.** The interview lets the employer understand your basic skills, core skills and personal qualities. Before it, **research your target employer thoroughly**: its products and services, its working environment and its areas of business operation.
