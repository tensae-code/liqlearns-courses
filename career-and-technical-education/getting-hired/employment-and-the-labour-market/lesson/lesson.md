# Employment and the Labour Market

## Do it first

Name three people you know who earn a living. For each one, answer: who pays them — an employer, or the customers who buy what they make or do? Keep your three answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Working for someone, or for yourself

Employment is getting a job to earn a living: a person doing **productive work** — producing useful products or services — and receiving income **in money or in kind** is employed.

Employment has **two kinds**:

- **Salary/wage employment** — working for an employer, who pays you a salary or wage.
- **Self-employment** — working for yourself, generating income directly from the people who buy your output.

Self-employment carries its own advantages: **freedom and flexibility**, doing work you love, setting your own hours, choosing what work to take, changing or stopping within legal requirements, **creating a job for yourself and for others**, and financial independence.

## Sub lesson 2 — The labour market — where jobs and workers meet

The **labour market** is the mechanism where potential employers and employees meet for employment. Employers seek people with knowledge and skills; skilled people seek jobs. The two sides find each other there.

A **local labour market** is the set of jobs a worker can apply for without travelling far — your own town or city. The **national market** covers the whole country.

Opportunities in Ethiopia are expanding: flower plantations, large-scale farming, sugar factories, rural road construction, telecommunications, electricity, and tractors and harvesting machines are opening jobs in both urban and rural areas.
