# Voluntary Work — Helping Freely, Gaining Experience

## Do it first

Think of one time someone helped your family or your street without being paid. Who was it? What did they give — time, energy, a skill? Keep that example in mind.

## Sub lesson 1 — What voluntary work is

**Voluntary work** (volunteerism) is giving your **time, energy and talent** of your **own free will, free of charge**, to help the community, a group, an individual or an organisation.

The book's examples: young people rebuilding elders' houses; doctors giving free service in a hospital; lawyers volunteering at legal centres for low-income people; youth supporting HIV patients at home.

No wage changes hands. The giving itself is the work.

## Sub lesson 2 — What voluntary work gives back

Voluntary work is unpaid, but it is not unrewarded. Its benefits:

- **personal satisfaction**;
- using the skills you have, or **developing new ones**;
- **real experience** that is useful for employment;
- **self-confidence**, from applying what school taught you;
- **meeting new people** and making friends;
- taking part in **community development**.

And one more, looking ahead to hiring: employers read voluntary service as a **sign of commitment, loyalty and a desire to contribute** — the volunteer looks like a worker who will show up.
