# Lesson plan — Workplace Problems — Conflict, Communication, Discrimination, Harassment, Dissatisfaction, Gossip

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Getting Hired · **Lesson:** Workplace Problems — Conflict, Communication, Discrimination, Harassment, Dissatisfaction, Gossip
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 9 — Getting Hired (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md ladder).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the six common workplace problems — conflict, poor communication, discrimination, harassment, job dissatisfaction and gossip — what each one is, what causes it, and what counters it.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the problem-solving lesson before it: that lesson gives the method; this one names the problems the method will meet.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this same title, in ladder order — and, for the whole title, the trades and career lessons earlier in the course. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump in here directly; this lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six common workplace problems.
2. Define each problem in one line.
3. Give the cause list for job dissatisfaction and the improvement list.
4. Say how harassment is prevented, and what counters gossip.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening task in lesson/lesson.md, before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of three labelled scenes — two workers clashing, a message lost between desk and boss, a worker passed over unfairly — the student names each problem before reading its label.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of whispers behind a colleague's back with a crossed-out speech bubble — the student says what gossip damages.
3. **Say it to your companion** — in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — told in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

- IMAGE (described slot only) — the crossed-out gossip bubble — the student lists what gossip damages.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student tells the lesson's main idea along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up dissatisfaction and gossip (the book's own review does this — see sources) → anchor by subject: dissatisfaction is a feeling about YOUR job; gossip is talk about OTHER people.
