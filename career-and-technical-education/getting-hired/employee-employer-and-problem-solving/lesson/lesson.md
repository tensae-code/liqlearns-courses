# Employee and Employer — and Solving Problems at Work

## Do it first

Two words: employee, employer. One hires, one is hired. Say which is which — then think of one shop you know and name both people in it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Employee and employer — who is who

An **employee** is an individual **hired by an employer to do a specific job** — hired after an application and interview process. If the applicant is selected, the person is called an employee.

An **employer** is the one who employs: an organisation, institution, government office, agency, company, nonprofit association, small business, supermarket, shop — or an individual — who employs or puts to work individuals, who may be called employees or staff members.

One shop can hold both words at once: the owner who hires is the employer; the assistant who was hired is the employee.

## Sub lesson 2 — Problem-solving in the workplace

**Problem-solving** is the process of **identifying a problem, developing possible solution paths, and taking the appropriate course of action**. A problem is a situation that needs to be changed.

Employers use the term for handling difficult or unexpected workplace situations and complex business challenges **calmly**. The skills that feed it: **active listening, analysis, research, creativity, communication, dependability, decision making and team-building**.

The skill is bigger than work: relationship building and day-to-day decision-making use it too. And effective problem-solving may also need industry-specific technical knowledge — the book's nurse example: people skills with patients, medical knowledge of diseases and medications, and knowing when to consult a doctor.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Latamo story — a conflict handled step by step

Mr. **Latamo's** team had one persistently negative, non-team employee, **Legesse**. Latamo talked with him, tried to inspire and encourage him, and **documented his verbal warnings** — but Legesse kept falling back.

When a disagreement between Legesse and another staff member broke out on the main floor, Latamo went there **immediately**, calmly stopped the conflict, and explained that workplace conflict is unacceptable — it disturbs the working environment and reduces productivity. Then he gathered all staff to discuss the reason: **harassment** — Legesse touching Miss **Sajitu's** hair without her permission.

Because a verbal warning was already documented, Latamo issued a **written warning letter** as the final step before termination, and the staff together agreed to create a peaceful working environment.

Read the moves in order: talk and encourage → document warnings → stop the incident at once → name why it is unacceptable → find the real reason with everyone → written warning, because the earlier step was documented → a shared agreement. Each step follows from the one before.
