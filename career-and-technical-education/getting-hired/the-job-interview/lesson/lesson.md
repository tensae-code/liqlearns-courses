# The Job Interview — Your Conversation with an Employer

## Do it first

Sit facing a chair. Imagine the employer behind it asks: 'Tell us about yourself.' Say your answer out loud, as well as you can right now, and time it. Keep it — you will answer it again at the end, better.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a job interview is

An **interview** is a conversation between two or more persons to gather information for a purpose. A **job interview** is the employer (the **interviewer**) questioning a candidate (the **interviewee**) to **select the right person for a job** — different from, for example, a doctor–patient interview, which gathers information for treatment.

It matters to **both sides**: it connects employers and job seekers and lets each decide whether the individual's skills and character align with the firm's needs and culture. The employer interviews to learn the knowledge, skills and qualities that a CV or resume **cannot show** — how you speak, think and carry yourself.

## Sub lesson 2 — The common seven questions — and the four-step answer

Employers open with **open-ended questions** to get to know the candidate — their experience, skills and interest. Questions vary by job, employer and situation, but the common ones are:

1. Tell us about yourself.
2. What are your greatest strengths?
3. How did you hear about this job?
4. Why should we hire you?
5. Why do you want this job?
6. What can you do for us that other candidates cannot?
7. What are your weaknesses?

Answer any of them well with the **four-step method**:

1. **Know the job description** — read what the employer is looking for, and reflect the role and the organisation in your answers.
2. **Give specific examples** — past experience in concrete actions shows future intention.
3. **Handle follow-up questions** — elaborate on what you said.
4. **Tie every answer to the job and the organisation.**

At the end: ask when results will be announced, express your interest in working there, and **thank the interviewer**.
