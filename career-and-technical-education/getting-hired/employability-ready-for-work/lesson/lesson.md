# Employability — Ready to Be Hired, and to Stay Hired

## Do it first

Think of two workers doing the same job. One is kept and promoted; the other is let go. What is different about them — what they know, what they can do, or how they behave? Write your guess.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four supports of employability

**Employability** is building the capability that makes a person more likely to choose and gain initial employment: a mix of **skills, abilities and personal qualities** that makes an individual competitive, builds self-confidence and a spirit to excel.

It rests on **four supports**:

- **Knowledge** — what you know.
- **Skills** — what you can do with what you know; the technical skills.
- **Attitudes** — how you approach things, positive or negative.
- **Presentation** — how you communicate those capabilities, orally and in writing, to potential employers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Deeper: skills AND behaviours

The deeper treatment insists on one thing: employability is the development of skills **and** an adaptable workforce — everyone capable of work is encouraged to develop the skills, knowledge, technology and adaptability to **enter and remain in employment throughout their working lives**, in their own business or hired in another's organisation.

**Employability skills are the set of skills AND behaviours necessary for every job.** Having only the job's technical skills does **not** guarantee career success without the behaviours.

The core set named at the unit's opening: the technical skills of the duties, plus professional and personal attributes — **honesty, reliability, punctuality, attendance, loyalty** — on a base of **reading, writing, listening, speaking and computing**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Deeper still: secure, keep, progress, re-enter

On the **International Labour Organization's (ILO)** framing, employability skills are the skills, knowledge and competencies that enhance a worker's ability to:

- **secure** a job, and **retain** it;
- **progress** at work;
- **cope with change**;
- **secure another job if laid off**;
- and **enter the labour market** more easily at different periods of the life cycle.

The most employable people combine broad-based education and training with basic and high-level skills — teamwork, problem-solving, ICT, communication and language. Employers want people who **keep learning and adapting**, read, write and compute competently, listen and communicate effectively, and think creatively and solve problems independently.
