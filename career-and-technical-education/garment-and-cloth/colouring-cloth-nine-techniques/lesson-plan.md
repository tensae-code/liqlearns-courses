# Lesson plan — Colouring cloth — nine techniques

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Garment and Cloth · **Lesson:** Colouring cloth — nine techniques
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Garment and Cloth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** nine distinct hand techniques for putting colour on cloth — freehand, dot, outliner, spray, block, stencil, finger, one-stroke and sun colouring.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Colouring Cloth — Preparing; now the nine ways themselves, each a hand skill.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Answer: Dot colouring is printed with the dipped end of a pencil or an ear bud.
2. Answer: Outliner painting uses a thin 00 liner brush or outliner.
3. Answer: Finger colouring is used when no brushes exist.
4. Answer: One-stroke colouring paints a petal or leaf in a single stroke, with a flat brush loaded with one or more shades.
5. Answer: A home block stamp can be made from cut potato or other vegetables.

## What you need
