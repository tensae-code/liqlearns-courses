# Colouring cloth — nine techniques

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: nine distinct hand techniques for putting colour on cloth — freehand, dot, outliner, spray, block, stencil, finger, one-stroke and sun colouring.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hand techniques

- **Freehand** — drawing by hand with no guides.
- **Dot colouring** — the dipped end of a pencil or an ear bud printing dots.
- **Outliner painting** — a thin 00 liner brush or outliner drawing the design's outline.
- **Finger colouring** — fingers applying colour directly when no brushes exist.
- **One-stroke colouring** — a flat brush loaded with one or more shades, painting a petal or leaf in a single stroke.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stamp, stencil and spray

- **Block colouring** — stamps pressed into colour then onto fabric: cut potato or other vegetables as home stamps, wooden blocks with letters or patterns.
- **Stencil colouring** — a stencil fixed on the fabric (a hard board inside a T-shirt stops colour soaking through), filled with brush or sponge, sprayed sticky on its back, then lifted carefully.
- **Spray colouring** — a sprayer bottle, or blowing colour through a straw — with the health rule: **cover nose and mouth** against spray chemicals.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sun colouring

- **Sun colouring** — colour washed over fabric, leaves, flowers or feathers laid on it, dried in full sun so the covered shapes remain as faint highlighted impressions.

## Say it to your companion

Say the main points back without looking: nine distinct hand techniques for putting colour on cloth — freehand, dot, outliner, spray, block, stencil, finger, one-stroke and sun colouring. Then name the steps or parts in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Dot drill (the book's practice).** Practise dot colouring with an ear bud: dip, print, lift — a row of even dots, then a flower made of dots.
2. **Make a stamp (the book's practice).** Cut a potato stamp (with a teacher's help with the knife), press it into colour, and print a repeating pattern. Then finger-colour a second cloth in class, as the book sets.
3. **One-stroke petal.** Load a flat brush with two shades side by side and sweep one petal in a single stroke. If you go back over it, start again — one stroke only.
