# Sewing — hand needle and machine

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: sewing stitches fabrics together with thread and needle, by hand or by machine.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — What sewing is

**Sewing** stitches fabrics together with **thread and needle**, by hand or by machine.

It is a near-universal, ancient craft. It produces clothes — coats, trousers, shirts, dresses — and household furnishings — curtains, bedclothes, table linens.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hand and machine

Before modern machines, Ethiopian sewing was **all by hand**.

The **sewing machine** stitches fabric and other materials together with thread. Models vary enormously in options, but the **basic operation is the same on every machine, old or new** — learn one machine's working and you have the key to the rest.

## Say it to your companion

Say the main points back without looking: sewing stitches fabrics together with thread and needle, by hand or by machine. Then name the steps or parts in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Hand-stitch a seam.** Thread a hand needle, knot the thread, and stitch two scrap cloth pieces together with even stitches. Keep the line straight and the spacing even.
2. **Compare hand and machine.** If a machine is available, look at a machine-sewn seam beside your hand seam. Name what is the same (thread joining fabric) and what differs (speed, evenness).
