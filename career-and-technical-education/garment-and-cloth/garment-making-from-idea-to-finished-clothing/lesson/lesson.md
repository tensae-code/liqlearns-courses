# Garment making — from idea to finished clothing

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: garment making is every process of making clothes, in sequence from the idea or design concept to the finished product.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — The process in order

A **garment** is a piece of clothing. **Garment making** is every process, activity and technology of making clothes.

It runs in sequence, in this order:

1. **Choose the fabric**
2. **Design**
3. **Cut**
4. **Stitch**
5. **Sew**
6. **Check**
7. **Press (iron)**
8. **Package for delivery**

Skip a step and the finished piece shows it: uncut fabric cannot be sewn, an unchecked garment can go out with a fault, an unpressed garment looks unfinished.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional clothes and the tailor's discipline

The lesson opens with how traditional Ethiopian clothes are prepared — garment making is an old, living craft here, not a factory invention.

Its discipline is the tailor's saying: **"measure ten times, then cut."** Measuring can be done again. A wrong cut cannot be undone. That saying guards the whole process.

## Say it to your companion

Say the main points back without looking: garment making is every process of making clothes, in sequence from the idea or design concept to the finished product. Then name the steps or parts in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Walk the eight stations.** Lay eight papers on the floor in order — fabric, design, cut, stitch, sew, check, press, package. Walk from one to the next, saying at each station what happens there and what goes wrong if it is skipped.
2. **Measure ten times.** Take a piece of scrap cloth. Measure one line on it, then measure it again nine more times before you are allowed to mark it. Feel why the saying exists.
