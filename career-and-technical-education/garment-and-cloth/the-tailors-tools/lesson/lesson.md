# The tailor's tools — measuring, marking, cutting, pressing

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: the tailor's hand tools beside the machine — for measuring, marking, cutting, holding, pressing and threading.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Measure, mark and cut

- **Scissors** — cutting fabric and thread, in kinds.
- **Measuring tape / ruler** — for size, width and curves; centimetres on one side, inches on the other.
- **Tailor's chalk** — removable fabric marking; ordinary teaching chalk can substitute.
- **Frixion erasable pens** — transferring pattern notches onto light fabrics.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hold, press and thread

- **Needles** — machine and hand; size follows fabric weight: lighter fabric, smaller needle.
- **Pins and pincushion** — holding fabric in place for cutting or sewing.
- **Iron and water spray** — smoothing fabric before and during sewing, with a press cloth against burning or bleaching, and an iron board.
- **Thimble** — the pitted finger cup that pushes a hand needle safely — and a **needle threader**.
- **Threads** — cotton for woven fabrics, polyester better for knits, nylon and woolly nylon.
- **Lubrication oil** — preventive maintenance, reducing friction between moving machine parts.

## Say it to your companion

Say the main points back without looking: the tailor's hand tools beside the machine — for measuring, marking, cutting, holding, pressing and threading. Then name the steps or parts in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Chalk a star.** As the book sets: chalk-draw a five-pointed star on cloth. Measure it first, mark with tailor's chalk (or teaching chalk), and check the points are even.
2. **Thread a needle three ways.** Thread a hand needle by eye, then with a needle threader, then help a partner. Name which thread you would pick for a woven cloth and which for a knit, and why.
3. **Pin and press drill.** Pin two scrap pieces together along a chalk line, keeping pins inside the line. Press a wrinkled scrap with an iron and press cloth — no scorch marks allowed.
