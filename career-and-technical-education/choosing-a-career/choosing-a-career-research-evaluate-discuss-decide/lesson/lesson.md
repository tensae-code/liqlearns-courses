# Choosing a Career — Research, Evaluate, Discuss, Decide

## Do it first

In a group of three, build the fullest list you can of careers actually available in your own area — before anyone says which ones they like. No judging yet. That list is Step 1 already running.

## Sub lesson 1 — Step 1 — Research what exists near you

**Career searching** means listing **ALL the careers actually available in your own area** — **comprehensively, before judging any of them**. The local families named are **animal production, crop production, manufacturing, construction and services**. Work in **groups of three** to build the full local list.

[Picture 11814 — research: the workplaces of one locality]

Run it as the guide runs it (teacher guide — evidence): brainstorm local options for **2–3 minutes**, then reflect on the list.

## Sub lesson 2 — Step 2 — Evaluate and compare

**Evaluation** compares the listed careers against each other — **availability of resources**, and the **knowledge level, skills and experience each requires** — so you understand a *collection* of careers in detail instead of fixating on one. The drill: **narrow the class list to five careers, and state the qualification each one demands**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Step 3 — Discuss it with others

In **group discussion**, say what you liked about each shortlisted career, **compare chosen against non-chosen**, **learn new job tracks from peers**, and test the requirements and possibilities of a shared interest. Know this plainly: **a preferred choice may be dropped at exactly this stage — and dropping it is part of the process**, not a failure of it. (Teacher guide — evidence: groups of **four to six**, discussing for **5–7 minutes**.)

## Sub lesson 4 — Step 4 — Decide on one (the Sarkabo story)

After research, evaluation and discussion, **select ONE career that fits**.

The cautionary story: **Sarkabo of Southern Ethiopia** spread himself across **barley, teff and coffee growing, a 50-chicken poultry farm and a selling shop**. When he did his **income-and-cost sums**, he found he **could not manage all of them profitably** — and finally **committed to poultry production alone**. Focus after comparison beats doing everything badly.

Then state your own chosen career, and why.

## Say it to your companion

Name the four steps in order, one breath each. Then tell the Sarkabo story in four sentences — ventures, sums, finding, commitment.

## Do It Yourself

1. Finish your group's comprehensive local list. Count it. (If it is under fifteen, research more.)
2. Narrow it to your five, each with its required qualification written beside it.
3. Write your decided career and your reason — and the name of the favourite you honourably dropped at discussion, if there was one.
