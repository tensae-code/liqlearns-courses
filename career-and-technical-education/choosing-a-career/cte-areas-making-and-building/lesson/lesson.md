# CTE Areas — Making and Building

## Do it first

Hold one raw material and one finished good side by side — cloth and a garment, timber and a stool, cane and sugar if you can find them. Say what happened between the two: whose hands, what tools, what work? That happening is this lesson's first door.

## Sub lesson 1 — Manufacturing — raw material in, finished goods out

**Manufacturing** turns **raw materials into finished goods**, using **labour, machinery, tools and processing**. Sugarcane goes in — **sugar** comes out. Cotton goes in — a **jacket** comes out. Manufacturing also includes **product design and material choice**: deciding what to make, and what to make it from.

Its four sub-areas:

- **Metal manufacturing** — sheets, machine parts, bolts, wires, and gold/silversmith jewellery.
- **Automotive technology** — building and repairing vehicles: engines, electrics, wheels, brakes, painting.
- **Textile and leather garment** — clothes and threads from fibres; shoes from hides.
- **Wood-work technology** — tables, chairs, beds and doors from timber.

## Sub lesson 2 — Construction — building, wiring, plumbing, finishing

**Construction** covers **planning, designing, building, altering, maintaining, repairing and finally demolishing** buildings, bridges, roads and railways — the whole life of a built thing, from first plan to last demolition.

Its four sub-areas:

- **Electricity** — designing, installing, testing and repairing electrical systems.
- **Plumbing** — laying and repairing water pipes, tanks, taps and fittings.
- **Carpentry** — cutting, shaping and installing timber: roofs, floors, doors, furniture.
- **Building finishing works** — plastering, tiling, painting, fixing doors; on furniture, polishing, sealing and varnishing.

## Say it to your companion

Your companion names a finished good — a bolt, a shoe, a painted wall, a repaired brake. You say which door and which sub-area made it, and what raw material it started from.

## Do It Yourself

1. List five manufactured goods in your own home. For each, write its raw material and its sub-area.
2. Walk one building near you, in your mind or in person. Write which of construction's four sub-areas have visibly worked on it, with the evidence you saw.
3. Star the sub-area (of all eight in this lesson) you would most like to watch a worker do for a full day, and say why.
