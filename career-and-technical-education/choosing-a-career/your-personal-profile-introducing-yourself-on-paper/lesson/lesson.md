# Your Personal Profile — Introducing Yourself on Paper

## Do it first

Introduce yourself to a stranger in writing — in five lines only. Who you are, what you are good at, what you have learned and done. Try it now, before the rules. You will rewrite it at the end, and compare.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a personal profile is — and why employers ask for it

A **personal profile** is a **short introduction setting out who a person is** — name, age, school, grade, birthplace and the like — **plus their characteristics, qualities, education, experience and skills**. Cooking, translating, singing and writing are the book's skill examples.

It introduces you in **personal relationships** — and for a job, it is a **requirement**: before hiring, **employers use it to judge whether an applicant has the skills, knowledge and experience the job needs**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing it in three moves — and the DOs and DON'Ts

A job profile is written **sequentially, briefly, and focused on what matters for that job**, in three moves:

1. **Describe yourself briefly** — name, address (region, zone, woreda, town, school), age, sex as the purpose requires.
2. **State your skills and abilities** — strengths such as translation, a language, knitting, drawing, plus character statements like hard-working, diligent, cooperative.
3. **Include education and experience** — schooling level and any task experience, even voluntary.

It must stay **short and concise, so it can be read quickly**.

The rule card (teacher guide — evidence — a graphic the student book never prints): **DO** introduce yourself, use relevant details, be direct to the point, and proofread. **DON'T** use flowery language, be redundant, exaggerate details, or make it too long.

## Say it to your companion

Read your companion your three moves aloud, in order. They listen with the DOs and DON'Ts card and must name one DO you kept and any DON'T you broke.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write your own full profile now, in the three moves, with assumed skills and experience where yours are still thin (the book's own instruction).
2. Proofread it against the card. Cut every flowery or repeated line.
3. Compare it with your Do-it-first five lines. Write one sentence: what did the method change?
