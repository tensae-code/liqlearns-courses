# Lesson plan — Your Personal Profile — Introducing Yourself on Paper

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Choosing a Career · **Lesson:** Your Personal Profile — Introducing Yourself on Paper
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Choosing a Career (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md ladder).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a personal profile introduces you on paper — who you are, your qualities, education, experience and skills — and it is written in three short moves.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Soft Skills: the learner can name their skills; now they learn to present them the way employers ask for.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **What a personal profile is — and why employers ask for it** — A short introduction: who you are, plus characteristics, qualities, education, experience and skills.
2. **Writing it in three moves — and the DOs and DON'Ts** — Describe yourself briefly; state skills and abilities; include education and experience — short, direct, proofread.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Soft Skills — the Skills Every Job Needs. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a personal profile holds and why employers require it.
2. Write a profile in the three moves.
3. Apply the DOs and DON'Ts card (teacher guide — evidence) to their own draft.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a ruler for the form's sections, if drawing the form by hand.

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes a life story instead of a profile → cut to the card: short, concise, readable quickly; every sentence must serve this job.
- Exaggerates a skill → the DON'T card is blunt: no exaggeration; a checked profile (hands-on) exposes it kindly, on paper, before an employer does.
