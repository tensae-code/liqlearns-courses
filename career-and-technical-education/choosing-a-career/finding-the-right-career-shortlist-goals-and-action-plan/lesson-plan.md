# Lesson plan — Finding the Right Career — Shortlist, Goals and Action Plan

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Choosing a Career · **Lesson:** Finding the Right Career — Shortlist, Goals and Action Plan
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Choosing a Career (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md ladder). The Grade 8 book's finer seven-step method.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** work a master list down to a shortlist, choose with firsthand knowledge, then turn the choice into goals and a written A-to-D action plan.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the four-step method: research/evaluate/discuss/decide gave one career; this finer method checks it — or finds it — with a funnel and a plan.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved:

1. **List, explore, narrow to a shortlist** — Master list → explore and cut to five-to-ten → eliminate to a shortlist of two-to-five.
2. **Inform yourself, choose, set goals, make an action plan** — Firsthand information, the choice (changeable), long/short goals, and the A-to-D action plan.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Choosing a Career — Research, Evaluate, Discuss, Decide. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Work the funnel: master list → 5–10 → shortlist of 2–5.
2. Gather firsthand information and choose — knowing the choice may change.
3. Set long- and short-term goals and write an A-to-D action plan.

## What you need
Paper (lined, for the shortlist — the guide's own material) and a pencil; a guest speaker from a local enterprise, where one can be invited (teacher guide — evidence).

## If the learner is stuck
- Cuts straight from master list to one → the funnel exists to protect the choice: explore before eliminating, inform before choosing.
- Treats the choice as a life sentence → the book's explicit permission: you may change your mind at any point in life; the plan is a roadmap, not a prison.
