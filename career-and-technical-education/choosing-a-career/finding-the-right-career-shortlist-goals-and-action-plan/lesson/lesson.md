# Finding the Right Career — Shortlist, Goals and Action Plan

## Do it first

Write down every occupation you have ever seriously considered — from every list, test and daydream you have. No crossing out. That pile is your master list, and this lesson's funnel is built for exactly it.

## Sub lesson 1 — List, explore, narrow to a shortlist

A finer method works a master list down, in three steps:

1. **List.** Combine every list from your self-assessment tools into **one master list of occupations to explore**.
2. **Explore.** Explore it with **basic information on each occupation** and cut it to **five to ten options**.
3. **Narrow.** Narrow further, **eliminating careers you no longer want**, until a **shortlist of two to five** remains.

## Sub lesson 2 — Inform yourself, choose, set goals, make an action plan

4. **Inform yourself.** Get **in-depth information** on the **three-to-five survivors**, **asking people already in those careers for firsthand knowledge**. (Teacher guide — evidence: invite a **guest speaker** from the surrounding area — agricultural office, construction, enterprises or cooperatives — to speak to the shortlisted careers' qualifications, market demand and opportunities.)
5. **Choose.** Make the choice that **promises most satisfaction** — with the book's explicit permission: **you may change your mind at any point in life**.
6. **Set goals.** Identify goals: **long-term at roughly three to five years; short-term at six months to three years**.
7. **Make an action plan.** Write a **career action plan — a roadmap document from point A to B to C to D** — and discuss it in a group.

[Picture 11816 — the action-plan roadmap: A to D]

## Say it to your companion

Recite the seven steps and the three funnel numbers (all → 5–10 → 2–5). Then tell your companion your current B stop — the first real stop on your own roadmap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build your real master list on lined paper (the guide's own material) and cut it, honestly, to your five-to-ten.
2. Narrow to your shortlist of two-to-five. Write the name of the person you will ask for firsthand knowledge on your first survivor.
3. Draft your action plan: A (where I am), B, C, D (the chosen career), with one long-term and one short-term goal written beside it.
