# What a Career Is — and What Career Selection Means

## Do it first

Write down every role you already hold in one week: learner, family helper, any paid work, any unpaid community work. Now ask: if all of those, over many years, add up to something — what would you call the whole thing? Hold your answer.

## The wider definition

You met *career* in First Steps. Here is the same word, wider — the Grade 8 climb of it:

A **career** is not one job. It **covers all the roles of a life — education, training, and paid and unpaid work — held over a significant period**, giving **progress and prosperity**.

[Picture 11806 — one career, many life roles]

Student. Trainee. Paid worker. Unpaid community worker. Different roles, one career underneath them — yours.

**Career selection** is the **choosing of that occupation**. And the choosing is not easy: in the 21st century it is made harder because **mass media shapes personal choices** — the loudest career is not always the right one for you. That is why this title teaches a method, not a guess: research, evaluate, discuss, decide — then list, explore, narrow, choose, plan.

## Say it to your companion

Define both words for your companion — *career* and *career selection* — then name one way mass media has already pushed on a choice of yours or someone you know.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw your own life-role strip for today: every role you hold now.
2. Extend the strip ten years: write the roles you hope it holds then, under one career heading of your choosing.
3. Write one sentence on what could push that choice off course — and one sentence on what will keep it yours.
