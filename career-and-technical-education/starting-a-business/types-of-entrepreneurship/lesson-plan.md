# Lesson plan — Types of Entrepreneurship — Small Business, Startup, Large Company, Social

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Starting a Business · **Lesson:** Types of Entrepreneurship — Small Business, Startup, Large Company, Social
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Starting a Business (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md ladder).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** entrepreneurs first choose which model they are building: a small business content to stay single, a scalable startup built to grow, a new venture inside a large company, or a social enterprise built to solve a community problem.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the idea and the founder story before it: having seen what an entrepreneur is, the learner now sorts ventures by what they are built to become.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four types of entrepreneurship.
2. Tell a small business from a scalable startup by intention.
3. Explain large-company entrepreneurship with the mango-juice case.
4. Say what makes social entrepreneurship different in mission and funding.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening task in lesson/lesson.md, before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the sub lessons in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a single grocery store with its founder-owner at the door and a 'not a chain' single-shop marker — the student says what the founder intends, and what they do not.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a community problem — a queue at a dry tap — with a small social enterprise's solution stall beside it — the student names the mission before the money.
3. **Say it to your companion** — in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — told in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts by size instead of intention → a startup starts as small as a shop. The sorting key is what it is BUILT to become: stay single, grow, grow inside a company, or serve a community problem.
