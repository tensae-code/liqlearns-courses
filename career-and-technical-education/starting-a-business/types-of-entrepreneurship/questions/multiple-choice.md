# Types of Entrepreneurship — Small Business, Startup, Large Company, Social — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A small business founder has —
a) a plan to build a chain  b) NO intention of growing it into a chain or corporation  c) only outside investors
**Answer: b) NO intention of growing it into a chain or corporation** — the single grocery store is the model example.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Small business entrepreneurs mostly invest —
a) from their own pockets  b) only bank loans  c) only grants
**Answer: a) from their own pockets** — own pockets is the book's funding note.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A scalable startup differs from a small business in —
a) starting size  b) its intention for long-term evolution — it is built to grow  c) having no product
**Answer: b) its intention for long-term evolution — it is built to grow** — both start small; intention separates them.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Outside investors are a common feature of —
a) small businesses  b) scalable startups  c) edir
**Answer: b) scalable startups** — investors are named for startups.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** In the book's case, the mango-juice venture is large-company entrepreneurship because —
a) it starts from nothing  b) it is a new business created inside an existing agro-processing operation  c) it is a charity
**Answer: b) it is a new business created inside an existing agro-processing operation** — the venture grows inside what exists.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** Social entrepreneurship is organised —
a) for profit first  b) for serving society, not for profit, though it may earn income to expand its services  c) to avoid all income
**Answer: b) for serving society, not for profit, though it may earn income to expand its services** — mission is its defining difference.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Social enterprises often raise funds through —
a) grants, sponsorships and small-donor fundraising in the community  b) only share markets  c) only bank overdrafts
**Answer: a) grants, sponsorships and small-donor fundraising in the community** — alternative fundraising is named in the lesson.
