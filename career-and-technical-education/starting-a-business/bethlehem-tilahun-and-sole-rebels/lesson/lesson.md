# An Ethiopian Entrepreneur — Bethlehem Tilahun and Sole Rebels

## Do it first

Think of a skill people around you have that nobody is paying for — a craft, a repair, a food, a making. Write it down. You are looking at your community the way this story's founder looked at hers.

## Sub lesson 1 — What she saw

**Bethlehem Tilahun**, born and raised in **Zenebework, Addis Ababa**, saw her community living with very few jobs available — while many of its unemployed people possessed **remarkable artisan skills that remained largely unexploited**.

The opportunity was not a new invention. It was skilled hands, already there, with no enterprise built on them. She resolved to **transform those skills into a sustainable enterprise**.

## Sub lesson 2 — What she built

In **2004**, with start-up capital sourced from her **husband and immediate family**, she mobilised the artistically gifted community members and founded **Sole Rebels** — hand-crafted sandals and shoes made from **recycled old tyres** ('wearies') and locally sourced natural fibres such as **jute and enset**.

Sole Rebels became one of **Africa's most recognisable footwear manufacturers**, sold in **over 30 countries** — a sustainable, world-class enterprise built from a local observation.

The book then characterises the entrepreneur generally from her example: the entrepreneur is **the visionary and dreamer in us** — a person with a **high need for achievement**, energetic, and a **moderate risk taker**.
