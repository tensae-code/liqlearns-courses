# Lesson plan — Saving — the Entrepreneur's Own Funding

**Course:** Career and Technical Education · **Title:** Starting a Business · **Lesson:** Saving — the Entrepreneur's Own Funding
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Starting a Business (CURRICULUM.md / TITLES.md ladder).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** saving is the part of income not spent on consumption — and it is the entrepreneur's own road to investment: save regularly, invest, and the enterprise grows.
**This lesson's own level:** It is the closing lesson of the title and of the course ladder: the plan is written, the character named — this lesson funds the start from the entrepreneur's own income.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define saving in the book's words.
2. Say what saving sacrifices and what it buys.
3. Give the chain: income, savings, investment, enterprise.
4. Say why personal funds are called the most attractive, simplest and safest method.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A real container or account to save in helps.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks saving is what is left after spending → the chain only works the other way: save a portion regularly, first, and let consumption take what remains. Leftovers rarely survive.
