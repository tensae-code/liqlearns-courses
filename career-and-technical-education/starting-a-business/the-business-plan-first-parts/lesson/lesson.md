# The Business Plan — Summary, Mission, Situation and Product

## Do it first

Choose the enterprise you would actually start from the trades you know — poultry, hairdressing, photocopying, baking, any of them. Write its name and what it will sell. Every part of this lesson and the next is written onto that choice.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a business plan is

The bridge from the trade lists to a real start is this: select **one enterprise** and start it properly. The key process is the **business plan** — a **formal written document** containing:

- the **goals** of the business;
- the **methods** for achieving them;
- and the **time/duration** for achieving them.

Not an idea in the head — a document, written down, that others (a lender, a partner, you in six months) can read and test.

## Sub lesson 2 — Executive summary and mission/vision

**Executive summary** — a **one-to-three-page overview of the whole plan**. It is the part a lender reads to understand the business quickly, so it must carry a **clear message**.

**Mission/vision statement** — the business's **purpose and drive**, with the practical facts: the **start date, founders, employee numbers, location, facilities/equipment, and products/services**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Situational analysis and product/service

**Situational analysis** — the business's **internal strengths and weaknesses** set against **external opportunities and threats**. Honest on all four sides: what you are good at, what you lack, what the market offers, what could hurt you.

**Product/service** — **what the business will offer the market**. Named plainly: the thing or service, for the buyer who needs it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the one thing this lesson teaches, in your own words: a business plan is the formal written document of a business's goals, its methods, and its timeline — and its first components are the executive summary, the mission/vision, the situational analysis, and the product or service. Your companion checks you against the lesson, then swaps roles.
