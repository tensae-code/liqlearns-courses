# Kinds of Entrepreneurs — by Development and by Motivation

## Do it first

Think of four shopkeepers or makers you know. One always tries new things first; one copies what works; one never changes anything; one refuses every new idea. Put each person in a group in your head — the lesson names your groups.

## Sub lesson 1 — By economic development — innovator, imitator, fabian, drone

Four classes, by how the entrepreneur treats innovation:

- **(A) Innovating entrepreneurs** — creative, seeing the opportunity to introduce a **new technique, product or market**, resulting in something new.
- **(B) Adoptive/imitative entrepreneurs** — **copying or adopting** suitable innovations made by the innovators; not innovating themselves.
- **(C) Fabian entrepreneurs** — **neither willing to introduce new changes nor desiring to adopt** new production methods; following set procedures, customs, traditions and religions; **risk-averse**, walking in their predecessors' footsteps.
- **(D) Drone entrepreneurs** — **refusing to copy or use** the opportunities that come their way; conventional, sticking to their set practices, products, methods and ideas; **struggling to survive rather than to grow**.

## Sub lesson 2 — By motivation — pure, induced, motivated

**Motivation** is the main force promoting the entrepreneur's effort, and it sorts entrepreneurs three ways:

- **(a) The pure entrepreneur** — motivated by **psychological, economical and ethical considerations**, undertaking the activity for **satisfaction in work or status**.
- **(b) The induced entrepreneur** — induced to take up the task by **government policy reforms** — assistance, incentives and facilities to start a venture. **Most small-scale entrepreneurs belong to this category.**
- **(c) The motivated entrepreneur** — driven by the **desire for self-fulfilment**, coming into being through the possibility of **making and marketing new products** for consumers, and rewarded by profit.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the one thing this lesson teaches, in your own words: entrepreneurs sort two ways: by how they treat new methods — innovating, imitative, fabian, drone — and by what motivates them — pure, induced, motivated. Your companion checks you against the lesson, then swaps roles.
