# Reception skills — communicating, serving, juggling, welcoming

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: four basic receptionist skills: clear communication, customer service, multitasking and prioritising, and welcoming body language.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Communication and customer service

**Communication** — effective, clear written and verbal communication makes the workday productive.

**Customer service** means being pleasant, accommodating and attentive — sometimes a smile and the right answer is the whole job.

## Sub lesson 2 — Multitasking and welcoming body language

**Multitasking and prioritising** means juggling holds, several phone conversations and the person who just walked in — and knowing which comes first.

**Welcoming body language** is catalogued from high fives, smiles, nods and handshakes to thumbs-up and a pat on the back.

## Sub lesson 3 — (teacher guide — evidence) How these skills are assessed

In the teacher guide, groups discuss three set prompts while the teacher observes and records: why making visitors feel welcome matters, three questions a receptionist may ask a visitor, and welcoming body language. The lesson overview adds: a receptionist must keep the welcome procedure's quality consistent, and make sure every staff member, contractor and supplier it touches knows it.

## Do It Yourself

1. **The juggle drill.** Three partners at once: one phones (speaking), one walks in, one holds a question on paper. Practise: greet the walker-in with a smile and raised hand, ask the caller politely to hold, answer in priority order. Swap roles.
2. **Group discussion (teacher guide — evidence).** In a group, discuss: why does making visitors feel welcome matter? List three questions a receptionist may ask a visitor. Show three pieces of welcoming body language.
