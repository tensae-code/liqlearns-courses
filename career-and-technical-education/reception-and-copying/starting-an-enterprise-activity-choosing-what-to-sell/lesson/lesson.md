# Starting an enterprise activity — choosing what to sell

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: an enterprise activity raises money, group or individual — and choosing the appropriate product or service is the building block of every venture.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — What an enterprise activity is

An **enterprise activity** is an activity that raises money. It can be a **group** activity — like baking cakes or washing cars — or an **individual** one — like making cards.

## Sub lesson 2 — Product selection — the building block

Choosing the appropriate product or service is the **building block of every venture**, because the product is the business's most important and visible first contact with buyers.

Customers' needs change faster than ever, so selection must stay current with what customers ask for and what the market trends are.

## Sub lesson 3 — (teacher guide — evidence) Resources and the debate assessment

The guide frames start-up resources as financial, human, educational, physical and emotional. Its assessment is a five-pupil debate: groups of five choose a product, say how it will be sold, identify the resources needed, select the target market, and choose advertising ways and places — then present to the class.

## Say it to your companion

Say the main points back without looking: an enterprise activity raises money, group or individual — and choosing the appropriate product or service is the building block of every venture. Then name the steps or parts in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Five-pupil product debate (teacher guide — evidence).** In a group of five: choose a product, say how it will be sold, list the resources needed, select the target market, choose advertising ways and places. Present to the class for appreciation and discussion.
2. **First-contact test.** Hold up your chosen product (or its drawing). A partner, seeing it for the first time, says what it is, who it is for, and why they would buy — the product is the first contact, so it must speak clearly.
