# Welcoming guests the Ethiopian way — greetings and the coffee ceremony

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: Ethiopian hospitality: warm greetings and handshakes, guests given priority, hosts not sitting before guests, and the coffee ceremony at the centre.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — How guests are welcomed in Ethiopia

Ethiopians are presented as welcoming, friendly, generous and respectful — and expecting the same in return:

- Warm greetings and handshakes
- Positive body language
- The guest given priority — **hosts do not sit before their guests**
- Asking after family, children, work and life is the proper conversation opening

Guests are asked to read these customs as respect inside a conservative, modest culture of hospitality that varies by ethnic and family background.

## Sub lesson 2 — The coffee ceremony

The **coffee ceremony** is the core cultural custom of gathering. Tea is served if coffee is politely declined.

## Sub lesson 3 — (teacher guide — evidence) Learning it by doing

The guide has pupils pair up to practise the greeting and welcoming procedures, start from the greeting methods of their own village, and observe a traditional coffee ceremony on their way to school for class discussion. The teacher is told to source pictures and videos of traditional welcoming procedures and the coffee ceremony as support materials.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Practise the greeting in pairs (teacher guide — evidence).** Pair up and practise: warm greeting, handshake, guest seated first, then ask after family, work and life. Start from the greeting methods of your own village and compare.
2. **Observe a coffee ceremony.** Watch a traditional coffee ceremony (at home or on the way to school). Note the order of serving, who is served first, and how the pour is done. Report to the class.
