# Copier troubles — paper jams and lines on the copy

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: paper jams come from wrong size or bad loading — remove, reload aligned, clean — and lines on copies come from dirty glass or mirrors, drum, developer or fuser faults.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Paper jams

A **paper jam** is the most common copier error. It comes from wrong paper size or bad loading making the machine pull paper through crooked, plus paper dust clogging the works.

The fix: manually remove the jammed sheet, reload the right size aligned properly, wipe feed tires and vacuum built-up dust.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lines on paper

Lines on copies come from:

- Foreign substances on scanner glass or mirrors
- Drum or developer-unit malfunction
- A drum-blade fault (large vertical lines)
- Fuser problems

## Do It Yourself

1. **Jam drill.** On a switched-off machine or model: find the jammed sheet, draw it out slowly in one steady pull — never torn. Reload the right size, aligned. Say the dust-cleaning steps.
2. **Diagnose the line.** You are given three faulty copies: one with a thick vertical line, one with a thin line, one clean but from dirty glass (line repeats in the same place). Match each to its likely cause from the lesson.
