# Copier troubles — toner, wrinkles, density and spots

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: four more copier faults: toner cartridge problems (replace), wrinkled pages (rollers, trays, humidity, fuser), light/dark copies (reset density), and spots (glass debris vs drum defect).

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Toner and wrinkles

**Toner problems** — low, empty or faulty toner cartridges cause cartridge problems; replacing the cartridge resolves them.

**Wrinkled pages** trace to worn feed/exit rollers, worn trays, humidity moisture, or fuser assemblies.

## Sub lesson 2 — Density and spots

**Too light / too dark** copies are usually disturbed density controls — reset the density levels.

**Spots** are either smudges/debris on the mirror or glass (dots in the **same place** every copy) or a defect in the drum (**random** spots throughout — replace the drum).

## Say it to your companion

Say the main points back without looking: four more copier faults: toner cartridge problems (replace), wrinkled pages (rollers, trays, humidity, fuser), light/dark copies (reset density), and spots (glass debris vs drum defect). Then name the steps or parts in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Fault-matching drill.** Lay out four faulty copies — faded (toner), wrinkled, too dark, spotty in the same place. For each: name the cause and the fix from the lesson, in order.
2. **Density reset drill.** On a copier or its diagram, find the density control and practise returning it to its middle setting — the fix for light/dark copies.
