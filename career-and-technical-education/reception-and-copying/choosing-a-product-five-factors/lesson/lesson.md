# Choosing a product — demand, funding, materials, return, people

## Do it first

Before any explanation, try this in your own words: five factors govern product selection: frequency of demand, adequate funding, raw materials, optimum return on investment, and qualified personnel.

Say what you already think, out loud or on paper. If parts will not come yet, that is exactly what this lesson is for.

## The five factors

1. **Frequency of need / demand** — the most-needed product has the greatest chance of success.
2. **Adequate funding** available for development, production, promotion, marketing and distribution.
3. **Raw materials** — their source, the quality to be achieved and the quantity needed.
4. **Optimum return on investment.**
5. **Qualified personnel** to run production and marketing — keeping manufacturing cost minimal by reducing wastage through competent, skilled manpower.

## Say it to your companion

Say the main points back without looking: five factors govern product selection: frequency of demand, adequate funding, raw materials, optimum return on investment, and qualified personnel. Then name the steps or parts in order.

## Do It Yourself

1. **Score a product on five scales.** Take one product idea. Score it 1–5 on each factor: demand, funding, materials, return, people. A low score names the work to do before starting.
2. **Cut the wastage.** List three places your chosen product could waste materials. For each, name the skilled action that prevents it — competent manpower is factor 5 in action.
