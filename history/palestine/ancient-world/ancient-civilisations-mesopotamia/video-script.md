# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Early humans signal with grunts, gestures, drawings and fire-smoke, and writing arrives with the Sumerian cuneiform the lesson names, before pigeons, telegraph and telephone carry messages further. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
The lesson's own test asks where the Babylonian and Assyrian civilisations rose — in Iraq, the Mesopotamian heartland of early states. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
In Iraq scribes press cuneiform wedges, a second ancient script the lesson sets beside Egypt's pictures. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
Between Tigris and Euphrates the Sumerians raise great cities like Ur, invent the plough and press cuneiform into clay tablets with square-headed pens. Tell it slowly: who is there, where they stand, what they want, and what stops them. The moment those things collide is the turn — and everything after it follows from that turn.
Amorite tribes settle and take Babylon, Chaldean king Nebuchadnezzar rules from it, and the Assyrians move their capital from Ashur to Nineveh under Sennacherib, decorating palace gates with giant winged animals. Watch how one step makes the next one possible. No step here is small: each person, each place and each choice pushes the story toward the turn that gives this part its meaning.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Palestine.
