# Video script — The Medieval World: The Ottomans

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Sulayman, grandfather of Osman, migrates from Turkmenistan to Anatolia; his son Ertugrul wins Seljuk land by fighting the Romans, and Osman inherits and founds the state on Rome's frontier. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
Chaldiran (1514) breaks the Safavids, Marj Dabiq (1516) takes al-Sham from the Mamluks, Ridaniya (1517) makes Egypt an Ottoman province, and the Sharif of Mecca brings the Hijaz in the same year. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
The system is sultan, grand vizier, sipahi and janissary army, defterdar over finance and Shaykh al-Islam over religion; in the late era feudal iltizam, heavy taxes, fleeing peasants and unsafe roads sink the Arab economy. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
Weakness piles up — European colonial penetration, the Committee of Union and Progress's Turkification from 1908, and foreign capitulations; Germany becomes the ally (Berlin–Baghdad and Hijaz railways, with Daraa–Haifa and Jerusalem–Jaffa lines in Palestine), the empire fights the First World War beside the Central Powers, and defeat brings Sèvres, the Paris settlement backing the mandate and Zionism, and the fall of the last caliphate. Tell it slowly: who is there, where they stand, what they want, and what stops them. The moment those things collide is the turn — and everything after it follows from that turn.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Palestine.
