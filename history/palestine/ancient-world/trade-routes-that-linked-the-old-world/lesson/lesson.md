# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Trade Routes That Linked the Old World** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 5 Lesson 2: economic relations between states in ancient times

Quraysh caravans journey to Yemen in winter and to al-Sham in summer, India trades spices with its neighbours, and Arabs and China trade silk across the old world's routes. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: economic life — Arab trade routes

Arabs herd, farm oases and make swords and pottery, and their caravans link Persia, Rome, Abyssinia and India by the eastern and western land roads and the sea road to East Africa, Quraysh running the winter journey to Yemen and the summer journey to al-Sham. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: how Islamic civilisation reached Europe

Europe borrows from the Muslims through the contact points of al-Andalus, Sicily, the Frankish (Crusader) wars, trade and scholarly missions. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 2 Lesson 5: trade and the Arabised coinage

Honest Muslim merchants carry Islam itself to lands no army reached, such as Indonesia and Malaysia, while Abd al-Malik's reform and Harun al-Rashid's mint-master free the coinage from Byzantine and Persian dependence — and coins are struck in Palestine bearing Ramla's name. Tell it slowly: who is there, where they stand, what they want, and what stops them. The moment those things collide is the turn — and everything after it follows from that turn.
