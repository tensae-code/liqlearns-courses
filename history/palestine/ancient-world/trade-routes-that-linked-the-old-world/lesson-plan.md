## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 5 Lesson 2: economic relations between states in ancient times** — Quraysh caravans journey to Yemen in winter and to al-Sham in summer, India trades spices with its neighbours, and Arabs and China trade silk across the old world's routes.
2. **Unit 4 Lesson 3: economic life — Arab trade routes** — Arabs herd, farm oases and make swords and pottery, and their caravans link Persia, Rome, Abyssinia and India by the eastern and western land roads and the sea road to East Africa, Quraysh running the winter journey to Yemen and the summer journey to al-Sham.
3. **Unit 2 Lesson 4: how Islamic civilisation reached Europe** — Europe borrows from the Muslims through the contact points of al-Andalus, Sicily, the Frankish (Crusader) wars, trade and scholarly missions.
4. **Unit 2 Lesson 5: trade and the Arabised coinage** — Honest Muslim merchants carry Islam itself to lands no army reached, such as Indonesia and Malaysia, while Abd al-Malik's reform and Harun al-Rashid's mint-master free the coinage from Byzantine and Persian dependence — and coins are struck in Palestine bearing Ramla's name.
