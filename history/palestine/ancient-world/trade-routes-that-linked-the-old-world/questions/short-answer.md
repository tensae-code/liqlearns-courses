# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Unit 5 Lesson 2: economic relations between states in ancient times”?
**Answer:** Quraysh caravans journey to Yemen in winter and to al-Sham in summer, India trades spices with its neighbours, and Arabs and China trade silk across the old world's routes. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Unit 4 Lesson 3: economic life — Arab trade routes” end?
**Answer:** Arabs herd, farm oases and make swords and pottery, and their caravans link Persia, Rome, Abyssinia and India by the eastern and western land roads and the sea road to East Africa, Quraysh running the winter journey to Yemen and the summer journey to al-Sham. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
