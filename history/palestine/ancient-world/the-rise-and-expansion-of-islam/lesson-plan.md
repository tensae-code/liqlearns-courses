## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 5 Lesson 1: Islam appears in Mecca** — Muhammad receives the revelation in Cave Hira at forty; the secret call gathers Khadijah, Ali, Abu Bakr, Zayd and Bilal, the public call brings Quraysh's torture — Sumayya becomes Islam's first martyr — and the believers flee to the just Negus in Abyssinia.
2. **Unit 5 Lesson 1: boycott, Taif, Isra and Mi'raj, and the Hijra** — Quraysh besiege Banu Hashim in Shi'b Abi Talib for three years, Taif stones the Prophet, the night journey carries him to al-Aqsa in Jerusalem, the Aqaba pledges open Yathrib, and the Hijra succeeds by planning — Ali in the bed, Abu Bakr beside him, Cave Thawr, a twisting road.
3. **Unit 5 Lesson 2: the Islamic state is founded in Madinah** — The Prophet builds Quba and the Prophet's Mosque, pairs Muhajirun with Ansar as brothers, and issues the Madinah Charter — read in class as a source text — binding Muslims and Jews into one community, Islam's first constitution.
4. **Unit 5 Lesson 2: Badr, Uhud and the Trench** — At Badr (2 AH) the Muslims cut Quraysh's Syria trade and win; at Uhud (3 AH) the archers' disobedience turns victory; at the Trench (5 AH) Salman the Persian's ditch saves Madinah from the confederate siege.
5. **Unit 5 Lesson 2: Hudaybiyyah and the opening of Mecca** — Quraysh stop the pilgrims and sign the Hudaybiyyah truce, break it by aiding Bakr against Khuza'a, and in 8 AH the Prophet enters Mecca with 10,000 men without a battle, smashes the idols and pardons its people.
6. **Unit 5 Lesson 3: the Rashidun Caliphate** — The Prophet dies on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH (632 CE); Abu Bakr, chosen at Saqifa by shura, steadies the community, and the four caliphs — Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali — follow by consultation.
7. **Unit 5 Lesson 3: the Ridda wars and Rashidun achievements** — Abu Bakr's campaigns of 11–12 AH reunite Arabia after the apostasy wars; the era collects the Qur'an under Zayd ibn Thabit, creates the diwans of army and kharaj, builds a navy under Uthman and police under Ali, and founds Basra, Kufa and Fustat.
8. **Unit 5 Lesson 4: the state's relations with its neighbours — letters and Mu'tah** — The Prophet's letter to Heraclius is read as a source; at Mu'tah in southern Jordan the three commanders Zayd, Ja'far and Abdullah fall in turn and Khalid ibn al-Walid saves the army by a planned withdrawal, and Tabuk faces down the Byzantine threat.
9. **Unit 5 Lesson 4: the conquests begin** — Abu Bakr insists on sending Usama's army, which reaches Palestine; Khalid takes al-Hira on the Euphrates, and four armies march on al-Sham — Yazid to Damascus, Shurahbil to Jordan, Abu Ubayda to Homs, Amr ibn al-As to Palestine.
10. **Unit 5 Lesson 5: decisive battles — Yarmouk and al-Qadisiyya** — At Yarmouk Khalid organises the army into centre, wings and vanguard and routs the chained Byzantine infantry; at al-Qadisiyya (15 AH) Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas's envoys offer Islam to Khosrow and Rustam, are refused, and Persia falls in days of battle.
11. **Unit 5 Lesson 5: the Battle of the Masts (Dhat al-Sawari)** — With coasts to defend, Muawiya and Abdullah ibn Sa'd build a fleet under Uthman and win Islam's first naval victory over the Byzantine fleet, ending Rome's hope of retaking Alexandria.
12. **Unit 5 Lesson 6: Muslim ethics in peace and war** — Badr's seventy prisoners are ransomed, and those who can read win freedom by teaching ten Muslims; Abu Bakr's charge to Usama's army — no killing of children, women, the old, no treachery — is set line by line beside Article 3 of the Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949.
13. **Unit 1 Lesson 1: Asia and the Islamic state's spread in it** — The conquest map is read in three colours — Rashidun, Umayyad, Abbasid — and under the Abbasids expansion slows to holding the thughur frontier towns and striking back, as when al-Mu'tasim takes Amorium and Tarsus is fortified.
14. **Unit 1 Lesson 2: Africa and the Islamic state's spread — the Maghreb** — Opening the Maghreb takes some eighty years against fierce Berber resistance and hard country, and it is the Muslims' tolerant policy that finally wins the Berbers into Islam and the Arab-Islamic civilisation.
15. **Unit 1 Lesson 3: Europe — the conquest of al-Andalus** — Tariq ibn Ziyad defeats the Spanish at Wadi Lakka in 92 AH / 711 CE, Musa ibn Nusayr follows by another road, and between them they open Cordoba, Seville, Merida, Toledo and Zaragoza, meeting in the north near Frankish lands.
16. **Unit 1 Lesson 3: Abd al-Rahman al-Dakhil and Umayyad al-Andalus** — The fugitive Umayyad prince Abd al-Rahman "the Falcon of Quraysh" escapes the Abbasid takeover, seizes al-Andalus in 138 AH / 756 CE and founds a hereditary Umayyad state that makes the land a beacon of learning.
17. **Unit 2 Lesson 1: administrative achievements — the heir-apparent institution** — Before his death in 60 AH Muawiya names his son Yazid heir, and succession turns from shura consultation into heredity, the pattern of all later Islamic ages.
18. **Unit 2 Lesson 1: diwans, judiciary and army** — Umar, worn out dividing Bahrain's money by hand, takes the Syrian kings' example and founds the diwan; Abd al-Malik organises the judiciary and conscription, Persian and Turkish soldiers enter the Abbasid army, and long Mediterranean coasts force the building of the first Islamic fleet.
19. **Unit 2 Lesson 2: architectural achievements** — Islamic building begins with mosques (Quba first) and grows into schools, palaces like Jordan's Qasr Amra and cities like Uqba ibn Nafi's Kairouan, its style marked by geometric and plant ornament and calligraphy instead of living figures.
20. **Unit 2 Lesson 4: scientific achievements — translation and the sciences** — Translators like Ibn al-Muqaffa' (Kalila wa Dimna) carry the world's books into Arabic; Muslims take the zero and numerals from India, perfect the astrolabe and astronomy for prayer times and qibla, and Baghdad's Bayt al-Hikma anchors the movement.
21. **Unit 2 Lesson 5: economic achievements — revenues and the treasury** — The Bayt al-Mal gathers kharaj, the fifth of spoils, fay', jizya from protected non-Muslims, zakat and the ushr tithe, and spends them on the state's people.
22. **Unit 4 Lesson 2: the Franks and their invasions of the Islamic East** — Muslim historians name Europe's invaders "al-Faranja"; driven by Europe's poverty, hunger for eastern trade, feudal ambition and church preaching, eight campaigns roll from 1096 to 1270 against al-Sham, Constantinople, Egypt and Tunis in the lesson's own table.
23. **Unit 4 Lesson 3: decisive battles on Palestine's land — Saladin prepares** — Saladin founds the Ayyubid state in Egypt, then spends over ten years patiently reuniting Egypt, al-Sham and Mosul, split since Nur al-Din Zengi's death, fortifying both lands before daring the Franks.
24. **Unit 4 Lesson 3: the Battle of Hattin (1187)** — Reynald of Kerak seizes a Muslim caravan and refuses its return, Saladin declares jihad, strikes Tiberias to draw the Franks out of watered Sephorie, and ambushes their thirst-marched army — "what we wanted has come to us".
25. **Unit 4 Lesson 3: the liberation of Jerusalem** — With the coastal cities freed, the Muslims besiege Jerusalem from the north by the Damascus Gate, the Franks surrender the city, and Saladin founds the Salahiyya school, khanqah and hospital, restores Muslim families and treats the Christians by Islamic ethics.
