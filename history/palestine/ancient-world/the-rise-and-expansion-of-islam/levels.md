# The Rise and Expansion of Islam — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 5 Lesson 1: Islam appears in Mecca
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Muhammad receives the revelation in Cave Hira at forty; the secret call gathers Khadijah, Ali, Abu Bakr, Zayd and Bilal, the public call brings Quraysh's torture — Sumayya becomes Islam's first martyr — and the believers flee to the just Negus in Abyssinia. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 5 Lesson 1: boycott, Taif, Isra and Mi'raj, and the Hijra
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Quraysh besiege Banu Hashim in Shi'b Abi Talib for three years, Taif stones the Prophet, the night journey carries him to al-Aqsa in Jerusalem, the Aqaba pledges open Yathrib, and the Hijra succeeds by planning — Ali in the bed, Abu Bakr beside him, Cave Thawr, a twisting road. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 5 Lesson 2: the Islamic state is founded in Madinah
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Prophet builds Quba and the Prophet's Mosque, pairs Muhajirun with Ansar as brothers, and issues the Madinah Charter — read in class as a source text — binding Muslims and Jews into one community, Islam's first constitution. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 5 Lesson 2: Badr, Uhud and the Trench
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: At Badr (2 AH) the Muslims cut Quraysh's Syria trade and win; at Uhud (3 AH) the archers' disobedience turns victory; at the Trench (5 AH) Salman the Persian's ditch saves Madinah from the confederate siege. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 5 Lesson 2: Hudaybiyyah and the opening of Mecca
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Quraysh stop the pilgrims and sign the Hudaybiyyah truce, break it by aiding Bakr against Khuza'a, and in 8 AH the Prophet enters Mecca with 10,000 men without a battle, smashes the idols and pardons its people. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 5 Lesson 3: the Rashidun Caliphate
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Prophet dies on 12 Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH (632 CE); Abu Bakr, chosen at Saqifa by shura, steadies the community, and the four caliphs — Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali — follow by consultation. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 5 Lesson 3: the Ridda wars and Rashidun achievements
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Abu Bakr's campaigns of 11–12 AH reunite Arabia after the apostasy wars; the era collects the Qur'an under Zayd ibn Thabit, creates the diwans of army and kharaj, builds a navy under Uthman and police under Ali, and founds Basra, Kufa and Fustat. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 5 Lesson 4: the state's relations with its neighbours — letters and Mu'tah
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Prophet's letter to Heraclius is read as a source; at Mu'tah in southern Jordan the three commanders Zayd, Ja'far and Abdullah fall in turn and Khalid ibn al-Walid saves the army by a planned withdrawal, and Tabuk faces down the Byzantine threat. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit 5 Lesson 4: the conquests begin
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Abu Bakr insists on sending Usama's army, which reaches Palestine; Khalid takes al-Hira on the Euphrates, and four armies march on al-Sham — Yazid to Damascus, Shurahbil to Jordan, Abu Ubayda to Homs, Amr ibn al-As to Palestine. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Unit 5 Lesson 5: decisive battles — Yarmouk and al-Qadisiyya
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: At Yarmouk Khalid organises the army into centre, wings and vanguard and routs the chained Byzantine infantry; at al-Qadisiyya (15 AH) Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas's envoys offer Islam to Khosrow and Rustam, are refused, and Persia falls in days of battle. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 11 — Unit 5 Lesson 5: the Battle of the Masts (Dhat al-Sawari)
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: With coasts to defend, Muawiya and Abdullah ibn Sa'd build a fleet under Uthman and win Islam's first naval victory over the Byzantine fleet, ending Rome's hope of retaking Alexandria. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 12 — Unit 5 Lesson 6: Muslim ethics in peace and war
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Badr's seventy prisoners are ransomed, and those who can read win freedom by teaching ten Muslims; Abu Bakr's charge to Usama's army — no killing of children, women, the old, no treachery — is set line by line beside Article 3 of the Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 13 — Unit 1 Lesson 1: Asia and the Islamic state's spread in it
Sub lesson 13 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The conquest map is read in three colours — Rashidun, Umayyad, Abbasid — and under the Abbasids expansion slows to holding the thughur frontier towns and striking back, as when al-Mu'tasim takes Amorium and Tarsus is fortified. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 14 — Unit 1 Lesson 2: Africa and the Islamic state's spread — the Maghreb
Sub lesson 14 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Opening the Maghreb takes some eighty years against fierce Berber resistance and hard country, and it is the Muslims' tolerant policy that finally wins the Berbers into Islam and the Arab-Islamic civilisation. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 15 — Unit 1 Lesson 3: Europe — the conquest of al-Andalus
Sub lesson 15 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tariq ibn Ziyad defeats the Spanish at Wadi Lakka in 92 AH / 711 CE, Musa ibn Nusayr follows by another road, and between them they open Cordoba, Seville, Merida, Toledo and Zaragoza, meeting in the north near Frankish lands. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 16 — Unit 1 Lesson 3: Abd al-Rahman al-Dakhil and Umayyad al-Andalus
Sub lesson 16 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The fugitive Umayyad prince Abd al-Rahman "the Falcon of Quraysh" escapes the Abbasid takeover, seizes al-Andalus in 138 AH / 756 CE and founds a hereditary Umayyad state that makes the land a beacon of learning. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 17 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: administrative achievements — the heir-apparent institution
Sub lesson 17 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Before his death in 60 AH Muawiya names his son Yazid heir, and succession turns from shura consultation into heredity, the pattern of all later Islamic ages. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 18 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: diwans, judiciary and army
Sub lesson 18 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Umar, worn out dividing Bahrain's money by hand, takes the Syrian kings' example and founds the diwan; Abd al-Malik organises the judiciary and conscription, Persian and Turkish soldiers enter the Abbasid army, and long Mediterranean coasts force the building of the first Islamic fleet. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 19 — Unit 2 Lesson 2: architectural achievements
Sub lesson 19 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Islamic building begins with mosques (Quba first) and grows into schools, palaces like Jordan's Qasr Amra and cities like Uqba ibn Nafi's Kairouan, its style marked by geometric and plant ornament and calligraphy instead of living figures. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 20 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: scientific achievements — translation and the sciences
Sub lesson 20 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Translators like Ibn al-Muqaffa' (Kalila wa Dimna) carry the world's books into Arabic; Muslims take the zero and numerals from India, perfect the astrolabe and astronomy for prayer times and qibla, and Baghdad's Bayt al-Hikma anchors the movement. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 21 — Unit 2 Lesson 5: economic achievements — revenues and the treasury
Sub lesson 21 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Bayt al-Mal gathers kharaj, the fifth of spoils, fay', jizya from protected non-Muslims, zakat and the ushr tithe, and spends them on the state's people. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 22 — Unit 4 Lesson 2: the Franks and their invasions of the Islamic East
Sub lesson 22 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Muslim historians name Europe's invaders "al-Faranja"; driven by Europe's poverty, hunger for eastern trade, feudal ambition and church preaching, eight campaigns roll from 1096 to 1270 against al-Sham, Constantinople, Egypt and Tunis in the lesson's own table. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 23 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: decisive battles on Palestine's land — Saladin prepares
Sub lesson 23 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Saladin founds the Ayyubid state in Egypt, then spends over ten years patiently reuniting Egypt, al-Sham and Mosul, split since Nur al-Din Zengi's death, fortifying both lands before daring the Franks. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 24 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: the Battle of Hattin (1187)
Sub lesson 24 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Reynald of Kerak seizes a Muslim caravan and refuses its return, Saladin declares jihad, strikes Tiberias to draw the Franks out of watered Sephorie, and ambushes their thirst-marched army — "what we wanted has come to us". — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 25 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: the liberation of Jerusalem
Sub lesson 25 questions from the type files. Pass mark: With the coastal cities freed, the Muslims besiege Jerusalem from the north by the Damascus Gate, the Franks surrender the city, and Saladin founds the Salahiyya school, khanqah and hospital, restores Muslim families and treats the Christians by Islamic ethics. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
