# Arabia before Islam: Trade and Tribes — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Unit 4 Lesson 1: religious and social life of the Arabs before Islam”?
**Answer:** In the Jahiliyya Arabs worship stones, trees, sun and moon beside the Kaaba's idols; the family and then the tribe rule life, women range from Queen Zenobia to daughters buried alive (wa'd), and Bedouin tent-dwellers live hard against settled townsfolk of Yemen, al-Sham, Iraq and the Hijaz. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Unit 4 Lesson 2: political life before Islam — kingdoms and empires” end?
**Answer:** Hereditary kingdoms (Ma'in, Saba and Himyar in Yemen, the Munadhira in Iraq, Nabataeans, Ghassanids and Palmyra in al-Sham) stand beside tribal sheikhdoms, while Persia and Rome occupy most Arab lands and the Hijaz cities — Mecca under Qusay's Quraysh, feuding Yathrib, Taif — keep their independence. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
