# History — Palestine course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Palestine inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Palestine's books in hand are the twelve Ministry of Education *Social Studies* student books for Grades 5–9 (Parts 1 and 2 each year) and the two Grade 10 *Geography of Palestine and its Modern and Contemporary History* student books — all in Arabic, all COMPLETE as filed. Together they tell one continuous story that keeps returning home: history begins with the first humans and is rooted at once in Palestine (Natufian caves, Jericho, Canaanite cities), walks the ancient civilisations around the Arab homeland, builds Islam's state lesson by lesson until Jerusalem opens to Umar, diagnoses decline and the Frankish and Mongol storms through battles fought on Palestinian soil, judges the Ottoman centuries from Palestine itself, teaches the modern world as the run-up to Palestine's own colonisation, and closes with Palestine's own timeline carried year by year to the Palestinian Authority. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder below follows the story as Palestine's own books present it, in its own eras. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Palestine's own Ministry of Education student books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps and the First Humans** — the historian's craft first — what history is, how the past is dated, and how written evidence rebuilds it — then the deep past itself: the first humans, stone tools, fire, farming, villages and the first cities, rooted at home in Natufian caves and Jericho — and heritage itself: the embroidered dress, the old sites and the living customs a child must learn to keep — 5 lessons.

2. **The Ancient World around Palestine** — the ancient civilisations region by region around the Arab homeland — Mesopotamia between the two rivers, Pharaonic Egypt and what Egypt gave, China's Great Wall, the trade routes that linked the old world — and at home the Canaanites of Palestine: their cities, their achievements and the first alphabet they gave the world — then Yemen's kingdoms, Rome at Palmyra, Carthage, and Arabia whole before Islam: its tribes, kingdoms, trade and fairs — 11 lessons.

3. **Islam Rises, Expands — and Palestine Opens to It** — Islam's story built step by step — the call in Mecca, the Hijra, the state founded in Madinah, the battles, the Rashidun caliphs and the conquests — landing where Palestine's books land it: Jerusalem opening to Caliph Umar and the Umariyya Covenant, Umayyad Palestine with Ramla founded and the Dome of the Rock raised, the Islamic state's spread across Asia, Africa and al-Andalus, its diwans, buildings, sciences and coinage — and how Muslims themselves wrote this history down — 4 lessons.

4. **Storms and Empires: Decline to the Ottoman Centuries** — decline taught as diagnosis — the Abbasid state hollowing out and breaking into separate states, the Frankish invasions answered at Hattin, Jerusalem and Ain Jalut, the Mongol storm and Baghdad's fall, the Mamluks defending Egypt and al-Sham, and al-Andalus splitting into taifa kingdoms until Granada falls — then a divided Arab map invites the Ottoman takeover, and the Ottoman centuries are judged from Palestine itself: its sanjaks, Zahir al-Umar and al-Jazzar breaking away, Suleiman's Jerusalem works, the Tanzimat and foreign influence creeping in — 6 lessons.

5. **The Modern World Arrives: Renaissance to Colonialism** — the wider world as the run-up to Palestine's own colonisation — the Renaissance fed in part by Arab-Islamic civilisation, the sea discoveries draining the old ports, steam and the factory splitting society into classes, the thirteen colonies and their revolution, France's revolution and Napoleon's march through Egypt to Acre — then colonialism as a system with named policies (partition, economic subordination, deliberate ignorance, repression), the race for colonies, the liberation movements that answer it, and the United Nations' postwar order that votes Palestine's partition in 1947 — 11 lessons.

6. **Palestine's Own Modern Story: Villages, Nakba and the Authority** — Palestine's own timeline carried whole — the village story running unbroken to 1948, when over 500 villages were destroyed and their people made refugees; settlement from Montefiore in 1856 through Basel, Balfour, the Mandate and the Great Revolt to the Nakba; then the years after: the All-Palestine Government, division, 1956, the PLO founded in Jerusalem, the 1967 Naksa, Karameh, settlement after 1967, October 1973, Lebanon, the two intifadas, Oslo and the Palestinian Authority — with the Islamic world answering the 1969 al-Aqsa arson by founding the OIC — 6 lessons.

**Totals: 43 lessons** (Level 1: 5 · Level 2: 11 · Level 3: 4 · Level 4: 6 · Level 5: 11 · Level 6: 6 — 135 placements stocked; all 43 lessons stand on student books, so every lesson is taught in 1 country (Palestine); 22 lessons carry sub lessons).
