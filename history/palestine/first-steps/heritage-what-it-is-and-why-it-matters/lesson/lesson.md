# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 3: heritage dress of peoples

Every community's traditional dress carries its identity, and Palestine's embroidered thobe for women and hatta, iqal and qumbaz for men are presented as living heritage to protect. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1 Lesson 2: threats to Palestine's antiquities and protecting them

Palestine's antiquities are looted and its history forged to erase Palestinian presence, so pupils must name historic sites near home and propose ways to protect them. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2 Lesson 3: dangers facing Islamic antiquities in Jerusalem

Since the gangs seized most of Palestine in 1948 and Jerusalem in 1967, a policy of erasing Arab-Islamic landmarks runs against Security Council Resolution 252 (1968), and even the al-Buraq Wall is renamed — heritage defence as a present duty. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 5 Lesson 4: protecting the human cultural heritage

Heritage faces natural dangers (climate cracking and eroding old buildings) and human ones — war, the excavations around al-Aqsa and the closure of Jerusalem's Orient House; the 1954 Hague Convention (Article 4) bans stealing or destroying cultural property, and after Palestine's full UNESCO membership on 13 October 2011 its sites, Jerusalem's Old Town first, enter the World Heritage list. Tell it slowly: who is there, where they stand, what they want, and what stops them. The moment those things collide is the turn — and everything after it follows from that turn.
