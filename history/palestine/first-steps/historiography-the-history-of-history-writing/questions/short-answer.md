# Historiography: The History of History Writing — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** What happens in “Unit 2 Lesson 4: Muslims write history and geography”?
**Answer:** Muslims begin by writing the Prophet's sira and campaigns, then produce al-Tabari's History of Nations and Kings, al-Ya'qubi's history, Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan and al-Idrisi's world map — and Abd al-Hamid al-Katib's letter is read as a founding text of Arabic prose. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** How does the story in “Unit 2 Lesson 4: Muslims write history and geography” end?
**Answer:** Muslims begin by writing the Prophet's sira and campaigns, then produce al-Tabari's History of Nations and Kings, al-Ya'qubi's history, Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan and al-Idrisi's world map — and Abd al-Hamid al-Katib's letter is read as a founding text of Arabic prose. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
