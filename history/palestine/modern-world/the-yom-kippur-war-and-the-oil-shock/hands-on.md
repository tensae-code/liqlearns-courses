# Hands-on — The Yom Kippur War and the Oil Shock

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Yom Kippur War and the Oil Shock — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: After the War of Attrition and the rejected Rogers Plan (1970), Egypt crosses the Suez Canal and storms the Bar Lev line while Syria advances into the Golan toward Mount Hermon and the Hula; Arab states cut oil production and embargo the United States, the Palestine Liberation Army fights on the Egyptian front, and Resolution 338 follows. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
