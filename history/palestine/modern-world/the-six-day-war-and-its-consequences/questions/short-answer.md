# The Six-Day War and Its Consequences — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** What happens in “Unit 5 Lesson 1: the June 1967 War (the Naksa)”?
**Answer:** After the "water war" over the Jordan diversion (1964–65), a surprise air strike on 5 June 1967 destroys the Egyptian, Jordanian and Syrian air forces on the ground; in six days the Golan, the West Bank, Gaza and Sinai are occupied, the Security Council answers with Resolution 242, and the occupation's first orders (the military governor's proclamation of 7 June 1967) subordinate the economy and demolish the Latrun villages Imwas, Yalu and Beit Nuba — Canada Park rises on Imwas's land. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** How does the story in “Unit 5 Lesson 1: the June 1967 War (the Naksa)” end?
**Answer:** After the "water war" over the Jordan diversion (1964–65), a surprise air strike on 5 June 1967 destroys the Egyptian, Jordanian and Syrian air forces on the ground; in six days the Golan, the West Bank, Gaza and Sinai are occupied, the Security Council answers with Resolution 242, and the occupation's first orders (the military governor's proclamation of 7 June 1967) subordinate the economy and demolish the Latrun villages Imwas, Yalu and Beit Nuba — Canada Park rises on Imwas's land. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
