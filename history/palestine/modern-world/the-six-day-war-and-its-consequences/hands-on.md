# Hands-on — The Six-Day War and Its Consequences

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Six-Day War and Its Consequences — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: After the "water war" over the Jordan diversion (1964–65), a surprise air strike on 5 June 1967 destroys the Egyptian, Jordanian and Syrian air forces on the ground; in six days the Golan, the West Bank, Gaza and Sinai are occupied, the Security Council answers with Resolution 242, and the occupation's first orders (the military governor's proclamation of 7 June 1967) subordinate the economy and demolish the Latrun villages Imwas, Yalu and Beit Nuba — Canada Park rises on Imwas's land. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
