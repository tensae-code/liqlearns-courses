# The Renaissance and Its Aftermath — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the heart of the story in “Unit 4 Lesson 1: the European Renaissance”?
a) Against a church-bound Middle Ages of superstition, free thought, humanism and art flower (Florence, da Vinci's Mona Lisa); among its causes the book counts the Crusades' contact with Islamic civilisation, Constantinople's fall in 1453 sending scholars west, and printing.  b) The past is rebuilt from written sources — scripture, histories like Ibn al-Athir's al-Kamil, official documents, land deeds (kawashin), travel books and personal memoirs.  c) Egyptians invent coinage that spreads worldwide, make paper from papyrus, and embalm their dead because they believe in life after death.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Against a church-bound Middle Ages of superstition, free thought, humanism and art flower (Florence, da Vinci's Mona Lisa); among its causes the book counts the Crusades' contact with Islamic civilisation, Constantinople's fall in 1453 sending scholars west, and printing.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What is the heart of the story in “Unit 4 Lesson 1: Arab-Islamic civilisation feeds the Renaissance”?
a) From al-Andalus, European students carry Muslim learning into France and, through Sicily, into southern Italy; Crusades and trade carry more from Egypt, al-Sham and Iraq — al-Farabi's philosophy, Ibn Rushd, and Arabic medical books among the cargo.  b) About 4,000 years ago the Canaanites of Palestine invent alphabetic writing, borrowing signs from their own world — the ox-head for the first letter — and the alphabet travels from them to the whole world.  c) An empire stretched from India's edge to the Atlantic outgrows its caliphs' grip; palace luxury, army interference, sectarian revolts and separatist governors hollow it out from the second Abbasid era, provinces slip into nominal loyalty, and al-Andalus is the first region to break away.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) From al-Andalus, European students carry Muslim learning into France and, through Sicily, into southern Italy; Crusades and trade carry more from Egypt, al-Sham and Iraq — al-Farabi's philosophy, Ibn Rushd, and Arabic medical books among the cargo.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
