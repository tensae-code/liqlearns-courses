# The Renaissance and Its Aftermath — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Unit 4 Lesson 1: the European Renaissance”?
**Answer:** Against a church-bound Middle Ages of superstition, free thought, humanism and art flower (Florence, da Vinci's Mona Lisa); among its causes the book counts the Crusades' contact with Islamic civilisation, Constantinople's fall in 1453 sending scholars west, and printing. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Unit 4 Lesson 1: Arab-Islamic civilisation feeds the Renaissance” end?
**Answer:** From al-Andalus, European students carry Muslim learning into France and, through Sicily, into southern Italy; Crusades and trade carry more from Egypt, al-Sham and Iraq — al-Farabi's philosophy, Ibn Rushd, and Arabic medical books among the cargo. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
