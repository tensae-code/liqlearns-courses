# Palestine after 1948: Nakba to the Palestinian Authority — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 4 Lesson 2: the All-Palestine Government
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: In 1948 Palestinians proclaim the All-Palestine Government in Gaza under Haj Amin al-Husseini — with its own flag and passports — refusing a mere civil administration, until international pressure aborts it. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: Palestine divided after 1948
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Inside the new state, laws do the demographic work — army land confiscation (1949), the Absentees' Property Law and the Law of Return (both 1950); the Jericho Conference (1 December 1948) resolves to join the West Bank to Jordan, and Gaza (365 km²) passes under Egyptian administration. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 4 Lesson 4: Palestine in the 1956 Tripartite Aggression
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Gaza is occupied during the attack on Egypt and pays in massacres — the lesson tabulates the martyrs by town (Gaza City 256, Khan Yunis and its surroundings 415, Rafah 197 among them) — and on the aggression's eve, 29 October 1956, the Kafr Qasim massacre is committed inside the state. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 4 Lesson 5: the PLO and the contemporary revolution
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The first Palestinian National Council meets in Jerusalem on 28 May 1964 with King Hussein present and founds the PLO under Ahmad Shuqairi; Fatah launches on 1 January 1965 with the Eilabun operation, Yahya Hammuda holds the chair in between, and Yasser Arafat takes the leadership in 1969. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 5 Lesson 2: the Battle of Karameh 1968
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: On the morning of Thursday 21 March 1968 the Palestinian resistance and the Jordanian army together repel the assault on Karameh; Arafat calls it the turning point between despair and hope, and the 1969 Cairo National Council rejects all settlement projects at odds with Palestinian rights and elects him PLO chairman. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 5 Lesson 3: settlement colonisation after 1967
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The old legal tools are reused — absentee-property and "state land" declarations — settlements climb the hilltops under plans like Allon's, the wall (begun 2002) follows, and the lesson sets the law against it: Geneva IV Article 49(6), The Hague 1907, and the International Court of Justice's 2004 advisory opinion. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 6 Lesson 1: the Palestinian revolution 1970–1976
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Driven from Jordan in 1971, the PLO rebuilds in Lebanon; Israel assassinates Kamal Nasser, Kamal Adwan and Abu Yusuf al-Najjar in Beirut in 1973, and the National Council's twelfth session (Cairo, 1974) adopts the Ten-Point phased programme — an independent national authority on any liberated part. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 6 Lesson 2: Palestine in the 1978 Egyptian–Israeli peace
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sadat's 1977 Jerusalem speech and the Camp David framework leave the West Bank and Gaza to staged negotiations; the Arab world erupts in rejection and the Steadfastness Front forms at the 1977 Tripoli summit — Syria, Libya, Algeria, Yemen and the PLO. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit 6 Lesson 3: the invasions of Lebanon 1978–1982
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: In 1982 the invasion reaches Beirut — the stand at Beaufort Castle, then an 80-day siege of West Beirut with power, food, medicine and fuel cut — until the revolution's fighters evacuate the city. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Unit 6 Lesson 4: the 1987 Intifada
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: On 8 December 1987 an Israeli truck kills four workers from Jabalia; the funerals turn to stone-throwing and a six-year popular uprising begins, women at its heart, while Israel assassinates its leaders abroad — Abu Jihad in Tunis (1988), Abu Iyad and Abu al-Hol (1991), Fathi Shaqaqi in Malta (1995) — and the 1988 Declaration of Independence proclaims the democratic state. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 11 — Unit 6 Lesson 5: the peace process and the Palestinian Authority
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: At Madrid (1991) Palestinians sit inside the Jordanian delegation; Oslo (1993) brings mutual recognition and an interim self-government of at most five years; the army withdraws from Gaza and Jericho in 1994, Arafat enters Gaza on 1 July 1994 as president of the new Authority, Oslo II is signed at Taba (28 September 1995), and a Legislative Council is elected in 1996. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 12 — Unit 6 Lesson 6: the al-Aqsa Intifada (2000) and after
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sharon's entry into the al-Aqsa courtyards sparks the second intifada, more militarised than the first, answered with bulldozed farmland and olive trees, closures and the annexation wall; after Arafat's death come the 2005 presidential elections, the unilateral Gaza disengagement evacuating the settlements (2005), full UNESCO membership (31 October 2011) and UN observer-state status. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
