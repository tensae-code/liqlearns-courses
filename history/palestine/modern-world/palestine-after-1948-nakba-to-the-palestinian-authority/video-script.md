# Video script — Palestine after 1948: Nakba to the Palestinian Authority

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

In 1948 Palestinians proclaim the All-Palestine Government in Gaza under Haj Amin al-Husseini — with its own flag and passports — refusing a mere civil administration, until international pressure aborts it. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
Inside the new state, laws do the demographic work — army land confiscation (1949), the Absentees' Property Law and the Law of Return (both 1950); the Jericho Conference (1 December 1948) resolves to join the West Bank to Jordan, and Gaza (365 km²) passes under Egyptian administration. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
Gaza is occupied during the attack on Egypt and pays in massacres — the lesson tabulates the martyrs by town (Gaza City 256, Khan Yunis and its surroundings 415, Rafah 197 among them) — and on the aggression's eve, 29 October 1956, the Kafr Qasim massacre is committed inside the state. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
The first Palestinian National Council meets in Jerusalem on 28 May 1964 with King Hussein present and founds the PLO under Ahmad Shuqairi; Fatah launches on 1 January 1965 with the Eilabun operation, Yahya Hammuda holds the chair in between, and Yasser Arafat takes the leadership in 1969. Tell it slowly: who is there, where they stand, what they want, and what stops them. The moment those things collide is the turn — and everything after it follows from that turn.
On the morning of Thursday 21 March 1968 the Palestinian resistance and the Jordanian army together repel the assault on Karameh; Arafat calls it the turning point between despair and hope, and the 1969 Cairo National Council rejects all settlement projects at odds with Palestinian rights and elects him PLO chairman. In 1982 the invasion reaches Beirut — the stand at Beaufort Castle, then an 80-day siege of West Beirut with power, food, medicine and fuel cut — until the revolution's fighters evacuate the city.
