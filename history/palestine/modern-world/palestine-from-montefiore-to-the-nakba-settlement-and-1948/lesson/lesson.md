## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1 Lesson 1: partition inside Palestine

The Zionist movement plants its first settlement, Montefiore, south-west of Jerusalem's wall in 1856, and the settlements double as training camps and arms stores; after the 1948 Nakba the occupation holds over 78% of Palestine. ## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1 Lesson 4: the laws that keep refugees out

The Absentees' Property Law hands the state the lands of Palestinians driven out in 1948, and the "present absentee" rule bars villagers like those of Ein Hawd — who hid in the Carmel forest from the Haganah and still live nearby — from ever returning to their own village. ## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 6 Lesson 2: racial discrimination — the 1948 catastrophe

The lesson names 1948 the great racist catastrophe: armed Zionist gangs occupy 77% of Palestine, commit massacres, destroy 531 Palestinian villages and drive about a million Palestinians into refuge in what remains of Palestine and in Jordan, Syria, Lebanon and the wider diaspora; the rest of Palestine is occupied in June 1967. ## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Zionist immigration and settlement colonisation in the late Ottoman era

The machinery is built — Jewish Colonial Trust (1899), Histadrut, the kibbutz — and the first settlements rise: a Jewish quarter outside Jerusalem's walls (1857), Petah Tikva on the lands of Mlabbis (1882), Zikhron Ya'akov on Zamarin; land is got by purchase and forged deeds through corrupt officials and consulates, the empire answers with the 1883 waqf law and the 1887 "red passport" three-month rule, and Palestinians answer in print — Ruhi al-Khalidi's History of Zionism and the papers al-Karmil and al-Manar. ## Sub lesson 10 — Unit 3 Lesson 3: Palestinian resistance to the mandate

Jaffa's Manshiyya quarter repels the attack of 1 May 1921 (Britain answers with the Palin inquiry), the 1929 al-Buraq revolt sends Jamjoum, Hijazi and al-Zir to the gallows in 1930, parties form (the Arab Party, National Defence), and Sheikh Izz al-Din al-Qassam — Syrian-born in 1871, a veteran of the fight against France — launches his 1935 revolt and is martyred in the Ya'bad woods near Jenin. ## Sub lesson 11 — Unit 3 Lesson 4: the Great Palestinian Revolt 1936–1939

Killings in Jaffa spark a strike that becomes a six-month general strike paralysing the country; national committees form the Arab Higher Committee under Haj Amin al-Husseini; Britain's Peel Commission (1937) proposes partition, London answers the revolt with troops and over two thousand recruited Zionist police, and the Zionist movement pivots to America at the Biltmore Conference in New York (1942). ## Sub lesson 12 — Unit 4 Lesson 1: the 1948 War (the Nakba)

Towns fall before any Arab army enters — Britain bars Arab intervention before 15 May 1948 and Abd al-Qadir al-Husseini is martyred at al-Qastal; some 15,000 Arab fighters (Egypt, Jordan, Iraq, Lebanon, Syria) then enter, the first truce (11 June 1948) is used by the Zionists to rearm with American and British help, and the war ends with about a million Palestinians displaced, over 60 massacres and 531 villages destroyed (al-Mansura, Saffuriyya, Deir Yassin, al-Faluja among them), Israel recognised and seated at the UN, and Resolution 194 affirming the refugees' right of return.
