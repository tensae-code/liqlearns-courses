# Hands-on — Colonialism and Its End

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Colonialism and Its End — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Colonialism is defined as seizing land by force, plundering its wealth and enslaving its people; its shared motives are markets and raw materials (Britain forcing Egypt to grow cotton) and strategic positions, and its forms run from military occupation and settlement to protectorate (France in Tunisia, 1883), mandate after the First World War, economic control (the Suez Canal) and cultural invasion. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
