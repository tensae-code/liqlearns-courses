# Colonialism and Its End — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Unit 5 Lesson 1: European colonialism in the Arab homeland”?
**Answer:** Colonialism is defined as seizing land by force, plundering its wealth and enslaving its people; its shared motives are markets and raw materials (Britain forcing Egypt to grow cotton) and strategic positions, and its forms run from military occupation and settlement to protectorate (France in Tunisia, 1883), mandate after the First World War, economic control (the Suez Canal) and cultural invasion. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Unit 1 Lesson 2: the colonial economic policy” end?
**Answer:** The Arab economy is made a dependent appendage — foreign goods favoured, local industry stunted, loans used as leverage; Arab answers include nationalisation and boycotts, while in Palestine the post-1967 occupation seizes land and turns the West Bank and Gaza into dumping grounds. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
