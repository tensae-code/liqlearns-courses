# Lesson plan — Colonialism and Its End

**Course:** History — Palestine · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Colonialism and Its End
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Modern World Arrives: Renaissance to Colonialism (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colonialism is defined as seizing land by force, plundering its wealth and enslaving its people; its shared motives are markets and raw materials (Britain forcing Egypt to grow cotton) and strategic positions, and its forms run from military occupation and settlement to protectorate (France in Tunisia, 1883), mandate after the First World War, economic control (the Suez Canal) and cultural invasion.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 5 Lesson 1: European colonialism in the Arab homeland** — Colonialism is defined as seizing land by force, plundering its wealth and enslaving its people; its shared motives are markets and raw materials (Britain forcing Egypt to grow cotton) and strategic positions, and its forms run from military occupation and settlement to protectorate (France in Tunisia, 1883), mandate after the First World War, economic control (the Suez Canal) and cultural invasion.
2. **Unit 1 Lesson 2: the colonial economic policy** — The Arab economy is made a dependent appendage — foreign goods favoured, local industry stunted, loans used as leverage; Arab answers include nationalisation and boycotts, while in Palestine the post-1967 occupation seizes land and turns the West Bank and Gaza into dumping grounds.
3. **Unit 1 Lesson 3: the policy of keeping people ignorant (tajhil)** — Colonial education policy aims to erase identity, steal and distort heritage, halt development and break resistance — France claims Algeria is France itself — squeezing mosques, zawiyas, schools and universities that spread awareness; in Palestine it continues as military orders on schooling, checkpoints, demolished schools and the Judaization of Jerusalem's education.
4. **Unit 1 Lesson 4: the policy of repression** — Repression's record is named massacre by massacre — Constantine in Algeria (1945), Deir Yassin and Tantura (1948), Kafr Qasim (1956) — and execution by execution: Italy hangs Omar al-Mukhtar before Libya's people, Britain hangs the Palestinian fighters Jamjoum, al-Zir and Hijazi in 1930.
5. **Unit 2 Lessons 4–5: independence and its challenges** — Independence shows itself in a national government, constitution, free elections, flag, currency and embassies — Palestine displays the trappings without the full thing — and after it come the hard parts: economic dependence and foreign debt, border amputations (Iran's seizure of Abu Musa and the Tunb islands from the Emirates), weak parties and constitutional life, and the fear of the chaos that swallowed Iraq, Syria, Yemen, Tunisia and Libya.
