# Colonialism and Its End — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 5 Lesson 1: European colonialism in the Arab homeland
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Colonialism is defined as seizing land by force, plundering its wealth and enslaving its people; its shared motives are markets and raw materials (Britain forcing Egypt to grow cotton) and strategic positions, and its forms run from military occupation and settlement to protectorate (France in Tunisia, 1883), mandate after the First World War, economic control (the Suez Canal) and cultural invasion. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1 Lesson 2: the colonial economic policy
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Arab economy is made a dependent appendage — foreign goods favoured, local industry stunted, loans used as leverage; Arab answers include nationalisation and boycotts, while in Palestine the post-1967 occupation seizes land and turns the West Bank and Gaza into dumping grounds. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 1 Lesson 3: the policy of keeping people ignorant (tajhil)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Colonial education policy aims to erase identity, steal and distort heritage, halt development and break resistance — France claims Algeria is France itself — squeezing mosques, zawiyas, schools and universities that spread awareness; in Palestine it continues as military orders on schooling, checkpoints, demolished schools and the Judaization of Jerusalem's education. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 1 Lesson 4: the policy of repression
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Repression's record is named massacre by massacre — Constantine in Algeria (1945), Deir Yassin and Tantura (1948), Kafr Qasim (1956) — and execution by execution: Italy hangs Omar al-Mukhtar before Libya's people, Britain hangs the Palestinian fighters Jamjoum, al-Zir and Hijazi in 1930. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 2 Lessons 4–5: independence and its challenges
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Independence shows itself in a national government, constitution, free elections, flag, currency and embassies — Palestine displays the trappings without the full thing — and after it come the hard parts: economic dependence and foreign debt, border amputations (Iran's seizure of Abu Musa and the Tunb islands from the Emirates), weak parties and constitutional life, and the fear of the chaos that swallowed Iraq, Syria, Yemen, Tunisia and Libya. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
