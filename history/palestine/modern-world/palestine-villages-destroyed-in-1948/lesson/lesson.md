# Palestine: Villages Destroyed in 1948

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Palestine: Villages Destroyed in 1948** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 4 Lesson 4: the Palestinian village and its destruction in 1948

The village grows from stone mountain houses to cement streets, then in 1948 the occupation destroys more than 500 villages, commits massacres such as Deir Yassin, and expels their people into exile as refugees. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: migration and the displacement of the Palestinians

After defining migration, the lesson turns to the Nakba: the 1948 war drives about a million Palestinians from their homes into camps in Jordan, Syria and Lebanon, and pupils are asked for a just solution to the refugee question. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
