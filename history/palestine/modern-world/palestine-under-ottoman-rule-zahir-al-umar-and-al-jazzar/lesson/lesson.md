# Palestine under Ottoman Rule: Zahir al-Umar and al-Jazzar

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Palestine under Ottoman Rule: Zahir al-Umar and al-Jazzar** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Palestine in the Ottoman era — its rulers

Palestine is governed as sanjaks (Jerusalem's first among them), but local power keeps breaking away — Fakhr al-Din al-Ma'ni ruling Lebanon and northern Palestine from 1603, Zahir al-Umar al-Zaydani (1689–1775) building his Galilee state, and Ahmad Pasha al-Jazzar installed at Acre after crushing Zahir in 1775. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Ottoman building works in Palestine

Suleiman the Magnificent rebuilds Jerusalem's wall, restores the al-Qibli mosque and its sanctuary gates and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre and monasteries, and the era leaves mosques and clock towers across Palestine. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: Palestine under the late Ottoman administration

The Tanzimat reach Palestine — the Humayun edict of 1856 grants the sects equal treatment and their own courts, the 1876 constitution follows — farming booms on European demand for olives, grapes, wheat and citrus before taxes drag it down; after Egypt's 1840 withdrawal al-Sham is re-divided, and Jerusalem becomes a mutasarrifate (1874) answering directly to Istanbul, partly to keep it from colonial rivalry and Zionist immigration. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 2 Lesson 2: foreign influence in nineteenth-century Palestine

A weakening empire lets the powers in — capitulations, consulates (Britain's foreign minister instructs his Jerusalem consul in 1858 to protect incoming Jews), Protestant then Catholic and Orthodox missions, the Russian Compound and Haifa's German colony, mission schools and hospitals doubling as cultural invasion. Tell it slowly: who is there, where they stand, what they want, and what stops them. The moment those things collide is the turn — and everything after it follows from that turn.
