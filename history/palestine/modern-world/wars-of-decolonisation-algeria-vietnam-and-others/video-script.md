# Video script — Wars of Decolonisation: Algeria, Vietnam and Others

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Resistance is as old as the occupation itself but surges after the Second World War on self-determination principles, new media and the example of Syria, Lebanon and Jordan winning independence; the UN General Assembly's Resolution 1514 declares colonialism a crime — with Palestine named the exception still under occupation. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
Resistance runs armed and peaceful side by side — the PLO adopts armed struggle from 1965 and the intifada renews it, while civil resistance boycotts goods, exposes the occupier's crimes in world forums and courts, and cuts economic dependence; everywhere it fights short of men, arms, unity and supply lines. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
Libya passes from Italy to Britain and France to a UN trusteeship and independence in 1951; Syria's Yusuf al-Azma falls with some 800 men at Maysalun (1920) against France, which splits Syria into Alawite, Druze and Aleppo statelets and meets revolts up to the Great Syrian Revolt; Iraq rises against Britain in 1920 after the San Remo mandate. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Palestine.
