## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2 Lesson 1: liberation movements emerge** — Resistance is as old as the occupation itself but surges after the Second World War on self-determination principles, new media and the example of Syria, Lebanon and Jordan winning independence; the UN General Assembly's Resolution 1514 declares colonialism a crime — with Palestine named the exception still under occupation.
2. **Unit 2 Lesson 2: forms of resistance** — Resistance runs armed and peaceful side by side — the PLO adopts armed struggle from 1965 and the intifada renews it, while civil resistance boycotts goods, exposes the occupier's crimes in world forums and courts, and cuts economic dependence; everywhere it fights short of men, arms, unity and supply lines.
3. **Unit 2 Lesson 3: models of Arab liberation — Libya, Syria, Iraq** — Libya passes from Italy to Britain and France to a UN trusteeship and independence in 1951; Syria's Yusuf al-Azma falls with some 800 men at Maysalun (1920) against France, which splits Syria into Alawite, Druze and Aleppo statelets and meets revolts up to the Great Syrian Revolt; Iraq rises against Britain in 1920 after the San Remo mandate.
