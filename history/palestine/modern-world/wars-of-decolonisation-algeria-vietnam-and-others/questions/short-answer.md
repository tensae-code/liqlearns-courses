# Wars of Decolonisation: Algeria, Vietnam and Others — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Unit 2 Lesson 1: liberation movements emerge”?
**Answer:** Resistance is as old as the occupation itself but surges after the Second World War on self-determination principles, new media and the example of Syria, Lebanon and Jordan winning independence; the UN General Assembly's Resolution 1514 declares colonialism a crime — with Palestine named the exception still under occupation. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Unit 2 Lesson 2: forms of resistance” end?
**Answer:** Resistance runs armed and peaceful side by side — the PLO adopts armed struggle from 1965 and the intifada renews it, while civil resistance boycotts goods, exposes the occupier's crimes in world forums and courts, and cuts economic dependence; everywhere it fights short of men, arms, unity and supply lines. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
