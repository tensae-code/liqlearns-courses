# Mamluk Egypt

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Mamluk Egypt** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 4 Lesson 2: the Mamluks defend the Islamic state

Slave-soldiers bought young by the Ayyubid sultans rise to rule Egypt and al-Sham, restore the Abbasid caliphate in Cairo after Baghdad's fall, and Sultan al-Ashraf Khalil ibn Qalawun expels the last Franks from Acre in 690 AH / 1291 CE, freeing Palestine for good. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: the Battle of Ain Jalut

Hulagu's Mongols take Damascus, Aleppo and Hama and threaten Egypt; Sultan Qutuz, with Baybars scouting ahead, meets them at Ain Jalut north-west of Beisan in Palestine and breaks the Mongol advance. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: the powers ruling the Arab homeland before the Ottomans

Three powers divide the map — Mamluks in Egypt, al-Sham and the Hijaz, Safavids in Iraq, Portuguese seizing Muscat, Bahrain and Hormuz — with local families in North Africa, and the lesson retells who the Mamluks were through the Ain Jalut campaign map. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
