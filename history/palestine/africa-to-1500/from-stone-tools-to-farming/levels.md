# From Stone Tools to Farming — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: aspects of ancient human life
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The first humans wander unsettled, hunting and gathering fruit, then farming settles them, barter gives way to coined money, and the Canaanites are already using coinage in Palestine five thousand years ago. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2 Lesson 2: the tools ancient humans used
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Need mothers invention as flint is knapped sharp for spears, axes, sickles and grain-pounders, so the age is named the Stone Age. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2 Lesson 2: discovering fire
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Lightning and volcanoes first show humans fire, then striking flint sparks it on demand, and the cave gains warmth, light, cooked food and a weapon against beasts. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 2 Lesson 3: ancient dwelling and clothing
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: People move from rock caves and skin tents to built houses, and from plant roots and hunted skins to cotton, wool and silk clothing. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Palestine, homeland of ancient humans — the Natufian culture and Jericho
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: In Wadi Natuf's caves and at Tell es-Sultan (ancient Jericho, called the world's oldest and first walled city) hunter-gatherers settle, farm and build round stone houses, taking the first step to village life. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 4 Lesson 1: humans turn to farming
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Suad spots strange plants sprouting where seeds fell, retelling how humans noticed dropped seeds grow, planted them on purpose in the Fertile Crescent, settled beside their fields and built houses. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 4 Lesson 2: domesticating animals
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Settled humans tame animals for food, skins, wool and carrying loads, and the animals become part of the household that keeps people settled. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: the farming villages
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Farming, safety and cooperation gather people into the first villages of fixed houses and grain stores, and ancient Jericho (Tell es-Sultan) is named the world's first farming village, with Jarmo in Iraq as its peer. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit 4 Lesson 5: the city
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Some villages swell with people who leave farming for crafts, trade and government work, and the village turns into a city — Jerusalem, Jericho, Hebron, Gaza and Haifa among Palestine's ancient-grown cities. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
