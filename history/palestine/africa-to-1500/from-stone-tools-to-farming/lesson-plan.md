## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2 Lesson 1: aspects of ancient human life** — The first humans wander unsettled, hunting and gathering fruit, then farming settles them, barter gives way to coined money, and the Canaanites are already using coinage in Palestine five thousand years ago.
2. **Unit 2 Lesson 2: the tools ancient humans used** — Need mothers invention as flint is knapped sharp for spears, axes, sickles and grain-pounders, so the age is named the Stone Age.
3. **Unit 2 Lesson 2: discovering fire** — Lightning and volcanoes first show humans fire, then striking flint sparks it on demand, and the cave gains warmth, light, cooked food and a weapon against beasts.
4. **Unit 2 Lesson 3: ancient dwelling and clothing** — People move from rock caves and skin tents to built houses, and from plant roots and hunted skins to cotton, wool and silk clothing.
5. **Unit 2 Lesson 4: Palestine, homeland of ancient humans — the Natufian culture and Jericho** — In Wadi Natuf's caves and at Tell es-Sultan (ancient Jericho, called the world's oldest and first walled city) hunter-gatherers settle, farm and build round stone houses, taking the first step to village life.
6. **Unit 4 Lesson 1: humans turn to farming** — Suad spots strange plants sprouting where seeds fell, retelling how humans noticed dropped seeds grow, planted them on purpose in the Fertile Crescent, settled beside their fields and built houses.
7. **Unit 4 Lesson 2: domesticating animals** — Settled humans tame animals for food, skins, wool and carrying loads, and the animals become part of the household that keeps people settled.
8. **Unit 4 Lesson 3: the farming villages** — Farming, safety and cooperation gather people into the first villages of fixed houses and grain stores, and ancient Jericho (Tell es-Sultan) is named the world's first farming village, with Jarmo in Iraq as its peer.
9. **Unit 4 Lesson 5: the city** — Some villages swell with people who leave farming for crafts, trade and government work, and the village turns into a city — Jerusalem, Jericho, Hebron, Gaza and Haifa among Palestine's ancient-grown cities.
