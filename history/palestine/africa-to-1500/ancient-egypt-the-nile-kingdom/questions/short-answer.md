# Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Unit 4 Lesson 6: ancient landmarks — the pyramids of Egypt”?
**Answer:** Pupils match the world's old civilisations to their surviving landmarks, and Egypt's pyramids stand for the Nile kingdom's state power. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Unit 5 Lesson 3: the invention of writing — Egyptian picture writing” end?
**Answer:** Writing begins as pictures in Pharaonic Egypt, one of humanity's oldest ways of sending thought across distance. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
