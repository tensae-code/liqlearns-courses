# Lesson plan — Ancient egypt what egypt gave

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Ancient egypt what egypt gave
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The ancient story retold as analysis: prehistory to Constantine, Herodotus in his own words, and the Acropolis investigated (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Ancient egypt what egypt gave as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — The ancient story retold as analysis: prehistory to Constantine, Herodotus in his own words, and the Acropolis investigated — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Book II, Unit 5 "Wonders" and "customs" of the Egyptians** — Egyptian customs invert Greek ones at every turn
2. **§2 Egyptian history and civilisation** — pyramid builders and empire makers rise, are invaded, rise again, and leave writing, medicine and monuments
3. **§3 Chapter 5.3: The relocation of the Abu Simbel temples** — the Aswan Dam's waters rise, and UNESCO cuts Ramses' temples into blocks and rebuilds them on higher ground
4. **§4 Chapter 5.4: The relocation of the Philae monuments** — the Isis temple island is dismantled stone by stone and re-erected on Agilkia before the Nile takes it
5. **§5 Chapter 2: Egypt — general character, women's and men's dress, fabrics and accessories** — white linen, pleats and broad collars fit a hot land and an eternal order; jewellery carries gods and rank
6. **§6 Chapter 1.1: Ancient Egypt — houses and palaces, furniture, dress, glasswork, goldwork and metalwork** — the Egyptian house and its carved furniture freeze a whole civilisation's daily life in tomb paintings
7. **§7 Chapter 2 (second part): The art of Egypt** — pyramids, temples and tomb painting obey an eternal canon meant to defeat death
