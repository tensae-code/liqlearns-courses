# Lesson plan — From stone tools to farming

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From stone tools to farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Myth first, then the first farmers: the gods and heroes, prehistory, and the Aegean civilisations, as Greece's youngest readers meet the past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of From stone tools to farming as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Myth first, then the first farmers: the gods and heroes, prehistory, and the Aegean civilisations, as Greece's youngest readers meet the past — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit 7, ch. 1 Palaeolithic Age: when humans were food-gatherers and hunters** — the first people live by gathering and hunting with stone tools, shelter in caves and leave rock paintings of their hunts
2. **§2 Unit 7, ch. 2 Neolithic Age: when humans became farmers and herders** — people learn to sow grain and tame animals, settle in villages, and grind, weave and make pots instead of wandering
3. **§3 Unit 7, ch. 1 Palaeolithic exercises** — pupils point out Greek Palaeolithic sites on the map
4. **§4 Unit 7, ch. 2 Neolithic exercises** — a comparison table sets wandering gatherers against settled farmers and herders
5. **§5 Revision lesson for Units 7, 8** — a diagram of the periods of Prehistory orders Stone Age and early Bronze Age into one timeline
6. **§6 Ch. A1 The Palaeolithic and Mesolithic Age** — from homo habilis in Africa 2.5 million years ago to homo sapiens burying his dead with grave goods, the human chain spreads over the world with pebble tools and fire
7. **§7 Ch. A2 The Neolithic Age** — farming and herding end the wandering life; villages, pottery and polished stone remake the human world by 3000 BC
8. **§8 Chapter 1: Palaeolithic and Neolithic art** — cave painters and the first farmers' figurines and settlements

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
