# From stone tools to farming

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From stone tools to farming" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 7, ch. 1 Palaeolithic Age: when humans were food-gatherers and hunters

The first people live by gathering and hunting with stone tools, shelter in caves and leave rock paintings of their hunts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 7, ch. 2 Neolithic Age: when humans became farmers and herders

People learn to sow grain and tame animals, settle in villages, and grind, weave and make pots instead of wandering.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 7, ch. 1 Palaeolithic exercises

Pupils point out Greek Palaeolithic sites on the map.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 7, ch. 2 Neolithic exercises

A comparison table sets wandering gatherers against settled farmers and herders.

## Sub lesson 5 — Revision lesson for Units 7, 8

A diagram of the periods of Prehistory orders Stone Age and early Bronze Age into one timeline.

## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. A1 The Palaeolithic and Mesolithic Age

From homo habilis in Africa 2.5 million years ago to homo sapiens burying his dead with grave goods, the human chain spreads over the world with pebble tools and fire.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. A2 The Neolithic Age

Farming and herding end the wandering life; villages, pottery and polished stone remake the human world by 3000 BC.

## Sub lesson 8 — Chapter 1: Palaeolithic and Neolithic art

Cave painters and the first farmers' figurines and settlements.
