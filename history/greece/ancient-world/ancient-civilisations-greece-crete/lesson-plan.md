# Lesson plan — Ancient civilisations greece crete

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Ancient civilisations greece crete
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Myth first, then the first farmers: the gods and heroes, prehistory, and the Aegean civilisations, as Greece's youngest readers meet the past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Ancient civilisations greece crete as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Myth first, then the first farmers: the gods and heroes, prehistory, and the Aegean civilisations, as Greece's youngest readers meet the past — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit 9, ch. 1 Minoan Crete** — King Minos's Crete grows rich as the oldest sea-power of the Aegean, trading from unwalled palace towns
2. **§2 Unit 9, ch. 2 The palace of Knossos** — the labyrinthine palace of Knossos rises with throne room, storerooms and plumbing
3. **§3 Unit 9, ch. 3 The daily life of the Minoans** — cheerful, nature-loving Minoans farm, harvest grapes, dress brightly and enjoy games and festivals
4. **§4 Unit 9, ch. 4 The religion and writing of the Minoans** — Minoans worship the Great Goddess in cave shrines and spring festivals, leap over bulls, and keep records in scripts we still cannot fully read
5. **§5 Unit 9, ch. 5 The art of the Minoans** — Minoan potters and painters fill palaces with dolphins, lilies and sea life in frescoes and fine vases
6. **§6 Unit 9, ch. 6 The eruption of the Thera volcano** — in one night the Thera volcano explodes, shaking sleepers from their beds and burying the island town of Akrotiri
7. **§7 Unit 9, ch. 1 Minoan Crete exercises** — exercises open the Minoan unit by placing Crete's palace cities on the sea routes
8. **§8 Unit 9, ch. 2 Palace of Knossos exercises** — pupils label the numbered rooms of Knossos
9. **§9 Unit 9, ch. 3 Daily life exercises** — two pictures are mined for what they reveal about Minoan dress, food and work
10. **§10 Unit 9, ch. 4 Religion and writing exercises** — pupils describe the goddess in the picture and her snakes
11. **§11 Unit 9, ch. 5 Art of the Minoans exercises** — exercises ask what helped Minoan art flourish
12. **§12 Revision lesson for Units 9, 10** — a sorting table
13. **§13 Ch. B3 The Minoan Civilisation** — palace Crete grows rich on sea trade without walls, until earthquake and Thera's eruption break its power
14. **§14 Ch. B4 The religion and art of the Minoans** — goddess worship, bull-leaping frescoes and sea-life pottery show a civilisation that loved nature and ceremony
15. **§15 Greek prehistory: the Aegean civilisations** — Cycladic islanders, Minoan Crete with its palaces, then a sea-trading world the mainland will inherit
16. **§16 Chapter 6.2: Knossos** — Evans uncovers the Minoan palace and rebuilds parts in concrete
17. **§17 Chapter 3: Ancient Greece, prehistoric times — Cycladic, Minoan and Mycenaean dress** — Minoan frescoes show fitted bodices and flounced skirts unlike anything around them; Mycenaean dress turns plainer and warlike
18. **§18 Chapter 1.2: Ancient Greece — Minoan, Mycenaean and later Greek houses, decoration, furniture, jewellery, dress and pottery** — from Knossos's light-wells to the classical house, Greek decoration balances measure and ornament
19. **§19 Chapter 3: The art of the Aegean — Cycladic, Minoan and Mycenaean civilisations** — marble figurines, frescoed palaces and gold masks
