## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 5 Great changes in the empire's administration

Diocletian and Constantine remake the empire's government, army and coinage to survive its crises.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 9 The Empire is divided into Eastern and Western

Theodosius's two sons split the empire for good; the West will fall, the Greek East will become Byzantium.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 5 Administration changes exercises

Exercises follow Diocletian's and Constantine's remaking of the empire.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 9 Empire divided exercises

Map work splits the empire between Theodosius's sons, East against West.

## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. J4 The 3rd century AD: the Roman Empire in crisis

Soldier-emperors, invasion and inflation nearly break the empire before Diocletian's surgery.

## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. 1.I.2 Developments to the early 6th century

The empire splits, the West falls to Germanic kingdoms, and the East pays, fights and survives.

## Sub lesson 7 — Rome completes its expansion (200–31 BC)

Roman legions take the East and the West in turn until the whole Mediterranean is one state's lake.

## Sub lesson 8 — The consequences of the conquests

Cheap grain and slaves ruin Italy's small farmers; reformers try and die, Italy unifies, and generals march armies into civil war.

## Sub lesson 9 — The crisis of the Empire in the 3rd century

Emperors are made and murdered by armies, money fails, society strains.
