# Lesson plan — Ancient civilisations rome divides

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Ancient civilisations rome divides
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Ancient civilisations rome divides as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit 5 Great changes in the empire's administration** — Diocletian and Constantine remake the empire's government, army and coinage to survive its crises
2. **§2 Unit 9 The Empire is divided into Eastern and Western** — Theodosius's two sons split the empire for good; the West will fall, the Greek East will become Byzantium
3. **§3 Unit 5 Administration changes exercises** — exercises follow Diocletian's and Constantine's remaking of the empire
4. **§4 Unit 9 Empire divided exercises** — map work splits the empire between Theodosius's sons, East against West
5. **§5 Ch. J4 The 3rd century AD: the Roman Empire in crisis** — soldier-emperors, invasion and inflation nearly break the empire before Diocletian's surgery
6. **§6 Ch. 1.I.2 Developments to the early 6th century** — the empire splits, the West falls to Germanic kingdoms, and the East pays, fights and survives
7. **§7 Rome completes its expansion (200–31 BC)** — Roman legions take the East and the West in turn until the whole Mediterranean is one state's lake
8. **§8 The consequences of the conquests** — cheap grain and slaves ruin Italy's small farmers; reformers try and die, Italy unifies, and generals march armies into civil war
9. **§9 The crisis of the Empire in the 3rd century** — emperors are made and murdered by armies, money fails, society strains
