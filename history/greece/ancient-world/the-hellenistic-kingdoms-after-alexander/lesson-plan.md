# Lesson plan — The hellenistic kingdoms after alexander

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** The hellenistic kingdoms after alexander
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The ancient world in one sweep: Dorians to Alexander, Athens and Sparta, the Persian Wars, and the road to Rome (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of The hellenistic kingdoms after alexander as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The ancient world in one sweep: Dorians to Alexander, Athens and Sparta, the Persian Wars, and the road to Rome — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Ch. 34 Alexander's empire is divided** — the Successors carve the empire into kingdoms
2. **§2 Ch. 35 Pyrrhus, king of Epirus** — Pyrrhus wins battles against Rome at such cost that "Pyrrhic victory" enters the language, and his dream dies with his third defeat
3. **§3 Ch. 36 Daily life in Hellenistic times** — festivals and fairs break the rhythm of work in great cosmopolitan cities where Greeks and easterners live side by side
4. **§4 Ch. 37 Arts and letters in Hellenistic times** — Aristarchus of Samos first puts the sun at the centre, Alexandria's library gathers the world's books, and Rhodes raises its Colossus
5. **§5 Ch. 34–35 Successors and Pyrrhus exercises** — ordering Pyrrhus's costly victories inside the division of Alexander's empire
6. **§6 Ch. 36 Hellenistic daily life exercises** — exercises picture festivals and city life across the Hellenistic kingdoms
7. **§7 Ch. 37 Hellenistic arts and letters exercises** — matching scholars and wonders
8. **§8 Ch. I1 The Hellenistic kingdoms** — Ptolemies, Seleucids and Antigonids rule Greek-speaking empires from Alexandria to Bactria
9. **§9 Ch. I2 The situation in the Greek lands** — the Achaean and Aetolian leagues federate the old cities in a last experiment of Greek freedom
10. **§10 Ch. I5 Letters and arts of Hellenistic times** — Alexandria's Museum and Library, Euclid and Archimedes carry Greek learning into science
