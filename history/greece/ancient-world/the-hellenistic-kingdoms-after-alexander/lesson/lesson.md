## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 34 Alexander's empire is divided

The Successors carve the empire into kingdoms.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 35 Pyrrhus, king of Epirus

Pyrrhus wins battles against Rome at such cost that "Pyrrhic victory" enters the language, and his dream dies with his third defeat.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 36 Daily life in Hellenistic times

Festivals and fairs break the rhythm of work in great cosmopolitan cities where Greeks and easterners live side by side.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. 37 Arts and letters in Hellenistic times

Aristarchus of Samos first puts the sun at the centre, Alexandria's library gathers the world's books, and Rhodes raises its Colossus.

## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. 34–35 Successors and Pyrrhus exercises

Ordering Pyrrhus's costly victories inside the division of Alexander's empire.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. 37 Hellenistic arts and letters exercises

Matching scholars and wonders.

## Sub lesson 8 — Ch. I1 The Hellenistic kingdoms

Ptolemies, Seleucids and Antigonids rule Greek-speaking empires from Alexandria to Bactria.

The Achaean and Aetolian leagues federate the old cities in a last experiment of Greek freedom.

## Sub lesson 10 — Ch. I5 Letters and arts of Hellenistic times

Alexandria's Museum and Library, Euclid and Archimedes carry Greek learning into science.
