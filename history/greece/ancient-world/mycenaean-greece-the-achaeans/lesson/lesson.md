# Mycenaean greece the achaeans

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 10, ch. 1 Achaeans, the first Greeks

The Achaeans settle Greece and build warrior kingdoms; their king at Mycenae leads the very expedition to Troy the myths described.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 10, ch. 2–3 Trade with Cyprus and the acropolis of Mycenae

Mycenaean merchants sail to Cyprus for its copper, while at home the citadel of Mycenae crowns its hill with palace and massive walls.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 10, ch. 4 The daily life of the Mycenaeans

Mycenaean ships trade across the Mediterranean while warriors, farmers and craftsmen live under the palace's scribes.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 10, ch. 5 The religion and writing of the Mycenaeans

Mycenaeans worship the Great Goddess and the later Olympians, write Greek in Linear B, and bury kings in tholos tombs with gold masks like "Agamemnon's".

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 10, ch. 6 The art of the Mycenaeans

Taught by Minoan art, Mycenaean craftsmen work gold, crystal and ivory into vases and weapons for their warrior kings.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 10, ch. 1 Achaeans exercises

Exercises open the Mycenaean unit with the arrival of the first Greeks.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 10, ch. 3 Acropolis of Mycenae exercises

Pupils read the citadel's walls and gate as a fortress built for war.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 10, ch. 4 Daily life exercises

A trade table of what Mycenaeans bought and sold maps their sea business.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit 10, ch. 5 Religion and writing exercises

Pupils imagine a Mycenaean explaining Linear B.

## Sub lesson 10 — Unit 10, ch. 6 Art of the Mycenaeans exercises

Listing objects by material shows the range of Mycenaean craft in gold, bronze and crystal.

## Sub lesson 11 — Ch. B5 The Mycenaean world

Warrior kings in citadels from Mycenae to Pylos trade and raid across the Mediterranean and keep palace accounts in Linear B.

## Sub lesson 12 — Ch. B6 Mycenaean religion and art

Gold masks, tholos tombs and frescoed palaces bury and celebrate the Mycenaean kings.
