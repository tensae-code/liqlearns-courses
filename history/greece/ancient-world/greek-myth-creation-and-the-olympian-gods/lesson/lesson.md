# Greek myth creation and the olympian gods

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Greek myth creation and the olympian gods" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1, ch. 1 The Titanomachy

Chaos gives birth to Earth and Sky, the Titans rule, and Zeus leads the younger gods to overthrow them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1, ch. 2 The creation of the world and the gods / The gods of Olympus

The twelve immortal gods settle on Olympus drinking nectar, each with a realm and symbols children learn to read in pictures.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 1, ch. 3 The goddesses of Olympus

Hera, Athena, Aphrodite and the other goddesses take their places and powers beside the gods, and Athena wins Athens from Poseidon with the gift of the olive tree.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 1, ch. 4 Prometheus, Pandora, Deucalion and Pyrrha

Prometheus steals fire for humans and is chained, Pandora opens the jar of evils, and Deucalion and Pyrrha survive the flood to remake humankind from stones.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 1, ch. 1 Titanomachy exercises

Children trace everyday words back to Titan and god names, re-entering the war of the gods through language.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 1, ch. 2 Gods of Olympus exercises

Pupils reconstruct a day of feasting and quarrelling on Olympus from the chapter's readings.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 1, ch. 3 Goddesses of Olympus exercises

Matching each god to symbols and realms drills how Greeks pictured divine power.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 1, ch. 4 Prometheus/Pandora exercises

Reading Prometheus's defiance of Zeus, pupils judge whether stealing fire for humans was worth his punishment.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit 1 revision lesson

Matching and gap-fill revision binds the creation stories into one family tree of gods and humans.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Greek myth creation and the olympian gods as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
