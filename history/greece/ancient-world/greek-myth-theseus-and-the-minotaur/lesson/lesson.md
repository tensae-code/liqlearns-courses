# Greek myth theseus and the minotaur

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Greek myth theseus and the minotaur" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 3, ch. 1 Theseus, the prince of Troezen

Young Theseus lifts the rock, takes his father Aegeus's sword and sandals, and chooses the dangerous land road to Athens over the safe sea.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 3, ch. 2 The exploits of Theseus / Theseus kills the Minotaur

Theseus clears the road of robbers, then sails to Crete, enters the Labyrinth with Ariadne's thread and kills the Minotaur, freeing Athens from its tribute of youths.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 3, ch. 3 Theseus returns to Athens

The returning ship forgets to change its black sail, Aegeus throws himself into the sea that takes his name, and Theseus becomes king.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 3, reading: Daedalus and Icarus

Imprisoned Daedalus builds wings of feathers and wax, Icarus flies too near the sun despite the warning, and falls into the sea.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 3, ch. 1 Theseus of Troezen exercises

Reading Aethra's and Theseus's thoughts in the picture at the lifting of the rock.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 3, ch. 2 Theseus kills the Minotaur exercises

Pupils draw Theseus's route through the Labyrinth.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 3, ch. 3 Return to Athens exercises

Class discussion on human effort and forgetfulness around the black sail and Aegeus's death.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Greek myth theseus and the minotaur as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
