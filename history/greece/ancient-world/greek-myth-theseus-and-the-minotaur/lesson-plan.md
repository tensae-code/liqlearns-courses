# Lesson plan — Greek myth theseus and the minotaur

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Greek myth theseus and the minotaur
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Myth first, then the first farmers: the gods and heroes, prehistory, and the Aegean civilisations, as Greece's youngest readers meet the past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Greek myth theseus and the minotaur as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Myth first, then the first farmers: the gods and heroes, prehistory, and the Aegean civilisations, as Greece's youngest readers meet the past — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit 3, ch. 1 Theseus, the prince of Troezen** — young Theseus lifts the rock, takes his father Aegeus's sword and sandals, and chooses the dangerous land road to Athens over the safe sea
2. **§2 Unit 3, ch. 2 The exploits of Theseus / Theseus kills the Minotaur** — Theseus clears the road of robbers, then sails to Crete, enters the Labyrinth with Ariadne's thread and kills the Minotaur, freeing Athens from its tribute of youths
3. **§3 Unit 3, ch. 3 Theseus returns to Athens** — the returning ship forgets to change its black sail, Aegeus throws himself into the sea that takes his name, and Theseus becomes king
4. **§4 Unit 3, reading: Daedalus and Icarus** — imprisoned Daedalus builds wings of feathers and wax, Icarus flies too near the sun despite the warning, and falls into the sea
5. **§5 Unit 3, ch. 1 Theseus of Troezen exercises** — reading Aethra's and Theseus's thoughts in the picture at the lifting of the rock
6. **§6 Unit 3, ch. 2 Theseus kills the Minotaur exercises** — pupils draw Theseus's route through the Labyrinth
7. **§7 Unit 3, ch. 3 Return to Athens exercises** — class discussion on human effort and forgetfulness around the black sail and Aegeus's death

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
