## Sub lesson 10 — Ch. I3 The Roman state gains great power

Rome unifies Italy and beats Carthage, turning east just as the Greek leagues quarrel.

## Sub lesson 11 — Ch. I4 The subjugation of the Greek world

Cynoscephalae, Pydna and the sack of Corinth in 146 BC end Greek independence.

## Sub lesson 12 — Ch. J1 The Roman Peace

Augustus's Pax Romana gives the Greek East two centuries of roads, cities and quiet.

## Sub lesson 13 — The Greeks of the West

Greek colonists in Sicily and Italy build rich cities; Syracuse becomes the great power of the western Greeks.

## Sub lesson 14 — The peoples of Italy and the forming of the Roman state

Etruscans, Latins and others share Italy; Rome is founded, throws out its kings and builds the Republic.

## Sub lesson 15 — The Empire at its peak: Augustus and his successors (27 BC–AD 193)

Augustus ends the civil wars and refounds Rome as an empire; two centuries of peace let Greek and Roman culture fuse.

## Sub lesson 16 — Chapter 5: Rome — general character, women's and men's dress, fabrics and accessories

The toga makes the citizen visible; its folds are a constitution worn on the body, while silk arrives from the far East.

## Sub lesson 17 — Chapter 1.3: Ancient Rome — the Roman private house, the minor arts and the Fayum portraits

The domus with its atrium and wall painting shows Rome living privately, while Fayum's painted faces look out from Egypt's graves.

## Sub lesson 18 — Chapter 6: Roman art

Concrete vaults, triumphal arches and truthful portraits put art at the empire's service.
