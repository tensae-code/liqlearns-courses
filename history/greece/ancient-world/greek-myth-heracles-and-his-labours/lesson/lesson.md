# Greek myth heracles and his labours

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Greek myth heracles and his labours" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2, ch. 1 The birth of Heracles

Baby Heracles, son of Alcmene, strangles the two snakes Hera sends to his cradle, and the frightened household sees the hero he will become.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2, ch. 2 The Nemean lion

Sent to serve Eurystheus, Heracles wrestles the unpierceable lion bare-handed and comes back wearing its skin, terrifying the king into a jar.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2, ch. 3 The Lernaean Hydra and the Erymanthian boar

Every Hydra head cut grows back two until Iolaus burns the necks, and Heracles then traps the giant boar in deep snow and carries it alive.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 2, ch. 4 The golden-horned deer, the Stymphalian birds, the Augean stables

A year chasing Artemis's deer, bronze-beaked birds scattered by noise, and a whole filthy stable cleaned in one day by diverting two rivers.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 2, ch. 5 More labours

The fire-breathing mares of Diomedes, Hippolyta's belt among the Amazons, and the cattle of three-bodied Geryon at the edge of the world.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 2, ch. 6 Cerberus of Hades and the golden apples of the Hesperides

Heracles wrestles the three-headed dog of the dead bare-handed and tricks Atlas into fetching the golden apples, then tricks him back under the sky.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 2, ch. 7 The end of Heracles

The poisoned shirt of Nessus burns Heracles, he dies on a pyre on Oeta, and Zeus raises him to Olympus as a god.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 2, ch. 1 Birth of Heracles exercises

Pupils read the feelings of the children in the snake-cradle picture.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit 2, ch. 2 Nemean lion exercises

Picture clues show how pupils can tell the lion fight could only be won bare-handed.

## Sub lesson 10 — Unit 2, ch. 3 Hydra and boar exercises

Pupils notice Heracles spares some beasts and captures them alive.

## Sub lesson 11 — Unit 2, ch. 4 Deer, birds and stables exercises

Exercises follow the year-long deer chase and the one-day river-cleaning of Augeas's stables.

## Sub lesson 12 — Unit 2, ch. 5 More labours exercises

Map and picture work follows Heracles to Thrace, the Amazons and the far west.

## Sub lesson 13 — Unit 2, ch. 6 Cerberus and the golden apples exercises

Pupils retell how Heracles outwitted Atlas and descended living into Hades.

## Sub lesson 14 — Unit 2, ch. 7 End of Heracles exercises

Pupils say what they would tell Heracles at his fiery end.

## Sub lesson 15 — Revision lesson for Units 2, 3, 4

Pupils answer why Heracles was the Greeks' most beloved hero and what good he did people.

## Say it to your companion
