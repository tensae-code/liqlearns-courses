# The fall of constantinople 1453

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "The fall of constantinople 1453" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 34 Constantine Palaiologos tries to save the capital

The last emperor begs the West for help, accepts a hated church union, and prepares a city of a few thousand defenders.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 35 The Turks besiege Constantinople

Mehmed II builds the throat-cutter castle, drags ships overland into the Golden Horn and points the great cannon at the Theodosian walls.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 36 The capture of Constantinople by the Turks

On 29 May 1453 the walls are breached, Constantine dies fighting at the gate, and the thousand-year empire ends.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 34 Constantine Palaiologos exercises

Exercises weigh the last emperor's appeals to the West.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 35 Siege exercises

Pupils follow Mehmed's cannon and the ships hauled overland.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 36 Fall exercises

Exercises close the Byzantine story on 29 May 1453.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. 4.III.2 The Fall of the City

Mehmed's cannon, the chain across the Horn, Giustiniani's wound and the last liturgy in Hagia Sophia.

## Sub lesson 8 — The Fall of Constantinople

Mehmed II's great cannon breach the walls on 29 May 1453; the last emperor dies on them.
