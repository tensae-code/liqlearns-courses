# Lesson plan — Ancient civilisations athens and democracy

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Ancient civilisations athens and democracy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The ancient world in one sweep: Dorians to Alexander, Athens and Sparta, the Persian Wars, and the road to Rome (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Ancient civilisations athens and democracy as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The ancient world in one sweep: Dorians to Alexander, Athens and Sparta, the Persian Wars, and the road to Rome — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Ch. 12 The old constitution of Athens** — Athens slides from kings to aristocrats while poor Attic farmers fall into debt-slavery on barren soil
2. **§2 Ch. 13 The laws of Solon; Peisistratus becomes tyrant** — Solon cancels debts and frees the debt-slaves but refuses to redivide the land, and Peisistratus later seizes the Acropolis as tyrant
3. **§3 Ch. 14 Cleisthenes founds democracy** — Cleisthenes mixes Attica's people into ten new tribes so no clan can rule, and every citizen gains a share in governing
4. **§4 Ch. 21 The constitution and society of Athens in Pericles's time** — citizens with two Athenian parents rule through assembly and courts, while metics trade without rights and slaves carry the city's labour
5. **§5 Ch. 12 Old Athenian constitution exercises** — true/false sorting tests how aristocrats took over from Athens's kings
6. **§6 Ch. 13 Solon and Peisistratus exercises** — pupils tick Solon's real acts
7. **§7 Ch. 14 Cleisthenes exercises** — exercises rebuild the ten tribes that broke clan power and founded democracy
8. **§8 Revision lesson, Archaic years** — sentence completion and a crossword lock in colonies, tyrants, Sparta and Solon's Athens
9. **§9 Ch. 21 Constitution and society exercises** — sorting citizens, metics and slaves rebuilds Periclean Athens's social pyramid
10. **§10 Ch. D4 Athens: from kingship to aristocracy** — Athens sheds its kings for archons and the Areopagus while debt crushes its farmers
11. **§11 Ch. D5 Athens: the road to democracy** — Solon shakes off the debts, Peisistratus rules as tyrant, and Cleisthenes rebuilds the citizen body into ten tribes
12. **§12 Ch. E2 The democratic constitution is stabilised — Pericles and democracy** — Ephialtes and Pericles open office and pay to ordinary citizens, and Athens becomes a full democracy
13. **§13 Ch. E3 The functioning of the constitution; the liturgies** — assembly, council, courts and sortition run the city while rich citizens fund triremes and choruses as liturgies
14. **§14 The Archaic age** — city-states spread colonies, tyrants fall, Sparta arms itself and Athens walks step by step toward democracy
15. **§15 The Classical age** — Athens and Sparta beat Persia together, then Athens' democracy and empire grow until the two allies destroy each other in war
16. **§16 Chapter 6.4: The Acropolis of Athens** — temple, church, mosque, fortress and ruin
17. **§17 Chapter 4: Ancient Greece, historical times — Geometric, Archaic, Classical and Hellenistic dress, fabrics and accessories** — one rectangle of cloth
