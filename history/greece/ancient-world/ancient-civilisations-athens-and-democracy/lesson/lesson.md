# Ancient civilisations athens and democracy

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Ancient civilisations athens and democracy" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 12 The old constitution of Athens

Athens slides from kings to aristocrats while poor Attic farmers fall into debt-slavery on barren soil.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 13 The laws of Solon; Peisistratus becomes tyrant

Solon cancels debts and frees the debt-slaves but refuses to redivide the land, and Peisistratus later seizes the Acropolis as tyrant.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 14 Cleisthenes founds democracy

Cleisthenes mixes Attica's people into ten new tribes so no clan can rule, and every citizen gains a share in governing.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. 21 The constitution and society of Athens in Pericles's time

Citizens with two Athenian parents rule through assembly and courts, while metics trade without rights and slaves carry the city's labour.

## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. 12 Old Athenian constitution exercises

True/false sorting tests how aristocrats took over from Athens's kings.

## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. 13 Solon and Peisistratus exercises

Pupils tick Solon's real acts.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. 14 Cleisthenes exercises

Exercises rebuild the ten tribes that broke clan power and founded democracy.

## Sub lesson 8 — Revision lesson, Archaic years

Sentence completion and a crossword lock in colonies, tyrants, Sparta and Solon's Athens.

## Sub lesson 9 — Ch. 21 Constitution and society exercises

Sorting citizens, metics and slaves rebuilds Periclean Athens's social pyramid.

## Sub lesson 10 — Ch. D4 Athens: from kingship to aristocracy

Athens sheds its kings for archons and the Areopagus while debt crushes its farmers.

## Sub lesson 11 — Ch. D5 Athens: the road to democracy

Solon shakes off the debts, Peisistratus rules as tyrant, and Cleisthenes rebuilds the citizen body into ten tribes.

## Sub lesson 12 — Ch. E2 The democratic constitution is stabilised — Pericles and democracy

Ephialtes and Pericles open office and pay to ordinary citizens, and Athens becomes a full democracy.

## Sub lesson 13 — Ch. E3 The functioning of the constitution; the liturgies

Assembly, council, courts and sortition run the city while rich citizens fund triremes and choruses as liturgies.

## Sub lesson 14 — The Archaic age

City-states spread colonies, tyrants fall, Sparta arms itself and Athens walks step by step toward democracy.

## Sub lesson 15 — The Classical age

Athens and Sparta beat Persia together, then Athens' democracy and empire grow until the two allies destroy each other in war.

## Sub lesson 16 — Chapter 6.4: The Acropolis of Athens

Temple, church, mosque, fortress and ruin.

## Sub lesson 17 — Chapter 4: Ancient Greece, historical times — Geometric, Archaic, Classical and Hellenistic dress, fabrics and accessories

One rectangle of cloth.
