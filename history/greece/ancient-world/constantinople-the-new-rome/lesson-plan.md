# Lesson plan — Constantinople the new rome

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Constantinople the new rome
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Constantinople the new rome as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit 6 A new capital, Constantinople** — Constantine founds a New Rome on the Bosporus, where Europe meets Asia and the sea lanes cross
2. **§2 Unit 7 Constantinople is fortified and adorned with works of art** — Theodosian walls ring the city while forums, columns and churches dress the new capital as an empress
3. **§3 Unit 6 New capital exercises** — pupils match other refounded capitals to Constantinople's story
4. **§4 Unit 7 Fortified and adorned Constantinople exercises** — picture work reads the walls and monuments as the city's armour and crown
5. **§5 Ch. J5 Constantine: the great turn** — Constantine wins under the cross, tolerates and favours Christianity, and founds Constantinople
6. **§6 Ch. 1.I.1 From Rome to the New Rome** — Constantine moves the empire's heart east and the Roman state begins its thousand-year Greek transformation

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
