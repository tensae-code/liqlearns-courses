# Lesson plan — Medieval world byzantium and justinian

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Medieval world byzantium and justinian
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Medieval world byzantium and justinian as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit 13 Justinian reforms administration and legislation** — Justinian and Tribonian gather Roman law into one code that will govern Europe for a thousand years
2. **§2 Unit 14 The Demes upset the capital with the Nika riot** — Blues and Greens unite in the cry "Nika!", burn half the city, and Theodora's refusal to flee saves Justinian's throne
3. **§3 Unit 15 Hagia Sophia, a masterpiece of architecture** — on the ashes of the riot Anthemius and Isidore raise Hagia Sophia's floating dome in under six years
4. **§4 Unit 16 The Byzantine state reaches the empire's old borders** — Belisarius reconquers Africa and Italy, and Justinian's map almost restores the old Roman world
5. **§5 Unit 13 Justinian reforms exercises** — exercises follow Justinian gathering the laws and tightening the state
6. **§6 Unit 14 Nika riot exercises** — pupils ask how the people spoke to the emperor then
7. **§7 Unit 15 Hagia Sophia exercises** — matching church plans and styles reads Hagia Sophia against older temples
8. **§8 Unit 16 Empire regains old borders exercises** — map work follows Belisarius's reconquests to the old Roman frontiers
9. **§9 Revision of Units 1–16** — picture description and chronological ordering bind Rome's transformation into Byzantium
10. **§10 General revision** — from "divide and rule" to picking the year's three key events, the whole Byzantine millennium is judged by the pupils themselves
11. **§11 Ch. 1.II.1 Justinian and his work** — law code, Hagia Sophia, reconquest of the West
12. **§12 The age of Justinian** — Justinian reconquers the West for a Roman world that is now Greek and Christian, and codifies its law
13. **§13 Letters and arts of late antiquity** — Christian learning and art grow inside Roman forms
14. **§14 From antiquity to the Middle Ages (introduction)** — cities shrink behind walls, temples become churches, and Rome's eastern half slowly turns into Christian, Greek Byzantium
15. **§15 Chapter 6: Byzantium — the empire's dress, women's and men's clothing, fabrics and accessories** — court dress becomes ceremony
16. **§16 Chapter 2.1: Byzantium** — icons, ivory, enamel and silk carry imperial and church splendour into the home and the palace
