# Medieval world byzantium and justinian

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Medieval world byzantium and justinian" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 13 Justinian reforms administration and legislation

Justinian and Tribonian gather Roman law into one code that will govern Europe for a thousand years.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 14 The Demes upset the capital with the Nika riot

Blues and Greens unite in the cry "Nika!", burn half the city, and Theodora's refusal to flee saves Justinian's throne.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 15 Hagia Sophia, a masterpiece of architecture

On the ashes of the riot Anthemius and Isidore raise Hagia Sophia's floating dome in under six years.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 16 The Byzantine state reaches the empire's old borders

Belisarius reconquers Africa and Italy, and Justinian's map almost restores the old Roman world.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 13 Justinian reforms exercises

Exercises follow Justinian gathering the laws and tightening the state.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 14 Nika riot exercises

Pupils ask how the people spoke to the emperor then.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 15 Hagia Sophia exercises

Matching church plans and styles reads Hagia Sophia against older temples.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 16 Empire regains old borders exercises

Map work follows Belisarius's reconquests to the old Roman frontiers.

## Sub lesson 9 — Revision of Units 1–16

Picture description and chronological ordering bind Rome's transformation into Byzantium.

## Sub lesson 10 — General revision

From "divide and rule" to picking the year's three key events, the whole Byzantine millennium is judged by the pupils themselves.

## Sub lesson 11 — Ch. 1.II.1 Justinian and his work

Law code, Hagia Sophia, reconquest of the West.

## Sub lesson 12 — The age of Justinian

Justinian reconquers the West for a Roman world that is now Greek and Christian, and codifies its law.

## Sub lesson 13 — Letters and arts of late antiquity

Christian learning and art grow inside Roman forms.

## Sub lesson 14 — From antiquity to the Middle Ages (introduction)

Cities shrink behind walls, temples become churches, and Rome's eastern half slowly turns into Christian, Greek Byzantium.

## Sub lesson 15 — Chapter 6: Byzantium — the empire's dress, women's and men's clothing, fabrics and accessories

Court dress becomes ceremony.

## Sub lesson 16 — Chapter 2.1: Byzantium

Icons, ivory, enamel and silk carry imperial and church splendour into the home and the palace.
