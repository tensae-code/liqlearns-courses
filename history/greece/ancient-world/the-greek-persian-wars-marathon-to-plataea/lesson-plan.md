# Lesson plan — The greek persian wars marathon to plataea

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** The greek persian wars marathon to plataea
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The ancient world in one sweep: Dorians to Alexander, Athens and Sparta, the Persian Wars, and the road to Rome (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of The greek persian wars marathon to plataea as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The ancient world in one sweep: Dorians to Alexander, Athens and Sparta, the Persian Wars, and the road to Rome — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Ch. 16 The Persian danger (with the Battle of Marathon)** — Darius's heralds demand earth and water; at Marathon the outnumbered Athenians charge at a run and drive the Persians to their ships
2. **§2 Ch. 17 The Battle of Thermopylae** — Leonidas and his 300 hold the narrow pass against Xerxes's host until a traitor's path lets the Persians surround them, and they fight to the last with hands and teeth
3. **§3 Ch. 18 The naval Battle of Salamis** — Themistocles lures Xerxes's great fleet into the narrow straits where Greek triremes ram it to pieces while the king watches from his throne
4. **§4 Ch. 19 The Battle of Plataea and the naval Battle of Mycale** — the united Greeks crush Mardonius at Plataea and burn the Persian fleet at Mycale on the same day, ending the invasion
5. **§5 Ch. 16 Persian danger exercises** — pupils order the sentences of Darius's and Xerxes's invasions, Marathon included, into one story
6. **§6 Ch. 17 Thermopylae exercises** — multiple-choice on Leonidas's stand checks the pass, the traitor and the last fight
7. **§7 Ch. 18 Salamis exercises** — an acrostic spells Themistocles
8. **§8 Ch. 19 Plataea and Mycale exercises** — gap-fill retells the twin victories that ended the Persian invasion
9. **§9 Ch. D7 Persians and Greeks: two worlds collide** — the Ionian revolt draws Darius's wrath; Marathon, Thermopylae and Salamis answer an empire with citizen hoplites and triremes
10. **§10 Ch. D8 The final removal of the Persian attack** — Plataea, Mycale and the Eurymedon drive Persia from the Aegean for good, and Athens inherits the war's leadership
11. **§11 Book V, Unit 9b The origin of the Macedonians; the Ionian Revolt (summary)** — Athens and Eretria burn Sardis with the Ionian rebels, and Darius orders a servant to say daily "Master, remember the Athenians"
12. **§12 Book VI, Unit 10 Before the Battle of Marathon** — Philippides runs to Sparta, the Plataeans march out alone to help Athens, and the generals vote by one vote to fight
13. **§13 Book VI, Unit 11 The Battle of Marathon** — the Athenians charge a mile at a run, fold the Persian wings inward, and lose 192 men against 6,400
14. **§14 Book VII, Unit 12 Dialogue of Xerxes and Demaratus** — the exiled Spartan king tells Xerxes that law is the Spartans' master and they will fight however few
15. **§15 Book VII, Unit 13 The Battle of Thermopylae** — Leonidas dismisses the allies, keeps his 300, and falls with them combing their hair before the last morning
16. **§16 Book VIII, Unit 14 Before the naval battle** — Artemisium's storms, Athens evacuated to Salamis, and Themistocles reads the wooden-walls oracle his way
17. **§17 Book VIII, Unit 15 The naval Battle of Salamis** — Themistocles's false message traps the Persian fleet in the straits where Aeginetans and Athenians destroy it
18. **§18 Book VIII, Unit 16 After the battle — the spirit of the Athenians** — Athens refuses Mardonius's bribes of land and gold: no Greek will betray the kinship of blood, language, gods and customs
19. **§19 Book IX, Unit 17 The Battle of Plataea — the end of the History** — Pausanias's united Greeks destroy Mardonius's army at Plataea while Mycale burns the Persian fleet
