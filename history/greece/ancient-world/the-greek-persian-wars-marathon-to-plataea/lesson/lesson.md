# The greek persian wars marathon to plataea

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "The greek persian wars marathon to plataea" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 16 The Persian danger (with the Battle of Marathon)

Darius's heralds demand earth and water; at Marathon the outnumbered Athenians charge at a run and drive the Persians to their ships.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 17 The Battle of Thermopylae

Leonidas and his 300 hold the narrow pass against Xerxes's host until a traitor's path lets the Persians surround them, and they fight to the last with hands and teeth.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 18 The naval Battle of Salamis

Themistocles lures Xerxes's great fleet into the narrow straits where Greek triremes ram it to pieces while the king watches from his throne.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. 19 The Battle of Plataea and the naval Battle of Mycale

The united Greeks crush Mardonius at Plataea and burn the Persian fleet at Mycale on the same day, ending the invasion.

## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. 16 Persian danger exercises

Pupils order the sentences of Darius's and Xerxes's invasions, Marathon included, into one story.

## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. 17 Thermopylae exercises

Multiple-choice on Leonidas's stand checks the pass, the traitor and the last fight.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. 18 Salamis exercises

An acrostic spells Themistocles.

## Sub lesson 8 — Ch. 19 Plataea and Mycale exercises

Gap-fill retells the twin victories that ended the Persian invasion.

## Sub lesson 9 — Ch. D7 Persians and Greeks: two worlds collide

The Ionian revolt draws Darius's wrath; Marathon, Thermopylae and Salamis answer an empire with citizen hoplites and triremes.

## Sub lesson 10 — Ch. D8 The final removal of the Persian attack

Plataea, Mycale and the Eurymedon drive Persia from the Aegean for good, and Athens inherits the war's leadership.

## Sub lesson 11 — Book V, Unit 9b The origin of the Macedonians; the Ionian Revolt (summary)

Athens and Eretria burn Sardis with the Ionian rebels, and Darius orders a servant to say daily "Master, remember the Athenians".

## Sub lesson 12 — Book VI, Unit 10 Before the Battle of Marathon

Philippides runs to Sparta, the Plataeans march out alone to help Athens, and the generals vote by one vote to fight.

## Sub lesson 13 — Book VI, Unit 11 The Battle of Marathon

The Athenians charge a mile at a run, fold the Persian wings inward, and lose 192 men against 6,400.

## Sub lesson 14 — Book VII, Unit 12 Dialogue of Xerxes and Demaratus

The exiled Spartan king tells Xerxes that law is the Spartans' master and they will fight however few.

## Sub lesson 15 — Book VII, Unit 13 The Battle of Thermopylae

Leonidas dismisses the allies, keeps his 300, and falls with them combing their hair before the last morning.

## Sub lesson 16 — Book VIII, Unit 14 Before the naval battle

Artemisium's storms, Athens evacuated to Salamis, and Themistocles reads the wooden-walls oracle his way.

## Sub lesson 17 — Book VIII, Unit 15 The naval Battle of Salamis

Themistocles's false message traps the Persian fleet in the straits where Aeginetans and Athenians destroy it.

## Sub lesson 18 — Book VIII, Unit 16 After the battle — the spirit of the Athenians

Athens refuses Mardonius's bribes of land and gold: no Greek will betray the kinship of blood, language, gods and customs.

## Sub lesson 19 — Book IX, Unit 17 The Battle of Plataea — the end of the History

Pausanias's united Greeks destroy Mardonius's army at Plataea while Mycale burns the Persian fleet.
