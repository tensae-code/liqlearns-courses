# History — Greece course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Greece inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Greece's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** the placements files' book order is the teaching order — earliest books first — and the ladder follows Greece's own progression through its books. Greece begins History as story: the youngest readers meet the past first through the tales the culture tells about itself — a whole first half of mythology (creation, Heracles, Theseus, the Argonauts, Troy, Odysseus) before prehistory, the first farmers and the Aegean civilisations (Grade 3, with its workbook). The ancient world then runs in one continuous sweep from the Dorians to the Roman conquest, told as biography and battle (Grade 4, with its workbook). Byzantium gets a whole year of its own, told as the biography of one city, Constantinople, from its founding to 1453 (Grade 5, with its workbook). The primary cycle closes with the modern national epic — a short European runway, then the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821 hero by hero, and the building of modern Greece (Grade 6, with its workbook). The gymnasium cycle retells the ancient story as analysis (Grade 7, with Herodotus read in translation as a book of his own and the Acropolis taught as an inquiry dossier), gives Byzantium the spine of medieval and modern history (Grade 8, with a dossier on wealth and poverty in Byzantine society), and closes by teaching two centuries as problems, Greek history woven into Europe's at every step (Grade 9 — the modern and contemporary book, filed in both placements files, with the ecclesiastical-history topics and the Heraklion dossier). The lyceum books then widen the frame: the ancient world whole, every Mediterranean people in the same four-part frame (Grade 10), beside a fast vocational survey from 1453 to today; the medieval and modern world 565–1815 in depth (Grade 11), beside the vocational arts books that teach history through clothing, decoration, graphics, art and the protection of cultural heritage; and the final surveys (Grade 12) — 1815 to today in Europe's frame, and modern Greece in five deep themes. Each level is one stage of that progression, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Where the build stands

The course is built in full from the two placements files: every one of the 951 placement lines is stocked into exactly one lesson, with exactly one provenance row in that lesson's sources.md. The two RECORD ONLY books (Grade 5 and Grade 6 Social and Political Education — pure Civics) carry no History placements and are named in ABOUT.md as record-only, as filed; they belong to the Civics folder work. No book in this filing is UNREAD and no book is PARTIAL. Lessons standing only on the three teacher folders (the Acropolis dossier, the Wealth and Poverty dossier, and the Heraklion dossier) are labelled teacher-guide evidence only, acceptance 0, in their sources and in ABOUT.md.
