# Lesson plan — The asia minor campaign and catastrophe 1922

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** The asia minor campaign and catastrophe 1922
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of The asia minor campaign and catastrophe 1922 as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit E, ch. 5 The Asia Minor Campaign and the Catastrophe** — the Greek army lands at Smyrna in 1919, drives deep into Anatolia, and is destroyed in 1922; Smyrna burns and a million and a half refugees cross to Greece
2. **§2 Unit E exercise dossier (Greece in the 20th century)** — maps marked with the Treaty of Sèvres gains and the Smyrna zone, timelines of 1940–49, the army's advance in Northern Epirus mapped, and EOKA research links for Cyprus
3. **§3 §35 Entente and Greek claims on the Ottoman Empire** — Sèvres promises Smyrna and Thrace; the Great Idea reaches its furthest map
4. **§4 §38 The Asia Minor War (1919–1922)** — advance to the Sangarios, stalemate, the August 1922 collapse, Smyrna's fire and the refugee exodus
5. **§5 §39 Developments in Greece and Turkey after the Asia Minor War** — Lausanne, the population exchange, the Trial of the Six in Athens and Ataturk's reforms in Ankara
