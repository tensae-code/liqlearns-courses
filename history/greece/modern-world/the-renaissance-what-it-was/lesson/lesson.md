# The renaissance what it was

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "The renaissance what it was" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit A, ch. 1 The Renaissance and the Religious Reformation

Art and learning are reborn in northern Italy and spread over Europe in two centuries, while the Reformation splits western Christianity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit A exercise dossier (Europe in modern times)

Pupils sort scholars, scientists and artists into Renaissance, Reformation and Enlightenment, and measure the Ottoman Empire on the student book's map.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 7.I.2 Renaissance and Humanism

Greek scholars fleeing west and Italian patrons revive antiquity in art, letters and the study of man.

## Sub lesson 4 — Spiritual and artistic developments of early modern Europe

Humanists revive antiquity and artists learn perspective while printing spreads every idea.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Renaissance in art

Brunelleschi, Leonardo and Michelangelo rebuild the visible world in perspective, marble and paint.

## Sub lesson 6 — From the Middle Ages to modern times

Money, cities and curiosity loosen the medieval order.

## Sub lesson 7 — The civilisation of the Renaissance (15th–16th centuries)

Courts, academies and workshops from Florence to the North remake art and thought.

## Sub lesson 8 — Chapter 5.9: Conservation of the Sistine Chapel frescoes, Vatican

Cleaning Michelangelo's ceiling reveals colours hidden for centuries and reopens the argument over what restoration may touch.

## Sub lesson 9 — Chapter 1: Renaissance dress

Italy, Germany, Spain and England each cut the new man differently.

## Sub lesson 10 — Chapters 4–5: The Renaissance

The palazzo interior is reborn on classical rules.

## Sub lesson 11 — Unit 10: The founding of the first printing houses in Europe's cities in the Renaissance

Within a generation presses open from Venice to Paris and the printed book remakes learning.
