# Lesson plan — The renaissance what it was

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** The renaissance what it was
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of The renaissance what it was as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit A, ch. 1 The Renaissance and the Religious Reformation** — art and learning are reborn in northern Italy and spread over Europe in two centuries, while the Reformation splits western Christianity
2. **§2 Unit A exercise dossier (Europe in modern times)** — pupils sort scholars, scientists and artists into Renaissance, Reformation and Enlightenment, and measure the Ottoman Empire on the student book's map
3. **§3 Ch. 7.I.2 Renaissance and Humanism** — Greek scholars fleeing west and Italian patrons revive antiquity in art, letters and the study of man
4. **§4 Spiritual and artistic developments of early modern Europe** — humanists revive antiquity and artists learn perspective while printing spreads every idea
5. **§5 The Renaissance in art** — Brunelleschi, Leonardo and Michelangelo rebuild the visible world in perspective, marble and paint
6. **§6 From the Middle Ages to modern times** — money, cities and curiosity loosen the medieval order
7. **§7 The civilisation of the Renaissance (15th–16th centuries)** — courts, academies and workshops from Florence to the North remake art and thought
8. **§8 Chapter 5.9: Conservation of the Sistine Chapel frescoes, Vatican** — cleaning Michelangelo's ceiling reveals colours hidden for centuries and reopens the argument over what restoration may touch
9. **§9 Chapter 1: Renaissance dress** — Italy, Germany, Spain and England each cut the new man differently
10. **§10 Chapters 4–5: The Renaissance** — the palazzo interior is reborn on classical rules
11. **§11 Unit 10: The founding of the first printing houses in Europe's cities in the Renaissance** — within a generation presses open from Venice to Paris and the printed book remakes learning
