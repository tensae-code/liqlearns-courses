# Greece economy and society in the 19th century

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Greece economy and society in the 19th century" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Greek economy and society in the 19th century

Currants, shipping and remittances carry a poor farming country; Athens grows while villages empty.

## Sub lesson 2 — Greek political, social and economic life in the 19th century

A poor agrarian kingdom lives on currants, shipping and the dream of the Great Idea.

## Sub lesson 3 — Theme 1 Introduction & A: The Greek economy after the Revolution

A war-emptied land, its people and its productive forces inside and outside the borders, and the Great Idea pulling money and migrants toward the unredeemed lands.

## Sub lesson 4 — Theme 1 B: Trade and merchant shipping (19th century)

Currants and grain fill Greek sails; island fleets move into steam and Greek merchant houses span the Mediterranean and the Black Sea.

## Sub lesson 5 — Theme 1 B: The distribution of the national lands

Who gets the old Ottoman estates? Decades of delay end in the 1870–71 laws that finally hand land to the cultivators.

## Sub lesson 6 — Theme 1 B: Mines and the creation of a banking system

Laurion's silver and lead draw foreign companies and scandals, while the National Bank (1841) and its rivals slowly build Greek credit.

## Sub lesson 7 — Theme 1 B: Industry

Mills, tanneries and workshops grow around Piraeus, Syros and the ports.

## Sub lesson 8 — Theme 1 B: Public works and the railway network

Roads, the Corinth Canal (1893) and a railway net finished only in 1909 stitch the little kingdom together.

## Sub lesson 9 — Theme 1 B: National loans, the bankruptcy of 1893, International Financial Control, and diaspora capital

Borrowed millions fund the state until the 1893 crash hands Greek revenues to foreign controllers, while diaspora Greeks' capital flows home.

## Sub lesson 10 — Theme 1 C: The agrarian question (20th century)

Thessaly's great estates, tenant farmers' revolts and the land reforms that follow the refugee settlement remake the countryside.

## Sub lesson 11 — Theme 1 C: Economic conditions 1910–1922 and the First World War

War mobilisation, inflation and blockade strain the economy through the Balkan Wars, the Great War and the Asia Minor campaign.

## Sub lesson 12 — Theme 1 C: Economic life 1922–1936 — the interwar economy, the great investments, the Bank of Greece and the crisis of 1932

Refugee labour powers an industrial spurt, foreign loans build dams and roads, then 1929's echo forces Greece off gold in 1932.
