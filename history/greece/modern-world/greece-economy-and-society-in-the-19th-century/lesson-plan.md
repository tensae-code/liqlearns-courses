# Lesson plan — Greece economy and society in the 19th century

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Greece economy and society in the 19th century
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7 — Two centuries as problems: modern and contemporary history woven with Europe's, the Church's travelling story, and Heraklion investigated (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Greece economy and society in the 19th century as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 7 — Two centuries as problems: modern and contemporary history woven with Europe's, the Church's travelling story, and Heraklion investigated — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Greek economy and society in the 19th century** — currants, shipping and remittances carry a poor farming country; Athens grows while villages empty
2. **§2 Greek political, social and economic life in the 19th century** — a poor agrarian kingdom lives on currants, shipping and the dream of the Great Idea
3. **§3 Theme 1 Introduction & A: The Greek economy after the Revolution** — a war-emptied land, its people and its productive forces inside and outside the borders, and the Great Idea pulling money and migrants toward the unredeemed lands
4. **§4 Theme 1 B: Trade and merchant shipping (19th century)** — currants and grain fill Greek sails; island fleets move into steam and Greek merchant houses span the Mediterranean and the Black Sea
5. **§5 Theme 1 B: The distribution of the national lands** — who gets the old Ottoman estates? Decades of delay end in the 1870–71 laws that finally hand land to the cultivators
6. **§6 Theme 1 B: Mines and the creation of a banking system** — Laurion's silver and lead draw foreign companies and scandals, while the National Bank (1841) and its rivals slowly build Greek credit
7. **§7 Theme 1 B: Industry** — mills, tanneries and workshops grow around Piraeus, Syros and the ports
8. **§8 Theme 1 B: Public works and the railway network** — roads, the Corinth Canal (1893) and a railway net finished only in 1909 stitch the little kingdom together
9. **§9 Theme 1 B: National loans, the bankruptcy of 1893, International Financial Control, and diaspora capital** — borrowed millions fund the state until the 1893 crash hands Greek revenues to foreign controllers, while diaspora Greeks' capital flows home
10. **§10 Theme 1 C: The agrarian question (20th century)** — Thessaly's great estates, tenant farmers' revolts and the land reforms that follow the refugee settlement remake the countryside
11. **§11 Theme 1 C: Economic conditions 1910–1922 and the First World War** — war mobilisation, inflation and blockade strain the economy through the Balkan Wars, the Great War and the Asia Minor campaign
12. **§12 Theme 1 C: Economic life 1922–1936 — the interwar economy, the great investments, the Bank of Greece and the crisis of 1932** — refugee labour powers an industrial spurt, foreign loans build dams and roads, then 1929's echo forces Greece off gold in 1932
