# American independence

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "American independence" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit A, ch. 3 The American and French Revolutions

The colonists rise against Britain declaring that men are born equal (1776), and France follows with its own revolution.

## Sub lesson 2 — §2 The American Revolution

Thirteen colonies reject a king across an ocean and found a republic on the rights of man.

## Sub lesson 3 — The American revolution

Britain's colonies refuse its taxes, declare independence, and win a republic across the Atlantic.

## Sub lesson 4 — The American Revolution

Thirteen colonies reject an empire and found a federal republic on Enlightenment paper.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of American independence as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
