# Lesson plan — Greeks under ottoman rule 1453 1821

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Greeks under ottoman rule 1453 1821
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Greeks under ottoman rule 1453 1821 as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit B, ch. 1 The conquest of the Greek peninsula** — after 1453 the Ottomans complete the conquest of the Greek lands, ending the long Byzantine decline in Turkish and Latin lordship
2. **§2 Unit B, ch. 2 The living conditions of the enslaved** — great Byzantine families flee west while the rayah peasants pay the head tax and carry the conqueror's burdens
3. **§3 Unit B, ch. 3 The religious and political organisation of the Greeks** — the Ottomans abolish Byzantine rule but tolerate the Church; the Patriarch becomes the nation's keeper under the millet order
4. **§4 Unit B, ch. 4 The Klephts and the Armatoloi** — mountain klephts rob and resist the Turks while armatoloi police the passes
5. **§5 Unit B, ch. 5 Economic life** — an agrarian empire of wheat and barley slowly fills with Greek merchants, shipowners and guilds trading from Smyrna to Vienna
6. **§6 Unit B, ch. 6 The Greeks of the diaspora communities and the Danubian Principalities** — emigrant Greeks build churches, schools and printing houses abroad, and Phanariots like Alexandros Mavrokordatos rise to rule Moldavia and Wallachia
7. **§7 Unit B, ch. 7 The Teachers of the Nation** — cleric-teachers, many schooled in the West, multiply schools
8. **§8 Unit B, ch. 9 The main revolutionary movements** — Maniots, Cretans and islanders rise again and again through three centuries, keeping alive the hope of liberation the sources record
9. **§9 Unit B, ch. 10 The struggles of the Souliots** — the shepherd-warriors of Souli defy Ali Pasha for years, write him defiant letters, and end in exile and the dance of Zalongo's shadow
10. **§10 Unit B exercise dossier (Greeks under Ottoman and Latin rule)** — term-matching, map place-names from Moschopolis to Smyrna, a research task on Kosmas the Aetolian and a timeline from Küçük Kaynarca to the Revolution
11. **§11 Ch. 7.II Hellenism under Venetian and Ottoman rule** — Crete blooms under Venice while the mainland lives under the Porte
12. **§12 §5 Hellenism from the mid-18th to the early 19th century** — merchants, schools, the diaspora and Rigas's and Korais's Enlightenment prepare a nation inside an empire
