# Lesson plan — The greek revolution 1821 1830

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** The greek revolution 1821 1830
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of The greek revolution 1821 1830 as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The modern national epic: a short European runway, the Ottoman centuries, the Revolution of 1821, and the building of modern Greece — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unit C, ch. 1 The Filiki Etaireia** — three merchants in Odessa found a secret society in 1814, swear members by oath, and spin a web of initiates across the diaspora
2. **§2 Unit C, ch. 2 The uprising in Moldavia-Wallachia** — Alexandros Ypsilantis crosses the Prut calling the Balkans to rise; the Sacred Band dies at Dragatsani and the northern rising is crushed
3. **§3 Unit C, ch. 3 The revolution in the Peloponnese** — the Morea rises in March 1821
4. **§4 Unit C, ch. 4 The revolution in Central Greece** — chieftains swear the rising at Levadeia, Odysseas Androutsos holds the inn at Gravia, and Athanasios Diakos dies impaled after Alamana
5. **§5 Unit C, ch. 5 The revolution in the Aegean islands** — Spetses rises first under Bouboulina; Hydra, Psara and Samos turn merchant fleets into a navy under Miaoulis and Papanikolis
6. **§6 Unit C, ch. 6 The revolution in Epirus, Thessaly and Macedonia** — returned Souliots strike in Epirus while the Karatasos father and son raise Olympus and Chalkidiki, and a young Macedonian exile writes home of the struggle
7. **§7 Unit C, ch. 7 The fall of Tripolitsa** — Kolokotronis rings the Ottoman capital of the Morea; after months of siege the city falls in a storm of killing that shadows the victory
8. **§8 Unit C, ch. 8 The struggles of Kanaris** — with only merchant ships against great war fleets, Greek captains send in fireships
9. **§9 Unit C, ch. 9 Dramali's campaign — Dervenakia** — Dramali's great army pours into the Morea and starves in the Argos plain; Kolokotronis destroys it in the Dervenakia passes
10. **§10 Unit C, ch. 10 Markos Botsaris** — after the disaster at Peta, Botsaris leads his Souliots in a night attack at Karpenisi and falls at the moment of victory
11. **§11 Unit C, ch. 11 Ibrahim in the Peloponnese — Papaflessas** — Egypt's Ibrahim lands with a modern army and burns the Morea; the monk Papaflessas refuses his offers and dies fighting at Maniaki
12. **§12 Unit C, ch. 12 The second siege of Missolonghi — Dionysios Solomos** — starving Missolonghi holds a year against Kioutachis and Ibrahim, then makes its Exodus by night; Solomos turns its free besieged into the national hymn
13. **§13 Unit C, ch. 13 The struggles of Karaiskakis** — after Missolonghi falls, Karaiskakis campaigns to save Athens, retakes Attica town by town, and is killed before the Acropolis he tried to relieve
14. **§14 Unit C, ch. 14 Philhellenism** — over 1,200 Europeans
15. **§15 Unit C, ch. 15 The intervention of the Great Powers and the Battle of Navarino** — Britain, France and Russia, pushed by philhellene opinion, send the fleets that sink the Ottoman-Egyptian navy at Navarino in 1827
16. **§16 Unit C, ch. 16 The National Assemblies and the political organisation of the Struggle** — while fighting, the Greeks argue constitutions at Epidaurus, Astros and Troezen
17. **§17 Unit C, ch. 17 Ioannis Kapodistrias and his work** — the Corfiot ex-foreign minister of Russia lands as Governor, builds schools, an orphanage, a currency and an army
18. **§18 Unit C, ch. 18 The end of the Revolution and Greek independence** — the London Protocol of 1830 recognises a small independent Greece
19. **§19 Unit C exercise dossier (The Great Revolution)** — gap-fill on Moldavia's rising, fighters sorted by theatre, Alamana set beside ancient Thermopylae, Ibrahim's route mapped from Egypt, and the Revolution's own acrostic
20. **§20 §7 The Filiki Etaireia and the proclamation of the Greek Revolution in the Danubian Principalities** — the secret Society crosses the Prut with Ypsilantis in February 1821 and the Greek rising begins
21. **§21 §8 The course of the Greek Revolution (1821–1827)** — Tripolitsa, Dervenakia, Missolonghi, civil wars and Ibrahim
22. **§22 §9 The revolutionary Greeks' first efforts to build a state** — assemblies at Epidaurus and Astros draft constitutions while the war still rages
23. **§23 §10 The Greek Revolution and Europe** — governments condemn, peoples volunteer
