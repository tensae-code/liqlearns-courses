# The greek revolution 1821 1830

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "The greek revolution 1821 1830" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit C, ch. 1 The Filiki Etaireia

Three merchants in Odessa found a secret society in 1814, swear members by oath, and spin a web of initiates across the diaspora.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit C, ch. 2 The uprising in Moldavia-Wallachia

Alexandros Ypsilantis crosses the Prut calling the Balkans to rise; the Sacred Band dies at Dragatsani and the northern rising is crushed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit C, ch. 3 The revolution in the Peloponnese

The Morea rises in March 1821.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit C, ch. 4 The revolution in Central Greece

Chieftains swear the rising at Levadeia, Odysseas Androutsos holds the inn at Gravia, and Athanasios Diakos dies impaled after Alamana.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit C, ch. 5 The revolution in the Aegean islands

Spetses rises first under Bouboulina; Hydra, Psara and Samos turn merchant fleets into a navy under Miaoulis and Papanikolis.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit C, ch. 6 The revolution in Epirus, Thessaly and Macedonia

Returned Souliots strike in Epirus while the Karatasos father and son raise Olympus and Chalkidiki, and a young Macedonian exile writes home of the struggle.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit C, ch. 7 The fall of Tripolitsa

Kolokotronis rings the Ottoman capital of the Morea; after months of siege the city falls in a storm of killing that shadows the victory.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit C, ch. 8 The struggles of Kanaris

With only merchant ships against great war fleets, Greek captains send in fireships.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit C, ch. 9 Dramali's campaign — Dervenakia

Dramali's great army pours into the Morea and starves in the Argos plain; Kolokotronis destroys it in the Dervenakia passes.

## Sub lesson 10 — Unit C, ch. 10 Markos Botsaris

After the disaster at Peta, Botsaris leads his Souliots in a night attack at Karpenisi and falls at the moment of victory.

## Sub lesson 11 — Unit C, ch. 11 Ibrahim in the Peloponnese — Papaflessas

Egypt's Ibrahim lands with a modern army and burns the Morea; the monk Papaflessas refuses his offers and dies fighting at Maniaki.

## Sub lesson 12 — Unit C, ch. 12 The second siege of Missolonghi — Dionysios Solomos

Starving Missolonghi holds a year against Kioutachis and Ibrahim, then makes its Exodus by night; Solomos turns its free besieged into the national hymn.

## Sub lesson 13 — Unit C, ch. 13 The struggles of Karaiskakis

After Missolonghi falls, Karaiskakis campaigns to save Athens, retakes Attica town by town, and is killed before the Acropolis he tried to relieve.

## Sub lesson 14 — Unit C, ch. 14 Philhellenism

Over 1,200 Europeans.

## Sub lesson 15 — Unit C, ch. 15 The intervention of the Great Powers and the Battle of Navarino

Britain, France and Russia, pushed by philhellene opinion, send the fleets that sink the Ottoman-Egyptian navy at Navarino in 1827.

## Sub lesson 16 — Unit C, ch. 16 The National Assemblies and the political organisation of the Struggle

While fighting, the Greeks argue constitutions at Epidaurus, Astros and Troezen.

## Sub lesson 17 — Unit C, ch. 17 Ioannis Kapodistrias and his work

The Corfiot ex-foreign minister of Russia lands as Governor, builds schools, an orphanage, a currency and an army.

## Sub lesson 18 — Unit C, ch. 18 The end of the Revolution and Greek independence

The London Protocol of 1830 recognises a small independent Greece.

## Sub lesson 19 — Unit C exercise dossier (The Great Revolution)

Gap-fill on Moldavia's rising, fighters sorted by theatre, Alamana set beside ancient Thermopylae, Ibrahim's route mapped from Egypt, and the Revolution's own acrostic.

## Sub lesson 20 — §7 The Filiki Etaireia and the proclamation of the Greek Revolution in the Danubian Principalities

The secret Society crosses the Prut with Ypsilantis in February 1821 and the Greek rising begins.

## Sub lesson 21 — §8 The course of the Greek Revolution (1821–1827)

Tripolitsa, Dervenakia, Missolonghi, civil wars and Ibrahim.

## Sub lesson 22 — §9 The revolutionary Greeks' first efforts to build a state

Assemblies at Epidaurus and Astros draft constitutions while the war still rages.

## Sub lesson 23 — §10 The Greek Revolution and Europe

Governments condemn, peoples volunteer.
