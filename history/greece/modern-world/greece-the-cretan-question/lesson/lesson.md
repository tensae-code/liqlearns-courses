# Greece the cretan question

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Greece the cretan question" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Cretan question (1821–1905)

Crete rises again and again against Ottoman rule, wins autonomy under Prince George, and waits for union with Greece.

## Sub lesson 2 — Worksheet 4: Toward the end of Ottoman rule (19th century)

Tanzimat reforms and the Halepa charter of 1878 loosen the old order; Christian townspeople rise as the Muslim landowning class declines.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Cretan Question

Crete's repeated risings force Europe to grant autonomy while union with Greece waits.

## Sub lesson 4 — Theme 4 A: The struggle of the national uprising in Crete (1821–1830)

Crete rises with the mainland in 1821, is crushed by Egyptian troops, and is left outside the first Greek state.

## Sub lesson 5 — Theme 4 B: The period 1830–1866

A decade of Egyptian rule, the failed 1841 revolt and quiet conspiracies keep the union demand alive.

## Sub lesson 6 — Theme 4 C: The Great Cretan Revolution (1866–1869)

The Arkadi explosion shakes Europe; three years of war end not in union but in an Organic Law conceding self-government.

## Sub lesson 7 — Theme 4 D: The Cretan question in the last period of Turkish rule (1869–1898)

The Halepa Pact of 1878, new revolts in 1889 and 1895–96, the disastrous 1897 war and the Heraklion massacre force the powers to impose autonomy.

## Sub lesson 8 — Theme 4 E: The period of autonomy and the union of Crete with Greece

Prince George and then Zaimis govern the Cretan State, Venizelos rebels at Theriso for union, and in 1913 the island finally joins Greece.
