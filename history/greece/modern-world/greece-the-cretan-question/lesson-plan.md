# Lesson plan — Greece the cretan question

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Greece the cretan question
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7 — Two centuries as problems: modern and contemporary history woven with Europe's, the Church's travelling story, and Heraklion investigated (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Greece the cretan question as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 7 — Two centuries as problems: modern and contemporary history woven with Europe's, the Church's travelling story, and Heraklion investigated — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 The Cretan question (1821–1905)** — Crete rises again and again against Ottoman rule, wins autonomy under Prince George, and waits for union with Greece
2. **§2 Worksheet 4: Toward the end of Ottoman rule (19th century)** — Tanzimat reforms and the Halepa charter of 1878 loosen the old order; Christian townspeople rise as the Muslim landowning class declines
3. **§3 The Cretan Question** — Crete's repeated risings force Europe to grant autonomy while union with Greece waits
4. **§4 Theme 4 A: The struggle of the national uprising in Crete (1821–1830)** — Crete rises with the mainland in 1821, is crushed by Egyptian troops, and is left outside the first Greek state
5. **§5 Theme 4 B: The period 1830–1866** — a decade of Egyptian rule, the failed 1841 revolt and quiet conspiracies keep the union demand alive
6. **§6 Theme 4 C: The Great Cretan Revolution (1866–1869)** — the Arkadi explosion shakes Europe; three years of war end not in union but in an Organic Law conceding self-government
7. **§7 Theme 4 D: The Cretan question in the last period of Turkish rule (1869–1898)** — the Halepa Pact of 1878, new revolts in 1889 and 1895–96, the disastrous 1897 war and the Heraklion massacre force the powers to impose autonomy
8. **§8 Theme 4 E: The period of autonomy and the union of Crete with Greece** — Prince George and then Zaimis govern the Cretan State, Venizelos rebels at Theriso for union, and in 1913 the island finally joins Greece
