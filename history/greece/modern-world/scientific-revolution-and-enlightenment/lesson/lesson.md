# Scientific revolution and enlightenment

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Scientific revolution and enlightenment" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 7.I.5 Developments in letters, sciences and arts in the 17th–18th c.

Telescopes, Newton and the philosophes turn the Scientific Revolution into the Enlightenment.

## Sub lesson 2 — §1 The age of the Enlightenment

Reason is turned on throne, altar and tradition, and the philosophes draft the blueprint of the modern world.

## Sub lesson 3 — The age of the Enlightenment

Thinkers question kings and churches, argue for reason and rights, and arm two revolutions with ideas.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Enlightenment movement

Reason is turned on throne and altar, and the rights of man become a programme.

## Sub lesson 5 — Scientific development (15th–18th centuries)

Copernicus moves the Earth, Galileo looks through a telescope, Newton binds heaven and earth in one law.

## Sub lesson 6 — The Enlightenment

Philosophes attack superstition and argue for toleration, rights and separated powers.

## Sub lesson 7 — Intellectual life in the 17th and 18th centuries

The scientific revolution matures into academies, encyclopaedias and salons.
