# From evidence to a written history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From evidence to a written history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Introduction: How we work with the History book

Before any past is told, pupils learn the historian's kit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Appendix of additional sources for the teacher

A bank of written, visual and digital sources (Gombrich on the temples, the Acropolis Museum guide on alliance decrees, Platon on conservation) models how a history is built from evidence.

## Sub lesson 3 — Closing assessment: whole-class evaluation exercises

Pupils must argue the folder's question.

## Sub lesson 4 — How the thematic folder works: the research question and the worksheets

Learners are given a question, not a chapter, and must build the answer out of sources in groups.

## Sub lesson 5 — Appendix: Parathemata (source extracts)

The book closes with numbered documents for each theme, so learners test the narratives against the evidence themselves.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of From evidence to a written history as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
