# Lesson plan — From evidence to a written history

**Course:** History — Greece · **Title:** First steps · **Lesson:** From evidence to a written history
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of From evidence to a written history as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — A thousand years of Byzantium: Constantinople founded, walled, besieged, lost and regained, to 1453 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Introduction: How we work with the History book** — before any past is told, pupils learn the historian's kit
2. **§2 Appendix of additional sources for the teacher** — a bank of written, visual and digital sources (Gombrich on the temples, the Acropolis Museum guide on alliance decrees, Platon on conservation) models how a history is built from evidence
3. **§3 Closing assessment: whole-class evaluation exercises** — pupils must argue the folder's question
4. **§4 How the thematic folder works: the research question and the worksheets** — learners are given a question, not a chapter, and must build the answer out of sources in groups
5. **§5 Appendix: Parathemata (source extracts)** — the book closes with numbered documents for each theme, so learners test the narratives against the evidence themselves

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
