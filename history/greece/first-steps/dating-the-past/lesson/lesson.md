# Dating the past

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Dating the past" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chronological table of the ancient world

The book closes by laying every people's story on one timeline so learners can see who lived when.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chronological tables (appendix)

The book's closing tables lay Greek and world events side by side on one timeline from 1815 to today.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chronological table (appendix)

The closing table pins every style and garment to its dates, a timeline of five centuries of dress.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Dating the past as Greece's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
