# Lesson plan — Whaling Today and Protecting the Whales

**Course:** History — Canada · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Whaling Today and Protecting the Whales
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Whaling in the Arctic: the Inuit Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Whales are now protected and counted — and Inuit whaling continues today as a small, community-run hunt under shared rules.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Whaling in the Arctic: the Inuit Story (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- **Whalers from Across the Sea** (`modern-world/canada-whalers-from-across-the-sea/`) is recommended first — it teaches the large-number hunting that protection answers (recommended, never forced). **Inuit Whaling, the Traditional Way** (`modern-world/canada-inuit-whaling-traditional/`) before that teaches the hunt that continues today.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Whales are now protected and counted — and Inuit whaling continues today as a small, community-run hunt under shared rules.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, telling the Arctic past as the reading content.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of people on the shore counting whales through binoculars as a bowhead's back breaks the surface — the student points to the whale and the counters and says what each is doing
- GIF — a short GIF of a bowhead whale rising slowly, blowing, and sinking back under the Arctic water — the student watches the whale surface and says why people count each one they see
- IMAGE — a picture of a small community boat setting out under a flag, with elders watching from the shore — the student points to the boat and says who runs this hunt and under what

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks protection means the Inuit hunt ended → protection guards the whales, and the hunt goes on inside it: small, run by the community itself, under rules everyone shares. What ended is the old large-number hunting, not the community's hunt.
- Go back to the one thing: Whales are now protected and counted — and Inuit whaling continues today as a small, community-run hunt under shared rules. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
