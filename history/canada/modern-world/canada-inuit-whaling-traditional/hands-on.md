# Hands-on — Inuit Whaling, the Traditional Way

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name the whale's gifts out loud, one by one: maktaq and meat for food, oil for the lamps, bones for tools and sleds. Now say who each gift was for: the whole community. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw a great whale, and around it draw its gifts — a bowl of food, a lamp, a tool, a sled — with a line from the whale to each one. Then draw a small open boat beside it with a crew rowing together. Say each part aloud as you draw it: the gift, and what the community did with it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see one thing feeding or supplying many people, or a team that only works when everyone does their part, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
