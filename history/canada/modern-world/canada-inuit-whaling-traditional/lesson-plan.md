# Lesson plan — Inuit Whaling, the Traditional Way

**Course:** History — Canada · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Inuit Whaling, the Traditional Way
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Whaling in the Arctic: the Inuit Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** For the Inuit of the Arctic, one whale could feed and supply a whole community — maktaq and meat for food, oil for the lamps, bones for tools and sleds — and the old way of the hunt ran on the elders' knowledge and crew teamwork, in open boats with hand harpoons, with the whole camp sharing the catch.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Whaling in the Arctic: the Inuit Story — it stands at the foot of Canada's ladder, on nothing before it in this course (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why hunt whales? What the whale gave** — Inuit hunters have taken whales since long ago because one whale fed and supplied a whole community: bones for tools and sleds, oil for lamps, skin and blubber (maktaq) for food.
2. **Long-ago story: whaling the old way** — Elders' knowledge and crew teamwork in open boats with hand harpoons bring the bowhead in, and the whole camp shares the catch.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: one whale fed and supplied a whole Inuit community, and the old hunt ran on elders' knowledge, teamwork, open boats and hand harpoons, with the catch shared by all.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a meal shared by many from one catch, one hunt or one big cooking, where everyone took a part.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, telling the Arctic past as the reading content.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the hunt was for one family's dinner → go back to the size of the whale: one whale is enormous, and that is the point — it fed and supplied a WHOLE community, not one hunter.
- Mixes up the old way with big ships → the old way is small and human: open boats, hand harpoons, the elders' knowledge, and everyone's hands sharing the catch. The big ships come in the next lesson.
- Go back to the one thing: For the Inuit of the Arctic, one whale could feed and supply a whole community — maktaq and meat for food, oil for the lamps, bones for tools and sleds — and the old way of the hunt ran on the elders' knowledge and crew teamwork, in open boats with hand harpoons, with the whole camp sharing the catch. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
