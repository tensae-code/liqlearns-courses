# Video script — Inuit Whaling, the Traditional Way

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
One whale could feed and supply a whole Inuit community — and the old way of the hunt ran on knowledge, teamwork, and sharing.

Look at the map. Travel far north, to the top of Canada — to Nunavut, in the Arctic, where the sea is cold and the ice comes and goes with the seasons. This is Inuit land, and it has been for a very long time.

Out in that cold sea swims the bowhead — a whale so big that one whale is like a whole storehouse. Why did the hunters go after such a giant? Because of everything it gave. Its skin and blubber — maktaq — and its meat were food. Its oil burned in the lamps, giving light and heat. Even its bones were useful: bones became tools, and bones became sleds. One whale, and the whole community ate, saw by lamplight, worked, and travelled.

But how do you catch a giant with small tools? That is the long-ago story. The crew went out in open boats — no roof, no engine, just the boat, the sea, and the people. They carried hand harpoons. And they carried something even more important: the elders' knowledge — where the whales travel, how the ice and water move, when to go and when to wait. Everyone in the boat had a job, and the boat only worked if the crew worked as one.

And when the bowhead was brought in, the story did not end with the hunters. The whole camp shared the catch. The whale that one crew caught fed everyone.

Remember it this way: one whale, one community — food, light, tools, and a catch shared by all.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
