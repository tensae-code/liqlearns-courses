# Video script — Whalers from Across the Sea

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Ships came from across the sea to hunt the Arctic whales — in large numbers — and Inuit life was changed.

Look at the map. Stay in the far north, in the Arctic waters of Nunavut. For a long time, only one kind of hunting happened here: the Inuit hunt, from small open boats, one whale feeding a whole community.

Now watch the edge of the map. Ships are coming — tall foreign ships, sailing in from across the sea. These are commercial whaling ships: hunting is their business. They are not here to feed one camp. They hunt whale after whale, in large numbers.

What do they take? Oil — barrel after barrel of whale oil. And baleen — the plates from the whale's mouth, which buyers across the sea pay well for. The whales leave the Arctic as cargo.

And what stays behind? Change. The ships bring trade: new goods arrive, and Inuit life begins to deal with the outsiders and their market. But the ships also leave depletion: take whales that fast, and the whales grow fewer. The sea holds less than it held before.

One sea, two kinds of hunting — and the second one changed the first one's world forever.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
