# History — Canada course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Canada inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Canada's book in hand is one book —
a Level 13 reader from Nunavut's Inuktitut reading programme,
where the Arctic past is the reading content itself. Its own
progression is the ladder, and it is one long story in three
turns: first the traditional hunt (why the Inuit hunted
whales, what one whale gave a whole community, and how the
old way worked — elders' knowledge, open boats, hand
harpoons, the catch shared by the whole camp); then the turn
from outside (foreign commercial whaling ships hunting in
large numbers for oil and baleen, changing Inuit life through
trade and depletion); then today (whales protected and
counted, and the Inuit hunt continuing small and
community-run under shared rules). One book, one level — the
ladder stops where the book in hand stops, not because Canada
teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Inuktitut reading-programme book from Nunavut — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Modern World → Whaling in the Arctic: the Inuit Story** —
   the whole arc of Arctic whaling as Nunavut's reader tells
   it, in 3 lessons: the traditional hunt and what the whale
   gave (one lesson with two parts that build in order);
   the whalers who came from across the sea; and whaling
   today, with the whales protected. 3 lessons, in one title:
   `modern-world/` (3 lessons).

**Totals: 3 lessons** (Level 1: 3 — all from one student book).
