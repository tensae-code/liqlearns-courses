# About the History — Canada course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Inuktitut reading-programme book from Nunavut. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Canada inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **one level**:

1. **Level 1 — whaling in the Arctic: the Inuit story.**
   Nunavut's reader tells the Arctic past as one story in
   three turns: the traditional Inuit hunt (why whales were
   hunted, what one whale gave, and the old way of the hunt);
   the foreign commercial whalers who arrived from across
   the sea; and whaling today, with the whales protected and
   counted. The course's 3 lessons all stand on a **student
   book** and count in the acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Whaling in the Arctic:* "First, the old way: one whale
  could feed and supply a whole community — meat and maktaq
  to eat, oil for the lamps, bones for tools and sleds — and
  the hunt ran on the elders' knowledge, open boats, hand
  harpoons, and a catch shared by the whole camp. Then the
  turn: big ships came from across the sea, hunting whales in
  large numbers for oil and baleen, and trade and depletion
  changed Inuit life. Then today: the whales are protected
  and counted, and the Inuit hunt goes on — small,
  community-run, under shared rules."
