# History — Bulgaria course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Bulgaria inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Bulgaria's books in hand are the three Рива (Riva) *История и цивилизации* (History and Civilizations) student books — for the 7th, 6th and 10th grades as filed — together telling one unbroken national story: the Grade 6 book runs medieval Europe and medieval Bulgaria in parallel columns and closes with the Bulgarian lands inside the Ottoman state; the Grade 7 book tells the modern national story from the 18th century to EU membership; the Grade 10 book tells the whole national epic end to end, from the Thracians and the Karanovo mound to Zhivkov and the transition. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder below follows the story as Bulgaria's own books present it, in its own eras: first steps and the ancient Balkans, medieval Europe and the First Bulgarian Empire, the Second Empire and the Ottoman centuries, the Revival and Liberation, the free state and the wars, and communism, 1989 and the Euro-Atlantic road. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Bulgaria's own Рива (Riva) *История и цивилизации* student books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps and the Ancient Balkans** — the historian's craft first (writing history from evidence, numbers and chronology as evidence, legends and elders' stories as sources), then the deep past of the Bulgarian lands — the first humans and fire, the Karanovo farming mound, the Thracians and their golden heritage, the peoples on the move, the Slavs settling the Balkans, and Kubrat's Old Great Bulgaria — 10 lessons.

2. **Medieval Europe and the First Bulgarian Empire** — medieval Europe whole (feudalism's heritage, Byzantium, Charlemagne's Franks, the Arabs and Islam) beside Bulgaria's own founding — Asparuh's state of 681, Krum and Omurtag, Boris I's baptism, Cyril and Methodius and the Slavonic letters, Simeon's empire and the Golden Age, and the fall under Peter and Samuil — closing with the medieval world lived: the Church, the other faiths, knights and castles, village and town, Byzantium's and Bulgaria's daily life, and the culture the Church carried — 18 lessons.

3. **The Second Empire and the Ottoman Centuries** — Bulgaria under Byzantine rule and the Asenevtsi resurrection (Peter and Asen, Kaloyan, Ivan Asen II), Europe's kingdoms, crusades, revived cities and Gothic cathedrals around it, Bulgarian society and its Church in the empire's last centuries, the last century of the Second Empire, then the Ottoman conquest and the centuries inside the empire — its state and society, Bulgarian daily life, the Orthodox Church keeping the people, resistance, and Bulgarian culture without a state — 16 lessons.

4. **Revival, Uprising and Liberation** — the Revival whole — the Ottoman world the Bulgarians wake inside, its economy and society changing, Paisii and Sofroniy the first awakeners, the new schools and Revival culture, the church independence won, the liberation movement organised, the April Uprising of 1876, the Russo-Turkish War and Liberation in 1878, statehood restored, and Tarnovo's constitution-makers building the Principality — 11 lessons.

5. **The Free State, the Wars and the Interwar Years** — the free state built and tested — Unification in 1885, Stambolov's modernisation, the century's close under Stoilov, the Bulgarians left outside the borders, economy and life transformed, full independence in 1908, the Balkan Wars, the First World War, the swing between dictatorship and democracy, interwar diplomacy seeking peaceful revision, the Second World War and the saved Bulgarian Jews, and interwar society and culture — 12 lessons.

6. **Communism, 1989 and the Euro-Atlantic Road** — the communist takeover of 1944 and the totalitarian regime imposed, Zhivkov's three decades, the planned economy and the socialist woman, life behind the Iron Curtain as the Warsaw Pact's most faithful member, society and spirit under the regime, the fall of 10 November 1989 and the transition to democracy, the painful road to a market economy, and NATO and EU membership closing the story — 9 lessons.

**Totals: 76 lessons** (Level 1: 10 · Level 2: 18 · Level 3: 16 · Level 4: 11 · Level 5: 12 · Level 6: 9 — 132 placements stocked; all 76 lessons stand on student books, so every lesson is taught in 1 country (Bulgaria); 51 lessons carry sub lessons).
