# The Barbarian Invasions and the Vikings

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **The Barbarian Invasions and the Vikings** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.


## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 2: The new peoples

New peoples pour into the Roman world and redraw the map of Europe. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lesson 4: The Great Migration of the peoples

Peoples on the move shatter the old Roman order across Europe. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: New peoples pour into the Roman world and redraw the map of Europe. Give one real example: Peoples on the move shatter the old Roman order across Europe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: compare your first map and line with the story you now know, and fix what was wrong.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: New peoples pour into the Roman world and redraw the map of Europe.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
