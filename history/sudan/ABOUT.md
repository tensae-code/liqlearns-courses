# About the History — Sudan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Sudan's own History and History and National Education books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Sudan inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **five levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: What History Is and How We Date It.** Sudan's books open with the method itself: what history is — the story of people's life on earth in the past, made by our ancestors and continued by us — the many names this land carried before it was called Sudan, and the two calendars, Gregorian and Hijri, that hang side by side in Sudanese life as clocks for reading the past. (2 lessons.)
2. **Level 2 — Sudan and Africa to 1500.** The long ancient story, Sudan first: Stone-Age peoples on the Nile 12,000 years ago, Kerma, Kush and Meroe, the Christian Nubian kingdoms, Darfur's stone cities; then Africa around it — Egypt, Axum, Carthage — and the two faiths arriving: Christianity, then Islam, which enters Sudan by treaty and migration, remakes the land in Arabic, and raises the Funj, Abdallab, Taqali and Fur sultanates, with the Islamic kingdoms of the Maghreb, Mali and the Swahili coast beside them. (30 lessons.)
3. **Level 3 — Arabia, the Islamic Caliphates and the Ancient World.** Arabia before Islam — its southern and northern kingdoms and its tribal society — then the Islamic state age by age: the Prophet's state, the four Rashidun caliphs one by one and the problems and administration of their state, the Umayyads and their conquests east to China's edge and west into al-Andalus, the Abbasids and Baghdad's fall to the Mongols, and the civilisation that flowers between; around it, Europe in its dark ages learning from Islam, the Renaissance, Greece and Rome, and the Ottoman state that grows from the medieval world. (21 lessons.)
4. **Level 4 — Sudan from the Sultanates to Independence, and Africa After 1500.** Sudan's own modern arc whole: the Islamic sultanates, the Turco-Egyptian conquest of 1821 and sixty years of rule and resistance, Gordon and the road to the Mahdiyya, the Mahdist revolution and state, the Anglo-Egyptian Condominium, the national movement, and independence in 1956 with the army years after it; beside it, Africa's colonisation — its motives, its kinds, the Berlin Conference, its effects — Usman dan Fodio's Sokoto Caliphate, the Congo from Leopold to Lumumba, decolonisation, and the founding of the OAU. (30 lessons.)
5. **Level 5 — The Modern World: Ottomans, World Wars and the Arab Liberation Century.** The wider modern world as Sudan's books teach it: the Ottoman state from Osman to its decline, the caliphate's abolition and secular Turkey, reform movements in the Islamic world; Europe's revolutions and machines — France 1789, the Industrial Revolution, Italy and Germany uniting; the two world wars, the United Nations founded in 1945, and the Cold War; Sudan after independence — its governments and the South Sudan question; and the Arab world's liberation century — the mandates, Egypt, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Syria and Lebanon, the Maghreb, and Palestine through the 1948 war. (30 lessons.)

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Sudan first, then the world:* "We begin with the calendar itself and with Sudan's own soil — Stone-Age Khartoum, Kerma, Kush, Christian Nubia, Darfur's stone cities. Then Arabia and the caliphs, caliph by caliph. Then Sudan again: the sultanates, the conquest of 1821, the Mahdi, the Condominium, and the flag going up in 1956. And around Sudan's story, the world's: Africa colonised and freed, the Ottomans, two world wars, and the Arab world's liberation century."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Book 1 — Sudan SD-G4** — history begins with the calendar itself, then walks Sudan's own soil in order — Stone-Age Khartoum, Kerma, Kush, Christian Nubia, Darfur's stone cities — before the Rashidun caliphs and a whole unit on Africa, so the child's own land is the spine of the past. *(Student book.)*

**Book 2 — Sudan SD-G9** — a pure History book in four sweeps — Sudan first (the Mahdiyya, the Condominium, the road to independence and the army years), then the Ottoman state, then Africa's colonisation and liberation, then the world wars and the Cold War — the nation always measured against the wider world. *(Student book.)*

**Book 3 — Sudan Grade 5** — a Sudanese child's first history book runs the home story first and longest — Stone Age Sudan, Kerma, Kush, Christian Nubia, the coming of Islam and the Funj and Fur sultanates — then the Islamic state's growth, then Africa, then Europe, so Sudan is the centre the world is viewed from. *(Student book.)*

**Book 4 — Sudan Grade 7** — Grade 7 re-reads the home story at full depth — prehistory, the nameless A/B/C-Group peoples, Kerma, Napata's kings one by one, Meroe — before Arabia's kingdoms and the building of the Islamic state, then Africa's civilisations and finally Greece and Rome. *(Student book.)*

**Book 5 — Sudan Grade 10** — at secondary level the book is two histories bound together — the Islamic state caliph by caliph (Rashidun, Umayyad, Abbasid), then Africa's own long story from before Islam to Ghana's and Algeria's liberation, with Sudan's Islamisation given the longest chapter of all. *(Student book.)*

**Book 6 — Sudan Grade 11** — the modern story is taught as foreign rule, then freedom, then the republic arguing with itself — Turco-Egyptian conquest and resistance, independence, six governments in thirty years, and the South's question running under everything; Europe's modern history follows as the second half. *(Student book.)*

**Book 7 — Sudan Grade 12** — the final year completes the national arc the Grade 11 book skipped — Mahdiyya, Condominium, national movement — then widens to the Ottoman caliphate's rise and fall and the whole Arab world's liberation century, taught as Sudan's own wider family story. *(Student book.)*

**Book 8 — Sudan Grade 6** — Grade 6 gives the child the conquest century whole — invasion, administration, khedives and Gordon — told frankly as foreign rule that built a state while it oppressed, then the Abbasid caliphate as Sudan's wider Islamic past, then Africa's Islamic kingdoms from Fez to Kilwa to Mali, and Europe's rebirth last; the Mahdiyya itself is held back for Grade 9. *(Student book.)*

**Book 9 — Sudan Grade 8** — Grade 8 is the sultanates book: the Arab entry and the Baqt, then each Sudanese Islamic state examined like a machine — how its king was crowned, who judged, what it taxed, what it sold — before the Turkiyya is audited in three lessons (its administration, its plunder, its resistance) and the child is sent on to the Umayyads, Islam's Africa, and Europe's revolutions. *(Student book.)*
