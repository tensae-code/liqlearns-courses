# Lesson plan — Rashidun Caliphate Uthman and Ali

**Course:** History — Sudan · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Rashidun Caliphate Uthman and Ali
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Arabia, the Islamic Caliphates and the Ancient World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Uthman gathers the Quran into one book so its reading is one, and builds a fleet to guard the state's coasts.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Arabia, the Islamic Caliphates and the Ancient World — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Uthman's caliphate** — Uthman gathers the Quran into one book so its reading is one, and builds a fleet to guard the state's coasts
2. **Ali's caliphate** — Ali, the Prophet's cousin raised in his house and the first boy to believe, closes the Rashidun age as the state's map reaches its widest
3. **Uthman part** — One written Qur'an for one growing empire, and grievances gathering at the centre
4. **Ali part** — The fourth caliph inherits a state already cracking, and the ummah splits for good

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Rashidun Caliphate Umars Conquests** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Uthman gathers the Quran into one book so its reading is one, and builds a fleet to guard the state's coasts.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Sudan, Africa and the world you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
