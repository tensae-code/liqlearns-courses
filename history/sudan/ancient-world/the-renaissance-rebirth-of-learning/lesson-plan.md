# Lesson plan — The Renaissance Rebirth of Learning

**Course:** History — Sudan · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Renaissance Rebirth of Learning
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Arabia, the Islamic Caliphates and the Ancient World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Europe wakes, and the textbook closes the circle that began in its dark ages.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Arabia, the Islamic Caliphates and the Ancient World — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The European Renaissance** — Europe wakes, and the textbook closes the circle that began in its dark ages
2. **The European Renaissance** — Learning is reborn and the modern world begins
3. **The Literary and Artistic Renaissance in Europe** — Within two centuries Europe's mind changes its language, its art and its picture of the heavens
4. **The Age of the European Renaissance** — Europe's rebirth is assembled from refugee scholars, a machine, free cities and Arab libraries
5. **The European Literary Renaissance** — Nation by nation, Europe's writers stop writing in Latin and start writing for their own peoples
6. **The European Artistic Renaissance** — The same rebirth, in stone and paint

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Europe wakes, and the textbook closes the circle that began in its dark ages.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Sudan, Africa and the world you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
