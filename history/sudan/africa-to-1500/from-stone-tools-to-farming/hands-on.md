# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Stone-Age civilisation in Sudan; The Ancient Civilisations of Sudan; Human Civilisation in Prehistoric Times; Sudan in the Stone Ages. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Our ancestors lived on the Nile's banks 12,000 years ago, and the Khartoum people of 8000–3200 BCE hunted, fished and made pottery where the capital stands today.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Our ancestors lived on the Nile's banks 12,000 years ago, and the Khartoum people of 8000–3200 BCE hunted, fished and made pottery where the capital stands today. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
