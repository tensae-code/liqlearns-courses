**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Sudan and Africa to 1500 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Kush civilisation** — From Napata the Kushite kings — Piankhy, who conquers Egypt, and his grandson Taharqa — rule from the first cataract to the sixth; then the capital…
2. **The Kush Civilisation** — The kings of Kush turn conqueror, wear Egypt's crown, and leave more pyramids than Egypt itself
3. **Models of the Ancient Civilisations of Africa (Meroe part)** — An iron-making African capital writes in its own script before an Axumite king ends it
4. **The Two Kingdoms of Kush (Napata part)** — An occupied land learns empire from inside the palace, then turns the lesson on its teachers
5. **The Most Important Kings of Napata (a) and (b)** — King by king, the Sudanese court at Napata becomes the court of Egypt itself
6. **The Kingdom of Meroe** — Kush steps out of Egypt's shadow into its own rainy heartland and trades with three worlds
7. **Ancient Sudan part** — The home antiquity retold at secondary depth

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: From Napata the Kushite kings — Piankhy, who conquers Egypt, and his grandson Taharqa — rule from the first cataract to the sixth;.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Sudan, Africa and the world you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
