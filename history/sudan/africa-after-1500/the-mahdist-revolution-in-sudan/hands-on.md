# Hands-on — The Mahdist Revolution in Sudan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The Mahdist Revolution 1881–1885, Its Causes and the Reasons for Its Success; The Liberation of El Obeid (1882) and the Hicks Pasha Expedition (November 1883); The Mahdist Revolution in Eastern Sudan, and The Liberation of Khartoum. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: A holy man with no army declares the empire's rule finished, and Sudan believes him.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A holy man with no army declares the empire's rule finished, and Sudan believes him. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
