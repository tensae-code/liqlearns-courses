# Video script — The Constitution and Democracy, 2008

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
A royal Kasho of 4 September 2001 orders a written constitution; the drafting committee delivers its first draft on 9 December 2002; the King tours the country discussing it, abdicates in 2006; National Council elections follow in December 2007, and the Constitution is signed on 18 July 2008.

Bhutan's democracy begins as a royal order. On 4 September 2001 the fourth King issues a Kasho — a royal decree — commanding a written constitution for Bhutan. The drafting committee delivers its first draft on 9 December 2002. Then the King does the thing no law required: he tours the country, discussing the draft with his people, district by district. In 2006 he abdicates, handing the throne to his son so the new reign and the new order begin together. National Council elections follow in December 2007 — and on 18 July 2008 the Constitution is signed. The Kathrim's long thread, from the legal-legacy lesson, reaches its end: Bhutan's supreme law, written, discussed and consented to.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
