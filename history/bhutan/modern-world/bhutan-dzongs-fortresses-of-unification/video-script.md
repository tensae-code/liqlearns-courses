# Video script — Dzongs: Fortresses of Unification

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Built of stone, wood and mud without nails around a towering central utse, Bhutan's dzongs are sorted into early medieval, medieval and modern — and read as the physical record of Bhutan being unified.

Stand inside a dzong and you are standing inside Bhutan's unification, built. The Class VIII book reads the architecture closely: walls of stone, wood and mud fitted without nails, rising around a towering central tower, the utse. Then it sorts the dzongs the way a historian sorts evidence — into early medieval, medieval and modern periods — and reads the sequence as a physical record: each generation of dzongs marks a stage of the country coming together, region by region, under one order. The Zhabdrung's state was not only proclaimed; it was constructed, and its construction still stands.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
