# Hands-on — Dzongs: Fortresses of Unification

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Build a tower in your mind with no nails at all — stone, wood and mud, fitted so well it stands for centuries. Then ask what such a building says about the country that raised dozens of them. This lesson reads dzongs as a record.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Built of stone, wood and mud without nails around a towering central utse, Bhutan's dzongs are sorted into early medieval, medieval and modern — and read as the physical record of Bhutan being unified. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
