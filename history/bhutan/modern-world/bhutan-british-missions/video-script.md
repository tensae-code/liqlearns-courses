# Video script — The British Missions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Hunting a trade road to Tibet, Britain sends mission after mission into Bhutan — Bogle in 1774, who plants potatoes at Tashichhodzong; Hamilton; Turner in 1783, whose sketches show Bhutan to the world; Kishan Kant Bose; Pemberton in 1838, rejected; and Ashley Eden in 1864, whose humiliation lights the fuse of war.

Britain wants a road. Behind Bhutan lies Tibet, and behind Tibet, in British hopes, the trade of inner Asia — so for ninety years the missions climb the passes. George Bogle comes first, in 1774, and leaves a gift still in Bhutanese fields: potatoes, planted at Tashichhodzong. Hamilton follows; then Samuel Turner in 1783, whose sketches show Bhutan to the outside world for the first time. Kishan Kant Bose comes as an Indian agent of the Company; Pemberton in 1838 is rejected. And in 1864 Ashley Eden's mission ends in humiliation — a forced signature, a wet ride home, and a grievance in London. The missions were meant to open a road; the last one opens a war instead.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
