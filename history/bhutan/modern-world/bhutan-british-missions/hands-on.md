# Hands-on — The British Missions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Six visitors over ninety years, each knocking on the same mountain door for the same reason: a road to Tibet. Line them up in order and watch the mood darken. This lesson is that line-up.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Hunting a trade road to Tibet, Britain sends mission after mission into Bhutan — Bogle in 1774, who plants potatoes at Tashichhodzong; Hamilton; Turner in 1783, whose sketches show Bhutan to the world; Kishan Kant Bose; Pemberton in 1838, rejected; and Ashley Eden in 1864, whose humiliation lights the fuse of war. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
