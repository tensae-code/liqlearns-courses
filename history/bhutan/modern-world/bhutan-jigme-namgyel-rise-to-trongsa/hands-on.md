# Hands-on — Jigme Namgyel: The Rise to Trongsa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A fifteen-year-old walks alone across the mountains to a fortress town to seek his fortune, carrying nothing but his name and his nerve. Follow his climb, rung by rung — this lesson counts the rungs.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A Pema Lingpa descendant born in 1825 walks to Trongsa at fifteen and rises from garpa retainer to zimpon by saving his Ponlop from assassins at Punakha — wearing the raven-headed helmet that becomes the prototype of the royal crown — until in 1853 he is made Trongsa Ponlop himself. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
