# Video script — Jigme Namgyel: The Rise to Trongsa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
A Pema Lingpa descendant born in 1825 walks to Trongsa at fifteen and rises from garpa retainer to zimpon by saving his Ponlop from assassins at Punakha — wearing the raven-headed helmet that becomes the prototype of the royal crown — until in 1853 he is made Trongsa Ponlop himself.

The Class VII book tells the rise like an adventure. Jigme Namgyel, a descendant of Pema Lingpa, is born in 1825 and walks to Trongsa at fifteen to seek his fortune. He starts at the bottom, as a garpa, a retainer — and climbs by being the man others can trust when it matters most: as zimpon, chamberlain, he saves his Ponlop from assassins at Punakha. He crushes the eastern revolt from Mongar. And in 1853 the climb reaches its rung: Jigme Namgyel is made Trongsa Ponlop himself, master of the fortress at Bhutan's centre — the seat from which Bhutan's future will be decided.
The Class VIII book adds the details that turn a career into a legend. The chamberlain twice shields the Trongsa Ponlop from assassins at Punakha. He wears the helmet of Lama Jangchub Tsondru, headed with a raven — the bird of Bhutan's guardian deity — and that helmet becomes the prototype of the royal Raven Crown his descendants will wear. In 1850 he marches east and reunifies the rebellious Sharchog Khorlo Tsibgye districts under Trongsa for the last time. Every piece of the future monarchy — the seat, the crown, the reunified east — is being assembled by this one climbing man.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
