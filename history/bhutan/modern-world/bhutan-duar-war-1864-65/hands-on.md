# Hands-on — The Duar War, 1864–65

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Four armies march at once against the foothills. The defenders cannot match their rifles — so they cut the water instead. This lesson tells Bhutan's hardest war and its bravest stand.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Britain attacks in four columns in November 1864 and takes the eighteen Duars — but Jigme Namgyal cuts the water at Dewathang and captures two howitzers before superior British rifles retake the forts and force a settlement. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
