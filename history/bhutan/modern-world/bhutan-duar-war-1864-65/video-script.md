# Video script — The Duar War, 1864–65

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Britain attacks in four columns in November 1864 and takes the eighteen Duars — but Jigme Namgyal cuts the water at Dewathang and captures two howitzers before superior British rifles retake the forts and force a settlement.

In November 1864 the fuse Eden lit burns down. Britain attacks the Bhutanese foothills in four columns at once and takes the eighteen Duars — the fertile doorways between the hills and the plains. But the war's famous stand belongs to Bhutan: at Dewathang, Jigme Namgyal — the Trongsa Ponlop of the earlier lessons — cuts the fort's water supply and captures two British howitzers, a humiliation the empire does not forget. Courage, though, cannot outlast industry: superior British rifles retake the forts one by one, and Bhutan is forced toward a settlement. The Duars are lost; the terms come next, at Sinchula.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
