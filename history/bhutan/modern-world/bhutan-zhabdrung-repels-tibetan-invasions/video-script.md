# Video script — The Zhabdrung Repels the Tibetan Invasions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Tibet invades the new Bhutan five times — the third invasion in 1639 — each time with local allies inside Bhutan, and the young state survives its maker, whose death is concealed while the invasions and intrigues continue.

Unification has a price, and Tibet presents the bill. The Class IX book counts five Tibetan invasions of the Zhabdrung's new state — the third in 1639 — and each invasion is uglier than a border war, because each time the invaders find local allies inside Bhutan itself: lords who preferred the old patchwork to the new unity. The state survives them all. And it survives something stranger: its maker's own death, concealed for years while invasions and intrigues continue, so that enemies and allies alike must reckon with a Zhabdrung who is no longer alive. The dual system's first proof is that it can outlive the man who built it — under fire.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
