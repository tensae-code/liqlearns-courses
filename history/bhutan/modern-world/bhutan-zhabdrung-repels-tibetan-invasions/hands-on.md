# Hands-on — The Zhabdrung Repels the Tibetan Invasions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A new state, barely built, is attacked five times by the great power it broke away from — and some of its own people side with the attackers. How does it survive? This lesson counts the invasions.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Tibet invades the new Bhutan five times — the third invasion in 1639 — each time with local allies inside Bhutan, and the young state survives its maker, whose death is concealed while the invasions and intrigues continue. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
