# Hands-on — A Democratic Constitutional Monarchy, 2008

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two ceremonies complete one year: a constitution signed in July, a king crowned in November. The youngest book in this course already knew the name for the result. This final lesson says the name and what it means.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From 2008 Bhutan is a Democratic Constitutional Monarchy — parties, an elected Assembly and the King as head of state — completed on 6–7 November 2008 when Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck, the People's King, is formally crowned. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
