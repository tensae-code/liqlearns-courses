# A Democratic Constitutional Monarchy, 2008

## Do it first

Two ceremonies complete one year: a constitution signed in July, a king crowned in November. The youngest book in this course already knew the name for the result. This final lesson says the name and what it means.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

The Class VI book, written for Bhutan's youngest citizens, states the result in one clean sentence: a kingdom ruled as a monarchy until 2006 votes itself a new order, and from 2008 Bhutan is a Democratic Constitutional Monarchy — political parties, an elected Assembly, and the King as head of state. It is the form the whole course has been travelling toward: the dual system's heir, the reforming kings' destination.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

The Class X book completes the year with its crowning. On 6–7 November 2008 Bhutan formally crowns Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck — the People's King — in the ancient ceremonies, months after the Constitution he reigns under was signed. The two acts belong together: the crown is placed on a head the Constitution has already defined. Bhutan's school story ends where its citizens now live — a king who reigns by a written constitution his own father gave away power to create, in the world's youngest democracy of its kind.
