# Hands-on — The Zhabdrung: The Dual System and the Dzongs

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
One ruler must run both a religion and a government. Draw two columns: what belongs to the monks, what belongs to the officials. The Zhabdrung drew exactly that line in 1651 — this lesson reads his drawing.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The unifier gives Bhutan its lasting shape: a distinct Drukpa Kagyu order, national dress and driglam namzha, laws, taxes and the great dzongs — and in 1651 the Chhoe-sid dual system, Je Khenpo for religion and Desi for temporal rule, under the Kathrim code whose spiritual law is 'like a silken knot' and secular law 'like a golden yoke'. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
