# Video script — The Hereditary Monarchy Begins, 1907

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
On 17 December 1907, with the theocratic dual system plainly failing and the last Druk Desi stepped down in 1906, the state council accepts Ugyen Dorji's proposal and seals the genja oath enthroning Gongsar Ugyen Wangchuck as the first hereditary Druk Gyalpo — the date Bhutan keeps as National Day.

Bhutan's youngest learners meet the monarchy as a symbol first: in the Class V book, National Day itself is the lesson. After the theocratic centuries of the dual system, 17 December 1907 crowns Gongsar Ugyen Wangchuck as the first hereditary Druk Gyalpo — Dragon King — and the date becomes the country's National Day, the birthday of the modern kingdom, celebrated every year since.
The Class VIII book gives the crowning its reasons. The dual system is plainly failing: the last Druk Desi has stepped down in 1906, and the office that once held Bhutan together now produces rivalry without rule. The state council accepts the proposal of Ugyen Dorji, and on 17 December 1907 seals the genja — the solemn oath-document — enthroning Ugyen Wangchuck as first Druk Gyalpo. The new king wastes no time justifying the choice: the 1910 Treaty of Punakha doubles his British subsidy, and he opens Bhutan's first modern schools. Hereditary monarchy begins not as a grab for power but as a settlement — one king, one line, an end to the contest for the top.
