# Hands-on — The Zhabdrung's Legal Legacy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Follow one thread: a law code written in 1651, a supreme law written in the 1900s, a constitution signed in 2008. Three documents, one thread. This short lesson pulls it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Kathrim tradition is traced forward through the Thrimzhung Chenmo to the 2008 Constitution — Bhutan's modern supreme law grows, in the books' telling, out of the Zhabdrung's 1651 code. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
