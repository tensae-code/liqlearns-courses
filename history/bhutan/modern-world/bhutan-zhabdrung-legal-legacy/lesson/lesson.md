# The Zhabdrung's Legal Legacy

## Do it first

Follow one thread: a law code written in 1651, a supreme law written in the 1900s, a constitution signed in 2008. Three documents, one thread. This short lesson pulls it.

The Class X book ends its Zhabdrung chapter by looking forward three and a half centuries. The Kathrim, his 1651 legal code, does not die with the dual system: its tradition is traced forward into the Thrimzhung Chenmo, the supreme law of the monarchy years, and from there into the Constitution of 2008, the modern supreme law promulgated under the fifth King. Bhutan's law, in this telling, is one long conversation — the silken knot and the golden yoke of 1651 still audible in the constitution under which Bhutanese vote today.

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Kathrim tradition is traced forward through the Thrimzhung Chenmo to the 2008 Constitution — Bhutan's modern supreme law grows, in the books' telling, out of the Zhabdrung's 1651 code. Give one real example: a school rulebook revised by each generation of students but keeping its first page — the Kathrim thread in miniature, and name the lesson's key words: Kathrim, Thrimzhung Chenmo, Constitution, legacy, supreme law. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
