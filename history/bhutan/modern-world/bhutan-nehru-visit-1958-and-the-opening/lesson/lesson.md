# Nehru's Visit, 1958, and the Opening

## Do it first

A prime minister crosses the highest passes on earth on horseback to visit a kingdom with almost no roads. At Paro, the king translates his speech himself. Hear what the guest promises. This lesson listens at Paro.

In September 1958 the outside world rides into Bhutan in person. Jawaharlal Nehru, prime minister of India, crosses the Himalayan passes with his daughter Indira Gandhi — there are barely roads to drive — and at Paro a great crowd gathers to hear him. The King himself translates the speech, sentence by sentence, and the promise at its heart is the one Bhutan most needs: India wishes Bhutan to remain independent, and to choose its own path. The books mark the visit as the hinge of the century: Bhutan's self-imposed isolation, kept for generations behind its mountains, begins to end — on Bhutan's own terms, spoken aloud by its king.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In September 1958 Jawaharlal Nehru rides over the Himalayan passes with Indira Gandhi and tells the crowd at Paro — in a speech the King himself translates — that India wishes Bhutan to remain independent and choose its own path; self-imposed isolation begins to end. Give one real example: a respected visitor who tells a small neighbour 'your choices are your own' — the Paro promise in everyday terms, and name the lesson's key words: Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Paro 1958, independence, end of isolation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
