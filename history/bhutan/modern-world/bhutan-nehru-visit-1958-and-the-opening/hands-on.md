# Hands-on — Nehru's Visit, 1958, and the Opening

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A prime minister crosses the highest passes on earth on horseback to visit a kingdom with almost no roads. At Paro, the king translates his speech himself. Hear what the guest promises. This lesson listens at Paro.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In September 1958 Jawaharlal Nehru rides over the Himalayan passes with Indira Gandhi and tells the crowd at Paro — in a speech the King himself translates — that India wishes Bhutan to remain independent and choose its own path; self-imposed isolation begins to end. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
