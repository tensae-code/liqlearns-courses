# Video script — Nehru's Visit, 1958, and the Opening

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
In September 1958 Jawaharlal Nehru rides over the Himalayan passes with Indira Gandhi and tells the crowd at Paro — in a speech the King himself translates — that India wishes Bhutan to remain independent and choose its own path; self-imposed isolation begins to end.

In September 1958 the outside world rides into Bhutan in person. Jawaharlal Nehru, prime minister of India, crosses the Himalayan passes with his daughter Indira Gandhi — there are barely roads to drive — and at Paro a great crowd gathers to hear him. The King himself translates the speech, sentence by sentence, and the promise at its heart is the one Bhutan most needs: India wishes Bhutan to remain independent, and to choose its own path. The books mark the visit as the hinge of the century: Bhutan's self-imposed isolation, kept for generations behind its mountains, begins to end — on Bhutan's own terms, spoken aloud by its king.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
