# Jigme Wangchuck's Tax Reforms

## Do it first

A tax so heavy that families abandon their homes to escape it is not revenue — it is ruin. The second king reads the tax rolls and starts abolishing. This lesson lists what he tears down.

The Class IX book's second portrait of Jigme Wangchuck is a king reading complaints. He finds a tax system so heavy that families flee their own homes to live beyond the taxman's reach — and a state that taxes people into flight is eating its own future. So he dismantles: whole layers of drungkhags, the sub-district offices, are taken away. He abolishes the Tsatong, the tax on vacant houses that punished the very families already driven off. And he ends the Sarim Woola, the labour relay that forced villagers to carry officials' loads day and night, village to village. Reform, in this chapter, is a king lifting weights off his people's backs, one named burden at a time.

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Finding taxes so heavy that families flee beyond the taxman's reach, the second King dismantles whole layers of drungkhags and abolishes the Tsatong vacant-house tax and the Sarim Woola labour relay that forced villagers to carry loads day and night. Give one real example: any unfair charge or chore at home or school that a fair leader scraps once they see its real cost — the tax reforms in miniature, and name the lesson's key words: tax reforms, drungkhags, Tsatong, Sarim Woola, relief. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
