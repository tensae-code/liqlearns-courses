# Video script — Jigme Wangchuck's Tax Reforms

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Finding taxes so heavy that families flee beyond the taxman's reach, the second King dismantles whole layers of drungkhags and abolishes the Tsatong vacant-house tax and the Sarim Woola labour relay that forced villagers to carry loads day and night.

The Class IX book's second portrait of Jigme Wangchuck is a king reading complaints. He finds a tax system so heavy that families flee their own homes to live beyond the taxman's reach — and a state that taxes people into flight is eating its own future. So he dismantles: whole layers of drungkhags, the sub-district offices, are taken away. He abolishes the Tsatong, the tax on vacant houses that punished the very families already driven off. And he ends the Sarim Woola, the labour relay that forced villagers to carry officials' loads day and night, village to village. Reform, in this chapter, is a king lifting weights off his people's backs, one named burden at a time.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
