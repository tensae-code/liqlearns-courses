# Hands-on — The Treaty of Sinchula, 1865

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A treaty that costs a country a fifth of its land — and buys it peace and a reopened road south. Was it a defeat or a rescue? Bhutan's books hold both truths at once. This lesson weighs them.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: On 11 November 1865 Bhutan permanently cedes all the Duars — a fifth of its territory — for peace, free trade and an annual British subsidy rising to Rs 50,000; and the Class X book re-reads the settlement through Jigme Namgyel, the man who fought the war and then rebuilt the relationship. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
