# The Indo-Bhutan Treaty, 1949

## Do it first

The empire at your border vanishes and a new republic takes its seat. Every old treaty is suddenly a question. Bhutan's answer comes in 1949. This lesson watches the renegotiation.

On 15 August 1947 India becomes independent, and every arrangement Bhutan made with British India — above all the 1910 Treaty of Punakha, with its guidance clause — is suddenly in doubt. Bhutan steps onto the new Asia's stage at once, attending the Asian Relations Conference, and in 1949 signs a fresh treaty with India. The Class IX book gives the 1949 treaty its full weight: it anchors Indo-Bhutan relations — the friendship, trade and guidance-in-new-form between the Himalayan kingdom and its great southern neighbour — and the anchor holds for nearly sixty years, until the revision of 2007 retells the relationship between equals.
