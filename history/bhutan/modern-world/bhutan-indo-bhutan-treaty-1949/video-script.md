# Video script — The Indo-Bhutan Treaty, 1949

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
With India independent on 15 August 1947 and the 1910 treaty's future in doubt, Bhutan attends the Asian Relations Conference and signs the 1949 treaty with India that anchors Indo-Bhutan relations until its 2007 revision.

On 15 August 1947 India becomes independent, and every arrangement Bhutan made with British India — above all the 1910 Treaty of Punakha, with its guidance clause — is suddenly in doubt. Bhutan steps onto the new Asia's stage at once, attending the Asian Relations Conference, and in 1949 signs a fresh treaty with India. The Class IX book gives the 1949 treaty its full weight: it anchors Indo-Bhutan relations — the friendship, trade and guidance-in-new-form between the Himalayan kingdom and its great southern neighbour — and the anchor holds for nearly sixty years, until the revision of 2007 retells the relationship between equals.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
