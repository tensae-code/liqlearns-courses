# Video script — The First Five-Year Plan, 1961

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Road-building from Phuentsholing to Paro starts in 1960, and in 1961 Bhutan launches its first Five-Year Plan — fully supported by India at 1,747 lakh rupees, with over 1,000 km of roads following.

The opening becomes a programme. In 1960 the road-builders start on the great link from Phuentsholing, on the Indian plains, up to Paro — the road Nehru's cavalcade had wished for. In 1961 Bhutan launches its first Five-Year Plan, the country's first written, dated plan for its own development, fully supported by India at 1,747 lakh rupees. The plan's first fruit is the thing Bhutan lacked most: roads — over 1,000 kilometres of them follow, stitching the sealed valleys into one reachable country. Every Bhutanese plan since, and the kingdom's whole development story, counts from this first one.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
