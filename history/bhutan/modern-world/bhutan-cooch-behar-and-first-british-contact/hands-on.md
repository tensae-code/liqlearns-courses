# Hands-on — Cooch Behar and the First British Contact

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A small kingdom seizes a neighbouring raja — and wakes a giant it has never dealt with: the British East India Company. Trace the chain from capture to treaty. This lesson follows it link by link.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bhutan's capture of Cooch Behar's raja in 1772 brings Captain John Jones's British troops south against it — Bhutan loses the foothill lands, Desi Zhidar flees to Tibet, and the Ten Points Treaty of 25 April 1774 turns enemies into trading friends, opening exclusive trade and ending duties on Bhutanese traders in Rangpur. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
