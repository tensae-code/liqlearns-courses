# Operation All Clear, 2003

## Do it first

Foreign armed groups have camped in your country's southern jungles for years, and talking has failed. The king does not send his army — he leads it. This lesson tells Bhutan's 2003 crisis.

Bhutan's hardest modern crisis grows quietly in its southern jungles. Three Indian militant groups — ULFA, the NDFB and the KLO — camp inside Bhutan's forests, using the kingdom as a base beyond India's reach. Bhutan talks first, for years: the camps must go. In late 2003 the talks fail, and the fourth King makes the decision the Class X book remembers above all his others: he personally leads the Royal Bhutan Army into the south and flushes the militants out. The books call it his selfless, self-sacrificing act for national security — a king who would give away his crown within three years first risks his life for the country's safety.
