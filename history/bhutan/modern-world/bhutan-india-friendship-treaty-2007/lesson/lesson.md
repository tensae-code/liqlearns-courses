# The Bhutan–India Friendship Treaty, 2007

## Do it first

A new king's first foreign visit is a message before a word is spoken. In February 2007 Bhutan's fifth King goes first to India — and signs away the word 'guidance'. This lesson reads the new treaty.

The fifth King's reign opens with a signature abroad. In February 2007 Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck makes India his first foreign visit — honouring the old friendship by going first to Delhi — and signs the revised Bhutan–India Friendship Treaty. The revision is small in words and enormous in meaning: the 1949 treaty's guidance clause, the heir of Punakha's 1910 bargain, is replaced with mutual cooperation between equals. Bhutan and India remain closest friends; what ends is the tutelage. The Class X book sets the treaty where it belongs — as the foreign-relations twin of the constitution being drafted at home: a country rewriting its terms with the world and with itself in the same years.
