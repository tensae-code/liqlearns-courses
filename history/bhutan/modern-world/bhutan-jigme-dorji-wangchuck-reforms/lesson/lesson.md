# Jigme Dorji Wangchuck's Reforms

## Do it first

Four dates, four foundations: 1953, 1956, 1959, 1968. An assembly, a freedom, a supreme law, a high court and a bank. This lesson lays the four cornerstones in order.

The third King, Jigme Dorji Wangchuck, is the books' Father of Modern Bhutan — and the title is earned in dated acts. In 1953 he establishes the National Assembly, the Tshogdu: the country's first national talking-and-deciding body. In 1956 he abolishes serfdom, unbinding the people still tied to masters. In 1959 comes the Thrimzhung Chenmo, the supreme law — the Kathrim's heir, from the earlier lesson. And in 1968 he completes the court ladder with the High Court and founds the Bank of Bhutan, giving the modern state its justice and its money in a single year. Assembly, freedom, law, courts, bank: the skeleton of modern Bhutan is this one reign's work.
