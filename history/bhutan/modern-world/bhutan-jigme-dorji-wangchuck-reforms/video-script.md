# Video script — Jigme Dorji Wangchuck's Reforms

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The Father of Modern Bhutan builds the modern state in fifteen years: the National Assembly (Tshogdu) in 1953, serfdom abolished in 1956, the Thrimzhung Chenmo supreme law in 1959, and the court ladder completed with the High Court and the Bank of Bhutan in 1968.

The third King, Jigme Dorji Wangchuck, is the books' Father of Modern Bhutan — and the title is earned in dated acts. In 1953 he establishes the National Assembly, the Tshogdu: the country's first national talking-and-deciding body. In 1956 he abolishes serfdom, unbinding the people still tied to masters. In 1959 comes the Thrimzhung Chenmo, the supreme law — the Kathrim's heir, from the earlier lesson. And in 1968 he completes the court ladder with the High Court and founds the Bank of Bhutan, giving the modern state its justice and its money in a single year. Assembly, freedom, law, courts, bank: the skeleton of modern Bhutan is this one reign's work.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
