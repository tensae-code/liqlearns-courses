# Decentralisation, 1981

## Do it first

Power that sits only in the capital cannot hear a village. In 1981 Bhutan's king starts pushing power downhill — to districts, then to village blocks. This lesson follows the power down.

Democracy in Bhutan is rehearsed before it is proclaimed, and the rehearsal is decentralisation. Beginning with the Dzongkhag Yargay Tshogdu, the district development assemblies, the fourth King formalises the devolution of power in 1981: dzongdags, district heads, are appointed to carry real responsibility, and planning is pushed down — from the capital to the twenty districts, and onward to the gewogs, the village blocks where Bhutanese actually live. The Class X book names the purpose plainly: districts and gewogs are the training ground for democracy. A people practised in deciding locally for twenty-five years will not be strangers to deciding nationally in 2008.
