# Hands-on — The Zhabdrung Arrives and Unifies Bhutan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A young religious prince must choose: surrender the sacred relic that proves who he is, or flee over the highest mountains on earth. He dreams of a raven pointing south. This lesson follows his road into Bhutan.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ngawang Namgyal, the Zhabdrung — born at Ralung in Tibet in 1594, driven south by a dispute over his incarnation and the relic he would not surrender — enters a Bhutan of warring local rulers through Gasa in 1616, and dzong by dzong brings the valleys under one rule. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
