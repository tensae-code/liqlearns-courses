# The Zhabdrung Arrives and Unifies Bhutan

## Do it first

A young religious prince must choose: surrender the sacred relic that proves who he is, or flee over the highest mountains on earth. He dreams of a raven pointing south. This lesson follows his road into Bhutan.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

Bhutan's unifier begins as Tibet's prince of religion. Born in 1594 at Ralung, seat of the Drukpa lineage, Ngawang Namgyal — the Zhabdrung, 'the one at whose feet one submits' — grows up as the lineage's holder. In 1616 he enters a Bhutan that is no country yet at all: a patchwork of valleys ruled by warring local lords, coming in through Gasa in the north. What he brings is not an army but a claim, a faith and a will — and from that entry, the land begins, slowly, to come under one rule.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

The Class VIII book tells why he had to come. Enthroned at Ralung as the incarnation of Kunkhen Pema Karpo, the young Zhabdrung is challenged by a rival claimant backed by the powerful Tsangpa ruler. The price of peace is the Rangjung Kharsapani, the self-created sacred relic that proves his incarnation — and he refuses to surrender it. Then comes the sign the books remember: a dream of a raven, Bhutan's guardian bird, leading him south. He turns the horse southward, and exile becomes destiny.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

The Class IX book picks him up on Bhutanese soil. The exile reaches Lhomon, the old southern land, and sets to work as Thuchen Ngawang Namgyal: dzong by dzong, valley by valley, he brings the western valleys under one rule. It is unification by fortress, law and faith together — each new dzong a seat of his government and his monk body at once. The state every later Bhutanese book inherits — Palden Druk, glorious Bhutan — is founded here, by a refugee who would not give up a relic.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ngawang Namgyal, the Zhabdrung — born at Ralung in Tibet in 1594, driven south by a dispute over his incarnation and the relic he would not surrender — enters a Bhutan of warring local rulers through Gasa in 1616, and dzong by dzong brings the valleys under one rule. Give one real example: anyone who has had to leave home to keep what matters most — the Zhabdrung's flight in ordinary life, and name the lesson's key words: Zhabdrung, Ngawang Namgyal, Ralung, Rangjung Kharsapani, Gasa. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
