# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing in your home that came from a grandparent, and one song, saying or habit that came with it. One you can touch, one you cannot. You have just split heritage in two — this lesson names both halves.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Heritage is what a people hands down — tangible things you can touch, like dzongs, relics and sacred sites, and intangible beliefs, rituals, songs and conduct — and Bhutan's books teach it as a living identity each generation must carry on. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
