# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

**Course:** History — Bhutan · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: What History Is and What We Inherit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Heritage is what a people hands down — tangible things you can touch, like dzongs, relics and sacred sites, and intangible beliefs, rituals, songs and conduct — and Bhutan's books teach it as a living identity each generation must carry on.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: What History Is and What We Inherit (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 4.1-4.3 Culture and Tradition / Sacred Places of Bhutan** — The Class VI book gives heritage its shape.
2. **Chapter Three, Cultural Heritage: Bhutanese National Expressions** — The Class VII book widens the same teaching to the whole country.
3. **Chapter Six, Driglam Choesum: Bhutanese Ethos** — The Class IX book brings heritage down into daily conduct.
4. **Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity** — The final book closes the circle: the identities the Zhabdrung created centuries ago — the dress, the etiquette, the architecture, the law — are presented as a still-living national heritage.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Heritage is what a people hands down — tangible things you can touch, like dzongs, relics and sacred sites, and intangible beliefs, rituals, songs and conduct — and Bhutan's books teach it as a living identity each generation must carry on.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a family's old cooking pot (tangible) and the grace said before meals (intangible) — both inherited, both heritage.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Bhutan, as the books themselves work.
