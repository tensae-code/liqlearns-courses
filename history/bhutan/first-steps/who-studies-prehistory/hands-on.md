# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pick up any old stone or tool you can find near home. Hold it and ask: if this could talk, what would it tell us about the hands that made it? That asking is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Where no writing exists, Bhutan's first people are read from things: namchag stone adzes, household fossils and megaliths like the Nabji oath pillar, sorted into Stone, Bronze and Iron Age periods. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
