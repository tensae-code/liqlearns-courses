# Video script — Who Studies Prehistory

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Where no writing exists, Bhutan's first people are read from things: namchag stone adzes, household fossils and megaliths like the Nabji oath pillar, sorted into Stone, Bronze and Iron Age periods.

Bhutan has no written records before the 7th century, so its first chapter is prehistory — the past read from things instead of pages. The finds are real and touchable: namchag, polished stone adzes dated to about 2000–1500 BC; fossils left in old households; and great standing stones, megaliths, like the oath pillar at Nabji, where people once swore their promises. Scholars sort this material world into periods by what people had learned to work — first stone, then bronze, then iron. Each age is a step in skill, and each object is a sentence in a book nobody wrote.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
