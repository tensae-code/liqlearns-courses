# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Bhutan · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: What History Is and What We Inherit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is the tool that explains what happened, when, why and how — the living past, rebuilt from sources — so the present can be understood and the future planned.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: What History Is and What We Inherit (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 5 introduction, Our Country** — Bhutan's youngest history learners meet the subject head-on: history is the tool that explains what happened, when it happened, why it happened and how it happened.
2. **Introduction** — The older book says it more deeply: history is the living past — people's own attempt, century after century, to reconstruct, describe and interpret what came before them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course. Nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History is the tool that explains what happened, when, why and how — the living past, rebuilt from sources — so the present can be understood and the future planned.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a grandmother explaining not only the year the family moved to its village, but why they moved and what the journey cost — the dates plus the reasons are history.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Bhutan, as the books themselves work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks History is just memorising dates → Dates are the frame, not the house: the lesson's history asks why and how, and tests every answer against its sources.
- Go back to the one thing: History is the tool that explains what happened, when, why and how — the living past, rebuilt from sources — so the present can be understood and the future planned. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
