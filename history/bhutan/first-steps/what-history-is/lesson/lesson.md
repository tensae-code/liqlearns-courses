# What History Is

## Do it first

Write down one thing that happened in your family long before you were born. Now write four questions beside it: what happened? when? why? how do you know? Those four questions are this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

Bhutan's youngest history learners meet the subject head-on: history is the tool that explains what happened, when it happened, why it happened and how it happened. It is not a drawer of old dates. Used well, it makes the present understandable — you can see why your village, your language and your country stand where they stand — and it helps people plan the future with their eyes open. Every later lesson in this course is this tool at work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

The older book says it more deeply: history is the living past — people's own attempt, century after century, to reconstruct, describe and interpret what came before them. Because it is rebuilt from sources, it is never one flat voice: there is historiography, the study of how histories are written, and there can be more than one honest interpretation of the same evidence. The historian's craft is to weigh the sources, not to invent what they cannot show.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History is the tool that explains what happened, when, why and how — the living past, rebuilt from sources — so the present can be understood and the future planned. Give one real example: a grandmother explaining not only the year the family moved to its village, but why they moved and what the journey cost — the dates plus the reasons are history, and name the lesson's key words: history, sources, historiography, interpretation, the living past. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
