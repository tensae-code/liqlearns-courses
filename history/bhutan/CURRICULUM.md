# History — Bhutan course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Bhutan inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Bhutan's books in hand are seven —
the integrated *Social Studies* books for Classes IV, V and VI,
then *Bhutan History, Civics & Citizenship Education* as a
subject of its own for Classes VII, VIII, IX and X — and the
placements file's book order is the teaching order, earliest
books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the eras as
Bhutan's own books present them: the historian's first steps —
what history is, the prehistory read from stones, and heritage
as the ground note every later book repeats (Level 1); the
ancient land — the faith before Buddhism, the spoken tales, the
clan valleys, the Buddha, the first temples, Guru Rinpoche, the
many names, the arriving schools, the first dzong, the thirteen
arts and Pema Lingpa (Level 2); the Zhabdrung's 17th-century
founding — arrival, dual system, dzongs, invasions survived and
the legal legacy (Level 3); Trongsa, the British encounter and
the road to kingship, 1825–1910 (Level 4); the reforming kings
and the opening to the world, 1927–1971 (Level 5); and the
living settlement — Gross National Happiness, decentralisation,
the 2003 crisis, the 2007 treaty between equals, and the
constitution and democracy of 2008 (Level 6). The ladder is a
recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Social Studies and History books for Classes IV–X (Royal
Government of Bhutan) — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: What History Is and What We Inherit** — the integrated Social Studies books open the course: what history itself is (Class IV, deepened at Class VIII), the prehistory read from stones where no writing exists (Class VII), and heritage — tangible and intangible — as the course's ground note, sounded at Classes VI, VII, IX and X. 3 lessons, in one title: `first-steps/` (3 lessons).

2. **Ancient Bhutan: Faith, Temples and Makers** — the ancient story as Classes IV, VII and VIII tell it: the faith before Buddhism, the spoken tales, the clan valleys, the Buddha's own story, the first temples, Guru Rinpoche, the land's many names, the arriving schools, the first dzong, the thirteen arts, and Pema Lingpa whole — revealer, treasures and lineages. 13 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (13 lessons).

3. **The Zhabdrung Unifies Bhutan** — the 17th-century founding: the Zhabdrung's flight and arrival, the dual system and its Kathrim law, the dzongs read as unification in stone, the five Tibetan invasions survived, and the legal thread that runs to 2008. 5 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (5 lessons).

4. **Trongsa, the British Encounter and the Road to Kingship** — the long 19th century and the crown it produces: Jigme Namgyel's climb, holding and Desi-ship; Cooch Behar, the missions, the Duar War and Sinchula; Ugyen Wangchuck's last civil battle, his diplomacy and knighthood, the 1907 enthronement and the 1910 Punakha settlement. 11 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (11 lessons).

5. **The Reforming Kings and the Opening** — the monarchy at work and the kingdom opening: the second King's consolidation and tax relief, the 1949 treaty with independent India, the third King's reforms, Nehru's 1958 visit, the first Five-Year Plan of 1961 and the UN seat of 1971. 7 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (7 lessons).

6. **Happiness, Decentralisation and Democracy** — the living settlement: the fourth King's Gross National Happiness, decentralisation as democracy's training ground, Operation All Clear, the 2007 treaty between equals, and the constitution and coronation of 2008 that close the school story. 6 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (6 lessons).

**Totals: 45 lessons** (Level 1: 3 · Level 2: 13 · Level 3: 5 · Level 4: 11 · Level 5: 7 · Level 6: 6 — 61 placements, all from seven student books; 12 lessons carry sub lessons).
