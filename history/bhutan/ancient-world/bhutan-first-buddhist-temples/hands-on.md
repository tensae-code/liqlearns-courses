# Hands-on — The First Buddhist Temples

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find Bumthang and Paro on a map of Bhutan — one in the centre, one in the west. Nearly fourteen centuries ago, a foreign king planted a temple in each. Why would two buildings start a country's recorded history? This lesson explains.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Tibetan king Songtsen Gampo (c.605–650) builds Jampa Lhakhang in Bumthang and Kyichu Lhakhang in Paro — and Bhutan's books take that 7th-century temple-building as the start of recorded history, the Early Diffusion of Buddhism. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
