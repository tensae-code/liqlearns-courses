# Early Inhabitants: Clans and Livelihoods

## Do it first

Look at a map of Bhutan's valleys in your mind: each one walled off by ridges. Imagine living where the next valley is a hard day's climb away. How would your people organise themselves? This lesson answers with clans.

Bhutan's mountains made its first society. Cut off from each other valley by valley, the early inhabitants lived in clan settlements — kin groups under their own chieftains, owing loyalty to family and locality before anything larger. Their living was fitted to the heights: herds of yaks moved down to winter pasture and back up with the seasons. In time the clans learned farming, including tseri, the shifting cultivation that clears, crops and rests the land in turn; then came crafts, and barter between neighbours; and at last the valleys reached outward, trading with Tibet to the north and India to the south. One people, many small worlds — that is the Bhutan the saints and unifiers would later enter.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Cut off valley by valley, early Bhutanese lived in clan settlements under chieftains — herding yaks to winter pasture, learning farming and tseri shifting cultivation, then crafts and barter, and finally trade with Tibet and India. Give one real example: families in a remote home valley who still settle disputes and share work through kin — the clan pattern in modern dress, and name the lesson's key words: clans, chieftains, yak herding, tseri, barter. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
