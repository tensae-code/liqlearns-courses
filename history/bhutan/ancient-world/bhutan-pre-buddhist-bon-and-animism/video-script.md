# Video script — Before Buddhism: Bon and Animism

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before Buddhism reached the Himalayas, people took refuge in mountains, forests and lakes, and animist and shamanist practice hardened into Bon — Black Bon with animal sacrifice, White Bon without — much of it surviving later as folk rite inside Buddhist Bhutan.

Long before any temple stood in Bhutan, the Himalayan valleys already had a religion. People took refuge in the powers around them — the high mountains, the deep forests, the still lakes — and animist and shamanist practice, honouring spirits in nature, slowly hardened into an organised faith called Bon. The books split it honestly in two: Black Bon, which kept animal sacrifice, and White Bon, which gave sacrifice up. And Bon did not simply vanish when Buddhism came: much of it survived, folded into the folk rites of Buddhist Bhutan, where an offering at a lake can be far older than the temple on the hill above it.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
