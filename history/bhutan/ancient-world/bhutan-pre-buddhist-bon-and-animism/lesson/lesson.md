# Before Buddhism: Bon and Animism

## Do it first

Stand where you can see a mountain, a forest or a river. People in Bhutan once treated such places as powers to be honoured, not just scenery. Hold that thought — this lesson is about the faith that came before Buddhism.

Long before any temple stood in Bhutan, the Himalayan valleys already had a religion. People took refuge in the powers around them — the high mountains, the deep forests, the still lakes — and animist and shamanist practice, honouring spirits in nature, slowly hardened into an organised faith called Bon. The books split it honestly in two: Black Bon, which kept animal sacrifice, and White Bon, which gave sacrifice up. And Bon did not simply vanish when Buddhism came: much of it survived, folded into the folk rites of Buddhist Bhutan, where an offering at a lake can be far older than the temple on the hill above it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Before Buddhism reached the Himalayas, people took refuge in mountains, forests and lakes, and animist and shamanist practice hardened into Bon — Black Bon with animal sacrifice, White Bon without — much of it surviving later as folk rite inside Buddhist Bhutan. Give one real example: offerings still left at a local lake or tree in your own area — the old refuge in nature, living on inside a Buddhist land, and name the lesson's key words: Bon, animism, shamanism, Black Bon, White Bon. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
