# Hands-on — Oral Tradition and the Earliest Stories

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Tell a partner the oldest story anyone has ever told you — from a grandparent, a neighbour, anyone. No book, no phone. That chain of telling is exactly how Bhutan's earliest stories survived. This lesson follows two of them.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before writing, Bhutan's past travelled mouth to ear: the tale of exiled Prince Drimed Kunden, who gave his eye and his children away on the road through Trongsa, still lives in the place-names Changra and Bubja — and the monk Tonpa's 5th-century journey hints Buddhism reached Bhutan before Tibet. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
