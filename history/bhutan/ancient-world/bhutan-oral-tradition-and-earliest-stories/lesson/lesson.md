# Oral Tradition and the Earliest Stories

## Do it first

Tell a partner the oldest story anyone has ever told you — from a grandparent, a neighbour, anyone. No book, no phone. That chain of telling is exactly how Bhutan's earliest stories survived. This lesson follows two of them.

Bhutan's first history was carried in mouths, not books. Its most famous travelling tale is Prince Drimed Kunden's: an exiled prince on the road through Trongsa who gives so completely that he gives his own eye to a blind man and his children to strangers who ask. The story did not fade — it settled into the land itself, in the place-names Changra and Bubja, which still carry the tale for anyone who asks why they are called that. Beside the tale stands a quieter clue: the monk Tonpa's journey in the 5th century, hinting that Buddhism may have reached Bhutan even before it reached Tibet. Oral culture, the book insists, is not the absence of history — it is history's first archive.

Give one real example: a place near home whose name comes from a story everyone tells — the name is the story's footprint, and name the lesson's key words: oral tradition, Drimed Kunden, Trongsa, Changra and Bubja, Tonpa. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
