# Video script — Oral Tradition and the Earliest Stories

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before writing, Bhutan's past travelled mouth to ear: the tale of exiled Prince Drimed Kunden, who gave his eye and his children away on the road through Trongsa, still lives in the place-names Changra and Bubja — and the monk Tonpa's 5th-century journey hints Buddhism reached Bhutan before Tibet.

Bhutan's first history was carried in mouths, not books. Its most famous travelling tale is Prince Drimed Kunden's: an exiled prince on the road through Trongsa who gives so completely that he gives his own eye to a blind man and his children to strangers who ask. The story did not fade — it settled into the land itself, in the place-names Changra and Bubja, which still carry the tale for anyone who asks why they are called that. Beside the tale stands a quieter clue: the monk Tonpa's journey in the 5th century, hinting that Buddhism may have reached Bhutan even before it reached Tibet. Oral culture, the book insists, is not the absence of history — it is history's first archive.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
