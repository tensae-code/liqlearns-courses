# Video script — Pema Lingpa's Lineages

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The tertön who revealed thirty-two treasures leaves more than texts: his Peling lineages — from Sumthrang Choeje to the Dungkar Choeje family — supply Bhutan's later rulers, linking the 15th-century treasure revealer directly to Jigme Namgyal and the Wangchuck kings.

The Class IX book returns to Pema Lingpa for his longest legacy. The tertön revealed thirty-two treasures — but he left more than texts. His descendants, the Peling lineages, run through Bhutan's later history like a golden thread: from the Sumthrang Choeje down to the Dungkar Choeje family, Peling blood supplies the country's later rulers. The line reaches Jigme Namgyal, the Trongsa unifier of the lessons ahead, and through him the Wangchuck kings themselves. So the 15th-century blacksmith's grandson of Bumthang stands, by descent, behind the modern monarchy — treasure revealer and dynastic root in one.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
