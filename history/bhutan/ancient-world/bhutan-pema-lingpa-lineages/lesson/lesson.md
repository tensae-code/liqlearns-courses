# Pema Lingpa's Lineages

## Do it first

A family tree can be a country's history in disguise. Draw yours back three generations. Now imagine one ancestor whose descendants include the founders of a royal dynasty. This lesson traces that tree in Bhutan.

The Class IX book returns to Pema Lingpa for his longest legacy. The tertön revealed thirty-two treasures — but he left more than texts. His descendants, the Peling lineages, run through Bhutan's later history like a golden thread: from the Sumthrang Choeje down to the Dungkar Choeje family, Peling blood supplies the country's later rulers. The line reaches Jigme Namgyal, the Trongsa unifier of the lessons ahead, and through him the Wangchuck kings themselves. So the 15th-century blacksmith's grandson of Bumthang stands, by descent, behind the modern monarchy — treasure revealer and dynastic root in one.

Give one real example: a trade, faith or saying in your own family that has travelled down five generations — a lineage in miniature, and name the lesson's key words: Peling, lineages, Sumthrang Choeje, Dungkar Choeje, thirty-two treasures. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
