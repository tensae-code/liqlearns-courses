# Hands-on — Pema Lingpa's Lineages

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A family tree can be a country's history in disguise. Draw yours back three generations. Now imagine one ancestor whose descendants include the founders of a royal dynasty. This lesson traces that tree in Bhutan.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The tertön who revealed thirty-two treasures leaves more than texts: his Peling lineages — from Sumthrang Choeje to the Dungkar Choeje family — supply Bhutan's later rulers, linking the 15th-century treasure revealer directly to Jigme Namgyal and the Wangchuck kings. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
