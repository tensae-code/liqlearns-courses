# Hands-on — The Buddhist Schools Arrive

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine teachers arriving in your valley from over the mountains, each with a slightly different way of teaching the same faith, and no one in charge to choose between them. For six centuries, that was Bhutan. This lesson watches the contest settle.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From the 11th to the 17th century, with no central authority to stop them, Tibetan masters of the Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya and Geluk traditions settle valley by valley — until Nyingma and Drukpa Kagyu absorb or outlast the rest, and Drukpa Kagyu becomes the state religion. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
