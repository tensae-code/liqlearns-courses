# Hands-on — Bhutan: The Many Names of the Land

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down every name you have ever been called — full name, short name, family nickname. Each name says something about who called you and when. Countries are the same. This lesson reads Bhutan's names like a life story.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: One land, many names, each one a chapter: Lhomon, the southern land of darkness before Buddhism; the four-gates land; Menjong, land of medicinal herbs; the land of cypresses; until Druk Yul, the Thunder Dragon land, and Bhutan, the high land, give it its lasting names. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
