# Hands-on — Zorig Chusum: The Thirteen Arts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Count on your fingers the skilled crafts you have seen with your own eyes — weaving, carving, painting, building, smithing. Bhutan counts thirteen traditional arts and teaches them as one inheritance. This lesson meets two of their masters.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Thirteen traditional arts grow out of Buddhist and pre-Buddhist practice in Bhutan, carried by master craftsmen — 13th-century weaver Khandro Sonam Peldon, and Thangtong Gyalpo, who forged eight iron-chain bridges from Bhutanese ore. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
