# Video script — Zorig Chusum: The Thirteen Arts

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Thirteen traditional arts grow out of Buddhist and pre-Buddhist practice in Bhutan, carried by master craftsmen — 13th-century weaver Khandro Sonam Peldon, and Thangtong Gyalpo, who forged eight iron-chain bridges from Bhutanese ore.

Bhutan's heritage has hands. Zorig Chusum — the thirteen traditional arts — grow out of Buddhist and pre-Buddhist practice: the crafts that build temples, weave cloth, paint sacred images and work metal, carried down by master craftsmen who teach with their hands. The books name two masters across the centuries. In the 13th century, the weaver Khandro Sonam Peldon carries the art of weaving to its height. And Thangtong Gyalpo, the great builder-saint, forges eight iron-chain bridges from Bhutanese ore — engineering in service of pilgrims, slung over gorges no ordinary bridge could cross. The thirteen arts are heritage you can use: a cloth, a bridge, a painted wall.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bhutan.
