# Zorig Chusum: The Thirteen Arts

## Do it first

Count on your fingers the skilled crafts you have seen with your own eyes — weaving, carving, painting, building, smithing. Bhutan counts thirteen traditional arts and teaches them as one inheritance. This lesson meets two of their masters.

Bhutan's heritage has hands. Zorig Chusum — the thirteen traditional arts — grow out of Buddhist and pre-Buddhist practice: the crafts that build temples, weave cloth, paint sacred images and work metal, carried down by master craftsmen who teach with their hands. The books name two masters across the centuries. In the 13th century, the weaver Khandro Sonam Peldon carries the art of weaving to its height. And Thangtong Gyalpo, the great builder-saint, forges eight iron-chain bridges from Bhutanese ore — engineering in service of pilgrims, slung over gorges no ordinary bridge could cross. The thirteen arts are heritage you can use: a cloth, a bridge, a painted wall.
