# Guru Rinpoche Brings Buddhism

## Do it first

A king is sick, and no local cure works — so the court sends far away for a master said to be born from a lotus. Picture the messenger's road. This lesson follows the master who answers the call.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

Bhutan's Class IV book tells the coming of its most beloved figure plainly. The lotus-born Padmasambhava — Guru Rinpoche, the Precious Master — enters Bhutan in the 8th century. In Bumthang he heals the sick king by subduing the local deity Shelging Karpo, the power troubling the kingdom. At Kurjey he leaves the print of his own body in the rock, a signature in stone. And before he goes, he hides treasures — teachings sealed away for discoverers of later ages. From his visit, Buddhism flourishes across the country: the faith now has a founder-figure every Bhutanese child knows by name.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter One, The Continued Development of Unique Bhutanese Identity

The Class VIII book deepens the same visit. Invited around 750 by the ailing King Sindha of Bumthang, Guru Rinpoche travels by way of Nabji, subduing the local spirits as he comes, and plants tantric Buddhism in the land. Bhutan itself becomes, in his blessing, a beyul — a hidden sanctuary land, seeded with his hidden treasures and ringed by his sacred places, the ney, from Taktshang, the Tiger's Nest above Paro, to Singye Dzong in the far east. The geography of Bhutanese devotion — the pilgrim paths children still walk — is the map this one visit drew.
