# The Dzong System Begins

## Do it first

Picture a building that is half fortress, half monastery, on a ridge above Paro — in the year 1194, centuries before the famous dzongs. Who builds the first one, and why does the idea then sleep for four hundred years? This lesson tells.

Bhutan's most famous buildings begin earlier and smaller than most people guess. In 1194, at Chelkha in Paro, Nyo Gyalwa Lhanangpa of the Lhapa Kagyu raises the first fortified monastic dzong — a single building that is already both things at once: a fortress that can be defended and a monastery that houses a religious community. But the experiment fades. In 1222 Phajo Drukgom Zhigpo brings the Drukpa Kagyu into Bhutan, the Lhapa moment passes, and no one builds dzongs in earnest again. The idea sleeps for four centuries — until the Zhabdrung wakes it and covers the country with dzongs, in the lessons ahead.
