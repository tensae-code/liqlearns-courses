# About the History — Montenegro course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — Montenegro's own
Istorija (History) student books for Grades 6–9 of primary
school. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box
is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Montenegro inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they
prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps and the Ancient World.** The
   historian's first tools first — what history is, how its
   evidence speaks, and how its time is cut and counted — then
   the deep past in the books' own order: stone and metal, the
   river states of the Ancient East and Egypt, Greece from
   Crete to Alexander, Rome from a Latin town to a fallen
   empire, closing on home ground with the Illyrian tribes and
   Roman ruins of Montenegro itself. 19 lessons, 23 placements,
   all on a **student book** and counted in the acceptance
   reckoning below.
2. **Level 2 — The Medieval World and Medieval Montenegro.**
   Europe and home in strict rhythm, as the Grade 7 book
   alternates them: migration and feudalism, Byzantium, the
   Church and Islam, then the Slavs arrive and the book stays
   with Duklja and Zeta — Vojislavljevići, Nemanjići, Balšići,
   Crnojevići — as late-medieval Europe, the Ottoman advance
   and Montenegro's first Ottoman centuries frame it. 18
   lessons, 22 placements (one lesson, The Ottoman Empire,
   carries one part from the Grade 7 book and one from the
   Grade 8 book, in file order), all on **student books** and
   counted below.
3. **Level 3 — Discoveries, Revolutions and Montenegro Builds
   a State.** Two world chapters and two home chapters
   alternating, as the Grade 8 book runs them: discoveries,
   Renaissance, Reformation and the first revolutions, then
   Montenegro 1697–1851 building a theocratic state under the
   Petrović Njegoš prince-bishops; the industrial and
   revolutionary world to 1914, then Montenegro 1851–1914
   racing to catch it — principality, wars, constitution and
   kingdom. 17 lessons, 19 placements, all on a **student
   book** and counted below.
4. **Level 4 — The Twentieth Century and Independence
   Regained.** The double story the Grade 9 book tells
   throughout — every world chapter (the wars, the revolutions,
   the -isms, the Cold War) immediately followed by its
   Montenegrin and Yugoslav chapter — ending where Montenegro's
   own story ends: the breakup of Yugoslavia and the renewal of
   Montenegrin independence. 23 lessons, 27 placements, all on
   a **student book** and counted below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Antiquity, ending at home:* "First, what history itself is —
  then stone and metal, the river kingdoms, Greece and Rome —
  and the ancient year ends standing in Doclea, in Montenegro's
  own hills."
- *The medieval rhythm:* "Europe, then home, then Europe, then
  home: feudalism and Byzantium, then the Slavs arrive and
  Duklja and Zeta rise, fall to greater powers, and hold on as
  the Ottomans close in."
- *Building a state:* "Ships, printing and revolutions remake
  the world — and in the mountains a line of prince-bishops
  turns a theocracy into a state, then a principality, then a
  kingdom with a constitution."
- *The double century:* "Every world storm of the twentieth
  century — two world wars, revolutions, dictatorships, the
  Cold War — is told twice: once for the world, once for
  Montenegro and Yugoslavia, until Montenegro stands alone
  again."

**How the books were read, as filed:** each book is a DAISY
talking-book ZIP (218–339 MB, almost all audio); the complete
text was read from each ZIP's dtbook.xml, fetched by HTTP range
requests against the release asset (central directory + the
dtbook.xml / package.opf / navigation.ncx entries only — the
audio was never downloaded), so no grabbed PDF was ever held
or deleted. Title pages verified from the DAISY front matter
(title + authors as printed in the book) against the record
titles. Printed page numbers are the books' own pagenum
markers in the DAISY text.

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Istorija 6 (Grade 6)** — a world-history year (history
  itself, prehistory, the Ancient East, Greece, Rome) that
  lands, in its final lesson, on home ground — the Illyrian
  tribes and Roman ruins of Montenegro itself, so a Montenegrin
  child ends antiquity standing in Doclea. *(Student book.)*
- **Istorija 7 (Grade 7)** — the medieval year alternates
  Europe and home in strict rhythm — migration, feudalism,
  Byzantium, the Church and Islam, then the Slavs arrive and
  the book stays with Duklja/Zeta (Vojislavljevići, Nemanjići,
  Balšići, Crnojevići) as Europe's late-medieval story frames
  it. *(Student book.)*
- **Istorija 8 (Grade 8)** — two world chapters and two home
  chapters alternate: Europe of discoveries and revolutions,
  then Montenegro 1697–1851 building a theocratic state; the
  world to 1914, then Montenegro 1851–1914 racing to catch it —
  principality, wars, constitution. *(Student book.)*
- **Istorija 9 (Grade 9)** — the twentieth century is taught
  as a double story throughout — every world chapter (the
  wars, the revolutions, the -isms) is immediately followed by
  its Montenegrin/Yugoslav chapter, and the book ends where
  Montenegro's own story ends: the breakup of Yugoslavia and
  the renewal of Montenegrin independence. *(Student book.)*

**A legend handled as the book handles it, as filed:** the
Grade 8 book itself teaches pupils to test a famous national
legend against the evidence — the myth of the "istraga
poturica" (the investigation/extermination of converts) is
examined as a myth in Ch.2.1 (continued), printed p. 40, and is
taught in this course (inside *Montenegro 1697–1773: The
Petrović Njegoš Dynasty Begins*) as exactly that: a legend the
book tests, told as a plot summary, not as fact.
