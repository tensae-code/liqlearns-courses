# Sparta and Athens against Sparta

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Sparta and Athens against Sparta". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Ch.IV §2: The Greek poleis; Ch.IV §5: The Peloponnesian War. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch.IV §2: The Greek poleis

Sparta, Athens, and Greek colonisation around the Mediterranean. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: one people splits into hundreds of rival city-states and plants them on every shore.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch.IV §5: The Peloponnesian War

Sparta exploits the discontent of Athens' allies and the two leading poleis ruin Greece between them. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: the Greeks who beat Persia together spend a generation beating each other.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: sparta, Athens, and Greek colonisation around the Mediterranean; Sparta exploits the discontent of Athens' allies and the two leading poleis ruin Greece between them. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
