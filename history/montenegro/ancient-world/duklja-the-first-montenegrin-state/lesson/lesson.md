# Duklja: The First Montenegrin State

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Duklja: The First Montenegrin State". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Ch.2.1 Duklja (Zeta); Ch.2.2 Zeta in the age of the Vojislavljevići. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch.2.1 Duklja (Zeta)

The first Montenegrin state, the state of the Dukljan Slavs, arises on land once held by Illyrian tribes and then by Roman provinces. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: on Rome's old frontier the Slavs of Duklja build the first state Montenegrins claim as their own.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch.2.2 Zeta in the age of the Vojislavljevići

Stefan Vojislav's line holds Zeta; Prince Mihailo (1046–1081) inherits, and the 1054 division of the Christian Church into Orthodox and Catholic shapes his politics between Byzantium and Rome. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: a small coastal kingdom plays two empires and two churches against each other to stay a kingdom.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
