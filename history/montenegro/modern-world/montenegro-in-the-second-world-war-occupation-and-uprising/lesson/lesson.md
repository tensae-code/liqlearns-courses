# Montenegro in the Second World War: Occupation and Uprising

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Montenegro in the Second World War: Occupation and Uprising". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Ch.4.2 Montenegro in the war to Italy's capitulation: in 1941 the Italians occupy Montenegro, annex Boka Kotorska, attach Ulcinj, Tuzi, Plav, Gusinje and Rožaje to Albania, and plan a nominally independent client state from the rest; Ch.4.4 Montenegro after Italy's capitulation: Italian divisions join the Yugoslav People's Liberation Army. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch.4.2 Montenegro in the war to Italy's capitulation: in 1941 the Italians occupy Montenegro, annex Boka Kotorska, attach Ulcinj, Tuzi, Plav, Gusinje and Rožaje to Albania, and plan a nominally independent client state from the rest

Answered by the July uprising. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: an occupier tries to resurrect Montenegro as a puppet, and Montenegro rises instead.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch.4.4 Montenegro after Italy's capitulation: Italian divisions join the Yugoslav People's Liberation Army

In December 1943 the Italian partisan division "Garibaldi" is formed in Pljevlja — while German units rush in from Albania and Herzegovina. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: yesterday's occupiers become comrades-in-arms in Montenegro's mountains.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
