# 1917 Twice: February and October in Russia

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "1917 Twice: February and October in Russia". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Ch.1.2 The Revolution in Russia. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Russia's ruling circles entered the war to block Austro-German supremacy and to take Constantinople from the Ottomans; war-weariness turns into the February and October revolutions. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: an empire that went to war for the Straits collapses into revolution at home.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: russia's ruling circles entered the war to block Austro-German supremacy and to take Constantinople from the Ottomans; war-weariness turns into the February and October revolutions. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
