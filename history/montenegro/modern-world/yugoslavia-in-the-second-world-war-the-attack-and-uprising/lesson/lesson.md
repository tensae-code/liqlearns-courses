# Yugoslavia in the Second World War: The Attack and Uprising

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Yugoslavia in the Second World War: The Attack and Uprising". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Ch.4.1 The fascist attack on Yugoslavia; Ch.4.3 The liberation war and revolution in Yugoslavia 1941–1945: the occupier's method is to encircle the main Partisan forces with superior strength and destroy them; Ch.4.5 The characteristics of the war in Yugoslavia 1941–1945. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch.4.1 The fascist attack on Yugoslavia

Through the war's first 18 months Yugoslavia tries neutrality and compromise on the Croatian question; the March days of 1941 (the 25th and 27th) end the balancing, and the Axis attacks. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: a coup against joining the Axis brings the Luftwaffe over Belgrade within days.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch.4.3 The liberation war and revolution in Yugoslavia 1941–1945: the occupier's method is to encircle the main Partisan forces with superior strength and destroy them

As in the great offensive against the free territory of the Užice Republic in western Serbia at the end of 1941. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: a guerrilla army survives seven offensives and turns a liberation war into a revolution.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch.4.5 The characteristics of the war in Yugoslavia 1941–1945

The occupiers stir religious and national hatred among Yugoslav peoples and arm collaborators against the People's Liberation Movement, so the anti-fascist struggle also carries elements of civil war. The turn of the story, as the book tells it: Yugoslavia's war is three wars at once.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
