# About the History — United Arab Emirates course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Social Studies and National Education book for Grade 1,
Term 3 volume. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for the United Arab Emirates inside the
History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grade the book comes from appears only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History
is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what
came of it.

The course as built has **one level**:

1. **Level 1 — heritage, the traveller, and the young
   nation.** The Emirates' Grade 1 book teaches history as
   national identity: heritage is a building you visit and
   a founder's instruction you memorise; the child's first
   named person from history is Ibn Battuta, the great
   traveller of the shared Arab past; and the modern nation
   is introduced through the museum that keeps its birth
   certificate and the words of the man who united it,
   Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan — with the story of
   how the country's own schooling began. The course's 3
   lessons all stand on a **student book** and count in the
   acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Heritage, the traveller, and the young nation:* "First,
  what heritage is: the old things our ancestors left us,
  kept in museums and old forts, to be proud of, preserved
  and developed — Sheikh Zayed's own teaching. Then meet
  Ibn Battuta of Morocco, who set out for the Hajj and
  travelled for twenty-seven years, through more than forty
  countries, and wrote it all down. Then the young nation
  itself: seven emirates made one country, the Etihad
  Museum that keeps the union's story, the landmarks old
  and new — and how schooling here grew from one teacher,
  the Mutawa'a, to schools for every child."
