# Lesson plan — The United Arab Emirates: Union and Zayed

**Course:** History — United Arab Emirates · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The United Arab Emirates: Union and Zayed
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Heritage, the Traveller, and the Young Nation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The United Arab Emirates is a young country — younger than the children's grandparents — made by uniting seven emirates, remembered at the Etihad Museum in Dubai, the house of the union, and in the "Zayed taught me" sayings of its founding president, Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan; and the same young country grew its schooling in one lifetime, from the Mutawa'a's circle to Al Qasimiyah School in Sharjah and formal education from 1953.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Heritage, the Traveller, and the Young Nation — it stands at the top of the Emirates' ladder as built (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Emirates is my country: the union and Sheikh Zayed** — A family visits the Etihad Museum in Dubai, the house of the union; the map shows the seven emirates as one country; the landmarks old and new stand for it; and through the whole book run the "Zayed taught me" sayings of the founding president, Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan.
2. **How schooling began in the Emirates** — Before there were school buildings there was the Mutawa'a, who taught the children Qur'an, reading, writing and arithmetic; the first regular school was Al Qasimiyah School in Sharjah; and modern formal education began in 1953.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- **Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters** (`first-steps/heritage-what-it-is-and-why-it-matters/`) is recommended first — it teaches the heritage the union's landmarks carry (recommended, never forced). **Ibn Battuta, Traveller of the Islamic World** (`ancient-world/ibn-battuta-traveller-of-the-islamic-world/`) before that teaches the old world this young country looks back to.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: seven emirates were united into one young country under Sheikh Zayed, its story is kept at the Etihad Museum, and its schooling grew from the Mutawa'a to schools for every child.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a landmark they have seen, a saying of Sheikh Zayed they have heard, their own school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, through a family's museum visit and the book's "Zayed taught me" spreads.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the country is very old → go back to the book's own measure: this state is younger than the children's grandparents. Its birth is so near that a museum keeps its story like a birth certificate, and the man who united it is remembered by name on almost every page.
- Mixes up the seven emirates with seven countries today → they were separate, and the union made them one country: one map, one nation, seven emirates inside it.
- Thinks school here always looked like their school → before the buildings there was one teacher: the Mutawa'a, teaching Qur'an, reading, writing and arithmetic in a circle; the first regular school was Al Qasimiyah in Sharjah, and formal education began in 1953.
- Go back to the one thing: The United Arab Emirates is a young country made by uniting seven emirates under Sheikh Zayed, remembered at the Etihad Museum and in his sayings — and its schooling grew in one lifetime from the Mutawa'a's circle to schools for every child. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
