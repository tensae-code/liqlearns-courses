# Video script — The United Arab Emirates: Union and Zayed

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A young country — younger than the children's grandparents — was made by joining seven emirates into one, and the man who united it is remembered on almost every page of the children's book.

Look at the map. Here is the land by the Gulf, in the south-east of the Arab world. On the map you can count the parts: one, two, three — seven emirates, each with its own ruler and its own towns.

For a long time, that is how it stayed: seven, side by side. Then came the turn. The seven were united into one country — the United Arab Emirates. And the man who led that union, the country's founding president, was Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan.

The story is so near to us that it has its own house. In Dubai stands the Etihad Museum — the museum of the union, the house where the country's birth is kept and told. In the book, a family visits it, the way a family here really can. And on the map inside, the child sees it whole: seven emirates, one country.

Then look at the country's face, old and new together. The Louvre Abu Dhabi. The Burj Khalifa, rising over Dubai. Al Maqta Fort in Umm Al Quwain. The Ajman Museum. Stone forts from the old days, and the tallest building in the world, in one small country. And running through the whole book, on almost every spread, is a heading: "Zayed taught me" — the sayings of Sheikh Zayed, taught to the children as the country's own lessons for life.

Now turn the map back, just a little, and ask: where did the children themselves learn, before this young country had schools? Long ago, there were no school buildings. There was one teacher — the Mutawa'a — and the children sat with him and learned the Qur'an, reading, writing and arithmetic. Then came the first regular school in the country: Al Qasimiyah School, in Sharjah. And in 1953, modern formal education began. Within one lifetime, the country went from the Mutawa'a's circle to schools for every child.

Remember it this way: seven emirates, one country, one founder remembered — and schools that grew from one teacher's circle to every child's classroom.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
