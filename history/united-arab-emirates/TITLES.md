# History — United Arab Emirates course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is the United
Arab Emirates' own — built from the Emirates' Social Studies
and National Education book for Grade 1, Term 3 volume
(student book, Arabic). Inside the course the organisation
is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below sit inside
Level 1, in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grade the book comes from lives only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: at Grade 1, history is
national identity — a visit to the Union Museum, the map of
the seven emirates, Sheikh Zayed's sayings under the heading
"Zayed taught me", and one great traveller of the shared
Arab past, Ibn Battuta, as the child's first named person
from history. The ladder runs the way the story runs in
time: the idea of heritage first, then the traveller of the
old world, then the young modern nation. That ladder IS the
path order (recommended, never forced — a learner can jump
in higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — heritage, the traveller, and the young nation**
(Social Studies and National Education, Grade 1, Term 3
volume, student book):

- `first-steps/` — Level 1, first title. Heritage: What It
  Is and Why It Matters (the museum as the place where the
  old antiquities are preserved, the old forts and museums
  among the country's landmarks, and Sheikh Zayed's teaching
  that the folk heritage the ancestors left must be kept
  with pride, preserved and developed for coming
  generations).
- `ancient-world/` — Level 1, second title. Ibn Battuta,
  Traveller of the Islamic World (the young Moroccan who set
  out for the Hajj and did not stop for twenty-seven years —
  more than forty countries by camel, horse and sailing
  ship, his book of travels, and the title Prince of the
  Muslim Travellers).
- `modern-world/` — Level 1, third title. The United Arab
  Emirates: Union and Zayed (the family's visit to the
  Etihad Museum in Dubai, the house of the union; the map
  of the seven emirates; the landmarks old and new; the
  "Zayed taught me" sayings of the founding president — and
  how schooling itself began in the Emirates, from the
  Mutawa'a to Al Qasimiyah School in Sharjah and formal
  education from 1953).
