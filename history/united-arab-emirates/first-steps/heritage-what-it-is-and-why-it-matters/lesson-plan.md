# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

**Course:** History — United Arab Emirates · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Heritage, the Traveller, and the Young Nation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Heritage is what our ancestors left us — the old things and the folk ways of the people before us — kept in museums, "the place where the old antiquities are preserved", and in the old forts among the country's landmarks; and Sheikh Zayed taught that this rich folk heritage must be held with pride, preserved and developed, so it stays a store for this homeland and for coming generations.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Heritage, the Traveller, and the Young Nation — it stands at the foot of the Emirates' ladder, on nothing before it in this course (recommended, never forced).

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course. Nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: heritage is what the ancestors left us, kept in museums and old forts, and Sheikh Zayed taught us to be proud of it, preserve it and develop it for coming generations.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — an old thing kept in their family, a fort or museum they have seen.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, defining the museum for a six-year-old and teaching heritage as a founder's instruction to memorise.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks heritage is only an idea in a book → in this book heritage is not an idea: it is a building you visit. The museum is the place where the old antiquities are preserved, and the old forts stand among the country's landmarks — you can walk into heritage.
- Thinks old things should be thrown away → Sheikh Zayed's teaching in the book is the opposite: the ancestors left a rich folk heritage, and it must be kept with pride, preserved and developed — a store for this homeland and for coming generations, not rubbish.
- Go back to the one thing: Heritage is what our ancestors left us, kept in museums and old forts, and Sheikh Zayed taught that it must be held with pride, preserved and developed for coming generations. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
