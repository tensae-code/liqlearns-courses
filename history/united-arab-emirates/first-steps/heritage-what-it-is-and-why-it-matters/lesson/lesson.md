# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Think of the oldest thing in your home — something that belonged to your grandparents, or to people even before them. Why does your family still keep it? Say out loud: "Old things from the people before us are not rubbish — they are ours to keep." This lesson tells you what that keeping is called, and where a whole country keeps its old things.

## The story

The word for all of it is **heritage**: what our **ancestors** — the people of our family and our land who lived before us — left behind for us. Their old things, and their folk ways: the way they built, worked, dressed and lived.

And in the Emirates, heritage is not left as an idea in a book. The book takes the child to a building. A **museum**, the book says in words for a six-year-old, is **the place where the old antiquities are preserved** — the place where the old things are kept safe, so that children can come, look at them, and learn where they come from.

The museum is not the only keeper. Among the country's own landmarks stand the **old forts** — strong stone buildings from before the young country existed — and the museums of the emirates. Walk into a fort and you are standing inside heritage itself.

Then the book gives the children their founder's teaching, under its "Zayed taught me" heading. **Sheikh Zayed** taught that our ancestors left us a **rich folk heritage** — and that we must do three things with it: be **proud** of it, **preserve** it, and **develop** it, so that it stays a store for this homeland and for coming generations.

That is why heritage matters: the ancestors left it, the museums and forts keep it, and each new generation is taught — by the founder himself — to hold it with pride and pass it on, grown and alive, to the next.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold Zayed's teaching on three fingers — proud, preserve, develop. Three words, and you have the whole lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: heritage is what our ancestors left us; the museum is the place where the old antiquities are preserved; the old forts stand among the country's landmarks; and Sheikh Zayed taught that our rich folk heritage must be held with pride, preserved and developed, so it stays a store for this homeland and for coming generations. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: heritage, ancestors, museum, preserve. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: find the oldest kept thing in your home, and say the three words aloud — proud, preserve, develop.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw a museum with old objects kept inside it and an old fort beside it, and write under it what Zayed taught about heritage.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — an old thing kept with care, a museum, a fort. Note it.
