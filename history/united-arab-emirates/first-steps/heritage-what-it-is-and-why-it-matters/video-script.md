# Video script — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Heritage is what the people who lived before us left behind — and in the Emirates, a child does not just hear about it. The child goes and visits it.

Look at the map. Stay in the Emirates, and walk into a building with a family. What is this building? The children's book gives the answer in words a six-year-old can hold: a museum is the place where the old antiquities are preserved — where the old things are kept safe, so people can come and see them and learn.

And the museum is not alone. Among the country's own landmarks stand the old forts — strong stone buildings from the days before this young country existed. The forts and the museums together say: this land had a life before us, and we still keep its things.

But why keep them? Here the book gives the children their founder's own teaching. Sheikh Zayed taught that our ancestors left us a rich folk heritage — the old things, and the old ways of the people. And his teaching has three parts: be proud of it. Preserve it — keep it safe. And develop it — let it grow and live on. Why? So that it stays a store for this homeland, and for the generations still to come.

So heritage here is not an idea floating in the air. It is a building you visit, a fort you walk into — and a founder's instruction you memorise and carry.

Remember it this way: the ancestors left it, the museum keeps it, and Zayed taught us — pride, preserve, develop.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
