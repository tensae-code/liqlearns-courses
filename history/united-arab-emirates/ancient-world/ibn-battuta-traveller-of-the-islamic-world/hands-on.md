# Hands-on — Ibn Battuta, Traveller of the Islamic World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the traveller's road out loud, in order: Morocco — Algeria — Egypt — Bahrain — India. Now say how he travelled: on camels and horses, and in sailing ships. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw a simple map line from Morocco to India, mark the places this lesson names on it, and draw a camel at one end and a sailing ship at the other. Under the line, write how long he travelled: twenty-seven years. Say each part aloud as you draw it: the place, and how he moved.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you hear a traveller's story, see a map of far countries, or pass a place named after someone from history, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
