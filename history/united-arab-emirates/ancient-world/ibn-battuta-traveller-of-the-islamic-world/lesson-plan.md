# Lesson plan — Ibn Battuta, Traveller of the Islamic World

**Course:** History — United Arab Emirates · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ibn Battuta, Traveller of the Islamic World
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Heritage, the Traveller, and the Young Nation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ibn Battuta — born in Morocco, his real name Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Muhammad al-Tanji — set out from home for the Hajj and did not stop: for twenty-seven years he crossed the lands on camels and horses and in sailing ships, visited more than forty countries, passed through Algeria, Egypt and Bahrain to India, and wrote down the customs and traditions of the peoples he saw, earning the title Prince of the Muslim Travellers.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Heritage, the Traveller, and the Young Nation (recommended, never forced).

## Before this lesson
- **Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters** (`first-steps/heritage-what-it-is-and-why-it-matters/`) is recommended first — it teaches the heritage this traveller's book became a part of (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Ibn Battuta left Morocco for the Hajj and travelled for twenty-seven years through more than forty countries, and his book became the map of a whole medieval world.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a journey they have taken, a traveller's story they have heard, the Ibn Battuta shopping centre in Dubai if they have seen it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, letting Ibn Battuta introduce himself to the child.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks he travelled for a short holiday → count the years with the learner: twenty-seven years of travel — longer than the learner has been alive, several times over. The Hajj was the start, not the whole journey.
- Thinks he travelled by car or plane → his world moved slowly: camels and horses on land, sailing ships on the sea. Every country on his list cost him days and weeks of travel.
- Go back to the one thing: Ibn Battuta set out from Morocco for the Hajj and did not stop for twenty-seven years, visiting more than forty countries and writing down what he saw, and his book became the map of a whole medieval world. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
