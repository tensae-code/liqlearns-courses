# Video script — Ibn Battuta, Traveller of the Islamic World

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A young man left home for a holy journey — and did not come back for twenty-seven years.

Look at the map. Start far in the west, in Morocco, in North Africa. Here a young man introduces himself: his name is Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Muhammad al-Tanji — the world knows him by his shorter name, Ibn Battuta.

He sets out for the Hajj, the pilgrimage. That was the plan: go, worship, come home. But watch the line on the map. It does not turn back. It goes on — through Algeria, through Egypt, and across the sea and the lands to Bahrain, and on, all the way to India.

How did he travel? The slow way, the only way his world had: on camels and horses over the land, and in sailing ships over the sea. And wherever he stopped, he did something precious: he looked, and he remembered, and later he wrote it down — the customs and traditions of the peoples he saw, how they lived, what they ate, how they prayed and traded and celebrated.

Count what his journeys added up to: twenty-seven years of travel. More than forty countries visited. And at the end of it, a book — the book of his travels — so that people who never left home could travel the whole medieval world inside its pages. That is why he carries the title Prince of the Muslim Travellers. In his book he even described far-away China as one of the safest countries to travel in.

And his name has not been forgotten here: in Dubai stands a great shopping centre named after him — the Ibn Battuta centre — so the children of the Emirates meet his name in their own city.

Remember it this way: one young man, one pilgrimage — and twenty-seven years later, a book that is a map of a whole world.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
