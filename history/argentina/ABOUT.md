# About the History — Argentina course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Ciencias Sociales teacher's notebook. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Argentina inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **one level**:

1. **Level 1 — building the Argentine state, 1860–1916.**
   Argentina's teacher's notebook teaches its History axis
   ("Las sociedades a través del tiempo") through one big
   case: the building of the Argentine state 1860–1916 —
   the national state built over the loose provinces, the
   great immigration the state engineered, and the conquest
   of indigenous territories, with the costs of "progress"
   taught in the same breath as the achievements. The
   course's 3 lessons all stand on a **teacher guide** and
   count nowhere in the acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Building the Argentine state:* "First, the building:
  after decades of division, leaders built one national
  state — army, laws, schools, railways — and photographs
  show Buenos Aires and the pampas transformed in one
  generation, with foreign capital building the railways
  and meat-packing plants of the export boom. Then the
  people: crisis and crowding pushed millions out of
  Europe, and the state pulled them in — Alberdi's 'to
  govern is to populate' made law in 1876, offices in
  Europe, passages paid — until nearly a third of the
  country was foreign-born, mostly from Italy and Spain.
  Then the cost: the same state sent its army against the
  indigenous peoples of the south and the Chaco and took
  their hunting and fishing lands, while immigrant families
  crowded into conventillo tenements — and the book asks
  who progress left out."

## Contents map (the combined book's parts named, not placed)

The book is a combined Ciencias Sociales volume built on
three axes. Only its History axis is placed in this course:

- **History — Axis 2, "Las sociedades a través del tiempo"**
  (the societies through time): the building of the
  Argentine state 1860–1916, printed pp. 59–100 as placed —
  PLACED, as this course's 3 lessons.
- **Geography — Axis 1, "Las sociedades y los espacios
  geográficos"**: Argentina's population today,
  quality-of-life statistics, Latin American comparison,
  Mercosur integration, printed pp. 32–55 — named, not
  placed.
- **Civics/social organisation — Axis 3, "Las actividades
  humanas y la organización social"**: types of integration,
  life on the Mercosur border, the South American gas
  pipeline case, printed pp. 104–129 — named, not placed.
