# History — Argentina course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Argentina inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Argentina's book in hand is one
book — a teacher's notebook (Cuadernos para el aula) for
Ciencias Sociales 6, whose History axis ("Las sociedades a
través del tiempo") is taught through one big case: the
building of the Argentine state, 1860–1916. Its own
progression is the ladder, and it is one story in three
turns: first the state built (one national state over the
loose provinces — army, laws, schools, railways — a country
changing visibly in one generation, its export boom built
with foreign capital); then the people fetched (the great
immigration — Europe's push, Argentina's pull, and the 1876
law that made Alberdi's "to govern is to populate" real);
then the cost counted (the conquest of the indigenous
territories of the south and the Chaco, and the conventillo
crowding of the welcomed newcomers — the book's own question,
who progress left out). One book, one level — the ladder
stops where the book in hand stops, not because Argentina
teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Ciencias Sociales teacher's notebook — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Modern World → Building the Argentine State,
   1860–1916** — the whole arc of the state-building era as
   Argentina's own teacher's notebook tells it, in 3 lessons:
   the national state built and the country changing fast,
   with foreign investment (one lesson with three parts that
   build in order); the great immigration (one lesson with
   two parts that build in order); and the conquest of
   indigenous territories, where the book asks who progress
   left out. 3 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/`
   (3 lessons).

**Totals: 3 lessons** (Level 1: 3 — all from one teacher
guide; acceptance 0, labelled — see ABOUT.md).
