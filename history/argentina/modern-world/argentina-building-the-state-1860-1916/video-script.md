# Video script — Argentina Building the State, 1860–1916

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A loose set of provinces became one country — because leaders built a national state, and the country changed faster than anyone had seen before.

Look at the map. Here is Argentina, in the south of South America: Buenos Aires on the coast, and the wide flat pampas stretching inland. For decades, this land was divided — province against province, no single state over all of it.

Now watch the years run: 1860, 1870, 1880, on to 1916. A national army forms. Laws are written for the whole country. Schools open. And lines spread across the map — railways, tying the pampas to the capital and the ports. The pieces are joining into one country.

Look closer, the way the book does, at the photographs. Buenos Aires: a busier port, new buildings, trains. The pampas: new towns where the trains stop. All of it in one generation — a parent could remember a different country from the one their child grew up in.

But follow the money. Much of it came across the ocean — British and other foreign capital built the railways and the meat-packing plants. Argentina's beef and grain sailed out to markets across the sea, and the new wealth was tied to those markets.

Remember it this way: one state built, one country joined — and its wealth tied by rail and ship to the world.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
