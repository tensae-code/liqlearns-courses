# Lesson plan — Argentina Building the State, 1860–1916

**Course:** History — Argentina · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Argentina Building the State, 1860–1916
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Building the Argentine State, 1860–1916 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** After decades of division, Argentina's leaders built one national state — an army, laws, schools and railways — and turned a loose set of provinces into a single country, with foreign capital building the railways and meat-packing plants of the export boom.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Building the Argentine State, 1860–1916 — it stands at the foot of Argentina's ladder, on nothing before it in this course (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A complex, controversial period: thinking about the State** — After decades of division, Argentina's leaders build one national state — army, laws, schools, railways — and turn a loose set of provinces into a single country
2. **Stories and images of a country changing fast** — Photographs and accounts show Buenos Aires and the pampas transformed in one generation — ports, trains and new towns remake daily life
3. **The State and foreign investment** — British and other foreign capital builds the railways and meat-packing plants of the export boom, tying Argentina's new wealth to markets across the ocean

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: after decades of division, one national state was built — army, laws, schools, railways — and the loose provinces became a single country.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a place that changed fast in one generation — a new road, station, port or town changing daily life around it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the past through stories, images, laws and statistics.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the provinces were always one country → go back to the start of the story: for decades Argentina was a loose set of provinces, divided; the single national state was BUILT, between 1860 and 1916, it did not simply exist.
- Thinks the railways were built with Argentina's own money alone → the book is plain that British and other foreign capital built the railways and meat-packing plants, and that tied the new wealth to markets across the ocean.
- Go back to the one thing: After decades of division, Argentina's leaders built one national state — an army, laws, schools and railways — and turned a loose set of provinces into a single country, with foreign capital building the railways and meat-packing plants of the export boom. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
