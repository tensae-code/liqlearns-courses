# Lesson plan — The Conquest of Indigenous Territories

**Course:** History — Argentina · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Conquest of Indigenous Territories
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Building the Argentine State, 1860–1916 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The same state that welcomed immigrants sent its army against the indigenous peoples of the south and the Chaco, taking their hunting and fishing lands — and in the cities immigrant families crowded into conventillo tenements — so the book asks who progress left out.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Building the Argentine State, 1860–1916 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- **Argentina: The Great Immigration** (`modern-world/argentina-the-great-immigration/`) is recommended first — it teaches the newcomers the same state welcomed in, whose other face this lesson shows (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the state that welcomed immigrants also sent its army against the indigenous peoples of the south and the Chaco and took their lands, while immigrant families crowded into conventillos — progress did not reach everyone.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a change that helped some people and hurt others at the same time, and who was left out of it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the past through stories, images, laws and statistics.
