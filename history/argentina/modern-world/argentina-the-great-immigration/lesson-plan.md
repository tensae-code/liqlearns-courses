# Lesson plan — Argentina: The Great Immigration

**Course:** History — Argentina · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Argentina: The Great Immigration
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Building the Argentine State, 1860–1916 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Crisis and crowding in Western Europe pushed millions to leave home, and Argentina pulled them across the Atlantic — its state made Alberdi's "to govern is to populate" into the 1876 Immigration and Colonisation Law, opened offices in Europe and paid passages, until by 1914 nearly a third of Argentina's people were foreign-born, mostly from Italy and Spain.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Building the Argentine State, 1860–1916 — it stands on the lesson before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The changes in Western Europe: why millions left home** — Crisis and crowding in Europe push millions to leave home; Argentina's promise of land and work pulls them across the Atlantic
2. **The State's role: the 1876 Immigration and Colonisation Law** — Alberdi's "to govern is to populate" becomes law — the state opens offices in Europe and pays passages, and by 1914 nearly a third of Argentina's people are foreign-born, mostly from Italy and Spain

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- **Argentina Building the State, 1860–1916** (`modern-world/argentina-building-the-state-1860-1916/`) is recommended first — it teaches the new state that now set out to populate the country (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: crisis and crowding pushed millions out of Europe, and Argentina's state pulled them in by law — offices in Europe, paid passages — until nearly a third of the country was foreign-born by 1914.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a family or neighbour who moved from far away for land or work, and what pushed and pulled them.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the past through stories, images, laws and statistics.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks people crossed the ocean only for adventure → the book teaches a push and a pull: crisis and crowding pushed them out of Europe, and Argentina's promise of land and work pulled them in — and the Argentine state worked on purpose to bring them.
- Mixes up who most of the newcomers were → by 1914 nearly a third of Argentina's people were foreign-born, and most of them came from Italy and Spain.
- Go back to the one thing: Crisis and crowding in Western Europe pushed millions to leave home, and Argentina pulled them across the Atlantic — its state made Alberdi's "to govern is to populate" into the 1876 Immigration and Colonisation Law, opened offices in Europe and paid passages, until by 1914 nearly a third of Argentina's people were foreign-born, mostly from Italy and Spain. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
