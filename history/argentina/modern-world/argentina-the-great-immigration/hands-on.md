# Hands-on — Argentina: The Great Immigration

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the push and the pull out loud: crisis and crowding pushed millions out of Europe; Argentina's promise of land and work pulled them across the Atlantic. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw the Atlantic as a wide band across the page: Europe on one side, Argentina on the other, and a ship in the middle. Write the push on the Europe side and the pull on the Argentina side, and under the ship write the year of the law — 1876 — and Alberdi's words: "to govern is to populate". Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you meet or hear of someone who moved from far away for work or land, note it: what pushed them, what pulled them, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
