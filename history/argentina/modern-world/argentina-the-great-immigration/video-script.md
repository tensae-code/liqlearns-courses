# Video script — Argentina: The Great Immigration

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Millions of people left Europe and crossed an ocean — because life pushed them out, and Argentina pulled them in.

Look at the map. Start in Western Europe: crowded villages, hard times, not enough land or work. Families look at the map and make a huge decision.

Now follow the ships. Across the Atlantic, week after week, to Buenos Aires. On the other side waits Argentina: wide land, new railways, harvests needing hands — the promise of land and work.

But Argentina did not just wait. Hear the words of Alberdi: "to govern is to populate." In 1876 those words became a law — the Immigration and Colonisation Law. The state opened offices in Europe. It paid passages. It went out and fetched its new people.

Now run the years to 1914 and look at the country the ships made: nearly one person in every three in Argentina was born across the ocean — most of them from Italy and Spain.

Remember it this way: pushed by crisis, pulled by promise, fetched by law — a third of a country, newly arrived.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
