# South Sudan Community Origins and Traditions — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Origin of a community
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Dinka sing that they came from the black mud of the Nile and their god Nhialic, and every child asks parents how their own community started. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Community rituals, ceremonies and festivals
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Rituals that cleanse a person or society, ceremonies and festivals are passed generation to generation as the community's living tradition. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Common practices of African communities in the past
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children study pictures of traditional practices, name their community's name for God and the sacrifices offered, and list practices they still know. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Relationship between ceremonies today and those from the past
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Today's ceremonies are matched against past ones so children see which traditions carried through and which changed. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Stories of origin
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The teacher reads where Garang and Abuk came from, classes discuss Bible and Koran creation stories, and other peoples say they came from the moon, a superior being, or the mountains. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — How stories of origin have changed our lives today
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children ask parents about other peoples in their state and their origin stories, and judge how those beginnings still shape life today. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
