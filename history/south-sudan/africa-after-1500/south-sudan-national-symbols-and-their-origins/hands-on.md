# Hands-on — South Sudan's National Symbols and Their Origins

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — South Sudan's National Symbols and Their Origins — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The anthem praises martyrs whose blood cemented the nation, and the flag's white stripes mark the 1972 Addis Ababa and 2005 Naivasha peace, red the blood, blue the Nile, yellow the guiding star, green the land. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
