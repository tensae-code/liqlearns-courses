# Hands-on — South Sudan: Turkiyya and Mahdiyya

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — South Sudan: Turkiyya and Mahdiyya — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Muhammad Ali invades in 1820 with cannon to build a slave army and seize slaves, gold, ivory and timber; about 30,000 Sudanese are conscripted in 1820-21 and many die on the road to Aswan; Khartoum is founded, Sudan is taxed through provinces, and the south supplies the slaves. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
