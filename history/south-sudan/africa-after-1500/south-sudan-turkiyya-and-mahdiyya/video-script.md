# Video script — South Sudan: Turkiyya and Mahdiyya

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Muhammad Ali invades in 1820 with cannon to build a slave army and seize slaves, gold, ivory and timber; about 30,000 Sudanese are conscripted in 1820-21 and many die on the road to Aswan; Khartoum is founded, Sudan is taxed through provinces, and the south supplies the slaves. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Weak successors, debt, anti-slavery campaigns that anger merchant traders, ivory violence and heavy taxes hollow out the Turkiyya, while South Sudanese resist forts and garrisons until disease and defection force their abandonment. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Muhammad Ahmad, a Dongola boat-builder's son, declares himself Mahdi in June 1881 against foreign rulers, corrupt government and brutal taxes; his Ansar destroy Hicks's army at Shaykan in 1883 and Khartoum falls, ending the Turkiyya. The Mahdiyya imposes Sharia and zakat in place of colonial taxes, models itself on the Prophet's Medina under the Mahdi and his successor Khalifa Abdullahi, then declines.
