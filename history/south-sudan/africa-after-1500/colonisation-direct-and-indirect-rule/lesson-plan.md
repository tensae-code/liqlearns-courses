# Lesson plan — Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule

**Course:** History — South Sudan · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Civilisations and Colonisation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Lewanika, who won Lozi independence from the Kololo in 1864, collaborates with the British from fear of Germans, Portuguese and Ndebele and hope of guns and mission education — yet Barotseland is still alienated and his people made landless.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Civilisations and Colonisation (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Collaboration: Lewanika of the Lozi** — Lewanika, who won Lozi independence from the Kololo in 1864, collaborates with the British from fear of Germans, Portuguese and Ndebele and hope of guns and mission education — yet Barotseland is still alienated and his people made landless.
2. **Colonial rule and administration** — Colonial systems of administration are compared, and pupils explain why a few African countries were never colonised.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
