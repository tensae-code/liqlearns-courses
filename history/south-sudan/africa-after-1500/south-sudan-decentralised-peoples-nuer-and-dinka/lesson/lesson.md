## Sub lesson 1 — The Nuer

A decentralised people of tribes and lineages whose leopard-skin religious chiefs settle disputes; cattle are wealth and prestige, raiding the Dinka is a duty blessed by women's rituals, and Kwoth is honoured through spirits, ghost marriages and marriages completed only at the third child. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Dinka

South Sudan's largest people, the Jieng, are decentralised pastoralists led by ritual chiefs, masters of the fishing spear; boys are scarified into warrior age-sets, name themselves after their cattle's colours, wrestle, and trade cattle, grain and ivory with the Nuer.
