# Video script — League of Nations and United Nations

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

After the First World War the world builds the League of Nations to keep peace, and pupils judge its record. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

After the Second World War the League's successor, the United Nations, takes up the promotion of peace. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Learners compare the League and the UN in aims and structure, then case-study UN peacekeeping in the Congo crisis, the Korean War and the Gulf War, and the UN's role in South Sudan or Africa. Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in South Sudan.
