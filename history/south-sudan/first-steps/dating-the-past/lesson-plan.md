## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How has your family changed over time?** — Okot meets grandparents Cucu and Ladu, cousins, aunts and uncles, and asks parents how the family changed.
2. **Timeline of objects** — Children investigate how objects at home and school changed over time and build a timeline, learning order by lining up classmates youngest to oldest.
3. **Changes that have happened in the place where we live** — Thatch huts become modern houses, donkeys give way to lorries and foot messengers to telephones, and children name what changed over time.
4. **Placing objects and events in chronological order** — Children build a timeline of changes they discovered, enhanced with pictures and objects, and learn why placing events in order matters.
5. **Life before and after technology** — Traditional and modern houses, ox-cart and pickup, three-stone fire and gas cooker are set side by side so children compare life before and after.
6. **Placing inventors and events in correct periods of time** — Learners investigate life before and after ploughs, steam engines and the printing press from first- and second-hand evidence, then place people and events in periods, building from family lifespans to centuries.
7. **Building a timeline of the journey to independence** — Working together, pupils produce a timeline of South Sudan's and South Africa's journeys, marking significant turning points and those who led the changes.
8. **Periods of history** — History is divided into ancient (to the fall of Rome, Han and Gupta; Bronze and Iron Ages; BC/BCE), post-classical or medieval (500-1500, world religions and trade networks grow), and modern (early modern, mid-modern, contemporary from 1914 and the Cold War).
9. **Characteristics of different periods of civilisation** — Each period is tested for its own features — complex political structures and states — and ancient life is debated against medieval life.
10. **Building a chronological framework** — Learners place the coming of Europeans into a chronological framework of periods in history, then compare migration into North and South America with the African story.
