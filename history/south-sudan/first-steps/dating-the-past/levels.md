# Dating the Past — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — How has your family changed over time?
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Okot meets grandparents Cucu and Ladu, cousins, aunts and uncles, and asks parents how the family changed. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Timeline of objects
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children investigate how objects at home and school changed over time and build a timeline, learning order by lining up classmates youngest to oldest. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Changes that have happened in the place where we live
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Thatch huts become modern houses, donkeys give way to lorries and foot messengers to telephones, and children name what changed over time. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Placing objects and events in chronological order
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children build a timeline of changes they discovered, enhanced with pictures and objects, and learn why placing events in order matters. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Life before and after technology
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Traditional and modern houses, ox-cart and pickup, three-stone fire and gas cooker are set side by side so children compare life before and after. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Placing inventors and events in correct periods of time
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Learners investigate life before and after ploughs, steam engines and the printing press from first- and second-hand evidence, then place people and events in periods, building from family lifespans to centuries. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Building a timeline of the journey to independence
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Working together, pupils produce a timeline of South Sudan's and South Africa's journeys, marking significant turning points and those who led the changes. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Periods of history
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: History is divided into ancient (to the fall of Rome, Han and Gupta; Bronze and Iron Ages; BC/BCE), post-classical or medieval (500-1500, world religions and trade networks grow), and modern (early modern, mid-modern, contemporary from 1914 and the Cold War). — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Characteristics of different periods of civilisation
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Each period is tested for its own features — complex political structures and states — and ancient life is debated against medieval life. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Building a chronological framework
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Learners place the coming of Europeans into a chronological framework of periods in history, then compare migration into North and South America with the African story. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
