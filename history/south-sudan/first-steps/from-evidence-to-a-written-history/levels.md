# From Evidence to a Written History — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Investigating family history
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children design questions, talk to family members about ancestors and where the family came from, then turn the answers into a family tree. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Investigating change from sources
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children use maps and photographs as second-hand resources, listen to stories from older people, and categorise causes of change — weather, conflict, building, population, technology. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Investigating the history of South Sudan from a range of resources
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Groups each take one aspect of life in the past — food, clothes, jobs, homes, religion — research it from broadcasts, books, artefacts and people, then teach the class. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — How do we know what happened long time ago?
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Two sources survive — artefacts (tools, weapons, jewellery; wood rots, stone and metal last) and written records by traders and visitors, like the Sahelian kingdom's 9th-century medical writing and hardwood masks. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Exploring evidence of change
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pupils investigate what led to conflict and how it was resolved from maps, stories, articles and artefacts, and interpret evidence of change in South Sudan and other countries. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Sources of information on civilisation
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Historians work from primary sources (artefacts, diaries, manuscripts, recordings), secondary sources (books, archives by people not present) and tertiary sources (radio, TV, internet). — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Importance of maps in understanding the development of civilisation
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Maps trace cultures, carry historical accounts from earliest to modern times, and are themselves artefacts of civilisation. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Using maps to explain settlement
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Learners study maps of where civilisations arose, looking for physical features and communication routes that explain why people settled there. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Sources of information on history
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Primary, secondary and tertiary sources are taught through resource people who witnessed events, archaeology (the Meroitic temple unearthed in 2011 and Sedeinga's pyramids), linguistics tracing migration through similar words, and anthropology living among a people. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Researching slave trade as a case study
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Learners research one country as a case study, study the founders and organisation of the slave trade in South Sudan, and draw sketch maps of Africa and South Sudan showing the routes slave traders used. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
