## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Investigating family history** — Children design questions, talk to family members about ancestors and where the family came from, then turn the answers into a family tree.
2. **Investigating change from sources** — Children use maps and photographs as second-hand resources, listen to stories from older people, and categorise causes of change — weather, conflict, building, population, technology.
3. **Investigating the history of South Sudan from a range of resources** — Groups each take one aspect of life in the past — food, clothes, jobs, homes, religion — research it from broadcasts, books, artefacts and people, then teach the class.
4. **How do we know what happened long time ago?** — Two sources survive — artefacts (tools, weapons, jewellery; wood rots, stone and metal last) and written records by traders and visitors, like the Sahelian kingdom's 9th-century medical writing and hardwood masks.
5. **Exploring evidence of change** — Pupils investigate what led to conflict and how it was resolved from maps, stories, articles and artefacts, and interpret evidence of change in South Sudan and other countries.
6. **Sources of information on civilisation** — Historians work from primary sources (artefacts, diaries, manuscripts, recordings), secondary sources (books, archives by people not present) and tertiary sources (radio, TV, internet).
7. **Importance of maps in understanding the development of civilisation** — Maps trace cultures, carry historical accounts from earliest to modern times, and are themselves artefacts of civilisation.
8. **Using maps to explain settlement** — Learners study maps of where civilisations arose, looking for physical features and communication routes that explain why people settled there.
9. **Sources of information on history** — Primary, secondary and tertiary sources are taught through resource people who witnessed events, archaeology (the Meroitic temple unearthed in 2011 and Sedeinga's pyramids), linguistics tracing migration through similar words, and anthropology living among a people.
10. **Researching slave trade as a case study** — Learners research one country as a case study, study the founders and organisation of the slave trade in South Sudan, and draw sketch maps of Africa and South Sudan showing the routes slave traders used.
