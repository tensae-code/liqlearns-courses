# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Find History in Your Own Locality** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Family history

Children compare old and new family pictures, build a family tree, and ask parents about ancestors who died before they were born. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gathering origin stories from people

Learners talk to a range of people about where they came from, family journeys, jobs, festivals and changes in law, and write to older children in neighbouring schools to learn about other peoples of their state. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
