# Fact or Opinion

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Fact or Opinion** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Arguing for and against change

Children compare changes, judge their impact on daily life, and build arguments to persuade others which changes helped and which they would reverse. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Debating the growth and collapse of pre-colonial trade

Groups examine and debate why Long Distance, Coastal and Trans-Saharan trade grew and why it collapsed by the end of the 19th century, presenting conclusions with their evidence. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Debating agreements and assessing the wars

Learners debate the outcomes and terms of the Addis-Ababa Agreement and assess the contribution of the Anya-Nya and SPLM/A wars to South Sudan's independence, giving reasons and evidence. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
