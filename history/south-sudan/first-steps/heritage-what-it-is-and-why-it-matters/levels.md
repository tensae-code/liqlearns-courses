# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Objects found at home
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children sort traditional and modern objects at home and at school and learn the past survives in things people keep and share. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Objects that were used long time ago and the ones used today
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Keji, Adek and Pita sort spears, skin dresses, pots and hoes of long ago against today's objects, and find some old things are still used. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Events that were practiced long time ago and today
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Old events and ceremonies are compared with today's, so children see traditions surviving, changing or disappearing. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Cultural features of the Kingdoms of South Sudan
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Azande kings' weapons, Anyuak royal regalia, the Asante royal stool and cloth, Meroe kings and a Kush stone incense burner are drawn and modelled as heritage children can still see. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — What past kingdoms left in present-day South Sudan
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Learners reflect on which characteristics of the old kingdoms are reflected in South Sudan now, and present them through drama and role play. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Benefits of civilisation to modern society
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Civilisation leaves modern society architecture, political structures beyond tribal law, cultural exchange, technology from shared ideas, and safety in numbers. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
