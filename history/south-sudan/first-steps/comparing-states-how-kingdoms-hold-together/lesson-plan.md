## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What happened elsewhere in Africa?** — A map of Africa's main kingdoms in 680 AD sets South Sudan beside Bunyoro-Kitara, the Zulu and Asante, and children ask why some kingdoms are called empires.
2. **Political, cultural and economic organisation of the kingdoms** — Centralised kings command armies yet a council of elders can remove a king; kingdoms share gods of rain and harvest, communal cultivation, iron smelting for spears and shields, and trade of farm produce for textiles.
3. **Comparing and evaluating kingdom governance** — Learners compare Azande, Shilluk, Anyuak and Otuho with kingdoms across Africa, evaluate their systems of governance, and ask which past systems still govern South Sudan today.
4. **Comparing civilisations in detail** — Learners compare at least two civilisations — Maya, Aztecs, Khmer Empire or Romans — feature by feature, and judge which factors were key to success and which to failure.
5. **Centralised and decentralised societies in South Sudan** — Centralised peoples (Shilluk, Azande, Anyuak) hold power in one king with a standing army, while decentralised peoples (Dinka, Nuer) share power among councils and elders with warriors for defence.
6. **Comparing colonial administration with pre-colonial administration** — Small groups compare the colonialists' systems of administration with the pre-colonial rule of kingdoms and chiefs, and write reports on what changed.
