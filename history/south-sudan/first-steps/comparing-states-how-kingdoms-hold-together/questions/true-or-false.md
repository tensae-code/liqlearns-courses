# Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** This lesson's teaching has nothing to do with the story of “What happened elsewhere in Africa?”.
**Answer: False** — A map of Africa's main kingdoms in 680 AD sets South Sudan beside Bunyoro-Kitara, the Zulu and Asante, and children ask why some kingdoms are called empires.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** Centralised kings command armies yet a council of elders can remove a king; kingdoms share gods of rain and harvest, communal cultivation, iron smelting for spears and shields, and trade of farm produce for textiles.
**Answer: True**
