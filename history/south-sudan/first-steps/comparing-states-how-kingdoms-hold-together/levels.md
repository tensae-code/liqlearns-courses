# Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — What happened elsewhere in Africa?
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: A map of Africa's main kingdoms in 680 AD sets South Sudan beside Bunyoro-Kitara, the Zulu and Asante, and children ask why some kingdoms are called empires. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Political, cultural and economic organisation of the kingdoms
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Centralised kings command armies yet a council of elders can remove a king; kingdoms share gods of rain and harvest, communal cultivation, iron smelting for spears and shields, and trade of farm produce for textiles. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Comparing and evaluating kingdom governance
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Learners compare Azande, Shilluk, Anyuak and Otuho with kingdoms across Africa, evaluate their systems of governance, and ask which past systems still govern South Sudan today. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Comparing civilisations in detail
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Learners compare at least two civilisations — Maya, Aztecs, Khmer Empire or Romans — feature by feature, and judge which factors were key to success and which to failure. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Centralised and decentralised societies in South Sudan
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Centralised peoples (Shilluk, Azande, Anyuak) hold power in one king with a standing army, while decentralised peoples (Dinka, Nuer) share power among councils and elders with warriors for defence. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Comparing colonial administration with pre-colonial administration
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Small groups compare the colonialists' systems of administration with the pre-colonial rule of kingdoms and chiefs, and write reports on what changed. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
