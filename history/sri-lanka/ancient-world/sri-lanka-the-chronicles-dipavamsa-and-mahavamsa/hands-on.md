# Hands-on — Sri Lanka: The Chronicles Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Sri Lanka: The Chronicles Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Dipavamsa comes first, then the monk Mahanama shapes the Mahavamsa in the 5th–6th century — 37 chapters ending with Mahasena — and later hands continue it as the Culavamsa, while Wilhelm Geiger's edition lets the world read them critically. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
