# The First World War

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **The First World War** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

Alliances and rivalry drag Europe into a new kind of war — aircraft, submarines and poison gas — that kills beyond all precedent. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Alliances and rivalry drag Europe into a new kind of war — aircraft, submarines and poison gas — that kills beyond all precedent. Give one real example: alliances and rivalry drag Europe into a new kind of war — aircraft, submarines and poison gas — that kills beyond all precedent. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
