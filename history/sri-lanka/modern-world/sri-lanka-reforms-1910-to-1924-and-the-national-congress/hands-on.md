# Hands-on — Sri Lanka: Reforms 1910 to 1924 and the National Congress

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Sri Lanka: Reforms 1910 to 1924 and the National Congress — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: 1910 brings the first elected member, the communities unite in the 1919 National Congress, and the Manning constitutions of 1920 and 1924 give elected majorities on paper while the Governor keeps the real power. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
