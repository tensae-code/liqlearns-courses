# The French Revolution

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **The French Revolution** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 11, new political thought and the French Revolution

Louis XIV boasts "I am the state", the Estates-General sleeps from 1614 to 1789, the Third Estate swears the Tennis Court Oath, the Bastille falls, rights are declared — and by 1793 the king and Marie Antoinette are guillotined. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 7, the French Revolution as a stage of mankind

1789 stands in the book's short list of the turns of human history, between America's independence and Russia's revolution. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
