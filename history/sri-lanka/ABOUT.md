# About the History — Sri Lanka course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Sri Lanka's own Educational Publications Department and National Institute of Education books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Sri Lanka inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the story as Sri Lanka's own books present it (see CURRICULUM.md for the derivation): Level 1 — the ancient world and the island's kingdoms (44 lessons); Level 2 — foreign rule, revival and the road to independence (27 lessons); Level 3 — the tank civilisation, Kandy from inside, and the world widens (21 lessons); Level 4 — colonial Ceylon and the world's great turns (11 lessons); Level 5 — Anuradhapura and the medieval kingdoms as scholarship (14 lessons); Level 6 — ancient India as archaeology (4 lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The arc whole:* "First, the world's river civilisations and the island's own kingdoms, told king by king from Anuradhapura's fall to Kotte and Jaffna. Then three centuries of foreign rule — Dutch, then British — the rebellions, the revivals, and the long constitutional road to independence in 1948 and the republic. Then home again to the tanks that made the dry land fertile and the Kandyan kingdom from inside, while Europe's revolutions widen the frame. Then colonial Ceylon and the world's great turns side by side, the island's ancient and medieval kingdoms read as scholarship, and last the neighbour's deep past read from stones and inscriptions."
