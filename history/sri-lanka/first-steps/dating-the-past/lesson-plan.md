## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 5, dividing European history into ancient, medieval and modern ages** — Historians draw the line at Rome's fall and again at the 15th century, and the middle age gets its name.
2. **Ch. 1, an introduction to history and its sources** — History is defined as the unending dialogue between past and present, and the evidence is paraded — Sigiriya's frescoes, Jetavanaramaya, a Nataraja bronze, Mirisavetiya coins — 2,500 years of written sources.
3. **Ch. 1, the main historical sources of the Anuradhapura period** — Before any king is named, the book puts its witnesses on trial — palm-leaf chronicles against inscriptions and ruins, asking what each kind of evidence can and cannot prove.
4. **Chs. 1–2, the Land and Time** — The book opens by setting the stage and the clock — the subcontinent's geography, then how deep time is measured before a single artefact appears.
5. **Notes and picture sources** — The book closes by showing its workings — notes, a list of where every picture came from, and notes on the plates — an early lesson in citing your evidence.
