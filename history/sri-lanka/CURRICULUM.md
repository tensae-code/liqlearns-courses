# History — Sri Lanka course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Sri Lanka inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Sri Lanka's books in hand are ten books as filed in the placements file — nine student books and one teacher guide (the Grade 9 guide, in English with parallel Sinhala and Tamil editions spot-checked as filed) — all COMPLETE as filed. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder below groups the books the way Sri Lanka's own story divides in them: the ancient world and the island's kingdoms first, then foreign rule, revival and the road to independence, the tank civilisation and Kandy with the world widening, colonial Ceylon beside the world's great turns, the ancient and medieval kingdoms read as scholarship at Advanced Level, and last ancient India read as archaeology. Each lesson sits at the level of the first book that teaches it. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Sri Lanka's own Educational Publications Department and National Institute of Education books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **The Ancient World and the Island's Kingdoms** — Sri Lanka's Grade 7 book opens the course: the world's first river civilisations, Greece, Rome, India and Arabia first, then the island's own story — Anuradhapura's fall to the Cholas, Polonnaruwa's great kings and queens, and the capitals' long move south-west through Dambadeniya to Kotte and Jaffna. — 44 lessons.

2. **Foreign Rule, Revival and the Road to Independence** — The Grade 9 book and its teacher guide tell the island's road from foreign rule to independence whole: the Dutch on the coast and Kandy's dealings with them, the British taking the coast and then Kandy itself, the rebellions of 1818 and 1848, the plantation economy, the Buddhist, Hindu and Muslim revivals, India's parallel freedom struggle held up as a mirror, and the constitutions from Colebrooke–Cameron in 1833 to independence in 1948 and the republics of 1972 and 1978. — 27 lessons.

3. **The Tank Civilisation, Kandy from Inside, and the World Widens** — The two Grade 10 books return home and widen the lens at once: how an ancient tank was built and the kings who built them, the island's crafts and its sea-trade with China, Rome and Arabia, the faiths that came by sea, the Kandyan kingdom ruled from inside, the Portuguese arriving in 1505, and Europe's own turns — the Industrial Revolution, the French Revolution and the idea of nationalism. — 21 lessons.

4. **Colonial Ceylon and the World's Great Turns** — The Grade 11 books run the island's colonial story and the world's in parallel: British Ceylon governor by governor and constitution by constitution, free education and health after 1931, the national movement organising — and beside it how world trade grew, the four great revolutions, colonialism named as a system, the two World Wars, the League of Nations and the United Nations Sri Lanka would join in 1955. — 11 lessons.

5. **Anuradhapura and the Medieval Kingdoms as Scholarship** — At Advanced Level the island's ancient and medieval history becomes real scholarship: the chronicles cross-examined before they are believed, the land that made the history, the first settlements, Buddhism's arrival with Mahinda, and Anuradhapura and the Polonnaruwa-to-Kotte centuries rebuilt topic by topic — government, economy, foreign policy, religion, literature and art. — 14 lessons.

6. **Ancient India as Archaeology** — The oldest book in the set, from 1968, teaches the neighbour's deep past as pure archaeology — land, time and stones: the first Indians' stone tools, the Indus cities dug from their mounds, the shift to the Ganges and Central India, South India's great stone monuments, and Asoka known through his own inscriptions. — 4 lessons.

**Totals: 121 lessons** (Level 1: 44 · Level 2: 27 · Level 3: 21 · Level 4: 11 · Level 5: 14 · Level 6: 4 — 167 placements stocked; 121 lessons stand on student books and 0 on teacher-guide evidence only; 31 lessons carry sub lessons).
