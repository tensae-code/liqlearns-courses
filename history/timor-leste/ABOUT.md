# About the History — Timor-Leste course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 6 Social Science lesson-plan book. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Timor-Leste inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **one level**:

1. **Level 1 — the occupation and the road back to freedom,
   1975–2002.** Timor-Leste's Grade 6 Social Science is one
   national story told in three periods — colonial times,
   independence and Indonesian occupation, then solidarity
   and human rights — and this course holds Period II, the
   middle period, in the book's own unit order: the
   Carnation Revolution and the proclamation of 28 November
   1975; the Indonesian invasion and the war of 1975–1979;
   the resistance reorganised as Falintil, with the three
   fronts and the 1983 ceasefire; the Church and the
   diplomatic front; the Santa Cruz massacre of 12 November
   1991; the clandestine resistance of the towns; the pupils'
   own oral-history research — interviews written up as
   mini-biographies, events adapted as drama; the referendum
   of 30 August 1999, the scorched-earth destruction, and
   the Restoration of Independence on 20 May 2002; and the
   annex of heroes and memory. Timor-Leste's own way, as
   filed: 1975–2002 taught as lived memory — children sing
   the resistance songs, act the events as drama, and
   interview ordinary people who survived the occupation.
   The course's 9 lessons all stand on a **teacher guide**
   and count nowhere in the acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *The occupation and the road back to freedom:* "First, a
  song in Lisbon topples a dictatorship, and in Dili a new
  republic is proclaimed — nine days of freedom. Then the
  giant neighbour invades, and the campaigns fall on the
  whole people; a poem called Torn Hopes carries what that
  felt like. The resistance rebuilds itself as guerrillas on
  three fronts; a church shelters the people while diplomats
  carry the claim to the United Nations. At a Dili cemetery
  in 1991, soldiers fire on young mourners — and a camera
  shows the world. In the towns, schoolchildren carry secret
  letters to the fighters in the bush. Then the course hands
  you the notebook: go and interview someone who lived
  through it, and write their life. At the end, after
  twenty-four years, the people vote, choose freedom by a
  landslide, watch their country burn for it — and raise
  their flag again in 2002. And the last word is memory:
  the heroes' names, and the truth written down, so nobody
  can make this nation forget."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**The Grade 6 Ciências Sociais lesson-plan book, Period 2**
— Grade 6 Social Science is one national story told in
three periods — colonial times, independence and Indonesian
occupation, then solidarity and human rights. Period II
teaches 1975–2002 as lived memory: children sing the
resistance songs, act the events as drama, and interview
ordinary people who survived the occupation, writing their
mini-biographies. The course keeps that shape: every lesson
is the unit's own lesson, in the unit's own order, told as
the story the book teaches. *(Teacher guide.)*
