# Video script — From Evidence to a Written History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

Every lesson so far in this course told a story from the past. This lesson turns the class around and asks: where do such stories come from — and who will keep them? The book's answer is a method, and the pupils themselves do it.

Each pupil interviews a person who lived through the occupation — a grandparent, a neighbour, anyone whose memory holds those years. The pupil listens, asks, and writes the experience up as a mini-biography: one real life, written down properly, the way a historian writes. In groups, pupils also adapt a historical event as drama — acting the event to understand it from inside — and the unit closes with final presentations and reflection, the class showing one another what it found.

See what the method really teaches. The occupation's history does not live only in books printed far away; it lives in the memories of ordinary survivors, and those memories close when their holders die. A child with a notebook, asking in time and writing carefully, is doing the nation's archiving. That is the step this lesson's title names: from evidence — a living person's remembered experience — to a written history that will outlast the remembering.

Remember it this way: History is made from evidence, and the evidence is alive: each pupil interviews a person who lived through the occupation, writes that person's experience up as a mini-biography, and groups adapt a historical event as drama — an eleven-year-old with a notebook becomes a historian, and a grandparent's memory becomes the nation's archive.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
