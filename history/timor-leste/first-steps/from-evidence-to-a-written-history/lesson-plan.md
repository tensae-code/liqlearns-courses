# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Timor-Leste · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is made from evidence, and the evidence is alive: each pupil interviews a person who lived through the occupation, writes that person's experience up as a mini-biography, and groups adapt a historical event as drama — an eleven-year-old with a notebook becomes a historian, and a grandparent's memory becomes the nation's archive.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the book itself works: pupils sing the resistance songs, act the events as drama, read photographs and poems, build a class timeline, and interview ordinary people who survived the occupation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks an interview is just a chat → the book's method has a product: the experience written up as a mini-biography, and events adapted as drama and presented — evidence turned into written history.
- Thinks history's evidence is only in documents → this lesson's evidence is living memory, gathered by interview before it is lost — the same elder-as-source method this course teaches as a first step.
- Go back to the one thing: History is made from evidence, and the evidence is alive: each pupil interviews a person who lived through the occupation, writes that person's experience up as a mini-biography, and groups adapt a historical event as drama — an eleven-year-old with a notebook becomes a historian, and a grandparent's memory becomes the nation's archive.
