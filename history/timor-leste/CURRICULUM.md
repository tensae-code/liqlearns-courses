# History — Timor-Leste course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Timor-Leste inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Timor-Leste's placements in hand
come from one book — the Grade 6 Ciências Sociais (Social
Science) teacher guide for Period 2 — and its own story is
the ladder. As filed, Grade 6 Social Science is one national
story told in three periods: colonial times (Period I),
independence and Indonesian occupation (Period II), then
solidarity and human rights (Period III). This book is
Period II, and it teaches 1975–2002 as lived memory in one
unit, in the unit's own lesson order: the Carnation
Revolution and the proclamation of 1975; the invasion and
the war of 1975–1979; the resistance reorganised — Falintil,
the three fronts, the 1983 ceasefire; the Church and the
diplomatic front; Santa Cruz, 1991; the clandestine front;
then the book's own method lesson, pupils interviewing
survivors and writing mini-biographies; the referendum of
1999 and the Restoration of 2002; and the annex of heroes
and memory that closes the book. One book, one unit, one
level — the ladder stops where the books in hand stop, not
because Timor-Leste teaches nothing more: the Period 1 and
Period 3 books and the Grade 6 student book itself are not
in this record set (see ABOUT.md).

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 6 Social Science lesson-plan book — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Modern World → First Steps** — Period II of Timor-Leste's
   own Grade 6 story, 1975–2002 as lived memory: the song that
   toppled a dictatorship in Lisbon and the nine days of the
   proclaimed republic; the invasion and the campaigns that
   devastated the population; the resistance rebuilt as
   guerrillas on three fronts, and the 1983 ceasefire; the
   Church that sheltered the people and the diplomats who
   carried the cause to the UN; Santa Cruz filmed for the
   world; the secret couriers of the towns; the book's own
   method — a child with a notebook interviewing a survivor;
   the referendum, the scorched earth, and the Restoration
   of 2002; and the heroes and the truth record a young
   republic writes down. 9 lessons, in two titles:
   `modern-world/` (8 lessons), with `first-steps/`
   (1 lesson) in the unit's own order between the clandestine
   front and the referendum — the book places its oral-history
   lessons (unit lessons 10–11 and 14–15) across that stretch
   of the unit.

**Totals: 9 lessons** (Level 1: 9 — all from one teacher
guide; acceptance 0, labelled — see ABOUT.md).
