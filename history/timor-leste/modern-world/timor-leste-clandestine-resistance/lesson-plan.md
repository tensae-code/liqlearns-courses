# Lesson plan — Timor-Leste: The Clandestine Resistance

**Course:** History — Timor-Leste · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Timor-Leste: The Clandestine Resistance
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The resistance also lived in the towns and even inside Indonesia itself: clandestine couriers carry letters, food and medicine to Falintil in the bush, student networks such as Renetil organise in secret, and Indonesian solidarity groups such as Solidamor stand with Timor — schoolchildren and students become the struggle's hidden messengers.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The resistance also lived in the towns and even inside Indonesia itself: clandestine couriers carry letters, food and medicine to Falintil in the bush, student networks such as Renetil organise in secret, and Indonesian solidarity groups such as Solidamor stand with Timor — schoolchildren and students become the struggle's hidden messengers.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a schoolchild carrying a folded clandestine letter hidden in school things, walking an ordinary street to an ordinary house — the resistance passing in plain sight.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the book itself works: pupils sing the resistance songs, act the events as drama, read photographs and poems, build a class timeline, and interview ordinary people who survived the occupation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the clandestine front was spying only → its work was supply and connection: letters, food and medicine to Falintil, and organising the towns and students into the struggle.
- Thinks every Indonesian supported the occupation → the book names Indonesian solidarity groups such as Solidamor standing with Timor against their own government's occupation.
- Go back to the one thing: The resistance also lived in the towns and even inside Indonesia itself: clandestine couriers carry letters, food and medicine to Falintil in the bush, student networks such as Renetil organise in secret, and Indonesian solidarity groups such as Solidamor stand with Timor — schoolchildren and students become the struggle's hidden messengers. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
