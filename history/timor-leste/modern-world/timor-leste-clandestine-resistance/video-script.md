# Video script — Timor-Leste: The Clandestine Resistance

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

The guerrillas in the mountains could not survive on mountains alone. They needed food, medicine, news and recruits — and all of that had to come, in secret, from the occupied towns. That secret work was the clandestine front, and this lesson is its story.

Inside the towns, ordinary people — and very often the young — became couriers and organisers. Letters were written in secret and carried to Falintil in the bush; food and medicine moved hand to hand under the occupier's nose. Students built networks of their own, such as Renetil, the student resistance network, organising where the occupation thought it ruled completely: in schools, universities and streets.

And the front reached further still — inside Indonesia itself. Indonesian solidarity groups, such as Solidamor, stood with Timor-Leste against their own government's occupation, carrying the cause inside the occupier's own country. See the whole shape: a guerrilla army in the bush, fed and informed by grandmothers, schoolchildren and students in the towns, and defended by friends inside Indonesia itself. That is why the occupation never faced only an army — it faced a whole society, organised in secret.

Remember it this way: The resistance also lived in the towns and even inside Indonesia itself: clandestine couriers carry letters, food and medicine to Falintil in the bush, student networks such as Renetil organise in secret, and Indonesian solidarity groups such as Solidamor stand with Timor — schoolchildren and students become the struggle's hidden messengers.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
