# Timor-Leste: The Clandestine Resistance

## Do it first

Say these words out loud, slowly, and think about each one as you say it: clandestine front, couriers, Renetil, Solidamor, letters to Falintil. Now say, in one sentence of your own, what you think this lesson's story will be about. Hold that sentence in mind — the story below will test it.

## The story

The guerrillas in the mountains could not survive on mountains alone. They needed food, medicine, news and recruits — and all of that had to come, in secret, from the occupied towns. That secret work was the clandestine front, and this lesson is its story.

Inside the towns, ordinary people — and very often the young — became couriers and organisers. Letters were written in secret and carried to Falintil in the bush; food and medicine moved hand to hand under the occupier's nose. Students built networks of their own, such as Renetil, the student resistance network, organising where the occupation thought it ruled completely: in schools, universities and streets.

And the front reached further still — inside Indonesia itself. Indonesian solidarity groups, such as Solidamor, stood with Timor-Leste against their own government's occupation, carrying the cause inside the occupier's own country. See the whole shape: a guerrilla army in the bush, fed and informed by grandmothers, schoolchildren and students in the towns, and defended by friends inside Indonesia itself. That is why the occupation never faced only an army — it faced a whole society, organised in secret.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: tell this story aloud to someone today, in your own words, without the page in front of you. A story you can retell is a story you own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The resistance also lived in the towns and even inside Indonesia itself: clandestine couriers carry letters, food and medicine to Falintil in the bush, student networks such as Renetil organise in secret, and Indonesian solidarity groups such as Solidamor stand with Timor — schoolchildren and students become the struggle's hidden messengers. Give one real example from the story — a schoolchild carrying a folded clandestine letter hidden in school things, walking an ordinary street to an ordinary house — the resistance passing in plain sight — and name the lesson's key words: clandestine front, couriers, Renetil, Solidamor, letters to Falintil. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
