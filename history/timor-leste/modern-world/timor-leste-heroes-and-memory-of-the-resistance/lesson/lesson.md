# Timor-Leste: Heroes and Memory of the Resistance

## Do it first

Say these words out loud, slowly, and think about each one as you say it: Rosa Muki Bonaparte, Maria Goretti Joaquim, Taur Matan Ruak, Rui Maria de Araújo, Soibada 1976, Nobel Peace Prize 1996, CAVR, CHEGA!. Now say, in one sentence of your own, what you think this lesson's story will be about. Hold that sentence in mind — the story below will test it.

## The story

A free country has one more war to win: the war against forgetting. This closing lesson is the annex of reference texts the book keeps for that fight — the names, dates and records a young republic hands to its children so the next generation inherits names, not silence.

First, the heroes, in biographies: Rosa 'Muki' Bonaparte and Maria Goretti Joaquim, women of the resistance's first hour; Taur Matan Ruak, a guerrilla commander who became a leader of the free state; and Rui Maria de Araújo. Around the names stand the records: the Soibada plenary assembly of 1976, where the resistance organised itself for the prolonged people's war; chronologies of 1976–78 and of 1983, the ceasefire year; and the world's own honour — the 1996 Nobel Peace Prize, given jointly to Bishop Belo, the Church's shelter made visible, and José Ramos-Horta, the diplomatic front's voice.

Last, the reckoning: the CAVR, the truth and reconciliation commission whose report is called CHEGA! — 'enough!' — recorded what was done to Timor-Leste under the occupation, testimony by testimony. Put the lesson's parts together and see its one thing: heroes named, a war chronicled, a prize remembered, and a truth written down. A nation that writes these things cannot be made to forget them.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: tell this story aloud to someone today, in your own words, without the page in front of you. A story you can retell is a story you own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A young republic writes down who its heroes were and what was done to it, so the next generation inherits names, not silence: biographies of Rosa 'Muki' Bonaparte, Maria Goretti Joaquim, Taur Matan Ruak and Rui Maria de Araújo; the Soibada assembly of 1976 and the prolonged people's war; chronologies; the 1996 Nobel Peace Prize to Bishop Belo and José Ramos-Horta; and the CAVR truth commission — CHEGA! — that recorded what was done. Give one real example from the story — a pupil reading the biography of Rosa 'Muki' Bonaparte and realising the hero of the first hour was a young woman not much older than the pupil's own mother was then — and name the lesson's key words: Rosa Muki Bonaparte, Maria Goretti Joaquim, Taur Matan Ruak, Rui Maria de Araújo, Soibada 1976, Nobel Peace Prize 1996, CAVR, CHEGA!. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
