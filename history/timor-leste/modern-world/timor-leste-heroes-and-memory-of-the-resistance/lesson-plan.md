# Lesson plan — Timor-Leste: Heroes and Memory of the Resistance

**Course:** History — Timor-Leste · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Timor-Leste: Heroes and Memory of the Resistance
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A young republic writes down who its heroes were and what was done to it, so the next generation inherits names, not silence: biographies of Rosa 'Muki' Bonaparte, Maria Goretti Joaquim, Taur Matan Ruak and Rui Maria de Araújo; the Soibada assembly of 1976 and the prolonged people's war; chronologies; the 1996 Nobel Peace Prize to Bishop Belo and José Ramos-Horta; and the CAVR truth commission — CHEGA! — that recorded what was done.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a pupil reading the biography of Rosa 'Muki' Bonaparte and realising the hero of the first hour was a young woman not much older than the pupil's own mother was then.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the book itself works: pupils sing the resistance songs, act the events as drama, read photographs and poems, build a class timeline, and interview ordinary people who survived the occupation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks this lesson is a list to memorise → its one thing is memory itself: names, chronologies, a prize and a truth commission are how a republic makes forgetting impossible.
- Thinks CHEGA! is a person's name → CHEGA! ('enough!') is the name of the CAVR truth and reconciliation commission's report, which recorded what was done under the occupation.
- Go back to the one thing: A young republic writes down who its heroes were and what was done to it, so the next generation inherits names, not silence: biographies of Rosa 'Muki' Bonaparte, Maria Goretti Joaquim, Taur Matan Ruak and Rui Maria de Araújo; the Soibada assembly of 1976 and the prolonged people's war; chronologies; the 1996 Nobel Peace Prize to Bishop Belo and José Ramos-Horta; and the CAVR truth commission — CHEGA! — that recorded what was done. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
