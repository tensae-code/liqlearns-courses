# Video script — Timor-Leste: The Church and the Diplomatic Front

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

Not every front of a resistance carries a gun. This lesson tells two fronts that carried something else: a church and a briefcase.

Inside Timor-Leste, the Catholic Church became the people's shelter. Under the occupation, the Church was the one institution that stood between ordinary people and the power occupying them — its parishes, priests and bishop gave protection, news and courage. When the Pope came and said mass at Tasi-Tolu, the great gathering itself became a demonstration: thousands of Timorese assembled in one place, visible to the world, a nation showing itself.

Outside the country, the diplomatic front worked the world's meeting rooms. Timor-Leste's diplomats carried the cause to the United Nations and to government after government, year after year, insisting on one claim: the Timorese people had never been freely asked, and their right to choose their own future still stood. Guns had not won the country back; the Church and the diplomats made sure the world could not honestly say the question was closed. Remember: shelter at home, a mass that became a demonstration, and a claim kept alive abroad.

Remember it this way: When guns cannot win, two other weapons keep a nation's claim alive: the Catholic Church, which shelters the people — and whose Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu becomes a demonstration — and the diplomats, who carry Timor-Leste's cause to the United Nations and the world.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
