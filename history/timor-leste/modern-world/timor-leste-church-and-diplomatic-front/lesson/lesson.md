# Timor-Leste: The Church and the Diplomatic Front

## Do it first

Say these words out loud, slowly, and think about each one as you say it: Catholic Church, Tasi-Tolu, the Pope's mass, diplomatic front, United Nations. Now say, in one sentence of your own, what you think this lesson's story will be about. Hold that sentence in mind — the story below will test it.

## The story

Not every front of a resistance carries a gun. This lesson tells two fronts that carried something else: a church and a briefcase.

Inside Timor-Leste, the Catholic Church became the people's shelter. Under the occupation, the Church was the one institution that stood between ordinary people and the power occupying them — its parishes, priests and bishop gave protection, news and courage. When the Pope came and said mass at Tasi-Tolu, the great gathering itself became a demonstration: thousands of Timorese assembled in one place, visible to the world, a nation showing itself.

Outside the country, the diplomatic front worked the world's meeting rooms. Timor-Leste's diplomats carried the cause to the United Nations and to government after government, year after year, insisting on one claim: the Timorese people had never been freely asked, and their right to choose their own future still stood. Guns had not won the country back; the Church and the diplomats made sure the world could not honestly say the question was closed. Remember: shelter at home, a mass that became a demonstration, and a claim kept alive abroad.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: tell this story aloud to someone today, in your own words, without the page in front of you. A story you can retell is a story you own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: When guns cannot win, two other weapons keep a nation's claim alive: the Catholic Church, which shelters the people — and whose Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu becomes a demonstration — and the diplomats, who carry Timor-Leste's cause to the United Nations and the world. Give one real example from the story — the crowd at the Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu — a prayer gathering that was also, without a single shot, a demonstration that the nation existed — and name the lesson's key words: Catholic Church, Tasi-Tolu, the Pope's mass, diplomatic front, United Nations. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
