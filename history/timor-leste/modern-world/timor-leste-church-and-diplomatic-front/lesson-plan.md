# Lesson plan — Timor-Leste: The Church and the Diplomatic Front

**Course:** History — Timor-Leste · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Timor-Leste: The Church and the Diplomatic Front
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** When guns cannot win, two other weapons keep a nation's claim alive: the Catholic Church, which shelters the people — and whose Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu becomes a demonstration — and the diplomats, who carry Timor-Leste's cause to the United Nations and the world.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: When guns cannot win, two other weapons keep a nation's claim alive: the Catholic Church, which shelters the people — and whose Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu becomes a demonstration — and the diplomats, who carry Timor-Leste's cause to the United Nations and the world.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — the crowd at the Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu — a prayer gathering that was also, without a single shot, a demonstration that the nation existed.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the book itself works: pupils sing the resistance songs, act the events as drama, read photographs and poems, build a class timeline, and interview ordinary people who survived the occupation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Church's role was only religious → in the book's telling the Church shelters the people under occupation, and the Tasi-Tolu mass becomes a demonstration — a public, political fact as well as a religious one.
- Thinks diplomacy was a side show → the diplomatic front kept the legal claim alive at the UN for decades; without it, the 1999 referendum (the course's later lesson) has no ground to stand on.
- Go back to the one thing: When guns cannot win, two other weapons keep a nation's claim alive: the Catholic Church, which shelters the people — and whose Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu becomes a demonstration — and the diplomats, who carry Timor-Leste's cause to the United Nations and the world. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
