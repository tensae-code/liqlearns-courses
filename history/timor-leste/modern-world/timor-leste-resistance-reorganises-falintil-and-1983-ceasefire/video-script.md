# Video script — Timor-Leste: The Resistance Reorganises — Falintil and the 1983 Ceasefire

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

An army that holds ground can be surrounded and destroyed — the campaigns of the first war years proved that. So the resistance did the hardest thing an army can do: it changed its whole way of fighting while the war was still going on. It reorganised itself.

The reorganised resistance fought as Falintil guerrillas — small, mobile fighters in the mountains, striking and vanishing, impossible to encircle the way a fixed army can be. And the struggle was organised on three fronts together: the armed front in the bush, the clandestine front in the towns (a later lesson tells its story in full), and the diplomatic front abroad (the next lesson but one). No front fought alone; together they kept a whole nation in the field.

In 1983 came a strange pause: a ceasefire, remembered as Kontaktu Dame — the peace contact — between the resistance and Indonesia. For a moment the guns quietened. But the ceasefire collapsed, and its collapse was followed by uprisings — the people rising again rather than accepting the occupation as settled. Remember the turn: reorganise or die — guerrillas, three fronts, a ceasefire used and lost, and uprisings after it.

Remember it this way: Beaten as a conventional army, the resistance reorganises: Falintil fights a guerrilla war, the struggle works on three fronts together, and in 1983 a ceasefire — Kontaktu Dame, the peace contact — pauses the fighting until its collapse sets off uprisings.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
