# Timor-Leste: The Indonesian Invasion and the War, 1975–1979

## Do it first

Say these words out loud, slowly, and think about each one as you say it: invasion December 1975, positional war, encirclement and annihilation, Xanana Gusmão, Esperanças Rasgadas. Now say, in one sentence of your own, what you think this lesson's story will be about. Hold that sentence in mind — the story below will test it.

## The story

The republic proclaimed on 28 November 1975 was nine days old when, in December 1975, Indonesia invaded. Indonesia is a giant beside small Timor-Leste, and it came with an army, ships and planes. The invasion did not ask the Timorese what they wanted; it arrived to take the country by force.

Fretilin organised the resistance as a war. At first it was positional war — holding ground, defending positions, an army facing an army. But Indonesia answered with great military operations, above all the encirclement-and-annihilation campaigns: whole areas were surrounded, bombed and cut off, and it was the ordinary population — farmers, families, villages — who were devastated.

The book does not teach this only as dates and operations. Pupils read the devastation through photographs, and through a poem: Esperanças Rasgadas — Torn Hopes — by Xanana Gusmão, who would become the resistance's leader. A poem carries what a casualty list cannot: what it felt like when a people's hopes were torn. Remember the shape: invasion, an army holding ground, campaigns that fell on the whole people — and a poem that kept the feeling of it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: tell this story aloud to someone today, in your own words, without the page in front of you. A story you can retell is a story you own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In December 1975 Indonesia — Timor-Leste's giant neighbour — invades the nine-day-old republic; Fretilin organises the war, first holding ground in positional war, and Indonesia's encirclement-and-annihilation campaigns devastate the population — a devastation the book reads through photographs and Xanana Gusmão's poem Esperanças Rasgadas, Torn Hopes. Give one real example from the story — a village family caught inside an encircled area during the campaigns — the war falling not only on soldiers but on the whole population — and name the lesson's key words: invasion December 1975, positional war, encirclement and annihilation, Xanana Gusmão, Esperanças Rasgadas. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
