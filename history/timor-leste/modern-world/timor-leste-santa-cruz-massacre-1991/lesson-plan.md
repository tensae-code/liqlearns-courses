# Lesson plan — Timor-Leste: The Santa Cruz Massacre, 1991

**Course:** History — Timor-Leste · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Timor-Leste: The Santa Cruz Massacre, 1991
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** On 12 November 1991 young mourners march to Santa Cruz cemetery in Dili; soldiers open fire on them — and foreign journalists film the shooting, so the occupation can no longer be hidden from the world. The lesson also recalls the earlier Kraras massacre.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: On 12 November 1991 young mourners march to Santa Cruz cemetery in Dili; soldiers open fire on them — and foreign journalists film the shooting, so the occupation can no longer be hidden from the world. The lesson also recalls the earlier Kraras massacre.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — the cameraman at Santa Cruz who kept filming while soldiers fired — his tape, not any army, is what changed the world's position.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the book itself works: pupils sing the resistance songs, act the events as drama, read photographs and poems, build a class timeline, and interview ordinary people who survived the occupation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Santa Cruz was a battle → it was a massacre: soldiers opened fire on unarmed young mourners at a cemetery.
- Thinks the massacre changed nothing → the journalists' film is the lesson's turn: shown worldwide, it ended the occupation's hiddenness and turned the world's eyes onto Timor.
- Go back to the one thing: On 12 November 1991 young mourners march to Santa Cruz cemetery in Dili; soldiers open fire on them — and foreign journalists film the shooting, so the occupation can no longer be hidden from the world. The lesson also recalls the earlier Kraras massacre. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
