# Timor-Leste: The Santa Cruz Massacre, 1991

## Do it first

Say these words out loud, slowly, and think about each one as you say it: Santa Cruz cemetery, 12 November 1991, Kraras, foreign journalists, filmed massacre. Now say, in one sentence of your own, what you think this lesson's story will be about. Hold that sentence in mind — the story below will test it.

## The story

On 12 November 1991, a funeral procession in Dili became a march. Young Timorese mourners walked to the Santa Cruz cemetery — remembering one of their own, and carrying their nation's banned flags and banners along the way. It was a march of the young: students and schoolchildren, unarmed.

At the cemetery, Indonesian soldiers opened fire on the crowd. Young people were shot among the gravestones — the Santa Cruz massacre. The book also recalls an earlier massacre, at Kraras, so pupils know Santa Cruz was not the first time the occupation answered Timorese defiance with mass killing — but Santa Cruz was different in one decisive way.

Foreign journalists were there, and they filmed it. One cameraman's tape of the cemetery shooting was smuggled out and shown around the world. Until that day, the occupation had been largely hidden — a closed country, a story told in whispers. After the film, the world had seen it with its own eyes, and the occupation could no longer be hidden. Remember the turn in one line: a cemetery, a camera, and a world that could not unsee what it had seen.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: tell this story aloud to someone today, in your own words, without the page in front of you. A story you can retell is a story you own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: On 12 November 1991 young mourners march to Santa Cruz cemetery in Dili; soldiers open fire on them — and foreign journalists film the shooting, so the occupation can no longer be hidden from the world. The lesson also recalls the earlier Kraras massacre. Give one real example from the story — the cameraman at Santa Cruz who kept filming while soldiers fired — his tape, not any army, is what changed the world's position — and name the lesson's key words: Santa Cruz cemetery, 12 November 1991, Kraras, foreign journalists, filmed massacre. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
