# Video script — Timor-Leste: The Santa Cruz Massacre, 1991

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

On 12 November 1991, a funeral procession in Dili became a march. Young Timorese mourners walked to the Santa Cruz cemetery — remembering one of their own, and carrying their nation's banned flags and banners along the way. It was a march of the young: students and schoolchildren, unarmed.

At the cemetery, Indonesian soldiers opened fire on the crowd. Young people were shot among the gravestones — the Santa Cruz massacre. The book also recalls an earlier massacre, at Kraras, so pupils know Santa Cruz was not the first time the occupation answered Timorese defiance with mass killing — but Santa Cruz was different in one decisive way.

Foreign journalists were there, and they filmed it. One cameraman's tape of the cemetery shooting was smuggled out and shown around the world. Until that day, the occupation had been largely hidden — a closed country, a story told in whispers. After the film, the world had seen it with its own eyes, and the occupation could no longer be hidden. Remember the turn in one line: a cemetery, a camera, and a world that could not unsee what it had seen.

Remember it this way: On 12 November 1991 young mourners march to Santa Cruz cemetery in Dili; soldiers open fire on them — and foreign journalists film the shooting, so the occupation can no longer be hidden from the world. The lesson also recalls the earlier Kraras massacre.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
