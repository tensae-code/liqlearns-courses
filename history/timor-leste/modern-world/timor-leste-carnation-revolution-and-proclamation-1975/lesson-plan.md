# Lesson plan — Timor-Leste: The Carnation Revolution and the Proclamation of 1975

**Course:** History — Timor-Leste · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Timor-Leste: The Carnation Revolution and the Proclamation of 1975
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Occupation and the Road Back to Freedom, 1975–2002 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A song used as a signal brings down Portugal's dictatorship in Lisbon — the Carnation Revolution — and decolonisation opens; in Dili, Fretilin proclaims the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste on 28 November 1975, Francisco Xavier do Amaral becomes its first president, and the anthem Pátria is sung for the first time — nine days of freedom before the invasion.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the book itself works: pupils sing the resistance songs, act the events as drama, read photographs and poems, build a class timeline, and interview ordinary people who survived the occupation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Carnation Revolution happened in Timor → it happened in Lisbon, Portugal; its gift to Timor was decolonisation — the opening, not the proclamation itself.
- Mixes up the proclamation and the later restoration → the proclamation is 28 November 1975; the restoration of independence comes in 2002, at the end of this course.
- Go back to the one thing: A song used as a signal brings down Portugal's dictatorship in Lisbon — the Carnation Revolution — and decolonisation opens; in Dili, Fretilin proclaims the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste on 28 November 1975, Francisco Xavier do Amaral becomes its first president, and the anthem Pátria is sung for the first time — nine days of freedom before the invasion. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
