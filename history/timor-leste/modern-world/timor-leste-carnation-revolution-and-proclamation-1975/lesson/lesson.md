# Timor-Leste: The Carnation Revolution and the Proclamation of 1975

## Do it first

Say these words out loud, slowly, and think about each one as you say it: Carnation Revolution, Grândola Vila Morena, Fretilin, 28 November 1975, Francisco Xavier do Amaral, Pátria. Now say, in one sentence of your own, what you think this lesson's story will be about. Hold that sentence in mind — the story below will test it.

## The story

The story starts far from Timor, in Lisbon, Portugal — the country that had ruled Timor as a colony for centuries. Portugal itself was ruled by a dictatorship, and soldiers who wanted change agreed on a secret signal: a song. When Zeca Afonso's song Grândola Vila Morena was played, the soldiers knew the moment had come. The dictatorship fell — the Carnation Revolution — and Portugal's new rulers began letting go of its colonies. Decolonisation had opened.

In Dili, Timor-Leste's capital, the Timorese party Fretilin moved fast. On 28 November 1975 Fretilin proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste — a free country of its own. Francisco Xavier do Amaral became its first president, and the anthem Pátria was sung for the first time: a new nation singing itself into being.

Hold the turn of this story clearly: a song in Lisbon opened a door, and a people walked through it and raised their own flag. But the freedom was measured in days — nine days later, in December 1975, Indonesia invaded. That invasion is the next lesson. This lesson is the nine days: the proclamation, the president, and the anthem a free Timor-Leste sang first.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: tell this story aloud to someone today, in your own words, without the page in front of you. A story you can retell is a story you own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A song used as a signal brings down Portugal's dictatorship in Lisbon — the Carnation Revolution — and decolonisation opens; in Dili, Fretilin proclaims the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste on 28 November 1975, Francisco Xavier do Amaral becomes its first president, and the anthem Pátria is sung for the first time — nine days of freedom before the invasion. Give one real example from the story — a radio in Lisbon playing a forbidden song as a signal — and, nine days of freedom later in the story's count, a crowd in Dili singing a brand-new anthem under a brand-new flag — and name the lesson's key words: Carnation Revolution, Grândola Vila Morena, Fretilin, 28 November 1975, Francisco Xavier do Amaral, Pátria. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
