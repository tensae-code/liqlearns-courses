## Sub lesson 1 — Foundations of Islamic civilisation

a civilisation is born at Medina on three foundations — the creed of Qur'an and Sunna, one Islamic order covering politics, economy and society, and the Arabic language, which carries the Qur'an to the world and absorbs Greek and Indian learning — built by a drive for knowledge, schools and mosques, translation and public libraries

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Social and cultural manifestations

the new society rests on piety, equality and brotherhood and lifts women from their pre-Islamic low place to rights of learning, opinion and property, while a Qur'anic culture grows sciences of its own — Arabic grammar, the science of history born from the need to know the Prophet's life, and geography for trade routes, the Hajj and the direction of prayer

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Islamic architectural art

mosque building grows from the Prophet's mud-and-palm mosque to minarets, mihrabs and domes, from the Great Mosque of Sanaa to al-Aqsa, Yemenis raise houses and cities in plain, mountain, wadi and desert, and the Muslims found whole cities — Kufa, Basra, Fustat, Qayrawan, Wasit, Baghdad, Samarra — each planned with water, a central mosque, markets and a wall

## Sub lesson 4 — Effect of Islamic civilisation on the European Renaissance

Europe's rebirth is fed from Islamic civilisation through named channels — students sent to Muslim universities and the House of Wisdom, Muslim scholars invited to teach, whole sciences translated from Arabic into Latin (algebra, chemistry, optics, astronomy, the compass, the astrolabe, paper, gunpowder), two centuries of Crusader contact in the East and eight centuries in Andalus, and transfer cities from Cordoba, Granada and Toledo to Sicily, Fez, Kairouan, Cairo, Damascus and Baghdad — a civilisation this book describes as universal and humane in its principles, from the brotherhood of mankind to freedom of belief

## Sub lesson 5 — Manifestations of the scientific renaissance (Unit 6 Lesson 2)

the sciences are paraded branch by branch — tafsir, readings, hadith and fiqh with their schools; grammar from Abu al-Aswad to Sibawayh; philosophy reconciling revelation and reason from al-Kindi to Ibn Rushd; al-Khwarizmi's algebra and the numerals Europe adopts; pharmacy's drugs and bread-mould for festering wounds; Ibn al-Haytham's optics and al-Biruni's specific weights; observatories, astrolabes and maps

## Sub lesson 6 — Effect of the Islamic scientific renaissance on the European renaissance (Unit 6 Lesson 3)

while Europe sits in its dark ages, the Islamic East shines, and the transfer runs through four gates — al-Andalus, Sicily, the Crusades and trade; Toledo under Archbishop Raymond becomes the translation mill where Gerard of Cremona alone renders some seventy works, Ibn Rushd is read at Paris despite the Church, al-Khwarizmi's algebra serves as a university text into the 16th century, and paper, glass, universities and even Alf Layla wa Layla pass west
