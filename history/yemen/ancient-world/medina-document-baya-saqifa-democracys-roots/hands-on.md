# Hands-on — The Medina Document, the Bay'a and the Saqifa: Democracy's Roots

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Landmarks of democracy in the Arab Islamic civilisation. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: four firsts are lined up as the seeds of modern institutions: the Medina Document as the first written constitution in history, the bay'a as the first election.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: four firsts are lined up as the seeds of modern institutions: the Medina Document as the first written constitution in history, the bay'a as the first election. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
