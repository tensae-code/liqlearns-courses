# Ancient Civilisations: Rome

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: The Roman invasion of Yemen; The Roman civilisation. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Roman invasion of Yemen

the emperor Augustus orders Yemen invaded in 24 BCE with 130 ships and 10,000 soldiers to seize the trade routes and Yemen's wealth, the Romans wreck towns on the road to Marib and besiege it, but Yemeni resistance, control of water and disease in the Roman ranks break the campaign

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Roman civilisation

Romulus founds Rome in 753 BCE on the Tiber, and the city passes from kings to a republic of consuls, Senate and popular bodies, then to empire under Augustus; Constantine moves the capital to Constantinople and the empire splits in 395 CE, the West falling in 476 CE and the East holding until Muhammad al-Fatih takes it in 1453; Latin spreads with the legions and survives as the Church's language, Livy writes history to glorify Rome, Pliny compiles the natural world, Roman relief carving and vaulted architecture feed Italy's later renaissance, and Christianity, born under Roman occupation in Palestine and persecuted in the arenas, becomes the empire's official religion in the 4th century

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
