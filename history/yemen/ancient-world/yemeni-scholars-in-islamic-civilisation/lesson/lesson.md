# Yemeni Scholars in Islamic Civilisation

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Role of Yemenis in Islamic civilisation; Yemeni figures in the Arab Islamic sciences (Unit 4 Lesson 4). As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Role of Yemenis in Islamic civilisation

Yemen becomes a scholarly centre of the Islamic world — Ahmad bin Hanbal, al-Shafi'i, Yahya bin Ma'in and Ishaq bin Rahwayh all study there, its schools shine at Zabid, Sanaa, al-Janad, Aden, Tarim, Kawkaban and Dhamar, and its scholars write the books others read, Wahb bin Munabih and al-Hasan al-Hamdani on history and the geography of Arabia, Abd al-Razzaq al-San'ani's Musannaf, Nashwan al-Himyari's Shams al-Ulum

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Yemeni figures in the Arab Islamic sciences (Unit 4 Lesson 4)

Sanaa's early stars — Tawus ibn Kaysan, Wahb ibn Munabbih, Ma'mar ibn Rashid, Abd al-Razzaq with his Musannaf — give way to Zabid's age of al-Umrani's al-Bayan and the Sufi Ibn Alwan, while al-Hamdani maps the Peninsula in Sifat Jazirat al-Arab and fills ten volumes of al-Iklil with Yemen's kings, and Nashwan al-Himyari gathers the language in Shams al-'Ulum

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
