# Yemeni Scholars in Islamic Civilisation — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Retell, in your own words, what happens in “Role of Yemenis in Islamic civilisation”.
**Answer:** Yemen becomes a scholarly centre of the Islamic world — Ahmad bin Hanbal, al-Shafi'i, Yahya bin Ma'in and Ishaq bin Rahwayh all study there, its schools shine at Zabid, Sanaa, al-Janad, Aden, Tarim, Kawkaban and Dhamar, and its scholars write the books others read, Wahb bin Munabih and al-Hasan al-Hamdani on history and the geography of Arabia, Abd al-Razzaq al-San'ani's Musannaf, Nashwan al-Himyari's Shams al-Ulum

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Who is in the story of “Yemeni figures in the Arab Islamic sciences (Unit 4 Lesson 4)”, where does it happen, and what turns?
**Answer:** Sanaa's early stars — Tawus ibn Kaysan, Wahb ibn Munabbih, Ma'mar ibn Rashid, Abd al-Razzaq with his Musannaf — give way to Zabid's age of al-Umrani's al-Bayan and the Sufi Ibn Alwan, while al-Hamdani maps the Peninsula in Sifat Jazirat al-Arab and fills ten volumes of al-Iklil with Yemen's kings, and Nashwan al-Himyari gathers the language in Shams al-'Ulum
