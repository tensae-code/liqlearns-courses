# The Zaydi State of Yemen: al-Hadi at Saada

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Founding of the Islamic (Zaydi) state in Yemen (284-298 AH); Yemeni states that contributed to the Islamic civilisation: the Zaydi state (Unit 4 Lesson 2). As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Founding of the Islamic (Zaydi) state in Yemen (284-298 AH)

Yemenis sick of Abbasid governors' wars invite Yahya bin al-Husayn al-Rassi, al-Hadi, from al-Rass near Medina; he reaches Yemen in 280 AH, settles disputes, founds a state on the Qur'an and the Sunna with its capital at Saada, unifies Yemen from Najran to Aden, and dies in 298 AH beside his mosque in Saada — and his imams, named for Zayd bin Ali, rule Yemen in stretches from then until the revolution of 1962

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Yemeni states that contributed to the Islamic civilisation: the Zaydi state (Unit 4 Lesson 2)

a delegation of Yemeni tribal leaders invites Yahya ibn al-Husayn al-Rassi from Medina; he takes the title al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, founds the Zaydi state at Saada (284 AH), and its schools and "hijar al-'ilm" make Saada a meeting point of scholars while al-Hadi himself writes works of tafsir, hadith and law

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
