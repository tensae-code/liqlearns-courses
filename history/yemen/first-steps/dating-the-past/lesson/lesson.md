# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Time; Importance of studying history (terms and timeline); Closing guidance appendix: how we understand the ages of history and date events. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Time

time is built up from 24 hours in a day and 7 days in a week to 12 months in a year, and the two year-counts are anchored to two journeys — the Hijri year to Muhammad's migration from Mecca to Medina, the Gregorian year to the birth of Jesus

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Importance of studying history (terms and timeline)

the lesson arms the student with its clocks — prehistory known only by word of mouth, BCE and CE counted from the birth of Jesus, "before the Mission" and the Hijri calendar from the Prophet's migration — and ends with the student drawing a timeline from Jesus' birth through the Mission and the Hijra to the present year

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Closing guidance appendix: how we understand the ages of history and date events

the book closes by teaching its own method — historians date by two reckonings, the Miladi from Isa's birth (BCE before it, CE after) and the Hijri from the Prophet's migration in 622 CE (AH before/after the Hijra); time itself is read in three dimensions, past, present and future, and human history divides into the pre-literate ages (stone, farming, metal) and the written ages — ancient (from ~5000 BCE to Islam's appearance), medieval, modern (from 1492) and contemporary — under the motto printed on the last page: whoever has no past has no present, and whoever has no present has no future

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
