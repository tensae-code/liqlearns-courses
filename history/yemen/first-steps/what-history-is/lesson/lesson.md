# What History Is

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — My family and my relatives

a child counts and names the people who make his family — grandfather, grandmother, father, mother, brothers and sisters — and learns uncles and aunts are the wider kin who hold it together

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Importance of studying history

history is defined as the science of past events studied for their lessons, heritage is everything the ancients left in buildings, language, knowledge and custom, and the student learns why nations that turn from justice fall, however strong they were

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — How do you read history?

reading history is not collecting what happened but tying events to the laws that raise and fell civilisations — guided ones flourish on justice, deviating ones decline however strong — with Sulayman's kingdom, which carried Bilqis's throne in the blink of an eye, as the proof of what a guided civilisation can reach

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — Historical summary from Adam to Ibrahim

one lesson sweeps the whole Grade 7 course into a single thread — Adam's mission, Idris's pen, Noah's 950 years and the flood, the first communities in south Arabia with Sanaa as the first city built, 'Ad destroyed by the barren wind and its survivors seeding Marib, Sanaa, Hadramawt and Tihama, Thamud felled by shout and earthquake, the Yemeni kingdoms, and Ibrahim facing Nimrod and rebuilding the Kaaba

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — Historical and time concepts

history is defined as the science that studies the past to uncover causes, relations and consequences and draw lessons for present and future; the age before writing (to ~3500 BCE) is prehistory, known through stories and remains, and history proper then runs ancient, medieval, modern, contemporary — with the "ancient era" ending at Rome's fall in 476 CE for European historians but at the Prophet's mission for Muslim historians

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
