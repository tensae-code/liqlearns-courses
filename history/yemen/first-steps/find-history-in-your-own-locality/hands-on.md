# Hands-on — Find History in Your Own Locality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: My family and my relatives (relatives section). Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: the child writes the names of his paternal and maternal uncles, aunts and grandfather, and sees the family on Eid gathered with relatives he does not live with.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the child writes the names of his paternal and maternal uncles, aunts and grandfather, and sees the family on Eid gathered with relatives he does not live with. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
