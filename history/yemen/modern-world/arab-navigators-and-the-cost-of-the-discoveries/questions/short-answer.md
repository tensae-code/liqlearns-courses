# Arab Navigators and the Cost of the Discoveries — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Retell, in your own words, what happens in “Effects of the geographical discoveries on the Arab homeland”.
**Answer:** the Arabs were there first — Yemeni ships crossing to India, China and East Africa a thousand years before Christ, Egyptian voyages to Punt, Hanno of Carthage rounding Africa, the Abbasid travellers Ibn Fadlan, al-Masudi and Ibn Battuta, and the Yemeni masters Ibn Majid and Sulayman al-Mahri, whom this book credits with guiding the Portuguese themselves — yet the discoveries turn the world's trade away from the Arab seas to the Atlantic, drain the Arab ports of their revenues, strangle the Mamluks until they fall in 1517, and bring Portuguese guns blockading Aden, al-Shihr and the Gulf

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Who is in the story of “Effects of the European geographical discoveries (Unit 1 Lesson 2)”, where does it happen, and what turns?
**Answer:** the book first credits Arab sailors of al-Andalus with crossing the Atlantic before Spain and Portugal used their maps; then da Gama reaches Calicut in 1498, and at Diu in 1509 the Mamluk fleet sent to defend Arab trade is betrayed by Diu's own ruler — the signal that the Indian Ocean, and thirty million enslaved Africans' homelands, pass under Western hegemony
