# European Renaissance and Reformation

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: The modern European Renaissance; Manifestations of the modern European renaissance (Unit 1 Lesson 1). As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — The modern European Renaissance

Italy, living next door to the flourishing Arab civilisation of Spain, Sicily and the Levant, wakes first: Constantinople's scholars flee west in 1453 with their manuscripts, rich trading cities pay for art and translation from Arabic, paper and printing spread the new learning, and over three centuries Europe climbs out of its middle ages — Luther attacks the sale of indulgences and the clergy's monopoly on the Gospel, kings gather power into national monarchies, and the humanities, Greek science and the Muslim method of doubt, observation and experiment remake the mind of a continent

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Manifestations of the modern European renaissance (Unit 1 Lesson 1)

Europe climbs out of its medieval darkness over three centuries, drawing on the Arab Islamic civilisation through al-Andalus, Sicily and Iraq; printing breaks the clergy's grip on knowledge, feudalism dissolves as cities and banks rise, national kingdoms form, Luther splits the Church, and gunpowder armies replace the feudal levy

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
