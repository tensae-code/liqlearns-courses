# Yemen: The 14 October 1963 Revolution and British Aden — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Retell, in your own words, what happens in “Revolution of 14 October”.
**Answer:** Britain seizes Aden on 19 January 1839 and splits the south into sultanates and protectorates to rule it, until the first spark flies from the Radfan mountains on 14 October 1963 under Sheikh Rajeh bin Ghaleb Labouza, martyred that first day, and the struggle drives the British out on 30 November 1967

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Who is in the story of “British ambitions in Yemen”, where does it happen, and what turns?
**Answer:** Britain, holding India — the jewel of its crown, taken from the French after the Seven Years War — needs the road to it guarded, and Yemen commands that road: Bab al-Mandab, revived by the Suez Canal in 1869, the watchtower islands of Perim, Kamaran and Socotra, and Aden, the finest servicing port on the way east, where the first British ship called in 1609; fearing Napoleon's drive from Egypt toward India, Britain seizes Perim in 1799, signs with the Sultan of Lahj in 1802, and in 1839, on the pretext of a looted Indian ship, the Daria Dawlat, occupies Aden itself and spreads over the southern protectorates
