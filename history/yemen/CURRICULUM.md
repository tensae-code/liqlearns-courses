# History — Yemen course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Yemen inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation. The path follows Yemen's own books' progression as filed — the placements file's book order is the teaching order, earliest books first — grouped into levels by the turns of Yemen's own story as its books present them: family and lived time first, then the celebrated national days; the prophets and ancient Yemen; Islam's rise and Yemen's Islamic states; ancient Yemen deepened; the prophets in Egypt and the Levant; the modern world opening and Yemen under foreign rule; the ancient world's civilisations whole; and the Arab Islamic civilisation with the modern world to today. A lesson sits at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; later books deepen lessons already placed, in file order inside each lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Yemen's own Social Studies and History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps and Yemen's Celebrated Days** — Yemen's earliest books in hand: history begins inside the child's own family and lived time, and national history arrives as four dated celebrations — the 26 September 1962 revolution, the 14 October 1963 revolution against British Aden, unification on 22 May 1990, and the 21 September 2014 revolution as this book teaches it. 7 lessons, 7 placements.
2. **Level 2 — Prophets and Ancient Yemen** — The first full History book: a chain of prophets — Adam, Noah, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim — then Yemen's own ancient kingdoms (Ma'in, Hadramawt, Saba, Himyar, Qataban, Awsan), the foreign ambitions its wealth draws, three occupations resisted, Arabia before Islam, and the birth of Islam. 15 lessons, 15 placements.
3. **Level 3 — Islam's Rise and Yemen's Islamic States Begin** — The Islamic Arabs' history with a Zaydi lens: the call in Mecca, the state in Medina, the campaigns and conquests, Yemen answering the Prophet's call, the Rightly Guided Caliphs and Imams, the Umayyad and Abbasid ages and Yemen under their governors, the Idrisids and the Alawites of Tabaristan, Saladin and the Ayyubids, and Yemen's own Zaydi, Sulayhid and Najahid states, closing with the foundations of Islamic civilisation and Yemen's scholars inside it. 13 lessons, 21 placements.
4. **Level 4 — Ancient Yemen Deepened, and the Old World Around It** — The method stated outright — what history is and how its clocks work — then the prophets retold in full, Adam's two sons, and ancient Yemen rebuilt in depth: its roots before writing, its kingdoms again, its political and economic life, its Musnad writing and sciences, its cities, palaces and dams, the Marib Dam and the Flood of al-Arim, and the Cisterns of Aden — with Mesopotamia, Ibrahim's road, and the Levant's city kingdoms around it. 9 lessons, 21 placements.
5. **Level 5 — Prophets in Egypt and the Levant, to the Birth of Isa** — How to read history, and its misreadings; the whole earlier story swept into one thread; Egypt's Nile civilisation; then the Israelite story at length — Yusuf in Egypt, Musa against Pharaoh, Israel in Sinai, Talut and Dawud, Sulayman and Bilqis — the Levant before Isa, Zakariya, Yahya and Maryam, Isa himself, and the world after Isa, closing with the northern Arab states and As'ad al-Kamil, the Tubba' who clothed the Kaaba. 15 lessons, 19 placements.
6. **Level 6 — The Modern World Opens, and Yemen Under Foreign Rule** — Europe's Renaissance fed by Islamic civilisation, the geographical discoveries and what they cost the Arab homeland, the Ottoman state rising and expanding east and west, its system of rule, Arab resistance to it, and Yemen's own Ottoman centuries — both rules, the resistance that expels the Ottomans in 1635 and 1918, British ambitions in Aden, and the foreign powers competing for Yemen's coffee and ports. 11 lessons, 13 placements.
7. **Level 7 — Civilisations of the Ancient World** — The secondary cycle returns to the ancient world whole: what history, civilisation and religion mean; Yemen as the Arabs' first homeland and source of their migrations; why the Arab civilisations rose, how they flourished in Asia and Africa, and how they fell; China, India, Japan, Persia, Greece, the Hellenistic world and Rome; the Aztecs, Maya, Inca, North America's first peoples and Aboriginal Australia; and the Arab caravan kingdoms — Nabataeans, Palmyra, Manadhira, Kinda — the markets before Islam, and Yemen on Islam's eve. 22 lessons, 27 placements.
8. **Level 8 — Arab Islamic Civilisation, and the Modern World to Today** — The Arab Islamic civilisation whole — its foundations in the Sharia's objectives, why it flourished, era by era from Prophethood to the Mamluks, its landmarks of democracy, shura, social justice, human rights and ijtihad, Yemen's own role carrying Islam to Asia, Africa and Europe and its Zaydi, Sulayhid, Rasulid and Tahirid states, the civilisation beyond the homeland and why its science rose and declined — then the modern world: Renaissance and discoveries again, colonialism and the Arab awakening, the French, American and Chinese revolutions, German, American, European and Arab unity beside Yemeni unity, the two world wars, the Cold War, the Arab–Zionist wars, colonialism's collapse, the new colonialism, the scientific-technological revolution and today's Arab issues. 46 lessons, 61 placements.

**Totals: 138 lessons, 184 placements** (Level 1: 7 lessons; Level 2: 15 lessons; Level 3: 13 lessons; Level 4: 9 lessons; Level 5: 15 lessons; Level 6: 11 lessons; Level 7: 22 lessons; Level 8: 46 lessons).
