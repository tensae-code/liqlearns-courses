# About the History — Yemen course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Yemen's own Social Studies and History books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Yemen inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **eight levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps and Yemen's Celebrated Days.** Yemen's earliest books in hand: history begins inside the child's own family and lived time, and national history arrives as four dated celebrations — the 26 September 1962 revolution, the 14 October 1963 revolution against British Aden, unification on 22 May 1990, and the 21 September 2014 revolution as this book teaches it. (7 lessons.)
2. **Level 2 — Prophets and Ancient Yemen.** The first full History book: a chain of prophets — Adam, Noah, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim — then Yemen's own ancient kingdoms (Ma'in, Hadramawt, Saba, Himyar, Qataban, Awsan), the foreign ambitions its wealth draws, three occupations resisted, Arabia before Islam, and the birth of Islam. (15 lessons.)
3. **Level 3 — Islam's Rise and Yemen's Islamic States Begin.** The Islamic Arabs' history with a Zaydi lens: the call in Mecca, the state in Medina, the campaigns and conquests, Yemen answering the Prophet's call, the Rightly Guided Caliphs and Imams, the Umayyad and Abbasid ages and Yemen under their governors, the Idrisids and the Alawites of Tabaristan, Saladin and the Ayyubids, and Yemen's own Zaydi, Sulayhid and Najahid states, closing with the foundations of Islamic civilisation and Yemen's scholars inside it. (13 lessons.)
4. **Level 4 — Ancient Yemen Deepened, and the Old World Around It.** The method stated outright — what history is and how its clocks work — then the prophets retold in full, Adam's two sons, and ancient Yemen rebuilt in depth: its roots before writing, its kingdoms again, its political and economic life, its Musnad writing and sciences, its cities, palaces and dams, the Marib Dam and the Flood of al-Arim, and the Cisterns of Aden — with Mesopotamia, Ibrahim's road, and the Levant's city kingdoms around it. (9 lessons.)
5. **Level 5 — Prophets in Egypt and the Levant, to the Birth of Isa.** How to read history, and its misreadings; the whole earlier story swept into one thread; Egypt's Nile civilisation; then the Israelite story at length — Yusuf in Egypt, Musa against Pharaoh, Israel in Sinai, Talut and Dawud, Sulayman and Bilqis — the Levant before Isa, Zakariya, Yahya and Maryam, Isa himself, and the world after Isa, closing with the northern Arab states and As'ad al-Kamil, the Tubba' who clothed the Kaaba. (15 lessons.)
6. **Level 6 — The Modern World Opens, and Yemen Under Foreign Rule.** Europe's Renaissance fed by Islamic civilisation, the geographical discoveries and what they cost the Arab homeland, the Ottoman state rising and expanding east and west, its system of rule, Arab resistance to it, and Yemen's own Ottoman centuries — both rules, the resistance that expels the Ottomans in 1635 and 1918, British ambitions in Aden, and the foreign powers competing for Yemen's coffee and ports. (11 lessons.)
7. **Level 7 — Civilisations of the Ancient World.** The secondary cycle returns to the ancient world whole: what history, civilisation and religion mean; Yemen as the Arabs' first homeland and source of their migrations; why the Arab civilisations rose, how they flourished in Asia and Africa, and how they fell; China, India, Japan, Persia, Greece, the Hellenistic world and Rome; the Aztecs, Maya, Inca, North America's first peoples and Aboriginal Australia; and the Arab caravan kingdoms — Nabataeans, Palmyra, Manadhira, Kinda — the markets before Islam, and Yemen on Islam's eve. (22 lessons.)
8. **Level 8 — Arab Islamic Civilisation, and the Modern World to Today.** The Arab Islamic civilisation whole — its foundations in the Sharia's objectives, why it flourished, era by era from Prophethood to the Mamluks, its landmarks of democracy, shura, social justice, human rights and ijtihad, Yemen's own role carrying Islam to Asia, Africa and Europe and its Zaydi, Sulayhid, Rasulid and Tahirid states, the civilisation beyond the homeland and why its science rose and declined — then the modern world: Renaissance and discoveries again, colonialism and the Arab awakening, the French, American and Chinese revolutions, German, American, European and Arab unity beside Yemeni unity, the two world wars, the Cold War, the Arab–Zionist wars, colonialism's collapse, the new colonialism, the scientific-technological revolution and today's Arab issues. (46 lessons.)

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Book 1 — Yemen Grade 3 G3 Social Studies** — History begins inside the child's own family — naming parents, grandparents, uncles and aunts and their bonds — and with lived time (day, week, Hijri and Gregorian years) before any nation or past civilisation appears. *(Student book.)*

**Book 2 — Yemen Grade 4 G4 Social Studies** — National history arrives as four dated celebrations in one unit — two revolutions, unification, and the 21 September 2014 revolution — each told as a people rising, a named first martyr or enemy, and a victory date to remember. *(Student book.)*

**Book 3 — Yemen Grade 5 G5 History** — History opens as a chain of prophets — Adam, Noah, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim — told from Qur'anic narrative, then turns to Yemen's own ancient kingdoms and to three foreign occupations (Roman, Abyssinian, Persian) that Yemenis resist, ending at the birth of Islam. *(Student book.)*

**Book 4 — Yemen Grade 6 G6 History** — Islamic history is told with a Zaydi lens — Ali and the Imams stand beside the caliphs in every age, Husayn's stand at Karbala is a turning point, and after each general age a Yemen lesson follows (Yemen in the Prophet's age, Yemen under the caliphs, Yemen's own Zaydi, Sulayhid and Najahid states). *(Student book.)*

**Book 5 — Yemen Grade 7 G7 History Part 1** — The preface states the method outright — history is read through the Qur'an as the truest document, prophets are the spine of every age, civilisations rise by justice and God's unity and fall by injustice, and the point is Yemeni faith identity; each prophet's story is told in full narrative scenes with pictures, then mined for lessons. *(Student book.)*

**Book 6 — Yemen Grade 8 G8 History Part 1** — The same Qur'an-centred method, now applied to the Israelite story at length — Yusuf, Musa, Dawud, Sulayman — with Bilqis and the Tubba' As'ad al-Kamil pulling Yemen into the sacred story, and a closing wall-chart that lines all of history up as one confrontation between truth and falsehood from Adam to the present day. *(Student book.)*

**Book 7 — Yemen Grade 9 G9 History Part 1** — An older-style textbook — dense text, maps and timelines — that teaches modern Arab history as one long struggle of the umma against foreign domination, with Yemen's own Ottoman and British centuries given a full unit of their own and the preface itself telling the story through 1962, the siege of Sanaa and the Seventy Days. *(Student book.)*

**Book 8 — Yemen Grade 10 G10 History Part 1** — The whole ancient world is told as one story of civilisational interaction whose heart is the ancient ARAB civilisations, with Yemen as the Arabs' southern gateway, first homeland and the first source of the Semitic migrations; every civilisation is weighed on the same grid — factors of rise, then political, economic, social, religious, intellectual and urban manifestations, then its relations with the Arabs — and the Qur'an is cited as testimony. *(Student book.)*

**Book 9 — Yemen Grade 11 G11 History Part 1** — Arab Islamic civilisation is told as one continuous story from the Prophet's Medina to the Mamluks, weighed era by era on the same grid — political, administrative, judicial, scientific, economic, military, urban and artistic manifestations — with Yemen placed at the civilisation's heart as a leading source of its spread into Asia, Africa and Europe; the final unit then reads shura, social justice, human rights and ijtihad back out of that history as the roots of democracy. *(Student book.)*

**Book 10 — Yemen Grade 11 G11 History Part 2** — Part 2 keeps Yemen at the centre of the whole Arab Islamic civilisation — its first unit is devoted entirely to Yemen's own role (conquerors, callers, states, scholars), and even the outside-the-homeland units keep routing the story through Yemeni merchants, sailors and emigrants; the book ends by weighing why the civilisation's science rose, what it gave Europe, and why it faded. *(Student book.)*

**Book 11 — Yemen Grade 12 G12 History** — World history is told with Yemen at its heart — the preface retells Yemen's own story inside the world story (26 September 1962, 14 October 1963, the 1967 evacuation, the lifting of Sanaa's siege, unity with democracy on 22 May 1990), a full lesson each is given to the Yemeni Revolution and to Yemeni unity, and every world event is closed by asking what it did to the Arab homeland. *(Student book.)*
