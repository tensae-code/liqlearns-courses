# The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

## Do it first

A war with no declaration, no final battle, and two armed camps facing each other for forty years. Its front line runs through Turkey's own straits. Say out loud: "Two armed camps, one frozen war." This lesson tells what the Cold War was.

## The story

The results lesson ended with two superpowers facing each other. This lesson names their contest: the **Cold War** — the struggle between the **Eastern bloc**, led by the Soviet Union, and the **Western bloc**, led by the United States, that organises the world from the late 1940s.

Why 'cold'? Because the two giants, armed with arsenals that could end civilisation — nuclear weapons among them — never fight each other **directly**. Instead the war is fought by every other means: **alliances** (NATO against the Warsaw Pact, ringing the blocs with member states), an **arms race**, rival **ideologies** preached to every new nation, espionage, propaganda — and hot little wars at the edges, fought through allies and clients, Korea first among them.

Every country is pressed to locate itself on the blocs' map — and the last lesson showed Turkey's answer. The book's beat for this part is geographic: **the world splits into two armed camps, and Turkey's straits put it on the front line** — the Western alliance's south-eastern bastion, facing the Soviet Union across the Black Sea and the Caucasus.

Three pages carry the definition; the rest of the book lives inside it. Hold the shape: two blocs, one frozen centre, hot edges — and Turkey posted at the gate between them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Cold War split the world into two armed camps — the Eastern and Western blocs — a war of alliances, arms and ideology fought without direct battle between the superpowers; and Turkey's Straits put it on the front line of that frozen conflict. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
