# Lesson plan — The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Cold War split the world into two armed camps — the Eastern and Western blocs — a war of alliances, arms and ideology fought without direct battle between the superpowers; and Turkey's Straits put it on the front line of that frozen conflict.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Cold War split the world into two armed camps — the Eastern and Western blocs — a war of alliances, arms and ideology fought without direct battle between the superpowers; and Turkey's Straits put it on the front line of that frozen conflict.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — two giants standing chest to chest in a doorway, neither daring to swing, while everyone else must pick a side of the doorway to stand on.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks 'cold war' means a minor or pretend war → the coldness is only at the centre: the superpowers never fought directly, but the arms race, the alliances and the hot proxy wars at the edges were deadly real — Korea cost the Turkish brigade of the last lesson its battles.
- Go back to the one thing: The Cold War split the world into two armed camps — the Eastern and Western blocs — a war of alliances, arms and ideology fought without direct battle between the superpowers; and Turkey's Straits put it on the front line of that frozen conflict. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
