# The Second World War: Causes

## Do it first

After the Great War, the world swore: never again. It built a League, signed pacts, outlawed war on paper. Twenty years later it was at war again, worse than before. Say out loud: "The world built paper walls — and the crash blew them down." This lesson tells why the peace failed.

## The story

Turkey's book pauses its national story to ask the world's question: why did the peace of 1919 collapse into a second world war within twenty years? Its answer comes in two parts — the walls built, and the wind that felled them.

The walls were **paper**: the **League of Nations**, the world organisation meant to settle disputes by talk instead of war; the **Locarno** treaties, guaranteeing borders in the west by mutual promise; and the **Kellogg-Briand Pact**, in which the nations of the world solemnly renounced war itself. On paper, war was abolished. In fact, the League had no army of its own, the promises bound only while governments wished them to, and aggressors learned they could tear the paper and survive.

The wind was the **1929 world economic crisis**: the crash that began in America and rolled across the world — factories idle, banks failing, millions unemployed, democracies discredited. In the despair, peoples turned to regimes that promised bread, work and national pride by any means — the dictatorships whose ambitions the paper walls could not stop.

Two pages carry the whole causation, and their beat is the lesson: **the world builds paper walls against war, and the crash blows them down**. The next lesson fights the war those failures made.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The road to the Second World War runs through failed peace-keeping — the League of Nations, Locarno and Kellogg-Briand, paper walls against war — and through the 1929 world economic crisis, which blew those walls down and lifted the regimes that marched the world back to war. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
