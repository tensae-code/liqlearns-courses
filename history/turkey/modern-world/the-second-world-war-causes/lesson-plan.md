# Lesson plan — The Second World War: Causes

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Second World War: Causes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The road to the Second World War runs through failed peace-keeping — the League of Nations, Locarno and Kellogg-Briand, paper walls against war — and through the 1929 world economic crisis, which blew those walls down and lifted the regimes that marched the world back to war.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The road to the Second World War runs through failed peace-keeping — the League of Nations, Locarno and Kellogg-Briand, paper walls against war — and through the 1929 world economic crisis, which blew those walls down and lifted the regimes that marched the world back to war.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a street that agrees, in writing, that nobody will fight — and then loses all its jobs in one winter. The paper agreement is still on the wall; nobody believes in it anymore.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Kellogg-Briand failed because nobody signed it → the opposite: the world signed; the failure is enforcement — paper promises with no force behind them, in a decade when the crash made peoples stop believing in them.
- Go back to the one thing: The road to the Second World War runs through failed peace-keeping — the League of Nations, Locarno and Kellogg-Briand, paper walls against war — and through the 1929 world economic crisis, which blew those walls down and lifted the regimes that marched the world back to war. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
