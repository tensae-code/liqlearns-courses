# Hands-on — The Second World War: Causes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
After the Great War, the world swore: never again. It built a League, signed pacts, outlawed war on paper. Twenty years later it was at war again, worse than before. Say out loud: "The world built paper walls — and the crash blew them down."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The road to the Second World War runs through failed peace-keeping — the League of Nations, Locarno and Kellogg-Briand, paper walls against war — and through the 1929 world economic crisis, which blew those walls down and lifted the regimes that marched the world back to war. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
