# Hands-on — Atatürk's Reforms Remake Turkey

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A revolution that cannot explain itself will not last. This one explains itself in six words — then changes the alphabet you read, the clock on your wall, the name you carry and the hat on your head. Say out loud: "Six words, and everything rebuilt to match."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Turkish Revolution explains itself in six principles — republicanism, nationalism, populism, statism, secularism, revolutionism — and then rebuilds everything to match: the state (sultanate abolished 1 November 1922, the Republic proclaimed, the caliphate abolished), the school, the alphabet, the clock and the name (Surname Law, 21 June 1934; rights for women), and the economy and health of the new nation, launched at the İzmir Economic Congress of 1923. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
