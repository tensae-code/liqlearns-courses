# Lesson plan — Atatürk's Reforms Remake Turkey

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Atatürk's Reforms Remake Turkey
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Turkish Revolution explains itself in six principles — republicanism, nationalism, populism, statism, secularism, revolutionism — and then rebuilds everything to match: the state (sultanate abolished 1 November 1922, the Republic proclaimed, the caliphate abolished), the school, the alphabet, the clock and the name (Surname Law, 21 June 1934; rights for women), and the economy and health of the new nation, launched at the İzmir Economic Congress of 1923.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The six principles and the new state** — The revolution's own name for its doctrine is **Atatürkism**, and it explains itself in **six principles** — the six arrows: **republicanism** (the nation's sovereignty, ruled as a republic), **nationalism** (the nation's unity and pride), **populism** (rule for the whole people, no privileged class), **statism** (the state steering the economy where private hands are too weak), **secularism** (religion and state affairs separated), and **revolutionism** (the reforms themselves, kept moving).
2. **Education, culture and social life** — Now the revolution walks out of the palace and into the street, the school and the home.
3. **Economy and health** — The revolution's third front is bread and health.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Drowns in the six principles → anchor each to its stroke: republicanism → the Republic proclaimed; secularism → caliphate abolished and education unified; statism → the state's factories where private capital fell short; revolutionism → the reforms kept moving; nationalism and populism → the nation's sovereignty for the whole people, no privileged class.
- Thinks the reforms were only political → the unit's own map is wider: education, alphabet, calendar and surnames, women's rights, the economy and health are the same revolution in other rooms.
- Go back to the one thing: The Turkish Revolution explains itself in six principles — republicanism, nationalism, populism, statism, secularism, revolutionism — and then rebuilds everything to match: the state (sultanate abolished 1 November 1922, the Republic proclaimed, the caliphate abolished), the school, the alphabet, the clock and the name (Surname Law, 21 June 1934; rights for women), and the economy and health of the new nation, launched at the İzmir Economic Congress of 1923. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
