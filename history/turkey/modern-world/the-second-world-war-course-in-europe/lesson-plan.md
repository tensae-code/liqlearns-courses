# Lesson plan — The Second World War: The Course of the War

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Second World War: The Course of the War
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Through the Second World War's course, Turkey — a country that had lost an empire in the last war — stayed out: armed, neutral and courted by both camps, balancing each against the other until a symbolic declaration of war in February 1945, at the very end.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Through the Second World War's course, Turkey — a country that had lost an empire in the last war — stayed out: armed, neutral and courted by both camps, balancing each against the other until a symbolic declaration of war in February 1945, at the very end.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a house between two street gangs, whose owner keeps both doors politely answered, the lights on, and a strong lock — and never joins either fight.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Turkey's neutrality was passivity → the book's three faces are active: armed to be believed, trading and treating with both camps, and refusing every invitation — a policy walked year by year, at great cost.
- Go back to the one thing: Through the Second World War's course, Turkey — a country that had lost an empire in the last war — stayed out: armed, neutral and courted by both camps, balancing each against the other until a symbolic declaration of war in February 1945, at the very end. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
