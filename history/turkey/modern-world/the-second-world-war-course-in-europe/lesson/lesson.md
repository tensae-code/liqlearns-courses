# The Second World War: The Course of the War

## Do it first

The whole world is fighting — and one country, sitting on the straits both sides need, refuses to fight at all. Both camps court it; both camps threaten it; it arms, waits, and balances. Say out loud: "Armed, neutral, and courted by both sides." This lesson tells Turkey's Second World War — the war it stayed out of.

## The story

The unit tells the war's **course** — the great campaigns that run from 1939 to 1945, Europe's war widening into a world war — but Turkey's book tells it with Turkey's own thread running through: what did the Republic do while the world burned?

Its answer is a policy with three faces. **Armed**: Turkey mobilised and kept a great army under arms for six years, an enormous burden for a poor country — the price of being believed. **Neutral**: it signed with neither camp's war — treaties and trade with both, membership of none. **Courted**: sitting on the Straits and the road between Europe and the Middle East, Turkey was wooed by Britain, Germany and the Soviets in turn — Churchill himself flying to meet its leaders — each camp bidding for its entry, or at least its continued quiet.

The balance was walked year by year: leaning where the danger was not, trading chrome and food, refusing every invitation that would make Turkey a battlefield again. The generation ruling it had itself fought 1914–1922; the book's beat is their motive: **a country that lost an empire in the last war spends this one armed, neutral and courted by both sides**.

Only at the very end, in **February 1945**, with the war decided, does Turkey make a **symbolic declaration of war** on the Axis — a signature that costs no battles and earns the new Turkey its seat among the founders of the coming United Nations. The next lesson counts the war's results.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Through the Second World War's course, Turkey — a country that had lost an empire in the last war — stayed out: armed, neutral and courted by both camps, balancing each against the other until a symbolic declaration of war in February 1945, at the very end. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
