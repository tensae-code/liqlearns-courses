# Turkey 1960–1990: Coups and Cyprus

## Do it first

Three times in thirty years, the army steps in. One island, just off the southern coast, pulls the country to the brink again and again — until 1974. And underneath the politics, a whole people moves from village to city. Say out loud: "Interventions, an island, and a country remade." This lesson is Turkey, 1960–1990.

## The story

The Turkey part of Unit 7 braids two strands: the foreign policy of the decades, and the decades themselves.

Abroad, one file dominates: **Cyprus** — the island off the southern coast, shared by Greek and Turkish Cypriots, independent in 1960 with Turkey among its guarantors, then sliding into intercommunal violence that repeatedly brings Turkey and **Greece** — NATO allies — to the brink of war with each other. The crisis **ends in 1974**: a Greek junta-backed coup on the island aiming at union with Greece, and Turkey's military intervention in reply — the island divided from that year to this. Around Cyprus stand the decades' other files: **relations with Greece** across the Aegean, and the **Armenian question** — the campaign of claims and, in the 1970s–80s, attacks on Turkish diplomats that poisons Turkey's foreign relations.

At home, the braid's other strand: **Turkey 1960–1990, its political, economic and socio-cultural developments** — in the book's beat, **three military interventions** (1960, 1971, 1980) punctuate the decades, each resetting the political order; while beneath them the country is **remade by migration and markets**: millions leave the villages for the cities and for work abroad in Europe, industry and exports grow, and the Turkey of 1990 — urban, commercial, media-filled — is a different society from the peasant republic of 1960.

Hold the braid whole: coups and Cyprus above, migration and markets below — a country arguing about its rule while transforming its life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Turkey's 1960–1990 decades are a double story: at home, three military interventions punctuate a country remade by migration and markets; abroad, foreign policy is dominated by the Cyprus problem — the island crisis that ends in 1974 — by relations with Greece, and by the Armenian question. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
