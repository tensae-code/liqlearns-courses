# Turkey Joins the West: Cold War and NATO

## Do it first

A party that founded a republic loses an election — and hands over power, peacefully. In the same five years, the country joins the West's councils and its alliance, and sends soldiers to Korea. Say out loud: "The republic votes — and anchors itself to the West." This lesson tells Turkey's 1945–1950 turn.

## The story

The postwar unit opens at home, with the change the between-wars lesson left waiting: the **passage to multi-party life, 1945–1950**. The founding party's monopoly ends; a real opposition — the **Democrat Party** — organises, campaigns on the people's grievances and hopes, and in the election of **1950 wins**. The book's beat is the wonder of it: **the republic votes its founders' party out** — and power changes hands by ballot, the founders' leader accepting the verdict. Democracy, promised since 1923, is practised at last.

The same five years turn the country's face outward. Facing Soviet pressure on the Straits and the eastern provinces — the new superpower demanding what the old Russia always wanted — Turkey chooses its camp: the **West**. The steps are dated: the **Council of Europe (1949)**, the club of Europe's democracies; then the proof in blood — a Turkish **brigade in the Korean War**, fighting under the United Nations flag on the far side of the world, whose stand earns Turkey its seat in the alliance: **NATO membership (1952)**.

The **Turkey of the 1950s** is the fruit of both turns: Democrat governments, American aid and alliance, tractors and roads remaking the villages, a freer press and market — the republic's second founding, western in alliance and (for the decade) democratic in form.

Hold the two halves together as the book binds them: in the same five years, the ballot box at home and the Western anchor abroad — each, in the telling, the other's guarantee.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In five years after 1945 the Republic changed camps and constitutions at once: the passage to multi-party life (1945–1950) ended with the Democrat Party's 1950 victory — the republic voting its founders' party out — and Turkey anchored itself to the West: the Council of Europe (1949), a brigade in the Korean War, and NATO membership, remaking the Turkey of the 1950s. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
