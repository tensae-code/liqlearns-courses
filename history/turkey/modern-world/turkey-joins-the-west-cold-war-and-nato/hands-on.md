# Hands-on — Turkey Joins the West: Cold War and NATO

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A party that founded a republic loses an election — and hands over power, peacefully. In the same five years, the country joins the West's councils and its alliance, and sends soldiers to Korea. Say out loud: "The republic votes — and anchors itself to the West."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In five years after 1945 the Republic changed camps and constitutions at once: the passage to multi-party life (1945–1950) ended with the Democrat Party's 1950 victory — the republic voting its founders' party out — and Turkey anchored itself to the West: the Council of Europe (1949), a brigade in the Korean War, and NATO membership, remaking the Turkey of the 1950s. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
