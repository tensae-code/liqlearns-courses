# The Middle East Wars, 1967–1988

## Do it first

South of Turkey, the map drawn in 1948 catches fire again and again: 1967, 1973, and then an eight-year war between two neighbours, Iran and Iraq. Say out loud: "Three generations of wars on the southern doorstep." This lesson surveys the Middle East wars of 1967–1988.

## The story

Unit 7's world part looks south, at the region the results lesson left redrawn: the **Middle East** of new states, among them Israel — and the conflict born with it, now fought out in open wars.

The **Arab-Israeli wars** of this period are wars of days and of years: in **1967**, the Six-Day War — Israel striking first and taking the Sinai, the West Bank, Gaza and the Golan in less than a week, a map redrawn at a stroke; in **1973**, Egypt and Syria striking back on Yom Kippur, the war that brought the superpowers to the brink and the oil weapon into the world's economy. Between and after them, Lebanon's civil war draws the region's armies in — the conflict metastasising across the neighbourhood.

Then the second fire: the **Iran-Iraq War (1980–1988)** — revolutionary Iran and Saddam's Iraq locked in eight years of trenches, missiles and enormous loss, the longest conventional war of the century, ending exhausted where it began.

Through all of it runs the period's great lever: **oil** — the region's weight in the world redrawn by the price and supply of its one treasure, felt in every capital including Ankara. The book's beat is Turkey's vantage: **Turkey's southern neighbours fight three generations of wars while oil redraws their weight in the world** — and Turkey, bordering Syria, Iraq and Iran alike, watches its own borders through every one of them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the age the book calls the age of social revolution, Turkey's southern neighbours fought three generations of wars — the Arab-Israeli wars and the long Iran-Iraq War — while oil redrew the region's weight in the world and Turkey watched its own borders. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
