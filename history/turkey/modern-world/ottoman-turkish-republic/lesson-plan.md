# Lesson plan — Ottoman to Turkish Republic: Mudanya and Lausanne

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Ottoman to Turkish Republic: Mudanya and Lausanne
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Empire's End and the War of Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** What the sword won, the pen kept: the Mudanya Armistice (11 October 1922) silenced the guns, and the Lausanne Peace Treaty (24 July 1923) won the peace — the world's recognition of the new Turkey and the burial of Sèvres.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — The Empire's End and the War of Independence (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: What the sword won, the pen kept: the Mudanya Armistice (11 October 1922) silenced the guns, and the Lausanne Peace Treaty (24 July 1923) won the peace — the world's recognition of the new Turkey and the burial of Sèvres.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — winning a match is one thing; getting the result written into the league's official record, recognised by everyone, is another. Lausanne is the writing-in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses the two documents → Mudanya (October 1922) is the armistice — it stops the fighting; Lausanne (July 1923) is the peace treaty — it wins recognition. Guns first, then the peace.
- Go back to the one thing: What the sword won, the pen kept: the Mudanya Armistice (11 October 1922) silenced the guns, and the Lausanne Peace Treaty (24 July 1923) won the peace — the world's recognition of the new Turkey and the burial of Sèvres. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
