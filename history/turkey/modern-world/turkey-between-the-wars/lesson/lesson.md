# Turkey Between the Wars

## Do it first

A new state, surrounded by the empires and kingdoms it fought yesterday. Its founder gives it one sentence of foreign policy: peace at home, peace in the world. Say out loud the sentence — then watch it practised, border by border. This lesson is Turkey between the wars.

## The story

At home first. The Republic's first decades are one-party rule with experiments: **early parties** are founded and the **multi-party tries fail** — opposition parties close or are closed when they become rallying points for reaction — and in **1926 an assassination attempt** on Atatürk is uncovered and punished. The revolution guards itself while it builds; full multi-party life waits for the next level's lesson.

Abroad, the maxim rules: **'peace at home, peace in the world.'** The book walks the borders one by one. With **Greece**, the former enemy: a settlement and then friendship — the two Aegean rivals become partners. With **France** and **Britain**, the wartime occupiers: relations normalised, and the hardest file, **Mosul** — the oil-rich province claimed under the National Pact — settled with Britain by negotiation (1926), accepted though it went Britain's way. With the **Soviets**, the northern neighbour: friendship and neutrality treaties from the struggle years maintained.

Then Turkey steps onto the world's stage as a joiner and builder: the **League of Nations (1932)** — invited in, a former enemy now a member in good standing; the **Balkan Entente (1934)** — Turkey, Greece and their Balkan neighbours guaranteeing their common borders; **Montreux (1936)** — the convention that restores full Turkish sovereignty over the **Straits**, with the right to fortify them, won at a conference table; the **Sadabad Pact (1937)** — the eastern counterpart with Iran, Iraq and Afghanistan. And at the very end of the period, **Hatay joins the homeland** (1939 by the process completed then) — the last territorial file of the National Pact closed by diplomacy.

Over it all stands the founder's clock, running out: on **10 November 1938**, at the 09:05 the Grade 2 poem remembers, **Atatürk dies**. The beat of the whole lesson: **'peace at home, peace in the world' is practised border by border while the founder's chair empties**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between the wars, Turkey practised Atatürk's maxim — 'peace at home, peace in the world' — border by border: settling with Greece, France, Britain (over Mosul) and the Soviets, joining the League of Nations (1932), building the Balkan Entente (1934), regaining the Straits at Montreux (1936) and the Sadabad Pact (1937), and bringing Hatay home — while at home the early multi-party tries failed, an assassination attempt was made (1926), and the founder died on 10 November 1938. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
