# Hands-on — Turkey Between the Wars

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A new state, surrounded by the empires and kingdoms it fought yesterday. Its founder gives it one sentence of foreign policy: peace at home, peace in the world. Say out loud the sentence — then watch it practised, border by border.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Between the wars, Turkey practised Atatürk's maxim — 'peace at home, peace in the world' — border by border: settling with Greece, France, Britain (over Mosul) and the Soviets, joining the League of Nations (1932), building the Balkan Entente (1934), regaining the Straits at Montreux (1936) and the Sadabad Pact (1937), and bringing Hatay home — while at home the early multi-party tries failed, an assassination attempt was made (1926), and the founder died on 10 November 1938. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
