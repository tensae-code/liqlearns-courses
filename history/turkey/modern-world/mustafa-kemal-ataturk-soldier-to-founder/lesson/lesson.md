# Mustafa Kemal Atatürk: Soldier to Founder

## Do it first

Before the general, there is a schoolboy. Before the founder of a republic, there are teachers whose names his country still knows. Say out loud: "First the schoolboy, then the soldier, then the founder." This lesson walks Mustafa Kemal's education and early military life.

## The story

Turkey's final-year history book does not open with an empire or a war. It opens with a **child in school** — Mustafa, the boy who will become **Mustafa Kemal**, and then **Atatürk**, founder of the Republic. The book's way is deliberate: the republic's founder is introduced the Turkish way, **as a schoolboy formed by teachers before he is a general**.

His schooling climbs a ladder. First **Şemsi Efendi's school** in Salonica — the modern school his father chose, against the traditional start others wanted for him; the choice itself is remembered as the first turn of his life. Then the **military schools**: Salonica's military middle school — where a teacher gives him the name **Kemal** — and **Monastir's** military high school, where history and literature fire the cadet's mind. Then Istanbul: the **War College** and the **War Academy**, where he graduates a staff officer, trained to plan wars, not only fight them.

The young officer's **postings** draw the map of a dying empire: **Damascus** in Syria, his first station, where he sees the empire's Arab lands and their discontents; service in the Balkan wars' world; and **Sofia**, as military attaché in Bulgaria — a soldier-diplomat watching Europe arm itself on the eve of the Great War.

Every station teaches the same double lesson the book wants drawn: the empire is failing — and a generation of officers is learning, in its schools and its far-flung posts, exactly why, and what a new state would need. The founder is being formed, lesson by lesson, before the founding begins.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Republic's founder is introduced the Turkish way — as a schoolboy formed by teachers before he is a general: Şemsi Efendi's school, the military schools of Salonica and Monastir, the War College and Academy, and postings from Damascus to Sofia that made the soldier who would found a state. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
