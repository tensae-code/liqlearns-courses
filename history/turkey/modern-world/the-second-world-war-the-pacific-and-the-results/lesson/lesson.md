# The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results

## Do it first

The guns stop. Now count what the war changed: who is strong, who is finished, what new lines are drawn on the map — including on Turkey's own doorstep. Say out loud: "Two superpowers inherit the world." This lesson surveys the results of the Second World War.

## The story

The results part of the unit is short — two pages — and total. It reads the war's balance sheet in three columns: political, social and economic.

**Politically**, the headline is the book's beat: **two superpowers inherit the world** — the **United States** and the **Soviet Union**, the only powers left standing at full strength, facing each other across a ruined Europe whose old empires — Britain's and France's among them — are waning. A new world organisation, the **United Nations**, rises from the League's failure, meant to keep the peace with the force the League lacked. The colonial world begins its long exit: independence movements everywhere take heart and arms.

**Socially and economically**, the counts are of ruin and rebuilding: tens of millions dead, cities in rubble, economies shattered — and the victors' worlds rebuilt on new plans, welfare states in the west, command economies in the east, with the superpowers' rivalry already organising the peace into camps — the Cold War of the next lessons.

And the book turns last to Turkey's own neighbourhood: **the developments in the Middle East** — the region next door redrawn as mandates end and new states appear, among them Israel (1948), and with it the Arab-Israeli conflict that fills a later unit of this very book. The results lesson thus ends where Turkey lives: between the superpowers' contest and a redrawn Middle East.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Second World War's results redrew the world: two superpowers — the United States and the Soviet Union — inherited it, the old empires waned, a new world organisation rose, and next door to Turkey the Middle East was redrawn, with new states and new conflicts. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
