# Lesson plan — The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Second World War's results redrew the world: two superpowers — the United States and the Soviet Union — inherited it, the old empires waned, a new world organisation rose, and next door to Turkey the Middle East was redrawn, with new states and new conflicts.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — The Republic Remade and Turkey in the World (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Second World War's results redrew the world: two superpowers — the United States and the Soviet Union — inherited it, the old empires waned, a new world organisation rose, and next door to Turkey the Middle East was redrawn, with new states and new conflicts.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — the morning after a great storm: two tallest trees still standing, the old fence lines gone, and every garden on the street redrawn — the results lesson is the morning-after survey.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Britain and France emerged as the war's chief winners → the book's headline is the opposite: the old empires waned; the inheritors are the two superpowers, the USA and the USSR.
- Go back to the one thing: The Second World War's results redrew the world: two superpowers — the United States and the Soviet Union — inherited it, the old empires waned, a new world organisation rose, and next door to Turkey the Middle East was redrawn, with new states and new conflicts. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
