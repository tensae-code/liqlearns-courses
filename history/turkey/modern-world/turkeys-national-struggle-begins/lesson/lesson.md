# Turkey's National Struggle Begins

## Do it first

The war is lost. Foreign soldiers are landing. The capital's government signs what it is told to sign. And in the Anatolian towns, officers and ordinary people begin to organise — a second state, in secret and then in the open. Say out loud: "Anatolia refuses." This lesson tells how the National Struggle was prepared.

## The story

The war ends for the empire with the **Mondros armistice** (1918): the guns stop, the army is to be demobilised, and the Allies take the right to occupy strategic points. Occupation follows — foreign troops in the capital and the ports, and in 1919 Greek forces land at İzmir. The empire of six centuries is being **occupied and disarmed** in its own house.

The first answer is local and irregular: the **Kuvâ-yi Milliye** — the National Forces — townsmen, veterans and officers resisting occupation in their own districts, buying time with rifles while something bigger is built. And the builder arrives: **Mustafa Kemal**, landed in Samsun in May 1919 with an inspector's papers — and a purpose far beyond them.

The building is done by documents and congresses, and the book names them in order. The **Amasya Circular** declares the principle: the nation's independence will be saved by the nation's own determination — and calls a congress. The **Erzurum** and **Sivas congresses** gather Anatolia's representatives, reject foreign mandates, and proclaim one indivisible homeland and one national resistance. In April 1920 the movement opens its own parliament in Ankara: the **Grand National Assembly** — a second state, organised in Anatolia.

Against all this stands the capital's signature: the **Treaty of Sèvres** (1920), partitioning the homeland among the victors — the empire's **death warrant**, signed in Istanbul. The book's beat holds both halves: **an occupied, disarmed empire's officers and towns organise a second state in Anatolia while the capital signs its death warrant**. The fronts of the next lesson are that refusal, armed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Out of defeat and occupation, the National Struggle is prepared: the Mondros armistice, the occupations, the Kuvâ-yi Milliye resistance, the Amasya Circular, the Erzurum and Sivas congresses, the opening of the Grand National Assembly — and the Treaty of Sèvres, the death warrant the capital signed and Anatolia refused. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
