# Turkey After the Cold War

## Do it first

The wall falls. The superpower that faced Turkey across the Black Sea dissolves. And in the same years, war comes to the neighbours — Iraq invades Kuwait, and the Gulf burns on Turkey's own border. Say out loud: "A new world, and the republic renegotiates its place." This final lesson brings Turkey to the twenty-first century's threshold.

## The story

The Grade 12 book's last unit is its longest, and its subject is the world remade: **Turkey and the world on the threshold of the twenty-first century** — the years after 1990, when the Cold War order that organised everything since 1945 simply ends.

**The wall falls**: the Berlin Wall comes down (1989), the Eastern bloc dissolves, and the **Soviet Union itself breaks up** (1991). Turkey's front-line geography of the Cold War lessons loses its meaning overnight: the Black Sea and the Caucasus are no longer a superpower's shore but a ring of new states — several of them Turkic republics, opening a whole new field of kinship and policy to the east.

But the neighbourhood does not calm; it **catches fire**. Iraq's invasion of Kuwait brings the **Gulf War (1991)** to Turkey's southern border — sanctions, refugees and a no-fly zone remaking the north of Iraq — and the decade's other fires, in the Balkans and the Caucasus, burn around Turkey's other edges. The front-line state becomes a **frontier state in every direction at once**.

So the republic **renegotiates its place between Europe and its region**: pressing its European vocation — customs union and candidacy steps toward the European Union — while building new weight as a bridge and a power in its own neighbourhood, Turkic east, Balkans and Middle East alike. The book closes there, deliberately open: the story beat is a renegotiation, not a settlement — **the wall falls, the neighbourhood catches fire, and the republic renegotiates its place between Europe and its region**.

That is also where this course ends: Turkey's own books have carried the learner from a seven-year-old's poems to the twenty-first century's threshold. The ladder stops where the books in hand stop.
