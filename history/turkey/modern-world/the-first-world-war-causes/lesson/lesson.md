# The First World War: Causes

## Do it first

A war starts in Europe between other people's empires. The Ottoman state does not have to enter it — and then it does, by one signature, betting everything. Say out loud: "The empire gambled its existence on one alliance." This lesson tells how the Great War took the Ottomans in.

## The story

The **First World War** begins in 1914 as a war of Europe's great powers and their alliance systems — empires bound to fight beside their partners, pulling one another into a general war. The book's Ottoman question is narrower: how did the war become **the empire's** war?

The answer is an **alliance**. The Ottoman government — the strained, indebted state of the last lesson, fearing Russia above all and hoping to recover lost ground — binds itself to **Germany and the Central Powers**. With that signature, and the first shots that follow it, the empire is in the war on every front its geography touches.

The book's story beat names the size of the bet: **the empire gambles its existence on one alliance**. This is not a border war it can lose and survive; the alliance stakes the state itself — win with its partners, and perhaps recover; lose, and there may be no empire left to save.

Two pages carry the causes and the entry; the fronts themselves fill the next lesson. Hold the proportion: the decision is small on paper and total in consequence — the last decision the old empire ever makes freely.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The First World War drew the Ottoman state in through alliance: the empire gambled its very existence on one alliance — joining the Central Powers — and so a European war became the empire's last war. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
