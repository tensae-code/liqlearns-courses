# Gallipoli and the Ottoman Fronts, 1914–1918

## Do it first

Count the fronts: east, south, west, and soldiers sent to fight in other people's countries entirely. One empire, bleeding on every side of the map. And one strait where it won the victory its people still sing about. Say out loud: "Many fronts — one immortal victory." This lesson walks the Ottoman fronts of the Great War.

## The story

The alliance of the last lesson had a geography, and geography made the Ottoman war enormous. The book sorts the fronts into three kinds, and the sorting is the lesson.

**Offensive fronts** — where the empire attacked: the **Caucasus**, against Russia in the snow of the east, and the **Canal** front, striking at Britain's Suez lifeline in Egypt. Both gambles failed at great cost; the Caucasus winter alone became a byword for disaster.

**Defensive fronts** — where the empire fought for its own ground: **Iraq**, **Syria-Palestine** and **Hijaz-Yemen** in the south, lost province by province to Britain and the Arab Revolt — and **Çanakkale — Gallipoli** — where the Allies tried to force the straits and take Istanbul, and were thrown back in the war's most famous Ottoman stand. At Çanakkale the defence was bound up with a divisional commander whose name the book has already taught: **Mustafa Kemal**. The story beat is exact: the empire's **one immortal victory is won by the colonel who will found the republic**.

And **aid fronts** — Ottoman soldiers sent beyond the empire entirely, to **Galicia, Romania and Macedonia**, fighting for allies on Europe's own battlefields. No other ally spread itself so wide: **the empire fights on more fronts than any ally** — and the width of the map is itself a cause of the exhaustion that ends the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The empire fought on more fronts than any ally — offensive fronts (Caucasus, the Canal), defensive fronts (Çanakkale/Gallipoli, Iraq, Syria-Palestine, Hijaz-Yemen) and aid fronts (Galicia, Romania, Macedonia) — and its one immortal victory, at Çanakkale, was won under the colonel who would found the Republic. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
