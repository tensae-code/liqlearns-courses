# Hands-on — Gallipoli and the Ottoman Fronts, 1914–1918

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Count the fronts: east, south, west, and soldiers sent to fight in other people's countries entirely. One empire, bleeding on every side of the map. And one strait where it won the victory its people still sing about. Say out loud: "Many fronts — one immortal victory."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The empire fought on more fronts than any ally — offensive fronts (Caucasus, the Canal), defensive fronts (Çanakkale/Gallipoli, Iraq, Syria-Palestine, Hijaz-Yemen) and aid fronts (Galicia, Romania, Macedonia) — and its one immortal victory, at Çanakkale, was won under the colonel who would found the Republic. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
