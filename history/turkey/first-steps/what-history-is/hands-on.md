# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Close your eyes and try to remember yesterday. Now try to remember a day a hundred years before you were born. You cannot — unless someone wrote it down, built it, buried it or told it. Say out loud: "History is how humankind remembers."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is the memory of humankind: it studies the human past with a method of its own, helped by sister sciences — and a people without memory cannot know itself, which is why nations learn it. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
