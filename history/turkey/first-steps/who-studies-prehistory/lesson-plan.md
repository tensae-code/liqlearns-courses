# Lesson plan — Who Studies Prehistory

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Who Studies Prehistory
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Before writing existed, the past left no documents — so the first humans and their Stone Ages are read by archaeology, from the only witnesses that survive: bones, stones and cave walls.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Before writing existed, the past left no documents — so the first humans and their Stone Ages are read by archaeology, from the only witnesses that survive: bones, stones and cave walls.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a detective working a case with no witnesses and no letters — only things left behind at the scene.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks prehistory means 'nothing is known' → much is known, but it is recovered from things, not read from documents: bones, stones and cave walls are witnesses, questioned by archaeology.
- Go back to the one thing: Before writing existed, the past left no documents — so the first humans and their Stone Ages are read by archaeology, from the only witnesses that survive: bones, stones and cave walls. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
