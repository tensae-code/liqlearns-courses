# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine a time with no writing at all. No books, no letters, no inscriptions — nobody could write anything down. How could we ever know those people lived? Say out loud: "The stones remember."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before writing existed, the past left no documents — so the first humans and their Stone Ages are read by archaeology, from the only witnesses that survive: bones, stones and cave walls. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
