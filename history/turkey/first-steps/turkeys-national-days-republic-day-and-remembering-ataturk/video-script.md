# Video script — Turkey's National Days: Republic Day and Remembering Atatürk

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Turkey's youngest children learn their country's memory as poems — three special days, learned by heart.
First, 29 October. Republic Day. The poem says: the season was autumn, the calendars showed 29 October, and the homeland's form of government became a republic. Picture the flags: the whole nation decks itself in red and white.
Then November. Atatürk Week, the 10th to the 16th. The poem remembers a clock stopped at 09:05 — five past nine — the moment the founder died. Picture the crowds walking up to Anıtkabir, his tomb in Ankara. The poem names him: patriotic, honest, determined, hardworking.
Then the newest day: 15 July, Democracy and National Unity Day. The poem tells of one night when the nation stood up for democracy — and won.
Three poems. Three dates. That is how a country first teaches its children who they are.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
