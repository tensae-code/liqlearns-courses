# Turkey's National Days: Republic Day and Remembering Atatürk

## Do it first

Say three dates out loud: 29 October. 10 November, at five past nine in the morning. 15 July. In Turkey, a child of seven can tell you what each of those days means — not from a history lesson, but from poems learned by heart. Say out loud: "A country teaches its children to remember." This lesson tells you the three days, and why they matter.

## The story

In Turkey, the school year is dotted with special days and weeks — and the youngest children meet them as **poems**, printed in big spreads in their very first school books, to be read aloud and learned by heart. Three of those poems carry the country's own story.

The first poem is for **29 October — Republic Day**. It tells the child: the season was autumn, the calendars showed 29 October, and our heaven-like homeland's form of government became a **republic**. On that day the whole nation decks itself in flags — the birthday of the Republic, celebrated by everyone, down to the smallest schoolchild with a flag in hand.

The second poem belongs to **Atatürk Week, 10–16 November**. It remembers the morning the founder of the Republic died — the moment of grief at **09:05**, five past nine. It tells of the crowds running to **Anıtkabir**, his great mausoleum in Ankara, and it names the man himself as the child should remember him: **patriotic, honest, determined and hardworking**.

The third poem is the newest: **15 July, Democracy and National Unity Day**. It retells the night when, in the poem's words, the nation and the army fought for democracy — and won. A seven-year-old learns it the same way as the other two: as a poem to recite, a day when ordinary people defended the country's democracy.

So before any history course begins, the memory is already planted: the Republic's birthday, its founder's death-day, and its newest day of unity — three poems, three dates, one country remembering itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Before Turkey's children learn history as a school subject, they learn their country's memory as poems: 29 October, Republic Day, when the homeland's form of government became a republic; Atatürk Week in November, remembering the founder at the 09:05 moment of his death; and 15 July, Democracy and National Unity Day, the night the nation stood up for democracy. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
