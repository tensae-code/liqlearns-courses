# Hands-on — Turkey's National Days: Republic Day and Remembering Atatürk

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say three dates out loud: 29 October. 10 November, at five past nine in the morning. 15 July. In Turkey, a child of seven can tell you what each of those days means — not from a history lesson, but from poems learned by heart. Say out loud: "A country teaches its children to remember."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before Turkey's children learn history as a school subject, they learn their country's memory as poems: 29 October, Republic Day, when the homeland's form of government became a republic; Atatürk Week in November, remembering the founder at the 09:05 moment of his death; and 15 July, Democracy and National Unity Day, the night the nation stood up for democracy. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
