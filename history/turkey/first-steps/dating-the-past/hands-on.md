# Hands-on — Dating the Past

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say a year out loud — any year. Now answer: which century is it in? Which age? If you hesitated, you have met this lesson's problem. Say out loud: "First, cut the time. Then tell the story."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before any story of the past can be told, time itself must be cut into ages, centuries and eras, and read on calendars and timelines — that cutting and ordering is chronology's gift to history. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
