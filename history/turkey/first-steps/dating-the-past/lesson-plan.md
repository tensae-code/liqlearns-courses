# Lesson plan — Dating the Past

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Before any story of the past can be told, time itself must be cut into ages, centuries and eras, and read on calendars and timelines — that cutting and ordering is chronology's gift to history.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

## Before this lesson
- **What History Is** (`first-steps/what-history-is/`) is recommended first — it names chronology as one of history's helpers; this lesson is that helper at work (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Before any story of the past can be told, time itself must be cut into ages, centuries and eras, and read on calendars and timelines — that cutting and ordering is chronology's gift to history.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — your own life cut into parts — before school, primary school, this year — the same cutting, done on one small life.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts centuries wrong (the 1400s called the 14th century's neighbour) → centuries are counted by their order number: the years 1401–1500 are the 15th century, because the first century is years 1–100.
- Go back to the one thing: Before any story of the past can be told, time itself must be cut into ages, centuries and eras, and read on calendars and timelines — that cutting and ordering is chronology's gift to history. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
