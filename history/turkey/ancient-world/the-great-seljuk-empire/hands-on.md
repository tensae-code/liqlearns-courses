# Hands-on — The Great Seljuk Empire

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A clan of herdsmen rides out of the steppe and defeats an empire's army. Within a lifetime, that clan rules from Central Asia to the Mediterranean — and protects the caliph himself. Say out loud: "The clan became the empire's shield."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Great Seljuks — a clan of Oghuz Turks who defeated the Ghaznavids at Dandanakan (1040) — built an empire from Central Asia to the Mediterranean and took up the caliphate's protection; and Nizam al-Mulk, its great vizier, built the machine that ran it — sultan, vizier, iqta — and the Nizamiyya madrasas that taught its servants. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
