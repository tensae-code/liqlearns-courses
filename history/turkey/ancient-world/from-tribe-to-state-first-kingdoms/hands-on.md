# Hands-on — From Tribe to State: The First Kingdoms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Your grandfather settles a quarrel by speaking, and everyone listens because he is the elder. Now imagine a city of fifty thousand strangers. Who settles their quarrels? Say out loud: "The chief's word became the king's law."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The state was born when kin groups outgrew the chief's personal word: a chief's word became a king's law, backed by officials, armies and taxes — rule over a land and its people, not just over relatives. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
