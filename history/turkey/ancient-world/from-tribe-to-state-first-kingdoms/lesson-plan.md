# Lesson plan — From Tribe to State: The First Kingdoms

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** From Tribe to State: The First Kingdoms
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The state was born when kin groups outgrew the chief's personal word: a chief's word became a king's law, backed by officials, armies and taxes — rule over a land and its people, not just over relatives.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The state was born when kin groups outgrew the chief's personal word: a chief's word became a king's law, backed by officials, armies and taxes — rule over a land and its people, not just over relatives.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a football team growing into a league: first one captain's say-so among friends, then written rules, referees and officials nobody is related to.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a king is just a stronger chief → the difference is the machinery and the reach: law standing beyond the ruler's presence, officials, courts, army and taxes, ruling a land's people — not relatives who know him.
- Go back to the one thing: The state was born when kin groups outgrew the chief's personal word: a chief's word became a king's law, backed by officials, armies and taxes — rule over a land and its people, not just over relatives. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
