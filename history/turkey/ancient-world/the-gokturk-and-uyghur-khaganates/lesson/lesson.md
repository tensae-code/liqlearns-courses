# The Göktürk and Uyghur Khaganates

## Do it first

A clan follows its chief because he is theirs. But how do a hundred clans follow one ruler? The steppe's answer is a word: kut. Say out loud: "Heaven grants the power." This lesson tells how the Göktürks and Uyghurs built the first Turkish empires — and wrote them in stone.

## The story

The steppe clans of the last lesson did not stay scattered. In the sixth century the **Göktürks** drew them into a **khaganate** — an empire of the steppe, ruled by a **kağan**, stretching at its height across the grass from Manchuria toward the Black Sea. After them came the **Uyghur** khaganate, holding the same steppe world in its turn.

What could hold a hundred clans to one man? The book's answer is **kut**: the ruler's power and fortune, **granted by Gök Tanrı — Heaven, the Sky God**. The kağan rules because Heaven gave him kut; lose Heaven's favour — through defeat, famine, misrule — and the kut can pass away, and with it the right to rule. Kingship on the steppe is a gift held on condition.

The book lets the rulers speak. **Mete Han's** letter to the Chinese emperor — from the older Hun world — declares the steppe ruler's pride and terms to the greatest settled power on earth. And **Bilge Kağan's** own words, carved in the **Orhun inscriptions**, warn his people how empires are won and lost.

Those stones are the lesson's crown: the Orhun stones are the empire **writing its own story in stone, in its own alphabet** — the Turks stepping out of other peoples' chronicles and into their own. A steppe state that writes is no longer only remembered by its neighbours; it remembers itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The steppe clans became states: the Göktürk and Uyghur khaganates, ruled by a kağan whose power — his kut — was granted by Gök Tanrı, Heaven; and in the Orhun inscriptions the empire wrote its own story in stone, in its own alphabet, as Bilge Kağan's words and Mete Han's letter declare. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
