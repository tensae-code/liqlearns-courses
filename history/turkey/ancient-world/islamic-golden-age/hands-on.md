# Hands-on — The Islamic Golden Age

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Picture a city where translators are paid in gold for the weight of the books they translate, where observatories track the stars nightly, and students cross continents to sit in its classrooms. Say out loud: "For a while, the world's classroom was Baghdad."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In the Islamic world's golden age of science — the eighth to twelfth centuries — translation, algebra, medicine and observatories made Baghdad and Cordoba the world's classrooms, and learning itself became the civilisation's glory. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
