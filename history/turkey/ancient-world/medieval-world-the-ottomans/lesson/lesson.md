# The Ottoman State at the Start of the Twentieth Century

## Do it first

Six hundred years old. Ruler of three continents within living memory. And yet — open its ledgers, walk its provinces, count its debts. Say out loud: "A great state, found wanting." This lesson measures the Ottoman Empire at the start of the twentieth century.

## The story

Before the Grade 12 book tells the fall, it takes the empire's measure — the **Ottoman State at the start of the twentieth century**, examined in three lights: the political, the social and the economic.

**Politically**, the state that had ruled from the Balkans to Arabia for six centuries is strained to its edges: national movements pull its peoples toward their own states; the great powers of Europe press on its borders and interfere in its affairs; reforms come and stall; the machinery of rule creaks under a map too wide and too varied for its strength.

**Socially**, the empire is a burdened mosaic: peoples of many faiths and languages, peasants carrying heavy taxes, towns caught between old ways and new schools and ideas arriving from Europe — a society arguing with itself about what it should become.

**Economically**, the verdict is the hardest: the treasury is indebted to foreign lenders, foreign capital and capitulations — privileges for foreign merchants — bind the empire's own markets, and its industry lags behind the Europe it trades with. Dependence abroad, strain at home.

That is the book's measure, and the lesson keeps its bluntness: **a six-hundred-year state is measured and found wanting on every page**. The next lessons — the war gamble, the fronts, the collapse — all grow out of this report.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Ottoman state that entered the twentieth century — six hundred years old — is measured on every page and found wanting: politically strained, socially burdened and economically dependent, an empire in its last decades. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
