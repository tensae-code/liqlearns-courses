# Lesson plan — The Ottoman State at the Start of the Twentieth Century

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Ottoman State at the Start of the Twentieth Century
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Empire's End and the War of Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Ottoman state that entered the twentieth century — six hundred years old — is measured on every page and found wanting: politically strained, socially burdened and economically dependent, an empire in its last decades.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — The Empire's End and the War of Independence (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Ottoman state that entered the twentieth century — six hundred years old — is measured on every page and found wanting: politically strained, socially burdened and economically dependent, an empire in its last decades.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — an old great house inspected room by room before a storm: the roof leaks, the money is owed abroad, and the family argues — the inspector's report is this lesson.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the empire fell from one defeat → this lesson is the prior report: political strain, social burden and economic dependence were already in place before the war; the war is the storm, not the whole weather.
- Go back to the one thing: The Ottoman state that entered the twentieth century — six hundred years old — is measured on every page and found wanting: politically strained, socially burdened and economically dependent, an empire in its last decades. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
