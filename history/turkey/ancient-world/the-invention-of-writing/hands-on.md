# Hands-on — The Invention of Writing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take a lump of clay in your mind and press a reed into it. Make one mark for one sheep. You have just done what a Sumerian did five thousand years ago — and the moment you did it, prehistory ended. Say out loud: "Writing makes words stay."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History proper begins when writing is invented: from tokens and picture-signs to Sumerian cuneiform pressed in clay, Egyptian hieroglyphs, and at last the alphabet — signs for sounds that anyone can learn. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
