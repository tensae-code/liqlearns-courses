# Hands-on — The Migration of Peoples: The Huns and the Fall of Rome

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A horseman appears on Europe's eastern edge — a stranger from the steppe. Behind him, a whole people is moving. Within a lifetime, an empire a thousand years old will be gone. Say out loud: "One migration can end an age."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Huns' westward push set the Germanic peoples moving in a chain across Europe — the Migration of Peoples (Kavimler Göçü) — and the pressure of that great movement helped end the Roman Empire in the west, closing the ancient world in Europe. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
