# Lesson plan — The Migration of Peoples: The Huns and the Fall of Rome

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Migration of Peoples: The Huns and the Fall of Rome
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Huns' westward push set the Germanic peoples moving in a chain across Europe — the Migration of Peoples (Kavimler Göçü) — and the pressure of that great movement helped end the Roman Empire in the west, closing the ancient world in Europe.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Huns' westward push set the Germanic peoples moving in a chain across Europe — the Migration of Peoples (Kavimler Göçü) — and the pressure of that great movement helped end the Roman Empire in the west, closing the ancient world in Europe.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a row of dominoes: push the first and every one falls in turn — the Huns push, the Germanic peoples move, Rome is pressed, and an age ends.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Huns conquered Rome directly → the chain matters: the Huns' push set the Germanic peoples moving into the empire; their pressure over a generation, on an already strained Rome, is what helped end the western empire.
- Go back to the one thing: The Huns' westward push set the Germanic peoples moving in a chain across Europe — the Migration of Peoples (Kavimler Göçü) — and the pressure of that great movement helped end the Roman Empire in the west, closing the ancient world in Europe. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
