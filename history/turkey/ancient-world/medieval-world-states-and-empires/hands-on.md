# Hands-on — Medieval World: States and Empires

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name a kind of ruler: an emperor. A king. A lord in a castle who owes service to a bigger lord. A khan on the steppe. In the medieval world, all of them ruled at the same time, in neighbouring lands. Say out loud: "One age, a dozen kinds of rule."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The medieval world held a dozen kinds of rule at once — Rome's heirs and Byzantium in the east, the Sassanid empire, feudal Europe's lords, and the steppe powers — and every one of those states stood or fell by how it raised and organised its army. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
