# Lesson plan — Medieval World: States and Empires

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Medieval World: States and Empires
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The medieval world held a dozen kinds of rule at once — Rome's heirs and Byzantium in the east, the Sassanid empire, feudal Europe's lords, and the steppe powers — and every one of those states stood or fell by how it raised and organised its army.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Political structures in the medieval world** — The ancient world's great empires give way to a crowded map.
2. **The army in the middle ages** — Every one of those states rested on soldiers, so the book reads the age through its **armies**: how does a medieval state raise men, pay them, arm them and hold them together?.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks 'medieval' means one kind of kingdom everywhere → the lesson's whole point is variety: Byzantine emperor, Sassanid King of Kings, feudal lords and steppe khans rule at the same time, in different ways.
- Thinks armies are a side topic → in this age the army is the state's skeleton: how men are raised — by obligation, by pay, or by a whole people's way of life — decides what the state can do.
- Go back to the one thing: The medieval world held a dozen kinds of rule at once — Rome's heirs and Byzantium in the east, the Sassanid empire, feudal Europe's lords, and the steppe powers — and every one of those states stood or fell by how it raised and organised its army. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
