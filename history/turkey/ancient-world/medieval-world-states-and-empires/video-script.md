# Video script — Medieval World: States and Empires

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. The medieval world held a dozen kinds of rule at once — and each kind stood on its own kind of army.
Walk the map. East first: Constantinople behind its walls — Byzantium, Rome's surviving heir, an emperor with paid officials. Beside it, Persia's Sassanid King of Kings. West: a patchwork of kingdoms and feudal lords, each castle a small state. North: the steppe, where the state itself rides on horseback.
Now look at the soldiers under each flag. Feudal knights serving for their land, a season at a time. Imperial professionals, paid from taxes all year. Steppe herdsmen who are archers by daily life.
Who rules, and how long they rule, follows from that. The medieval world: many states, many armies, one crowded age.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
