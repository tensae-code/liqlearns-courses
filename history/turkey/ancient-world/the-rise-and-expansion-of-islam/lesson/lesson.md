# The Rise and Expansion of Islam

## Do it first

A small group, driven out of its own city. Twenty years later it rules Arabia. Fifty years later, an empire. Say out loud: "A handful became a state; a state became an empire." This lesson tells how Islam rose and spread.

## The story

The story opens in **Mecca**, where the Prophet Muhammad's preaching wins a small band of believers — and the city's rulers answer with persecution. The handful survives by leaving: the migration to **Medina**, where the Prophet is invited to rule. There the persecuted handful becomes something new: a **state**, with a community, laws and allies. The book's clock starts here.

Then come the years of struggle with Mecca, each with its name and date, and the book expects them known: **Badr (624)**, the small Muslim force's famous victory against the odds; **Uhud (625)**, the hard lesson of a battle turned by disobedience; **the Trench (627)**, when Medina dug a ditch no cavalry could cross and the great siege failed; and **Hudaybiyya (628)**, not a battle but a **treaty** — a truce that looked like compromise and opened the road to everything after. Within a few years Mecca itself is won, and Arabia is united under the new faith.

After the Prophet's death, the **Four Caliphs** — his successors — carry the state outward. In thirty years their armies take the tired empires' richest lands: Sassanid Persia falls entirely; Byzantium loses Syria, Egypt and more. The state of Medina has become an **empire** spanning from Persia to the Mediterranean's far shore.

Hold the beat the book gives it: **in twenty years a persecuted handful becomes a state; in thirty more, an empire** — one of the fastest great turns in all the history this course tells.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In twenty years a persecuted handful in Mecca became a state at Medina — through the battles and treaties of Badr (624), Uhud (625), the Trench (627) and Hudaybiyya (628) — and in thirty more, under the Four Caliphs, that state became an empire. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: lay the story out in order, part by part, the way the lesson tells it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
