# The Umayyad Caliphate

## Do it first

The first caliphs were chosen. Then a father handed the rule to his son — and nobody chose again for ninety years. Say out loud: "Election gave way to inheritance." This lesson tells how the caliphate became a dynasty at Damascus.

## The story

The Four Caliphs of the last lesson were raised to rule by the community's choice, from among the Prophet's companions. After them, power passed to one family: the **Umayyads**, and with them the nature of the office changed.

The Umayyad caliph ruled from **Damascus**, in Syria — and he ruled as a dynast: the caliphate now passed **by inheritance**, father to son, within the family. The book's story beat is exact: **rule by election gives way to rule by inheritance**. The caliph is becoming a king.

As a state, the dynasty was formidable. The empire reached its **greatest extent** under Umayyad rule — from the borders of Central Asia and India in the east to Spain in the far west — governed from Damascus through governors, an Arabic-speaking administration, and a coinage and court of imperial scale.

But the change of principle carried a lasting argument inside it: many never accepted that the Prophet's succession should be a family's property. That argument outlived the dynasty — the Umayyads fell within a century, overthrown by the revolution that brings the Abbasids of the next lesson to power.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Under the Umayyads the caliphate became a dynasty: rule by election among the Prophet's companions gave way to rule by inheritance at Damascus, and the empire reached its greatest extent as a family monarchy. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
