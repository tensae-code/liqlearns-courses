# The Arabian Peninsula Before Islam

## Do it first

Two great empires, worn out from fighting each other. Between them, a desert peninsula of tribes, markets and caravans — and a city where traders gather from every direction. Say out loud: "The stage is set." This lesson shows the world on the eve of Islam.

## The story

Every great change has a stage, and this lesson builds it: the world as it stood **when Islam was born**, at the start of the seventh century.

North and east stand the age's two great powers, **Byzantium** and **Sassanid Persia** — the same rivals from the medieval lesson — now **tired**: drained by long wars against each other, their treasuries and armies worn thin, their borderlands restless. The old world's two giants have spent their strength on each other.

Between and below them lies **Arabia**: a peninsula of deserts, oasis towns and caravan routes. Its people live largely in **tribes**, loyal to kin, moving herds between pastures or — in the trading towns — loading caravans that carry goods between the empires and the sea. **Mecca**, its famous sanctuary town, draws pilgrims and traders together; the peninsula is poor in empires but rich in movement, poetry and news.

Hold the picture as the book frames it: **two tired empires and a trading peninsula wait for a prophet**. What happens next — the next lesson — begins in Mecca, and changes every border on this map.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: On the eve of Islam, Arabia was a trading peninsula of tribes and caravan cities, squeezed between two tired empires — Byzantium and Sassanid Persia — and the world was waiting for a prophet. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
