# Lesson plan — The Arabian Peninsula Before Islam

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Arabian Peninsula Before Islam
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Islam Rises and the Turks Enter Islam (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** On the eve of Islam, Arabia was a trading peninsula of tribes and caravan cities, squeezed between two tired empires — Byzantium and Sassanid Persia — and the world was waiting for a prophet.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Islam Rises and the Turks Enter Islam (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: On the eve of Islam, Arabia was a trading peninsula of tribes and caravan cities, squeezed between two tired empires — Byzantium and Sassanid Persia — and the world was waiting for a prophet.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a town on a busy road between two great tired kingdoms — everyone passes through, nobody rules it for long, and news travels fast along its caravans.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Arabia was an empire like its neighbours → it was a peninsula of tribes and trading towns, ruled by kin loyalty, not by an emperor — that looseness is part of the story.
- Go back to the one thing: On the eve of Islam, Arabia was a trading peninsula of tribes and caravan cities, squeezed between two tired empires — Byzantium and Sassanid Persia — and the world was waiting for a prophet. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
