# Lesson plan — The First Law Codes

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The First Law Codes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Justice changed forever when law was written down and set up in public: from Ur-Namma's code to Hammurabi's stone, the rules stopped being the strong man's mood and became a stone everyone could read.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — First Steps: What History Is and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Justice changed forever when law was written down and set up in public: from Ur-Namma's code to Hammurabi's stone, the rules stopped being the strong man's mood and became a stone everyone could read.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — the rules of a game written on the wall of the playground, instead of the biggest child deciding each quarrel as he likes.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the codes matter only for their penalties → the penalties can be hard; the turn is the form: written, public, fixed law that binds judge and judged alike, instead of a ruler's changeable mood.
- Go back to the one thing: Justice changed forever when law was written down and set up in public: from Ur-Namma's code to Hammurabi's stone, the rules stopped being the strong man's mood and became a stone everyone could read. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
