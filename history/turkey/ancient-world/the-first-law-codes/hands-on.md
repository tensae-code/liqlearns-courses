# Hands-on — The First Law Codes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine being judged by a ruler who invents the rule on the spot — kind today, cruel tomorrow, and nobody can argue, because the rule is whatever he says it is. Now imagine the rules carved on a stone in the market square. Say out loud: "Now everyone can read the law."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Justice changed forever when law was written down and set up in public: from Ur-Namma's code to Hammurabi's stone, the rules stopped being the strong man's mood and became a stone everyone could read. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
