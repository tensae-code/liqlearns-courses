# Video script — Trade in the Medieval World

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Farms fed towns, towns made guilds, and caravans and ships stitched the medieval world together.
Start in a field: better tools, more grain — more than the village can eat. That surplus is the seed. Follow it into a town: bakers, smiths, weavers at work, each craft organised in its guild, training apprentices, guarding its standards.
Now pull back to the whole map. A caravan is crossing the desert with silk. A ship is leaving harbour with grain and cloth. The routes run between every state in the last lesson — rivals in war, partners in trade.
Under the divided politics ran one shared economy — richer in its stitching than the wars were in their tearing.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
