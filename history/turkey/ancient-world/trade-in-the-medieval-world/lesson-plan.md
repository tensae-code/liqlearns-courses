# Lesson plan — Trade in the Medieval World

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Trade in the Medieval World
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Medieval farming fed growing towns, towns bred craftsmen organised in guilds, and caravans and ships on the great trade routes stitched the whole medieval world together — richer than its wars divided it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Medieval farming fed growing towns, towns bred craftsmen organised in guilds, and caravans and ships on the great trade routes stitched the whole medieval world together — richer than its wars divided it.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a market day in your own town — farmers, makers and traders meeting in one place, each needing the others.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks trade is separate from farming → the lesson's chain is one piece: farm surplus frees hands, freed hands fill towns and guilds, towns' goods fill the caravans. Break the first link and the rest starve.
- Go back to the one thing: Medieval farming fed growing towns, towns bred craftsmen organised in guilds, and caravans and ships on the great trade routes stitched the whole medieval world together — richer than its wars divided it. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
