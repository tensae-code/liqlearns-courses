# Hands-on — The First Turks of the Steppe

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Picture a sea with no water: grass to every horizon, wind, and no trees, no walls, no cities. To live there, everything you own must move. Say out loud: "The steppe wrote the Turks' way of life."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The first Turks appear in Eurasia's steppe — the Huns and the peoples around them — and the grassland itself writes their whole way of life: the horse, the felt tent and the herds are answers to a land with no walls and no plough. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
