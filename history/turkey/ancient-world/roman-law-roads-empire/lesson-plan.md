# Lesson plan — Roman Law, Roads and Empire

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Roman Law, Roads and Empire
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Rome fell as a state, but its law did not: Roman law's legacy — written, ordered, reasoned law — was carried into the medieval world and kept governing long after the empire that made it was gone.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Medieval World and the Turks of the Steppe (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Rome fell as a state, but its law did not: Roman law's legacy — written, ordered, reasoned law — was carried into the medieval world and kept governing long after the empire that made it was gone.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a game whose inventor has left, but whose rulebook everyone still plays by — the empire ended; its rules kept ruling.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Rome's fall ended everything Roman → the state fell; the law was carried on — codified in Byzantium, practised in the west, and revived by medieval jurists as the model of ordered law.
- Go back to the one thing: Rome fell as a state, but its law did not: Roman law's legacy — written, ordered, reasoned law — was carried into the medieval world and kept governing long after the empire that made it was gone. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
