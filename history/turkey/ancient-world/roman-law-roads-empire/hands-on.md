# Hands-on — Roman Law, Roads and Empire

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
An empire dies. Its armies scatter, its emperors vanish. But in a courtroom a thousand years later, a judge opens a book — and the dead empire is still speaking. Say out loud: "Rome fell; its law kept governing."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Rome fell as a state, but its law did not: Roman law's legacy — written, ordered, reasoned law — was carried into the medieval world and kept governing long after the empire that made it was gone. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
