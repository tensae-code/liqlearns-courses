# Video script — Roman Law, Roads and Empire

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Rome fell — but Roman law kept governing the medieval world.
Picture the empire at its height: one legal order from Britain to Mesopotamia — courts, jurists, written law for property, contract, crime and family.
Now watch the west fall: armies scatter, emperors vanish, the map breaks into the kingdoms of the last lessons. But follow the law: east to Byzantium, where it is kept and codified; west into church law and court practice; and later, medieval jurists opening the old Roman books again as their model.
Roads crumble. A system of law — definitions, reasons, order — travels on paper and in trained minds. That is Rome's longest road of all.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
