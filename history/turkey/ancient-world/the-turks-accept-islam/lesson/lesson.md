# The Turks Accept Islam

## Do it first

Two worlds meet beside a river in 751. One carries the steppe in its blood; the other carries a new faith and great cities. Over the next two hundred years, they become one story. Say out loud: "A thousand-year meeting begins at Talas." This lesson tells how the Turks accepted Islam — and what it changed, and what it did not.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Turks' acceptance of Islam

The meeting begins, in the book's telling, beside a river: **Talas, in 751**, where Turkish peoples encountered the Arab armies of the expanding caliphate in Central Asia. Not a conversion in a day — an **encounter**: the door opening between the steppe world and the Islamic world.

Through that door, over generations, the Turks walked — as the hired soldiers of the Abbasid lesson, as traders along the routes, as neighbours and allies of Muslim states. Islam suited much in the Turks' own outlook — one Heaven above all, a ruler's duty under God — and tribe by tribe, then state by state, the steppe began to profess the faith.

The milestone the book marks is a state: the **Karakhanids** — the **first Turkish Muslim state**, a khaganate whose rulers took Muslim names and titles while governing a Turkish people in the old steppe-and-city land of Central Asia. With them, 'Turkish' and 'Muslim' begin their thousand-year companionship.

Hold the pace honestly: this was the work of **generations**, not a single decree — a battle beside a river in 751 starts it; states like the Karakhanids crown it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Islam's effect on Turkish state and society

What did the new faith change? This part walks the change through the state's own house. The **khagan's titles** change: the ruler now styles himself with Muslim titles alongside the old, and his rule is preached as God's trust, not Heaven's alone. **Law** changes: Islamic law takes its place beside — and over — steppe custom in the cities and courts. And the **city** changes: mosque, madrasa and market square become the Turkish town's new centre, with Arabic and Persian words flowing into Turkish speech and writing.

But the book is careful, and the lesson keeps its care: **the steppe keeps its customs too**. The old ways — in family life, in warfare, in the ruler's council, in the people's tales — do not vanish at the mosque door. Turkish-Islamic civilisation is a **meeting**, not an erasure: faith from the cities of Islam, sinew from the steppe.

That double inheritance — mosque and madrasa built by horsemen's grandsons — is the seed of everything the rest of this course tells: Seljuks next, then Ottomans, then the Republic.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: From the Talas encounter of 751 to the Karakhanids — the first Turkish Muslim state — the Turks accepted Islam over generations; and the faith changed the khagan's titles, the law and the city, while the steppe kept many of its own customs too. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
