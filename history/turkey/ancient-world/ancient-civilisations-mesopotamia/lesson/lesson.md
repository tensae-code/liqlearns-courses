# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

## Do it first

Picture a dry land — and through it, two great rivers. Where the rivers flood, the earth turns green and grain grows thick. Say out loud: "Between the rivers, the first cities rose." This lesson tells how civilisation began in Mesopotamia.

## The story

**Mesopotamia** means the land between the rivers — the **Tigris** and the **Euphrates**. Their floods watered dry earth, and farming there could feed far more people than hunting ever could. Where food gathers, people gather: villages grew into the world's **first cities**.

The first city-builders were the **Sumerians**, in the south: city-states with temples, kings, scribes and the cuneiform writing of the last lesson. Then power passed hand to hand across the basin: **Akkad** united the cities under one rule; **Babylon** rose to fame as a great capital, known for its king and his law code; and **Assyria**, in the north, built a hard military empire that at its height ruled the whole land and beyond.

Four names, one stage: the same rivers, the same flood-fed fields, the same crowded cities — with kingship, writing, law and empire all being invented and reinvented on it. That is why the book calls this a **civilisation basin**: a place where civilisation pooled and deepened, and from which its gifts — the city, the code, the written word — spread outward.

Hold the order: Sumer first, then Akkad, Babylon, Assyria — the first page of the city-story, written between two rivers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between the Tigris and the Euphrates, where two rivers flood, the first cities, kings and law codes grew — Sumer, Akkad, Babylon and Assyria in turn ruling the land between the rivers. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
