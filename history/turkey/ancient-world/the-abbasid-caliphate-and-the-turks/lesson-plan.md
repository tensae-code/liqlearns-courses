# Lesson plan — The Abbasid Caliphate and the Turks

**Course:** History — Turkey · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Abbasid Caliphate and the Turks
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Islam Rises and the Turks Enter Islam (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Abbasid caliphate at Baghdad leaned on Turkish soldiers — Turks rose inside the empire as its guards, commanders and strongmen — so the empire that would in time convert the Turks first hired them.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Islam Rises and the Turks Enter Islam (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Abbasid caliphate at Baghdad leaned on Turkish soldiers — Turks rose inside the empire as its guards, commanders and strongmen — so the empire that would in time convert the Turks first hired them.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a company that hires guards from one tough town, until the guards' sons are running the company — hiring becomes rising, and rising becomes ruling.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Turks entered the Islamic world first as converts → in this lesson they enter first as soldiers: hired by the Abbasids, rising through command — conversion's great wave comes in the lessons after.
- Go back to the one thing: The Abbasid caliphate at Baghdad leaned on Turkish soldiers — Turks rose inside the empire as its guards, commanders and strongmen — so the empire that would in time convert the Turks first hired them. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
