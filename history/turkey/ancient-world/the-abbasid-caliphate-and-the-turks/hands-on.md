# Hands-on — The Abbasid Caliphate and the Turks

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
An empire hires soldiers from the steppe because they are the best riders alive. A few generations later, those soldiers' families hold the empire's real power. Say out loud: "First they were hired. Then they rose."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Abbasid caliphate at Baghdad leaned on Turkish soldiers — Turks rose inside the empire as its guards, commanders and strongmen — so the empire that would in time convert the Turks first hired them. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
