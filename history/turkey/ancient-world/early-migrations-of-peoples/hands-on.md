# Hands-on — Early Migrations of Peoples

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pick up everything your family owns and walk — for months — toward a land you have only heard of. People did exactly that, again and again, in the ancient world. Say out loud: "Peoples move, and the world changes."

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Migration is the engine of the ancient world: driven by climate, hunger and pressure from other peoples, early peoples moved — and tools, seeds and languages moved with them, stitching the ancient world together. Draw it or lay it out with objects, part by part, and say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
