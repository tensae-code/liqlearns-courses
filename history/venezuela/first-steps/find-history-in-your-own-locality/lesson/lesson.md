# Find history in your own locality

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Find history in your own locality" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## The school invites the community: Los Chimichimitos

An old song-and-dance at the school party is traced back to indigenous peoples and to Africans the Spanish brought by force long ago

## The teacher invites us to know the community's present and past

Children are sent out to ask their own community what it was before, making the neighbourhood the first archive

## The Barrio Charter: local history and collective memory

A barrio writes its own charter from its founding, its first settlers and their shared struggles — the settlement's past becomes its identity document

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Find history in your own locality as Venezuela's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
