# Video script — Heritage what it is and why it matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Heritage what it is and why it matters.

Mateo's Añú grandparents pass legends down at night, and foods indigenous children ate long ago are still on Venezuelan tables today

The circle's centre is for eating, talking, singing and assembly — a house shape that itself teaches how a community lives together

Venezuela's population is still "people of the maize and the yuca" — indigenous foods from before the conquest remain the country's daily bread

Families keep the Velorio de la Cruz de Mayo with songs, prayers and improvised décimas — a living tradition children join, not just watch

The comfortable Cumanagoto chair called "putaca" keeps its indigenous name — colonisers took the word because the thing came with the people

Nothing comes from nothing — everything is born of mother nature, the pachamama of many peoples, and that inheritance is humanity's treasure

Today's indigenous Venezuelans and Americans are set beside their ancestors — the same peoples, photographed in the present, not vanished into the past

An indigenous myth-and-legend tradition and the trickster tales of Uncle Tiger and Uncle Rabbit are carried as the feeling of being Venezuelan

The Castillo de Santa Rosa, built on Margarita, stands in a photograph as patrimony children must learn to keep

Heroes and heroines, anthem, shield and flag, plus indigenous and Afro-descendant contributions and the festive calendar, are named as the material and immaterial references that make the nation

Language, religion, food and festivals from the colony are still lived today — the past projecting into the present

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
