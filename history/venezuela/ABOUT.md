## The way of the books

Venezuela's own way, as filed book by book:

- **Book 1 (Venezuela Grade 1 g1 Viva Venezuela):** starts History from the child's own day, family and street — before/now contrasts, feast days and Bolívar as a child before any national chronology.
- **Book 2 (Venezuela Grade 2 g2 Viva Venezuela):** Grade 2 carries a complete national story — arrival by sea, founded cities, cacao wealth, first rebellions, Miranda, 1810, 1811, Carabobo, Gran Colombia, Sucre — all told through grandparents, houses and two children thinking aloud.
- **Book 3 (Venezuela Grade 3 g3 Viva Venezuela):** a spiral timeline runs from remote ancestors through slavery, the agricultural republic and Zamora to the oil republic, interleaved with a state-by-state tour where each state is introduced by its history and its hero's name.
- **Book 4 (Venezuela Grade 4 g4 Venezuela y Su Gente):** the book's spine is a named journey, “Viaje por nuestro pasado indígena y colonial” — indigenous past first, then encounter, resistance, empire, mestizaje and the road to 1810 — framed by the house, the street and the map of today.
- **Book 5 (Venezuela Grade 5 g5 Venezuela y Su Gente):** Grade 5 turns to the nineteenth-century republic — told as three tries at a republic (second victory, second failure, third the charm), then separation from Gran Colombia, the Federal War and coffee — with geography units on land, ports and oil basins wrapped around it.
- **Book 6 (Venezuela Grade 6 g6 Venezuela y Su Gente):** Grade 6 is the oil century told whole — Gómez to Chávez — using period caricatures as sources and one Maracaibo woman's lifetime (Ana Graciela, b. 1924) as the thread that makes national change personal.
- **Book 7 (Venezuela Grade 7 g7 Patria y Ciudadanía):** a Civics book framed historically — citizenship is introduced as the unfinished dream of “the two Simons” (Rodríguez and Bolívar) and of Zamora, and national identity is built from indigenous and Afro-Venezuelan origins.
- **Book 8 (Venezuela Grade 7 g7 Historia):** history explicitly against eurocentrism and rote memorisation — the continent is Abya Yala, “a continent that had owners”, the conquest is named invasion, and emancipation is told for all of Our America, not Venezuela alone.
- **Book 9 (Venezuela Grade 8 g8 Nuestra Historia Republicana):** the republic told in three ages — agrarian, transition, oil — government by government, with each chapter opened from a present-day reference and documents and photographs as sources.
- **Book 10 (Venezuela Grade 8 g8 Historia de la Humanidad):** a full world history for Venezuelan students that starts in Africa as “our mother continent”, insists “we do not descend from the monkey”, and reads every age through work, class struggle and liberation — ending in Latin America's own revolutions.
- **Book 11 (Venezuela Grade 10 g10 Historia Contemporánea):** teaches contemporary history through three “axes” — coffee, oil and violence — under one thesis, dependence: foreign commercial houses, then the Seven Sisters oil companies, then PDVSA's own managers keep the country a mono-producer; time is taught as recurrent, not a straight line.
