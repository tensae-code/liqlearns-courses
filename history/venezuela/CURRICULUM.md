# History — Venezuela course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Venezuela inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Venezuela's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** Venezuela's books teach the story in a spiral, in the placements file's own teaching order, earliest books first: the youngest books start from the child's own day, family and street and tell the founding story whole (arrival by sea, founded cities, cacao, Miranda, 1810, 1811, Carabobo, Gran Colombia, Sucre); the next books run the spiral again more deeply — remote ancestors, the indigenous and colonial journey, slavery and abolition, Zamora, and the first sight of the oil republic; then the nineteenth-century republic (three tries at a republic, separation, the Federal War, coffee) and the oil century told whole from Gómez to Chávez; then the full histories — Venezuela and Our America whole, the republic government by government, and a complete history of humanity from Africa to the Cold War; and finally contemporary Venezuela read through dependence and its three axes — coffee, oil and violence. The ladder follows that progression: each level is one turn of the spiral, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest turn that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
