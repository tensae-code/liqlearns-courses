# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The past is counted from a fixed starting point: years gather into centuries and millennia, writing divides prehistory from history, and the great periods stand in order on a timeline.

How long is the past? The lesson teaches children to count it. Years are gathered into bigger measures: a century is a hundred years, a millennium is a thousand. Counting starts from a fixed starting point and runs forward and back, so that an event can be given its place — this many centuries ago, in that millennium. With these measures, the huge stretch of the past stops being a blur and becomes something a child can hold: a line with places on it.

The older children's book takes the line further. For most of the past there was no writing at all — that long age is prehistory, known only from tools and traces. Then writing is invented, and history proper begins: events can now be recorded as they happen. The lesson divides the whole line into its great periods and sets them in order on a timeline, so a learner can say not just when something happened, but in which great age of the human story it stands.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
