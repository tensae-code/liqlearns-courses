# Hands-on — Dating the Past

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a long line on paper. Mark where you were born, where your parents were born, and where your grandparents were born. Now imagine the line running back far, far beyond them. This lesson teaches how that long line is counted and divided.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the past is counted from a fixed starting point: years gather into centuries and millennia, writing divides prehistory from history, and the great periods stand in order on a timeline. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
