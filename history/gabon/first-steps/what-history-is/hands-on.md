# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at any photograph of a big moment — a celebration, a new bridge, a family's first day in a new home. Ask out loud: what happened here, and why do we keep this picture? Hold the question. This lesson is about that question.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: history begins with a question about an event: something happened, and we keep the past because what happened still matters — a photograph of the first Moon landing opens the lesson and asks what an event is and why the past is kept. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
