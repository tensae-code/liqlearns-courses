# What History Is

## Do it first

Look at any photograph of a big moment — a celebration, a new bridge, a family's first day in a new home. Ask out loud: what happened here, and why do we keep this picture? Hold the question. This lesson is about that question.

The lesson opens with a photograph: the first Moon landing, people standing where no one had stood before. Before any dates or names, the class asks the founding question — what is an event? An event is something that happened, at a place, at a time, to people. And why do we keep it? Because the past explains the present: who we are comes from what happened before us. History, the lesson says, is the past kept and questioned — not forgotten, and not just believed, but asked about until it can be told truly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: history begins with a question about an event: something happened, and we keep the past because what happened still matters — a photograph of the first Moon landing opens the lesson and asks what an event is and why the past is kept. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: event, history. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
