# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pick up one old object near you — a tool, a pot, a coin, an old shoe. Ask it three questions out loud: who made you, who used you, what can you tell me? This lesson is about questioning traces of the past in exactly that way.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: before any story is told, the past is questioned: objects and traces left behind are treated like witnesses, asked what they are, who made them, and what they can prove. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
