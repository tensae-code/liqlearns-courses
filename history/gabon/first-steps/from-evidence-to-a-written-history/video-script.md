# Video script — From Evidence to a Written History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before any story is told, the past is questioned: objects and traces left behind are treated like witnesses, asked what they are, who made them, and what they can prove.

Nobody was there to write down most of the past, so how can a history be written at all? The lesson answers: the past left witnesses behind. An old tool, a broken pot, a mark in stone, a story told by grandparents — these are the sources of history. Like witnesses, they are questioned before they are believed: What are you? Who made you? How old are you? What do you prove, and what can you not prove? Only after the questioning does the telling begin. A written history is what survives that questioning.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
