# From Evidence to a Written History — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Leçon 4 Les sources de l'Histoire": who, where, and what turned?
**Answer:** Nobody was there to write down most of the past, so how can a history be written at all? The lesson answers: the past left witnesses behind. An old tool, a broken pot, a mark in stone, a story told by grandparents — these are the sources of history. Like witnesses, they are questioned before they are believed: What are you? Who made you? How old are you? What do you prove, and what can you not prove? Only after the questioning does the telling begin. A written history is what survives that questioning.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
**Answer:** Before any story is told, the past is questioned: objects and traces left behind are treated like witnesses, asked what they are, who made them, and what they can prove.
