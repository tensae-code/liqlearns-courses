# History — Gabon course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Gabon inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Gabon's books in hand are three —
the *Super Efficace en Histoire Géographie Citoyenneté*
teacher guides for the 3e, 4e and 5e années (Grades 3, 4 and
5), all COMPLETE as filed — and their own progression is the
ladder. The first guide starts from the child's own country:
what history is, its sources, its measures of time, the first
people, Gabon's own first peoples and migrations, the French
arrival, Libreville and independence, and daily life changing
(Level 1). The second guide runs one long Gabon-centred arc —
prehistory deepened, the making of the Gabonese people, the
great sea voyages, explorers and Brazza, the slave trade,
French Congo and the AÉF, resistance and décolonisation
(Level 2). The third guide widens out to the ancient worlds
— Egypt, Greece, Rome — then returns to Gabon and Africa:
autochthones and Bantu, colonisation, Berlin, the two world
wars, and the march to independence told through Léon Mba's
speech of 16 August 1960 (Level 3). Every lesson stands on
teacher-guide evidence only — the acceptance reckoning in
ABOUT.md says so plainly.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Super Efficace Histoire Géographie Citoyenneté books —
except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the
people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book
contents map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps and Gabon's First Peoples** — the craft
   first (what history is; the sources questioned like
   witnesses; dating the past in centuries and millennia),
   then the deep beginning (Africa, cradle of humankind; from
   stone tools to farming), then Gabon itself (its first
   peoples and migrations and the gabão name; the Bantu
   migrations), and the first book's own Gabon arc (France in
   Gabon and the founding of Libreville; independence 1960;
   daily life changing in houses, trade and transport).
   10 lessons, in three titles: `first-steps/` (3 lessons),
   `africa-to-1500/` (4 lessons), `africa-after-1500/`
   (3 lessons).

2. **Ships, Trade, Colonisation and Resistance** — the sea
   voyages of the 15th and 16th centuries; Europeans on the
   coast, trading on African terms, their posts hardening
   into establishments; the explorers and Brazza on the
   Ogooué; colonisation's slide from treaties to rule; the
   Atlantic slave trade — why Africa, and its triangular
   map and human cost; Gabon folded into French Congo and
   the AÉF; and Gabonese resistance from the first treaties.
   9 lessons, in two titles: `modern-world/` (1 lesson),
   `africa-after-1500/` (8 lessons).

3. **The Wider World and the Road to Independence** — the
   ancient worlds (Egypt on the Nile and what Egypt gave;
   Greece and its city-states; Rome's empire), then Africa
   again (resistance across the continent; colonial
   exploitation; the Berlin Conference), the two world wars
   and the world after 1945, and last the continent's march
   to independence — the road Gabon walks to 1960.
   12 lessons, in four titles: `africa-to-1500/`
   (2 lessons), `ancient-world/` (2 lessons),
   `africa-after-1500/` (4 lessons), `modern-world/`
   (4 lessons).

**Totals: 31 lessons** (Level 1: 10 · Level 2: 9 · Level 3:
12 — 52 placements, all from three teacher guides, evidence
only, acceptance 0).
