# About the History — Gabon course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Super Efficace Histoire Géographie Citoyenneté books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Gabon inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **three levels**, following the
three books' own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — first steps and Gabon's first peoples.** What
   history is, its sources and its measures of time; Africa
   the cradle of humankind and the road from stone tools to
   farming; Gabon's first peoples, the migrations and the
   Bantu meeting; the French arrival, Libreville, the
   independence of 1960, and daily life changing.
2. **Level 2 — ships, trade, colonisation and resistance.**
   The great sea voyages; trade on the coast and the posts
   that hardened into establishments; the explorers and
   Brazza; colonisation; the slave trade; French Congo and
   the AÉF; and Gabonese resistance.
3. **Level 3 — the wider world and the road to
   independence.** Egypt, Greece and Rome; Africa's
   resistance, colonial exploitation and Berlin; the two
   world wars and the world after 1945; and the continent's
   march to independence.

All thirty-one lessons stand on **teacher guides** and
count nowhere in the acceptance reckoning below — they are
evidence only, labelled as such in every lesson.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *First the craft and the first peoples, then the ships and
  the colony, then the whole world and the flag:* "First,
  learn what history is and question its witnesses — then
  walk from Africa's first people to Gabon's first peoples,
  and watch a river mouth shaped like a cloak give a country
  its name. Then the ships come: trade on the beach, Brazza
  on the Ogooué, treaties sliding into rule, the slave
  trade's triangle, Gabon folded into an empire's map — and
  Gabonese names refusing, one by one. Last, the wider
  world: Egypt, Greece, Rome, Berlin, two world wars — and
  the march that ends at 23:58 on 16 August 1960, with
  Gabon's own flag rising."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Super Efficace, Grade 3 (teacher guide)** — History,
Geography and Citizenship rotate lesson by lesson inside 5
"paliers"; History starts from the child's own country —
first peoples of Gabon, the French arrival, Libreville and
independence — inside a French-language inquiry routine
(observe, question, research, retain). *(Teacher guide.)*

**Super Efficace, Grade 4 (teacher guide)** — the year runs
as one long Gabon-centred arc — prehistory, the making of
the Gabonese people, explorers and Brazza, the slave trade,
French Congo/AÉF, resistance and decolonisation — each
topic taught through documents in the pupil manual.
*(Teacher guide.)*

**Super Efficace, Grade 5 (teacher guide)** — the final
primary year widens out — ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome
first, then Gabon and Africa again (autochthones, Bantu,
colonisation, Berlin, the two world wars) ending with the
march to independence, told through Léon Mba's speech of
16 August 1960. *(Teacher guide.)*
