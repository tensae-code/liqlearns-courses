# African Resistance to Colonisation

## Do it first

Say the names of three places far apart on an imaginary map, and for each one mime a raised shield. This lesson widens Gabon's 'no' to a continent's.

Gabon's resistance, met in the lesson before, was not alone. This lesson widens to the whole continent: across Africa, peoples take up arms and arguments against the invaders. Kings refuse, armies fight, communities argue their rights in the colonisers' own languages and courts. The forms differ from place to place; the refusal is the same. Colonisation, the book teaches, was contested everywhere it went — Africa resisted as a continent, not only village by village.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: across the whole continent, peoples take up arms and arguments against the invaders — Gabon's resistance is one front of an African refusal. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: invaders, continent. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: across the whole continent, peoples take up arms and arguments against the invaders — Gabon's resistance is one front of an African refusal.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
