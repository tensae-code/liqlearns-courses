# Hands-on — Europeans on the Coast: Trade on African Terms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Hold out one hand as a trader on a beach and the other as a captain stepping off a boat. Trade something imaginary between them. Then plant a small flag in the sand. This lesson is about both moments — the trade, and the foothold that follows it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Gabon's coastal communities meet European ships and trade begins on the beach; the small coastal posts and forts planted for economic reasons harden into permanent footholds — the beginning of a new, unequal relationship. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
