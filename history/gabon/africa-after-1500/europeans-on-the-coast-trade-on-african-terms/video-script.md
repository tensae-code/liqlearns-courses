# Video script — Europeans on the Coast: Trade on African Terms

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Gabon's coastal communities meet European ships and trade begins on the beach; the small coastal posts and forts planted for economic reasons harden into permanent footholds — the beginning of a new, unequal relationship.

The first contacts happen on the beach. Gabon's coastal communities — peoples who know the sea and trade already — meet the European ships that anchor off their shore. Goods are laid out, terms are struck, and trade begins. At this stage the meeting is between traders: the ships need what the coast has, and the coast sets much of the terms. It is the opening scene of a relationship that will not stay equal.

From the beach, the ships' presence hardens into places: the comptoirs, the first European trading posts on the coast. A post is small — a storehouse, a few men, a flag — but it does not sail away at the season's end. The lesson names these posts for what they become: small footholds that later grow into colonies.

The final book re-tells the arrival from the top: European ships reach Africa's coasts, and a new relationship begins between the continent and Europe. It starts in trade and curiosity, but the book is honest about its direction — the relationship is unequal from early on, and grows more so. Gabon's beach meetings are one shore of a change that touches the whole continent.

Then the establishments: forts and factories — the factories are trading stations, not workshops — planted on the coast for economic reasons, to hold trade in one place. What was planted for trade becomes permanent, and permanence becomes power. The lesson closes the arc of this lesson's story: contact, post, establishment — each step harder to undo than the last.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
