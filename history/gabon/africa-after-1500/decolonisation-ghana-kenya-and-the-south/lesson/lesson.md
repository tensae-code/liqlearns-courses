# Decolonisation: Ghana, Kenya and the South

## Do it first

Walk in place, faster and faster, while flags on an imaginary wall fall one by one. This lesson is the march — the whole continent moving the way Gabon moved.

The last history lesson of the primary books widens to the whole continent's march. After 1945, the demands for autonomy grow across Africa — colony by colony, the pressure builds, the powers yield ground, and until the flags of the empires fall and the flags of free African states rise in their place. Ghana's, Kenya's and the southern countries' roads are the famous paths of this march; Gabon's own road, told in this course's independence lesson, is walked inside the same movement. The continent does not receive independence; it marches to it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: after 1945 the demands for autonomy grow across the continent until the flags fall — Africa's march to independence, the road Gabon walks to 1960. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: autonomy, march to independence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
