# Video script — Gabon Resistance to Colonisation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
From the first treaties, Gabonese people refuse French domination — around King Denis's court and beyond, and in named risings: Emane Ntole, Nyonda Makita (Mavurulu), Mbombè and the Binzima movement.

The colony was never simply accepted. From the first treaties, people around King Denis's court — the great Gabonese ruler of the estuary — and beyond refuse French domination. Refusal takes many forms: papers not honoured, orders not obeyed, authority defended against the newcomers' claims. The lesson plants resistance at the very start of the colonial story, not at its end: as long as there is colonisation in Gabon, there is Gabonese resistance beside it.

The final book names the resisters. Emane Ntole rises against French rule. Nyonda Makita — Mavurulu — resists. Mbombè resists. And the Binzima movement carries refusal through the forest country. Name by name, the lesson builds the roll of Gabon's resistance: leaders and movements who would not bow, in different places and years, against the same domination. Their risings were defeated; their refusal is the country's inheritance.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
