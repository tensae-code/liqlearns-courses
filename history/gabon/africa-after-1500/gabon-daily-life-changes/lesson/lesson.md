# Gabon Daily Life Changes

## Do it first

Mime three journeys: paddling a canoe, walking a path, riding in a car. Then mime paying twice: handing over goods, then handing over coins. This lesson is about how everyday life itself changed in Gabon.

## Sub lesson 1 — Leçon 39 L'évolution des modes de vie (1): l'habitat

History also lives in houses. The traditional Gabonese house — planks and beaten earth, built from what the land gives — is the starting point. Then the lesson walks into town: there the house changes into villas and apartment blocks, built of new materials, stacked and planned. Neither house is just shelter; each one shows how its people live, work and gather. When the habitat changes, daily life changes with it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Leçon 42 L'évolution des échanges et du commerce

Trade changes shape too. It begins with barter between villages: what my village has in plenty for what yours has — goods for goods, no money between. Then money arrives, and with it markets where anything can be priced and sold. The lesson follows the change: barter, then money, then the market — and each step widens the world a Gabonese trader can reach.

## Sub lesson 3 — Leçon 45 L'évolution des modes de vie: les transports

And journeys change. For ages, Gabon moves by canoe on its rivers, on foot, and along bush paths through the forest. Then roads are cut, and vehicles come: journeys that took days take hours, and goods and people move as never before. The lesson closes the three changes — houses, trade, transport — with one idea: the modes de vie, the ways of living, evolve, and Gabon's history is written in that evolution too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Gabonese daily life keeps changing shape: the traditional house of planks and beaten earth gives way in town to villas and apartment blocks, barter between villages gives way to money and markets, and canoes, feet and bush paths give way to roads and vehicles. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: barter, habitat. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
