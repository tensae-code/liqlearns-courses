# Video script — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Why Africa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The lesson defines the slave trade — the traite négrière — and asks the hard question directly: why did Africa's coasts become its hunting ground?

The lesson begins with a definition, because the thing must be named exactly: the traite négrière is the trade in enslaved Africans — people seized, bought and shipped across the ocean as if they were goods. Then the book asks its hard question directly: why Africa? Why did this trade fasten on Africa's coasts as its hunting ground? The lesson works the question with the class — the ships, the demand across the ocean, the trade goods offered on the beach — and refuses to let the crime sit unexplained. Naming and questioning come first; the map of the trade comes next.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
