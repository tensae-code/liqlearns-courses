# The Atlantic Slave Trade: Why Africa — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Leçon 31 La traite négrière (1)": who, where, and what turned?
**Answer:** The lesson begins with a definition, because the thing must be named exactly: the traite négrière is the trade in enslaved Africans — people seized, bought and shipped across the ocean as if they were goods. Then the book asks its hard question directly: why Africa? Why did this trade fasten on Africa's coasts as its hunting ground? The lesson works the question with the class — the ships, the demand across the ocean, the trade goods offered on the beach — and refuses to let the crime sit unexplained. Naming and questioning come first; the map of the trade comes next.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
**Answer:** The lesson defines the slave trade — the traite négrière — and asks the hard question directly: why did Africa's coasts become its hunting ground?
