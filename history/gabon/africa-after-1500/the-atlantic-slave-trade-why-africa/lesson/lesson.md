# The Atlantic Slave Trade: Why Africa

## Do it first

Stand still and ask the lesson's own question out loud: 'Why Africa?' Do not answer yet. This lesson defines the slave trade first, then faces the question honestly.

The lesson begins with a definition, because the thing must be named exactly: the traite négrière is the trade in enslaved Africans — people seized, bought and shipped across the ocean as if they were goods. Then the book asks its hard question directly: why Africa? Why did this trade fasten on Africa's coasts as its hunting ground? The lesson works the question with the class — the ships, the demand across the ocean, the trade goods offered on the beach — and refuses to let the crime sit unexplained. Naming and questioning come first; the map of the trade comes next.
