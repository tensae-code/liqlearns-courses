# Brazza and the Exploration of the Ogooué

## Do it first

Paddle slowly up an imaginary great river, stopping at villages to talk and sign papers. This lesson follows the most famous of those river journeys — and what its papers led to.

One explorer's name stands over this age: Brazza. He takes the great road into Gabon's interior — the Ogooué river — and ascends it, meeting the peoples of the river and signing treaties with their rulers as he goes. The journey is famous as exploration; the lesson teaches its second half too. The treaties Brazza carries home open Gabon — and Congo beyond it — to France. The explorer's paddle and the treaty paper together prepare the colony that follows.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Brazza ascends the Ogooué river and signs the treaties that open Gabon and Congo to France — exploration turning into claim. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Ogooué, Brazza. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
