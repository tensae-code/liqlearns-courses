# Video script — France in Gabon and the Founding of Libreville

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
French ships and treaties turn into a permanent settlement on the estuary, and a settlement of freed slaves becomes Libreville — the free town that grows into Gabon's capital.

First come the ships: French vessels on the estuary, trading and treating with the peoples of the shore. Then come the papers: treaties signed between French officers and Gabonese rulers. And then the turn — what began as visits becomes an installation, a permanent French settlement planted on the estuary. The lesson marks this as the moment Gabon's story bends: a foreign power is no longer passing by; it has moved in.

The settlement gains a town with a story of its own. Libreville — the name means the free town — begins as a settlement of freed slaves, people taken for the slave trade and set free, given this shore as their home. From that beginning the town grows street by street until it is the capital of Gabon. The capital's name still carries its founding: a town of the freed.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
