# Hands-on — France in Gabon and the Founding of Libreville

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a shoreline and mark three things in order: a ship anchored, a paper signed, a town rising. This lesson is how those three marks, in that order, made a capital.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: French ships and treaties turn into a permanent settlement on the estuary, and a settlement of freed slaves becomes Libreville — the free town that grows into Gabon's capital. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
