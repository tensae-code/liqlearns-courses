# Video script — Colonisation: The Road to the Scramble

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
European powers, needing raw materials and markets, move from coast to conquest: in Gabon, treaties between Europeans and Gabonese peoples slide into direct foreign rule.

The lesson gives the turn its name: la colonisation. Treaties between Europeans and Gabonese peoples — papers signed for trade and friendship — slide, step by step, into direct foreign rule. What was agreed between rulers becomes command over subjects. Gabon passes under a foreign administration, and decisions about Gabonese land and life begin to be made in the coloniser's interest. The slide, not a single blow, is how the colony comes.

The older book asks why. European powers, their industries hungry, need raw materials and markets — places to take wealth from and places to sell to. So the movement runs from coast to conquest, in Africa generally and in Gabon in particular: first the shore, then the interior, then rule. Colonisation begins, the lesson says, when trade is no longer enough for the powers and they reach for the land itself.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
