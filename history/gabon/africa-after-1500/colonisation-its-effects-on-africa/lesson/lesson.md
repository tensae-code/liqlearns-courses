# Colonisation: Its Effects on Africa

## Do it first

Pretend to load a ship: timber, rubber, wealth of the land, box after box — all sailing away, nothing coming back. This lesson asks who colonial rule was organised FOR.

What was colonial rule FOR? The lesson answers without softening: exploitation. Colonial rule in Africa — and in Gabon, named in the lesson's own title — is organised to extract the continent's wealth for Europe's benefit. The land's timber, rubber and minerals flow out through the ports; the labour is African; the profit is not. Roads and posts serve the extraction first. To understand the colony, the book says, follow the wealth — and see which way it sails.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: colonial rule is organised to extract Africa's wealth for Europe's benefit — the exploitation coloniale under which Gabon and the continent labour. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: exploitation coloniale, extract. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
