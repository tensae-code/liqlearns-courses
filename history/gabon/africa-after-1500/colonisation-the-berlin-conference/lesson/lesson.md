# Colonisation: The Berlin Conference

## Do it first

Spread an imaginary map on a table in a cold faraway room, and draw lines across it with a ruler. Nobody in the room lives inside the lines. This lesson is about that room.

In Berlin, the capital of a European power, the powers sit down together — and agree the rules for dividing Africa between them. Who may claim what coast, how a claim is made good, how the continent is to be partitioned: all of it settled at a table in Europe, on a map, by men who did not live inside the lines they drew. No African ruler sits at that table. The lesson is short and cold: much of the map Gabon and its neighbours still live in was drawn in that room.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: in Berlin, far from Africa, the European powers agree the rules for dividing Africa between them — a continent partitioned on paper by people who did not live there. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Berlin Conference, partition. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
