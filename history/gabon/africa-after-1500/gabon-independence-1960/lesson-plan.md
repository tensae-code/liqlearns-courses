# Lesson plan — Gabon Independence 1960

**Course:** History — Gabon · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Gabon Independence 1960
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and Gabon's First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** after years inside French rule the colony unwinds step by step — Aubame is elected, Léon Mba becomes prime minister, and at 23:58 on 16 August 1960 independence is proclaimed and Gabon raises its own flag.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and Gabon's First Peoples — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

3. **Leçon 47 La marche du Gabon vers l'indépendance** — The final book walks the last steps with names and an hour.

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: after years inside French rule the colony unwinds step by step — Aubame is elected, Léon Mba becomes prime minister, and at 23:58 on 16 August 1960 independence is proclaimed and Gabon raises its own flag.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Gabon and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks independence fell in one day → the books teach steps: elections, a prime minister, then the proclamation — a march, not a jump.
- Go back to the one thing: after years inside French rule the colony unwinds step by step — Aubame is elected, Léon Mba becomes prime minister, and at 23:58 on 16 August 1960 independence is proclaimed and Gabon raises its own flag. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
