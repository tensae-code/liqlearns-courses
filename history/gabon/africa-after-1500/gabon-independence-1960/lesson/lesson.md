# Gabon Independence 1960

## Do it first

Raise an imaginary flag slowly while you count: colony, steps, flag. This lesson walks the steps Gabon took to its own flag — and the exact night it rose.

## Sub lesson 1 — Leçon 32 L'indépendance du Gabon

The youngest children's book ends Gabon's long story under French rule with its turning point: independence. After years inside French rule, Gabon raises its own flag in 1960 and rules itself. The lesson keeps it simple and proud — a country that was governed from outside becomes a country governed by its own people, and the flag is the sign of the change.

## Sub lesson 2 — Leçon 45 La décolonisation

The older book gives the turn its name and its steps: décolonisation. A colony does not end all at once; it unwinds. Demands grow, powers are handed over piece by piece, institutions pass into Gabonese hands — step by step, the lesson insists — until the road reaches the independence of 1960. What the younger book shows as a flag rising, this book shows as a road walked.

## Sub lesson 3 — Leçon 47 La marche du Gabon vers l'indépendance

The final book walks the last steps with names and an hour. Jean-Hilaire Aubame is elected; Léon Mba becomes prime minister. And then the night itself: at 23:58 on 16 August 1960, independence is proclaimed — two minutes before midnight, Gabon becomes free, and Mba's speech carries the moment into the country's memory. The march that began with the first treaties ends here, with Gabonese hands on Gabon's future.
