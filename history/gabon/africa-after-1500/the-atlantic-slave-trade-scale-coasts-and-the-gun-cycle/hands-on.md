# Hands-on — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a big triangle in the air: Europe, Africa, the Americas. Put a word at each corner: goods, captives, plantations. This lesson walks that triangle and counts what it cost.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the triangular trade is mapped — goods, captives, plantations — across three continents, and the lesson counts the human cost: people who are not free, bought and shipped. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
