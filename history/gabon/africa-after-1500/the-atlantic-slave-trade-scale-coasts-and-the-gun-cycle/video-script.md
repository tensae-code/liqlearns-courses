# Video script — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The triangular trade is mapped — goods, captives, plantations — across three continents, and the lesson counts the human cost: people who are not free, bought and shipped.

The second slave-trade lesson draws the map. The trade runs in a triangle across the Atlantic: from Europe, trade goods sail to Africa's coasts; from Africa, captives are shipped across the ocean — the crossing that kills so many; from the Americas, the produce of plantations worked by the enslaved sails back to Europe. Three continents are chained into one system, and every side of the triangle feeds the others. The map explains the machine; the next book counts what the machine did to people.

The older book looks at the same trade from the captives' side. People who are not free are bought and shipped — that plain sentence is the lesson's centre. It counts the human cost: the seized, the marched, the shipped, the dead, and the villages and families torn behind them. The triangle on the map becomes, in this telling, a count of persons — and Gabon's coast, where the ships anchored, is part of the shore it happened on.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
