# Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

## Do it first

Hold up fingers one by one and name them as separate small cities, each with its own rulers — then make them argue and vote. This lesson is about the Greeks, who turned ruling itself into an argument.

Across the Mediterranean from Egypt, another world takes shape: Greece. It is not one kingdom but many city-states, each a city ruling itself, competing and trading around the sea. And the Greeks invent something new under the sun: ways of ruling and thinking that are argued about openly — citizens debating how a city should be governed, thinkers asking how the world works and refusing the first answer. The lesson places Greece after Egypt deliberately: younger than the Nile kingdom, and the inventor of questions Europe still lives by.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Greek city-states, gathered around the Mediterranean, invent new ways of ruling and thinking that the world still uses. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: city-state, Mediterranean. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
