# Ancient Civilisations: Rome

## Do it first

Start with one finger on one spot — a city. Then spread both arms wide around an imaginary sea. This lesson is how the one city became the arms around the whole sea.

Rome begins as one city among many. Then, century by century, it grows — conquering, absorbing, organising — until the whole Mediterranean is ringed by a single empire and the sea itself is a Roman lake. Roads run out from the one city to every province; one law and one army hold lands that had been dozens of states. The lesson closes the ancient trio — Egypt, Greece, Rome — with the ancient world's largest lesson in power: how a city becomes an empire, and what an empire does to everyone inside it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rome grows from one city into an empire ringing the whole Mediterranean — the largest single power the ancient world in this course has seen. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: empire, Rome. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
