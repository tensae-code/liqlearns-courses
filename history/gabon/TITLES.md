# History — Gabon course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Gabon's own —
built from Gabon's *Super Efficace en Histoire Géographie
Citoyenneté* teacher guides for the 3e, 4e and 5e années, in
French. Inside the course the organisation is by **levels,
not grades**: the titles below sit inside Levels 1–3, in path
order — what the learner proves, step by step. The grades the
books come from live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the books': the historian's first
steps and Gabon's first peoples first (the craft, the deep
African beginning, the peopling of Gabon, the French arrival,
Libreville, independence, daily life changing), then ships,
trade, colonisation and resistance (the voyages, the coast,
Brazza, the slave trade, French Congo and the AÉF, the
refusals), and last the wider world and the road to
independence (Egypt, Greece, Rome; Berlin and the world
wars; the continent's march). That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — first steps and Gabon's first peoples:**

- `first-steps/` — Level 1, the opening title. What History
  Is (an event, and why the past is kept, opened by the first
  Moon landing photograph) · From Evidence to a Written
  History (objects and traces questioned like witnesses) ·
  Dating the Past (centuries and millennia; writing divides
  prehistory from history on a timeline).
- `africa-to-1500/` — Levels 1–3, the deep past. Africa,
  Cradle of Humankind (the first ancestors, millions of years
  back; the first peoples on the move) · From Stone Tools to
  Farming (tools, fire mastered, settling and the first
  villages — four parts) · Gabon's First Peoples and
  Migrations (the autochthones first, arrivals and mixtures,
  the gabão name, the origin of the Gabonese people — four
  parts) · The Bantu Migrations (the meeting in Gabon —
  negotiate, trade, intermarry — and the continental spread
  of farming, iron and languages).
- `africa-after-1500/` — Levels 1–3, Gabon from the French
  arrival onward. France in Gabon and the Founding of
  Libreville (ships and treaties become a settlement; the
  free town of freed slaves becomes the capital) · Gabon
  Independence 1960 (the flag of 1960; décolonisation step
  by step; Aubame, Mba, and 23:58 on 16 August 1960) · Gabon
  Daily Life Changes (houses, barter-to-money trade,
  canoes-to-roads transport).

**Level 2 — ships, trade, colonisation and resistance:**

- `modern-world/` — Levels 2–3, the world's turns as Gabon's
  books teach them. Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes
  (the 15th- and 16th-century voyages in search of routes
  and riches).
- `africa-after-1500/` — Level 2, in path order: Europeans
  on the Coast: Trade on African Terms (beach trade, the
  comptoirs, the forts and factories — four parts) ·
  Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior (Du Chaillu
  and the gorillas) · Brazza and the Exploration of the
  Ogooué (the river ascent and the treaties) · Colonisation:
  The Road to the Scramble (treaties slide into direct rule;
  Europe's need for raw materials and markets) · The
  Atlantic Slave Trade: Why Africa (the trade defined, the
  question asked) · The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts
  and the Gun Cycle (the triangular trade mapped; the human
  cost counted) · Gabon in French Congo and the AEF (folded
  into French Congo, then one territory among four in the
  AÉF) · Gabon Resistance to Colonisation (King Denis's
  court and beyond; Emane Ntole, Nyonda Makita (Mavurulu),
  Mbombè, the Binzima movement).

**Level 3 — the wider world and the road to independence:**

- `africa-to-1500/` — Level 3: Ancient Egypt: The Nile
  Kingdom (pharaohs, pyramids, scribes) · Ancient Egypt:
  What Egypt Gave (the stars mapped, the flood counted, the
  afterlife prepared).
- `ancient-world/` — Level 3: Ancient Civilisations: Greece
  and Crete (the city-states and their new ways of ruling
  and thinking) · Ancient Civilisations: Rome (one city
  ringing the whole Mediterranean with an empire).
- `africa-after-1500/` — Level 3: African Resistance to
  Colonisation (arms and arguments across the continent) ·
  Colonisation: Its Effects on Africa (exploitation —
  wealth extracted for Europe's benefit) · Colonisation:
  The Berlin Conference (the rules for dividing Africa,
  agreed in Berlin) · Decolonisation: Ghana, Kenya and the
  South (after 1945 the demands grow until the flags fall).
- `modern-world/` — Level 3: The First World War: Course of
  the War (the empires at war, the fighting fixed into
  trenches) · The First World War: Versailles and the
  Results (Germany's colonies lost; Africa's soldiers and
  porters pay heavily) · The Second World War: Course in
  Europe (a second war engulfs Europe and reaches the
  colonies again) · The World After 1945: Losses, Division
  and Rebuilding (the UN born; colonial peoples demand their
  freedom).
