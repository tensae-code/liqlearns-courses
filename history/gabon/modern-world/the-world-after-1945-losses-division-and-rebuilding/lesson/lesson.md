# The World After 1945: Losses, Division and Rebuilding

## Do it first

Mime rubble being cleared, then a new building rising, then a crowd raising its hands and voices. This lesson is what the world did with the war's end.

The second war ends in ruin — and in two beginnings. The nations, determined that the world needs a common house, found the United Nations. And across the colonial world, peoples who carried the empires' wars draw the conclusion the empires did not intend: if freedom was worth fighting for in Europe, it is owed in Africa and Asia too. The demands for freedom rise everywhere at once. The road to Gabon's 1960 — and to the whole continent's independences — starts in this lesson, in 1945.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the war ends, the United Nations is born, and colonial peoples demand their freedom — the war's end becomes the colonies' beginning. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: United Nations, freedom. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
