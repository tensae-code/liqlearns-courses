# The First World War: Course of the War

## Do it first

Dig an imaginary trench with a spade, then crouch in it and wait. On the other side of an imaginary field, another trench waits. This lesson is how a war became those two waiting lines.

In 1914 the empires of Europe go to war with each other. The armies march expecting a short war; instead the fighting fixes into trenches — lines of dug earth facing each other across ruined ground, where men live, fight and die for years over small strips of land. And because the fighters are empires, the war does not stay in Europe: it reaches into the colonies, drawing in African soldiers and African land. Gabon's book teaches the war as the empires' war — with the colonies inside it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Europe's empires go to war in 1914 and the fighting fixes into trenches — a war of the empires that reaches into their colonies. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: trench, empire. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
