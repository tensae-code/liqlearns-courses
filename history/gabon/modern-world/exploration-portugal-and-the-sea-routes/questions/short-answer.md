# Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Leçon 17 Les grands voyages maritimes des 15e et 16e siècles": who, where, and what turned?
**Answer:** In the 15th and 16th centuries, Europe's sailors take to the oceans in earnest. Portuguese ships push down the African coast cape by cape — it is on this push that Gabon's river mouth gets its gabão name — and other voyages reach around the whole world. The motive is named plainly: routes and riches. Whoever finds a sea route to the goods of the East, and to Africa's own wealth, holds the trade of the age. These voyages open the door through which everything else in this level — trade, posts, colonies — will walk.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
**Answer:** In the 15th and 16th centuries European sailors push down the African coast and around the world, in search of routes and riches — the voyages that first bring European ships to Gabon's shore.
