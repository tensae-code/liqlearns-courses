# Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes

## Do it first

Trace a line down an imaginary coastline with your finger, then around a whole round world. This lesson follows the sailors who first drew that line.

In the 15th and 16th centuries, Europe's sailors take to the oceans in earnest. Portuguese ships push down the African coast cape by cape — it is on this push that Gabon's river mouth gets its gabão name — and other voyages reach around the whole world. The motive is named plainly: routes and riches. Whoever finds a sea route to the goods of the East, and to Africa's own wealth, holds the trade of the age. These voyages open the door through which everything else in this level — trade, posts, colonies — will walk.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: in the 15th and 16th centuries European sailors push down the African coast and around the world, in search of routes and riches — the voyages that first bring European ships to Gabon's shore. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: sea route, voyage. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
