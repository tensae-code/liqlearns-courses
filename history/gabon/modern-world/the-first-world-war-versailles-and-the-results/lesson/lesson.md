# The First World War: Versailles and the Results

## Do it first

Carry an imaginary heavy load on your shoulders a long way, then set it down in an empty village. This lesson counts what the war cost — and who paid.

The war ends, and the settlement redraws the world. Germany, defeated, loses its colonies — they are handed to the victors, their peoples consulted no more than before. Prices crash in the disorder that follows. And the book insists on the African count: Africa's soldiers fought in the empires' armies, and Africa's porters carried the war on their backs, and they pay heavily — in lives, in labour, in lands traded over their heads. The consequences of the First World War are African consequences too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the war ends with Germany losing its colonies and prices crashing — and Africa's soldiers and porters pay heavily for a war that was not theirs. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Versailles, porter. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the war ends with Germany losing its colonies and prices crashing — and Africa's soldiers and porters pay heavily for a war that was not theirs.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
