# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Mime three actions: chipping a stone, warming your hands at a fire, planting a seed. This lesson is the story of how those three actions, one after another, changed how people live.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: each invention changes daily life: the first people shelter, hunt and make tools, tame fire, and at last stop wandering — they plant, herd, and the first villages appear. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
