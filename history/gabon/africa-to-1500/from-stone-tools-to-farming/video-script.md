# Video script — From Stone Tools to Farming

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Each invention changes daily life: the first people shelter, hunt and make tools, tame fire, and at last stop wandering — they plant, herd, and the first villages appear.

How did the first people live, day by day? They shelter where they can — a cave mouth, a screen of branches. They hunt and gather what the land gives. And they make tools: a stone struck against a stone becomes a blade, and the blade changes everything — meat is cut, skins are scraped, wood is worked. The lesson's point is the chain: each invention changes daily life, and a people with better tools lives differently from the people before them.

The older book names the whole age: prehistory, the long time before writing. Nothing in it was written down, so it is told through what survives — tools, bones, the remains of fires and shelters. It opens Gabon's story deliberately: before the country's name, before its peoples, there is this deep African beginning, and the tools in the ground are its first documents.

Then comes the taming of fire. The first people learn to keep a flame alive, to feed it and guard it — and life bends around it. Food is cooked and becomes safer and softer. Nights gain warmth and light. Wild animals keep their distance from the fire's circle. Fire is the first great tool that is not held in the hand, and mastering it changes cooking, warmth and safety forever.

The last turn is the biggest: people stop wandering. They learn that a seed kept and planted returns as food — agriculture — and that animals can be kept and bred — élevage. A family that plants must stay to harvest, so huts become houses and camps become the first villages. Food can now be stored, and life can be planned. Almost everything later in history — towns, trades, kingdoms — stands on this settling down.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
