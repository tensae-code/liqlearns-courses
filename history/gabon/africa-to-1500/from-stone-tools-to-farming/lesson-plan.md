# Lesson plan — From Stone Tools to Farming

**Course:** History — Gabon · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From Stone Tools to Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and Gabon's First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** each invention changes daily life: the first people shelter, hunt and make tools, tame fire, and at last stop wandering — they plant, herd, and the first villages appear.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and Gabon's First Peoples — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Leçon 13 Le mode de vie des premiers hommes** — How did the first people live, day by day? They shelter where they can — a cave mouth, a screen of branches.
2. **Leçon 1 La Préhistoire africaine** — The older book names the whole age: prehistory, the long time before writing.
3. **Leçon 4 La Préhistoire: la maîtrise du feu** — Then comes the taming of fire.
4. **Leçon 9 La sédentarisation: agriculture et élevage** — The last turn is the biggest: people stop wandering.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: each invention changes daily life: the first people shelter, hunt and make tools, tame fire, and at last stop wandering — they plant, herd, and the first villages appear.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the Gabon they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Gabon and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

- Go back to the one thing: each invention changes daily life: the first people shelter, hunt and make tools, tame fire, and at last stop wandering — they plant, herd, and the first villages appear. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
