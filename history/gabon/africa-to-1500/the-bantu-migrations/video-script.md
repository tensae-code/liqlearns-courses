# Video script — The Bantu Migrations

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Bantu-speaking peoples spread across central Africa carrying farming, iron and languages, and in Gabon they meet the first peoples — and the two worlds negotiate, trade and intermarry.

Into Gabon come the Bantous — Bantu-speaking newcomers moving into the forest country. They meet the first peoples who are already there. What happens next is not one thing: the two worlds negotiate where each will live, they trade what each has and the other wants, and they intermarry, so that families themselves become the meeting place. Gabon is changed by the encounter — new languages, new ways in the forest — and the newcomers are changed by the land and its first people too.

The older book widens the lens to the whole region. The Bantu are not one village on the move but a great spreading of peoples across central Africa, over long ages. They carry three things that remake every land they enter: farming, the working of iron, and their languages, which take root so deeply that Bantu languages are spoken across half a continent today. Gabon's meeting with the Bantu, in the lesson before, is one chapter of this continental story.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
