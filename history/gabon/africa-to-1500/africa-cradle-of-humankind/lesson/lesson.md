# Africa, Cradle of Humankind

## Do it first

Crouch down and pretend to brush dust gently off something buried in the ground, the way a careful digger does. What you are uncovering, in this lesson, is the oldest story of all: our own beginning.

## Sub lesson 1 — Leçon 10 Les premiers hommes

Deep in the earth, archaeologists brush the soil from ancient bones. The bones are not of kings or farmers — they are of the very first people, humankind's first ancestors, dated millions of years back. And the ground that gives them up is African ground. The lesson plants the flag of the whole human story here: before there were countries, languages or towns, the first people walked in Africa. Every later story in this course — and every people's story on earth — grows out of this beginning.

## Sub lesson 2 — Leçon 7 La Préhistoire: les déplacements des populations

The first people did not stay in one valley. The older book follows their movements: prehistoric groups keep moving across Africa, following the game they hunt, the water they need, and the climate as it shifts. A dry season empties one land and fills another; a herd's path becomes a people's path. Movement is the first habit of humankind — Gabon's own first peoples, met later in this course, are part of this same long walking.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: humankind begins in Africa: archaeologists brush ancient bones from the earth and date the first ancestors millions of years back, and those early people move, spreading across the continent. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: archaeologist, ancestor. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
