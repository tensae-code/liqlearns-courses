# Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

## Do it first

Cup your hands like a river valley, then stack three blocks into a pyramid on the table. This lesson travels to the Nile, where Africa built one of the world's first great kingdoms.

The final primary year widens out from Gabon to the great old worlds — and it starts in Africa. On the Nile, in the continent's north-east, a kingdom rises that will last thousands of years: Egypt. Its kings are the pharaohs; its monuments are the pyramids, still standing; its memory is kept by scribes, the trained writers whose signs cover its walls. The lesson gives Egypt first place in the wider story on purpose: before Greece, before Rome, Africa's Nile kingdom is already great.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: on the Nile a great kingdom rises in Africa's north-east — pharaohs, pyramids and scribes — one of the oldest civilisations on earth, and an African one. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: pharaoh, Nile, scribe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
