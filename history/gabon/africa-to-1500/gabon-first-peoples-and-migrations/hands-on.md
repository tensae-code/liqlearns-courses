# Hands-on — Gabon's First Peoples and Migrations

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand at an imaginary river mouth and look: forest behind you, water in front. Say who was here first, and who came later by land and by sea. This lesson tells both arrivals.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Gabon has been inhabited for thousands of years: its first peoples — the autochthones, called pygmées in the books — hold the forest first, then other populations arrive, settle and mix, and over centuries the mixtures make the Gabonese people; even the country's name comes from this meeting of worlds, from the Portuguese gabão. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
