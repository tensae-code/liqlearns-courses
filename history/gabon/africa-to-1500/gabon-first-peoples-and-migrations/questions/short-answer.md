# Gabon's First Peoples and Migrations — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Leçon 19 Le peuplement du Gabon, les migrations": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** Gabon was not empty waiting to be discovered. The lesson teaches that the land has been inhabited for thousands of years: first peoples settle in the forest and along the rivers, living by what the land gives. Then, over long ages, other populations arrive — migrations, peoples on the move looking for land, water and safety. They settle beside the first peoples, and the two worlds mix. The peopling of Gabon is this layering: first peoples first, then arrival upon arrival.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Gabon has been inhabited for thousands of years: its first peoples — the autochthones, called pygmées in the books — hold the forest first, then other populations arrive, settle and mix, and over centuries the mixtures make the Gabonese people; even the country's name comes from this meeting of worlds, from the Portuguese gabão.
