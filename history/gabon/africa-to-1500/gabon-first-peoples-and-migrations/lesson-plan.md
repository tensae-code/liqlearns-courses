# Lesson plan — Gabon's First Peoples and Migrations

**Course:** History — Gabon · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Gabon's First Peoples and Migrations
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and Gabon's First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Gabon has been inhabited for thousands of years: its first peoples — the autochthones, called pygmées in the books — hold the forest first, then other populations arrive, settle and mix, and over centuries the mixtures make the Gabonese people; even the country's name comes from this meeting of worlds, from the Portuguese gabão.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and Gabon's First Peoples — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Leçon 19 Le peuplement du Gabon, les migrations** — Gabon was not empty waiting to be discovered.
2. **Leçon 26 L'origine du nom « Gabon »** — Even the country's name tells the story of a meeting.
3. **Leçon 14 L'origine du peuple gabonais** — The older book draws the threads together and asks: where does the Gabonese people come from? The answer is mixture.
4. **Leçon 11 Les peuples autochtones du Gabon** — The final primary book returns to the very first root.

## If the learner is stuck
- Makes the first peoples latecomers → the books are plain: the autochthones hold the forest long before later arrivals; the Gabonese people are made by the meeting and mixing that follows.
- Go back to the one thing: Gabon has been inhabited for thousands of years: its first peoples — the autochthones, called pygmées in the books — hold the forest first, then other populations arrive, settle and mix, and over centuries the mixtures make the Gabonese people; even the country's name comes from this meeting of worlds, from the Portuguese gabão. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
