# About the History — Myanmar course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 6 History teacher guide. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Myanmar inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **three levels**, following the
book's own two arcs as filed:

1. **Level 1 — Myanmar's own past.** Prehistory — technology
   climbing from the Rock Age through the Bronze Age to the
   Metal Age, in harmony with the environment; the emergence
   of cities — the Pyu city-states one by one; and the Bagan
   Empire, its rise and its economy, society, literature and
   architecture, judged critically.
2. **Level 2 — the first people of the world: hunters,
   farmers and the river kingdoms.** The origin of human
   society — the Rock Age hunters and their mastery of fire,
   then agriculture and country life; Mesopotamia in its
   four ages; and Egypt on the Nile.
3. **Level 3 — the great ancient civilisations.** Greece,
   Rome, China and India, in the guide's own order — what
   each built, and how far each one's reach ran.

All ten lessons stand on a **teacher guide** and count
nowhere in the acceptance reckoning below — they are
evidence only, labelled as such in every lesson.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Myanmar first, then the world:* "Start in Myanmar itself:
  the first people knapping stone, then casting bronze, then
  working iron; the Pyu cities rising behind their walls —
  Beikthano, Hanlin, Sri Ksetra; and Bagan gathering the
  Irrawaddy plain into an empire of pagodas. Then step out
  to the whole human story: the hunters who mastered fire,
  the first farmers, the kingdoms between two rivers and on
  the Nile — and the great civilisations, Greece, Rome,
  China and India, whose ideas travelled the world."
