# Hands-on — The Origin of Human Society: Hunters and First Farmers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one meal you ate this week. Trace it backwards with your finger on the table: the plate, the shop or field, the plant or animal, the person who gathered, hunted or grew it. At the end of the line ask: did somebody chase this food, or grow it? That one question divides the two halves of this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: human society begins with the hunters — bands of the early, middle and late Rock Age who developed weapons and tools and mastered fire — and turns when farming begins: planting replaces chasing, villages replace camps, and agriculture gradually becomes part of human civilisation, in the Mesopotamia region and Egypt. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
