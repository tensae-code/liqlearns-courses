# Ancient Civilisations: Rome

## Do it first

Take a drawing a friend made and copy it — then change one thing to make it your own and useful in a new place. Ask: is the copy still your friend's, or has it become yours too? Rome did this with the culture of a whole civilisation.

Rome's story begins with a debt honestly owned: much of what Rome became, it **learned from Greek culture** — Greek gods, Greek learning, Greek arts and ways of building came into Roman life and were made Roman. The book teaches Roman culture as exactly this meeting: what Rome built, and what it learned from Greece.

What Rome built on that foundation was its own. **Roads**: straight, lasting roads binding city to city, so that armies, traders and laws could travel. **Law**: rules written, argued and applied across a vast land, so that distant peoples lived under one known order. And an **empire's reach**: a single rule stretching over lands and peoples far beyond Italy.

So the turn of Rome's story is double: Greece gave Rome a culture; Rome gave that culture roads, law and reach — and carried it further than Greece itself ever did.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rome took Greek culture and carried it further than Greece ever reached: what Rome learned from the Greeks it joined to what Rome itself built — roads, law and an empire's reach. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Rome, Roman culture, Greek culture, roads, law, empire. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
