# Hands-on — The Pyu City-States of Ancient Myanmar

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a small settlement: a few houses scattered by a river. Now draw what it needs to become a city — a wall around it, fields that feed many mouths, a market, a place of worship. Under each thing you add, write the skill that makes it possible. You have drawn the birth of the Pyu city-states.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Myanmar's first cities were the Pyu city-states: farming skill, craft knowledge and growing capability gathered people behind walls, and a chain of walled, irrigated, Buddhist-and-Hindu trading cities — Beikthano, Hanlin, Sri Ksetra, Maingmaw, Thuwunnabumi and Waitharli — flourished for centuries before any Myanmar empire existed. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
