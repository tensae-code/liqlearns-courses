# Hands-on — Myanmar Prehistory: Rock, Bronze and Metal Ages

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find three things near you: a stone, something made of a soft metal, and something made of iron or steel — a spoon, a nail, a tool. Put them in a row, softest and simplest first. Ask of each one: what could the person who made this do that the person before could not? You have just laid out Myanmar's prehistory in your hands.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: in Myanmar's prehistory, people's technology climbed step by step — from the Rock Age, when they knapped stone, through the Bronze Age, when they cast bronze, to the Metal Age, when they worked iron — and each new material bought them new power over nature, while they lived in harmony with their environment. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
