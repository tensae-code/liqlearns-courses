# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

## Do it first

Draw two wavy lines side by side on paper — two rivers. Shade the land between them. Now write four names down the land in a column: Sumerian, Babylonian, Assyrian, Chaldean. Ask: how can one piece of land have four ages? This lesson walks them in order.

The land between two rivers — that is what **Mesopotamia** means, and the farming that began there grew some of the world's first cities. The book walks its story in **four ages**, one after another on the same river plain.

First the **Sumerian** age: city people led by their **priests**, serving their gods in great temples, organising the water and the fields. Then the **Babylonian** age: the age of **law-giving kings**, whose written laws held a wide land together. Then the **Assyrian** age: an age of **iron armies**, feared conquerors ruling by force. And last the **Chaldean** age: the **star-watchers**, whose priests read the heavens and mapped the skies.

Four ages, four kinds of power — the priest, the law-giver, the army, the star-watcher — succeeding one another between the same two rivers. That is the shape of Mesopotamia's story: the land stays, the ages turn.
