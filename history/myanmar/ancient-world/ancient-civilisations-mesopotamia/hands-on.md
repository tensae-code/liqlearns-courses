# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw two wavy lines side by side on paper — two rivers. Shade the land between them. Now write four names down the land in a column: Sumerian, Babylonian, Assyrian, Chaldean. Ask: how can one piece of land have four ages? This lesson walks them in order.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: between two rivers, Mesopotamia runs through four ages in turn — Sumerian, Babylonian, Assyrian and Chaldean — city priests, law-giving kings, iron armies and star-watchers succeeding one another on the same river plain. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
