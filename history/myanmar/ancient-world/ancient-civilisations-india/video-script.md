# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: India

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
India's ancient civilisation reached far beyond India: its gods and its scripts travelled with its traders — Shiva worship, and Buddhism arriving and spreading — until Indian culture was building temples in Cambodia, in the Indianised city of Angkor.

India's lesson is a lesson about reach. The book teaches India's ancient civilisation together with how far it travelled: India's culture did not stay inside India. It moved with India's traders, across the seas to the lands of Southeast Asia — the very region Myanmar itself belongs to.

What travelled can be named. Worship: the book names the worship of Shiva, the Hindu god, carried to new lands and raised in new temples. Faith: Buddhism, born in India, arriving in land after land and spreading through them — the same Buddhism that would shape Myanmar's own cities and empires in this course's first level. And scripts: India's letters travelled too, giving written words to peoples who adopted them.

The journey's far marker is Angkor in Cambodia — a great city the book calls Indianised, where India's gods, scripts and temple-building had taken root so deeply that they raised one of the wonders of the ancient world. Gods and scripts travel with traders: that is how a civilisation's reach is measured — not by its armies, but by what other peoples chose to keep.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
