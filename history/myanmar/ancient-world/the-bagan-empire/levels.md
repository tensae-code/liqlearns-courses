# The Bagan Empire — depth

This lesson has 2 sub lessons, each opening when the one before it is proved; the gate pool for each is that sub lesson's whole question set (no hardness ranking is written in questions/). Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — from the Pyu world a new power gathered the Irrawaddy plain into the Bagan Empire, Myanmar's first great empire — rich on taxes paid in rice, served by a sangha of monks, literate, and built in stone with thousands of pagodas — and the book asks learners to judge that greatness critically — in their own words, give one real example from their own life, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new home, school or street situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
