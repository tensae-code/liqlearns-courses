# Hands-on — The Bagan Empire

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand a row of small stones or blocks on a table, one for each village you know of. Now move them slowly together into one cluster inside a ring. Ask: what changed when they came under one rule — what got easier, and what did it cost? Keep your answers; the Bagan Empire is that cluster, grown vast.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: from the Pyu world a new power gathered the Irrawaddy plain into the Bagan Empire, Myanmar's first great empire — rich on taxes paid in rice, served by a sangha of monks, literate, and built in stone with thousands of pagodas — and the book asks learners to judge that greatness critically. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
