# Lesson plan — The Bagan Empire

**Course:** History — Myanmar · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Bagan Empire
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Myanmar's Own Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** from the Pyu world a new power gathered the Irrawaddy plain into the Bagan Empire, Myanmar's first great empire — rich on taxes paid in rice, served by a sangha of monks, literate, and built in stone with thousands of pagodas — and the book asks learners to judge that greatness critically.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Myanmar's Own Past — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How Bagan emerged and became powerful** — From the Pyu world, a new power gathers the Irrawaddy plain into Myanmar's first great empire.
2. **Bagan's economy, society, literature and architecture — judged critically** — Taxes in rice, a sangha of monks and thousands of pagodas make Bagan rich, literate and stone-built — and the book asks what it cost.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **The Pyu City-States of Ancient Myanmar** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Myanmar and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Bagan's greatness is only its pagodas → the pagodas stand on an economy — taxes in rice — and a society — the sangha and its learning; the book asks for the cost to be judged too.
- Go back to the one thing: from the Pyu world a new power gathered the Irrawaddy plain into the Bagan Empire, Myanmar's first great empire — rich on taxes paid in rice, served by a sangha of monks, literate, and built in stone with thousands of pagodas — and the book asks learners to judge that greatness critically. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
