# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Ancient Greece was not one kingdom but many small, proud city-states, whose economy, religion and social structures — their trading, their worship and their arguing — wove them into one shared Greek civilisation.

Greece's civilisation did not grow inside one great kingdom. It grew in small, proud city-states — each city ruling itself, jealous of its own ways, and yet all of them Greek together. The book studies that civilisation through three windows: its economy, its religion and its social structures.

Its economy was movement: the city-states traded — goods, oil, wine, craftwork and ideas moving from port to port, tying the cities to each other and to the wider sea. Its religion was shared: the same gods worshipped, the same festivals kept, so that a Greek travelling to another city still stood on familiar ground before the gods. And its social structures ordered each city's people — who ruled, who worked, who counted as a citizen — argued over, endlessly, in public.

That arguing is part of the story: the Greek city-states traded, worshipped and argued their way into a shared culture. No single throne made Greece one; the sharing did.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
