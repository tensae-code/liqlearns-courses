# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw five small circles on a page, each its own little city with its own wall. Draw dotted lines between them — ships, goods, travellers, ideas. Ask: if no king rules all five, what can still make them one civilisation? Greece's answer is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: ancient Greece was not one kingdom but many small, proud city-states, whose economy, religion and social structures — their trading, their worship and their arguing — wove them into one shared Greek civilisation. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
