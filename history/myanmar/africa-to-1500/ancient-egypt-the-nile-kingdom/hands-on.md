# Hands-on — Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a long river down a page, through dry land. Mark the months when its water rises. Under the drawing write three words: calendar, granary, kingdom. Ask: how can one river's rising water become all three? Egypt's story is the answer.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: in Egypt, the Nile's flood became a calendar, a granary and a kingdom: plough agriculture on the river's gift of water and soil carried a whole civilisation — its culture and its technology — on one river. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
