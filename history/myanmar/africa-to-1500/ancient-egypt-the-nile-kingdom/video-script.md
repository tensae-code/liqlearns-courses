# Video script — Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
In Egypt, the Nile's flood became a calendar, a granary and a kingdom: plough agriculture on the river's gift of water and soil carried a whole civilisation — its culture and its technology — on one river.

Egypt's civilisation stands on one river: the Nile. Every year the river floods, spreading water and rich soil over the fields beside it — and Egyptian farmers built their lives on that rhythm, ploughing and planting as the waters fell. This is plough agriculture on the Nile: the river does the watering, and a whole land eats.

Because the flood came on a rhythm, it could be measured — and so the river's flood became a calendar, telling farmers and priests when to plant, when to harvest, when to celebrate. Because the harvests were rich and regular, they could be stored — and the flood became a granary, grain kept against lean times. And because somebody had to organise the water, the measuring and the storing, the flood became a kingdom: rulers, officials and builders raised on the river's gift.

Out of that organisation grew Egypt's culture and technology — the building, writing, craft and knowledge the book follows. One river, three gifts: a calendar, a granary and a kingdom.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
