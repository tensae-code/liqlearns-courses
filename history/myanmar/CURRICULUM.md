# History — Myanmar course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Myanmar inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Myanmar's book in hand is one —
the Grade 6 *Teacher Guide — History* — and its own year is
the ladder. The guide's own filing says the year is split in
two arcs: Myanmar's own past first (prehistory, the Pyu
city-states, Bagan), then world history (hunters, first
farmers, Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, Rome, China, India) —
and every lesson in it is taught in a fixed
Introduce–Teach–Practice–Review (I-T-P-R) shape with
student-centred activities. So the ladder is: Myanmar's own
past first (Level 1, the guide's Chapters 1–3, three
lessons), then the world's first people — the hunters, the
first farmers, and the two river kingdoms their farming
built (Level 2, Chapters 4.1–4.2.2), and last the great
ancient civilisations the guide walks in order — Greece,
Rome, China, India (Level 3, Chapters 4.3.1–4.3.4). The
ladder stops where the book in hand stops, not because
Myanmar teaches nothing more. Every lesson stands on
teacher-guide evidence only — the acceptance reckoning in
ABOUT.md says so plainly.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 6 History teacher guide — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Myanmar's Own Past** — the guide's first arc, the past
   of Myanmar itself: prehistory, told as the slow climb of
   technology from the Rock Age through the Bronze Age to
   the Metal Age, with people living in harmony with their
   environment; the emergence of cities — the Pyu
   city-states, Beikthano, Hanlin, Sri Ksetra, Maingmaw,
   Thuwunnabumi and Waitharli, walled, irrigated,
   Buddhist-and-Hindu trading cities flourishing centuries
   before any empire; and the Bagan Empire — how it emerged
   and became powerful, and its economy, society, literature
   and architecture, judged critically. 3 lessons, in one
   title: `ancient-world/` (3 lessons).

2. **The First People of the World: Hunters, Farmers and
   the River Kingdoms** — the guide's world arc opens with
   the origin of human society: the Rock Age hunters of the
   early, middle and late ages, their weapons and tools and
   their mastery of fire; then agriculture and country life,
   farming gradually becoming part of human civilisation in
   the Mesopotamia region and Egypt; then the two kingdoms
   that farming built — Mesopotamia in its four ages
   (Sumerian, Babylonian, Assyrian, Chaldean) and Egypt, its
   culture and technology and plough agriculture on the
   Nile. 3 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/`
   (2 lessons) and `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson).

3. **The Great Ancient Civilisations** — the guide's closing
   walk, in its own order: Greece — the economy, religion
   and social structures of its small proud city-states;
   Rome — what Rome built, and what it learned from Greek
   culture; China — the region where Chinese culture was
   born on the Yellow River and how it developed; and India
   — its ancient civilisation and its reach, its gods and
   scripts travelling with its traders as far as Angkor.
   4 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (4 lessons).

**Totals: 10 lessons** (Level 1: 3 · Level 2: 3 · Level 3: 4
— 13 placements, all from one teacher guide, evidence only,
acceptance 0).
