# History — Mozambique course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Mozambique inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation. The path follows Mozambique's own books' progression as filed — the placements file's book order is the teaching order, earliest books first — grouped into levels by the turns of Mozambique's own story as its books present them: the first distance cycle (method, origins, the first states; the world opening to empire; the twentieth century followed home to freedom), then the second cycle (historiography itself; the invasion and domination of Africa; nationalism and decolonisation; Mozambique's own deep past and states; and Portuguese colonialism in Mozambique to the launching of the armed struggle). A lesson sits at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; later books deepen lessons already placed, in file order inside each lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Mozambique's own PESD (Programa do Ensino Secundário à Distância) History modules — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps, Human Origins and the First States** — Mozambique's first distance module (Book 1 as filed): history opens with method — what history is, its sources, heritage sites in the learner's own locality, dating and periodisation — then human origins in Africa, the first class societies and the first states of Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece and Rome, and only then Mozambique's own Khoisan and Bantu past, Great Zimbabwe and Mutapa, and the West African empires. 25 lessons, 34 placements.
2. **Level 2 — The World Opens: Expansion, Revolutions and Empire** — Book 2 as filed: feudal Europe cracks open — the three estates, European expansion and colonial plunder, Renaissance, Reformation and absolutism, the English and French revolutions, industrialisation and its workers, socialist answers, the Paris Commune, imperialism and the Scramble, and the First World War, with Africa and Mozambique woven in at every stage. 35 lessons, 41 placements.
3. **Level 3 — The Twentieth Century and Mozambique's Road to Freedom** — Book 3 as filed: October 1917, the Depression, fascism and Nazism — and fascism in Portugal — the Second World War, Potsdam and the UN, the Cold War and the Non-Aligned Movement, each followed home: Salazar's Estado Novo in the colonies, the first nationalist movements, FRELIMO's founding, the armed struggle, independence in 1975, the 1990 Constitution and the Rome peace. 20 lessons, 27 placements.
4. **Level 4 — How History Is Written: Historiography and the Historian's Method** — Book 4 as filed: the second cycle opens with historiography itself — how history-writing evolved from Greece and Rome through Christian, medieval, Renaissance, rationalist and nineteenth-century schools to the Annales and the New History — then African historiography, its problems, tasks and methods, and the question whether history is a science. 5 lessons, 12 placements.
5. **Level 5 — The Invasion of Africa: Scramble, Resistance and Colonial Domination** — Books 5–6 as filed: Africa faces the colonial challenge — the motives, the Berlin Conference, the partition, the road from treaties to military conquest, the redrawn map and the theories of partition — then resistance, region by region (North, East, Central and Southern Africa) and its ideology, and the colonial period dissected: structures, the colonial economy, the French possessions and the British territories, and colonialism's impact on Africa. 20 lessons, 21 placements.
6. **Level 6 — African Nationalism and Decolonisation** — Book 7 as filed: nationalism's emergence, its driving forces and its parties, the colonial powers facing it, and the roads to independence compared empire by empire — the British colonies and Ghana, the French territories and the loi-cadre, the Belgian Congo and Lumumba, British Central Africa — closing with independent Africa's challenges, the OAU becoming the African Union, and SADCC becoming SADC in Mozambique's own region. 13 lessons, 16 placements.
7. **Level 7 — Mozambique's Own Past: First Communities, States and Merchant Penetration** — Books 8–9 as filed: Mozambique's story rebuilt from the start — periodising it and facing its sources problem, the Khoisan hunter-gatherers, the Bantu settlement, lineages straining toward the state, Zimbabwe and Mutapa, the Maravi states, and three foreign-trade eras — gold, ivory, slaves — with the prazos da coroa as the bridge from trade to territorial power, and the Gaza state closing the level. 11 lessons, 11 placements.
8. **Level 8 — Portuguese Colonialism in Mozambique and the Road to Liberation** — Books 10–11 as filed: the conquest of Mozambique region by region — Gaza in the south, the Mozambique Company in the centre, the Makua, Yao and Islamised coast in the north — the colonial state and economy built on chartered companies and migrant labour to South Africa's mines, then Salazar's Estado Novo from 1930: forced cotton, rice and tea, mental colonisation, everyday resistance, the exile movements, and FRELIMO's creation and the launching of the armed struggle. 19 lessons, 21 placements.

**Totals: 148 lessons, 183 placements** (Level 1: 25 lessons; Level 2: 35 lessons; Level 3: 20 lessons; Level 4: 5 lessons; Level 5: 20 lessons; Level 6: 13 lessons; Level 7: 11 lessons; Level 8: 19 lessons).
