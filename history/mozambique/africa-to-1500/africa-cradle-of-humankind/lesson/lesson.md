# Africa, Cradle of Humankind

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Lição 8: África — berço da humanidade. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Lição 8: África — berço da humanidade

The oldest hominid bones keep turning up in African soil, so the human story starts in Africa and spreads out from there.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this story, where it happens, and what changes because of it — that turn is the lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the oldest hominid bones keep turning up in African soil, so the human story starts in Africa and spreads out from there. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Africa, Cradle of Humankind. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the oldest hominid bones keep turning up in African soil, so the human story starts in Africa and spreads out from there.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson's story echoes in real life. Note it.
