# Lesson plan — The First World War Causes

**Course:** History — Mozambique · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The First World War Causes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The World Opens: Expansion, Revolutions and Empire (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rival powers lock themselves into alliances and military blocs, fight the first imperialist clashes, and slide toward world war.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The World Opens: Expansion, Revolutions and Empire — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lição 30: Formação das alianças e blocos militares e os primeiros conflitos entre as potências imperialistas** — Rival powers lock themselves into alliances and military blocs, fight the first imperialist clashes, and slide toward world war.
2. **Lição 31: As causas da 1ª Guerra Mundial** — Imperialist economic competition hardens into rivalries until the powers choose war — the causes of the First World War laid out.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **The Great Capitalist Powers 1870–1914** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: rival powers lock themselves into alliances and military blocs, fight the first imperialist clashes, and slide toward world war.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Mozambique, Africa and the world you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
