# About the History — Mozambique course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Mozambique's own PESD (Programa do Ensino Secundário à Distância) History modules, first and second cycle. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Mozambique inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **eight levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps, Human Origins and the First States.** Mozambique's first distance module (Book 1 as filed): history opens with method — what history is, its sources, heritage sites in the learner's own locality, dating and periodisation — then human origins in Africa, the first class societies and the first states of Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece and Rome, and only then Mozambique's own Khoisan and Bantu past, Great Zimbabwe and Mutapa, and the West African empires. (25 lessons.)
2. **Level 2 — The World Opens: Expansion, Revolutions and Empire.** Book 2 as filed: feudal Europe cracks open — the three estates, European expansion and colonial plunder, Renaissance, Reformation and absolutism, the English and French revolutions, industrialisation and its workers, socialist answers, the Paris Commune, imperialism and the Scramble, and the First World War, with Africa and Mozambique woven in at every stage. (35 lessons.)
3. **Level 3 — The Twentieth Century and Mozambique's Road to Freedom.** Book 3 as filed: October 1917, the Depression, fascism and Nazism — and fascism in Portugal — the Second World War, Potsdam and the UN, the Cold War and the Non-Aligned Movement, each followed home: Salazar's Estado Novo in the colonies, the first nationalist movements, FRELIMO's founding, the armed struggle, independence in 1975, the 1990 Constitution and the Rome peace. (20 lessons.)
4. **Level 4 — How History Is Written: Historiography and the Historian's Method.** Book 4 as filed: the second cycle opens with historiography itself — how history-writing evolved from Greece and Rome through Christian, medieval, Renaissance, rationalist and nineteenth-century schools to the Annales and the New History — then African historiography, its problems, tasks and methods, and the question whether history is a science. (5 lessons.)
5. **Level 5 — The Invasion of Africa: Scramble, Resistance and Colonial Domination.** Books 5–6 as filed: Africa faces the colonial challenge — the motives, the Berlin Conference, the partition, the road from treaties to military conquest, the redrawn map and the theories of partition — then resistance, region by region (North, East, Central and Southern Africa) and its ideology, and the colonial period dissected: structures, the colonial economy, the French possessions and the British territories, and colonialism's impact on Africa. (20 lessons.)
6. **Level 6 — African Nationalism and Decolonisation.** Book 7 as filed: nationalism's emergence, its driving forces and its parties, the colonial powers facing it, and the roads to independence compared empire by empire — the British colonies and Ghana, the French territories and the loi-cadre, the Belgian Congo and Lumumba, British Central Africa — closing with independent Africa's challenges, the OAU becoming the African Union, and SADCC becoming SADC in Mozambique's own region. (13 lessons.)
7. **Level 7 — Mozambique's Own Past: First Communities, States and Merchant Penetration.** Books 8–9 as filed: Mozambique's story rebuilt from the start — periodising it and facing its sources problem, the Khoisan hunter-gatherers, the Bantu settlement, lineages straining toward the state, Zimbabwe and Mutapa, the Maravi states, and three foreign-trade eras — gold, ivory, slaves — with the prazos da coroa as the bridge from trade to territorial power, and the Gaza state closing the level. (11 lessons.)
8. **Level 8 — Portuguese Colonialism in Mozambique and the Road to Liberation.** Books 10–11 as filed: the conquest of Mozambique region by region — Gaza in the south, the Mozambique Company in the centre, the Makua, Yao and Islamised coast in the north — the colonial state and economy built on chartered companies and migrant labour to South Africa's mines, then Salazar's Estado Novo from 1930: forced cotton, rice and tea, mental colonisation, everyday resistance, the exile movements, and FRELIMO's creation and the launching of the armed struggle. (19 lessons.)

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Method, Africa, the world — then home:* "First learn what history is and where humans begin, in Africa. Watch the world open, empires rise and crack, and follow every turn home to Mozambique — its first peoples and states, the conquest and the colonial years, FRELIMO, independence in 1975, the 1990 Constitution and the Rome peace. Then learn how history itself is written, and walk Mozambique's whole story a second time, deeper."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Book 1 — PESD I Módulo 1, História** — Self-study distance module: history opens with method (what history is, sources, heritage sites — incl. Mozambican sites such as Nwadjahane, birthplace of Eduardo Mondlane), then human origins in Africa, then the world's first states, and only then Mozambique's own Khoisan and Bantu past and the Shona states — the national story is reached through the African story. *(Student book.)*

**Book 2 — PESD I Módulo 2, História** — A Marxist-framed world history: feudalism gives way to capitalism, expansion, revolutions and imperialism — with Mozambique and Africa woven in at every stage (Portuguese expansion, colonial plunder, monopoly companies, Africans in WWI), so world events are always read for what they did to Africa. *(Student book.)*

**Book 3 — PESD I Módulo 3, História** — The 20th century is taught as the road to Mozambican freedom: world wars, fascism and the Cold War are each followed home — Salazar's Estado Novo in the colonies, the first nationalist stirrings, FRELIMO's founding, the armed struggle, independence in 1975, and finally the 1990 Constitution and the Rome peace that ended the 16-year war. *(Student book.)*

**Book 4 — PESD II Módulo 1, Introdução à História** — The second cycle opens not with events but with historiography itself — a full module on how history-writing evolved from Greece to the Annales school, ending with African historiography and its special problems (scarce sources, chronology, myth), training learners to ask how any history was made. *(Student book.)*

**Book 5 — PESD II Módulo 2, A Invasão do Continente Africano** — The Scramble is taught from the receiving end and at length: half the module is African resistance — its ideology, and region-by-region case studies (North, East, Central and Southern Africa) — so conquest is never told as an African non-event. *(Student book.)*

**Book 6 — PESD II Módulo 3, O Período de Dominação Colonial em África** — Colonial rule is dissected comparatively — French possessions and British territories each get their own lessons on structures and economies — before the module judges colonialism's political, economic and social consequences for Africa as a whole. *(Student book.)*

**Book 7 — PESD II Módulo 4, O Nacionalismo Africano** — Decolonisation is taught comparatively, empire by empire — British, French, Belgian and British-Central-African roads to independence each get full lessons (Ghana and the Congo as case studies, Lumumba named) — and the story continues past the flags into the OAU→AU passage and SADC integration in Mozambique's own region. *(Student book.)*

**Book 8 — PESD II Módulo 5, Moçambique: Das Comunidades de Caçadores e Recolectores às Sociedades de Exploração** — The second cycle now turns to Mozambique's own deep past as a full module: it opens with method again (periodising Mozambique, the problem of its sources) and then rebuilds the national story from hunter-gatherers through the Bantu settlement to the birth of social differentiation and the state. *(Student book.)*

**Book 9 — PESD II Módulo 6, Os Estados em Moçambique e a Penetração Mercantil Estrangeira** — A whole module of national economic history: Mozambique's states are followed through three foreign-trade eras — gold, then ivory, then the slave cycle — with the prazos da coroa land system dissected as the bridge from trade to territorial power. *(Student book.)*

**Book 10 — PESD II Módulo 7, O Colonialismo Português em Moçambique de 1890 a 1930** — Forty years of Portuguese colonialism get a module of their own, region by region: the conquest of the south (Gaza), centre (by the Mozambique Company) and north (Makua confederations, Yao states, Islamised coast) is told as Mozambican resistance history, and the colonial economy is followed into migrant labour for South African mines. *(Student book.)*

**Book 11 — PESD II Módulo 8, O Colonialismo Português a partir de 1930** — The final module follows Salazar's economic nationalism into forced cotton, rice and tea cultivation and continued labour export, then turns to resistance in its everyday forms — strikes, associations, student nuclei, exile movements (UDENAMO, UNAMI, MANU named with their leaders) — ending where the armed struggle begins, with FRELIMO's creation. *(Student book.)*
