# Video script — Historiography: The History of History-Writing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Greece is named the birthplace of history because the Greeks first treated the past scientifically, unlike the older literary traditions.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 2: Historiografia grego-romana. Greece is named the birthplace of history because the Greeks first treated the past scientifically, unlike the older literary traditions.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 3: A historiografia cristã antiga. A birth in Bethlehem becomes the hinge of the calendar; early Christian writing reframes all history around Christ.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 4: A historiografia medieval. Christianity reshapes medieval thought, and history-writing becomes providential — events read as God's plan unfolding.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 5: A História nos séculos XV e XVI. The Renaissance renews culture and history-writing with it; progress made in the 15th–16th centuries is carried into the Enlightenment.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 6: A historiografia racionalista. After a thousand medieval years, Enlightenment reason produces a new rationalist way of writing history in the 18th century.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 7: A historiografia na primeira metade do século XIX. The French Revolution and the Industrial Revolution shake political, economic and intellectual life, and 19th-century history-writing changes with them.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 8: A historiografia na segunda metade do século XIX. Revolutionary euphoria cools into serene rationality; Positivism, Historicism and Scientific Socialism compete to explain the past.

On the map, follow this part: Lição 9: A História no século XX. History enters the 20th century in crisis; the Annales journal's campaign rescues it and the New History is institutionalised in the 1940s–50s.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Then go and do the lesson.
