## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lição 2: Historiografia grego-romana** — Greece is named the birthplace of history because the Greeks first treated the past scientifically, unlike the older literary traditions.
2. **Lição 3: A historiografia cristã antiga** — A birth in Bethlehem becomes the hinge of the calendar; early Christian writing reframes all history around Christ.
3. **Lição 4: A historiografia medieval** — Christianity reshapes medieval thought, and history-writing becomes providential — events read as God's plan unfolding.
4. **Lição 5: A História nos séculos XV e XVI** — The Renaissance renews culture and history-writing with it; progress made in the 15th–16th centuries is carried into the Enlightenment.
5. **Lição 6: A historiografia racionalista** — After a thousand medieval years, Enlightenment reason produces a new rationalist way of writing history in the 18th century.
6. **Lição 7: A historiografia na primeira metade do século XIX** — The French Revolution and the Industrial Revolution shake political, economic and intellectual life, and 19th-century history-writing changes with them.
7. **Lição 8: A historiografia na segunda metade do século XIX** — Revolutionary euphoria cools into serene rationality; Positivism, Historicism and Scientific Socialism compete to explain the past.
8. **Lição 9: A História no século XX** — History enters the 20th century in crisis; the Annales journal's campaign rescues it and the New History is institutionalised in the 1940s–50s.
