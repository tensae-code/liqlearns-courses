# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Lição 5: O tempo em História: a contagem do tempo; Lição 13: O facto e tempo histórico. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lição 5: O tempo em História: a contagem do tempo

Before stories can be ordered, time itself must be counted — calendars, centuries and the before/after-Christ divide turn events into a sequence.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lição 13: O facto e tempo histórico

The historical fact is the key element of reconstruction, and time is what gives facts their historicity — two concepts every history stands on.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
