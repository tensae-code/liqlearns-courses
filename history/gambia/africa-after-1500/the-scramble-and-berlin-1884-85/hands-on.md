# Hands-on — The Scramble and Berlin 1884–85

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at a political map of West Africa today: straight lines cut across peoples and languages as if drawn with a ruler. Maps like that are not natural — somebody drew them, quickly. How quickly? This lesson gives the book's measure: about forty years for a whole region to change hands. Hold the speed of it. Then read how.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In about forty years the map of West Africa flipped: the scramble for and partition of the region left, by 1910, only Liberia and Ethiopia outside European rule. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
