# Lesson plan — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

**Course:** History — Gambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Captives taken at the Senegal river in 1442 began the trans-Atlantic slave trade — a traffic in people that spread along the coasts, fed a cycle of guns and captives, and ended only in 1866.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Captives taken at the Senegal river in 1442 began the trans-Atlantic slave trade — a traffic in people that spread along the coasts, fed a cycle of guns and captives, and ended only in 1866.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a vicious cycle the learner knows from ordinary life — where the cure being sold makes the sickness worse — and the learner saying why such cycles are hard to escape.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in West Africa, as the book itself works.
