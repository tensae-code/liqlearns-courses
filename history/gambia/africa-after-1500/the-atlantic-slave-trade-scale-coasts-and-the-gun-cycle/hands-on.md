# Hands-on — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Some trades move goods. This one moved people — against their will, across an ocean. Before you read, sit with the dates: it begins in this course in 1442 and ends only in 1866. Count roughly how many generations that is. Hold the weight of that span. This lesson follows the trade's course and scale.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Captives taken at the Senegal river in 1442 began the trans-Atlantic slave trade — a traffic in people that spread along the coasts, fed a cycle of guns and captives, and ended only in 1866. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
