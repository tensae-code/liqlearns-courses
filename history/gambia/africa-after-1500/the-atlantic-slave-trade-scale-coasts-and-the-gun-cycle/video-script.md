# Video script — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Captives taken at the Senegal river in 1442 began the trans-Atlantic slave trade — a traffic in people that spread along the coasts, fed a cycle of guns and captives, and ended only in 1866.

The book dates the **trans-Atlantic slave trade** from the first captives taken at the **Senegal river in 1442** — the same shore, the same year, where the last lesson's meeting of strengths began. What began as seizure grew into a system: European demand for labour in the Americas turned human beings into the coast's most sought-after export.

The trade's **course and scale** widened over centuries — from early raids to organised buying along more and more of the coast, ships carrying captives across the Atlantic in their millions over the whole span. It lasted, in the book's dating, until **1866** — more than four centuries of traffic in people.

Inside the system ran the **gun cycle**: captives were exchanged for guns; guns made the wars and raids that produced more captives; more captives bought more guns. The cycle armed the trade against the very peoples it fed on, and made refusing it dangerous for states that stayed out while their neighbours armed.

Hold the shape plainly: a trade that began with captives seized at a river mouth in 1442 became an Atlantic system — and its ending, in 1866, came only after centuries. The next lesson counts what it cost the people who remained.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
