# Europeans on the Coast: Trade on African Terms

## Do it first

A stranger arrives at your market wanting to buy. Who sets the price — the stranger, or the people whose market it is? Who decides what may be sold, and where the stranger may go? Hold those questions. This lesson opens the age of the ships with the answers West Africa first gave.

In **1442**, Portuguese ships reached the **Senegal river** — the first European sails on this coast in the story the book tells. A new kind of trader had arrived in West Africa: not across the desert by camel, but from the sea.

What the newcomers met was not an empty shore. West Africa had states, markets, armies and laws, and its leaders received the Portuguese as one power dealing with another. Trade on the coast ran **on African terms**: African rulers decided where Europeans could land and trade, what goods were wanted, and what the strangers must pay for the privilege.

So the first European contact belongs in this course as a meeting of strengths. The Portuguese brought new goods and a new sea-road to Europe; West African leaders controlled the land, the markets and the terms. That balance — Europeans on the coast, Africans in command of the shore — is where the story starts, before later centuries bent it.
