# Hands-on — Europeans on the Coast: Trade on African Terms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A stranger arrives at your market wanting to buy. Who sets the price — the stranger, or the people whose market it is? Who decides what may be sold, and where the stranger may go? Hold those questions. This lesson opens the age of the ships with the answers West Africa first gave.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: When Portuguese ships reached the Senegal river in 1442, West African leaders dealt with the Europeans from strength — the first coastal trade ran on African terms. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
