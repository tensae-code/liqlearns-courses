# Video script — After the Flag: Debt, Drought and the Integration Answer

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Small, indebted states looked to each other for the scale none had alone — regional integration was West Africa's answer to economic problems no single small state could solve.

The last lesson ended with the shadows: debt among them. This one follows the **economic problems** of the independent states to the answer their leaders built. The problems were structural: the new states were mostly **small** — small markets, small budgets — and **indebted**, owing money abroad while their export earnings swung with prices they did not control.

No single small state could bargain, build or borrow on equal terms with the giants of the world economy. So West Africans reasoned as traders do: **together, the region had the scale none of its states had alone**. Bigger shared markets, shared bargaining power, shared projects too large for one budget — this was the **regional-integration answer**.

Integration did not erase the problems; debt and drought did not read treaties. But it changed the question — from what one small state could do alone, to what West Africa could do as a region. The next lesson names the bodies built on that reasoning, at home and in the wider world.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
