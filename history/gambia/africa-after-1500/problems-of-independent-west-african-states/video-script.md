# Video script — Problems of Independent West African States

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Independence opened with schools, roads and clinics — the new West African states building fast — and then coups, debt and one-party rule shadowed the promise.

When the flags changed, the new **independent West African states** began with building. **Schools, roads and clinics** rose — the visible promise of independence: children in classrooms their grandparents never entered, roads joining regions the empires had kept apart, clinics where there had been none.

But the new states carried heavy inheritances: economies still shaped for export, borders drawn by the scramble, armies and administrations built by empires. And the promise began to be **shadowed** — by **coups**, as soldiers seized power from elected governments; by **debt**, as borrowing for development became a burden that ate the budgets; and by **one-party rule**, as leaders who had fought for freedom narrowed the freedom of others to organise against them.

The lesson is not that independence failed. It is that independence was the beginning of a harder problem: how to govern states whose shapes, economies and institutions were made by the very rule they had escaped. The next lesson follows one answer West Africans gave — turning toward each other.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
