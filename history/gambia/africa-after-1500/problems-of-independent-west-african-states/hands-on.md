# Hands-on — Problems of Independent West African States

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Picture a family finally getting its own house after generations in someone else's. The first year is joy and building — new rooms, new plans. What could shadow the years after: quarrels over who leads, money owed, promises too big for the purse? Hold both pictures — the building and the shadow. This lesson tells both.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Independence opened with schools, roads and clinics — the new West African states building fast — and then coups, debt and one-party rule shadowed the promise. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
