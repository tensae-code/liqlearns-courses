# Hands-on — Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine claiming a field by shouting from the road that it is yours. Would the village accept it? A claim means little until you actually occupy and run the place. European powers wrote exactly that rule for their scramble. Hold the rule. This lesson shows what it forced them to build.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Berlin Act's rule — a claim counts only if effectively occupied — turned paper claims into administrations, and colonial rule took shape as systems of governing the partitioned lands. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
