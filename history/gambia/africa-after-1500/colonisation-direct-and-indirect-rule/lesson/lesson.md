# Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule

## Do it first

Imagine claiming a field by shouting from the road that it is yours. Would the village accept it? A claim means little until you actually occupy and run the place. European powers wrote exactly that rule for their scramble. Hold the rule. This lesson shows what it forced them to build.

The scramble produced paper empires — flags and lines far beyond any real control. The **Berlin Act** answered with a hard rule: **a claim counts only if effectively occupied**. To hold a territory, a power had to show real occupation — officials, posts, administration on the ground.

That rule **turned paper claims into administrations**. Across West Africa the empires now had to govern what they had grabbed: collecting revenue, keeping order, running the machinery of rule in every district they claimed. Colonisation stopped being a map exercise and became a system of government imposed on the partitioned peoples.

But how, exactly, to govern? The empires answered differently — ruling through existing chiefs, or directly through their own officials — and the comparison of those **systems of rule** is the next lesson's work. Here, hold the hinge: Berlin's effective-occupation rule is what converted claims into colonies.
