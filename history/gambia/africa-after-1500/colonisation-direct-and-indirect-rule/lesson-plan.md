# Lesson plan — Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule

**Course:** History — Gambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Berlin Act's rule — a claim counts only if effectively occupied — turned paper claims into administrations, and colonial rule took shape as systems of governing the partitioned lands.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Berlin Act's rule — a claim counts only if effectively occupied — turned paper claims into administrations, and colonial rule took shape as systems of governing the partitioned lands.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: any claim the learner knows — to land, to a title, to a place in a team — that counted only once the claimant actually showed up and did the work.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in West Africa, as the book itself works.
