# Hands-on — Women and Authority in West African History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name the most powerful person in the oldest stories your family tells. Now ask a harder question: in the old days of your people's history, could a woman hold authority — real authority, over men as well as women? Many assume not. Hold your assumption loosely. This lesson, a chapter the book's authors added beyond the exam syllabus, tests it twice.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Women held real authority in West African history — spiritual and political power in the precolonial world, held at times over men; and in Ahebi Ugbabe, a woman who rose under colonial rule to become warrant chief and king, the only female king in British Africa. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
