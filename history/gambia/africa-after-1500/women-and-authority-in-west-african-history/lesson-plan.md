# Lesson plan — Women and Authority in West African History

**Course:** History — Gambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Women and Authority in West African History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Independence and the Wider Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Women held real authority in West African history — spiritual and political power in the precolonial world, held at times over men; and in Ahebi Ugbabe, a woman who rose under colonial rule to become warrant chief and king, the only female king in British Africa.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Independence and the Wider Story — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Female power in precolonial West Africa** — Long before colonial patriarchy, women hold spiritual and political authority, at times over men
2. **Ahebi Ugbabe, the female king of colonial Nigeria** — Ahebi Ugbabe rises to become warrant chief and king — the only female king in British Africa

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: African Unity: The Founding of the OAU — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Women held real authority in West African history — spiritual and political power in the precolonial world, held at times over men; and in Ahebi Ugbabe, a woman who rose under colonial rule to become warrant chief and king, the only female king in British Africa.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a woman of authority in the learner's own community — a market leader, a religious leader, an elder whose word settles disputes — and the learner saying what her authority rests on.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in West Africa, as the book itself works.
