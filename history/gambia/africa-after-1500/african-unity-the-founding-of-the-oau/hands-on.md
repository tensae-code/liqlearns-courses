# Hands-on — African Unity: The Founding of the OAU

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A village joins the district council — and also keeps its own village association for its own affairs. Two memberships, two purposes. Why would a country do the same — join the whole world's organisation and build a regional one too? Hold the two purposes apart. This lesson names the bodies and what each was for.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: After 1945 West Africa joined the world's bodies and built its own — the United Nations, then the OAU, later the AU, and ECOWAS with its peacekeepers: unity organised, at home and in the world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
