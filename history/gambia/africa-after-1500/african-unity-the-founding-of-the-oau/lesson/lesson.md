# African Unity: The Founding of the OAU

## Do it first

A village joins the district council — and also keeps its own village association for its own affairs. Two memberships, two purposes. Why would a country do the same — join the whole world's organisation and build a regional one too? Hold the two purposes apart. This lesson names the bodies and what each was for.

The integration answer of the last lesson took shape as organisations. After **1945**, the new and coming West African states entered the world's own body — the **United Nations** — taking seats among the nations, where the region's voices could be heard on the world's stage.

But West Africans also built **their own**. The **OAU** — the Organisation of African Unity — gathered the continent's states to defend their independence and speak together; in time it was succeeded and reshaped as the **AU**, the African Union. Nearer home, **ECOWAS** bound West Africa's states in one community — the integration answer with an address — and in the region's wars its **peacekeepers** marched, West Africans keeping peace among West Africans.

So the course's long story lands here: a region once partitioned by others' lines now drawing its own connections — member of the world, organised as a continent, united as a region. Unity, in this lesson, is not a slogan; it is machinery, built body by body.
