# Lesson plan — What Colonial Rule Did to Africa

**Course:** History — Gambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** What Colonial Rule Did to Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colonial rule worked through taxes, forced labour and export economies — and in working, it remade West African life: what people grew, how they worked, and what their land was for.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Colonial rule worked through taxes, forced labour and export economies — and in working, it remade West African life: what people grew, how they worked, and what their land was for.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a money demand the learner knows — school fees, a levy — that forces a family to sell labour or produce, and changes what the family grows or does.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in West Africa, as the book itself works.
