# Video script — What Colonial Rule Did to Africa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Colonial rule worked through taxes, forced labour and export economies — and in working, it remade West African life: what people grew, how they worked, and what their land was for.

Systems of rule are recipes; this lesson tastes the meal. Colonial rule in West Africa **worked** through three great levers, and each lever moved ordinary life.

The first lever was **taxes** — demanded in money. A farming family that had fed itself now had to find cash: by selling labour, or by growing crops for sale. The tax book quietly reorganised the village year around the administration's demands.

The second lever was **forced labour** — work commanded, not hired: roads, porterage, public works taken from people's time and strength by order of the administration and its chiefs. The third lever was the **export economy**: land and labour steered toward crops and minerals for shipment out — groundnuts, cocoa, palm produce, gold — while food for home took what was left.

Together the levers **remade West African life**: new crops and new hungers, men absent on labour or migration, chiefs turned into tax collectors, whole regions valued only for what could be exported from them. That remaking — not the flags — is what colonial rule did, and its shapes long outlasted the rule itself.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
