# Hands-on — What Colonial Rule Did to Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A tax can be a quiet order: pay in money, and suddenly everyone must earn money — must work for wages or grow what sells. Think about what a single money-tax would change in a farming village that mostly fed itself. Hold that chain of changes. This lesson follows it through a whole region.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Colonial rule worked through taxes, forced labour and export economies — and in working, it remade West African life: what people grew, how they worked, and what their land was for. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
