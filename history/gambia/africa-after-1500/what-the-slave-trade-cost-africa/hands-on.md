# Hands-on — What the Slave Trade Cost Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
When a whole economy bends around one demand, everything else twists: what people grow, how they live, whom they fear. Think of a town where one buyer takes everything and sets every rule. Now scale it to kingdoms and centuries. Hold that picture. This lesson counts the cost at home, among the people who were not shipped away.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The slave trade bent West African kingdoms, farms and families around the demand for captives — its cost was paid in politics, in farming and in daily life. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
