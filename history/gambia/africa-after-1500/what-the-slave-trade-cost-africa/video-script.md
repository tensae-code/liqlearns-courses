# Video script — What the Slave Trade Cost Africa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The slave trade bent West African kingdoms, farms and families around the demand for captives — its cost was paid in politics, in farming and in daily life.

The last lesson followed the ships. This one stays on shore, with the people who remained — for the trade's deepest damage was done in West Africa itself, as life bent around the **demand for captives**.

In **politics**, kingdoms were warped by the trade: wars fought to take captives, rulers grown rich and armed by selling people, neighbours turned into hunting grounds. The gun cycle of the last lesson lived here — power measured in guns and captives, and peace made expensive.

In **farming**, the cost fell on the fields: people seized in planting or harvest seasons, villages abandoned to raiders, labour drained away in its strongest years. A land can lose its future farmers faster than it loses its people.

And in **daily life**, the cost was fear woven into the ordinary — journeys made in company, settlements moved to safer ground, families broken by a single raid and never rejoined. The trade ended in 1866; the bending it left in kingdoms, farms and families outlasted the ships.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
