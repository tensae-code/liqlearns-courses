# Company Rule, Direct Rule, Assimilation

## Do it first

Two head teachers run neighbouring schools. One rules through the prefects and class captains, leaving much as it was. The other replaces every captain with her own staff and requires everyone to speak and dress exactly as at her own old school. Same region, opposite recipes. Hold both schools in mind. This lesson compares two empires the same way.

The empires had to govern, and they chose **opposite recipes**. Britain's answer was **indirect rule**: govern through the existing chiefs and emirs, who kept their thrones and courts but answered to British officials above them. The system was cheap, used the authority people already knew — and froze that authority under foreign command.

France's answer began as **direct rule**: French officials governing directly, with African chiefs reduced to the administration's messengers. Behind it stood **assimilation** — the idea that the colonies' people could, in principle, become French: French language, French law, French ways, with a narrow door to citizenship for the few who fully assimilated.

The comparison is the lesson: **the same region, ruled by opposite recipes** — one empire working through thrones it did not create, the other reaching past them to remake its subjects. And the people who **lived the difference** were West Africans: a farmer's taxes, a child's school, a chief's power and a family's future all depended on which side of an imperial border the scramble had placed them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Britain's indirect rule and France's direct rule and assimilation were opposite recipes for ruling the same region — ruling through chiefs on one side, ruling through French officials to make Frenchmen on the other — and West Africans lived the difference. Give one real example: two neighbouring schools, offices or local governments the learner knows that run the same kind of place by opposite recipes — through local leaders, or directly from the centre, and name the lesson's key words: indirect rule, direct rule, assimilation, British and French systems, chiefs. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
