# Hands-on — Company Rule, Direct Rule, Assimilation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two head teachers run neighbouring schools. One rules through the prefects and class captains, leaving much as it was. The other replaces every captain with her own staff and requires everyone to speak and dress exactly as at her own old school. Same region, opposite recipes. Hold both schools in mind. This lesson compares two empires the same way.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Britain's indirect rule and France's direct rule and assimilation were opposite recipes for ruling the same region — ruling through chiefs on one side, ruling through French officials to make Frenchmen on the other — and West Africans lived the difference. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
