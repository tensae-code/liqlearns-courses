# Hands-on — Colonisation: The Partition and Fashoda

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A line on a map in a faraway capital is only paper — until someone walks it, plants flags along it and makes people obey it. And what happens when two empires' lines, drawn from opposite directions, meet in the middle? Hold that meeting in your mind. This lesson carries the partition from the conference table to the ground.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The partition was carried out on the ground — boundaries drawn and enforced by expeditions — until rival empires, pushing toward each other across the continent, nearly collided, as at Fashoda. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
