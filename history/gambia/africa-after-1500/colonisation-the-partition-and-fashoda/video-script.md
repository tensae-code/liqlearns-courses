# Video script — Colonisation: The Partition and Fashoda

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The partition was carried out on the ground — boundaries drawn and enforced by expeditions — until rival empires, pushing toward each other across the continent, nearly collided, as at Fashoda.

The scramble was declared at conference tables, but it was **carried out on the ground**. Expeditions marched inland, treaties were extracted, flags were planted, and **boundaries were drawn and enforced** — lines that cut through peoples and grazing lands, defended by the claiming power's soldiers and officials.

As the empires pushed, their lines of advance ran toward each other. Britain's ambitions ran one way across the continent, France's another — and where advancing columns met, the partition nearly turned into a war between the partitioners themselves. The famous meeting was **Fashoda**, where rival empires **nearly collided** over a post in the interior.

Fashoda's lesson is the partition's own logic exposed: the lines were drawn for the empires' rivalry with each other as much as for rule over Africans. The collision was avoided by diplomacy, the boundaries settled between the powers — and the peoples inside the lines were not consulted in either the drawing or the settling.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
