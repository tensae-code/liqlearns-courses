# Hands-on — The Environment in West African History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Every story in this course so far has starred people: traders, kings, missionaries, soldiers. But the rains decided harvests, fevers decided where armies could march, and rivers decided where towns could stand. List three ways weather or land has changed plans in your own life this year. Hold the list. This final lesson gives the environment its seat among the causes.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Climate, disease and landscape shaped West African states and trade as surely as kings did — the environment is not the stage of the region's history but one of its actors. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
