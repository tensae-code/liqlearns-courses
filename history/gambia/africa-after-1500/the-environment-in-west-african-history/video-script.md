# Video script — The Environment in West African History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Climate, disease and landscape shaped West African states and trade as surely as kings did — the environment is not the stage of the region's history but one of its actors.

The book closes with a chapter its authors added beyond the syllabus — a claim about all the chapters before it: **the environment is an actor in West African history**, not merely its scenery.

Consider **climate**: the rains and the dry seasons set the calendar of farming, the reach of the savanna and the forest, the very routes caravans could take and the months they could take them. Consider **disease**: fevers that guarded the coast against outsiders for centuries, sicknesses that emptied regions or broke armies and settlements. Consider **landscape**: rivers that carried trade, soils that fed cities, a desert that made the camel a ship.

None of this removes the people from the story — traders still chose, kings still decided, states still rose and fell. But they chose and decided **inside** a world of rain, fever and land that rewarded some choices and punished others. The historian who ignores the environment tells only half the causes.

So the course ends where its first lesson began — with what history is. History is the understanding of people in their world; and their world, this chapter insists, was always part of the explanation.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
