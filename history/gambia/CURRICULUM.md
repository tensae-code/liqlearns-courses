# History — Gambia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Gambia inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Gambia's book in hand is one — the
Grade 10 WASSCE *History Textbook: West African Senior School
Certificate Examination* student book (WASSCE History Textbook
project, King's College London / ASAUK, AHRC-funded, 2018) —
a free, scholar-written regional textbook built for the WAEC
exam shared by the five Anglophone West African states, telling
West Africa and the wider world from earliest times to 2000 in
twelve chapters. So the ladder follows the book's own chapter
order, grouped the way the region's story divides: the
historian's craft first (Level 1, Ch. 1), then trade and faith
building the early region (Level 2, Ch. 2–3), then contact,
slavery and colonial rule (Level 3, Ch. 4–8), and last
independence and the wider story the book's authors added
(Level 4, Ch. 9–12). The ladder stops where the book in hand
stops, not because Gambia teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 10 WASSCE History student book — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: How History Is Made** — the book's Ch. 1:
   the historian's goal is understanding people, not
   collecting facts; history-writing itself has a history,
   and African history fought its way into the academy; a
   written history is built by cross-examining oral
   tradition, written records and material evidence; and
   where no writing exists, archaeologists read stones and
   bones. 4 lessons, in one title: `first-steps/`
   (4 lessons).

2. **Trade and Faith: West Africa to 1500** — the book's
   Ch. 2–3: long before Islam, camels, salt and gold
   organise the trans-Saharan trade; the trade grows towns,
   states and scholarship across the savanna; Islam crosses
   the Sahara with traders and scholars and takes root in
   courts and towns; and the new faith reshapes law,
   learning and kingship without erasing the older ways.
   4 lessons, in two titles: `africa-to-1500/` (3 lessons)
   and `ancient-world/` (1 lesson).

3. **Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule** — the book's
   Ch. 4–8: Portuguese ships reach the Senegal river in
   1442 and trade runs on African terms; the trans-Atlantic
   slave trade runs 1442–1866 on the gun cycle, and its
   cost is paid at home in politics, farming and daily
   life; missions preach the three Cs; the scramble flips
   the map in forty years, the partition is enforced on
   the ground to Fashoda, Berlin's effective-occupation
   rule turns claims into administrations, Britain's
   indirect rule and France's direct rule and assimilation
   are compared, and taxes, forced labour and export
   economies remake West African life. 9 lessons, in one
   title: `africa-after-1500/` (9 lessons).

4. **Independence and the Wider Story** — the book's
   Ch. 9–12: independence opens with schools, roads and
   clinics, then coups, debt and one-party rule shadow the
   promise; small indebted states answer with regional
   integration; after 1945 the region joins the UN and
   builds the OAU/AU and ECOWAS; and the two chapters the
   authors added beyond the syllabus close the course —
   women and authority (female power in the precolonial
   world, and Ahebi Ugbabe, the female king), and the
   environment as an actor in the region's history.
   6 lessons, in one title: `africa-after-1500/`
   (6 lessons; one carries 2 sub lessons).

**Totals: 22 lessons** (Level 1: 4 · Level 2: 4 · Level 3: 9
· Level 4: 6 — 23 placements, all from one student book).
