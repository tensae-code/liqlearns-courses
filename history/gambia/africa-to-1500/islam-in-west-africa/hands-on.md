# Hands-on — Islam in West Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
When something new enters a community — a new language, a new tool, a new belief — it rarely wipes the slate clean. More often the new and the old negotiate. Think of one example you know. Hold it. This lesson tells how a whole region did that with a new faith.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Islam reshaped West African law, learning and kingship without erasing the older ways — the new faith and the old customs lived side by side and changed each other. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
