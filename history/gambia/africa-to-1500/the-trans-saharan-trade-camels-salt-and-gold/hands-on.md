# Hands-on — The Trans-Saharan Trade: Camels, Salt and Gold

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Hold a pinch of salt. In your kitchen it costs almost nothing. Now imagine carrying it across a desert so wide that men died crossing it — and at the far side, people paying for it almost in gold. Why would salt be worth that journey, and gold worth sending back? Hold the two goods in your mind. This lesson organises a whole trade around them.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Long before Islam, trade routes already linked West Africa to the Mediterranean, and the traffic across the Sahara was organised by camels, salt and gold — the desert crossed like a sea, by caravan. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
