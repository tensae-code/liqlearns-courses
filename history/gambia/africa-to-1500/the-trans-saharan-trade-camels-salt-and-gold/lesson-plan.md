# Lesson plan — The Trans-Saharan Trade: Camels, Salt and Gold

**Course:** History — Gambia · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** The Trans-Saharan Trade: Camels, Salt and Gold
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Trade and Faith: West Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Long before Islam, trade routes already linked West Africa to the Mediterranean, and the traffic across the Sahara was organised by camels, salt and gold — the desert crossed like a sea, by caravan.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Trade and Faith: West Africa to 1500 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Long before Islam, trade routes already linked West Africa to the Mediterranean, and the traffic across the Sahara was organised by camels, salt and gold — the desert crossed like a sea, by caravan.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a lorry route or market chain the learner knows, where goods scarce in one place are carried to where they are precious — and the staging towns in between.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in West Africa, as the book itself works.
