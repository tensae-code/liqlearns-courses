# The Trans-Saharan Trade: Camels, Salt and Gold

## Do it first

Hold a pinch of salt. In your kitchen it costs almost nothing. Now imagine carrying it across a desert so wide that men died crossing it — and at the far side, people paying for it almost in gold. Why would salt be worth that journey, and gold worth sending back? Hold the two goods in your mind. This lesson organises a whole trade around them.

The Sahara looks like an ending — sand to the horizon. West Africa's traders treated it as a **sea**: dangerous, crossable, with ports on both shores and a vessel built for the crossing. That vessel was the **camel**, which could walk for days without water, carrying heavy loads where no cart could go.

The trade was old — **long before Islam**, routes already linked West Africa to the Mediterranean world. What organised the traffic was a pair of goods that each shore lacked. From the desert mines of the north came **salt**, which the south needed to live and to preserve food, in a land where salt was scarce. From the goldfields of the south came **gold**, which the north and the Mediterranean craved.

So caravans moved in both directions — salt south, gold north — with staging towns where loads were rested, taxed, traded and reloaded. To organise all this took knowledge: of wells, seasons, guides and markets. The trans-Saharan trade was not wandering; it was a system.

Hold the system's shape, because everything in the next lessons grows from it: towns, states and scholarship all rise along these routes.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Long before Islam, trade routes already linked West Africa to the Mediterranean, and the traffic across the Sahara was organised by camels, salt and gold — the desert crossed like a sea, by caravan. Give one real example: a lorry route or market chain the learner knows, where goods scarce in one place are carried to where they are precious — and the staging towns in between, and name the lesson's key words: trans-Saharan trade, camel, caravan, salt, gold. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
