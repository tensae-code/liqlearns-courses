# Hands-on — How Africa's Regions Traded with Each Other

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find the biggest market you know. Ask: why is it there, and not somewhere else? Markets are not accidents — they grow where roads meet and goods change hands. Now scale that up to a whole region crossed by caravan roads. Hold the picture. This lesson shows what the trade built.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The trans-Saharan trade grew towns, states and scholarship across the savanna — where caravans stopped, markets rose; where markets rose, rulers taxed and protected them, and learning travelled the same roads as goods. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
