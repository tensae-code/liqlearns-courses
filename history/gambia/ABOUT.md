# About the History — Gambia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 10 WASSCE History student book. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Gambia inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **four levels**, following the
book's own twelve chapters in order, as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Made.** The
   historian's goal (understanding people, not collecting
   facts); how history-writing itself has changed, and
   African history's fight into the academy; the three
   sources cross-examined; archaeology and the deep past.
2. **Level 2 — Trade and Faith: West Africa to 1500.** The
   trans-Saharan trade (camels, salt and gold) and its
   effects — towns, states and scholarship; Islam's spread
   with traders and scholars, and its effects on law,
   learning and kingship.
3. **Level 3 — Contact, Slavery and Colonial Rule.**
   European contact on African terms from 1442; the
   trans-Atlantic slave trade, its gun cycle and its cost
   at home; the missions and the three Cs; the scramble,
   the partition on the ground, Berlin's rule, the systems
   of rule compared, and colonial rule's working and
   effects.
4. **Level 4 — Independence and the Wider Story.** The
   problems of the independent states; the integration
   answer; the UN, OAU/AU and ECOWAS; and the authors' two
   added chapters — women and authority, and the
   environment.

All twenty-two lessons stand on a **student book** and
count in the acceptance reckoning below.

  towns, states and scholarship grow on the roads, and a
  new faith take root beside the older ways. Then the
  ships come: first trade on African terms, then the long

  That is West Africa's book, written by its own
  historians, and it is yours."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**History Textbook: West African Senior School Certificate
Examination (student book)** — A free, scholar-written
regional textbook built for the WAEC exam shared by the
five Anglophone West African states — West Africa's whole
story told by West African and diaspora historians, with
two chapters (women and authority; the environment) the
authors added beyond the syllabus, and Gambian historian
Hassoum Ceesay on the author team. *(Student book.)*
