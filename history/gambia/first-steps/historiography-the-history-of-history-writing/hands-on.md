# Hands-on — Historiography: The History of History-Writing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask two older people about the same event from long ago — a celebration, a dispute, a famous year. Notice how their tellings differ: each keeps different parts. Now imagine whole ages doing the same with the past of nations. Hold that thought. This lesson is about how the writing of history itself changes.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History-writing itself has a history: each age rewrites the past in its own way, and African history had to fight its way into the academy before scholars took it seriously. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
