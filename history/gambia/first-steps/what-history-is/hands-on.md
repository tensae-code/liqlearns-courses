# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one event from your own family's past — a move, a marriage, a hard year. Write down its date if you know it. Now ask: is the date enough? Or do you really want to know what the people felt, feared and hoped, and why they did what they did? Hold that difference. This lesson is about it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The historian's goal is not collecting facts but understanding the people who lived the past — a history is written so that the people in it, their choices and their world, become understandable. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
