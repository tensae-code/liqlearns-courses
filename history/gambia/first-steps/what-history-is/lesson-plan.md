# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Gambia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Made (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The historian's goal is not collecting facts but understanding the people who lived the past — a history is written so that the people in it, their choices and their world, become understandable.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Made — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course. Nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The historian's goal is not collecting facts but understanding the people who lived the past — a history is written so that the people in it, their choices and their world, become understandable.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: an elder in their family explaining not just when the family came to its town, but why — what they were leaving, what they hoped to find.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in West Africa, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks history is memorising dates → dates are the skeleton of the story, but understanding the people is its flesh; a skeleton alone is not a person.
- Go back to the one thing: The historian's goal is not collecting facts but understanding the people who lived the past — a history is written so that the people in it, their choices and their world, become understandable. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
