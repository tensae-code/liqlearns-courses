# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine three witnesses to one event: a grandmother who was told it by her grandmother, a letter written at the time, and an old pot dug up where it happened. Each one knows something the others do not — and each one can be wrong. How would you decide what really happened? Hold your method. This lesson teaches the historian's.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written history is built by cross-examining three kinds of sources — oral tradition, written records and material evidence — the griots' words, documents and dug-up objects checked against each other before a history is written. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
