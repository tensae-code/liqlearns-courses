# From Evidence to a Written History — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — A written history is built by cross-examining three kinds of sources — oral tradition, written records and material evidence — the griots' words, documents and dug-up objects checked against each other before a history is written. — in their own words, give one real example from their own life, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new home, school or street situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
