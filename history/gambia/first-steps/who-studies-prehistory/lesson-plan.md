# Lesson plan — Who Studies Prehistory

**Course:** History — Gambia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Who Studies Prehistory
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Made (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Where no writing exists, the deep past is studied by archaeologists, who read stones and bones — prehistory is recovered from things, not texts.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Made — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Where no writing exists, the deep past is studied by archaeologists, who read stones and bones — prehistory is recovered from things, not texts.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: an old tool, coin or pot found in the ground near their home — and the learner saying what an archaeologist could read from where and how it was found.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks nothing can be known about times before writing → archaeologists read stones, bones and buried remains; the position of a find is itself evidence.
- Go back to the one thing: Where no writing exists, the deep past is studied by archaeologists, who read stones and bones — prehistory is recovered from things, not texts. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
