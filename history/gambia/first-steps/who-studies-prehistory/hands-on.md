# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Close your eyes and go back — past your grandparents, past the oldest story anyone in your family can tell, past writing itself. People lived, hunted, cooked and buried their dead for ages before anyone wrote a word. How could we know anything about them at all? Hold the question. This lesson names the people who answer it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Where no writing exists, the deep past is studied by archaeologists, who read stones and bones — prehistory is recovered from things, not texts. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
