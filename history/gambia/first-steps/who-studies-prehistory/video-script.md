# Video script — Who Studies Prehistory

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Where no writing exists, the deep past is studied by archaeologists, who read stones and bones — prehistory is recovered from things, not texts.

Most of the human past has no documents. Writing is a late invention; for the long ages before it — **prehistory** — there are no letters, no chronicles, no griots' genealogies reaching that far. If the past were only what is written, almost everything human would be darkness.

Into that darkness goes the **archaeologist**. Where the historian reads texts, the archaeologist reads the ground: **stones** shaped into tools, **bones** of animals eaten and people buried, ash from hearths, broken pots, the floors of vanished houses. Each find is a sentence in a language of things.

Reading that language takes patience and method. Where a thing lay, what lay beside it, how deep it rested — the position is part of the evidence, so digging is recording, not treasure-hunting. From such readings, archaeologists rebuild how the first peoples lived, moved and changed.

So the course's first steps end with a widening: history's evidence is bigger than writing. Stones and bones, cross-examined like any other source, let the deep past speak.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in West Africa.
