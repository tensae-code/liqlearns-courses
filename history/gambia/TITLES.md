# History — Gambia course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Gambia's
own — built from Gambia's Grade 10 WASSCE *History
Textbook: West African Senior School Certificate
Examination* student book (WASSCE History Textbook
project, King's College London / ASAUK, AHRC-funded,
English). Inside the course the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: the titles below sit inside
Levels 1–4, in path order — what the learner proves,
step by step. The grade the book comes from lives only
in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: West Africa and the
wider world from earliest times to 2000, told in twelve
chapters by West African and diaspora historians — the
historian's craft first, then the region built by trade
and faith, then contact, slavery and colonial rule, and
last independence, unity, and the two chapters the
authors added beyond the syllabus (women and authority;
the environment). That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in
higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — first steps: how history is made** (WASSCE
History, Ch. 1), in `first-steps/`:

- What History Is (the historian's goal is understanding
  the people who lived the past, not collecting facts) ·
  Historiography: The History of History-Writing (each
  age rewrites the past; African history fights its way
  into the academy) · From Evidence to a Written History
  (oral tradition, written records and material evidence
  cross-examined) · Who Studies Prehistory
  (archaeologists read stones and bones where no writing
  exists).

**Level 2 — trade and faith: West Africa to 1500**
(Ch. 2–3), in `africa-to-1500/` and `ancient-world/`:

- `africa-to-1500/` — The Trans-Saharan Trade: Camels,
  Salt and Gold (routes link West Africa to the
  Mediterranean long before Islam) · How Africa's
  Regions Traded with Each Other (the trade grows towns,
  states and scholarship across the savanna) · Islam in
  West Africa (the faith reshapes law, learning and
  kingship without erasing the older ways).
- `ancient-world/` — The Rise and Expansion of Islam
  (Islam crosses the Sahara with traders and scholars
  and takes root in courts and towns).

**Level 3 — contact, slavery and colonial rule**
(Ch. 4–8), in `africa-after-1500/`:

- Europeans on the Coast: Trade on African Terms
  (Portuguese ships reach the Senegal river in 1442;
  African leaders deal from strength) · The Atlantic
  Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle (captives
  taken in 1442 begin a traffic ending only in 1866) ·
  What the Slave Trade Cost Africa (kingdoms, farms and
  families bent around the demand for captives) ·
  Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior (missions
  arrive with the anti-slavery conscience, preaching the
  three Cs) · The Scramble and Berlin 1884–85 (in forty
  years the map flips; by 1910 only Liberia and Ethiopia
  escape) · Colonisation: The Partition and Fashoda
  (boundaries enforced on the ground until rival empires
  nearly collide) · Colonisation: Direct and Indirect
  Rule (Berlin's rule — a claim counts only if
  effectively occupied) · Company Rule, Direct Rule,
  Assimilation (Britain's indirect rule against France's
  direct rule and assimilation) · What Colonial Rule Did
  to Africa (taxes, forced labour and export economies
  remake West African life).

**Level 4 — independence and the wider story**
(Ch. 9–12), in `africa-after-1500/`:

- Problems of Independent West African States (schools,
  roads and clinics first — then coups, debt and
  one-party rule) · After the Flag: Debt, Drought and
  the Integration Answer (small indebted states look to
  each other for scale) · African Unity: The Founding of
  the OAU (UN, OAU/AU and ECOWAS with its peacekeepers) ·
  Women and Authority in West African History (2 sub
  lessons — female power in precolonial West Africa;
  Ahebi Ugbabe, the female king of colonial Nigeria) ·
  The Environment in West African History (climate,
  disease and landscape shape states and trade as surely
  as kings do).
