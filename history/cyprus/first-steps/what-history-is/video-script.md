# Video script — What History Is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A child becomes a historian by asking questions of traces — starting with their own name, family and life — and every age is asked the same four questions: how and why do we move, how do we spend our day, what do we think, and how do we organise ourselves; across every age some things change and some stay the same.

Look at the map. Here is Cyprus, an island in the eastern Mediterranean Sea.

Teachers learn that a child becomes a historian by asking questions of traces, not by receiving a finished past.

A child starts history with their own name, family and life, discovering they themselves have a past.

What people believe explains what they build, bury and draw, in every period.

Each age thinks its own thoughts and leaves its own marks, from cave images to writing to today's screens.

The child guides Hector the archaeologist and Aphrodite recap the journey from stone to bronze and promise a voyage through Cyprus's ancient centuries.

From syllabic inscriptions to Hellenistic letters, Cypriots keep finding new ways to say what they think.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
