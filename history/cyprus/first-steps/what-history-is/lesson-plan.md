# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Becoming a Historian: Your Own Past and the Evidence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a child becomes a historian by asking questions of traces — starting with their own name, family and life — and every age is asked the same four questions: how and why do we move, how do we spend our day, what do we think, and how do we organise ourselves; across every age some things change and some stay the same.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Becoming a Historian: Your Own Past and the Evidence (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theoretical framework: developing historical literacy** — Teachers learn that a child becomes a historian by asking questions of traces, not by receiving a finished past.
2. **Intro unit proposal 1: Who am I?** — A child starts history with their own name, family and life, discovering they themselves have a past.
3. **Overviews proposal 3: What do we think? — ideas and beliefs framework** — What people believe explains what they build, bury and draw, in every period.
4. **Cards 7–8, Thinking strand: what people think; how they express their ideas** — Each age thinks its own thoughts and leaves its own marks, from cave images to writing to today's screens.
5. **Introduction: what we learned in Grade 3; what we will learn this year** — The child guides Hector the archaeologist and Aphrodite recap the journey from stone to bronze and promise a voyage through Cyprus's ancient centuries.
6. **Cards 7–8, Thinking strand: what people think; how they express their ideas** — From syllabic inscriptions to Hellenistic letters, Cypriots keep finding new ways to say what they think.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Cyprus's books themselves work, asking the same enquiry questions of every age.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: a child becomes a historian by asking questions of traces — starting with their own name, family and life — and every age is asked the same four questions: how and why do we move, how do we spend our day, what do we think, and how do we organise ourselves; across every age some things change and some stay the same. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
