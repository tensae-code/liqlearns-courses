# Lesson plan — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Becoming a Historian: Your Own Past and the Evidence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every group must decide who leads, who belongs and how disputes end — from a small band to a village to a city-kingdom: bands become villages become cities, leaders appear where groups grow, and groups trade or fight across every age.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Becoming a Historian: Your Own Past and the Evidence (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Overviews proposal 4: How do we organise ourselves? — political/social organisation framework** — Every group must decide who leads, who belongs and how disputes end, from band to city.
2. **Cards 9–11, Organisation strand: how big groups are; who their leaders are; how groups relate to each other** — Bands become villages become cities, leaders appear where groups grow, and groups trade or fight across every age.
3. **Cards 9–11, Organisation strand: how big groups are; who their leaders are; how groups relate** — City-kingdoms give way to one Ptolemaic province, kings to governors, on one comparison grid.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: every group must decide who leads, who belongs and how disputes end — from a small band to a village to a city-kingdom: bands become villages become cities, leaders appear where groups grow, and groups trade or fight across every age.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Cyprus story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Cyprus's books themselves work, asking the same enquiry questions of every age.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of three settlements side by side — a small band's camp, a farming village and a walled city with a king's palace → the student points to the people and the place and says who is who and where it happens.
   - ADD GIF: a short GIF of a small camp growing house by house into a village and then a walled city on an animated map → the student watches and says what changed.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing and gives a real example (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: every group must decide who leads, who belongs and how disputes end — from a small band to a village to a city-kingdom: bands become villages become cities, leaders appear where groups grow, and groups trade or fight across every age. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
