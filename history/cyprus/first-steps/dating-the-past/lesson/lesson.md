# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: take a blank page and write the four questions Cyprus's books ask of every age — How and why do we move? How do we spend our day? What do we think? How do we organise ourselves? Keep the page beside you. This lesson answers them for its own story: past, present and future are placed on one line: a single life changes over time, and the historian's master question — what changes and what stays the same — is asked of every age on one comparison grid, from the Palaeolithic to today and even to an imagined future.

## Sub lesson 1 — Intro unit proposal 3: Who was I? Who will I be?

Past, present and future selves are placed on one line, and the child sees a single life changing over time.

That is the turn of this part. Hold it against the four questions on your page: which of them does this part answer — movement, the day, thought, or organisation — and what changed here, and what stayed the same?

## Sub lesson 2 — Introduction: what changes and what stays the same

Children learn the year's master question — across every age, some things change and some stay the same, and spotting both is the historian's job.

## Sub lesson 3 — Introduction: what changes and what stays the same
