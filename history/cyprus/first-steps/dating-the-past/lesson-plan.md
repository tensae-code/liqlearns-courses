# Lesson plan — Dating the Past

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Becoming a Historian: Your Own Past and the Evidence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** past, present and future are placed on one line: a single life changes over time, and the historian's master question — what changes and what stays the same — is asked of every age on one comparison grid, from the Palaeolithic to today and even to an imagined future.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Becoming a Historian: Your Own Past and the Evidence (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Intro unit proposal 3: Who was I? Who will I be?** — Past, present and future selves are placed on one line, and the child sees a single life changing over time.
2. **Introduction: what changes and what stays the same** — Children learn the year's master question — across every age, some things change and some stay the same, and spotting both is the historian's job.
3. **Introduction: what changes and what stays the same** — The grid's columns are whole ages of Cyprus, and the child's task is to watch one island change across a thousand years and into an imagined future.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Cyprus's books themselves work, asking the same enquiry questions of every age.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: past, present and future are placed on one line: a single life changes over time, and the historian's master question — what changes and what stays the same — is asked of every age on one comparison grid, from the Palaeolithic to today and even to an imagined future. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
