# Hands-on — Cyprus Bronze Age: Copper and the First Cities

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: copper and bronze change Cyprus: the island's copper becomes the treasure outsiders sail for, ships and trade routes tie Cyprus to Egypt and the Levant, coastal cities rise where the boats land, and cities need rulers, laws, gods and sanctuaries of their own. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a Bronze Age harbour in Cyprus, with a ship loading copper ingots shaped like ox-hides), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see something that answers one of this course's four enquiry questions — how and why do we move? how do we spend our day? what do we think? how do we organise ourselves? — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
