# Lesson plan — Cyprus Bronze Age: Copper and the First Cities

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Cyprus Bronze Age: Copper and the First Cities
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Stone, Farming and Bronze: Cyprus's First Ages (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** copper and bronze change Cyprus: the island's copper becomes the treasure outsiders sail for, ships and trade routes tie Cyprus to Egypt and the Levant, coastal cities rise where the boats land, and cities need rulers, laws, gods and sanctuaries of their own.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Stone, Farming and Bronze: Cyprus's First Ages (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bronze Age proposal 1: A new material changes people's lives** — Copper and bronze reach Cyprus, and the island's copper becomes the treasure outsiders sail for.
2. **Bronze Age proposals 2–3: Daily life, food and dress in the Bronze Age** — Richer houses, new foods and finer clothes mark a world of farmers, smiths and traders.
3. **Bronze Age proposals 4–6: Why and how Bronze Age people moved, and where they built cities** — Ships and trade routes tie Cyprus to Egypt and the Levant, and coastal cities rise where the boats land.
4. **Bronze Age proposals 8–9: What Bronze Age people thought and how they expressed it** — Gods, sanctuaries, figurines and early signs show belief and communication growing more elaborate.
5. **Bronze Age proposals 10–11: How Bronze Age people organised themselves; relations between groups** — Cities need rulers and laws, and Cyprus's kingdoms deal with great powers abroad as partners and prizes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

- Go back to the one thing: copper and bronze change Cyprus: the island's copper becomes the treasure outsiders sail for, ships and trade routes tie Cyprus to Egypt and the Levant, coastal cities rise where the boats land, and cities need rulers, laws, gods and sanctuaries of their own.
