# Lesson plan — Cyprus Archaic Age: Kingdoms and Conquerors

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Cyprus Archaic Age: Kingdoms and Conquerors
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The City-Kingdoms: Geometric and Archaic Cyprus (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in the Archaic age (750–475 BC) Cyprus is split into kingdoms, each with its walled city, king, farmers and craftspeople — and Assyrians, Egyptians and Persians take the island in turn, so Cypriot kings pay tribute to survive each new master.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The City-Kingdoms: Geometric and Archaic Cyprus (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2 ch. 5: A Cypriot kingdom — where the cities were built; how ordinary people lived (750–475 BC)** — Cyprus is split into kingdoms, each with its walled city, king, farmers and craftspeople living under foreign shadows.
2. **Unit 2 ch. 6: Conquerors in Cyprus** — Assyrians, Egyptians and Persians take the island in turn, and Cypriot kings pay tribute to survive each new master.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: in the Archaic age (750–475 BC) Cyprus is split into kingdoms, each with its walled city, king, farmers and craftspeople — and Assyrians, Egyptians and Persians take the island in turn, so Cypriot kings pay tribute to survive each new master.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Cyprus story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Cyprus's books themselves work, asking the same enquiry questions of every age.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: in the Archaic age (750–475 BC) Cyprus is split into kingdoms, each with its walled city, king, farmers and craftspeople — and Assyrians, Egyptians and Persians take the island in turn, so Cypriot kings pay tribute to survive each new master. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
