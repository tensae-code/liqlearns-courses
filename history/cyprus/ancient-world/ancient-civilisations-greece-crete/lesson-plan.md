# Lesson plan — Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Classical Cyprus, Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Cyprus lives inside the Greek world in its own way: Cypriots write Greek in their own syllabic script, their artists mix Greek, Phoenician and eastern styles in sculpture and pottery, gymnasia school the island's youth in Greek letters, and Zeno of Kition leaves Cyprus to found Stoicism in Athens.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Classical Cyprus, Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2 ch. 8: How did the Cypriots write?** — Cypriots write Greek in their own syllabic script, a local invention that outlasts alphabets around it.
2. **Unit 2 ch. 9: The arts in the Cypro-Archaic age — sculpture, pottery, plastic art** — Stone votaries, painted pots and terracottas show Cypriot artists mixing Greek, Phoenician and eastern styles.
3. **Unit 3 ch. 18: The arts in the Cypro-Classical age — sculpture, pottery, miniature craft** — Athenian influence refines Cypriot sculpture and pottery while local workshops keep their own hand.
4. **Unit 4 ch. 27: Letters — gymnasia; Zeno of Kition** — Gymnasia school the island's youth in Greek letters, and Zeno of Kition leaves Cyprus to found Stoicism in Athens.
5. **Cards 4–6, Daily-life strand: what people eat and wear; what technology they use; how they spend their day** — Geometric farmers, classical symposiasts and Hellenistic townsfolk eat, dress and work differently on the same island.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

- Go back to the one thing: Cyprus lives inside the Greek world in its own way: Cypriots write Greek in their own syllabic script, their artists mix Greek, Phoenician and eastern styles in sculpture and pottery, gymnasia school the island's youth in Greek letters, and Zeno of Kition leaves Cyprus to found Stoicism in Athens.
