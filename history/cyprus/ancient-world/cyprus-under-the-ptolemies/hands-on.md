# Hands-on — Cyprus Under the Ptolemies

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Alexander's successors abolish Cyprus's kings, and Ptolemaic governors rule the island as one province of an Egyptian empire (325–30 BC): Cypriot copper, timber, wine and harbours feed Ptolemaic power — a merchant ship sunk off Kyrenia still tells the trade story — while Hellenistic Cypriots live in Greek-style cities, watch plays in stone theatres, and blend their old cults with Greek gods and ruler worship. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (the Kyrenia merchant ship under sail with a cargo of wine jars, a Cyprus harbour behind it), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see something that answers one of this course's four enquiry questions — how and why do we move? how do we spend our day? what do we think? how do we organise ourselves? — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
