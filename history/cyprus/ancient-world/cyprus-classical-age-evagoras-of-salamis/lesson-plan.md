# Lesson plan — Cyprus Classical Age: Evagoras of Salamis

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Cyprus Classical Age: Evagoras of Salamis
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Classical Cyprus, Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sea power decides Classical Cyprus's fate: the trireme, rowed by free crews, is the weapon every king must have, and Evagoras of Salamis rebuilds his city, courts Athens, then fights the Persian Great King for a decade before a negotiated peace leaves him king.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Classical Cyprus, Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 3 ch. 14: 'And a dead man wins' — the trireme, a powerful warship (475–325 BC)** — Sea power decides Cyprus's fate, and the trireme, rowed by free crews, becomes the weapon every king must have.
2. **Unit 3 ch. 15–16: Evagoras and his plans for Cyprus; Evagoras's war against the Persians** — Evagoras of Salamis rebuilds his city, courts Athens, then fights the Great King for a decade before a negotiated peace leaves him king.
3. **Unit 3 ch. 17: Masters of the seas** — Cypriot ships and sailors serve, and sometimes defy, the great powers contesting the eastern Mediterranean.
4. **Unit 3 ch. 19: Daily life — how the ancients dressed; symposia, the diet of the ancients** — Clothes, banquets and everyday food show a Greek-style city life lived Cypriot-fashion.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: sea power decides Classical Cyprus's fate: the trireme, rowed by free crews, is the weapon every king must have, and Evagoras of Salamis rebuilds his city, courts Athens, then fights the Persian Great King for a decade before a negotiated peace leaves him king. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
