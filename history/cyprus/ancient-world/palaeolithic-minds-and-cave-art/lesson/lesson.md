# Palaeolithic Minds and Cave Art

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: take a blank page and write the four questions Cyprus's books ask of every age — How and why do we move? How do we spend our day? What do we think? How do we organise ourselves? Keep the page beside you. This lesson answers them for its own story: Palaeolithic people thought deeply but wrote nothing: their burials, images and objects are the only voice they left, and that voice is read as evidence of what they believed and imagined.

Burials, images and objects are the only voice left by people who thought deeply but wrote nothing.

That is the whole story of this lesson, in one part. Hold it against the four questions on your page: which of them does it answer — movement, the day, thought, or organisation — and what changed here, and what stayed the same?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Palaeolithic people thought deeply but wrote nothing: their burials, images and objects are the only voice they left, and that voice is read as evidence of what they believed and imagined. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: cave art, burial. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
