# Lesson plan — From Stone Tools to Farming

**Course:** History — Cyprus · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From Stone Tools to Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Stone, Farming and Bronze: Cyprus's First Ages (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** people in every age move for food, safety and opportunity, and settling down changes everything: Palaeolithic hunter-gatherers follow game and seasons with nothing stored for tomorrow, then Neolithic farmers invent the working day of fields, animals and stored food, with polished stone, pottery and woven things as their new tools.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Stone, Farming and Bronze: Cyprus's First Ages (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Overviews proposal 1: How and why do we move? — settlement and migration framework** — People in every age move for food, safety and opportunity, and settling down changes everything that follows.
2. **Overviews proposal 2: How do we spend our day? — daily-life framework** — Work, food, clothes and shelter are the daily stage on which every period's story is played.
3. **Palaeolithic proposal 1: Why so much moving in the Palaeolithic?** — Hunter-gatherers follow game and seasons, so home is wherever food is tonight.
4. **Palaeolithic proposal 2: How and where did Palaeolithic people move?** — On foot and by water, small bands cross a landscape with no settled villages anywhere.
5. **Palaeolithic proposals 3–4: How Palaeolithic people spend their day** — The day is hunting, gathering, making stone tools and sheltering, with nothing stored for tomorrow.
6. **Palaeolithic proposals 7–8: Organisation of Palaeolithic groups and relations between groups** — Small bands cooperate, share and meet other bands without kings, writing or money.
7. **Palaeolithic proposal 9: Life from the Palaeolithic to today** — The four enquiry questions are asked one last time across all ages, and children judge what truly changed.
8. **Neolithic proposal 4: What technology did Neolithic people use?** — Polished stone, pottery and woven things replace the old toolkit as farming life demands new tools.
9. **Neolithic proposal 5: How Neolithic people spent their day** — Fields, animals and stored food fill the day of the first farmers, a working day invented for the first time.
10. **Neolithic proposals 8–9: Organisation of Neolithic groups and relations between groups** — Villages must share land, water and work, and meet neighbouring villages in trade or tension.
11. **Cards 1–3, Movement strand: why people move; how and where they move; where and how they build their dwelling** — Hunters move after food, farmers settle by their fields, and today's families move again, each age answering the same three questions differently.
12. **Cards 4–6, Daily-life strand: what people eat and wear; what technology they use; how they spend their day** — Gathered food becomes farmed food becomes shop food, and stone tools become bronze, then machines, on one comparison grid.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Cyprus's books themselves work, asking the same enquiry questions of every age.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: people in every age move for food, safety and opportunity, and settling down changes everything: Palaeolithic hunter-gatherers follow game and seasons with nothing stored for tomorrow, then Neolithic farmers invent the working day of fields, animals and stored food, with polished stone, pottery and woven things as their new tools. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
