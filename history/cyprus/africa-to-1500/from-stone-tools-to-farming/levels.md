# From Stone Tools to Farming — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Overviews proposal 1: How and why do we move? — settlement and migration framework
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: people in every age move for food, safety and opportunity, and settling down changes everything that follows — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Overviews proposal 2: How do we spend our day? — daily-life framework
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: work, food, clothes and shelter are the daily stage on which every period's story is played — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Palaeolithic proposal 1: Why so much moving in the Palaeolithic?
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: hunter-gatherers follow game and seasons, so home is wherever food is tonight — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Palaeolithic proposal 2: How and where did Palaeolithic people move?
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: on foot and by water, small bands cross a landscape with no settled villages anywhere — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Palaeolithic proposals 3–4: How Palaeolithic people spend their day
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the day is hunting, gathering, making stone tools and sheltering, with nothing stored for tomorrow — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Palaeolithic proposals 7–8: Organisation of Palaeolithic groups and relations between groups
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: small bands cooperate, share and meet other bands without kings, writing or money — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Palaeolithic proposal 9: Life from the Palaeolithic to today
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the four enquiry questions are asked one last time across all ages, and children judge what truly changed — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Neolithic proposal 4: What technology did Neolithic people use?
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: polished stone, pottery and woven things replace the old toolkit as farming life demands new tools — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Neolithic proposal 5: How Neolithic people spent their day
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: fields, animals and stored food fill the day of the first farmers, a working day invented for the first time — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Neolithic proposals 8–9: Organisation of Neolithic groups and relations between groups
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: villages must share land, water and work, and meet neighbouring villages in trade or tension — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 11 — Cards 1–3, Movement strand: why people move; how and where they move; where and how they build their dwelling
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: hunters move after food, farmers settle by their fields, and today's families move again, each age answering the same three questions differently — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 12 — Cards 4–6, Daily-life strand: what people eat and wear; what technology they use; how they spend their day
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: gathered food becomes farmed food becomes shop food, and stone tools become bronze, then machines, on one comparison grid — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
