# Video script — From Stone Tools to Farming

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
People in every age move for food, safety and opportunity, and settling down changes everything: Palaeolithic hunter-gatherers follow game and seasons with nothing stored for tomorrow, then Neolithic farmers invent the working day of fields, animals and stored food, with polished stone, pottery and woven things as their new tools.

Look at the map. Here is Cyprus, an island in the eastern Mediterranean Sea.

People in every age move for food, safety and opportunity, and settling down changes everything that follows.

Work, food, clothes and shelter are the daily stage on which every period's story is played.

Hunter-gatherers follow game and seasons, so home is wherever food is tonight.

On foot and by water, small bands cross a landscape with no settled villages anywhere.

The day is hunting, gathering, making stone tools and sheltering, with nothing stored for tomorrow.

Small bands cooperate, share and meet other bands without kings, writing or money.

The four enquiry questions are asked one last time across all ages, and children judge what truly changed.

Polished stone, pottery and woven things replace the old toolkit as farming life demands new tools.

Fields, animals and stored food fill the day of the first farmers, a working day invented for the first time.

Villages must share land, water and work, and meet neighbouring villages in trade or tension.

Hunters move after food, farmers settle by their fields, and today's families move again, each age answering the same three questions differently.

Gathered food becomes farmed food becomes shop food, and stone tools become bronze, then machines, on one comparison grid.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
