# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: people in every age move for food, safety and opportunity, and settling down changes everything: Palaeolithic hunter-gatherers follow game and seasons with nothing stored for tomorrow, then Neolithic farmers invent the working day of fields, animals and stored food, with polished stone, pottery and woven things as their new tools. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a Palaeolithic band walking after game on one side and a Neolithic family harvesting a field beside their village on the other), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see something that answers one of this course's four enquiry questions — how and why do we move? how do we spend our day? what do we think? how do we organise ourselves? — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
