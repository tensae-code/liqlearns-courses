# About the History — Cyprus course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Cyprus's own
Ministry of Education and Culture / Pedagogical Institute of
Cyprus History books for primary school. The one exception is
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Cyprus inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **four levels**:

1. **Level 1 — Becoming a Historian: Your Own Past and the
   Evidence.** What history is, evidence and graded certainty,
   history found in your own name, family, class and school,
   dating the past, the archaeologists, and how groups
   organise themselves.
2. **Level 2 — Stone, Farming and Bronze: Cyprus's First
   Ages.** From stone tools to farming, Palaeolithic minds,
   Choirokoitia and the first villages, Neolithic beliefs,
   Bronze Age copper and the first cities, and myth tested
   against history.
3. **Level 3 — The City-Kingdoms: Geometric and Archaic
   Cyprus.** The founding of the city-kingdoms, the
   Phoenicians in Cyprus, kingdoms and conquerors, and
   Salamis and King Onesilos.
4. **Level 4 — Classical Cyprus, Alexander, the Ptolemies
   and Rome.** Cyprus in the Greek world, Evagoras of
   Salamis, worship in Classical Cyprus, Alexander the Great
   and Cyprus, Cyprus under the Ptolemies, and Rome's
   annexation.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Becoming a historian:* "First, become a historian yourself:
  question a photograph like a witness, trace your own name,
  and put your past, present and future on one line."
- *The first ages:* "Then ask the same four questions of the
  deep past: hunters who moved after food, farmers who
  settled at Choirokoitia, and a Bronze Age island whose
  copper the whole sea sailed for."
- *The city-kingdoms:* "Then Cyprus fills with kingdoms:
  settlers found walled cities, Phoenicians build temples,
  great empires take tribute in turn, and Onesilos of Salamis

- *The wider world:* "Last, the island inside bigger worlds:
  Evagoras and his triremes, Alexander's fleets, three
  centuries under the Ptolemies — and Rome arriving in
  58 BC."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**The Grade 3 proposals and cards** — History is built
through four recurring enquiry questions — How and why do we
move? How do we spend our day? What do we think? How do we
organise ourselves? — asked identically of the child's own
life and of the Palaeolithic, Neolithic and Bronze Age, so
change-and-continuity is felt, not memorised; evidence work
(sources, questions, graded-certainty language) comes before
any period content. The child fills in the same comparison
card for every age — Palaeolithic, Neolithic, Bronze Age,
the 21st century and even the imagined 31st century — so
change-and-continuity is a habit of filling one grid, and the
future is treated as history-in-waiting. *(Teacher guide
with its student work cards.)*

**The Grade 4 student book and cards** — the island's whole
ancient story is told through two child guides, a boy
archaeologist and Aphrodite herself, period by period
(Geometric, Archaic, Classical, Hellenistic), each unit
ending in a play-and-check review; Cyprus is never a footnote
to Greece — Greek, Phoenician, Persian, Macedonian and Roman
actors all arrive on Cypriot ground. The Grade 3 comparison
grid grows up with the child — the same 11 cards now run
Geometric, Archaic, Classical, Hellenistic, today and the
imagined 31st century side by side. *(Student book with its
student work cards.)*
