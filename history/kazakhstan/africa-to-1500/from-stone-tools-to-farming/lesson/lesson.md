# From stone tools to farming

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From stone tools to farming" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — §2 Why Lascaux cave walls were painted

Hunter bands paint bulls and hunts deep in the Lascaux cave, leaving the first art as a record of their life.

## Sub lesson 2 — §3 Why people began farming and herding

Gathering fails to feed growing bands, so people plant grain and tame animals and settle down.

## Sub lesson 3 — §3–4 The emergence of humankind

The first people appear and spread, reaching the lands that become Kazakhstan.

## Sub lesson 4 — §5–6 Palaeolithic, the Old Stone Age

Stone-tool hunters survive the ice-age steppe in bands.

## Sub lesson 5 — §7 The formation of human society

Bands become clans and tribes as people organise to survive together.

## Sub lesson 6 — §8 Occupations of Palaeolithic people

Hunting, gathering and fire-tending fill the first working day on the steppe.

## Sub lesson 7 — §9 Mesolithic, the Middle Stone Age (12th–5th millennia BC)

The ice retreats, bows and microliths arrive, and life speeds up.

## Sub lesson 8 — §10 Neolithic, the New Stone Age (5th–3rd millennia BC)

Polished stone, pottery and the first farming and herding transform the steppe.

## Sub lesson 9 — §11 Changes in Neolithic people's lives

Settled villages and stored food remake family and work.

## Sub lesson 10 — §12–13 The Eneolithic in Kazakhstan

Copper appears beside stone and horse-herding Botai settlements rise.

## Sub lesson 11 — 1.1 Life of the first people

The first inhabitants wrest a living from a harsh steppe with stone tools.

## Sub lesson 12 — 1.2 Stone Age sites in Kazakhstan

Named camps and caves across Kazakhstan prove people lived here from the deepest past.

## Sub lesson 13 — 1.3 Changes in economy and life; tools improve

Better tools change what people eat, wear and build, step by step.

## Sub lesson 14 — §2 Life of primitive people

The first chopper tool, the spear and fire remake the daily life of the earliest bands.

## Sub lesson 15 — §3 Types of productive economy

Gathering gives way to farming and herding — an economy that produces instead of only taking.

## Sub lesson 16 — §4 Culture of primitive society

Cave art, totems and the first beliefs show minds at work before writing.

## Sub lesson 17 — §2 The Stone Age

The longest age of all: stone tools carry humanity through hundreds of thousands of years.

## Sub lesson 18 — §3 Development and appearance of ancient people

Ape-like ancestors become tool-making humans and spread over the steppe.

## Sub lesson 19 — §4 Life of the first people

First families hunt, gather and shelter together against a hard climate.

## Sub lesson 20 — §5 Stone Age archaeological finds in Kazakhstan

Named sites from Karatau to the Irtysh prove the first settlement of the country.

## Sub lesson 21 — §6 Tools and weapons of Stone Age inhabitants

Choppers, scrapers and spears are read as evidence of growing skill.

## Sub lesson 22 — §7 Economic development at the end of the Stone Age

Herding and farming appear and the gathering economy begins to end.

## Sub lesson 23 — §8 Mesolithic and Neolithic tools and weapons

Bows, microliths and polished axes speed up hunting and work.

## Sub lesson 24 — §9 Why it is called the Copper Age

Copper joins stone and the metal ages quietly begin.

## Sub lesson 26 — 1.2 Stone Age sites in Kazakhstan (lesson plans)

## Sub lesson 27 — 1.3 Changes in economy and improving tools (lesson plans)

Lessons trace how better tools changed ancient work step by step.

## Sub lesson 28 — §2 Why Lascaux's walls were painted (lesson plan)

Pupils read cave paintings as the first historical sources.

## Sub lesson 29 — §3 Why people began farming and herding (lesson plan)

The Neolithic turn is built as a problem lesson on hunger and settlement.
