# Lesson plan — From stone tools to farming

**Course:** History — Kazakhstan · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From stone tools to farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The ancient world and ancient Kazakhstan — first humans, river civilisations, Greece and Rome, and the steppe tribes from the Stone Age to the Huns (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of From stone tools to farming as Kazakhstan's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The ancient world and ancient Kazakhstan — first humans, river civilisations, Greece and Rome, and the steppe tribes from the Stone Age to the Huns — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§2 Why Lascaux cave walls were painted** — hunter bands paint bulls and hunts deep in the Lascaux cave, leaving the first art as a record of their life
2. **§3 Why people began farming and herding** — gathering fails to feed growing bands, so people plant grain and tame animals and settle down
3. **§3–4 The emergence of humankind** — the first people appear and spread, reaching the lands that become Kazakhstan
4. **§5–6 Palaeolithic, the Old Stone Age** — stone-tool hunters survive the ice-age steppe in bands
5. **§7 The formation of human society** — bands become clans and tribes as people organise to survive together
6. **§8 Occupations of Palaeolithic people** — hunting, gathering and fire-tending fill the first working day on the steppe
7. **§9 Mesolithic, the Middle Stone Age (12th–5th millennia BC)** — the ice retreats, bows and microliths arrive, and life speeds up
8. **§10 Neolithic, the New Stone Age (5th–3rd millennia BC)** — polished stone, pottery and the first farming and herding transform the steppe
9. **§11 Changes in Neolithic people's lives** — settled villages and stored food remake family and work
10. **§12–13 The Eneolithic in Kazakhstan** — copper appears beside stone and horse-herding Botai settlements rise
11. **1.1 Life of the first people** — the first inhabitants wrest a living from a harsh steppe with stone tools
12. **1.2 Stone Age sites in Kazakhstan** — named camps and caves across Kazakhstan prove people lived here from the deepest past
13. **1.3 Changes in economy and life; tools improve** — better tools change what people eat, wear and build, step by step
14. **§2 Life of primitive people** — the first chopper tool, the spear and fire remake the daily life of the earliest bands
15. **§3 Types of productive economy** — gathering gives way to farming and herding — an economy that produces instead of only taking
16. **§4 Culture of primitive society** — cave art, totems and the first beliefs show minds at work before writing
17. **§2 The Stone Age** — the longest age of all: stone tools carry humanity through hundreds of thousands of years
18. **§3 Development and appearance of ancient people** — ape-like ancestors become tool-making humans and spread over the steppe
19. **§4 Life of the first people** — first families hunt, gather and shelter together against a hard climate
20. **§5 Stone Age archaeological finds in Kazakhstan** — named sites from Karatau to the Irtysh prove the first settlement of the country
21. **§6 Tools and weapons of Stone Age inhabitants** — choppers, scrapers and spears are read as evidence of growing skill
22. **§7 Economic development at the end of the Stone Age** — herding and farming appear and the gathering economy begins to end
23. **§8 Mesolithic and Neolithic tools and weapons** — bows, microliths and polished axes speed up hunting and work
24. **§9 Why it is called the Copper Age** — copper joins stone and the metal ages quietly begin
25. **1.1 Life of the earliest people (lesson plans)** — pupils demonstrate the first people's life and anthropological features in class tasks
26. **1.2 Stone Age sites in Kazakhstan (lesson plans)** — map and artefact cards turn named sites into evidence pupils handle
27. **1.3 Changes in economy and improving tools (lesson plans)** — lessons trace how better tools changed ancient work step by step
28. **§2 Why Lascaux's walls were painted (lesson plan)** — pupils read cave paintings as the first historical sources
29. **§3 Why people began farming and herding (lesson plan)** — the Neolithic turn is built as a problem lesson on hunger and settlement
