# Lesson plan — Kazakh khanate economy and culture

**Course:** History — Kazakhstan · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Kazakh khanate economy and culture
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The medieval steppe and the medieval world — Turkic khaganates, Silk Road cities, the Mongol age and the Kazakh Khanate, beside feudal Europe, Islam and the Crusades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Kazakh khanate economy and culture as Kazakhstan's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The medieval steppe and the medieval world — Turkic khaganates, Silk Road cities, the Mongol age and the Kazakh Khanate, beside feudal Europe, Islam and the Crusades — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§58 Spiritual culture of the Kazakhs, 16th–17th c.** — zhyrau poets and biys' eloquence carry the khanate's memory
2. **§59 Traditional material culture and applied art** — yurt furnishings, jewellery and weapons show a mature nomad craft
3. **§60 Agriculture and auxiliary crafts develop** — farming oases and crafts grow beside the herding economy
4. **§59 Spiritual culture of the Kazakhs, 16th–17th c. (lesson plan)** — zhyrau poetry carries the khanate's memory in class reading
5. **§60 The yurt — traditional material heritage (lesson plan)** — the yurt is assembled piece by piece as applied art's masterpiece
6. **§61 Building the winter dwelling and the builder's art (lesson plan)** — winter houses show settled building craft beside nomadism
7. **§62 Agriculture and auxiliary crafts develop (lesson plan)** — oasis farming and crafts close the course's economy arc
8. **§50 Social structure of Kazakh society** — tore, biys, batyrs and common herders take their ranked places
9. **§51–52 Political and legal structure, 16th–17th c.** — khanly power, kurultai and customary law run the state
10. **§53 The Kazakhs' traditional economy, 15th–17th c.** — the nomad herd economy reaches its classic form
11. **§54 Cities and villages, 15th–17th c.** — Turkestan, Syganak and farming villages anchor settled life
12. **§55 Spiritual culture, 15th–17th c.** — zhyrau epics and Islamic learning share the khanate's soul
13. **§56 Kazakh everyday life and customs, 15th–17th c.** — weddings, funerals and hospitality rules fill the ethnographic chapters
14. **§57 Historical literature, 16th–17th c.** — Dulati's Tarikh-i Rashidi and Jalairi's chronicle write the khanate's own history
15. **6.7 What role did social groups play in Kazakh society?** — khans, biys, batyrs and common herders each hold a defined place
16. **7.1 How is folk life reflected in the Kazakhs' spiritual culture of the 16th–17th centuries?** — epics, laments and customs carry the people's memory
17. **7.2 Why is the felt yurt counted the highest model of the nomads' material culture?** — the kiiz ui perfects portable dwelling for steppe life
18. **7.3 Why did Aktamberdi Zhyrau sing 'is there wealth beyond the herds...'?** — the warrior-poet's verse voices the nomad world's values
