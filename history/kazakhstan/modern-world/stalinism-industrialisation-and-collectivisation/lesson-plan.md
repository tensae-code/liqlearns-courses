# Lesson plan — Stalinism industrialisation and collectivisation

**Course:** History — Kazakhstan · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Stalinism industrialisation and collectivisation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The New Time — industry, empire and revolution from 1640 to 1945 in the Grade 8 world books, taught as inquiry and as survey (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Stalinism industrialisation and collectivisation as Kazakhstan's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The New Time — industry, empire and revolution from 1640 to 1945 in the Grade 8 world books, taught as inquiry and as survey — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§21 I. V. Stalin and totalitarianism in the USSR; industrialisation; collectivisation** — Stalin's five-year plans force factories up and peasants into collective farms at a terrible human cost
2. **§21 Stalin and totalitarianism in the USSR; industrialisation; collectivisation** — five-year plans and forced collectivisation at a terrible human cost
3. **§6.1 How did the Stalinist political regime change the country?** — one party, one leader, terror as routine government
4. **§6.2 How did the USSR's economy develop in the 1920s–30s?** — from NEP markets to command plans and collective farms
5. **§6.3 "Engineers of human souls": how did art serve power?** — socialist realism conscripts writers and painters into the plan

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
