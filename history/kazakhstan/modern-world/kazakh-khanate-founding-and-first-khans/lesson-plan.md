# Lesson plan — Kazakh khanate founding and first khans

**Course:** History — Kazakhstan · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Kazakh khanate founding and first khans
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The medieval steppe and the medieval world — Turkic khaganates, Silk Road cities, the Mongol age and the Kazakh Khanate, beside feudal Europe, Islam and the Crusades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Kazakh khanate founding and first khans as Kazakhstan's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The medieval steppe and the medieval world — Turkic khaganates, Silk Road cities, the Mongol age and the Kazakh Khanate, beside feudal Europe, Islam and the Crusades — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§48–49 Creation of the unified Kazakh state: Khans Kerey and Janibek** — Kerey and Janibek lead their people away from Abulkhair and found the Kazakh Khanate
2. **§50 Kasym Khan** — Kasym unites a million Kazakhs and his 'bright road' of laws orders the khanate
3. **§51 Haknazar Khan and the khanate's revival** — Haknazar rebuilds the broken khanate and recovers its cities
4. **§52 Tauekel Khan's policy of strengthening the Kazakh lands** — Tauekel takes Tashkent and Bukhara's suburbs at the khanate's widest reach
5. **§53 Esim Khan's struggle for the khanate's unity** — Esim the Brave Giant holds the khanate together against rivals and Tashkent
6. **§54 Jangir Khan and the Kazakh Khanate** — Jangir faces the rising Dzungar threat with a shrinking state
7. **§55 The Battle of Orbulak** — 400 Kazakhs under Jangir ambush a Dzungar army in the Orbulak gorge
8. **§56 The Kazakh Khanate under Tauke Khan** — Tauke and the three biys give the khanate its Zheti Zhargy law code
9. **§57 The system of power in old Kazakh society** — khans, sultans, biys and batyrs share rule in the nomad state
10. **§49 Creation of the unified Kazakh state: Kerey and Janibek (lesson plan)** — the secession to Chu and Kozy-Basy is staged as the founding act
11. **§50 Kasym Khan (lesson plan)** — Kasym's unification and his Bright Road laws fill two lessons
12. **§51 Haknazar Khan and the khanate's revival (lesson plan)** — Haknazar rebuilds the state city by city
13. **§52 Tauekel Khan's policy of uniting the Kazakh lands (lesson plan)** — Tauekel's Tashkent campaigns mark the khanate's widest reach
14. **§53 Esim the Brave's struggle for the khanate's unity (lesson plan)** — Esim holds unity against Tashkent and rivals
15. **§54 Jangir Khan and the Kazakh Khanate (lesson plan)** — Jangir faces the Dzungar storm with a weakened state
16. **§55 The Battle of Orbulak (lesson plan)** — the Orbulak ambush is reconstructed as a tactics case
17. **§56 The Kazakh Khanate under Tauke Khan (lesson plan)** — Tauke and the biys codify Zheti Zhargy in the lessons
18. **§57 Foreign-policy ties develop under Tauke Khan (lesson plan)** — embassies to Russia and Bukhara open the diplomacy lessons
19. **§58 The system of power in old Kazakh society (lesson plan)** — khan, sultans, biys and batyrs are charted as one system
20. **§40–41 The Kazakh Khanate is created** — Kerey and Janibek's secession founds the khanate in Chu valley
21. **§42 Kazakh lands unite, late 15th–early 16th c.** — the Syr Darya cities and steppe tribes gather under one khan
22. **§43 Kasym Khan's domestic and foreign policy (1511–1518)** — Kasym at his peak rules a million people and treaties with Moscow begin
23. **§44 The khanate under Haknazar Khan (1538–1580)** — Haknazar restores unity after civil strife and takes Turkestan
24. **§45 Tauekel Khan's reign (1583–1598); the khanate at the 16th c.'s end** — Tauekel seizes Tashkent and breaks with Bukhara
25. **§46 The khanate in the early 17th c.: Esim Khan (1598–1628)** — Esim's 'old road' of law steadies the state he inherits divided
26. **§47 Fighting the Dzungar invasion: Jangir Khan (1629–1652)** — Jangir and the Orbulak victory hold back the Dzungars
27. **§48–49 The khanate under Tauke Khan (1680–1715/18)** — Tauke's Zheti Zhargy and the council of biys crown the khanate's order
28. **5.1 Why is the Kazakh Khanate counted Central Asia's first national state?** — Kerei and Janibek's breakaway founds a state of one people
29. **6.1 Why did Kadyrgali Zhalayiri write that Kasym Khan was the most famous of Janibek's sons?** — Kasym's laws and million-strong host earn the chronicler's praise
30. **6.2 Why is Khaknazar Khan's rule called the Khanate's 'revival'?** — Khaknazar reunites the zhuzes after civil war
31. **6.3 What policy did Tauekel Khan follow to unite the Kazakh lands?** — Tauekel balances Bukhara, Moscow and the steppe to gather the land
32. **6.4 How did Esim Khan preserve the Khanate's integrity?** — Esim's 'old path' laws hold the union through war with Bukhara
33. **6.5 What place does the Battle of Orbulak hold in world military-art history?** — Zhangir's 600 warriors ambush a Dzungar army in a mountain gorge
