# From evidence to a written history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From evidence to a written history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Introduction: historical sources

Written, material and oral traces are sorted so the past can be rebuilt from evidence.

## Sub lesson 2 — §5 How we can learn about river-valley civilisations

Archaeologists read ruins, tablets and tools to recover worlds that left no living witnesses.

## Sub lesson 3 — §15 Archaeological discoveries of Alkey Margulan

Margulan's expeditions uncover central Kazakhstan's bronze-age world for science.

## Sub lesson 4 — §5 How we learn about river-valley civilisations (lesson plan)

Archaeology's toolkit is demonstrated on real finds.

## Sub lesson 5 — The guide's method: research questions and source work in every lesson plan

Each of Book 8's topics is taught by posing a research question and working documents from the Reader before the textbook narrative.
