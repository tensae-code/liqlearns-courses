# Dating the past

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Dating the past" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Introduction: chronology, timeline, BC/AD

Events are lined up oldest to newest on a timeline ribbon so dates stop being a jumble.

## Sub lesson 2 — Introductory lesson: the periods of ancient Kazakhstan's history

Stone, bronze and iron ages are laid on one timeline from 2.6 million years ago to the 6th century.

## Sub lesson 3 — Introductory lesson plan: periods of ancient Kazakhstan

The teacher stages the timeline game that sorts stone, bronze and iron ages.

## Sub lesson 4 — §1 The concept of the contemporary era and its features

History is cut into periods and the newest era (1900–today, split at 1945, with the 1918–1939 "Interbellum" between the wars) is named and framed.

## Sub lesson 5 — Chronological index (appendix)

Every key date of 1900–1945 is lined up in one table so pupils can place events in order.

## Sub lesson 6 — §1 The concept of the New Time and its features

A new period of history is bounded and named, from the English Revolution to the turn of the 20th century.

## Sub lesson 7 — Chronological index (appendix)

The dates of 1640–1900 are set in one ordered table for revision.

## Sub lesson 8 — Introduction: the New Time as a period (periodisation table of world history)

The whole of history is laid out in one table and the New Time (1640–1914) is cut out as this year's territory.

## Sub lesson 9 — Chronological index of the most important events (appendix)

Two centuries of Kazakh history are lined up date by date.

## Sub lesson 10 — "Main events" chronological table (appendix)

The two centuries are lined up as one dated spine.

## Sub lesson 11 — Chronological index (appendix)

1900–1945 in Kazakhstan is set out date by date, from the 1916 revolt to the 1931–33 famine and the Panfilov feat.

## Sub lesson 12 — Chronological index of the main events

The book closes with the dates of the post-war era.

## Sub lesson 13 — §1 The concept of the contemporary era and its features

The newest era is framed — 1900 to today, split at 1945, with the "Interbellum" between the wars.

## Sub lesson 14 — Chronological index

The dates of 1900–1945 in one table.

## Sub lesson 15 — Chronological index

The dates of 1945 to the present in one table.

## Sub lesson 16 — Key dates (appendix)

The era's turning dates in one list.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Dating the past as Kazakhstan's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
