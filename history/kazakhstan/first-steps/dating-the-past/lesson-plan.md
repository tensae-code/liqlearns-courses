# Lesson plan — Dating the past

**Course:** History — Kazakhstan · **Title:** First steps · **Lesson:** Dating the past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The ancient world and ancient Kazakhstan — first humans, river civilisations, Greece and Rome, and the steppe tribes from the Stone Age to the Huns (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Dating the past as Kazakhstan's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The ancient world and ancient Kazakhstan — first humans, river civilisations, Greece and Rome, and the steppe tribes from the Stone Age to the Huns — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Introduction: chronology, timeline, BC/AD** — events are lined up oldest to newest on a timeline ribbon so dates stop being a jumble
2. **Introductory lesson: the periods of ancient Kazakhstan's history** — stone, bronze and iron ages are laid on one timeline from 2.6 million years ago to the 6th century
3. **Introductory lesson plan: periods of ancient Kazakhstan** — the teacher stages the timeline game that sorts stone, bronze and iron ages
4. **§1 The concept of the contemporary era and its features** — history is cut into periods and the newest era (1900–today, split at 1945, with the 1918–1939 "Interbellum" between the wars) is named and framed
5. **Chronological index (appendix)** — every key date of 1900–1945 is lined up in one table so pupils can place events in order
6. **§1 The concept of the New Time and its features** — a new period of history is bounded and named, from the English Revolution to the turn of the 20th century
7. **Chronological index (appendix)** — the dates of 1640–1900 are set in one ordered table for revision
8. **Introduction: the New Time as a period (periodisation table of world history)** — the whole of history is laid out in one table and the New Time (1640–1914) is cut out as this year's territory
9. **Chronological index of the most important events (appendix)** — two centuries of Kazakh history are lined up date by date
10. **"Main events" chronological table (appendix)** — the two centuries are lined up as one dated spine
11. **Chronological index (appendix)** — 1900–1945 in Kazakhstan is set out date by date, from the 1916 revolt to the 1931–33 famine and the Panfilov feat
12. **Chronological index of the main events** — the book closes with the dates of the post-war era
13. **§1 The concept of the contemporary era and its features** — the newest era is framed — 1900 to today, split at 1945, with the "Interbellum" between the wars
14. **Chronological index** — the dates of 1900–1945 in one table
15. **Chronological index** — the dates of 1945 to the present in one table
16. **Key dates (appendix)** — the era's turning dates in one list
