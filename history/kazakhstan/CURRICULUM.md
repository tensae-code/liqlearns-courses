# History — Kazakhstan course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Kazakhstan inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Kazakhstan's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** the placements files' book order is the teaching order — earliest books first — and the ladder follows Kazakhstan's own progression through its books. The course opens at Grade 5 with two tellings side by side: the ancient world (the NIS inquiry book and its Atamura counterpart, in Russian and Kazakh) and ancient Kazakhstan itself (Atamura, NIS and Mektep versions — the steppe from the Stone Age through the Sakas, Usuns, Kangly, Sarmatians and Huns), with the two Grade 5 teacher guides stocked as evidence inside the same lessons. Grade 6 gives the medieval millennium: Kazakhstan from the Turkic khaganates through the Silk Road cities and the Mongol age to the Kazakh Khanate (Mektep, Atamura and their guides), beside the medieval world of feudalism, Byzantium, Islam and the Crusades. Grade 7 re-teaches the medieval world as open inquiry (the NIS books, in Kazakh and English for the national book) and moves the national story into the 18th–19th centuries in the 2025 editions. The Grade 8 world books then teach the New Time, 1640–1945, as source disputes and as survey, while the Grade 8 national books tell the Dzhungar wars, the annexation, the enlighteners, and 1900–1945 — Alash, the famine, the repressions and the Great Patriotic War; their teacher guides and the Uzbek post-war volume (1945–2019) close that stage. Grade 9 closes the course: the world from 1900 to the present (the Aldabek two-part survey and the NIS inquiry book) and Kazakhstan from 1900 to the present (1900–1945, then the post-war books to independence and the building of the independent state). Each level is one stage of that progression, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Where the build stands

The course is built in full from the two placements files: every one of the 1262 placement lines is stocked into exactly one lesson, with exactly one provenance row in that lesson's sources.md. One book is RECORD-ONLY as filed (file A Book 4, the 156 Akhmetova Russian-edition record — a mismatch holding a duplicate scan of the Kazakh edition) and carries no placements; it is named in ABOUT.md. No book in this filing is UNREAD. Three books are PARTIAL as filed (file A Book 5 as a file, file B Book 5, and file B Book 9 in a minor way) with their labels carried exactly in ABOUT.md. The five teacher-guide entries are evidence only, acceptance 0, labelled — and every guide placement sits inside a lesson that also stands on a student book, so no lesson in this course stands on teacher-guide evidence alone.
