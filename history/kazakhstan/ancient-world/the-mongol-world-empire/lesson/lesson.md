# The mongol world empire

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "The mongol world empire" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — §12 The Mongols' world empire

Chinggis's heirs rule the largest land empire the world has seen.

## Sub lesson 2 — §13 Eurasia's political map after the Mongol conquests

Four khanates divide Eurasia and a new order settles over the steppe.

## Sub lesson 3 — §12 The Mongol Empire forms

Chinggis unites the steppe and his sons conquer half the world.

## Sub lesson 4 — §13 The Mongol invasion's effect on Eurasia's political map

Khanates replace old states from China to Rus'.

## Sub lesson 5 — 5.1 Why did the Mongols win quick victories over their enemies?

Discipline, horses and siege engineers outmatch settled armies.

## Sub lesson 6 — 5.2 How much did the Mongols and conquered peoples change each other?

Conquerors adopt religions and scripts while subjects adopt Mongol order.

## Sub lesson 7 — §12 Chinggis Khan; the Mongol Empire is created

Temujin's kurultai of 1206 launches the largest land empire.

## Sub lesson 8 — §13 The Mongols' military art

Decimal ranks, composite bows and feigned flight win battles before they start.

## Sub lesson 9 — §14–15 Mongol campaigns' influence on Eurasia's ethnopolitical map

Peoples are moved, merged and renamed across the conquered steppe.

## Sub lesson 10 — §12 Chinggis Khan

Temujin's rise from orphan to world conqueror opens the Mongol age.

## Sub lesson 11 — §13 The Mongol Empire's structure

Uluses, the yam post and the Yasa organise the giant empire.

## Sub lesson 12 — §14 The Mongols' military art

Tumen discipline and siege trains defeat larger settled armies.

## Sub lesson 13 — §15 The Mongol conquests' effect on Eurasia's political map

New khanates and peoples settle over the conquered continent.
