# Lesson plan — Medieval world the dark age and feudalism

**Course:** History — Kazakhstan · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Medieval world the dark age and feudalism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The medieval steppe and the medieval world — Turkic khaganates, Silk Road cities, the Mongol age and the Kazakh Khanate, beside feudal Europe, Islam and the Crusades (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Medieval world the dark age and feudalism as Kazakhstan's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The medieval steppe and the medieval world — Turkic khaganates, Silk Road cities, the Mongol age and the Kazakh Khanate, beside feudal Europe, Islam and the Crusades — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 The concept 'Middle Ages'; fall of the Western Roman Empire** — Rome falls to the barbarians and Europe fragments into kingdoms
2. **§5 Main features of feudal society** — land-for-service binds lord and vassal into the feudal pyramid
3. **§6 Social order of feudal society** — those who pray, fight and work — three estates freeze everyone's place
4. **§1 The concept of the Middle Ages in history** — a thousand years between Rome's fall and the modern age get their name and borders
5. **§2 The fall of the Western Roman Empire** — barbarian kingdoms carve up the West while the East survives
6. **§5 The feudal economy** — land, peasants and dues form feudalism's economic base
7. **§6 Feudal society** — lords, clergy and peasants lock into the three-estate order
8. **§18 Periods of the feudal states' development** — early, high and late feudalism are summed as one arc
9. **1.1 Why did the Roman Empire fall in the West?** — historians' rival versions of 476 are weighed — no single cause carries the fall
10. **2.1 What were feudal society and economy?** — land-for-service and the manor are modelled as a working system
11. **§1 The concept of the Middle Ages; periodisation of medieval history** — early, high and late Middle Ages are bounded and named
12. **§2 The Great Migration of Peoples; fall of the Western Roman Empire** — tribes on the move push Rome over in 476
13. **§5 The concept of feudalism; features of the feudal economy** — fief, vassal and manor define the feudal order's working parts
14. **§6 Social structure of feudal society** — three estates assign every person a prayed, fought or worked place
15. **§1 The concept of the Middle Ages in history** — the medieval thousand years are named, bounded and divided into three ages
16. **§2 The Great Migration of Peoples and the fall of the Western Roman Empire** — migrating tribes overwhelm the West's frontiers and Rome falls
17. **§5 Features of the feudal economy's forms** — natural economy and feudal rent define how the manor worked
18. **§6 Feudal society's social structure** — the three estates and the vassal ladder order medieval life
19. **§20 Stages of the feudal state's development** — estate monarchy succeeds feudal fragmentation as the state matures
