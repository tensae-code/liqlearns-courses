# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. The oldest person in your village is a textbook — and this lesson sends you to read them before the book closes.

Look at the classroom. The teacher does not open a reader. The teacher sends the class out — to an elder. Sit down. Ask. Listen. What does the elder know of the sacred and traditional practices? What were the traditional fishing practices, and which fish were caught?

None of that is in the printed readers. It lives in one person's memory, carried there across a whole lifetime. And here is the turn: memory has a deadline. What is not asked for, and written down, is lost when its holder is gone.

So the class writes. Practice by practice, fish by fish, the elder's remembering becomes the class's record — history found in its own locality, saved by the children who went and asked.

Remember it this way: ask the elder, write it down, before it is lost.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
