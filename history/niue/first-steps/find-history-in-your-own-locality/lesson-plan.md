# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Niue · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Pacific Past Through the Sea and the Land (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is not only in books: in your own locality, the oldest person is a living source — pupils are sent to talk to an elder about what they know of sacred and traditional practices, and to list traditional fishing practices and the fish that were caught, writing down what the elder remembers before it is lost.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Pacific Past Through the Sea and the Land — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- **Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters** (`first-steps/heritage-what-it-is-and-why-it-matters/`) is recommended first — it teaches the heritage this lesson sends the learner out to collect from an elder's memory (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: an elder in your own locality is a source of history — ask, listen, and write down what they remember before it is lost.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — one elder or older person near them, and one thing that person remembers that no book near them holds.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the guide itself works, grounding the regional books in the child's own island through elders as the source of traditional knowledge.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks history lives only in printed books → the guide's own method is the opposite: it sends pupils out of the classroom to an elder, because the elder's memory holds what no reader holds.
- Thinks asking is enough, without writing → the lesson's point is the writing: the class writes down what the elder remembers *before it is lost* — memory unwritten dies with its holder.
- Go back to the one thing: the oldest person in the village becomes the textbook, and the class writes down what the elder remembers before it is lost. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
