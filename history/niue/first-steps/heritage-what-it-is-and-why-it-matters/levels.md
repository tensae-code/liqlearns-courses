# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sharks, turtles and whales in Pacific culture
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: sharks carry strong cultural meaning and symbolism, turtles hold high cultural significance, and whales are told of across the Pacific as guardians — the animals of science are also ancestors and guardians in the people's stories, and losing them would erase identity — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional uses of the reef and ocean — traditional fishing in Tuvalu and Niue
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: fishing was a community craft governed by custom and sharing — whole communities herding fish with coconut-leaf uloa/pola; in Niue, a raised coral atoll, each district traditionally owning and sharing its stretch of coast; men making the nets and spears — Niue's custom held up as the living example of fishing by rule and sharing — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — People and the natural environment — knowledge passed down
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pacific people passed their special understanding of the environment and its natural resources down through the generations — knowing when to plant, fish and spare the land was an inheritance carried in memory from grandparents to children — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Case study 1 — taro in Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Niue's main food crops — taro, sweet potato, yams and more — and how they are grown; Niue's gardens are a living archive of what its ancestors learned to grow on a raised coral atoll — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Case study 2 — the amazing coconut tree
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: coconuts in Niue, whose name breaks down as Niu (coconut) + e (here) — "here is the coconut" — the island's name is a one-line history: the first thing its founders wanted remembered is the tree that greeted them — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the teacher guide has teachers discuss with the class how knowledge is passed down through generations, using the coconut palm's place in Pacific life — a lesson about a tree becomes a lesson about inheritance, what a people chooses to hand on — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
