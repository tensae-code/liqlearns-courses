# History — Niue course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Niue inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Niue has no national book of its
own in this set — the Pacific past is taught regionally,
through the sea and the land, in the USP/UNESCO Education
for Sustainable Development readers Niue's record carries
(the same files are also shelved under Nauru — see ABOUT.md).
But Niue is inside the regional story by name: its local
advisor Birtha Togahai is named in Reader 1's credits, its
districts' shared ownership of the coast is held up as the
model of traditional practice, its taro gardens are a named
case study, and the coconut that gives the island its name
carries a whole lesson. The readers' own order is the
ladder. Reader 1, *People and the Sea*, opens with the
voyaging past — one large exchange community of islands
joined by regular voyaging — and then turns to heritage:
the sea animals of Pacific culture and Niue's own
traditional fishing. Reader 3, *People and the Natural
Environment*, is the landward half: farming knowledge
handed down through the generations, in Niue's taro gardens
and the coconut tree. The Teacher Guide closes the set by
sending the learner out of the book altogether — to an
elder in their own locality, before what the elder
remembers is lost — and by naming the coconut lesson
itself a lesson about inheritance. One set, one level —
the ladder stops where the books in hand stop, not because
Niue teaches nothing more: Niue's shelf holds no Niue
national history or social-studies book at all (see
ABOUT.md's honesty notes).

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — the regional
USP/UNESCO ESD readers carried in Niue's record — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Ancient World → First Steps** — the Pacific past as
   Niue's readers tell it: first the voyaging and island
   exchange that made one sea join many homes; then heritage
   itself, in six parts that build in order (the sharks,
   turtles and whales of Pacific culture; traditional
   fishing in Tuvalu and in Niue, where each district
   traditionally owned and shared its stretch of coast;
   environmental knowledge passed down through the
   generations; the taro case study of Tonga, Niue and
   Tuvalu; the amazing coconut tree that gives Niue its
   name; and the teacher guide's coconut lesson about
   inheritance); and last the method that finds history at
   home — the elder as the textbook.
   3 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/` (1 lesson),
   then `first-steps/` (2 lessons).

**Totals: 3 lessons** (Level 1: 3 — 2 lessons on student
books, 1 of them with a teacher-guide part named as
evidence; 1 lesson on teacher-guide evidence only,
acceptance 0, labelled — see ABOUT.md).
