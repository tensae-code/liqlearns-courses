# Hands-on — Pacific Voyaging and Island Exchange

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw your nine dots again and join them with routes again, as the opening act taught. Say the lesson's line aloud as you finish: one sea joining many homes.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw the exchange community: nine islands as dots or small shapes — Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue, Rotuma, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Futuna and Uvea — with a route line between islands that exchanged with each other. Along three of the routes, write the three things that circulated: wealth, skills, goods. Say each island's name aloud as you draw it, and ring Niue — your own home in the web.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for exchange in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time something moves between homes or people and makes them more one community — goods carried, a skill taught, help given — note it: what moved, between whom, and what the lesson would say it joined. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
