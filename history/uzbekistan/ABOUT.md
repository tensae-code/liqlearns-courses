# About the History — Uzbekistan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Uzbekistan's own history books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns, marked exactly "Tip — from us, not from the books". Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Uzbekistan inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade a book comes from appears only in this ABOUT and each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. Levels derive from the books' own progression as filed (see CURRICULUM.md).

The course as built has **five levels**: Level 1 Homeland and How History Works (4 lessons) · Level 2 The Ancient World (54) · Level 3 Khanates and the Early Modern World (67) · Level 4 Conquest, Colonies and Empires to 1918 (46) · Level 5 Wars, the Soviet Years and Independence (16). Totals: 187 lessons, 213 placements.

## The way of the books

Uzbekistan's own way, as filed per book: history enters as short narrative "stories" (Grade 5 — one homeland lesson first, then the whole world story in 56 little chapters, Central Asia's own heroes woven through every age, closing in independent Uzbekistan); a full ancient-world course written from Central Asia outward (Grade 6, with a whole section reserved for Central Asia); from Grade 8 the homeland alone — three centuries of the Bukhara, Khiva and Kokand khanates and the Karakalpaks, state by state — beside a world "new history" that gives Asia a full chapter of its own in each period; Grade 9 tells the colonial half-century as a national story (conquest campaign by campaign, the colonial system dissected, the Jadid answer, culture under dependence) beside the world from the 1870s to 1918 in 43 short paragraphs framed as lessons for building Uzbekistan's own statehood.
