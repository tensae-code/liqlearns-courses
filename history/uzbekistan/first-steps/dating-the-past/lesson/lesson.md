# Dating the Past

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — 3. Reckoning of time in history and 4. The calendar and its meaning

centuries, eras and BC/AD counting are taught as the historian's ruler, then calendars — solar, lunar, Julian and Gregorian — as humanity's way of keeping the years

## Sub lesson 2 — Appendix: The most important historical dates

the course's whole span compressed into one dated list, teaching learners to hold 350 years on a single line

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of dating the past — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Dating the Past. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
