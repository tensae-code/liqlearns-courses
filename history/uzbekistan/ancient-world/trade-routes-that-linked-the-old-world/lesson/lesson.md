# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — 16. The Great Silk Road

caravans link China to the Mediterranean through Samarkand and Bukhara; silk, paper and ideas travel the same road and make the homeland the world's crossroads

## Sub lesson 2 — §46 The Great Silk Road

Zhang Qian's journey opens the road; through Kushan and Sogdian hands silk, glass, horses and religions travel between China, India, Iran and Rome

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of trade routes that linked the old world — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Trade Routes That Linked the Old World. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
