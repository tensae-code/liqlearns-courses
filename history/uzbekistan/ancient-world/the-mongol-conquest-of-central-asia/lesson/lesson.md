# The Mongol Conquest of Central Asia

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

in 1219–21 the Mongol storm breaks Bukhara, Samarkand and Khorezm; cities, canals and libraries are destroyed in the homeland's greatest catastrophe

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of the mongol conquest of central asia — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: The Mongol Conquest of Central Asia. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story of the mongol conquest of central asia — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern happens in real life. Note it.
