# Lesson plan — Russian Conquest of Central Asia

**Course:** History — Uzbekistan · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Russian Conquest of Central Asia
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Conquest, Colonies and Empires to 1918 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of russian conquest of central asia — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Conquest, Colonies and Empires to 1918 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **37. The dream of conquering Central Asia and 38. Defence of the Homeland** — Russia advances fortress by fortress from the steppe; Tashkent, Samarkand and Khiva fall despite desperate local defence, and the khanates become a colony's borderland
2. **Ch. 2 §§6–7 The beginning of the Russian Empire's conquests in Central Asia** — fortress lines creep down the Syr Darya; Ak-Mechet, Chimkent and Aulie-Ata fall as the empire closes on Tashkent
3. **Ch. 2 §8 The continuation of the campaigns of conquest in Central Asia** — Chernyaev takes Tashkent (1865), Kaufman takes Samarkand (1868) — in five years the map of the homeland is redrawn by two generals

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the story of russian conquest of central asia — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
2. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, what came of it — without looking back.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of the region in the story if you have one. Nothing else.

- Retells names but not the turn → ask: what changed in this story, and for whom? The turn is the proof.
- Go back to the one thing: the story of russian conquest of central asia — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
