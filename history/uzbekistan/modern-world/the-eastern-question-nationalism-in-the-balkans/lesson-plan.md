# Lesson plan — The Eastern Question Nationalism in the Balkans

**Course:** History — Uzbekistan · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Eastern Question Nationalism in the Balkans
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Khanates and the Early Modern World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of the eastern question nationalism in the balkans — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Khanates and the Early Modern World — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ch. IV §20 The foreign policy of Russia** — Russia fights Turkey (1877–78), Osman Pasha surrenders at Plevna, and the San Stefano treaty draws a big Bulgaria — until Britain's fleet and the Berlin Congress cut the gains back, teaching Russia that Balkan victories are settled in Europe's chancelleries
2. **Ch. IV §25 The Balkan states at the end of the 19th – beginning of the 20th century** — Bulgaria's April rising (1876) is drowned in blood — some 30,000 killed — and poet Hristo Botev falls fighting; the Tarnovo Constitution (1878) makes the freed principality a constitutional monarchy, which then quarrels with its Russian liberator over Prince Battenberg

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the story of the eastern question nationalism in the balkans — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
2. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, what came of it — without looking back.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of the region in the story if you have one. Nothing else.
