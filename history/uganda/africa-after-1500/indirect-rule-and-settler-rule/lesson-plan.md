## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 8: Colonial Administration — Indirect Rule** — Captain Lugard introduces indirect rule — local leaders supervise cash crops and collect taxes for the British; natives learn leadership and modern law, and lose their independence.
2. **SST Term 2: Indirect and direct rule** — Indirect rule works through the kingdoms of Buganda, Tooro and Ankole without disturbing local leaders; where there was resistance — Kigezi, Bugisu, West Nile, Bunyoro — the British rule directly, through Baganda agents.
3. **Topic 4: Indirect rule** — The book weighs indirect rule fairly — Africans learn new administration and keep their kingdoms and cultures; the colonialists get cheap, quiet rule.
4. **Term 2: Indirect rule** — The system is explained by the school itself — prefects ruling pupils for the administration — and works where strong centralised governments already exist, through district, county and sub-county African chiefs.
5. **Senior Four Term 1: Indirect rule in West Africa** — Britain rules cheaply through chiefs content with gifts of outdated guns and second-hand cloth, leaving the Emirs their tax powers so the system is welcomed.
