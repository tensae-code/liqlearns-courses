## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 8: Colonial Administration — Indirect Rule

Captain Lugard introduces indirect rule — local leaders supervise cash crops and collect taxes for the British; natives learn leadership and modern law, and lose their independence.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — SST Term 2: Indirect and direct rule

Indirect rule works through the kingdoms of Buganda, Tooro and Ankole without disturbing local leaders; where there was resistance — Kigezi, Bugisu, West Nile, Bunyoro — the British rule directly, through Baganda agents.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 4: Indirect rule

The book weighs indirect rule fairly — Africans learn new administration and keep their kingdoms and cultures; the colonialists get cheap, quiet rule.

## Sub lesson 4 — Term 2: Indirect rule

The system is explained by the school itself — prefects ruling pupils for the administration — and works where strong centralised governments already exist, through district, county and sub-county African chiefs.

Britain rules cheaply through chiefs content with gifts of outdated guns and second-hand cloth, leaving the Emirs their tax powers so the system is welcomed.
