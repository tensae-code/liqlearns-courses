# Video script — Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Speke leaves the sick Grant at King Rumanika's Karagwe and reaches Kabaka Muteesa I's palace; Stanley comes three times, first hunting the missing Livingstone for two newspapers — and the explorers rename Nalubaale "Lake Victoria" and open the way to missionaries.

On the map, follow this part: Topic 7: European Explorers in Uganda. Speke leaves the sick Grant at King Rumanika's Karagwe and reaches Kabaka Muteesa I's palace; Stanley comes three times, first hunting the missing Livingstone for two newspapers — and the explorers rename Nalubaale "Lake Victoria" and open the way to missionaries.

On the map, follow this part: SST Term 2: Foreign groups and the explorers' journeys. The child draws the explorers' routes across the sketch map of Uganda and names the foreign groups who followed them in.

On the map, follow this part: Topic 4: European exploration in East Africa. Speke and Burton come first into the interior; Grant falls sick at Karagwe under King Rumanika's care while Speke pushes on into Buganda; Bellefonds meets Stanley at the Kabaka's palace in April 1875 and backs his call for missionaries.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 6, Topic: Religions in East Africa; Lesson 1: Christian Missionaries in East Africa. The missionaries come to preach, civilise, replace the slave trade with legitimate trade — and, the book says plainly, to pave the way for imperialism; they build Namirembe and Lubaga and the schools.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 6: European Christian missionaries. The missionaries walk very long distances from a coast where Arabs oppose them and rulers see a threat; in Buganda they end up as intermediaries in the 1900 Agreement itself.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 6 (guide): Problems of the missionaries. Livingstone and Stanley die of tropical diseases like malaria with their work unfinished, because supplies and medicines could not reach them — communication with home was too poor.

On the map, follow this part: Term 1: Explorers, traders and colonisation. The explorers exaggerate what they saw, the traders sign treaties the home governments later use to occupy East Africa, and the Africans who welcomed them "with open hands" did not know what the acts would lead to.

On the map, follow this part: Term 1: Christian missionaries. The missionaries preach respect, brotherhood, forgiveness and nonviolence — so that, the book argues, the colonialists who followed would meet less resistance.

On the map, follow this part: Term 3: Christian missionaries in Southern Africa. The London, Church and Scottish missionary societies face a vast area, hostile ground, no transport and Africans committed to their own religion.

On the map, follow this part: Senior Four Term 1, Topic: Christian Missionary Activities in West Africa. Portuguese missions begin as early as 1456 and fail against Islam's spread; the 19th-century missionaries come, the book says, partly to wipe the slave trade's image from Europe's face — and teach farming, bricklaying and carpentry.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Then go and do the lesson.
