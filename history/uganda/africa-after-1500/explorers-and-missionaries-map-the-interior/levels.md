# Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 7: European Explorers in Uganda
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Speke leaves the sick Grant at King Rumanika's Karagwe and reaches Kabaka Muteesa I's palace; Stanley comes three times, first hunting the missing Livingstone for two newspapers — and the explorers rename Nalubaale "Lake Victoria" and open the way to missionaries. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — SST Term 2: Foreign groups and the explorers' journeys
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The child draws the explorers' routes across the sketch map of Uganda and names the foreign groups who followed them in. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 4: European exploration in East Africa
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Speke and Burton come first into the interior; Grant falls sick at Karagwe under King Rumanika's care while Speke pushes on into Buganda; Bellefonds meets Stanley at the Kabaka's palace in April 1875 and backs his call for missionaries. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — Chapter 6, Topic: Religions in East Africa; Lesson 1: Christian Missionaries in East Africa
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The missionaries come to preach, civilise, replace the slave trade with legitimate trade — and, the book says plainly, to pave the way for imperialism; they build Namirembe and Lubaga and the schools. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 5 — Chapter 6: European Christian missionaries
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The missionaries walk very long distances from a coast where Arabs oppose them and rulers see a threat; in Buganda they end up as intermediaries in the 1900 Agreement itself. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 6 — Chapter 6 (guide): Problems of the missionaries
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Livingstone and Stanley die of tropical diseases like malaria with their work unfinished, because supplies and medicines could not reach them — communication with home was too poor. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 7 — Term 1: Explorers, traders and colonisation
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The explorers exaggerate what they saw, the traders sign treaties the home governments later use to occupy East Africa, and the Africans who welcomed them "with open hands" did not know what the acts would lead to. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 8 — Term 1: Christian missionaries
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The missionaries preach respect, brotherhood, forgiveness and nonviolence — so that, the book argues, the colonialists who followed would meet less resistance. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 9 — Term 3: Christian missionaries in Southern Africa
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The London, Church and Scottish missionary societies face a vast area, hostile ground, no transport and Africans committed to their own religion. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.

## Sub lesson 10 — Senior Four Term 1, Topic: Christian Missionary Activities in West Africa
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Portuguese missions begin as early as 1456 and fail against Islam's spread; the 19th-century missionaries come, the book says, partly to wipe the slave trade's image from Europe's face — and teach farming, bricklaying and carpentry. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example from the story.
