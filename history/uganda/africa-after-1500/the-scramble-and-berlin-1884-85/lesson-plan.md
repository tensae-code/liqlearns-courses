## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 7: The Imperial British East Africa Company and the Scramble** — In 1888 Britain charters the IBEA Company to administer its 1886 treaty share; Kabaka Mwanga begs the Company's Jackson for help in 1889, and Emin Pasha's Equatoria falls to Kitchener's Anglo-Egyptian force in 1898.
2. **Topic 4: The scramble, partition and the Heligoland treaty** — In 1890 Britain trades the Heligoland islands to Germany for a free hand in Uganda — an island in Europe swapped for a kingdom in Africa.
3. **Chapter 8, Topic: Scramble, Partition and Colonisation of East Africa** — Berlin 1884–85 starts the rush; Britain wants the Nile's source to guard Egypt and the sea route to India; missionaries soften hearts with the Bible while chartered companies administer on the cheap.
4. **Chapter 8: The Scramble and Colonisation of East Africa** — Mombasa, the Nile's source and Dar es Salaam are the strategic prizes; missionaries preach legitimate trade against the slave trade while the powers position for the ports and the river.
5. **Term 2, Topic 2: European Imperialism in East Africa** — After partition the Europeans settle the Kenya highlands, Africans lose land to reserve camps like the Maasai's, and new boundaries are drawn without them.
6. **Senior Four Term 1, Topic: The Scramble for and Partition of West Africa** — Berlin 1884–85 encourages the rush; Goldie's Royal Niger Company acquires colonies for Britain while missionaries soften the ground.
