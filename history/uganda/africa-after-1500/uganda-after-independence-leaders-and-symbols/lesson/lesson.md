# Uganda After Independence: Leaders and Symbols

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Topic 10: Uganda as an Independent Nation; SST Term 3, Topic: Uganda as an Independent Nation; Topic 4: Post-independence Uganda. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 10: Uganda as an Independent Nation

The presidents file past — Obote twice, Amin, Lule's 68 days — Amin brings Muteesa's body home to Kasubi in 1972; the expelled Asians are allowed back, UPE and USE are introduced, and the flag, anthem and crested-crane coat of arms teach the new nation's meanings.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — SST Term 3, Topic: Uganda as an Independent Nation

The flag's red is brotherhood, the coat of arms — designed by Cecil Todd — joins drum, spear and shield to coffee and cotton, and the child draws the crested crane and asks why it was chosen.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 4: Post-independence Uganda

Muteesa II, Kabaka from 1939, dies in 1969; Amin expels the Asians in 1972 and rules by decree until exiles of the UNLF and Tanzania overthrow him in 1979; the leaders' list runs to Museveni, 1986 to date.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.
