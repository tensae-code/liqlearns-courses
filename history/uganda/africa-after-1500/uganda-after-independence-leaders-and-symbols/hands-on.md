# Hands-on — Uganda After Independence: Leaders and Symbols

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a simple map or timeline for this story: mark where it happens, put the parts in order, and label the turn. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The presidents file past — Obote twice, Amin, Lule's 68 days — Amin brings Muteesa's body home to Kasubi in 1972; the expelled Asians are allowed back, UPE and USE are introduced, and the flag, anthem and crested-crane coat of arms teach the new nation's meanings. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act the story with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — a place name, a building, a story an elder tells, a date on a calendar. Each time you meet it, note it: what it was, and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
