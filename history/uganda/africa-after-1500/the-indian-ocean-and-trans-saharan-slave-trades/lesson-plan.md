## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 7: The Slave Trade and its Abolition** — Slaves are got by raiding villages, buying war captives from chiefs and seizing the lonely; Livingstone campaigns against the trade in Parliament's hearing, Seyyid Said helps the British fight the traders at sea, and Sharp and Clarkson organise abolition.
2. **Term 1: Slave trade in northern Uganda** — Sir Samuel Baker builds Fort Patiko to shield Acholi from the Khartoumer slave traders, yet the trade resists abolition because kings and chiefs grow rich on it.
3. **Chapter 7, Sub-topic 7.1: Slavery and Slave Trade in East Africa** — Domestic slavery was already in the family culture when Arab and Persian traders made it a commerce; slaves carry the ivory to the coast and are sold themselves; the Masai fight the traders off, but families are broken and whole districts depopulated.
4. **Chapter 7: Local and External Trade Contacts** — By the early 19th century about 30,000 people a year are sold from eastern Africa through Zanzibar, until Britain deliberately abolishes the trade — and the chapter closes by asking about the slave trade of today.
5. **Chapter 7 (guide): Slave trade and its abolition** — Arabs and Persians turn slavery into a business around 1000 AD; abolition then fails at sea — the Indian Ocean is too large to patrol, funds and manpower too short, the interior feared.
6. **Term 1; Lesson 4: Slave Trade in East Africa** — Slavery is old in Africa before the colonialists; the trade that grows on it interrupts East Africa's social and economic development, even as a merchant class and new beliefs — Christianity and expanding Islam — come with it.
7. **Term 2 (West Africa), Topic: Trans-Saharan Trade in West Africa** — Camels and horses multiply the loads of gold and kola nuts northbound, salt and silk southbound; the Arab conquest of North Africa in 640 and 660 AD pushes the Berbers into the trade, and a crossing takes three months.
