# The 1900 Buganda Agreement and Uganda's Boundaries

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 8: How Uganda Became a Nation

The 1900 Buganda Agreement settles land, governance and gun and hut taxes; the 1901 Ankole Agreement enlarges Ankole, and Uganda's borders are trimmed piece by piece — the north-east to Kenya in 1914, the Lake Albert shore to Belgian Congo in 1910.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — SST Term 2: How Uganda became a nation

Before the treaties, the British form the Imperial British East Africa Company; then agreements are signed with the kings of Buganda, Ankole, Bunyoro and Toro, one kingdom at a time.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 4: The agreements

The 1900 Buganda Agreement is signed by Johnston and Apollo Kaggwa while Kabaka Mwanga sits exiled in the Seychelles; Bunyoro's follows in 1933, Ankole's dates to 1901.

## Sub lesson 4 — Chapter 8: Methods of colonisation

Where it is peaceful, the treaty does the work — Buganda 1900, Toro 1900, Ankole 1901 — signed with promises of protection from Bunyoro.

## Sub lesson 5 — Chapter 8: The 1900 Buganda Agreement, case study of the peaceful method

Johnston signs with the three regents of the child Kabaka Daudi Chwa II — Kagwa, Mugwanya, Kisingiri; Buganda expects British guns against Bunyoro and Egypt, and the Kabaka keeps his throne as "His Highness" — under close British supervision.

## Sub lesson 6 — Term 2; Lesson 10: The Buganda Agreement

The agreement exists in two versions, English and Luganda — and where they conflict, the English one rules; it is also signed to settle Buganda's boundary quarrel with Bunyoro after Kabalega's rebellion.
