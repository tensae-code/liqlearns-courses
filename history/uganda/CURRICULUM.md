# History — Uganda course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Uganda inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation. The path follows Uganda's own books' progression as filed — the placements file's book order is the teaching order, earliest books first — grouped into levels by the turns of Uganda's own story as its books present them: the district's past collected from the elders first, then the national march in the Primary 5 books, then East Africa whole in Primary 6, then the same story with causes at Senior One, then outside pressures and replies at Senior Two, and last Southern and West Africa at Senior Three. A lesson sits at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; later books deepen lessons already placed, in file order inside each lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Uganda's own Social Studies and History and Political Education books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book contents maps, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — Beginnings at Home: Legends, Peoples and Kingdoms of Uganda** — Uganda's Primary 4 and Primary 5 books (Books 1–3 as filed): history enters through the elders — origin legends, tribes and clans — then the national story as a straight march: stone-age man, the ethnic migrations, the kingdoms, Arabs, explorers, missionaries, company, protectorate, agreements, LEGCO, parties and independence, retold in the COVID home-study twin from the kitchen table. 29 lessons.
2. **Level 2 — East Africa Whole: Migrations, Kingdoms, Trade and Resistance** — Uganda's Primary 6 books (Books 4–5 as filed): the lens widens from Uganda to all East Africa — one shared past of migrations, kingdoms, coastal trade and rebellions, told country by country but always as one region's story, closing with the East African Community itself as a history lesson. 18 lessons.
3. **Level 3 — East Africa with Causes: State Formation, Religions, Trade and Colonisation** — Uganda's Senior One books and their teacher's guide (Books 6–8 as filed): the same East African story with causes and consequences — migrations explained by pasture, water and population pressure, state formation in two forms, religions and their wars, long-distance trade, colonisation by treaty and by force, and the replies East Africans made, with the guide's added content as labelled evidence. 5 lessons.
4. **Level 4 — Pressures and Replies: Egypt, Treaties, the Railway and Colonial Rule** — Uganda's Senior Two book (Book 9 as filed): pressures from outside — Egypt pushing down the Nile, explorers whose treaties become title deeds, and the railway — and the replies, from the Nandi's spears to the Maji-Maji water, with federation's early failures. 3 lessons.
5. **Level 5 — Southern and West Africa: The Shared Continental Past** — Uganda's Senior Three book (Book 10 as filed): Uganda's learners leave East Africa entirely — Shaka and Moshesh, the Trek and the Mfecane, then Ghana, Mali, Asante and Samori Toure, the trans-Atlantic trade, abolition, the jihads and Nkrumah — the shared African past the continent's classrooms all teach. 22 lessons.

**Totals: 77 lessons, 170 placements** (Level 1: 29, Level 2: 18, Level 3: 5, Level 4: 3, Level 5: 22).
