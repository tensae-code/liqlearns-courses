# About the History — Uganda course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Uganda's own Social Studies and History and Political Education books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Uganda inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **five levels**, derived from Uganda's own books' progression as filed (the placements file's book order is the teaching order, earliest books first), grouped by the turns of Uganda's own story as its books present them. A lesson sits at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; later books deepen lessons already placed, in file order inside each lesson:

1. **Level 1 — Beginnings at Home: Legends, Peoples and Kingdoms of Uganda.** Uganda's Primary 4 and Primary 5 books (Books 1–3 as filed): history enters through the elders — origin legends, tribes and clans — then the national story as a straight march: stone-age man, the ethnic migrations, the kingdoms, Arabs, explorers, missionaries, company, protectorate, agreements, LEGCO, parties and independence. 29 lessons.
2. **Level 2 — East Africa Whole: Migrations, Kingdoms, Trade and Resistance.** Uganda's Primary 6 books (Books 4–5 as filed): the lens widens from Uganda to all East Africa — one shared past told country by country but always as one region's story, closing with the East African Community itself as a history lesson. 18 lessons.
3. **Level 3 — East Africa with Causes: State Formation, Religions, Trade and Colonisation.** Uganda's Senior One books and their teacher's guide (Books 6–8 as filed): the same East African story with causes and consequences, the guide's added content as labelled evidence. 5 lessons first appear here; the Senior One books deepen many Level 1–2 lessons.
4. **Level 4 — Pressures and Replies: Egypt, Treaties, the Railway and Colonial Rule.** Uganda's Senior Two book (Book 9 as filed): Egypt pushing down the Nile, explorers whose treaties become title deeds, the railway, and the replies East Africans made. 3 lessons first appear here.
5. **Level 5 — Southern and West Africa: The Shared Continental Past.** Uganda's Senior Three book (Book 10 as filed): Shaka and Moshesh, the Trek and the Mfecane, then Ghana, Mali, Asante and Samori Toure, abolition, the jihads and Nkrumah. 22 lessons.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Beginnings, the region, the causes, the replies, the continent:* "First the elders' legends and the peoples of Uganda, and the kingdoms they built. Then East Africa whole — migrations, trade and resistance as one region's story. Then the same story with its causes. Then the pressures from outside and the replies. Last, the whole continent's shared past — Southern and West Africa as Uganda's own classrooms teach them."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Book 1 (Primary 4 home-study)** — history enters through the elders: the child is sent to ask grandparents for the legends, the tribe's first home and the year the family settled, so the district's past is collected as oral testimony before it is ever a textbook fact. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 2 (Excel Primary 5)** — a commercial pupil's book that teaches the national story as a straight march — stone-age man, the ethnic migrations, the kingdoms, then Arabs, explorers, missionaries, company, protectorate, agreements, LEGCO, parties, independence — with a named hero, villain or martyr at nearly every step. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 3 (Primary 5 merged home-study)** — the COVID home-study version of the same national story, compressed into self-study steps: the child maps the ethnic groups' arrows of entry, lists the agreements, and names the four independence heroes (Musaazi, Kiwanuka, Obote, Muteesa II) from the kitchen table. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 4 (Excel Primary 6)** — P6 widens the lens from Uganda to all East Africa: one shared past of migrations, kingdoms, coastal trade and rebellions, told country by country but always as one region's story, ending with the East African Community itself as a history lesson. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 5 (Primary 6 home-study)** — the home-study twin of the Excel P6 course: the same East African past, but the child must ask parents, grandparents and elders for the clan names, the gods' names and the products the foreigners brought. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 6 (Senior 1 home-study)** — at Senior One the story gains causes and consequences: migrations explained by pasture, water and population pressure; resistance explained, and its failure explained too, down to the Jumbe Magoya who betrayed the hiding places. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 7 (Senior One textbook, PROTOTYPE)** — the new lower-secondary curriculum teaches East Africa whole: every chapter opens with a story or a village problem (a banana plantation dug up for a hall reveals buried artefacts) and ends by making the learner solve a present-day situation with the past. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 8 (Senior One teacher's guide, PROTOTYPE)** — the guide tells the teacher to teach the past by storytelling, field trips and guided discovery, and keeps tying each chapter to the learner's own present, down to comparing the Bantu migrations with today's labour migration to the Middle East. *(Teacher's guide — evidence only.)*
- **Book 9 (Senior Two home-study)** — Senior Two turns to pressures from outside — Egypt pushing down the Nile, explorers whose treaties become title deeds, and the railway that kills the Nandi's Orkoiyot — and to the replies East Africans made, from the Nandi's spears to the Maji-Maji water. *(Student book.)*
- **Book 10 (Senior Three home-study)** — Senior Three leaves East Africa entirely: Uganda's learners study Shaka and Moshesh, the Trek and the Mfecane, then Ghana, Mali, Asante and Samori Toure, as the shared African past their own continent's classrooms all teach. *(Student book.)*
