# Lesson plan — From Stone Tools to Farming

**Course:** History — Uganda · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From Stone Tools to Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Beginnings at Home: Legends, Peoples and Kingdoms of Uganda (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Middle Stone Age man lives in caves, roasts meat, hunts with traps, sleeps under animal skins and tames the first animal, the dog.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Beginnings at Home: Legends, Peoples and Kingdoms of Uganda — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 6: The People of Pre-Colonial Uganda — early man** — Middle Stone Age man lives in caves, roasts meat, hunts with traps, sleeps under animal skins and tames the first animal, the dog.
2. **Term 1: The Stone Age** — Stone-age man hunts, gathers fruit, fishes and collects wild honey; his tools name his age, and the Leakeys' 1959 skull at Olduvai Gorge makes East Africa the classroom's cradle of mankind.
3. **Chapter 2: The Origin of Man** — Dogs are the first animals kept at home, for hunting and cave protection; then come advanced tools and pottery — the Stone Age told as a series of firsts.
4. **Chapter 2 (guide): Stages of man's development** — Fire is invented, then domesticated plants lead to agriculture; the model answers send learners to Olduvai Gorge and to the elders outside school.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Uganda and East Africa you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
