# Buganda Kingdom and the Kintu Legend

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Topic 6: Buganda kingdom; Term 1: Growth of Buganda kingdom; Term 2: Buganda Kingdom, how it was founded; Chapter 5: Buganda Kingdom. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 6: Buganda kingdom

The Kabaka holds absolute power under crown, stool, spear and drum; Buganda grows stronger through the 1700s, and the legend of Kintu, first Muganda, teaches the kingdom's morals — later kabakas run from infant Daudi Chwa of the 1900 Agreement to Muteesa II, who refused an East African federation.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Term 1: Growth of Buganda kingdom

Strong leaders, unity, a standing army and a strategic shore on Lake Victoria make 18th-century Buganda expand.

## Sub lesson 3 — Term 2: Buganda Kingdom, how it was founded

The Baganda divide into 52 clans named for animal, plant and bird totems, and believe in ancestral gods — Musoke, Dungu, Kibuuka, Mukasa.

## Sub lesson 4 — Chapter 5: Buganda Kingdom

Kyabagu, Junju and Suna unite a small kingdom and restructure it — bataka clan chiefs guard the land, collect taxes, conscript the army and present page boys to the Kabaka, while the Balubaale spirits are consulted through mediums.
