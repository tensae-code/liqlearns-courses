# Video script — Buganda Kingdom and the Kintu Legend

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Kabaka holds absolute power under crown, stool, spear and drum; Buganda grows stronger through the 1700s, and the legend of Kintu, first Muganda, teaches the kingdom's morals — later kabakas run from infant Daudi Chwa of the 1900 Agreement to Muteesa II, who refused an East African federation.

On the map, follow this part: Term 1: Growth of Buganda kingdom. Strong leaders, unity, a standing army and a strategic shore on Lake Victoria make 18th-century Buganda expand.

On the map, follow this part: Term 2: Buganda Kingdom, how it was founded. The Baganda divide into 52 clans named for animal, plant and bird totems, and believe in ancestral gods — Musoke, Dungu, Kibuuka, Mukasa.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 5: Buganda Kingdom. Kyabagu, Junju and Suna unite a small kingdom and restructure it — bataka clan chiefs guard the land, collect taxes, conscript the army and present page boys to the Kabaka, while the Balubaale spirits are consulted through mediums.
