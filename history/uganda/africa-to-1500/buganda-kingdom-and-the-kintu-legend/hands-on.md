# Hands-on — Buganda Kingdom and the Kintu Legend

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a simple map or timeline for this story: mark where it happens, put the parts in order, and label the turn. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Kabaka holds absolute power under crown, stool, spear and drum; Buganda grows stronger through the 1700s, and the legend of Kintu, first Muganda, teaches the kingdom's morals — later kabakas run from infant Daudi Chwa of the 1900 Agreement to Muteesa II, who refused an East African federation. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act the story with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — a place name, a building, a story an elder tells, a date on a calendar. Each time you meet it, note it: what it was, and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
