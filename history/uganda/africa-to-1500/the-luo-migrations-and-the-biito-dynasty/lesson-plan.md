# Lesson plan — The Luo Migrations and the Biito Dynasty

**Course:** History — Uganda · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** The Luo Migrations and the Biito Dynasty
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Beginnings at Home: Legends, Peoples and Kingdoms of Uganda (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Luo speakers migrate from Bahr-el-Ghazal in South Sudan to the lakes, bringing millet, sorghum and sweet potatoes, founding the Luo-Biito dynasty — and displacing those they find.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Beginnings at Home: Legends, Peoples and Kingdoms of Uganda — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 6: The River-Lake Nilotes** — Luo speakers migrate from Bahr-el-Ghazal in South Sudan to the lakes, bringing millet, sorghum and sweet potatoes, founding the Luo-Biito dynasty — and displacing those they find.
2. **Term 1: The River-Lake Nilotes** — The Luo speakers leave Bahr-el-Ghazal in South Sudan and settle by the lakes and rivers that name them.
3. **Chapter 3: The Luo** — The River-Lake Nilotes come from Bahr el Gazel in Southern Sudan, pastoralists whose family fills the map — Dinka, Alur, Luo, Shilluk, Bor, Anuak, Acholi.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Uganda and East Africa you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
