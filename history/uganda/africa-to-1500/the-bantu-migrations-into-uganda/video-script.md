# Video script — The Bantu Migrations into Uganda

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Bantu peoples settle the lake region and the mountain slopes — Bakonzo on Rwenzori, Bafumbira and Bakiga on Mufumbiro, Bagisu on Elgon.

On the map, follow this part: Topic 6: Major Ethnic Groups — the Bantu. Bantu peoples settle the lake region and the mountain slopes — Bakonzo on Rwenzori, Bafumbira and Bakiga on Mufumbiro, Bagisu on Elgon.

On the map, follow this part: SST Term 2; Lesson 2: The origin of different Ethnic groups in Uganda. Each group is fixed by work and place — Bantu farmers in the interlacustrine region, Luo cattle-keepers and fishermen, Plain Nilotes in the semi-arid north, Highland Nilotes on Elgon's slopes.

On the map, follow this part: Term 1: The Bantu. The largest people of East Africa are named by their own word — the "ntu" in their languages — farmers out of the Cameroon highlands: Baganda largest in Uganda, Kikuyu in Kenya, Sukuma in Tanzania, Hutu in Rwanda and Burundi.

On the map, follow this part: Term 2: Ethnic groups and their origins. The matching exercise pairs each people with its homeland — Bantu to the Cameroon Highlands — farmers whose languages carry "NTU" and who settle the interlacustrine region.

On the map, follow this part: Topic: Migration and Settlement into East Africa since 1000 AD; Lesson 1: Bantu Migrations into East Africa. The Bantu leave southern West Africa for pasture, water and room after population explosion; they bring irrigation, mixed farming, short-horned cattle, the assegai and cow-horn formation, barter — and the "Ntu" languages.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 3: Migration and Settlement into East Africa since 1000 AD. Children of the cradle land, loving adventure, move out as numbers of people and animals grow — Mukasa enters between Lake Albert and Lake Edward — and the class builds a timeline for every group's arrival.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 3 (guide): Migration and Settlement. The guide's closing task makes the young historian compare the causes of the ancient migrations with the reasons Ugandans today depart for work in the Middle East.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Then go and do the lesson.
