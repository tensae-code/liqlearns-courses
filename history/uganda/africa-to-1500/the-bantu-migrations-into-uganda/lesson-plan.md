## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 6: Major Ethnic Groups — the Bantu** — Bantu peoples settle the lake region and the mountain slopes — Bakonzo on Rwenzori, Bafumbira and Bakiga on Mufumbiro, Bagisu on Elgon.
2. **SST Term 2; Lesson 2: The origin of different Ethnic groups in Uganda** — Each group is fixed by work and place — Bantu farmers in the interlacustrine region, Luo cattle-keepers and fishermen, Plain Nilotes in the semi-arid north, Highland Nilotes on Elgon's slopes.
3. **Term 1: The Bantu** — The largest people of East Africa are named by their own word — the "ntu" in their languages — farmers out of the Cameroon highlands: Baganda largest in Uganda, Kikuyu in Kenya, Sukuma in Tanzania, Hutu in Rwanda and Burundi.
4. **Term 2: Ethnic groups and their origins** — The matching exercise pairs each people with its homeland — Bantu to the Cameroon Highlands — farmers whose languages carry "NTU" and who settle the interlacustrine region.
5. **Topic: Migration and Settlement into East Africa since 1000 AD; Lesson 1: Bantu Migrations into East Africa** — The Bantu leave southern West Africa for pasture, water and room after population explosion; they bring irrigation, mixed farming, short-horned cattle, the assegai and cow-horn formation, barter — and the "Ntu" languages.
6. **Chapter 3: Migration and Settlement into East Africa since 1000 AD** — Children of the cradle land, loving adventure, move out as numbers of people and animals grow — Mukasa enters between Lake Albert and Lake Edward — and the class builds a timeline for every group's arrival.
7. **Chapter 3 (guide): Migration and Settlement** — The guide's closing task makes the young historian compare the causes of the ancient migrations with the reasons Ugandans today depart for work in the Middle East.
