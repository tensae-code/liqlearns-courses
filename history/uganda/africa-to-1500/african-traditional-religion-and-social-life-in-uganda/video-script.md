# Video script — African Traditional Religion and Social Life in Uganda

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Before the foreign religions, each society names God its own way — Katonda for the Baganda, Rugaba for the Banyoro, Ruhanga for the Banyankole, Lokusuban for the Iteso — and clan ceremonies, taboos and traditional education hold the people together.

On the map, follow this part: Topic 6: Social Organisation. Before the foreign religions, each society names God its own way — Katonda for the Baganda, Rugaba for the Banyoro, Ruhanga for the Banyankole, Lokusuban for the Iteso — and clan ceremonies, taboos and traditional education hold the people together.

On the map, follow this part: Term 1: Culture and religion. One God, many names on the classroom table — Katonda for Buganda, Ruhanga for Ankole, Were for Bugishu, Ngai for the Kikuyu.

On the map, follow this part: Term 2: Culture and religions of East Africa. The child is sent to the elders to name the religions and Christian sects of East Africa and to weigh how western culture is changing the old ways.

On the map, follow this part: Topic: Culture and Ethnic Groupings in East Africa. Culture is taught at family level — values, rituals, acceptable and unacceptable behaviour — and Buganda's Ekisaakaate, the Nabagereka's holiday camp, coaches the young in the old values.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 6: Religions in East Africa. In traditional African religion the ancestors are present at every major event — wedding, birth, death — and at the small ones too, a job gained, a degree finished.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 6 (guide): Traditional religions. The chapter opens at the Nakayima tree in Mubende and a traditional shrine in Nyamwezi — living shrines, not museum pieces.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Then go and do the lesson.
