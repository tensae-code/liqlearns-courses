## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 6: Economic Organisation of Pre-Colonial Uganda** — Peoples organise in trade communities by their traditional work — cultivation, pastoralism, fishing — barter first, then the Arabs bring cowrie shells from the Maldives and trade turns monetary.
2. **Term 1: Long-distance trade and the Zenj empire** — Caravans move together against wild animals and bandits; Baganda, Nyamwezi, Kamba and Yao trade ivory, hoes, salt and honey by barter until cowrie shells arrive.
3. **Chapter 7, Sub-topic 7.2: Long-Distance Trade** — Guns make the caravan routes safe, women farm while men trade, and the trade's dark twin — the slave trade inside it — hands the colonialists their excuse: abolition.
4. **Chapter 7: Long-distance trade** — Guns, silk, swords and clothes from Europe and India are exchanged for slaves, ivory, gold and skins, and the Bantu tribes control the road between the interior and the coast.
5. **Chapter 7 (guide): Collapse of long-distance trade** — The great trade dies with its great traders — Nyungu ya Mawe, Muteesa I and the chiefs — and with the intertribal wars.
