# How Africa's Regions Traded with Each Other

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 6: Economic Organisation of Pre-Colonial Uganda

Peoples organise in trade communities by their traditional work — cultivation, pastoralism, fishing — barter first, then the Arabs bring cowrie shells from the Maldives and trade turns monetary.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Term 1: Long-distance trade and the Zenj empire

Caravans move together against wild animals and bandits; Baganda, Nyamwezi, Kamba and Yao trade ivory, hoes, salt and honey by barter until cowrie shells arrive.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 7, Sub-topic 7.2: Long-Distance Trade

Guns make the caravan routes safe, women farm while men trade, and the trade's dark twin — the slave trade inside it — hands the colonialists their excuse: abolition.

## Sub lesson 4 — Chapter 7: Long-distance trade

Guns, silk, swords and clothes from Europe and India are exchanged for slaves, ivory, gold and skins, and the Bantu tribes control the road between the interior and the coast.
