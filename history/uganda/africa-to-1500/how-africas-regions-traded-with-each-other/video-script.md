# Video script — How Africa's Regions Traded with Each Other

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

On the map, follow this part: Term 1: Long-distance trade and the Zenj empire. Caravans move together against wild animals and bandits; Baganda, Nyamwezi, Kamba and Yao trade ivory, hoes, salt and honey by barter until cowrie shells arrive.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 7, Sub-topic 7.2: Long-Distance Trade. Guns make the caravan routes safe, women farm while men trade, and the trade's dark twin — the slave trade inside it — hands the colonialists their excuse: abolition.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 7: Long-distance trade. Guns, silk, swords and clothes from Europe and India are exchanged for slaves, ivory, gold and skins, and the Bantu tribes control the road between the interior and the coast.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 7 (guide): Collapse of long-distance trade. The great trade dies with its great traders — Nyungu ya Mawe, Muteesa I and the chiefs — and with the intertribal wars.
