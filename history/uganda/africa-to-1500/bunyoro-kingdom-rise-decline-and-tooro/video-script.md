# Video script — Bunyoro Kingdom: Rise, Decline and Tooro

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Bunyoro, once the region's great power, declines because it is too big for one ruler, torn by internal conflict and pressed by rising neighbours — Tooro and Buganda — before the colonialists arrive.

On the map, follow this part: Topic 6: Bunyoro and Tooro kingdoms. Bunyoro, once the region's great power, declines because it is too big for one ruler, torn by internal conflict and pressed by rising neighbours — Tooro and Buganda — before the colonialists arrive.

On the map, follow this part: Term 1: Bunyoro kingdom. Bunyoro grows under Omukama Kamurasi, and his son Kabalega inherits a kingdom whose districts the book maps onto today's Hoima, Masindi and Buliisa.

On the map, follow this part: Term 2: Bunyoro shrinks. Bunyoro Kingdom is reduced in size, and Toro breaks away from it — the old empire's map redrawn by secession.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 5, Topic: State Formation in East Africa; Lesson 1: Bunyoro Kingdom. The Batembuzi demi-gods vanish around 1200 AD, the Chwezi follow, and a centralised kingdom grows under the hereditary Omukama — provinces, tax-collecting chiefs — so strong it resists colonisation until Kabaleega is forced into asylum in 1896.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Then go and do the lesson.
