# Video script — Kingdoms of East Africa: Centralised and Decentralised

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Peoples ruled by kings live under centralised monarchy, those under chiefs under a decentralised system; every kingdom has one hereditary king, special regalia, classes, and royal tombs for its dead kings.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 5: State Formation in East Africa. The decentralised peoples — Maasai, Chagga, Kikuyu, Nyamwezi, Acholi — move youths through age grades by initiation at about 18, while the Nyamwezi ntemi rules through gunguli leaders who collect tribute, farm his fields and raise his armies.

On the map, follow this part: Chapter 5 (guide): State Formation in East Africa. The guide frames the whole chapter as two forms of society, centralised and non-centralised, whose organisational features the learner must be able to explain.
