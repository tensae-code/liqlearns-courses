# About the History — Laos course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Laos's own History books for the secondary years. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Laos inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Laos's books tell one long story twice: the lower-secondary years tell it first, and the upper-secondary years tell it again at greater depth. The ladder follows that progression — seven levels, one per year-arc of the books, earliest first. A lesson stands at the level of the earliest arc that teaches it; every later telling of the same story is stocked into the same lesson, so a lesson's sources may carry tellings from several books. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

The year opens, in the earliest book, with world prehistory told as stages of society (primitive, slave-owning, the ancient East), then turns to Laos itself — archaeology and the old mueang, Fa Ngum's unification, Lan Xang's rise, fall and resistance — closing with the pupils' own local archaeology, the Plain of Jars as the worked example. The next year tells world history as the march of systems — feudalism, capitalism, the workers' movement, imperialism, 1917 — and turns the same lens on Laos: France seizes the country and rules it 1893–1945, and the Lao answer region by region. The interwar year frames Versailles, the USSR, fascism and the Second World War, and inside it gives Laos's 1930–1954 story two full parts. The post-1945 year surveys blocs, decolonisation and the scientific-technical revolution, then Laos's thirty years of war and revolution 1954–1975 and the building of the Lao PDR — ending, unusually, with the country's heritage sites taught through tourism. The upper-secondary years then restart the whole story at greater depth: Stone Age to feudal Asia, Laos from its archaeology to Lan Xang's golden age; bourgeois revolutions and imperialism, then Laos lesson by lesson from Lan Xang's division (1695) through Siamese rule, French rule and the first independence declaration of 1945; and finally the world wars, the Cold War and 1991, Laos's thirty-year struggle 1945–1975 in the closest detail of the whole series, renovation-era Laos, and the history of Lao tourism. Every year closes by sending pupils back to their own locality — its sites, objects and elders — to write its history themselves.
