# Hands-on — Western Powers Come to the Malay World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three things your town sells to the world and three things a faraway factory country might want from it. The wanting is the engine of this lesson — now read who comes, and why.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The West comes to a prosperous country — strategic ports trading minerals, fertile soil and sea produce, with a flourishing intellectual life — because industrial revolutions demand markets and raw materials, the Suez Canal and steamship shorten the road east, and the Western powers race one another with trade, treaty and force. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
