The Bangkok Treaty of 1909 splits the northern Malay states between Siam and Britain. The answers differ by state. Terengganu's sultan, ulama and chiefs reject the treaty outright; Kelantan's Sultan Muhammad IV hears the news from the British officer J.S. Mason. Johor answers differently: Sultan Abu Bakar, with a capable and loyal Majlis Jemaah Menteri, continues Temenggong Daeng Ibrahim's modernisation of administration, finance and law — precisely to leave Britain no opening to intervene. Wisdom, in this chapter, is strategy.
