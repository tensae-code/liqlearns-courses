# Malaya's Road to Merdeka: Elections, Reid and 1957

## Do it first

Climb stairs with your finger on paper: municipal, state, federal elections; then a commission writing; then a stadium in the rain. Independence in this lesson is climbed, not granted in one step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bab 7: the machinery of independence

Independence is machined before it is declared. The Communities Liaison Committee draws the races' leaders together. The Member System (Sistem Ahli) apprentices local leaders in government. A national education system is designed. And parties multiply — KMM, PKMM, API, UMNO, MCA, MIC, Hizbul Muslimin, PAS and the IMP among them. The road to Merdeka runs through committees and parties as much as through protests.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bab 8: the first general election

Elections climb from municipal to state to federal level. The 1955 general election — proclaimed by Sir Donald MacGillivray — fills the Federal Legislative Council, and Tunku Abdul Rahman becomes Chief Minister at the head of the first cabinet. Self-government is now practised by elected Malaysians, two years before it is proclaimed.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bab 9–10: Reid, the Agreement, and Merdeka

At Lancaster House, London (18 January–6 February 1956), the Rulers' representatives and the Alliance win agreement on self-government. The Reid Commission — announced 8 March 1956, at work by late June, receiving 131 written memoranda — drafts the constitution. And after the midnight lowering of the Union Jack at Padang Kelab Selangor, some 20,000 people stand in the rain from 6 a.m. at Stadium Merdeka on 31 August 1957 to hear independence proclaimed — the First Malaya Plan 1956–1960 already charting the economy.
