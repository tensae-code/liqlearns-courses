# Colonialism and Its End

## Do it first

Draw two circles: a mine-and-estate town with wages and shops, and a village with its own fields. Draw the gap between them and label what crosses it and what does not. That gap is this lesson's warning.

What does Western rule actually change? The book's answer is the economy and the society itself. The West introduces a modern economy — large-scale tin mining and estate agriculture — and with it a transformed society: immigrant labour, new towns, a money economy, and widening gaps between the modern sector and the village world. Administration is the surface; transformation is the depth.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Western administration transforms the land: a modern economy of large-scale tin mining and estate agriculture brings immigrant labour, new towns and a money economy — and a widening gap between the modern sector and the village world. Give one real example: large-scale tin mining and estate agriculture are the twin engines of the new economy, and name the lesson's key words: modern economy, tin mining, estate agriculture, immigrant labour, money economy, plural society. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
