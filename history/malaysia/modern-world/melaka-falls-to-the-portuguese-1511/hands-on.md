# Hands-on — Melaka Falls to the Portuguese, 1511

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw Melaka, then draw an arrow retreating from it to Bentan, and three arrows striking back. The arrows that fail are still part of the story — this lesson is about a fall that does not end a line of kings.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In 1511 the Portuguese take Melaka; Sultan Mahmud Shah retreats through his dependencies to Bentan, attacks three times between 1515 and 1519 to win it back, and withdraws to Kampar in 1526 — the sultanate ends in Melaka but not in exile. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
