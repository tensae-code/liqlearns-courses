# Northern Malay Kingdoms and Adat Perpatih

## Do it first

Draw your family line two ways: down through fathers, then down through mothers. Negeri Sembilan's Adat Perpatih in this lesson follows the mother's line — say one thing that changes when inheritance follows mothers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bab 8–9.1: Kedah, Kelantan and Perlis and their heritage

The northern kingdoms deal with their Malay neighbours and leave a living heritage. Kedah keeps its war poem, the Syair Sultan Maulana, from the reign of Sultan Ahmad Tajuddin Halim Shah II (1804–1845). Kelantan keeps the Hikayat Seri Kelantan (1914), its wau kites and its batik — and wayang kulit, shadow play, raised to national heritage. Heritage here is not in a museum only; it is flown, worn and performed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bab 9.2: Negeri Sembilan's Adat Perpatih

Minangkabau migrants settle Rembau, Sungai Ujong, Johol and Naning, bringing Adat Perpatih — the matrilineal custom. Descent and inheritance run through the mother; a husband joins his wife's suku as orang semenda. The people — Perut, Suku and Luak — appoint their adat chiefs, the Ibu Soko, Buapak and Lembaga, whose four Undang elect the Yamtuan Besar. A whole state ordered through its mothers and its custom chiefs.
