# Hands-on — Japanese Occupation of Malaya, 1942–1945

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write two columns: 'what the occupier imposes' and 'how the people answer'. Fill both from this lesson — occupation is only half its story; resistance is the other half.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Japan occupies the country and rules by Nippon Seishin — English and Chinese schools closed, Japanese imposed, the Emperor revered — while the people fight back through Force 136 and heroes like Gurchan Singh and Sybil Kathigasu, until the atomic bombs of 6 and 9 August 1945 end the occupation. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
