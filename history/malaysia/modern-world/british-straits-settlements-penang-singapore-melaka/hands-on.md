# Hands-on — British Straits Settlements: Penang, Singapore and Melaka

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line down a map of the Malay world. Label one side British, one Dutch, the year 1824 on the line. One treaty line remakes a whole region in this lesson — draw it before you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Raffles and Farquhar land in Singapore in January 1819 and sign a preliminary treaty with Temenggung Abdul Rahman — who insists it needs Sultan Abdul Rahman's ratification — and the London Treaty of 1824 splits the Malay world into British and Dutch spheres, joining Penang, Singapore and Melaka as the Straits Settlements. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
