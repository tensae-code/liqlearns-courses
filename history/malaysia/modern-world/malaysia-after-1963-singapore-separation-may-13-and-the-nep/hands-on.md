# Hands-on — Malaysia After 1963: Singapore Separation, May 13 and the NEP

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a house after a storm: one wall gone (Singapore), cracks inside (insurgency, riots), then scaffolding — a unity charter, schools, an economic plan. Malaysia after 1963 in this lesson is damage, then rebuilding.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Malaysia is stress-tested after 1963 — Singapore separates in August 1965, the communist insurgency renews in Sarawak and the peninsula, and the race riots of 13 May 1969 bring MAGERAN's emergency rule — then rebuilt through the Rukun Negara, education re-knit around Bahasa Melayu, and the New Economic Policy (1970–1990) against poverty and imbalance. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
