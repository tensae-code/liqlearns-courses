# Video script — Malaysia After 1963: Singapore Separation, May 13 and the NEP

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Malaysia is stress-tested after 1963 — Singapore separates in August 1965, the communist insurgency renews in Sarawak and the peninsula, and the race riots of 13 May 1969 bring MAGERAN's emergency rule — then rebuilt through the Rukun Negara, education re-knit around Bahasa Melayu, and the New Economic Policy (1970–1990) against poverty and imbalance.

The federation's first great change is a loss. Tun Abdul Razak's negotiations with Lee Kuan Yew fail across 29 June–1 July 1965. The Tunku directs Razak to settle separation with Dr. Ismail Abdul Rahman, Tan Siew Sin and V.T. Sambanthan in early August 1965 — and Singapore leaves Malaysia.
The communist threat does not end with the first Emergency. In Sarawak the army's operations — Hammer (1965), RASCOM's and Sri Aman among them — break the communist movement division by division, while the peninsula faces its own renewed insurgency. The second insurgency is fought on two fronts, Borneo and peninsula together.
On 13 May 1969, race riots force an emergency. The Yang di-Pertuan Agong consents to the National Operations Council — MAGERAN — under Tun Abdul Razak Hussein, which governs from 16 May 1969 to 23 February 1971 to restore order. The tragedy is named plainly in the book: an inter-ethnic tragedy, answered by emergency government until order returns.
Rebuilding follows. The Rukun Negara is proclaimed as the nation's unity charter. Education is re-knit — the Cabinet Committee of September 1974 reviews the national system with Bahasa Melayu at its centre. And the New Economic Policy (Dasar Ekonomi Baru, 1970–1990), launched through the First Outline Perspective Plan, sets out to eradicate poverty and restructure society's economic imbalances. Unity chartered, schools re-knit, economy rebalanced: that is the rebuilding's three strands.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Malaysia.
