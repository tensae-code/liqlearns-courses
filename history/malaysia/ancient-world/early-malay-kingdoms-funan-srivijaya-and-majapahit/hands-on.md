# Hands-on — Early Malay Kingdoms: Funan, Srivijaya and Majapahit

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a page, draw a river running to the sea. Dot five towns along the coasts and straits and give each a kingdom's name from this lesson. Trade boats between them: that network is the early Malay world.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From the first centuries, kingdoms rise across the Malay world — Funan first, then Kedah Tua, Srivijaya, Mataram and Majapahit among many — trading spices, tin and gold from river-mouth ports, exchanging envoys with China and India, and leaving Hindu-Buddhist monuments and one shared civilisation. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
