# Lesson plan — Early Malay Kingdoms: Funan, Srivijaya and Majapahit

**Course:** History — Malaysia · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Early Malay Kingdoms: Funan, Srivijaya and Majapahit
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Malay Kingdoms and the Melaka Sultanate (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From the first centuries, kingdoms rise across the Malay world — Funan first, then Kedah Tua, Srivijaya, Mataram and Majapahit among many — trading spices, tin and gold from river-mouth ports, exchanging envoys with China and India, and leaving Hindu-Buddhist monuments and one shared civilisation.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Malay Kingdoms and the Melaka Sultanate (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 7 Kerajaan Melayu Awal: the early Malay kingdoms** — From the first centuries, kingdoms rise across the Malay world.
2. **Bab 1: the famous kingdoms of Alam Melayu** — The Form 2 book opens the heritage year with the famous kingdoms of Alam Melayu.
3. **Bab 1: Majapahit, Kedah Tua and Gangga Nagara** — Majapahit is founded in 1294 by Raden Vijaya with Mongol help, rules from Trowulan, and is steadied by Patih Gajah Mada under Queen Tribhuwana Tunggadewi.
4. **Bab 2–4: how the Alam Melayu kingdoms lived** — Beneath the many crowns lies one civilisation in many kingdoms: the Malay language and its writing, a literature of hikayat and syair, candi architecture, a ranked social structure, and tin-gold-spice economies run from river-mouth ports.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
