# Ancient Civilisations: Rome

## Do it first

Build the tallest tower you can from books or blocks, then say one thing a strong builder needs besides strength. Rome in this lesson pairs power with building — and with Greek ideas taken in.

Rome begins by learning: it inherits Greek free thought. Then it fights — defeating its rivals until it stands as the Mediterranean's strongest power. And it builds on a scale that outlasts its armies: the Colosseum above all, the great arena that still defines classical Europe. Thought inherited, rivals defeated, stone raised — that is Rome's three-part story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rome inherits Greek free thought, defeats its rivals to become the Mediterranean's strongest power, and builds on a scale — the Colosseum above all — that still defines classical Europe. Give one real example: the Colosseum still stands in Rome as the scale-mark of Rome's building, and name the lesson's key words: Rome, Colosseum, Mediterranean, Greek free thought, classical Europe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
