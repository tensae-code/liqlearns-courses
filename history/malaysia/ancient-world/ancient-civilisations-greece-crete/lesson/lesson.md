# Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

## Do it first

Ask one question about your own life today — why do I do this, is it right — and write it down. Asking that kind of question is the examined life Greece leaves behind in this lesson.

Between 1000 and 800 BC, Greece sparks Europe's development. Its bequest is named plainly: democracy, science, and a model of the examined life — a life that asks questions about itself. Small cities around a sea thus set a pattern the whole of Europe, and then the world, inherits.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between 1000 and 800 BC, Greece sparks Europe's development and bequeaths democracy, science and a model of the examined life to every generation after it. Give one real example: Greece's gifts named in the lesson are democracy, science and the examined life — ideas still in daily use, and name the lesson's key words: Greece, democracy, science, the examined life, Europe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
