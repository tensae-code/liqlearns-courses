# Hands-on — The Melaka Sultanate: Parameswara to Hang Tuah

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Act the founding sign with your hands: a tiny mousedeer, a big dog, a splash. Then say why a small creature beating a bigger one marked the lucky spot. That sign begins Melaka in this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Melaka is founded by the Palembang prince Parameswara at the Bertam river — where a white mousedeer kicks his hunting dog into the water — and grows into the strait's great entrepot ruled through the Pembesar Berempat, served by Bendahara Tun Perak and Laksamana Hang Tuah, and remembered later as Malaysia's first nation-state. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
