# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

## Do it first

List seven things a big town needs that a single farm family does not — leaders, builders, writers of records, and more. You have just listed what makes a civilisation, as this lesson teaches.

First, what is a civilisation? The book defines it as a society's highest achievement, known by its marks: farming and trade, government, cities, specialised work, writing and records, religion and art. Then the first of all is named: Mesopotamia, growing between the Tigris and the Euphrates — its very name means the land between two rivers. Every civilisation in the lessons after this one is measured against these marks.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A civilisation is a society's highest achievement — farming and trade, government, cities, specialised work, writing and records, religion and art — and the first of all grows in Mesopotamia, the land between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers. Give one real example: Mesopotamia means the land between two rivers — the Tigris and the Euphrates — where the first civilisation of all grows, and name the lesson's key words: civilisation, Mesopotamia, Tigris, Euphrates, cities, writing. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
