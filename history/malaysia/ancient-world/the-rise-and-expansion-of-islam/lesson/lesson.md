# The Rise and Expansion of Islam

## Do it first

Write four hats on four slips of paper: ruler, builder of society, organiser of livelihoods, commander. Read the lesson and match each hat to one thing the Prophet does. One leader, four works — that is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bab 8.2–8.3: the rise of Islamic civilisation and the Prophet as leader

Muhammad is born to the Quraysh on 12 Rabiulawal, the Year of the Elephant — 20 April 570. As leader he does four works at once: he drafts the Madina Charter, a constitution for a plural city; and he leads as head of state, society-builder, economic organiser and commander. Islam's rise is thus a city's order as much as a faith's preaching.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bab 8.4–8.5: Islamic civilisation's gifts to the world

The civilisation that grows from this rise balances material and spiritual progress, and contributes in politics, economy and society. Its signature in stone is the mosque — architecture shaped for worship, in its spaces, its light and its feeling. A civilisation can be recognised, the lesson shows, by what it builds to pray in.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Islam rises with the Prophet Muhammad — born to the Quraysh in the Year of the Elephant (570) — who leads as head of state, society-builder, economic organiser and commander, drafts the Madina Charter for a plural city, and founds a civilisation balancing material and spiritual progress, signed in its mosque architecture. Give one real example: the Madina Charter is drafted as a constitution for a plural city — many communities under one agreed order, and name the lesson's key words: Muhammad, Quraysh, Year of the Elephant, Madina Charter, mosque, Islamic civilisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
