# Arabia Before Islam: Trade and Tribes

## Do it first

Draw a desert with an oasis town on one side and a moving camp with camels on the other. Label the settled life and the nomadic life. Arabia before Islam, in this lesson, holds both at once.

Before Islam, the Arabian Peninsula — a land ringed by desert — is already a full society. Arabs live two ways: settled lives in towns and oases, and nomadic lives moving with their herds. Both ways have their own governments, economies and social orders. This is the world on the eve of Islam: not an emptiness waiting, but peoples with orders of their own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: On the eve of Islam, the desert-ringed Arabian Peninsula holds Arabs living two ways — settled and nomadic — with their own governments, economies and social orders. Give one real example: the peninsula's two lives — settled townsfolk and nomadic tribes — each have their own order before Islam comes, and name the lesson's key words: Arabian Peninsula, settled, nomadic, tribes, eve of Islam. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
