# About the History — Malaysia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Sejarah books for Tahun 4 and Tingkatan 1–5 (Dewan Bahasa
dan Pustaka, Kuala Lumpur, for the Ministry of Education
Malaysia, in Malay). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**:
a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips
are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Malaysia inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
Legends and founding stories — Parameswara and the white
mousedeer, Hang Tuah's adventures as the books tell them —
are told as the books tell them, as plot summaries in our
own words.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the books'
own progression from Tahun 4 to Tingkatan 5, as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home.** What
   history is (Tahun 4, deepened at Tingkatan 1); sources,
   research methods and interpretation; chronology, cause
   and effect; and history found in the child's own family,
   school and hometown.
2. **Level 2 — The Deep Past and the World's
   Civilisations.** The Ice Age and the four prehistoric
   ages proven by Malaysia's caves; Mesopotamia, Egypt,
   India, China, Greece, Athens, Rome, China's dynasties,
   Arabia before Islam, and the rise of Islam.
3. **Level 3 — Malay Kingdoms and the Melaka Sultanate.**
   Funan to Majapahit; Melaka's society, founding, Tun Perak
   and Hang Tuah, its glory and its nation-state recall; the
   fall of 1511; Johor Riau and the successor sultanates;
   the northern kingdoms and Adat Perpatih; Borneo's local
   governments before Brooke.
4. **Level 4 — Western Powers and Colonial Malaya.** Why
   the West came; the Straits Settlements; the Federated and
   Unfederated Malay States; Brooke's Sarawak and the North
   Borneo Company; colonial transformation; the named
   resistance leaders; Bangkok 1909 and Johor's answer.
5. **Level 5 — War, Occupation and the Road to Merdeka.**
   Nationalism in print from Al-Imam (1906); the world wars;
   the Japanese occupation and Force 136; the Malayan Union
   rejected and the Federation of 1948; the Emergency; the
   elections, the Reid Commission and Merdeka, 1957.
6. **Level 6 — Malaysia Formed and Rebuilt.** The 1963
   formation with its referendum, safeguards and UN mission;
   Singapore's separation; the second insurgency; 13 May
   1969 and MAGERAN; the Rukun Negara and the New Economic
   Policy; and the OIC of 1969.

All forty lessons stand on **student books** and count in
the acceptance reckoning below.

the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *One land, told whole:* "Begin at home — your family's

  Then walk the deep past: ice melting over the Sunda Shelf,
  tools climbing from stone to metal in Malaysia's caves,
  and the world's first civilisations rising on their
  rivers. Come home to the Malay kingdoms and to Melaka —
  the mousedeer at the Bertam river, Tun Perak's torches,

## The way of the books

Malaysia's own way, as filed book by book: history begins at
home — the child's own biodata, family tree, school and
hometown — then widens to the Ice Age, the cave sites, the
early Malay kingdoms, and Melaka told through three named
heroes (Tahun 4). Secondary history opens as "our history
and the world's": method first, the deep past, then a
world-civilisations tour in which Malaysia's own prehistory
is placed inside the world story (Tingkatan 1). A whole year
is national heritage — the Malay world as one Austronesian
family, Melaka's glory and fall, each successor sultanate in
turn (Tingkatan 2). The Western arrival is taught state by
state, and the second half belongs to the Malaysians who
fought back or outmanoeuvred the British, each named
(Tingkatan 3). The nation-building year runs nationalism →
occupation → the rejected Union → the Federation → the
Emergency → elections, Reid and Merdeka, with the documents
and dates (and the rain at Stadium Merdeka) all named
(Tingkatan 4). The final year is Malaysia itself — the 1963
federation assembled step by step, stress-tested, and
rebuilt through the Rukun Negara and the New Economic Policy
(Tingkatan 5).
