# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Find a stone outside and a metal object at home. Hold one in each hand and say which age each belongs to, and what its maker could do that the other could not. That climb is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 5 Zaman Air Batu: the Ice Age

The earth has frozen five times. In the last Ice Age — the Quaternary, its cold Pleistocene part giving way to the warmer Holocene from 11,700 years ago — humans learn fire. Then the warming melts the ice, the seas rise over the Sunda Shelf, and people and animals move across Southeast Asia, while the mammoth and the sabre-toothed tiger die out. Climate turns, and human life turns with it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 6 Zaman Prasejarah: prehistory in Malaysia

Before writing, life in Malaysia climbs four steps: Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Metal Age. The proof is Malaysia's own ground: Gua Niah in Sarawak, Lenggong in Perak, Gua Cha in Kelantan, Bukit Tengkorak in Sabah. Cave-dwellers who gather and hunt become farmers, settled and planting — and their tool-making is the root of today's technology.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bab 2 Zaman Air Batu: the Ice Age

The secondary book retells the last Ice Age — its features and its changes — and follows its Southeast Asian sequel: rising seas remaking the region's coasts and driving the movement of people and animals across the drowned lands. The world's climate story lands, here, on Malaysia's own shores.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bab 3 Zaman Prasejarah: world prehistory

World prehistory is traced in the same steps: the Stone Age's three parts and then the Metal Age, followed through world sites and the changing life of cave, shore and farm. The pattern is worldwide — tools improve, food is grown instead of only gathered, and settlements grow.

## Sub lesson 5 — Bab 3.5: prehistory in Malaysia

Malaysia's own chapter is dated: humans have lived in Malaysia for 40,000 years, since the Palaeolithic. Southeast Asia's Mesolithic has its own name — the Hoabinhian. And Gua Niah's artefacts prove occupation through every prehistoric age: one cave holding the whole climb, from first stone tools to metal.

Give one real example: Gua Niah in Sarawak holds artefacts proving people lived through every prehistoric age in Malaysia — humans have lived there for 40,000 years, and name the lesson's key words: Ice Age, Pleistocene, Holocene, Sunda Shelf, Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Metal Age, Gua Niah, Hoabinhian. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
