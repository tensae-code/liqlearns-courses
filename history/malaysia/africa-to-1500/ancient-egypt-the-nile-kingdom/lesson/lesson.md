# Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

## Do it first

Draw one long river down a page. Mark three stretches along it and label them Old, Middle and New. You have drawn Egypt's story as this lesson tells it — one river, three kingdoms.

Egypt's civilisation is a river's gift. On the 6,650 km Nile, the world's longest river, rise the pyramids and the pharaohs. The story is told in three ages — the Old Kingdom, the Middle Kingdom and the New Kingdom — one kingdom growing, changing and enduring along a single river through the desert.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: On the Nile — at 6,650 km the world's longest river — Ancient Egypt raises the pyramids and the pharaohs through three ages: the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms. Give one real example: the pyramids still stand as the Old Kingdom's mark on the Nile's banks, and name the lesson's key words: Nile, pharaoh, pyramids, Old Kingdom, Middle Kingdom, New Kingdom. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
