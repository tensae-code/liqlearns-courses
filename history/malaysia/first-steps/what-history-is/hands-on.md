# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one thing that happened in your family before you were born. Ask an older person about it, then write down what happened and one cause of it. That asking and telling is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is the study of events that truly happened in the past — told by Herodotus as the story of human actions and their causes, by Ibn Khaldun as the story of civilisation and change, and framed at secondary level as the study of a society's and a nation's past. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
