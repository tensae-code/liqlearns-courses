# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Ask a grandparent or elder to name their parents and grandparents, and draw your family tree. Then ask when your school or your street began. You have just done this lesson.

The first history a Malaysian child studies is their own. The child builds a family salasilah — a family tree — from a grandfather's telling. The school has a history too: the book reads the timeline of SK Permatang Tok Mahat, founded in 1890. And the hometown has a founding story: Johor Bahru begins as Tanjung Puteri, founded by Temenggong Daeng Ibrahim in 1855 and renamed by Sultan Abu Bakar in 1866, while Ranau in Sabah was once a fighters' stronghold. Family, school, town — the past begins where you stand.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History starts at home: a family's salasilah (family tree), a school's timeline, and a hometown's founding story — like Johor Bahru beginning as Tanjung Puteri in 1855 — are real history, found in your own locality. Give one real example: Johor Bahru begins as Tanjung Puteri, founded by Temenggong Daeng Ibrahim in 1855 and renamed by Sultan Abu Bakar in 1866, and name the lesson's key words: salasilah, family tree, locality, SK Permatang Tok Mahat, Tanjung Puteri, Johor Bahru, Ranau. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
