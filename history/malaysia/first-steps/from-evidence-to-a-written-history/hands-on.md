# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find two things at home that tell about the past: one old object itself, and one book or photo caption about the past. Decide which is the original evidence and which only tells about it. That sorting is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written history is built from evidence: primary sources in their original form — fossils, artefacts, manuscripts — and secondary sources like books and newspapers, worked by archaeology, the oral method and written records, then interpreted with care. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
