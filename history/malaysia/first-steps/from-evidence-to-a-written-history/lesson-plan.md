# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Malaysia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A written history is built from evidence: primary sources in their original form — fossils, artefacts, manuscripts — and secondary sources like books and newspapers, worked by archaeology, the oral method and written records, then interpreted with care.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: A written history is built from evidence: primary sources in their original form — fossils, artefacts, manuscripts — and secondary sources like books and newspapers, worked by archaeology, the oral method and written records, then interpreted with care.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: a family's old keris is primary evidence in its original form; a newspaper article about keris is a secondary source.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
