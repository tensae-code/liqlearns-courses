# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Historians order the past on one straight line called chronology — decades, centuries and millennia, split by Before Masihi and Masihi — and every event on that line has a cause and an effect.

How do we keep the past in order? Historians lay events out by chronology, measuring time in decades, centuries and millennia, split into the years Before Masihi and the years of Masihi. And the line is not bare: every event has a cause and an effect. The book's worked example is home-grown — Britain colonises Tanah Melayu; the people struggle against it; and independence follows in 1957. Cause, struggle, effect: that is how the line tells a story.
The secondary book says it as one image: events are strung on one straight line called chronology — decade, century, millennium, Sebelum Masihi and Masihi — so the past can be measured and ordered. A learner who can place an event on the line, and say what came before and after it, holds the map every later lesson travels on.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Malaysia.
