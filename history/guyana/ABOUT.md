# About the History — Guyana course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Social Studies book for Grade 1. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Guyana inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **one level**:

1. **Level 1 — our nation.** Guyana's first social-studies
   book brings the nation's story in at the very start of
   schooling, through two recurring children, Ron and Sue:
   first that Guyana is an independent state (independent on
   26 May 1966, led by a President), then the national
   symbols, the National Anthem and the National Pledge, and
   last the celebrations — Remembrance Day and Mashramani.
   The course's one lesson stands on a **student book** and
   counts in the acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Our nation:* "First the big day: 26 May 1966, when Guyana
  became independent, with a President to lead it. Then the
  face of the new nation: its flag, its coat of arms, its
  national bird, its anthem to sing and its pledge to say.
  Then the days Guyana keeps together: poppies on Remembrance
  Day for those who went to war, and Mashramani, the festival
  for the day Guyana became a Co-operative Republic."
