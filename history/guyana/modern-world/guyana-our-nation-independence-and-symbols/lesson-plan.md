# Lesson plan — Our Nation: Independence and Symbols

**Course:** History — Guyana · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Our Nation: Independence and Symbols
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Our Nation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Guyana became an independent nation on 26 May 1966, led by a President — and the new nation gave itself a face (flag, coat of arms, national bird, anthem and pledge) and days to keep together (Remembrance Day, and Mashramani for the Co-operative Republic of 23 February 1970).
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Our Nation — it stands at the foot of Guyana's ladder, on nothing before it in this course (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Guyana is an independent state** — Guyana became independent on 26 May 1966, and the independent nation is led by a President.
2. **Our national symbols, anthem and pledge** — The flag, the coat of arms, the national bird and the anthem give the new nation a face its children can draw and sing, and the National Pledge gives it words to say together.
3. **Celebrations: Remembrance Day and Mashramani** — Poppies on Remembrance Day honour those who went to war, and Mashramani celebrates Guyana becoming a Co-operative Republic on 23 February 1970.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Guyana became independent on 26 May 1966 and is led by a President, and the nation knows itself by its symbols, its anthem and pledge, and its celebration days.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a flag seen flying, the anthem sung, a festival day kept.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Guyana and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works, with its two children, Ron and Sue.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the two big dates up → independence came first: 26 May 1966 made Guyana independent; 23 February 1970, kept by Mashramani, made it a Co-operative Republic. One date gave the nation its start, the other its new name and shape.
- Go back to the one thing: Guyana became an independent nation on 26 May 1966, led by a President — and the new nation gave itself a face and days to keep together. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
