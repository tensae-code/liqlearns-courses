# Video script — Our Nation: Independence and Symbols

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Guyana became an independent nation on 26 May 1966 — and the new nation gave itself a face, and days to keep together.

Look at the map. Here is Guyana, on the north coast of South America, by the sea.

For a long time, other people far away ruled this land. Then came the turn: on 26 May 1966, Guyana became independent — its own country, standing on its own. And who leads the new nation? A President.

A new nation needs a face, so its children can know it and love it. So Guyana has its flag to fly, its coat of arms to show, its national bird, and its National Anthem to sing. And it has a National Pledge — words the whole nation can say together.

And a nation keeps days together. On Remembrance Day, people wear red poppies, to honour those who went to war and did not come home. And on 23 February, Mashramani fills the streets with colour and music — because on 23 February 1970, Guyana became a Co-operative Republic.

Two dates to hold: 26 May 1966, independence. 23 February 1970, the Republic.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
