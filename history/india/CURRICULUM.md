# History — India course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for India inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** India's book in hand is ONE book —
NCERT's *Our Pasts-I*, the Class VI History textbook — and the
ladder is the book's own journey, chapter by chapter. NCERT
teaches history as a story journey: it begins with a present-day
child's question (how can anyone know what happened a hundred
years ago?) and walks the subcontinent's past in one unbroken
story — the historian's craft first, then the first farmers,
the first cities, the Vedic world, kingdoms and republics, the
new religions, the Mauryan empire and Ashoka, the working world
of villages and trade, the new empires and kingdoms, and last
the buildings, paintings and books the past left behind. The
six levels below are that journey's own six movements (the
book's eleven chapters grouped by the turns of its story), and
a lesson sits at the level of the chapter where it first
appears. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own NCERT book — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list
and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps → First Steps: How We Know, and the First Farmers** — what history is and how anyone can know, the land's rivers and its two names, evidence and dating, the moving hunter-gatherers, the first ages named, and the turn to farming — villages, storage and Mehrgarh's buried goats, with France's painted caves and Çatal Höyük as the world's mirrors. 11 lessons, in titles: `first-steps/` (4 lessons), `ancient-world/` (6 lessons), `africa-to-1500/` (1 lessons).

2. **Ancient World → The First Cities and the Vedic World** — the Harappan cities whole — their finding, their plan, their crafts, their Gujarat ports and their unexplained end — then the Vedic world: the Rigveda carried in memory, the language families, cattle raids and rajas, varna and its resisters, the megalith graves, and China's bone-writing beside it. 11 lessons, in title: `ancient-world/` (11 lessons).

3. **Ancient World → Kingdoms, Republics and New Questions** — the ashvamedha's walking challenge, janapadas walled and taxed, Magadha's rise and Vajji's republic, Athens judged beside them, and the age of new questions — the Buddha, the Upanishadic thinkers, Mahavira, the sangha, the ashrama answer, and Zoroaster across the sea. 11 lessons, in title: `ancient-world/` (11 lessons).

4. **Ancient World → Empire and Exchange: Ashoka to the Pilgrims** — the Mauryan machine and Ashoka's turn — Kalinga's casualty list, dhamma carved in stone, China's wall as the counter-answer — then the working world the empire left: villages and irrigation, Sangam poems, ring wells, punch-marked coins, Mathura, the guilds, the three crowned kings, Buddhism's two journeys, the pilgrims and Nalanda, bhakti's open door, and Christianity's beginning. 14 lessons, in title: `ancient-world/` (14 lessons).

5. **Ancient World → New Empires and Kingdoms** — Samudragupta's four policies and the Gupta climb, Harsha stopped at the Narmada, the Pallava–Chalukya raids and their boasting poems, hereditary offices and samantas, southern assemblies — and ordinary people at the gate, in the market and under the army's dust — closing on Arabia and Islam reaching Sind. 6 lessons, in title: `ancient-world/` (6 lessons).

6. **Ancient World → Buildings, Paintings and Books** — the book's own closing wonders: the iron pillar that will not rust, stupas and rock-cut temples and who paid for them, Ajanta's torch-lit painters, the Tamil epics of Kannagi's fire, the Puranas and the two great epics opened to everyone, the monkey king's sacrifice, and Aryabhata's turning earth, the zero, and Ayurveda. 7 lessons, in title: `ancient-world/` (7 lessons).

**Totals: 60 lessons** (Level 1: 11, Level 2: 11, Level 3: 11,
Level 4: 14, Level 5: 6, Level 6: 7) — 83 placements stocked,
every one on a student book.
