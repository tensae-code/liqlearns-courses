# Lesson plan — From Stone Tools to Farming

**Course:** History — India · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From Stone Tools to Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know, and the First Farmers (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** About 12,000 years ago the world warmed, grasslands spread, and hunters who had learned the habits of deer, sheep and cattle began herding them, while grain-collectors became the first farmers — the dog was tamed first of all.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: About 12,000 years ago the world warmed, grasslands spread, and hunters who had learned the habits of deer, sheep and cattle began herding them, while grain-collectors became the first farmers — the dog was tamed first of all.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a family that stops buying vegetables and starts growing them — the same turn, from taking what grows to tending what grows.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

- Thinks farming was invented in a day → the book shows a slow learning: watching animals, then herding them; gathering grain, then planting it.
- Go back to the one thing: About 12,000 years ago the world warmed, grasslands spread, and hunters who had learned the habits of deer, sheep and cattle began herding them, while grain-collectors became the first farmers — the dog was tamed first of all. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
