# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Watch a patch of wild grass through a year in your mind: when is it green, when dry, when full of seed? Who eats it besides you? This lesson is the story of the people who watched exactly that — and changed the world.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: About 12,000 years ago the world warmed, grasslands spread, and hunters who had learned the habits of deer, sheep and cattle began herding them, while grain-collectors became the first farmers — the dog was tamed first of all. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
