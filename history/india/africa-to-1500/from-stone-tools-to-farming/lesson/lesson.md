# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Watch a patch of wild grass through a year in your mind: when is it green, when dry, when full of seed? Who eats it besides you? This lesson is the story of the people who watched exactly that — and changed the world.

The turn begins with the weather. About 12,000 years ago the world warmed, the ice drew back, and grasslands spread where cold desert had been. Grass meant grain — and animals that eat grass. Hunters who had followed deer, sheep and cattle for generations knew their habits intimately: where they grazed, when they lambed, how they moved. Knowledge became control: instead of only hunting herds, people began keeping and herding them. And those who had always gathered wild grain began to tend it — the first farmers. The very first animal tamed, long before, was the dog, the hunter's partner.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: About 12,000 years ago the world warmed, grasslands spread, and hunters who had learned the habits of deer, sheep and cattle began herding them, while grain-collectors became the first farmers — the dog was tamed first of all. Give one real example from your own life — a family that stops buying vegetables and starts growing them — the same turn, from taking what grows to tending what grows — and name the lesson's key words: From Stone Tools to Farming, herding, first farmers, the dog. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
