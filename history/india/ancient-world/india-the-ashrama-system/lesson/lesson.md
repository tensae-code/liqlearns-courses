# India: The Ashrama System

## Do it first

Draw your life as four chapters. What belongs in each? This lesson shows the four-chapter map the brahmins drew — and asks who was allowed to walk the whole map.

Faced with the Buddha's and Mahavira's challenge — leave home to find truth — the brahmins answered with a map that made room for leaving, but in its proper season. A life, they taught, has four ashramas, stages: the student, learning under a teacher; the householder, marrying, working and raising a family; the forest meditator, withdrawing as age comes; and finally the renouncer, who leaves everything for liberation. Renunciation was honoured — but placed last, after duty. And the map had borders: in full it was open only to men of the three upper varnas; women were expected to follow their husbands' stages. A brilliant compromise, drawn for some.

Give one real example from your own life — school, work, retirement — modern lives still fall into stages; the ashrama map made the pattern sacred — and name the lesson's key words: India The Ashrama System, four stages, student householder meditator renouncer. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
