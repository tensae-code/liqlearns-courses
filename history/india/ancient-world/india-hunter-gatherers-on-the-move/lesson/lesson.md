# India: Hunter-Gatherers on the Move

## Do it first

Imagine your home has no fridge, no shop and no stored food — only what the land around you offers this week. When would you have to move? This lesson follows the people who lived exactly that way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why hunter-gatherers moved, and their stone tools

Why did hunter-gatherers keep moving? Because staying meant starving. A band that gathers a valley's plants soon eats that valley bare. The herds they hunt migrate, and the hunters must follow. Fruit ripens in one place this month and another the next, and in the dry season everything — people and animals alike — must walk to water. So they never settled. What survives of them is stone: their tools, shaped to cut meat, scrape hides clean, and tip the spears and arrows of the hunt.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing a place to live in; rock paintings; finding out about fire

Moving people still chose their halts with care. At Bhimbetka, near the Narmada, natural rock shelters gave cover from rain, sun and beasts — and on those cave walls the hunters painted the wild animals around them, with real skill: a picture archive of what they watched every day. And they were not defenceless against the dark. In the Kurnool caves, archaeologists found ash — the fingerprint of fire, tamed and kept: for light in the cave, for roasting food, and for scaring wild animals away from the sleeping band.

Give one real example from your own life — a picnic that runs out — you must go where the food is; for hunter-gatherers, that was every week of life — and name the lesson's key words: India Hunter-Gatherers on the Move, Bhimbetka, stone tools, fire. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
