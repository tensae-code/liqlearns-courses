# Hands-on — India: First Coins and Barter

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pay for something without money: trade a thing you have for a thing you need. What goes wrong? This lesson watches the subcontinent use both systems at once — stamped silver and sacks of rice — for five hundred years.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Punch-marked coins, stamped with symbols and uninscribed, circulated for 500 years across the subcontinent — while a Sangam poem's salt merchants still carted white paddy to swap for coastal salt, whole families travelling roads 'through sands white as moonlight'. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
