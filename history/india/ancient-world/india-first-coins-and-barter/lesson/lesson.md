# India: First Coins and Barter

## Do it first

Pay for something without money: trade a thing you have for a thing you need. What goes wrong? This lesson watches the subcontinent use both systems at once — stamped silver and sacks of rice — for five hundred years.

Money arrives quietly. The subcontinent's first coins are punch-marked: small pieces of silver stamped — punched — with symbols, a sun, an animal, a tree. They carry no king's name and no writing at all, yet they circulated for some 500 years, trusted across the whole subcontinent. But coins did not end the older way. A Sangam poem follows salt merchants who load their carts with white paddy — rice in the husk — and travel to the coast to swap it, measure for measure, for salt. Whole families make the journey together, along roads the poem sees 'through sands white as moonlight'. Two economies, one age: the stamped coin and the swapped sack.
