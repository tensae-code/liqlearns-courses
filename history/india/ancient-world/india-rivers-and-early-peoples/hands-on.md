# Hands-on — India: Rivers and Early Peoples

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a rough map of the subcontinent from memory — just its big rivers. Mark where you think people would have lived first, and why. This lesson checks your guesses against the book's own map of beginnings.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: People have lived along India's rivers for hundreds of thousands of years: gatherers on the Narmada, the first wheat and barley farmers in the Sulaiman and Kirthar hills about 8000 years ago, then cities on the Indus about 4700 years ago and on the Ganga about 2500 years ago. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
