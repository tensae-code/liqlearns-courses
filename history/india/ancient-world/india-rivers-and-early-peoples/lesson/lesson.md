# India: Rivers and Early Peoples

## Do it first

Draw a rough map of the subcontinent from memory — just its big rivers. Mark where you think people would have lived first, and why. This lesson checks your guesses against the book's own map of beginnings.

Where did people live? The book answers with a map and a very long clock. Along the river Narmada, people who gathered and hunted lived for hundreds of thousands of years. In the hills of Sulaiman and Kirthar, in the north-west, people first grew wheat and barley, about 8000 years ago. Much later, about 4700 years ago, real cities rose along the Indus. And about 2500 years ago cities rose along the Ganga too, where the rulers of Magadha grew powerful. Rivers are the thread: water first, then farms, then cities, then kingdoms.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: People have lived along India's rivers for hundreds of thousands of years: gatherers on the Narmada, the first wheat and barley farmers in the Sulaiman and Kirthar hills about 8000 years ago, then cities on the Indus about 4700 years ago and on the Ganga about 2500 years ago. Give one real example from your own life — the river nearest your own home — people have always settled where water, food and routes meet — and name the lesson's key words: India Rivers and Early Peoples, Narmada, Indus, Ganga. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
