# Hands-on — Faiths That Shaped States

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A faith changes how it pictures its founder — from a footprint and an empty throne to a human face. This lesson follows that face across half of Asia.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Kanishka's Buddhist council and Ashvaghosha's Sanskrit life of the Buddha mark a turn: Mahayana Buddhism now carves the Buddha's image instead of symbols, and Bodhisattvas who postpone their own release to teach others carry the faith across Central Asia to China, Korea and Japan, while Theravada sails to Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
