# Faiths That Shaped States

## Do it first

A faith changes how it pictures its founder — from a footprint and an empty throne to a human face. This lesson follows that face across half of Asia.

Under the Kushana king Kanishka, Buddhism changed its face — literally. His court held a great Buddhist council, and the poet Ashvaghosha wrote a life of the Buddha in Sanskrit. The new form, Mahayana, began carving the Buddha as a man — serene images from the workshops of Mathura and Taxila — where earlier art had used only symbols: a footprint, an empty seat, a wheel. Mahayana's heroes are Bodhisattvas, beings who postpone their own final release to stay and teach others — the perfect missionaries. Carried by such devotion, the faith crossed Central Asia to China, Korea and Japan. The older Theravada form took the sea road instead, to Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. One teaching, two great journeys.
