# India: Ashoka's Dhamma

## Do it first

An emperor decides his real conquest will be kindness. What laws, officers and buildings would that take? This lesson lists Ashoka's answer — carved in stone across a continent.

What replaced conquest? Dhamma. Ashoka's dhamma is not a religion of gods and sacrifices — it is a code of conduct. Be gentle with servants and slaves. Respect elders. Show compassion to animals. And hold this rule toward other faiths, in his own carved words: praising your own religion while criticising another's only harms your own. To spread it he created special officers, dhamma mahamattas, who toured the empire teaching. His messengers carried dhamma beyond India — to Syria, Egypt, Greece and Sri Lanka. And he built it into the land itself: roads planted with shade trees, wells, rest houses, and medical care — for animals as well as people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ashoka's dhamma needs no god or sacrifice: gentleness to servants, respect for elders, compassion to creatures, and respect for other religions — taught by touring dhamma mahamattas, carried by messengers to Syria, Egypt, Greece and Sri Lanka, and built into roads, wells, rest houses and medical care for people and animals. Give one real example from your own life — a public-health campaign or a kindness rule in a school — dhamma was an empire-wide programme of conduct, not a private belief — and name the lesson's key words: India Ashoka's Dhamma, dhamma mahamattas, respect for other religions. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
