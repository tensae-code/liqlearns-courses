# Hands-on — India: Ashoka's Dhamma

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
An emperor decides his real conquest will be kindness. What laws, officers and buildings would that take? This lesson lists Ashoka's answer — carved in stone across a continent.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ashoka's dhamma needs no god or sacrifice: gentleness to servants, respect for elders, compassion to creatures, and respect for other religions — taught by touring dhamma mahamattas, carried by messengers to Syria, Egypt, Greece and Sri Lanka, and built into roads, wells, rest houses and medical care for people and animals. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
