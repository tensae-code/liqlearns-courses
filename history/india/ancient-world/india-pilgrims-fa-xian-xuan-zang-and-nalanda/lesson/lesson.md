# India: Pilgrims Fa Xian, Xuan Zang and Nalanda

## Do it first

Your boat is sinking in a storm. You can save your luggage or your books — not both. Two Chinese pilgrims faced that choice for real. This lesson sails and walks with them.

They came for books, and nearly died for them. Fa Xian, the earlier pilgrim, sailed home from India through a storm that lasted 13 days; as the ship struggled, he threw his own belongings into the sea — but clung to his manuscripts and Buddha statues, and reached Java only after 90 days. Xuan Zang, two centuries later, lost 50 manuscripts when his boat capsized in the Indus — yet still hauled some 600 texts home on 20 horses, and spent the rest of his life translating them into Chinese. Both had studied at Nalanda, the great monastery-university, where admission itself was an ordeal: the gatekeeper scholar asked such hard questions that seven or eight of every ten hopefuls were turned away at the gate.
