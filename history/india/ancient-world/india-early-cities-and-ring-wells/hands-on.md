# Hands-on — India: Early Cities and Ring Wells

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Archaeologists find a row of clay rings sunk in the ground of an ancient house plot. Toilet, drain or rubbish pit? This lesson digs the city's unglamorous side — and then sails to its richest port.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Rows of ceramic rings sunk in house plots served as toilets, drains and rubbish pits — the humble ring well is the archaeologist's sign of a city house — while a Greek sailor's port-guide lists Barygaza's imports and exports and the singing boys and fine cloth its merchants gifted to the king. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
