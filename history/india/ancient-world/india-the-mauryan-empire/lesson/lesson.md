# India: The Mauryan Empire

## Do it first

A kingdom rules one land. An empire must rule many lands, peoples and roads at once — with no telephones. This lesson opens the Mauryan machine and looks at its wiring.

## Sub lesson 1 — A very big kingdom = an empire

What is an empire? The book is blunt: a very big kingdom — so big it cannot be run like one. More than 2300 years ago Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan empire, advised by the famously shrewd Chanakya, whose ideas on ruling fill a whole book, the Arthashastra. The dynasty runs in three great names: Chandragupta, his son Bindusara, and his grandson Ashoka. But size changes everything. An empire swallowing many kingdoms needs bigger armies to hold them and far more officials to collect the taxes that pay for it all. The Mauryan story is the story of that machinery.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ruling the empire

How was it ruled? In rings. Around the capital, Pataliputra, the emperor ruled directly, through salaried officials, swift messengers — and spies, reporting on everyone, officials included. Beyond lay provinces, governed from cities like Taxila in the north-west and Ujjain in the west, usually by royal princes. Between the centres, the state gripped the roads and rivers, taking tax and tribute: blankets from the north-west, gold from the south. And at the empire's forest edges, peoples stayed near-independent, sending what the forest gives — elephants, timber, honey and wax. One empire, several kinds of rule, fitted to distance.
