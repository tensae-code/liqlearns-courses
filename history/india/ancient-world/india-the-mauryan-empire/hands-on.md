# Hands-on — India: The Mauryan Empire

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A kingdom rules one land. An empire must rule many lands, peoples and roads at once — with no telephones. This lesson opens the Mauryan machine and looks at its wiring.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Chandragupta Maurya founded an empire more than 2300 years ago, advised by Chanakya, whose ideas fill the Arthashastra; around Pataliputra the emperor ruled directly through salaried officials, messengers and spies, while provinces answered from Taxila and Ujjain under royal princes and forest peoples stayed near-independent, sending elephants, timber, honey and wax. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
