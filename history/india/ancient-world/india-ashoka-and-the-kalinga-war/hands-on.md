# Hands-on — India: Ashoka and the Kalinga War

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A king wins his greatest victory — and the list of the dead makes him lay down his sword forever. This lesson reads the numbers that stopped an emperor.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ashoka, the first king to speak to his people through inscriptions in Prakrit and Brahmi, conquered coastal Kalinga — then read his own casualty figures, a lakh and a half captured and more than a lakh killed, and horrified, renounced war: the only king in world history to give up conquest after winning. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
