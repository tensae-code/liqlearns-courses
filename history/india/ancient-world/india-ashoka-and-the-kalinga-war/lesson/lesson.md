# India: Ashoka and the Kalinga War

## Do it first

A king wins his greatest victory — and the list of the dead makes him lay down his sword forever. This lesson reads the numbers that stopped an emperor.

Ashoka is the first king in India who speaks to us in his own words: he carved inscriptions on stone, in Prakrit, the people's language, written in the Brahmi script, across his empire. Early in his reign he did what emperors do — he conquered Kalinga, on the east coast. Then he counted. His own inscription records it: a lakh and a half of people captured, more than a lakh killed, and many more dead of disease and hardship. The figures horrified the victor. Ashoka renounced war and conquest altogether — the book calls him the only king in world history to give up conquest after winning it.
