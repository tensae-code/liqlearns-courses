# Lesson plan — India: The Harappan Cities End

**Course:** History — India · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** India: The Harappan Cities End
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The First Cities and the Vedic World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Around 3900 years ago people walked away from the Harappan cities: writing, seals and weights fell out of use, long-distance raw materials stopped coming — and nobody yet knows why.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The First Cities and the Vedic World (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Around 3900 years ago people walked away from the Harappan cities: writing, seals and weights fell out of use, long-distance raw materials stopped coming — and nobody yet knows why.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a closed factory town today — when the one system that holds a place together stops, people drift away.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

- Thinks the cities must have been destroyed by invaders → the book reports no single destroyer: people leave, crafts fade, and the cause is still unknown.
- Go back to the one thing: Around 3900 years ago people walked away from the Harappan cities: writing, seals and weights fell out of use, long-distance raw materials stopped coming — and nobody yet knows why. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
