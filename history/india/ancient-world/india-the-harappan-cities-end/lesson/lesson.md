# India: The Harappan Cities End

## Do it first

Imagine everyone in your town quietly leaving over a few years — houses emptied, markets silent. What could make that happen? This lesson is honest about a mystery historians still cannot solve.

Every civilisation in this course so far has risen. This one ends — quietly, and unexplained. Around 3900 years ago, people began leaving the great Harappan cities. Streets emptied. The script stopped being written; the seals stopped being carved; the standard weights fell out of use. The long-distance trade that had brought copper, tin and gold dried up. Life went on — in smaller settlements, in villages — but the city system was over. Why? Rivers may have shifted, rains may have failed, trade may have collapsed first. The book gives the mystery its true name: nobody yet knows. History includes honest unanswered questions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Around 3900 years ago people walked away from the Harappan cities: writing, seals and weights fell out of use, long-distance raw materials stopped coming — and nobody yet knows why. Give one real example from your own life — a closed factory town today — when the one system that holds a place together stops, people drift away — and name the lesson's key words: India The Harappan Cities End, 3900 years ago, mystery. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
