# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Your class must decide something big. Who gets a seat in the room — everyone, or only some? This lesson visits the first famous democracy and counts who was left standing outside.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Around 2500 years ago Athens let all free men over 30 sit in an assembly meeting forty times a year and filled offices by lottery — yet women, foreigners and thousands of slaves were shut out, and the book asks: do you think this was a true democracy? Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
