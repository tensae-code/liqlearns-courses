# India: The Three Crowned Kings

## Do it first

Three crowned kings share one southern land for centuries. None destroys the others. This lesson asks what held that balance — gifts, poets and two capitals each — and what rose in the west meanwhile.

Along the southern coasts, power wore three crowns. The muvendar — the 'three crowned ones' — were the Chola, Chera and Pandya chiefs, famous from Sangam song. Each held two capitals, one inland and one on the coast — Puhar for the Cholas, Madurai for the Pandyas — gripping both the farm country and the sea trade. They did not tax as northern kings did; they took gifts and tribute, and they spent lavishly on the praise-poets who made their fame, rewarding them with gold, horses and elephants. Two hundred years later the west found its own power: the Satavahanas, whose greatest king, Gautamiputra Shri Satakarni, styled himself lord of the dakshinapatha — the southern road — and marched his armies to three coasts.
