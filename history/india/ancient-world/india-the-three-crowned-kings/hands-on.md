# Hands-on — India: The Three Crowned Kings

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Three crowned kings share one southern land for centuries. None destroys the others. This lesson asks what held that balance — gifts, poets and two capitals each — and what rose in the west meanwhile.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The muvendar — Chola, Chera and Pandya chiefs — held an inland and a coastal capital each, took gifts and tribute instead of regular taxes, and rewarded praise-poets with gold, horses and elephants; 200 years later the Satavahanas rose in the west, Gautamiputra Shri Satakarni styling himself lord of the dakshinapatha and marching to three coasts. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
