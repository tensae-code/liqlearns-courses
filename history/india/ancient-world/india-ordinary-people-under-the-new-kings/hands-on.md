# Hands-on — India: Ordinary People Under the New Kings

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
History from the palace is praise-poems. History from the roadside is a fisherman shaken down by gatekeepers. This lesson stands at the roadside and watches the great kings pass.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In Kalidasa's play a poor fisherman who returns the king's ring found in a fish is accused of theft by the gatekeepers, then rewarded — and the officials demand a cut; Fa Xian records untouchables striking wood to warn others away in markets; Banabhatta's royal army on the march swallows the world in dust, trampling villagers' huts as it passes. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
