# India: The Sangha and the Viharas

## Do it first

Could you learn more by leaving home with nothing but a bowl and a robe? Two great teachers said yes. This lesson builds the communities — and eventually the stone monasteries — that their answer created.

Both the Buddha and Mahavira taught that true knowledge requires leaving home. So their followers — monks and nuns together, the sangha — lived with almost nothing, under a rule-book, the Vinaya Pitaka, that fixed even the hours for begging their food. At first they wandered, sheltering only in the rains. But gifts change things: kings and merchants gave land, and the rainy-season shelters hardened into permanent monasteries — viharas — first of wood, then brick, then whole halls cut into living rock. The wandering life had built itself a home, and the viharas became the schools and libraries of the new faiths.
