# Hands-on — India: The Sangha and the Viharas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Could you learn more by leaving home with nothing but a bowl and a robe? Two great teachers said yes. This lesson builds the communities — and eventually the stone monasteries — that their answer created.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Buddha and Mahavira held that true knowledge needs leaving home, so monks and nuns lived simply under the Vinaya Pitaka's rules, begging at fixed hours — and their rainy-season shelters hardened into permanent viharas of wood, brick and rock-cut cave, on land gifted by kings and merchants. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
