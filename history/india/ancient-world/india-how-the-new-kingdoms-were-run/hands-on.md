# Hands-on — India: How the New Kingdoms Were Run

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
One man is judge, minister and foreign secretary. A landlord owes soldiers instead of rent. A village assembly runs the water supply for centuries. This lesson opens the new kingdoms' engine room.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Offices turned hereditary and piled up in one man — Harishena was judge, minister and war-and-peace minister at once — unpaid samantas held land grants and owed troops, growing independent whenever the king weakened, while in the south the sabha, ur and nagaram assemblies ran irrigation, roads and temples for centuries. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
