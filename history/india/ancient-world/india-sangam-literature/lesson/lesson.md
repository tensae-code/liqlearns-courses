# India: Sangam Literature

## Do it first

A poem names the landowner, the ploughman and the labourer of a village. Two thousand years later, a historian reads it as a census. This short lesson shows how poetry becomes evidence.

The earliest Tamil literature was not written in a study; it was gathered in company. Around 2300 years ago, poets met in assemblies at Madurai — sangams, gatherings — where poems were composed, heard and collected. These Sangam poems sing of love and war, kings and coasts — and, almost in passing, they preserve the Tamil words for the people of the village: the vellalar, the uzhavar, the kadaisiyar. That is why historians treasure them. A poem made for beauty becomes, across two thousand years, a source: evidence of how a society named and ordered itself, kept in verse.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Poems composed around 2300 years ago were gathered in assemblies — sangams — of poets at Madurai, preserving the Tamil words for the village's people and becoming a historian's source in verse. Give one real example from your own life — a song from your own region that names old jobs and places — it is already a historical source, whether it meant to be or not — and name the lesson's key words: India Sangam Literature, Madurai, Tamil poems, assemblies of poets. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
