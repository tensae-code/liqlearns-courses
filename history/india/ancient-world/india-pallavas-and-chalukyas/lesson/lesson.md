# India: Pallavas and Chalukyas

## Do it first

Two kingdoms raid each other's capitals for generations — and both employ poets to turn the raids into glory. This lesson reads the boasting carefully, and watches both proud houses fall to newer powers.

In the south, two dynasties made war a habit. The Pallavas of Kanchipuram and the Chalukyas of Aihole and Vatapi raided each other's rich capitals for generations, carrying off wealth and glory in turn. Their wars survive in a famous boast: the prashasti composed by Ravikirti, court poet of Pulakeshin II — the same king who stopped Harsha. The poem gloats that after his defeat, Harsha — whose name means happiness — 'was no longer Harsha', his joy melted by fear; and that Pulakeshin drove the Pallava king to cower behind the walls of Kanchipuram. It is magnificent propaganda, and the book teaches us to enjoy it as exactly that. The sober ending: for all their raiding, both houses soon gave way to newer powers, the Rashtrakutas and the Cholas.
