# India: Vajji, the Early Republic

## Do it first

Imagine a country where not one king but thousands of leaders must agree before anything is done. Slow? Or strong? This lesson asks the Buddha himself to judge.

Not every land chose a king. The Vajjis were a gana — a republic — where not one raja but thousands ruled together, meeting in assembly to discuss and debate until they decided. King Ajatasattu of Magadha wanted to conquer them, and sent his minister to ask the Buddha how it could be done. The Buddha's answer was a lesson in political science: the Vajjis will prosper, he said, as long as they meet fully and often, act together in concord, honour their elders, and protect their women and their shrines. A united republic, he implied, cannot be conquered from outside. Such gana-sanghas proved him right — they outlasted kingdom after kingdom, surviving until the Gupta age, a thousand years on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the Vajji gana, thousands of rajas ruled together by assembly, discussion and debate — and when Ajatasattu asked the Buddha how to conquer them, the answer was that they would prosper while they met fully and often, acted together, honoured elders and protected their women and shrines. Give one real example from your own life — a school council or a village meeting that truly decides together — the gana ran a whole country that way — and name the lesson's key words: India Vajji the Early Republic, gana, assembly, the Buddha's answer. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
