# Hands-on — India: Vajji, the Early Republic

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine a country where not one king but thousands of leaders must agree before anything is done. Slow? Or strong? This lesson asks the Buddha himself to judge.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In the Vajji gana, thousands of rajas ruled together by assembly, discussion and debate — and when Ajatasattu asked the Buddha how to conquer them, the answer was that they would prosper while they met fully and often, acted together, honoured elders and protected their women and shrines. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
