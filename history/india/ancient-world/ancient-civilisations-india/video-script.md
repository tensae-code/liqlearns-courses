# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: India

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Harappan cities were planned before they were lived in: a raised citadel and a larger lower town behind baked-brick walls, courtyard houses with private baths and wells, covered drains with inspection holes — while farmers with ploughs and stored water fed them all.

Railway builders carried off thousands of ancient bricks for track ballast and destroyed whole buildings; eighty years ago archaeologists realised Harappa was among the subcontinent's oldest cities, and every sister site takes its name — Harappan.

Each Harappan city split into a raised citadel and a larger lower town behind interlocked baked-brick walls; Mohenjodaro's Great Bath, fire altars at Kalibangan and Lothal, and great storehouses crowned the citadel.

Harappan houses stood around courtyards, one or two storeys high, with private bathing areas and wells, and the streets had covered drains with inspection holes — all planned and built together.

In the Harappan city, rulers planned and hoarded gold and beads, scribes cut seals in a script we still cannot read, craftspeople worked at home and in workshops, and children played with terracotta toys.

Countryside farmers grew wheat, barley, pulses, rice, sesame and mustard with a new tool, the plough, stored water against the dry months, and drove herds far for summer pasture so the cities could eat.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
