# India: Magadha Becomes Great

## Do it first

Pick the best spot on a map to build a power: it needs water, food, building material, weapons metal and roads. This lesson shows one kingdom that had all five — and used them.

Why Magadha? The book answers like a geographer. Its rivers gave transport and fertile fields. Its forests gave war-elephants — the tanks of ancient battle — and timber. Its hills held iron mines, for weapons and tools. On these advantages, kings built: Bimbisara and Ajatasattu conquered janapada after janapada from their capital Rajagriha; Mahapadma Nanda later pushed Magadha's power to the north-west; and the capital moved to Pataliputra, at the meeting of rivers. The proof of Magadha's weight is a retreat: Alexander of Macedon, conqueror of Persia, reached the river Beas — and turned back, because his soldiers refused to face India's vast armies of foot, chariots and elephants.
