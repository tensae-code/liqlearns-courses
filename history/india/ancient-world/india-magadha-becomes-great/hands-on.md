# Hands-on — India: Magadha Becomes Great

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pick the best spot on a map to build a power: it needs water, food, building material, weapons metal and roads. This lesson shows one kingdom that had all five — and used them.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Magadha rose on its advantages — rivers for transport and fertility, forests full of war-elephants and timber, iron mines for weapons — as Bimbisara and Ajatasattu conquered janapada after janapada, Mahapadma Nanda pushed to the north-west, the capital moved to Pataliputra, and Alexander turned back at the Beas before India's vast armies. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
