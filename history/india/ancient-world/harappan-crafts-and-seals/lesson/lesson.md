# Harappan Crafts and Seals

## Do it first

Hold a bead, a button or a coin from your home. How many skilled steps did it take to make? This lesson meets the specialists whose hands made a civilisation's small, perfect things.

A city lets people specialise — do one thing all day, and become superb at it. Harappan specialists cut, polished and bored long beads of carnelian, a hard red stone, drilling holes finer than a needle's eye. They chipped stone into blades and into weights of exact, standard sizes — the honesty of every market deal depended on them. They carved the animal seals. And they performed a small magic: they took sand, glazed it, and fired it into faience — a shining blue-green material that nature never made. Faience is invention itself: a material humans dreamed up, then manufactured.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Harappan specialists cut, polished and bored carnelian beads, shaped precise stone weights and blades, carved animal seals, and glazed sand into blue-green faience — a material invented, not found. Give one real example from your own life — a watchmaker or a jeweller today — a specialist who does one thing superbly because a whole city wants it — and name the lesson's key words: Harappan Crafts and Seals, carnelian beads, faience, stone weights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
