# India: The Tamil Epics

## Do it first

A wronged wife confronts a king who has killed her innocent husband — and her rage sets his city on fire. This lesson tells the Tamil epics' blazing story, and how the books themselves were lost and found.

The Tamil epics tell a story of love, injustice and fire. Kovalan, a rich merchant, abandons his devoted wife Kannagi for the courtesan Madhavi. Ruined, he returns to Kannagi, and the two go to Madurai to rebuild their lives. There Kovalan is falsely accused of stealing the queen's anklet — and the Pandya king, without a fair inquiry, has him executed. Kannagi storms into the court, proves her husband's innocence by breaking open her own anklet — rubies inside, not the queen's pearls — and her grief and rage at the injustice burn the city of Madurai down. Ilango wrote her story as the Silappadikaram, the Tale of an Anklet; Sattanar's sequel, the Manimekalai, follows Kovalan and Madhavi's daughter. Then both epics vanished — lost for centuries, surviving in a few palm-leaf copies, and rediscovered only about a hundred years ago. Even immortal books can nearly die.
