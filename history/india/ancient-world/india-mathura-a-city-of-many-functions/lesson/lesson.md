# India: Mathura, a City of Many Functions

## Do it first

Some towns do one thing: a port, a fort, a shrine. This lesson visits a city that did everything at once — and asks what a crossroads does to a place.

Most cities have a job. Mathura had five. It stood where the route from the north-west to the east crossed the route running north to south, and trade made it rich: a walled city fed by a wide hinterland of farms. Its sculptors carved some of the age's finest images. The Kushana emperors made it their second capital. And it was holy three times over — Buddhist monasteries, Jaina shrines, and temples of Krishna, whose homeland this was, all inside one city. Even its generosity is documented: gift-inscriptions on its shrines name the donors' trades — goldsmiths, weavers, basket makers, perfumers — a whole workforce signing its work in stone.
