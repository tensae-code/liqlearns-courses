# Hands-on — India: Mathura, a City of Many Functions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Some towns do one thing: a port, a fort, a shrine. This lesson visits a city that did everything at once — and asks what a crossroads does to a place.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: At the crossroads of the north-west–east and north–south routes, walled Mathura fed itself from its hinterland, carved superb sculpture, became the Kushanas' second capital, and hosted Buddhist monasteries, Jaina shrines and Krishna worship — its gift-inscriptions naming the city's goldsmiths, weavers, basket makers and perfumers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
