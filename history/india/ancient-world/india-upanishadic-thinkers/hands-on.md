# Hands-on — India: Upanishadic Thinkers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Argue this: if the same soul lives in every person, what follows for how you must treat the poorest stranger? This lesson watches India's boldest thinkers chase that question — and get caught by it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Upanishadic teachers hunted what survives death — atman, the soul, one with brahman, the universal soul — while a hungry beggar shamed two sages with their own doctrine, women like Gargi debated in royal courts, the slave's son Satyakama Jabala became a famed thinker, and Panini wrote Sanskrit grammar in 3,000 algebra-like formulae. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
