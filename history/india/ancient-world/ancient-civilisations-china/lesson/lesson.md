# Ancient Civilisations: China

## Do it first

Write a message two ways: scratch it on something hard, then write it on paper. Which would you trust to travel the world? This lesson follows China's two gifts to every later student — early writing, and paper itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Writing in China

The book's 'Elsewhere' eye turns to China at the very moment it tells of the Rigveda. Around 3500 years ago, at the same depth of time, Chinese writers were carving characters onto animal bones — oracle bones, used to ask questions of the ancestors. It is among the first writing anywhere on earth. The parallel is the lesson: at the same age, one civilisation was perfecting a book carried in memory, and another was inventing a book carried on bone. Civilisations begin their stories side by side, in different materials.

## Sub lesson 2 — The invention of paper

The course's last 'Elsewhere' belongs to the material of books themselves. About 1900 years ago, in China, Cai Lun beat plant fibre, old cloth, rope and bark into pulp, spread it and dried it — and made paper: cheap, light, smooth. China guarded the secret, but secrets leak. Papermaking travelled to Korea and Japan, then to Baghdad, and from there to Europe, Africa and India. Every page since — including the textbook this very lesson comes from — rides on that one invention. The history of books is also a history of what books are made of.
