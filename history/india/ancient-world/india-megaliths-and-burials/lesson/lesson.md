# India: Megaliths and Burials

## Do it first

Walk past any graveyard and read the stones: big ones, small ones, plain ones, grand ones. What do the differences confess about the living? This lesson reads graves the same way, 3,000 years deep.

## Sub lesson 1 — Silent sentinels — the story of the megaliths

Across the Deccan, the south, the north-east and Kashmir stand circles and chambers of enormous stones: megaliths, the big-stone burials that spread from about 3000 years ago. Dig beneath them and the dead appear with their goods — Black and Red Ware pots, iron tools, weapons, horse gear. But the graves are not equal. At Brahmagiri, one burial held 33 gold beads and rich goods; nearby graves held a few pots and little else. The stones are silent sentinels over a new truth: society now has rich and poor, chiefs and followers — and it shows even in death.

## Sub lesson 2 — A special burial at Inamgaon; occupations at Inamgaon

At Inamgaon, one burial stands apart. A man was laid cross-legged inside a huge four-legged clay jar, buried in the courtyard of the largest house in the settlement — five rooms, with its own granary. Archaeologists read the honour plainly: this was likely a chief. Around him, the rest of the village's life can be rebuilt from small things: seeds show what was farmed, cut-marked animal bones show what was herded and hunted, and the remains of fish complete the menu. One site, read patiently, returns a whole working village — and its one honoured man.
