# Hands-on — India: Megaliths and Burials

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Walk past any graveyard and read the stones: big ones, small ones, plain ones, grand ones. What do the differences confess about the living? This lesson reads graves the same way, 3,000 years deep.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From about 3000 years ago, big-stone burials — megaliths — spread across the Deccan, the south, the north-east and Kashmir: the dead laid with pots, iron tools and horse gear, and one Brahmagiri grave with 33 gold beads beside others with a few pots tells of rich and poor, chiefs and followers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
