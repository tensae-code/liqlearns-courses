# Hands-on — India: Stories Ordinary People Told

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A king of monkeys lets his whole troop walk across his own stretched-out body to safety — and dies doing it. This lesson asks why ordinary people's animal tales were carved on the holiest monuments.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Jataka and Panchatantra tales were written down and carved on stupa railings — a monkey king of 80,000 followers makes his own body the last span of a branch-bridge over the Ganga so his troop escapes a human king, who, watching him die, mourns and honours him. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
