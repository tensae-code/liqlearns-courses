# Lesson plan — India: Stories Ordinary People Told

**Course:** History — India · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** India: Stories Ordinary People Told
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Buildings, Paintings and Books (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Jataka and Panchatantra tales were written down and carved on stupa railings — a monkey king of 80,000 followers makes his own body the last span of a branch-bridge over the Ganga so his troop escapes a human king, who, watching him die, mourns and honours him.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — Buildings, Paintings and Books (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Jataka and Panchatantra tales were written down and carved on stupa railings — a monkey king of 80,000 followers makes his own body the last span of a branch-bridge over the Ganga so his troop escapes a human king, who, watching him die, mourns and honours him.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a fable you heard as a child with an animal hero and a moral — the Jatakas are its ancestors.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
