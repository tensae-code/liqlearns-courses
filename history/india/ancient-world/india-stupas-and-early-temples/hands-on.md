# Hands-on — India: Stupas and Early Temples

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Who pays for a great building? A king, surely. This lesson reads the donor lists carved on the railings — and finds ivory workers, monks, nuns and hundreds of ordinary names.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A stupa grows around a relic casket — earth, brick, carved stone slabs, a railed pradakshina path devotees walk clockwise — Sanchi's brick core is Ashokan, its gateways later gifts; early temples centre on the garbhagriha under a shikhara tower with a mandapa hall, and at Mahabalipuram whole temples are cut top-down from single rocks — all paid for by kings, queens and hundreds of ordinary devotees whose names survive carved on railings. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
