# Hands-on — India: The Gupta Empire

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
One conqueror, four kinds of enemy, four different treatments — destroy, defeat-and-release, bind, or accept gifts from afar. This lesson studies conquest as a menu, chosen dish by dish.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Samudragupta conquered with four policies — Aryavarta's nine kings uprooted and annexed, Dakshinapatha's twelve defeated and restored, neighbours bound by tribute and court attendance, and distant Kushana, Shaka and Sri Lankan rulers submitting with gifts and daughters — while prashasti genealogies show the family climbing from maha-raja to maharaj-adhiraja, and Chandragupta II finished the Shakas in the west. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
