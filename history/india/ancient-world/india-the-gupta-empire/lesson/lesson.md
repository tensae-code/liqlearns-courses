# India: The Gupta Empire

## Do it first

One conqueror, four kinds of enemy, four different treatments — destroy, defeat-and-release, bind, or accept gifts from afar. This lesson studies conquest as a menu, chosen dish by dish.

Samudragupta's prashasti reads like a strategy manual, because it sorts his enemies into four rings and treats each ring differently. In Aryavarta, the near north, nine kings were uprooted and their lands annexed outright. In Dakshinapatha, the far south, twelve kings were defeated — then restored to their thrones as subordinates; too far to rule directly, they were ruled through gratitude and fear. Neighbouring lands — Assam, Nepal, the old gana-sanghas — were bound by tribute and personal attendance at court. And the distant powers, Kushanas, Shakas and the king of Sri Lanka, merely submitted with gifts and daughters for the imperial family. The family's own genealogies chart the climb: from maha-raja, great king, to maharaj-adhiraja, king over kings. His son Chandragupta II completed the design, destroying the Shakas of the west at last.
