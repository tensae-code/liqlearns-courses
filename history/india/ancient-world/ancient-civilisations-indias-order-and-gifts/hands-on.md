# Hands-on — India's Order and Gifts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine being told on the day you are born what work you must do forever, and that your children must do the same. This lesson tells how that sorting was described, how it hardened — and how people resisted it from the start.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The hymns sort people into orders — brahmin priests and rajas, jana and vish communities, Aryas against Dasas — and the later Vedic books harden the sorting into four varnas by birth, branding some people untouchable; but many pushed back against the idea that birth should fix a life. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
