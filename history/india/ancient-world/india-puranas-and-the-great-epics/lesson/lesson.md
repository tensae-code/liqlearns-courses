# India: Puranas and the Great Epics

## Do it first

Two cousins' families go to war over a throne, and a prince is exiled to the forest for fourteen years. You may already know these stories. This lesson asks when they were written down, and who was allowed to hear the sacred books at all.

Beside the Vedas — which women and shudras were barred from hearing — grew books meant for everyone. The Puranas retell the stories of gods, the creation of the world and the genealogies of kings in simple Sanskrit verse, composed to be recited aloud to any listener. And the two great epics reached the forms we know about 1500 years ago. The Mahabharata tells of a war between cousins, the Pandavas and Kauravas, for the throne of Hastinapura — and carries inside it the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna's counsel to the hesitant warrior Arjuna. The Ramayana tells of Rama, exiled to the forest, his wife Sita abducted by the demon-king Ravana, and Rama's victorious return. Written down late, carried in memory and performance for centuries: these are the subcontinent's shared story-store.
