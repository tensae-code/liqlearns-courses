# Hands-on — India: Puranas and the Great Epics

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two cousins' families go to war over a throne, and a prince is exiled to the forest for fourteen years. You may already know these stories. This lesson asks when they were written down, and who was allowed to hear the sacred books at all.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Puranas retell gods, creation and kings in simple Sanskrit verse meant to be heard by everyone — women and shudras included, though barred from the Vedas — while the Mahabharata's cousin-war for Hastinapura, with the Gita inside it, and the Ramayana's exiled Rama, abducted Sita and victorious return were written down in the forms we know about 1500 years ago. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
