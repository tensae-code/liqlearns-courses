# Hands-on — India: Janapadas and Mahajanapadas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a border around your town and call everything inside it yours to rule. What do you suddenly need that a wandering chief never did? This lesson lists the bill — walls, soldiers, taxes — and who paid it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Rajas of peoples become rajas of lands — janapadas, where 'the jana set its foot' — ringed by fortified capitals, fed by Painted Grey Ware feasts, and soon needing salaried year-round armies paid by real taxes: bhaga, a sixth of the crop, as iron ploughshares and transplanted paddy multiply the harvest on the bent backs of dasas and landless kammakaras. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
