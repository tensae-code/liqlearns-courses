# India: Harshavardhana

## Do it first

A younger son never meant to rule becomes an emperor — then meets the one river he cannot cross. This lesson follows Harsha's rise, his revenge, and his wall.

Harsha was never meant to be king. He was a younger son of Thanesar; two deaths — his father's, then his elder brother's — put the kingdom on his shoulders, and a third death gave him his cause: his brother-in-law, the king of Kanauj, was murdered. Harsha avenged him, took Kanauj for himself, and conquered eastward to Bengal, becoming master of the north. Then he marched south — and at the river Narmada met the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II, who stopped him cold. The Narmada stayed his border. We know this reign unusually well, twice over: his court poet Banabhatta wrote his biography, the Harshacharita, and the pilgrim Xuan Zang lived at his court and described it from outside. Two witnesses, one king.
