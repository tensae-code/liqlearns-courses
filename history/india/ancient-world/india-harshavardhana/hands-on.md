# Hands-on — India: Harshavardhana

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A younger son never meant to rule becomes an emperor — then meets the one river he cannot cross. This lesson follows Harsha's rise, his revenge, and his wall.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Not the eldest son, Harsha inherited Thanesar on two deaths, avenged his murdered brother-in-law by taking Kanauj and conquering east to Bengal — but at the Narmada the Chalukya Pulakeshin II stopped him cold; his court poet Banabhatta's biography and Xuan Zang's stay at his court give us the reign twice over. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
