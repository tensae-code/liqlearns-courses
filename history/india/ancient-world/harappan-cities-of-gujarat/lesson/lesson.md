# Harappan Cities of Gujarat

## Do it first

Write your name as big as you can on a board. Who is it meant for — people near, or people far away? This lesson visits a city that wrote its letters giant-sized, and a port that stamped its cargo shut.

Two Gujarat cities show the Harappan world at its most inventive. Dholavira divides not into two parts but three, each walled — and above its gateway hung a signboard of giant letters, ten signs of the Harappan script inlaid in white stone, one of the earliest public signs in the world. Lothal, by the sea, has a great brick tank that may have been a dockyard where ships floated in to load. Its warehouse held seals and sealings — lumps of clay stamped with a seal and tied to goods-bags. Break the clay and you broke the proof: the bag had been opened. Intact, it guaranteed the contents, across deserts and seas.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Dholavira splits into three walled parts and displays giant letters of the Harappan script in white stone, while Lothal's great tank may be a dockyard, its warehouse full of seals and clay sealings that proved a goods-bag had arrived untampered. Give one real example from your own life — a shop sign and a sealed parcel today — display and proof of untampered goods are both Harappan ideas — and name the lesson's key words: Harappan Cities of Gujarat, Dholavira, Lothal, sealings. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
