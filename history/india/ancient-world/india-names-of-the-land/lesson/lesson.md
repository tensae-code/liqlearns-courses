# India: Names of the Land

## Do it first

Say the names of your own country, town or street out loud. Where did each name come from — who gave it, and in what language? This lesson tells how one great land got two names from two directions.

Names travel and change in other people's mouths. Travellers from Iran and Greece reached a great river the local people called the Sindhu. In Iranian ears it became Hindos; in Greek, Indos — and the land lying east of that river became, to the outside world, 'India'. The land's other name came from inside: the Rigveda, the oldest book of the subcontinent, names a people called the Bharata, and from them the land is also Bharat. Two names, two directions — one from a river heard by strangers, one from a people remembered in song.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The land has two great names with two different stories: 'India' comes from the river Sindhu, heard by Iranians and Greeks as Hindos and Indos, while 'Bharat' comes from the Bharata people named in the Rigveda. Give one real example from your own life — your own name — it was given by somebody, in some language, for some reason, and it carries a story — and name the lesson's key words: India Names of the Land, Sindhu, Bharat. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
