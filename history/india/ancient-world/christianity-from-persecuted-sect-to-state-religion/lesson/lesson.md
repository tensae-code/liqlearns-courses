# Christianity: From Persecuted Sect to State Religion

## Do it first

A teaching starts in one small province of an empire that will try to crush it. Within a few centuries the empire itself adopts it. This lesson opens that astonishing reversal — the chapter's 'Elsewhere' window.

The chapter of traders and pilgrims closes by looking elsewhere — to West Asia, about 2000 years ago. There, the teaching of Jesus began a new faith: Christianity. It started small, one teacher and his followers inside a vast empire, and it would be persecuted before it was powerful. But it was born in a connected world — the same world of monsoon sailors, Silk Route caravans and port cities this course has been tracing — and along those very routes the faith would travel, east toward India and west toward Rome, until the persecuted sect became the state religion of empires. Ideas, like silk and pepper, move along roads built for trade.
