# Persia: Cyrus, Roads, Post and Zoroaster

## Do it first

Three short rules for a whole life: think good, speak good, do good. Could a faith stand on just that? This lesson crosses to Iran, India's old cousin, to meet the prophet who taught it.

The 'Elsewhere' of this chapter is Iran — the subcontinent's old cousin. Its prophet Zoroaster taught a faith summed in six words: Good thoughts, Good Words and Good Deeds. Its hymns, the Avesta, are written in a language so close to Vedic Sanskrit that the two peoples clearly share deep roots. Zoroaster's faith was Iran's for a thousand years. Then history turned: migrants carrying the faith sailed east and landed on the coasts of Gujarat and Maharashtra. Their descendants are the Parsis of India today — a small community carrying one of the world's oldest living religions, planted by a sea voyage.
