# Video script — World Religions: The Five Main Faiths

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From India's sixth-century-BCE ferment come teachings the whole world still lives by: the Buddha's path out of suffering through moderation and kindness, taught in the people's Prakrit; Mahavira's Jain ahimsa so strict it spares even insects; and, centuries later, bhakti — devotion to a chosen deity, open to rich and poor, high caste and low, men and women alike.

The Buddha taught that suffering comes from tanha — an unquenchable thirst for more — and ends in moderation and kindness; he taught in Prakrit so all could understand, and Kisagotami, sent for mustard seed from a house where no one had died, learned that every house knows death.

Vardhamana Mahavira, the 24th tirthankara, taught ahimsa so strict that followers must not kill even insects; traders embraced it, farmers struggled, and the teachings, carried orally for centuries, were written down at Valabhi about 1500 years ago.

Bhakti — devotion to a chosen deity such as Shiva, Vishnu or Durga — opened worship to rich and poor, high caste and low, men and women alike, without elaborate sacrifice; the Gita's counsel and the Tamil poet Appar's hymn carried it across the country.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
