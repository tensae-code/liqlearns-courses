# India: The Ajanta Paintings

## Do it first

Paint a picture in a dark cave, by torchlight, on a rock wall — and make it glow for 1,500 years. This lesson enters Ajanta, where artists did exactly that and signed nothing.

At Ajanta, monks cut their monasteries and prayer halls into a horseshoe of cliffs — and artists covered the dark interiors with paintings. Picture the working conditions: no windows, light only from torches and oil lamps, walls of rough rock smoothed with plaster. In that darkness, unknown artists painted vivid, crowded scenes — kings and beggars, animals, dancers, stories of the Buddha's lives — in colours ground from plants and minerals. Fifteen hundred years later, the colours still glow. We do not know a single painter's name. The lesson of Ajanta is double: supreme art, and total anonymity — the work was the signature.
