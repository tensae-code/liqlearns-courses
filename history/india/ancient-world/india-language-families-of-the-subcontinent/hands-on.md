# Hands-on — India: Language Families of the Subcontinent

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say 'mother' in every language you know or have heard. Listen to how alike some of them sound. This lesson turns that small surprise into a map of the whole subcontinent.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The subcontinent's languages fall into great families: Sanskrit, Hindi and English share one Indo-European family — matr, ma, mother — while Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam are Dravidian, and the north-east and central India hold Tibeto-Burman and Austro-Asiatic tongues. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
