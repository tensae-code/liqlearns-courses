# India: Language Families of the Subcontinent

## Do it first

Say 'mother' in every language you know or have heard. Listen to how alike some of them sound. This lesson turns that small surprise into a map of the whole subcontinent.

Listen to three words: Sanskrit matr, Hindi ma, English mother. They are too alike to be an accident — they are cousins, descended from one ancestor language. Sanskrit and Hindi belong to the Indo-European family, which stretches from India to Europe and includes English itself. But the subcontinent holds other great families too. Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam form the Dravidian family of the south, with roots of their own. The north-east speaks Tibeto-Burman languages; central India keeps Austro-Asiatic ones. The map of languages is a map of many peoples, layered over thousands of years.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The subcontinent's languages fall into great families: Sanskrit, Hindi and English share one Indo-European family — matr, ma, mother — while Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam are Dravidian, and the north-east and central India hold Tibeto-Burman and Austro-Asiatic tongues. Give one real example from your own life — the languages in one classroom or market — a living map of the same families the book draws — and name the lesson's key words: India Language Families of the Subcontinent, Indo-European, Dravidian, matr ma mother. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
