# India: Ancient Science and Medicine

## Do it first

Nothing — write it as a number. You just used an Indian invention. This lesson meets the scientists who gave the world zero, spun the earth to explain the day, and founded a medicine still practised.

The age wrote science as carefully as scripture. Aryabhata's Aryabhatiyam explains day and night not by the sun circling us, but by the earth turning on its own axis; it gives eclipses their true, scientific cause — shadows, not demons — and a value for a circle's circumference startlingly close to the modern one. Indian mathematicians of this world made an even quieter revolution: they invented the symbol for zero. Nothing, given a sign, made place-value arithmetic possible — and the invention travelled, carried by Arab scholars to Europe and then the world. Medicine had its own founders: Charaka, whose Samhita systematised Ayurveda's healing, and Sushruta, whose Samhita describes surgery itself. The course that began with stone tools ends with the turning earth, the zero, and the surgeon — one civilisation, still teaching.
