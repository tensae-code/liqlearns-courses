# Hands-on — India: Ancient Science and Medicine

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Nothing — write it as a number. You just used an Indian invention. This lesson meets the scientists who gave the world zero, spun the earth to explain the day, and founded a medicine still practised.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Aryabhata's Aryabhatiyam explains day and night by the earth's own rotation and gives eclipses a scientific cause and a near-modern value for a circle's circumference; Indian mathematicians invented the symbol for zero, which travelled via the Arabs to Europe and the world; Charaka and Sushruta founded Ayurveda's medicine and surgery in their Samhitas. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
