# Hands-on — India: The Ashvamedha and the New Rajas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine proving you are the strongest leader around without fighting a single battle — by sending one horse walking through everyone's land. This lesson explains how that strange, brilliant test worked.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A new kind of raja proves his power with the ashvamedha: a sacrificial horse wanders a year under guard — kings who stop it must fight, kings who let it pass admit his power and come bearing gifts to the sacrifice, where he sits enthroned while his charioteer sings his glory. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
