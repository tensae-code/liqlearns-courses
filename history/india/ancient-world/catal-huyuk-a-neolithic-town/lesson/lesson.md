# Çatal Höyük: A Neolithic Town

## Do it first

Look at three objects in your home that came from far away. How did they get to you? This lesson's town had the same puzzle — 9,000 years ago, with no trucks, no ships' containers, only pack animals.

The second 'Elsewhere' window opens on Turkey, at Çatal Höyük — one of the great Neolithic farming towns. Its houses and graves hold a surprise: things that do not belong to its soil at all. Flint, the sharp stone for tools, came from far-off Syria. Cowrie shells came from the Red Sea. Other shells came from the Mediterranean. None of these travelled by themselves: they arrived on the backs of pack animals, passed hand to hand along long routes. The lesson is plain — a farming town was never a world apart. From the very beginning, settlements were plugged into exchange that crossed hundreds of kilometres.
