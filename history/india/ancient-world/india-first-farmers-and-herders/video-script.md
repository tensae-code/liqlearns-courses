# Video script — India: First Farmers and Herders

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Farming forced invention and a new kind of life: surplus grain needed pots, baskets and storage pits, tended animals became a living store of milk and meat, and at Burzahom and Mehrgarh the settled village appears — houses, grinding stones, and the dead buried with goats for the next world.

Surplus grain forced invention — clay pots, woven baskets and storage pits; tended animals became a living store of milk and meat; burnt grain and animal bones are how scientists identify the first farming sites.

At Burzahom in Kashmir people dug pit-houses with steps and cooked indoors or out by the weather; polished Neolithic tools, mortars for grinding, cooking pots and woven cotton cloth mark the settled turn.

At Mehrgarh, by the Bolan Pass route to Iran, square houses stored the barley and wheat people first grew there; a dead person was buried with goats — food, the mourners believed, for the next world.
