# Hands-on — India: First Farmers and Herders

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine your family harvests more grain than you can eat this month. What do you do with the rest — and what new problems does 'extra' create? This lesson is how the first farming villages answered.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Farming forced invention and a new kind of life: surplus grain needed pots, baskets and storage pits, tended animals became a living store of milk and meat, and at Burzahom and Mehrgarh the settled village appears — houses, grinding stones, and the dead buried with goats for the next world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
