# Hands-on — India: The Buddha

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A young prince has everything — comfort, family, a future throne. He walks away from all of it to ask why people suffer. This lesson follows his road, step by step.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Siddhartha Gautama, a kshatriya of the small Sakya gana, left home comfort, debated other thinkers for years, then meditated under a peepal tree at Bodh Gaya until enlightenment made him the Buddha; he taught first at Sarnath and walked and taught until his death at Kusinara. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
