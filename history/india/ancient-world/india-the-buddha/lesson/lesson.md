# India: The Buddha

## Do it first

A young prince has everything — comfort, family, a future throne. He walks away from all of it to ask why people suffer. This lesson follows his road, step by step.

Siddhartha Gautama was a kshatriya, a prince of the Sakya gana — one of the republics of the age. Troubled by the sight of suffering, old age, sickness and death, he left the comfort of home to find an answer. For years he debated with other thinkers and practised hard disciplines. Then, at Bodh Gaya, he sat in meditation beneath a peepal tree and stayed until understanding came: enlightenment. From that moment he was the Buddha, the Awakened One. He gave his first teaching at Sarnath, near Varanasi — and then simply walked, teaching from village to town, for the rest of his long life, until his death at Kusinara.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Siddhartha Gautama, a kshatriya of the small Sakya gana, left home comfort, debated other thinkers for years, then meditated under a peepal tree at Bodh Gaya until enlightenment made him the Buddha; he taught first at Sarnath and walked and taught until his death at Kusinara. Give one real example from your own life — anyone who has left a comfortable path to find a truer one — the Buddha's road begins with that same refusal — and name the lesson's key words: India The Buddha, Siddhartha Gautama, Bodh Gaya, Sarnath. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
