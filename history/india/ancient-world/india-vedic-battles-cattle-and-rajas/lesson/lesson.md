# India: Vedic Battles, Cattle and Rajas

## Do it first

If wealth walked on four legs and could be stolen in a night raid, how would your whole society organise itself? This lesson enters a world where cattle were money, and prayer asked for herds.

What did people pray for? The Rigveda's hymns answer plainly: cattle, sons and horses. Cattle were wealth — milk, meat, and more cattle — and wealth that walks can be raided. So battles were fought for herds, for pasture, for water, and for captives. The spoils of victory fed great fire-sacrifices, where the chief shared out his gains in public. And who led? Assemblies of men chose the bravest warriors as rajas. But do not picture a later king: these rajas had no capital city, no palace, no standing army and no taxes. Leadership was earned in the raid and renewed in the assembly.

Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
