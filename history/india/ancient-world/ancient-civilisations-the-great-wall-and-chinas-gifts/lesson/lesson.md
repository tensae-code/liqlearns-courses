# Ancient Civilisations: The Great Wall and China's Gifts

## Do it first

Two empires face the same problem — powerful neighbours beyond the border. One builds a wall thousands of kilometres long. The other sends messengers with a teaching. This lesson sets the two answers side by side.

The chapter's 'Elsewhere' sets two empires' answers side by side. Somewhat before Ashoka's time, the emperors of China began joining and raising walls along their northern frontier — a barrier of stone and brick, eventually some 6,400 kilometres long, against the pastoral peoples of the steppe. Nor was it one emperor's work: rulers kept adding to the Great Wall for 2,000 years. The book leaves the comparison to the reader. China answered its neighbours with the longest wall on earth. Ashoka, facing the same world, sent messengers carrying dhamma. Wall, or word — history keeps both experiments running.
