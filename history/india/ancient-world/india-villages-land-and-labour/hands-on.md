# Hands-on — India: Villages, Land and Labour

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Follow one sack of grain in a village: who grew it, who takes a share, who eats last? This lesson maps the village as a working machine — and a stacked one.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Kings tax farmers, farmers demand surer crops, irrigation follows; and the village stacks its own order — in Tamil country vellalar landowners, uzhavar ploughmen and landless kadaisiyar and adimai labourers, while in the north the hereditary grama bhojaka is landlord, tax-collector, judge and policeman in one. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
