# India: The Iron Pillar and Metallurgy

## Do it first

Iron rusts. Everyone knows iron rusts. This lesson stands before 3 tonnes of iron that has refused to rust for 1,500 years of monsoons — and asks how.

The last chapter opens with a riddle in metal. At Mehrauli in Delhi stands an iron pillar, 7.2 metres tall and weighing over 3 tonnes, raised about 1500 years ago in honour of a Gupta king named Chandra. Iron pillars should rust — that is what iron does, especially through fifteen centuries of monsoon rain. This one has not rusted. Its surface stays smooth and bright, a standing proof of the skill of India's early metal-workers, heirs of the Harappan bronze-masters who opened this course. How exactly they achieved it remains part riddle — scientists still study the pillar's skin. Some lessons end with an answer. This one ends with a monument that is still asking its question.
