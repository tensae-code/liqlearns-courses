# Hands-on — India: The Iron Pillar and Metallurgy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Iron rusts. Everyone knows iron rusts. This lesson stands before 3 tonnes of iron that has refused to rust for 1,500 years of monsoons — and asks how.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Delhi's Mehrauli iron pillar — 7.2 m tall, over 3 tonnes, raised about 1500 years ago for a Gupta king named Chandra — has not rusted in fifteen centuries, a standing riddle from the heirs of Harappan bronze-masters. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
