# India: Craft Guilds and Women's Work

## Do it first

A guild that trains you, buys your materials, and banks money like a modern bank. And a rule-book that tells a boss he may not chat with the women spinning for him. This lesson opens the workshop doors of the ancient city.

The city's crafts ran on organisation. Potters fired the mirror-black luxury called Northern Black Polished Ware; the weavers of Varanasi and Madurai made cloth famous across the land. Behind the crafts stood the shreni — the guild. A shreni trained workers, bought raw material for all its members, set standards — and acted as a bank, accepting deposits whose interest could be gifted to endow a monastery forever. And the state's own rule-book, the Arthashastra, shows us the workforce's hidden half: widows, and women retired from temple service, spinning thread for wages in state workshops — under a superintendent so strictly regulated that he was forbidden to speak to them of anything but the work.
