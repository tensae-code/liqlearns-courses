# Hands-on — India: Craft Guilds and Women's Work

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A guild that trains you, buys your materials, and banks money like a modern bank. And a rule-book that tells a boss he may not chat with the women spinning for him. This lesson opens the workshop doors of the ancient city.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Craft and merchant guilds — shrenis — trained workers, bought raw material and even banked deposits whose interest endowed monasteries, while the Arthashastra's workshop rules show widows and retired temple women spinning for wages under a superintendent forbidden even to talk to them of anything but work. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
