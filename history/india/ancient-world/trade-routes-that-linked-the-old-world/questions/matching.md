# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World — matching

Match each part with its meaning. Every item is marked with its sub lesson.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) In search of raw materials — a)

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A closer look — Arikamedu — b)

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A poem about trade; sea routes and monsoon winds — c)

**4.** (Sub lesson 4) The story of the Silk Route — d)


Meanings:
a) The Harappan cities reached out for raw materials: copper from Rajasthan and Oman, tin from Afghanistan and Iran, gold from Karnataka, precious stones from Gujarat, Iran and Afghanistan..
b) At Arikamedu on the Puducherry coast, a brick warehouse port of 2200–1900 years ago unloaded Mediterranean amphorae and Italian Arretine ware and made Roman-designed pottery locally..
c) A Sangam poem lists Puhar's imports — horses by ship, pepper by cart, Himalayan gems, southern pearls, foodstuffs from Sri Lanka, pottery from Myanmar — and sailors learned to ride the south-west monsoon across open sea..
d) China's guarded silk secret travelled by foot, horse and camel until Rome's rich wore it at ruinous prices; the Kushanas held the route's heart from Peshawar and Mathura, protected and taxed the traders, and minted some of the subcontinent's earliest gold coins..

**Answer key:** 1. a; 2. b; 3. c; 4. d
