# Video script — Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From Harappan ports reaching to Oman to the Roman warehouse at Arikamedu, from monsoon sailors to the Silk Route the Kushanas guarded, India's trade always ran on the same fuel: raw materials and luxuries moving between people who had different things.

The Harappan cities reached out for raw materials: copper from Rajasthan and Oman, tin from Afghanistan and Iran, gold from Karnataka, precious stones from Gujarat, Iran and Afghanistan.

At Arikamedu on the Puducherry coast, a brick warehouse port of 2200–1900 years ago unloaded Mediterranean amphorae and Italian Arretine ware and made Roman-designed pottery locally.

A Sangam poem lists Puhar's imports — horses by ship, pepper by cart, Himalayan gems, southern pearls, foodstuffs from Sri Lanka, pottery from Myanmar — and sailors learned to ride the south-west monsoon across open sea.

China's guarded silk secret travelled by foot, horse and camel until Rome's rich wore it at ruinous prices; the Kushanas held the route's heart from Peshawar and Mathura, protected and taxed the traders, and minted some of the subcontinent's earliest gold coins.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
