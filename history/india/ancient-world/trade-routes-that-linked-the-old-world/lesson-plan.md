# Lesson plan — Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

**Course:** History — India · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Trade Routes That Linked the Old World
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The First Cities and the Vedic World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From Harappan ports reaching to Oman to the Roman warehouse at Arikamedu, from monsoon sailors to the Silk Route the Kushanas guarded, India's trade always ran on the same fuel: raw materials and luxuries moving between people who had different things.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The First Cities and the Vedic World (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **In search of raw materials** — The Harappan cities reach out — copper from Rajasthan and Oman, tin from Afghanistan and Iran, gold from Karnataka, precious stones from Gujarat, Iran and Afghanistan.
2. **A closer look — Arikamedu** — On the Puducherry coast a brick warehouse port (2200–1900 years ago) unloads Mediterranean amphorae and Italian Arretine ware and makes Roman-designed pottery locally — India's sea-trade with Rome dug out of the ground.
3. **A poem about trade; sea routes and monsoon winds** — A Sangam poem inventories Puhar's imports — horses by ship, pepper by cart, Himalayan gems, southern pearls, Sri Lankan foodstuffs, Myanmar pottery; sailors learn to ride the south-west monsoon across the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.
4. **The story of the Silk Route** — China's guarded silk secret travels by foot, horse and camel until Rome's rich wear it at ruinous prices; the Kushanas grip the route's heart from Peshawar and Mathura, protect traders, tax them, and mint early gold coins.
