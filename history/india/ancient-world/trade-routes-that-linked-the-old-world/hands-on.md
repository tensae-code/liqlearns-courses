# Hands-on — Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List everything in your home that came from another country. Now imagine every one of those journeys done by camel, cart or a wooden ship waiting for the wind. This lesson walks those journeys in the ancient world.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From Harappan ports reaching to Oman to the Roman warehouse at Arikamedu, from monsoon sailors to the Silk Route the Kushanas guarded, India's trade always ran on the same fuel: raw materials and luxuries moving between people who had different things. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
