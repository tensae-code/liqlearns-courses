# About the History — India course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
NCERT *Our Pasts-I*, the Textbook in History for Class VI
(Social Science). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for India inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they
prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade
the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six levels**, derived from the book's
own story journey, in the book's order:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know, and the First
   Farmers.** The historian's craft learned from a child's
   question — many pasts, three clue-trails, one dating grid —
   then the land itself: its rivers, its two names, the moving
   hunter-gatherers of Bhimbetka and Kurnool, the three ages of
   prehistory, and the turn to farming at Burzahom and
   Mehrgarh, with France's painted caves and Çatal Höyük as
   the book's 'Elsewhere' mirrors.
2. **Level 2 — The First Cities and the Vedic World.** The
   Harappan cities whole — their near-loss and finding, the
   planned citadel and lower town, crafts and faience, the
   Gujarat ports, the trade in copper and tin, and their
   unexplained end — then the world of the hymns: the Rigveda
   carried in memory, the language families, cattle wealth and
   chosen rajas, varna claimed and contested, and the megalith
   graves that confess rich and poor.
3. **Level 3 — Kingdoms, Republics and New Questions.** The
   ashvamedha's walking challenge, walled janapadas and the
   first regular taxes, Magadha's geographic rise, Vajji's
   republic of thousands of rajas — and Athens set beside it
   and judged — then the sixth-century ferment: the Buddha and
   Kisagotami, the Upanishadic thinkers and the wise beggar,
   Mahavira's ahimsa, the sangha and its viharas, the ashrama
   answer, and Zoroaster's faith sailing to India.
4. **Level 4 — Empire and Exchange: Ashoka to the Pilgrims.**
   The Mauryan machine, Ashoka's horror at Kalinga and his
   dhamma of conduct, China's Great Wall as the counter-answer
   — then the world the empire framed: irrigated villages and
   their stacks, Sangam poetry as evidence, ring wells and
   Barygaza, punch-marked coins beside barter, many-functioned
   Mathura, the guilds and the women spinners, the three
   crowned kings, Buddhism's two great journeys, Fa Xian and
   Xuan Zang risking the seas for books, bhakti's open door,
   and Christianity's beginning in West Asia.
5. **Level 5 — New Empires and Kingdoms.** Samudragupta's
   four policies, Harsha stopped at the Narmada, the
   Pallava–Chalukya raids and Ravikirti's boasting poem,
   hereditary offices and land-holding samantas, the south's
   enduring assemblies — and the view from the roadside:
   Kalidasa's fisherman, Fa Xian's untouchables, Banabhatta's
   dust — closing on Arabia, Islam's first century, and Sind.
6. **Level 6 — Buildings, Paintings and Books.** The iron
   pillar that will not rust; stupas and early temples, their
   building stages and their hundreds of named donors;
   Ajanta's anonymous torch-lit masters; Kannagi's fire in the
   Tamil epics; the Puranas and the two great epics opened to
   every hearer; the monkey king's sacrifice from the people's
   story-store; and Aryabhata's turning earth, the invented
   zero, and Charaka's and Sushruta's medicine.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *One child's question, one long answer:* "Start with
  Rasheeda's question — how can anyone know? — and walk the
  whole answer: rivers and first farmers, brick cities and
  unread seals, hymns memorised for centuries, a republic of
  thousands, an emperor who gave up war, pilgrims who chose
  books over luggage, and painters who lit caves with colour —
  until the story lands on the paper it is printed on."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

India's own way, in this one book: NCERT teaches history as a
story journey — every chapter opens with a present-day child
(Rasheeda, Tushar, Jaspal…) whose question starts the chapter,
primary sources are quoted in boxes (the Rigveda dialogue,
Ashoka's own inscription, Fa Xian, Banabhatta), an 'Elsewhere'
box sets India beside the wider world each chapter, and kings
are taught alongside ordinary people — farmers, fishers,
weavers, slaves — with the book's title itself plural: Our
Pasts, because different groups lived different pasts.
*(Student book.)*
