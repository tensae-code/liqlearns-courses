# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Lay out three clues about one event in your own life — a photograph, something you own, and a story someone tells about it. Which clue could mislead you? This lesson is the book's masterclass in exactly that question.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written history is built from evidence that must be read critically: manuscripts and inscriptions, the archaeologist's dig, a hymn mined for the life inside it, a foreign ambassador's amazed account, a flattering praise-poem checked against the map, and even a made-up tale used carefully beside sculpture and travellers' reports. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
