# From Evidence to a Written History — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the parts of this lesson, in order.
**Answer:** Finding out about the past; How historians study the Rigveda; The emperor and the capital city; Finding out about cities; Prashastis and what they tell us.

**2.** (Sub lesson 5) Why does this lesson matter to a learner in India?
**Answer:** Because a written history is built from evidence that must be read critically: manuscripts and inscriptions, the archaeologist's dig, a hymn mined for the life inside it, a foreign ambassador's amazed account, a flattering praise-poem checked against the map, and even a made-up tale used carefully beside sculpture and travellers' reports. — and a learner who understands it can take their real place in that shared story.
