# From Evidence to a Written History — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Palm-leaf manuscripts, stone inscriptions — like Ashoka's Kandahar inscription carved in Greek and Aramaic — and the archaeologist's dig for tools, pots and coins are the three clue-trails to the past.
**Answer: True**

**2.** (Sub lesson 5) This lesson's teaching has nothing to do with the life of ordinary people in India.
**Answer: False** — A written history is built from evidence that must be read critically: manuscripts and inscriptions, the archaeologist's dig, a hymn mined for the life inside it, a foreign ambassador's amazed account, a flattering praise-poem checked against the map, and even a made-up tale used carefully beside sculpture and travellers' reports.
