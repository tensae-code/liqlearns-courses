# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Write your birth year on a line. Now mark where your grandparents were born, and where your town was founded. Which of those can you give an exact year for — and which only a 'about'? This lesson gives you the grid historians use.

What do dates mean? Every chapter of the book carries boxes of 'some important dates', and they all stand on one grid. Years are counted from the birth of Christ. Years after it are AD — Anno Domini — also written CE, Common Era, and they climb: 500, 1000, 2000. Years before it are BC — Before Christ — also written BCE, and they count down as they come forward: 1000 BC is earlier than 500 BC. For the deepest past, where exact years dissolve, historians use BP: before present. One grid, three labels — learn it once and every date in this course unlocks.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Dates are counted on a grid the whole world shares: years run forward from the birth of Christ (AD, also called CE) and backwards before it (BC, also called BCE), with BP — before present — for the deep past beyond counting. Give one real example from your own life — your own family timeline — exact dates for the living, 'about' dates for the long dead, exactly like the historian's grid — and name the lesson's key words: Dating the Past, AD CE, BC BCE, BP. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
