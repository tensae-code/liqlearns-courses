# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — India · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know, and the First Farmers (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is detective work on the past: nobody alive saw it happen, so we rebuild it from the clues it left behind — and there was never just one past, because kings and herders, farmers and fishers each lived a different one.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know, and the First Farmers (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Rasheeda's question** — A schoolgirl reads a headline about a hundred years ago and asks how anyone can know — historians answer by following clues like detectives.
2. **One past or many?** — Kings kept records of their victories while ordinary people kept none, so the past was different for different people — and archaeology must speak for those who left no records.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson on the path — nothing is recommended before it; a learner can start here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History is detective work on the past: nobody alive saw it happen, so we rebuild it from the clues it left behind — and there was never just one past, because kings and herders, farmers and fishers each lived a different one.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — your own family's past — a grandparent's childhood is a past no newspaper recorded, and it is still real history.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks history is only the story of kings → the book opens by insisting the past of herders and farmers was not the past of kings, and both are history.
- Go back to the one thing: History is detective work on the past: nobody alive saw it happen, so we rebuild it from the clues it left behind — and there was never just one past, because kings and herders, farmers and fishers each lived a different one. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
