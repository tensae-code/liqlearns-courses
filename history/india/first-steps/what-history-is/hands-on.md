# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Read a newspaper headline about something that happened long before you were born. Ask out loud: how can anyone know that? Write down every way you can think of for finding out. This lesson is the book's own answer to that question.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is detective work on the past: nobody alive saw it happen, so we rebuild it from the clues it left behind — and there was never just one past, because kings and herders, farmers and fishers each lived a different one. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
