# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Sort three handfuls of imaginary tools: big rough stones, tiny sharp flakes, and smooth polished axes. Which handful is oldest? This short lesson gives you the archaeologists' own sorting system.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Archaeologists organise the deep past before writing into three ages: the Palaeolithic (2 million to 12,000 years ago), the Mesolithic of the tiny microliths, and the Neolithic — labels that are themselves a lesson in how prehistory is studied. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
