# Who Studies Prehistory

## Do it first

Sort three handfuls of imaginary tools: big rough stones, tiny sharp flakes, and smooth polished axes. Which handful is oldest? This short lesson gives you the archaeologists' own sorting system.

Who studies prehistory, and how do they keep it in order? Archaeologists slice the deep time before writing into three ages, named from the Greek for stone. The Palaeolithic, the Old Stone Age, runs from about 2 million years ago to 12,000 years ago — the long age of rough stone tools and hunting-gathering. The Mesolithic, the Middle Stone Age, is marked by microliths: tiny stone blades, set in rows into wood or bone to make tools. Then comes the Neolithic, the New Stone Age, of polished tools and the first farming. The labels are not decoration — each one names a different way of living.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Archaeologists organise the deep past before writing into three ages: the Palaeolithic (2 million to 12,000 years ago), the Mesolithic of the tiny microliths, and the Neolithic — labels that are themselves a lesson in how prehistory is studied. Give one real example from your own life — sorting old family objects into 'very old, less old, recent' — the archaeologist does the same, with stricter rules — and name the lesson's key words: Who Studies Prehistory, Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
