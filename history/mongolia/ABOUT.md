# About the History — Mongolia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Mongolia's own
Ministry of Education, Culture, Science and Sports student
books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box
is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Mongolia inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **six levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How Mongolia Learns Its Past.**
   What history is, dating the past, from evidence to a
   written history, and heritage — 4 lessons, 19 placements.
2. **Level 2 — The Ancient World and the First Steppe
   States.** From stone tools to farming, the first
   settlements and civilisations (Mesopotamia, Egypt, India,
   China, Greece, Rome), and the steppe's own first states —
   Xiongnu, Xianbei and Rouran, the Turkic khaganates —
   14 lessons, 21 placements.
3. **Level 3 — The Medieval World Before Chinggis.** The
   Mongols before Chinggis, the Great Migration, the Franks,
   feudal Europe, Byzantium, Kievan Rus', the Maya, Aztecs,
   Islam, Tang and Song China, medieval India and Japan —
   15 lessons, 21 placements.
4. **Level 4 — Chinggis and the Mongol Empire.** Chinggis
   and the founding of the Mongol state, the empire and its
   khanates (Yuan, Golden Horde, Chagatai, Ilkhanate), and
   the high medieval West — 6 lessons, 18 placements.
5. **Level 5 — Mongolia and the World, 15th–Early 20th
   Centuries.** Mongolia after the empire, under the Manchu
   Qing, its Buddhism, life and culture and resistance —
   interleaved, as the books interleave it, with
   exploration, the Reformation, the Renaissance, the
   Enlightenment, capitalism and the Industrial Revolution
   — 22 lessons, 38 placements.
6. **Level 6 — Restored, Socialist and Democratic Mongolia,
   1911–Today.** The 1911 restoration, the Bogd Khanate,
   1921, the socialist path, collectivisation, the
   repressions, the Second World War, the UN, the collapse
   of socialism, the 1990 democratic revolution, the market
   transition and the third-neighbour foreign policy —
   23 lessons, 51 placements.

**Totals: 84 lessons, 168 placements stocked** — every
placement in the filing stocked into exactly one lesson.
All 84 lessons stand on **student books** and count in the
acceptance reckoning below.

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

Mongolia's books teach Mongolian and world history as ONE
interleaved story — Mongolia first in each period, then the
world — so pupils always meet their own khans, treaties and
rebellions beside Europe's and Asia's. The first History
book opens with a detective story (Ötzi the Iceman) to teach
what a source is, then runs the whole ancient world always
bending back to Mongolia: the nomad ancestors, their
monuments, and the Xiongnu as the nomads' first state. The
medieval book is organised around one hinge — the "Chinggis
era": everything before it is "the world before Chinggis",
everything after is measured against the empire. Modern
history is one national arc in three acts — the restored
state (1911–1924), socialist Mongolia (1924–1990) and
democratic Mongolia (1990–today). The senior books re-read
the whole national past at a higher level, every chapter
built around a key question, a source to analyse and a
"think historically" problem. Every topic in the younger
books is built from source extracts, timelines and "ask a
question, pose a problem, hypothesise" boxes. This course
keeps that shape: the same lessons deepen in place as the
senior books revisit them.
