# First Settlements and the Birth of Civilisation

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — First Settlements and the Birth of Civilisation. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 3.1 Where and how did people first settle?

Beside the great rivers people settle for good, raise the first towns, and divide work between farmer, herder, craftworker and ruler. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 3.2 How did civilisation develop?

Writing, the wheel, irrigation and laws appear; a settled people's inventions become the pattern every later civilisation inherits. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: state your guesses from the start, then say how the real story was different.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a timeline or map of the story, with the turn marked.
