# The Mongol Empire and Its Khanates

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — The Mongol Empire and Its Khanates. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 4.2.1 Ögödei Khan consolidates the Great Mongol Empire

Ögödei builds Karakorum's walls, extends the yam and the law, and sends the armies that take Rus' and Hungary. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 4.2.2 The empire under Güyük and Möngke Khans

Regencies and succession struggles end with Möngke, whose census, religious tolerance and world-embracing court make the empire a single system. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 4.2.3 The Great Yuan state

Khubilai moves the capital to Khanbaliq (Beijing), takes the Chinese dynastic name Yuan in 1271 and completes the conquest of the Song. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. 4.2.4 The Mongols' Golden Horde state

Batu's ulus on the Volga rules the Russian principalities for two centuries — the "Mongol yoke" of Russian history. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. 4.2.5 The Mongols' Chagatai state

Chinggis's second son's ulus holds Central Asia's oases and steppe, astride the Silk Road. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. 4.2.6 The Mongols' Ilkhanate state

Hülegü takes Baghdad in 1258 and founds the Ilkhanate; his heirs adopt Islam and Persian culture. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. 3.4 The Mongol Empire after Chinggis Khan

Ögödei, Güyük, Möngke and Khubilai extend and administer the largest land empire ever — and its succession quarrels begin. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 8 — Ch. 3.5 The empire's breakup: Yuan, Golden Horde, Chagatai and Ilkhanate

One empire becomes four khanates, each going its own way — Yuan in China, the Horde on the Volga, Chagatai in Central Asia, the Ilkhans in Persia. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
