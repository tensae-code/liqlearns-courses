# Video script — The Mongol Empire and Its Khanates

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Ögödei builds Karakorum's walls, extends the yam and the law, and sends the armies that take Rus' and Hungary; Regencies and succession struggles end with Möngke, whose census, religious tolerance and world-embracing court make the empire a single system.

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: Ögödei builds Karakorum's walls, extends the yam and the law, and sends the armies that take Rus' and Hungary.

Then: Regencies and succession struggles end with Möngke, whose census, religious tolerance and world-embracing court make the empire a single system.

Then: Khubilai moves the capital to Khanbaliq (Beijing), takes the Chinese dynastic name Yuan in 1271 and completes the conquest of the Song.

Then: Batu's ulus on the Volga rules the Russian principalities for two centuries — the "Mongol yoke" of Russian history.

Then: Chinggis's second son's ulus holds Central Asia's oases and steppe, astride the Silk Road.

Then: Hülegü takes Baghdad in 1258 and founds the Ilkhanate; his heirs adopt Islam and Persian culture.

Then: Ögödei, Güyük, Möngke and Khubilai extend and administer the largest land empire ever — and its succession quarrels begin.

Then: One empire becomes four khanates, each going its own way — Yuan in China, the Horde on the Volga, Chagatai in Central Asia, the Ilkhans in Persia.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
