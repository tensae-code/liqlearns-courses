# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — Dating the Past. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Overview timeline spread: Mongolia and the world, 15th–early 20th c.

One timeline runs from five khans in twelve years (1388–1400) through Dayan Khan's enthronement, Ligdan Khan's fall, the Dolon Nor submission of 1691 and the Nerchinsk and Kyakhta treaties to the first Russian consulate and telegraph. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 1.1 What will we study in medieval history?

The Middle Ages run from Rome's fall in 476 to the 15th c.; pupils convert years to centuries and meet the eras peoples dated by — creation (5508 BC), the Hijra (622), the Mongolian lunar count (from 1027) and Chinggis's birth (1162). That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 1.1 How to study modern Mongolian history

Pupils build the period timeline themselves — modern Mongolian history begins in 1911 and falls into three periods (1911–1924, 1924–1990, 1990–today) whose boundaries they must justify. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
