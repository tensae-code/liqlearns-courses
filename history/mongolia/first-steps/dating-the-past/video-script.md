# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
One timeline runs from five khans in twelve years (1388–1400) through Dayan Khan's enthronement, Ligdan Khan's fall, the Dolon Nor submission of 1691 and the Nerchinsk and Kyakhta treaties to the first Russian consulate and telegraph; The Middle Ages run from Rome's fall in 476 to the 15th c.; pupils convert years to centuries and meet the eras peoples dated by — creation (5508 BC), the Hijra (622), the Mongolian lunar count (from 1027) and Chinggis's birth (1162).

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: One timeline runs from five khans in twelve years (1388–1400) through Dayan Khan's enthronement, Ligdan Khan's fall, the Dolon Nor submission of 1691 and the Nerchinsk and Kyakhta treaties to the first Russian consulate and telegraph.

Then: The Middle Ages run from Rome's fall in 476 to the 15th c.; pupils convert years to centuries and meet the eras peoples dated by — creation (5508 BC), the Hijra (622), the Mongolian lunar count (from 1027) and Chinggis's birth (1162).

Then: Pupils build the period timeline themselves — modern Mongolian history begins in 1911 and falls into three periods (1911–1924, 1924–1990, 1990–today) whose boundaries they must justify.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
