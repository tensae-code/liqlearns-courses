# From Evidence to a Written History

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — From Evidence to a Written History. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 1.2 "How do we know history?"

The past speaks only through what it leaves behind — tools, bones, ruins, writing — and the historian's craft is to question each trace before trusting it. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 1.2 How do we study medieval history?

Castles, coins, contracts and chronicles are the medieval world's leavings; a worked example reads the Secret History's verse and Guillaume de Rubruck's silver-tree fountain at Karakorum as evidence. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
