# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Mongolia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How Mongolia Learns Its Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History has no body without its sources; pupils learn to rebuild the past from written records and to draw lessons from it for today's problems; Hikers find Ötzi in the Alps in 1991; scientists, working like detectives on his body, clothes and the arrowhead in his shoulder, rebuild a 5,200-year-old herdsman's last day — and the pupil learns to ask when, where and what happened.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How Mongolia Learns Its Past — it stands on the levels before it in this course as a recommended path (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Opening section: how to study Mongolia and the world in the new era (15th–early 20th c.), with statesman A. Amar's call to know the national history** — History has no body without its sources; pupils learn to rebuild the past from written records and to draw lessons from it for today's problems.
2. **Ch. 1.1 "When, where, what?", opening story "The secret under the ice"** — Hikers find Ötzi in the Alps in 1991; scientists, working like detectives on his body, clothes and the arrowhead in his shoulder, rebuild a 5,200-year-old herdsman's last day — and the pupil learns to ask when, where and what happened.
3. **Ch. 1.2 What will we study in modern history?** — The modern past leaves rich sources — photographs, film, newspapers, laws, memoirs — and the class sorts and classifies them before trusting any.
4. **Introduction: Our past and present** — The senior course opens by asking what the past is for — national history as the memory that lets a people know who they are and argue about who they should be.
5. **Introduction: Let us study our motherland's history with reverence** — The final-year course (17th c. to today) opens on why the past matters — history as the ancestors' deeds and lessons, and every Mongol's duty to carry the story on.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
