# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: History has no body without its sources; pupils learn to rebuild the past from written records and to draw lessons from it for today's problems; Hikers find Ötzi in the Alps in 1991; scientists, working like detectives on his body, clothes and the arrowhead in his shoulder, rebuild a 5,200-year-old herdsman's last day — and the pupil learns to ask when, where and what happened. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw the story as a timeline or a simple map: mark where it happens, who is there, the turn, and what came of it. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's pattern in real life — at home, at school, in the street: a place, a group or a rule changing the way this story's turn changed things. Note what happened, who was there, and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
