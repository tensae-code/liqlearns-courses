# Video script — What History Is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
History has no body without its sources; pupils learn to rebuild the past from written records and to draw lessons from it for today's problems; Hikers find Ötzi in the Alps in 1991; scientists, working like detectives on his body, clothes and the arrowhead in his shoulder, rebuild a 5,200-year-old herdsman's last day — and the pupil learns to ask when, where and what happened.

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: History has no body without its sources; pupils learn to rebuild the past from written records and to draw lessons from it for today's problems.

Then: Hikers find Ötzi in the Alps in 1991; scientists, working like detectives on his body, clothes and the arrowhead in his shoulder, rebuild a 5,200-year-old herdsman's last day — and the pupil learns to ask when, where and what happened.

Then: The modern past leaves rich sources — photographs, film, newspapers, laws, memoirs — and the class sorts and classifies them before trusting any.

Then: The senior course opens by asking what the past is for — national history as the memory that lets a people know who they are and argue about who they should be.

Then: The final-year course (17th c. to today) opens on why the past matters — history as the ancestors' deeds and lessons, and every Mongol's duty to carry the story on.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
