# What History Is

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — What History Is. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Opening section: how to study Mongolia and the world in the new era (15th–early 20th c.), with statesman A. Amar's call to know the national history

History has no body without its sources; pupils learn to rebuild the past from written records and to draw lessons from it for today's problems. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 1.1 "When, where, what?", opening story "The secret under the ice"

Hikers find Ötzi in the Alps in 1991; scientists, working like detectives on his body, clothes and the arrowhead in his shoulder, rebuild a 5,200-year-old herdsman's last day — and the pupil learns to ask when, where and what happened. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. 1.2 What will we study in modern history?

The modern past leaves rich sources — photographs, film, newspapers, laws, memoirs — and the class sorts and classifies them before trusting any. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Introduction: Our past and present

The senior course opens by asking what the past is for — national history as the memory that lets a people know who they are and argue about who they should be. The final-year course (17th c. to today) opens on why the past matters — history as the ancestors' deeds and lessons, and every Mongol's duty to carry the story on.
