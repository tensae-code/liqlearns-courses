## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ch. II.8: Life and culture of the world's peoples in the 15th–18th c.** — Houses, dress, food and festivals on four continents show how ordinary people lived through the centuries of discovery and empire.
2. **Ch. II.7: Life and culture of Western countries in the 18th–early 20th c.** — Factory towns, railways, schools and newspapers change how Western families live, dress, read and celebrate.
3. **Ch. II.8: Life and culture of Eastern countries in the 18th–early 20th c.** — From Istanbul to Edo, Eastern peoples keep their own dress, crafts and festivals while new goods and ideas arrive.
4. **Ch. 3.4 Monuments of the ancient nomads in Mongolia** — Deer stones, khirigsuurs and rock carvings still stand on the steppe; the pupil learns to read them as the nomads' own records.
5. **Ch. 2.4 The ancient states' culture** — Turkic runic stelae, Uyghur cities, Nestorian and Buddhist traces — the steppe states wrote, built and believed, and their monuments are Mongolia's heritage.
6. **Ch. 2.1 We are proud of our homeland's heritage culture** — Culture is sorted from personal to all-human, and the homeland's share — language, customs, songs, monuments and history itself — is the inheritance each generation must hand on.
7. **Ch. 2.1 We are proud of our nation's culture** — Naadam and Tsagaan Sar, the morin khuur and throat-singing, and the festivals of Mongolia's own peoples (Buryat Altargana, Kazakh eagle festival) — national culture as the shared inheritance that names who "we" are.
8. **Ch. 2.2 How do we study cultural heritage?** — UNESCO's 1972 World Heritage Convention (Mongolia joined 1990) and the 2003 Intangible Heritage Convention give the tools — list it, protect it, hand it on.
9. **Ch. 2.3 How do we explain the unified character of Mongolian culture?** — One culture from many peoples — Khalkha, Kazakh, Dörvöd, Buryat and the rest share the nomad inheritance of language, Naadam and hospitality, and its treasures (urtiin duu, khöömii, morin khuur) are now world heritage.
