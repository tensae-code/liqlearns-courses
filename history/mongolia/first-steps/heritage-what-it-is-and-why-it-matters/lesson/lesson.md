## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. 2.1 We are proud of our homeland's heritage culture

Culture is sorted from personal to all-human, and the homeland's share — language, customs, songs, monuments and history itself — is the inheritance each generation must hand on. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. 2.1 We are proud of our nation's culture

Naadam and Tsagaan Sar, the morin khuur and throat-singing, and the festivals of Mongolia's own peoples (Buryat Altargana, Kazakh eagle festival) — national culture as the shared inheritance that names who "we" are. ## Sub lesson 8 — Ch. 2.2 How do we study cultural heritage?

UNESCO's 1972 World Heritage Convention (Mongolia joined 1990) and the 2003 Intangible Heritage Convention give the tools — list it, protect it, hand it on. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 9 — Ch. 2.3 How do we explain the unified character of Mongolian culture?

One culture from many peoples — Khalkha, Kazakh, Dörvöd, Buryat and the rest share the nomad inheritance of language, Naadam and hospitality, and its treasures (urtiin duu, khöömii, morin khuur) are now world heritage. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
