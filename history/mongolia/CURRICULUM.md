# History — Mongolia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Mongolia inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Mongolia's books in hand are the Ministry of Education, Culture, Science and Sports' History and Social Studies student books, in Mongolian, in the placements file's book order — the teaching order, earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the eras as Mongolia's own books present them. The youngest History book opens with the historian's craft — a detective story (Ötzi the Iceman), sources, dating (Level 1). Then the ancient world whole, always bending back to Mongolia — stone age, first settlements, Egypt, India, China, Greece, Rome, and the steppe's own first states, the Xiongnu and their successors (Level 2). Then the medieval millennium as the books frame it — everything before the hinge of the "Chinggis era": the steppe aimags, the Franks, Byzantium, Islam, Tang China (Level 3). Then Chinggis himself and the empire and its khanates, with the high medieval West beside them (Level 4). Then Mongolia and the world from the 15th to the early 20th century, told the way the books tell it — Mongolia first in each period, then the world: fragmentation, the Manchu Qing, Buddhism, and beside them exploration, Reformation, Renaissance, Enlightenment, capitalism and the Industrial Revolution (Level 5). And last the modern national arc in its three acts — the restored state (1911–1924), socialist Mongolia (1924–1990) and democratic Mongolia (1990–today) — with the World Wars, October, the Cold War and the UN admitted exactly where they bend Mongolia's own road (Level 6). The senior books re-read the whole past at a higher level; their placements stock the same lessons, deepening them in place. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Mongolia's own Ministry of Education, Culture, Science and Sports student books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How Mongolia Learns Its Past** — 4 lessons: What History Is, Dating the Past, From Evidence to a Written History, Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters.

2. **Level 2 — The Ancient World and the First Steppe States** — 14 lessons: From Stone Tools to Farming, Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom, First Settlements and the Birth of Civilisation, Nomads of the Steppe: Our Ancestors, Bronze Age Steppe and the Birth of Nomadic Civilisation, Xiongnu: The First Nomad Empire, Xianbei and Rouran: Steppe States, Mongolia and the Turkic Khaganates, 6th–10th Centuries, Economy and Technology of the Ancient Steppe States, Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia, Ancient Civilisations: India, Ancient Civilisations: China, Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete, Ancient Civilisations: Rome.

3. **Level 3 — The Medieval World Before Chinggis** — 15 lessons: Mongolia Before Chinggis: Peoples and States, Mongolian Life and Culture, The Great Migration and the Barbarian Kingdoms, The Frankish Kingdom, Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism, Christianity: From Persecuted Sect to State Religion, The Vikings and Europe, Medieval World: Byzantium and Justinian, Kievan Rus: The First Russian State, The Maya, The Aztecs: Tenochtitlan, The Rise and Expansion of Islam, China's Dynastic Cycle, Medieval India, Medieval Japan.

4. **Level 4 — Chinggis and the Mongol Empire** — 6 lessons: Chinggis Khan and the Founding of the Mongol State, The Mongol Empire and Its Khanates, Medieval European Towns, The Crusades and the Medieval Church, Western Europe: Struggle and Unification, 12th–15th Centuries, Medieval Western European Culture.

5. **Level 5 — Mongolia and the World, 15th–Early 20th Centuries** — 22 lessons: Mongolia After the Empire: Fragmentation, Mongolia Under the Manchu Qing, Mongolian Life and Culture, Buddhism in Mongolia, Kalmyk and Buryat Mongols Under Russia, Mongol Resistance Under the Qing, Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan, The Reformation: Luther and Protestantism, The Renaissance: What It Was, Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment, Capitalism: Private Ownership, Factories, New Classes, Europe in the 15th–18th Centuries, Asia in the 15th–18th Centuries, The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First, Asia in the 18th–Early 20th Centuries, Empires and Colonies in Asia, American Independence, The French Revolution Breaks Out, 1789, Napoleon: Reformer, Conqueror, Fallen, Magna Carta and the First Parliament, The Long Struggle for Human Rights, The Industrial Revolution.

6. **Level 6 — Restored, Socialist and Democratic Mongolia, 1911–Today** — 23 lessons: Mongolia 1911: Independence Restored, Mongolia: Bogd Khanate Society, Economy and Culture, Mongolia: Struggle to Unite the Mongols, The First World War: Causes, 1917 Twice: February and October in Russia, Mongolia Between Revolutions, 1919–1921, Mongolia 1921: People's Revolution, Mongolia: Development Debate, 1921–1924, Mongolia: Socialist Path and Soviet Influence, Mongolia: MPR Economy, Collectivisation and Plans, Mongolia: MPR Education, Culture and Welfare, Mongolia: Lamas' Uprising and the Repressions, The Second World War: Causes, Mongolia in World War II, The Cold War: What It Was, Mongolia and the United Nations, The Collapse of Socialism, 1989–1991, Mongolia: Democratic Revolution, 1990, Mongolia: Market Transition, Mongolia: Democratic Society and Foreign Policy, Mongolia: Building Socialism, Plans and Negdels, Mongolia: New Turn Policy, 1932–1940, Mongolia: Cold War Foreign Relations.

**Totals: 84 lessons** (Level 1: 4 · Level 2: 14 · Level 3: 15 · Level 4: 6 · Level 5: 22 · Level 6: 23 — 168 placements stocked, all from student books; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only).
