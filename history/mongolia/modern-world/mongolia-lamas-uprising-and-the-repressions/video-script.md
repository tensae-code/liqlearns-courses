# Video script — Mongolia: Lamas' Uprising and the Repressions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In 1932 monks and herders rise against collectivisation and monastery closures across the western aimags; the rising is crushed with Soviet help; The 1937–39 terror executes tens of thousands — lamas, nobles, Buryats, intellectuals — a wound the book names plainly as the black page of the national story.

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: In 1932 monks and herders rise against collectivisation and monastery closures across the western aimags; the rising is crushed with Soviet help.

Then: The 1937–39 terror executes tens of thousands — lamas, nobles, Buryats, intellectuals — a wound the book names plainly as the black page of the national story.

Then: Khövsgöl's Rashaant monastery rises; the revolt spreads across the west, tens of thousands flee toward Xinjiang, and Soviet armour helps crush it — civil war in all but name.

Then: The 1937-39 terror — over 20,000 executed, some 17,000 lamas killed, 700+ monasteries destroyed, and the nation's leaders (Amar, Genden, Dogsom, Losol) shot after Soviet-run trials.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
