## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 3.9 The lamas' "counter-revolutionary uprising"

In 1932 monks and herders rise against collectivisation and monastery closures across the western aimags; the rising is crushed with Soviet help. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 3.10 The dark spot in Mongolian history

The 1937–39 terror executes tens of thousands — lamas, nobles, Buryats, intellectuals — a wound the book names plainly as the black page of the national story. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. III.3 The 1932 armed uprising and the exodus movement

Khövsgöl's Rashaant monastery rises; the revolt spreads across the west, tens of thousands flee toward Xinjiang, and Soviet armour helps crush it — civil war in all but name. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. III.5 Political repression: its course and consequences

The 1937-39 terror — over 20,000 executed, some 17,000 lamas killed, 700+ monasteries destroyed, and the nation's leaders (Amar, Genden, Dogsom, Losol) shot after Soviet-run trials. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
