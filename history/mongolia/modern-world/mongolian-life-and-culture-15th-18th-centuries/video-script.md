# Video script — Mongolian Life and Culture

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Herding life goes on under the khans; Erdene Zuu rises on Karakorum's ruins, Zanabazar casts his Buddhas, and in 1662 Sagan Setsen writes the Erdeniin Tovch chronicle; Taxes, corvée and debt to Chinese firms grind the herders while the banner princes and monasteries hold the land and herds.

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: Herding life goes on under the khans; Erdene Zuu rises on Karakorum's ruins, Zanabazar casts his Buddhas, and in 1662 Sagan Setsen writes the Erdeniin Tovch chronicle.

Then: Taxes, corvée and debt to Chinese firms grind the herders while the banner princes and monasteries hold the land and herds.

Then: Chronicles, medicine, printing and temple art keep a national culture alive under foreign rule.

Then: Buddhism's second coming, Altan Khan and the Dalai Lama title, Erdene Zuu's founding — faith remakes Mongol culture before the Qing remakes its politics.

Then: Khoshuu banners freeze herders to their lords' land; alba taxes, Chinese merchant debt and great monasteries reshape life, while learning, law codes and chronicles quietly flourish.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
