# Mongolia and the United Nations

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — Mongolia and the United Nations. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 3.14 The UN and Mongolia

After the 1945 referendum confirms independence and China recognises it, Mongolia's long-blocked UN application finally succeeds in 1961. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. III.6 (second part) Independence confirmed, 1945

Yalta's status-quo promise, the 20 October 1945 referendum (98.4% for independence), and China's recognition in 1946 make Mongolia's sovereignty de jure at last.
