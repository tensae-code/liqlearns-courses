# Mongolia After the Empire: Fragmentation

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — Mongolia After the Empire: Fragmentation. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? ## Sub lesson 1 — Part II, Ch. I.1: Mongolia in the 15th–18th centuries

After the empire breaks, khans rise and fall in quick succession and the aimags pull apart while Ming China presses from the south. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. I.2: The struggle to restore united power

The seven-year-old Dayan Khan is raised on Chinggis's throne before the Eight White Gers and reunites the Mongols; a century later Altan Khan founds Khökh Khot and in 1577 receives the title Dalai Lama from Sonam Gyatso. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. I.4: Mongolia's foreign environment in the 15th–18th c.

A divided Mongolia is squeezed from three sides — Ming China, the rising Manchu, and Russia pushing across Siberia to Lake Baikal. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. 4.1 Mongolia centred on its homeland

After Yuan falls in 1368 the khans return to the steppe; the Northern Yuan fights Ming and itself in turn. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
