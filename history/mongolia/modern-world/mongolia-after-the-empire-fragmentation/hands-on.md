# Hands-on — Mongolia After the Empire: Fragmentation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: After the empire breaks, khans rise and fall in quick succession and the aimags pull apart while Ming China presses from the south; The seven-year-old Dayan Khan is raised on Chinggis's throne before the Eight White Gers and reunites the Mongols; a century later Altan Khan founds Khökh Khot and in 1577 receives the title Dalai Lama from Sonam Gyatso. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw the story as a timeline or a simple map: mark where it happens, who is there, the turn, and what came of it. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's pattern in real life — at home, at school, in the street: a place, a group or a rule changing the way this story's turn changed things. Note what happened, who was there, and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
