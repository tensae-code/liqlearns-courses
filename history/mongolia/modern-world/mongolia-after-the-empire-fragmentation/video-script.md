# Video script — Mongolia After the Empire: Fragmentation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
After the empire breaks, khans rise and fall in quick succession and the aimags pull apart while Ming China presses from the south; The seven-year-old Dayan Khan is raised on Chinggis's throne before the Eight White Gers and reunites the Mongols; a century later Altan Khan founds Khökh Khot and in 1577 receives the title Dalai Lama from Sonam Gyatso.

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: After the empire breaks, khans rise and fall in quick succession and the aimags pull apart while Ming China presses from the south.

Then: The seven-year-old Dayan Khan is raised on Chinggis's throne before the Eight White Gers and reunites the Mongols; a century later Altan Khan founds Khökh Khot and in 1577 receives the title Dalai Lama from Sonam Gyatso.

Then: A divided Mongolia is squeezed from three sides — Ming China, the rising Manchu, and Russia pushing across Siberia to Lake Baikal.

Then: After Yuan falls in 1368 the khans return to the steppe; the Northern Yuan fights Ming and itself in turn.

Then: Dayan Khan's reunification and his sons' appanages — Khalkha, Chakhar, Ordos, Tümed — set the map the Manchu will conquer piece by piece.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
