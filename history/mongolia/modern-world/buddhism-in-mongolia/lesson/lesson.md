# Buddhism in Mongolia

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — Buddhism in Mongolia. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. I.3: Buddhism spreads widely in Mongolia

Monasteries multiply across Khalkha, the Bogd Jebtsundamba's Ikh Khüree becomes a city of temples, and a large share of men live as lamas. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 3.8 Buddhism and socialism

The socialist state first taxes and restricts the monasteries, then moves to destroy them; a living church of hundreds of temples is reduced to almost none. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
