# Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

Columbus reaches America in 1492, Vasco da Gama rounds Africa to India in 1497–98, Magellan's ships circle the globe in 1519–22 — and the new sea roads become the first colonial empires. That is the whole story of this lesson: who is there, where it happens, what turns, and what comes of it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: tell the story back to yourself once, in your own words, before you answer any question — if you can tell it, you know it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Columbus reaches America in 1492, Vasco da Gama rounds Africa to India in 1497–98, Magellan's ships circle the globe in 1519–22 — and the new sea roads become the first colonial empires. Tell the story in order, and name the lesson's key words from the parts above. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: state your guesses from the start, then say how the real story was different.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a timeline or map of the story, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where a similar turn happens in real life. Note it.
