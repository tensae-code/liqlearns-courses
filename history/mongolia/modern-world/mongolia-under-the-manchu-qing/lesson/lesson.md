# Mongolia Under the Manchu Qing

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — Mongolia Under the Manchu Qing. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. I.3: Mongolia (mid-17th–18th c.)

Khalkha splits into Tüsheet, Sechen and Jasagtu khanates, the Oirats rise in the west under Galdan, and Mongol-on-Mongol war opens the door to the Manchu. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. I.5: Mongolia loses its independence

Ligdan, the last great khan, is defeated by the Manchu and dies in flight in 1634; Inner Mongolia submits in 1636, and in 1691 the Khalkha nobles at Dolon Nor accept Qing rule. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Part III, Ch. I.1: How the Mongols fought for freedom and independence

Under Qing rule the resistance flares in Chingünjav's 1756 uprising in Khotgoid and Amarsanaa's Jungar war — both crushed, both remembered. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. I.2: The Manchu Qing policy that ruled Mongolia

The Qing bind Mongolia with banners and hereditary jasag princes, forbid Mongol-Chinese mixing and movement, and rule through the Lifan Yuan. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 5 — Ch. I.6: Manchu Qing policy toward Mongolia changes

The late Qing "new policy" opens Mongolia to Chinese farming colonists and firms like the Mongolor gold company (1900) — and the pressure pushes the nobles toward the 1911 break. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 6 — Ch. I.1 The Mongolian state falls under Manchu domination

Divided Mongols fall in sequence — Inner Mongolia 1636, Khalkha at Dolon Nuur 1691, the Khoshud in Tibet, and finally the Jungar state destroyed 1755-58. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 7 — Ch. I.3 The causes of Mongolia's loss of independence

The book weighs internal division, the khans' rivalries and Ligdan's failed unification against Ming decline, Russian advance and Manchu statecraft. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
