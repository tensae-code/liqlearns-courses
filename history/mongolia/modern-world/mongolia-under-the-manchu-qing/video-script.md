# Video script — Mongolia Under the Manchu Qing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Khalkha splits into Tüsheet, Sechen and Jasagtu khanates, the Oirats rise in the west under Galdan, and Mongol-on-Mongol war opens the door to the Manchu; Ligdan, the last great khan, is defeated by the Manchu and dies in flight in 1634; Inner Mongolia submits in 1636, and in 1691 the Khalkha nobles at Dolon Nor accept Qing rule.

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: Khalkha splits into Tüsheet, Sechen and Jasagtu khanates, the Oirats rise in the west under Galdan, and Mongol-on-Mongol war opens the door to the Manchu.

Then: Ligdan, the last great khan, is defeated by the Manchu and dies in flight in 1634; Inner Mongolia submits in 1636, and in 1691 the Khalkha nobles at Dolon Nor accept Qing rule.

Then: Under Qing rule the resistance flares in Chingünjav's 1756 uprising in Khotgoid and Amarsanaa's Jungar war — both crushed, both remembered.

Then: The Qing bind Mongolia with banners and hereditary jasag princes, forbid Mongol-Chinese mixing and movement, and rule through the Lifan Yuan.

Then: The late Qing "new policy" opens Mongolia to Chinese farming colonists and firms like the Mongolor gold company (1900) — and the pressure pushes the nobles toward the 1911 break.

Then: Divided Mongols fall in sequence — Inner Mongolia 1636, Khalkha at Dolon Nuur 1691, the Khoshud in Tibet, and finally the Jungar state destroyed 1755-58.

Then: The book weighs internal division, the khans' rivalries and Ligdan's failed unification against Ming decline, Russian advance and Manchu statecraft.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
