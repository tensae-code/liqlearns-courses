# Mongolia 1911: Independence Restored

## Do it first

Before you read anything, try this: say out loud what you think this story is about — Mongolia 1911: Independence Restored. Who do you think is in it? Where does it happen? What do you think turns — what changes? Keep your guesses. This lesson tells the story properly, and at the end you will check your guesses against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 2.1 The Mongolian national revolution of 1911

As Qing collapses, Khalkha nobles and lamas send a secret delegation to Russia, then on 29 December 1911 proclaim independence and enthrone the Eighth Bogd as khan of Mongolia. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story has many parts, give each part one short name of your own and say the names in order — the whole story will stay in your head.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 2.2 Independent Mongolia

The Bogd Khanate builds ministries, an army and a parliament of two chambers, and wins the 1915 Kyakhta treaty — autonomy under Chinese suzerainty, less than the full independence proclaimed. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ch. II.1 The victory and significance of the 1911 revolution

On 29 December 1911 the Bogd is enthroned and independence declared; the book weighs 1911 as the national revolution that restarted Mongolian statehood. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ch. II.2 The beginning of Mongolia's restored rise, 1911–1920

A two-chamber khural, an army, schools, a bank and 370 enterprises — the Bogd Khanate builds a modern state inside the limits Kyakhta allowed. That is the heart of this part of the story: hold on to who is there, where it happens, and what it leads to, because the next part builds on it.
