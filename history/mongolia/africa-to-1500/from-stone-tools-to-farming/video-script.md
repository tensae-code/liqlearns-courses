# Video script — From Stone Tools to Farming

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
For almost three million years people live by hunting and gathering, chipping stone tools and taming fire, spreading slowly across the earth; People learn to herd animals and sow grain; producers replace foragers, villages grow, and life can be stored against the winter.

Look at the map. Find Mongolia — and the wider world its books set beside it, because Mongolia's books always tell the two stories together.

First: For almost three million years people live by hunting and gathering, chipping stone tools and taming fire, spreading slowly across the earth.

Then: People learn to herd animals and sow grain; producers replace foragers, villages grow, and life can be stored against the winter.

Then: For most of 800,000 years Mongolia's first people hunt, gather and chip stone; their sites and tools are the only record.

Then: Grinding stone, pottery and the first herding and sowing — people begin to produce food instead of only finding it.

Then: Lenski's ladder of societies begins here — hunting-and-gathering bands with stone tools, then hoe-farming (horticultural) societies from about 4000 BC, then plough-and-city agrarian states, first in Egypt.

Remember it this way: who was there, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
