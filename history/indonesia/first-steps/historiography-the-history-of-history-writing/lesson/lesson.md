# Historiography: The History of History Writing

## Do it first

Take one event you know. Write one sentence about it as a palace servant would, one as a foreign officer would, and one as a citizen of your own country would. Seeing the three versions is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — C. Penulisan Sejarah (Historiografi)

Who writes history changes what history says. The book sorts Indonesian history-writing into three kinds. Traditional historiography is palace-centred: the past told from the throne, to glorify the ruler. Colonial historiography tells the past from the coloniser's seat, with the company and the empire as the heroes. Modern historiography is written for nation-building: the nation itself is the main character, and the story is told to hold it together. None of the three is a camera. The trap named for every writer is bias — letting the seat you write from bend the facts — and the modern writer's job is to know the trap and avoid it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Panduan Umum / Materi Sejarah Indonesia Kelas XI

The Grade 11 teacher guide hands the teacher the whole of the national course as one argument, in four chapters: colonialism and resistance, the national movement, the Japanese tyranny, and the proclamation of independence. And it frames the teacher's own place in historiography: the teacher is a fellow researcher alongside the pupils, someone who must keep revising the story as new findings come in. The history of history-writing is not finished — the classroom is one of the places it continues.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History-writing itself has a history: Indonesian historiography is sorted into traditional (palace-centred), colonial and modern (nation-building) kinds — and bias is the trap every writer must avoid. Give one real example: A palace chronicle tells the past from the king's throne; a colonial history tells it from the company's office; a modern history tells it for the nation — same events, three different books. and name the lesson's key words: Historiography, Bias. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
