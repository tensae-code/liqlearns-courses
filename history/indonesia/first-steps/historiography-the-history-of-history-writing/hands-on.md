# Hands-on — Historiography: The History of History Writing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one event you know. Write one sentence about it as a palace servant would, one as a foreign officer would, and one as a citizen of your own country would. Seeing the three versions is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History-writing itself has a history: Indonesian historiography is sorted into traditional (palace-centred), colonial and modern (nation-building) kinds — and bias is the trap every writer must avoid. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
