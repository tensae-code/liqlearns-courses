# Lesson plan — Historiography: The History of History Writing

**Course:** History — Indonesia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Historiography: The History of History Writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History-writing itself has a history: Indonesian historiography is sorted into traditional (palace-centred), colonial and modern (nation-building) kinds — and bias is the trap every writer must avoid.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **C. Penulisan Sejarah (Historiografi)** — Who writes history changes what history says.
2. **Panduan Umum / Materi Sejarah Indonesia Kelas XI** — The Grade 11 teacher guide hands the teacher the whole of the national course as one argument, in four chapters: colonialism and resistance, the national movement, the Japanese tyranny, and the proclamation of independence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History-writing itself has a history: Indonesian historiography is sorted into traditional (palace-centred), colonial and modern (nation-building) kinds — and bias is the trap every writer must avoid.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: A palace chronicle tells the past from the king's throne; a colonial history tells it from the company's office; a modern history tells it for the nation — same events, three different books.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Indonesia, as the books themselves work.

- Go back to the one thing: History-writing itself has a history: Indonesian historiography is sorted into traditional (palace-centred), colonial and modern (nation-building) kinds — and bias is the trap every writer must avoid. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
