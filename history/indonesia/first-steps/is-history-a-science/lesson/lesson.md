# Is History a Science?

## Do it first

Pick one thing in your town that changed in your lifetime — a market, a road, a phone in every hand. Write one sentence explaining the change using an idea about how society works. That explaining is this lesson.

For a long time history stood among the social sciences, then in the 19th century it broke away and went its own road, proud of its own method. Later the two rejoined: historians found that ideas about how societies work could help them explain what their sources showed. The book presents Kuntowijoyo's Indonesian synthesis — history written with social theory as a tool of explanation. His cases are Indonesian: among them, women's voices in the magazine Dunia Wanita, read not as gossip but as evidence of how a society was changing. The lesson's answer is balanced: history keeps its own craft of evidence, and borrows theory to explain — neither a science in a laboratory coat, nor a story cut off from thought.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History broke away from the social sciences in the 19th century, then rejoined them — and used well, social theory helps the historian explain the past, not just list it. Give one real example: Women's voices in the magazine Dunia Wanita become evidence a historian can explain with social theory — a magazine turns into a window on a whole society. and name the lesson's key words: Social theory, Synthesis. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
