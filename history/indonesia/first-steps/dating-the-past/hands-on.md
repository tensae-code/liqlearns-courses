# Hands-on — Dating the Past

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down five events from your own life in the order they happened. Then draw a line and cut it into periods — before school, first school years, now. That ordering and cutting is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Events are pinned in time by chronology and cut into periods, so that change and continuity across time can be measured — and chronology, from Greek chronos, is the historian's ordering tool. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
