# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Events are pinned in time by chronology and cut into periods, so that change and continuity across time can be measured — and chronology, from Greek chronos, is the historian's ordering tool.

Once you know what history is, you need a way to hold events still. The book's tool is chronology: events are pinned by their dates and set in order, then the long line of time is cut into periods. For Indonesia the cut runs from prehistory, through the Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms, and on to the age when the Industrial Revolution's waves reached the archipelago. Periods are not just labels. When time is cut this way, a learner can measure change — what is different in one period from the last — and continuity, what carried on unchanged across the cut.
The teacher guide gives chronology its Greek root: chronos, time. Chronology is taught as the historian's ordering tool, and pupils practise it on a real institution: they build the timeline of Bank Indonesia, from the recognition of Indonesian sovereignty through to the nationalisation of De Javasche Bank, the old colonial bank. A bank may sound dry, but that is the point — if you can order a bank's life, you can order anything's past.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
