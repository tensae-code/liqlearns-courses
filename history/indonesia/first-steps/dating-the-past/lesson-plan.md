# Lesson plan — Dating the Past

**Course:** History — Indonesia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Events are pinned in time by chronology and cut into periods, so that change and continuity across time can be measured — and chronology, from Greek chronos, is the historian's ordering tool.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Events are pinned in time by chronology and cut into periods, so that change and continuity across time can be measured — and chronology, from Greek chronos, is the historian's ordering tool.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: Indonesia's past is cut into periods — prehistory, the Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms, and on to the Industrial Revolution's waves — so a learner can see what changed between one period and the next, and what stayed the same.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Indonesia, as the books themselves work.
