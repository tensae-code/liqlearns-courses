# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Indonesia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A written history is built in steps: start from sources — primary ones taken straight from the event, secondary ones written after it — then work through heuristics and criticism, until the evidence can be written up as history.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: A written history is built in steps: start from sources — primary ones taken straight from the event, secondary ones written after it — then work through heuristics and criticism, until the evidence can be written up as history.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: A typhus epidemic in Cirebon and a beduk drum in Pontianak both become history the same way: find the sources, test them, then write what they prove.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Indonesia, as the books themselves work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the first source found settles the matter → Sources must be criticised — tested for who made them, when and why — before anything is written.
- Go back to the one thing: A written history is built in steps: start from sources — primary ones taken straight from the event, secondary ones written after it — then work through heuristics and criticism, until the evidence can be written up as history. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
