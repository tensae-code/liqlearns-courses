# From Evidence to a Written History

## Do it first

Take one object in your home that is older than you. Write what it tells you directly, then one thing someone told you about it. The first is a primary source, the second a secondary one — sorting them is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — B. Penelitian Sejarah

Research, in this book, is a craft with steps. It starts from sources, and the sources come in two kinds. Primary sources are taken straight from the event — the thing itself, or words written by someone who was there. Secondary sources are written afterwards, by people working from the primary ones. The researcher gathers sources (the book calls this step heuristics), then criticises them — testing who made each source, when, and whether it can be trusted — and only then writes the history. The book works the method on Indonesian cases: a typhus epidemic in Cirebon, and the beduk, the great drum of Pontianak, each rebuilt from its evidence.

## Sub lesson 2 — Panduan Umum, Strategi Umum Pembelajaran

The Grade 11 teacher guide turns the same craft into the class's daily strategy. Inquiry and open questions are the recommended way of teaching, every day — not lectures to memorise. And project-based historical research is compulsory: at least twice in the Class XI year, inside the year's 72 lesson-hour allocation, pupils must run the full craft themselves, from sources to a written history. The method is not a chapter to finish; it is homework for the whole year.

## Sub lesson 3 — Panduan Umum D.1, Inkuiri Sejarah

By Class XII the guide makes inquiry the master strategy of the whole course. Pupils annotate primary sources themselves — revolution-era propaganda posters are the named example — build timelines and concept maps from what the sources say, and report their findings in writing. The staircase from the first book — find, test, weigh, write — is now climbed on the hardest material of all: the recent past of their own country.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A written history is built in steps: start from sources — primary ones taken straight from the event, secondary ones written after it — then work through heuristics and criticism, until the evidence can be written up as history. Give one real example: A typhus epidemic in Cirebon and a beduk drum in Pontianak both become history the same way: find the sources, test them, then write what they prove. and name the lesson's key words: Primary source, Criticism. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
