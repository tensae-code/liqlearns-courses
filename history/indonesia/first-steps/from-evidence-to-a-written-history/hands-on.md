# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one object in your home that is older than you. Write what it tells you directly, then one thing someone told you about it. The first is a primary source, the second a secondary one — sorting them is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written history is built in steps: start from sources — primary ones taken straight from the event, secondary ones written after it — then work through heuristics and criticism, until the evidence can be written up as history. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
