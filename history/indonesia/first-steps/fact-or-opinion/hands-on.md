# Hands-on — Fact or Opinion

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down one thing 'everybody knows' about the past. Then ask: how could we check it? Who wrote it down, and where? That checking habit is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Every claim about the past is checkable: the wrong versions of events circulating among pupils must be hunted down and corrected against credible references and new findings. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
