# Video script — Fact or Opinion

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Every claim about the past is checkable: the wrong versions of events circulating among pupils must be hunted down and corrected against credible references and new findings.

This lesson comes from the Grade 11 teacher guide's standing section on straightening out misconceived concepts — in the book's own words, meluruskan konsep yang salah kaprah, putting right the concepts that have gone wrong. Teachers are instructed to hunt down the wrong versions of events that circulate among pupils — the garbled story, the confident rumour, the half-remembered tale — and correct them against credible references and new findings. The rule underneath is bigger than any one correction: every textbook claim is checkable. A fact is what survives the checking; an opinion, or a rumour, is what has not been checked yet.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
