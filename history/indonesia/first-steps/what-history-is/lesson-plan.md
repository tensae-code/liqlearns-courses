# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Indonesia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is a continuing dialogue between the historian and the facts — a reconstruction of real past events, explained by their causes, not myth and not memorised dates.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course. Nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History is a continuing dialogue between the historian and the facts — a reconstruction of real past events, explained by their causes, not myth and not memorised dates.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: The 1883 Krakatau eruption is history because real records let us rebuild what happened, when, and what it did to people — a made-up tale about the volcano would not be history.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Indonesia, as the books themselves work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks history is a list of dates to memorise → Dates are only the frame: this lesson's history always asks how an event happened and what it meant, explained by cause and effect.
- Go back to the one thing: History is a continuing dialogue between the historian and the facts — a reconstruction of real past events, explained by their causes, not myth and not memorised dates. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
