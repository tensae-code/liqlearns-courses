# Video script — What History Is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History is a continuing dialogue between the historian and the facts — a reconstruction of real past events, explained by their causes, not myth and not memorised dates.

Indonesia's own history course does not begin with a king or a battle. It begins with a cup of coffee and a volcano. In 1883 Mount Krakatau exploded, and the whole world heard about it. The book opens there because an eruption like that is a perfect first case: it really happened, it can be dated, and people wrote it down. From that case the book builds its definition. History is a continuing dialogue between the historian and the facts — the historian asks questions, the facts answer back, and together they reconstruct real past events. History is not myth, and it is not a story invented to please anyone.
The teacher's guide for the same course tells teachers to hold that line in every lesson. The core of the subject is process: how an event happened and what it meant. Every event is explained by cause and effect — sometimes a single cause, sometimes many causes working together. A pupil who can only recite a date has not yet learned history; a pupil who can say what caused an event, and what the event caused in turn, has begun. That is the standard this whole course is taught to.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
