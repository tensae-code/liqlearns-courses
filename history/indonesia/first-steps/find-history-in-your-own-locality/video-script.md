# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History lives where you live: your own family's story, and local practices with centuries behind them — like sasi in Maluku — are real research subjects, and museums and heritage sites are primary classrooms.

The method book ends by sending pupils home. First, write your own family history: ask, record, and build the story of the people you came from. Then research a local practice — and the book's example is sasi, the Maluku custom, running since the 16th century, that closes the lompa fish harvest: no one may take the fish until the community itself opens the harvest again. A custom like that is history still alive — it has rules, a past, and keepers who remember. The pupil who researches sasi is doing exactly what the research chapter taught, on the shore of their own island.
The teacher guide takes the same lesson out of the classroom altogether. Teachers are given a full protocol for learning at museums, at archaeological sites and at heritage-preservation offices — how to prepare the visit, what pupils should do there, and how to work with what they find. Local sites are treated as primary classrooms, not as treats after the real lessons. The past is not only in the book; it is standing in the pupil's own district, waiting to be visited properly.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
