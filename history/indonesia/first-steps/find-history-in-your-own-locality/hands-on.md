# Hands-on — Find History in Your Own Locality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask at home: what is the oldest custom your family or neighbourhood still keeps? Write who keeps it, how old it is, and how you know. That is local history research — this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History lives where you live: your own family's story, and local practices with centuries behind them — like sasi in Maluku — are real research subjects, and museums and heritage sites are primary classrooms. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
