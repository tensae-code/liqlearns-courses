# Video script — Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Long before Europeans arrived, Nusantara sailors traded across Asia — Barus camphor crossed the seas, Ptolemy's map named the Barussae islands, and Borobudur's stone ships show the archipelago's own maritime world.

Begin with a smell: camphor, the sharp white crystals from the forests around Barus on Sumatra's west coast. Long before any European ship appeared, Nusantara sailors were carrying Barus camphor across Asia, and the trade was old enough and big enough to be written into the world's geography: Ptolemy's map, drawn in the 2nd century, names the Barussae islands. The proof is also carved at home — the stone ships of Borobudur, reliefs of great sailing vessels with outriggers and sails, showing the archipelago's own maritime world. Nusantara was never a shore waiting to be discovered. It was a crossroads that already had sailors, cargoes and maps.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
