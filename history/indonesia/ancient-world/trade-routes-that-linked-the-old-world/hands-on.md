# Hands-on — Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing made or grown near your home that travels far away today. Trace its road on a map with your finger, as far as you can. That tracing is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Long before Europeans arrived, Nusantara sailors traded across Asia — Barus camphor crossed the seas, Ptolemy's map named the Barussae islands, and Borobudur's stone ships show the archipelago's own maritime world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
