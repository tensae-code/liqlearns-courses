# About the History — Indonesia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Sejarah books for Kelas X–XII (Kemendikbudristek,
Jakarta, in Bahasa Indonesia). The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Indonesia inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
Legends and religious-history stories are told as the books
tell them, as plot summaries in our own words.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the books'
own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How History Works.** The Grade 10
   method book whole: what history is, chronology, research
   from evidence, historiography, history and social theory,
   local history — with the teacher guides' discipline
   (cause and effect, fact against misconceived concepts,
   inquiry as the daily strategy) stocked as parts.
2. **Level 2 — The Spice World and the Coming of Europe.**
   Nusantara's own trade world, India's zero, the West's
   three motives, 1453 and the sea routes, Banda's monopoly.
3. **Level 3 — Colonial Rule and the Wars of Resistance.**
   The VOC and Batavia; the resistance atlas war by war;
   Daendels, the British interregnum, Tanam Paksa, Krakatau,
   and Surabaya's trams.
4. **Level 4 — The Awakening: Schools, Press and
   Organisation.** Colonial education's unintended elite, the
   Ethical Policy, press and Islamic modernism, Asia's
   networks, Budi Utomo to the Youth Pledge and the Women's
   Congress — and the world's storms read from Batavia.
5. **Level 5 — Occupation, Proclamation and Revolution.**
   Kalijati, the occupation, BPUPKI and Pancasila, the road
   to 17 August 1945, the state formed under fire, the
   battles, Surabaya, diplomacy with guerrilla war, and RIS
   dissolved back into the NKRI.
6. **Level 6 — Democracy, the New Order and Reformasi.**
   Bandung and Bebas Aktif, the polarised 1950s, the 1955
   election and Nasakom, the regional rebellions, G30S as an
   evidence problem, Tritura and Supersemar, the New Order's
   ledger and its resistance, May 1998, and Reformasi to the
   pupils' own lifetime.

68 of the 71 lessons stand on **student books** and count in
the acceptance reckoning below; 3 stand on teacher-guide
evidence only.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *One archipelago, told whole:* "Begin with a volcano and a
  question — what is history, and how do we know? Learn the
  craft: chronology, sources, the traps of bias. Then sail
  the spice world that drew the world east, and walk the
  wars of those who refused the companies, island by island,
  for three hundred years. Watch a colonised people organise
  instead — schools, newspapers, congresses, a pledge spoken
  by its youth. Endure the occupation, draft a republic in an
  admiral's house, proclaim it on an August morning, and
  defend it in fire from Medan to Makassar. Then live its
  lifetime: Bandung's refusal of two empires' blocs, the
  first election, the long New Order read without slogans,
  and Reformasi — the open era your own parents can tell you
  about. That is Indonesia's own story, from its own school
  books, and it is yours."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Book 1 (Grade 10 student, Kurikulum Merdeka)** — a whole
  book on how history works — humans, space and time — taught
  through Indonesian cases (a volcano, a tram system, a drum,
  a magazine) and hero biographies, before any chronological
  survey begins.
- **Book 2 (Grade 10 teacher guide)** — trains teachers to
  teach thinking, not facts: diachronic (kronologis) versus
  synchronic analysis, mono- and multi-causal explanation,
  and learning outside the classroom at museums and heritage
  sites.
- **Book 3 (Grade 11 student, Edisi Revisi 2024)** — the
  national story told as resistance and awakening: every
  colonial episode paired with the Indonesians who fought,
  organised, wrote or negotiated against it; world events
  (1453, Versailles, the Depression, Hiroshima) admitted
  only where they change Indonesia's fate.
- **Book 4 (Grade 11 teacher guide)** — epistemic discipline
  for teachers: a standing section on straightening out
  misconceived concepts (meluruskan konsep yang salah
  kaprah), inquiry as the default strategy, and at least two
  research projects a year.
- **Book 5 (Grade 11 Semester 1 student, Kurikulum 2013)** —
  a war-by-war resistance atlas, each region's fight in its
  own section in geographic order, framed by Sukarno's
  courtroom words on imperialism and by the question of why
  political independence has not yet meant economic
  independence.
- **Book 6 (Grade 12 teacher guide)** — insists the recent
  past not be taught black-and-white: pupils must evaluate
  the Revolution, the Old Order's collapse, Suharto's New
  Order and Reformasi from multiple perspectives, with oral
  history from their own families for the Reformasi chapter.
- **Book 7 (Grade 12 student, Kurikulum Merdeka)** — the
  school story ends in the pupils' own lifetime: revolution,
  parliamentary experiment, Guided Democracy, the New Order
  and Reformasi, with contested events (Supersemar, 30
  September 1965, May 1998) presented as evidence problems,
  not settled slogans.
