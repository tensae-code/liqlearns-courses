# Hands-on — Reformasi, 1998 and After

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Choose one event from before you were born that your parents lived through. Write three interview questions for them. That interview is this lesson's method.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Reformasi opened politics and expression, reformed the bureaucracy and state enterprises, widened education and built infrastructure — studied partly through pupils' own oral-history interviews with parents who lived it; the book first asks what separates revolution from reform, then replays the seconds before B.J. Habibie was sworn in as president. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
