# Reformasi, 1998 and After

## Do it first

Choose one event from before you were born that your parents lived through. Write three interview questions for them. That interview is this lesson's method.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bab 4, Indonesia pada Masa Reformasi (Gambaran Umum)

The student book opens its Reformasi chapter with a distinction: what separates revolution from reform? A revolution replaces a world at a stroke; a reform reopens and rebuilds one piece by piece. Then it slows to seconds: the moments before B.J. Habibie, Suharto's vice-president, was sworn in as president on the day of the resignation — the hinge on which the old order swung into the open democratic era. Reformasi, as the chapter teaches it, is the era of opening: politics and expression unbound, and a democracy rebuilt in public after thirty-two years of supervision.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bab 4.A Reformasi: Awal Demokrasi Politik yang Terbuka

The teacher guide gives the era's full ledger and its method. Reformasi opened politics and expression; it reformed the bureaucracy and the state enterprises; it widened education and built infrastructure. And the guide sets the era's study partly in the home: pupils conduct oral-history interviews with their own parents, who lived through 1998 and after. The recent past, the guide insists, is not to be taught black-and-white either — it is to be evaluated from multiple perspectives, beginning with the witnesses at the family table.
