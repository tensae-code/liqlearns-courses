# Hands-on — Diplomacy and Guerrilla War, 1946–1949

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw two hands: one holding a pen over a treaty, one holding a rifle in the hills. Write which hand kept the Republic alive, and defend your answer. That argument is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: At the table, Linggarjati (1946) and Renville (1948) shrank the Republic while Dutch aggressions broke them; the answer was Sudirman's guerrilla war plus Roem–Royen and the Round Table Conference, which finally transferred sovereignty. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
