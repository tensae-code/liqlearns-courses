# Video script — Diplomacy and Guerrilla War, 1946–1949

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
At the table, Linggarjati (1946) and Renville (1948) shrank the Republic while Dutch aggressions broke them; the answer was Sudirman's guerrilla war plus Roem–Royen and the Round Table Conference, which finally transferred sovereignty.

The Republic defended itself on two tracks, and this chapter runs them together. At the table: the Linggarjati agreement of 1946 recognised the Republic over Java, Madura and Sumatra — and then a Dutch 'police action' broke it and shrank the Republic by force. The Renville agreement of 1948, signed on an American warship, shrank it further, and a second Dutch aggression broke that too, capturing the capital and the president himself. The answer from the hills was General Sudirman's guerrilla war: the army refused to die, and the Republic survived as a moving government of the countryside. Pressure from a watching world then forced the end: the Roem–Royen talks reopened the road, and the Round Table Conference at The Hague finally transferred sovereignty in December 1949. Neither track alone had won. Together, they closed the revolution.
