# Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes

## Do it first

Find Constantinople and Malacca on a map. Draw the land road that was closed, then the sea road that replaced it. That rerouting is this lesson.

In 1453 Constantinople fell to Sultan Mehmed al-Fatih, and the old spice roads through it closed to the West. Spices still had to reach Europe — so Portugal answered at sea. Vasco da Gama's route around Africa opened the ocean road to India, and then Afonso de Albuquerque seized Malacca, the great market where the archipelago's spices changed hands. With Malacca taken, the European age stood at Nusantara's door: the sea road built to reach the spices now ended inside the spice islands' own world. The chapters of resistance that follow all begin here.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: When Constantinople fell and the spice roads closed, Portugal answered at sea — da Gama's route and Albuquerque's seizure of Malacca brought the European age to Nusantara's door. Give one real example: One city's fall in 1453 rerouted the world's spice trade onto the open ocean — and the ocean road ended at Malacca, at Nusantara's own door. and name the lesson's key words: Da Gama, Malacca. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
