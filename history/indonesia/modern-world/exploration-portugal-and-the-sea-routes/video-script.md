# Video script — Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
When Constantinople fell and the spice roads closed, Portugal answered at sea — da Gama's route and Albuquerque's seizure of Malacca brought the European age to Nusantara's door.

In 1453 Constantinople fell to Sultan Mehmed al-Fatih, and the old spice roads through it closed to the West. Spices still had to reach Europe — so Portugal answered at sea. Vasco da Gama's route around Africa opened the ocean road to India, and then Afonso de Albuquerque seized Malacca, the great market where the archipelago's spices changed hands. With Malacca taken, the European age stood at Nusantara's door: the sea road built to reach the spices now ended inside the spice islands' own world. The chapters of resistance that follow all begin here.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
