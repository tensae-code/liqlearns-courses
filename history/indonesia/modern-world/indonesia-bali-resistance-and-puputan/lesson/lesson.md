# Bali's Resistance and the Puputan

## Do it first

Write what a people might value more than survival itself. Then think of a court choosing that value in front of an army. That choice is this lesson.

Bali was the atlas's island of holdouts. Its kingdoms resisted Dutch landings for decades, expedition after expedition, long after Java's wars had ended. And when resistance finally reached its end, Bali gave the colonial century its most terrible image: the puputan. A defeated court — king, family, priests and followers, dressed in ceremonial white — marched out of the palace straight into the Dutch guns, choosing death in ritual order over surrender and capture. The puputan is not told as madness. It is told as the last form resistance could take when every other form was gone: a whole court refusing, together, to be ruled.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Bali's kingdoms resisted Dutch landings for decades — a resistance that ended in the puputan, the ritual marches of whole courts into gunfire rather than surrender. Give one real example: When Bali's courts finally fell, they did not surrender: dressed in white, they marched into the guns — the puputan is resistance carried to its last step. and name the lesson's key words: Puputan, Holdout. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
