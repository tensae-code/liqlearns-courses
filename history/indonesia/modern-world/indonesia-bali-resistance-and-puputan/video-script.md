# Video script — Bali's Resistance and the Puputan

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Bali's kingdoms resisted Dutch landings for decades — a resistance that ended in the puputan, the ritual marches of whole courts into gunfire rather than surrender.

Bali was the atlas's island of holdouts. Its kingdoms resisted Dutch landings for decades, expedition after expedition, long after Java's wars had ended. And when resistance finally reached its end, Bali gave the colonial century its most terrible image: the puputan. A defeated court — king, family, priests and followers, dressed in ceremonial white — marched out of the palace straight into the Dutch guns, choosing death in ritual order over surrender and capture. The puputan is not told as madness. It is told as the last form resistance could take when every other form was gone: a whole court refusing, together, to be ruled.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
