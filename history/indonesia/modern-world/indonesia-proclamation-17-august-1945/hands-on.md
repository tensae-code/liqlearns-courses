# Hands-on — The Proclamation, 17 August 1945

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the shortest declaration you can — two sentences — that says a new state exists. Then list what else the morning needs: a flag, witnesses, a way to spread the news. That list is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: On 17 August 1945 Sukarno read the short proclamation text, the red-and-white sewn by Fatmawati rose, IPPHOS photographers fixed the moment, and the Domei news agency carried the words to the world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
