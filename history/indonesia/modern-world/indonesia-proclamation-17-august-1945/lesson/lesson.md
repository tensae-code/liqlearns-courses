# The Proclamation, 17 August 1945

## Do it first

Write the shortest declaration you can — two sentences — that says a new state exists. Then list what else the morning needs: a flag, witnesses, a way to spread the news. That list is this lesson.

The book slows down for the seconds themselves. On the morning of 17 August 1945, in Jakarta, Sukarno read the proclamation — a short text, two sentences at its heart, declaring Indonesia's independence. Beside him stood Hatta. The flag that rose over the house was the red-and-white sewn by Fatmawati, Sukarno's wife, her stitches in the nation's first banner. The moment was fixed for history by the photographers of IPPHOS, the Indonesian press photo service, whose images of the reading the whole nation would come to know. And the words travelled: the Domei news agency carried the proclamation to the world. A state had been declared — by its own people, in its own words, on its own morning. What it would cost to keep it fills the rest of this level.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: On 17 August 1945 Sukarno read the short proclamation text, the red-and-white sewn by Fatmawati rose, IPPHOS photographers fixed the moment, and the Domei news agency carried the words to the world. Give one real example: A short text, a hand-sewn flag, a camera shutter: the Republic was proclaimed in minutes, and every element of the morning was preserved as it happened. and name the lesson's key words: Proclamation, IPPHOS. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
