# Mohammad Hatta

## Do it first

Write one rule you would keep even if breaking it cost you money and no one would ever know. That rule, kept, is this lesson.

The second inspiring life in the method book is Mohammad Hatta's. He was born in Bukittinggi, in West Sumatra, on 12 August 1902, and rose through the movement and the exile years to become the Republic's first Vice-President, at Sukarno's side in the Proclamation. But the book does not teach his life for the office. It teaches it for a gesture: offered state money as an ex-vice-president — money that was his to take — Hatta handed it back. In a course that will soon reach the struggles over the Republic's character, the method book sets this standard early: public office is a trust, and integrity is what a leader does with money when no one could blame him for keeping it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Born in Bukittinggi on 12 August 1902, Mohammad Hatta rose to Vice-President of the Republic — and, offered state money as an ex-vice-president, handed it back: a life used to teach integrity in public office. Give one real example: The state offered its former vice-president money he was entitled to take. He handed it back. The book teaches his life as the measure of an honest official. and name the lesson's key words: Bukittinggi, Integrity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
