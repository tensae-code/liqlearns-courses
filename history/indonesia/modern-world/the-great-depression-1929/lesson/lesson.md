# The Great Depression, 1929

## Do it first

Take one thing your town sells to the world. Halve its price overnight, on paper. List three things that change by the end of the month. That chain is this lesson.

The book opens the Depression with a photograph's worth of scene: Chicago, 1931, the unemployed queuing at a free soup kitchen. Then it follows the crash across the Pacific. The Indies lived by exports — sugar, rubber, copra, oil — and the Depression gutted their prices. Plantations cut, wages fell, and the colonial economy that Tanam Paksa had built showed how completely it depended on buyers an ocean away. And the book looks past the crash to its consequence: in a Europe ruined and embittered by the Depression, Hitler was rising. The war his rise signalled — the war of the next chapters — is the war that would end Dutch rule in the Indies forever. A soup queue in Chicago stands, by the book's chain of causes, at the head of the road to 1945.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Chicago's unemployed queued at a free soup kitchen in 1931 while the Depression gutted Indies export prices — and Hitler's rise in Europe signalled the war that would end Dutch rule. Give one real example: A soup queue in Chicago and a collapsed copra price in the Indies were the same event — the Depression wired the colony's fate to the world's crash. and name the lesson's key words: Great Depression, Export prices. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
