# Video script — The Great Depression, 1929

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Chicago's unemployed queued at a free soup kitchen in 1931 while the Depression gutted Indies export prices — and Hitler's rise in Europe signalled the war that would end Dutch rule.

The book opens the Depression with a photograph's worth of scene: Chicago, 1931, the unemployed queuing at a free soup kitchen. Then it follows the crash across the Pacific. The Indies lived by exports — sugar, rubber, copra, oil — and the Depression gutted their prices. Plantations cut, wages fell, and the colonial economy that Tanam Paksa had built showed how completely it depended on buyers an ocean away. And the book looks past the crash to its consequence: in a Europe ruined and embittered by the Depression, Hitler was rising. The war his rise signalled — the war of the next chapters — is the war that would end Dutch rule in the Indies forever. A soup queue in Chicago stands, by the book's chain of causes, at the head of the road to 1945.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
