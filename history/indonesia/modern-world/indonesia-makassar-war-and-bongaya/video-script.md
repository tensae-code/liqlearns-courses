# Video script — The Makassar War and Bongaya

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Sultan Hasanuddin's Makassar defended Fort Samba Opu against the VOC until 1669, and the imposed Treaty of Bongaya opened South Sulawesi to company rule.

Makassar, the Gowa kingdom of South Sulawesi, was the great trading power of the eastern archipelago, and Sultan Hasanuddin its fighting king. When the VOC demanded that the eastern trade obey its monopolies, Makassar refused, and war came. The company attacked Fort Samba Opu, Makassar's stronghold, with allies drawn from Makassar's own rivals — the same company method as at Banten: find the local quarrel, and stand behind it. The fort held out until 1669. The peace imposed was the Treaty of Bongaya, and its terms opened South Sulawesi to company rule. The east's greatest harbour had been made a company doorstep.
The Kurikulum 2013 book tells the war from the sailors' side: Gowa's Makassarese — seafarers and sultans together — fought the company for the eastern trade itself, for the right of their own ships to carry their own goods. Defeat, when it came, was sealed at Bongaya. In the atlas's order, Makassar stands for the sea peoples' war: not a kingdom defending a palace, but a maritime world defending its routes.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
