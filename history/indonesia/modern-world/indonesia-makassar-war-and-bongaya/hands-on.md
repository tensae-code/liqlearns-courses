# Hands-on — The Makassar War and Bongaya

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a harbour with ships from many islands. Then imagine one company demanding all trade pass through its own counter. What breaks first — the harbour's freedom, or the company? That contest is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Sultan Hasanuddin's Makassar defended Fort Samba Opu against the VOC until 1669, and the imposed Treaty of Bongaya opened South Sulawesi to company rule. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
