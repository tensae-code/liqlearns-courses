# Lesson plan — The Makassar War and Bongaya

**Course:** History — Indonesia · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Makassar War and Bongaya
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Colonial Rule and the Wars of Resistance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sultan Hasanuddin's Makassar defended Fort Samba Opu against the VOC until 1669, and the imposed Treaty of Bongaya opened South Sulawesi to company rule.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Colonial Rule and the Wars of Resistance (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bab I.B** — Makassar, the Gowa kingdom of South Sulawesi, was the great trading power of the eastern archipelago, and Sultan Hasanuddin its fighting king.
2. **Bab 2.A.5 Perlawanan Goa** — The Kurikulum 2013 book tells the war from the sailors' side: Gowa's Makassarese — seafarers and sultans together — fought the company for the eastern trade itself, for the right of their own ships to carry their own goods.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Sultan Hasanuddin's Makassar defended Fort Samba Opu against the VOC until 1669, and the imposed Treaty of Bongaya opened South Sulawesi to company rule.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: Makassar's sailors ruled the eastern seas — until the company, with local allies, broke their fort and wrote their surrender as a treaty.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Indonesia, as the books themselves work.
