# Ki Hadjar Dewantara

## Do it first

Write what you would teach first if you opened a school for people told they were born to obey. Your first lesson plan is this lesson.

The method book tells his life as an inspiring story, and it begins with a renunciation. Suwardi Suryaningrat was a Javanese aristocrat; he dropped his noble title and took the name Ki Hadjar Dewantara, a teacher's name. From 1912, with Cipto Mangunkusumo and Douwes Dekker — the three who launched the Indische Partij — he stood in the front rank of the movement. His own foundation was Taman Siswa, the Garden of Learners: schools built under colonial rule to give Indonesians a national education of their own, teaching dignity and independence of mind in place of obedience. The book quotes the spirit he chose for the work: 'nothing but a happy heart' — preferred over wealth. A prince who trades a title for a classroom is the awakening in one life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Javanese aristocrat who dropped his noble title, founded Taman Siswa, and with Cipto Mangunkusumo and Douwes Dekker from 1912 built national education under colonial rule — choosing 'nothing but a happy heart' over wealth. Give one real example: A prince gave up his title to become a schoolmaster — and his schools taught a colonised people to stand upright. and name the lesson's key words: Taman Siswa, Ki Hadjar Dewantara. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
