# The Revolution, 1945–1950: Defending Independence

## Do it first

Write two diaries for the same month of 1946: one of an official building a ministry, one of a fighter in the hills. Where do they meet? That meeting is this lesson.

This lesson stands on the Grade 12 teacher guide's framing chapter, and its frame is the level's master key: 1945–1950 was state-forming under fire. A government was built in weeks — ministries, an army, a currency, provinces — while the early revolution shook with upheavals of its own. The defence ran on two tracks at once: diplomacy and guerrilla war, side by side. The guide names its markers: Bandung Lautan Api, the sea of fire in which Bandung's defenders burned their own city rather than surrender it; and the Linggarjati talks, which foreign journalists covered in November 1946, carrying the Republic's case to the world. The road the frame ends on runs from the federal RIS imposed at the settlement, back to the unitary NKRI — the one Republic the revolution had meant from the start.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The 1945–1950 revolution was state-forming under fire: a government built in weeks, early-revolution upheavals, diplomacy and guerrilla war side by side — Bandung Lautan Api, the Linggarjati talks — on the road from RIS back to the unitary NKRI. Give one real example: The Republic had to be invented and defended at the same time — ministries one week, burning cities and guerrilla war the next. and name the lesson's key words: State-forming under fire, NKRI. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
