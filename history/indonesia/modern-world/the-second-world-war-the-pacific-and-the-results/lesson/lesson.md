# The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results

## Do it first

Write how long three hundred years is in generations. Then write how many weeks it took to end. The contrast is this lesson.

Japan came south wearing a slogan: the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, Asia for the Asians, liberation from the Western empires. What it came for, the book shows, was plainer: resources — above all oil. Japanese forces struck Tarakan, the oil island off Borneo, among their first targets. The Dutch East Indies, whose rulers had survived three centuries of resistance, collapsed in weeks. At Kalijati airfield in March 1942, Lieutenant-General Ter Poorten and Governor-General Tjarda van Starkenborgh surrendered the Indies unconditionally to Japan. One empire had replaced another at a signature — and everything Indonesians did for the next three years was done inside that fact.

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Japan sold its Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, struck Tarakan's oil, and at Kalijati airfield in March 1942 received the unconditional surrender of the Dutch East Indies from Ter Poorten and Governor-General Tjarda. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
