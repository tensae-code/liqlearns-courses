# Video script — The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Japan sold its Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, struck Tarakan's oil, and at Kalijati airfield in March 1942 received the unconditional surrender of the Dutch East Indies from Ter Poorten and Governor-General Tjarda.

Japan came south wearing a slogan: the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, Asia for the Asians, liberation from the Western empires. What it came for, the book shows, was plainer: resources — above all oil. Japanese forces struck Tarakan, the oil island off Borneo, among their first targets. The Dutch East Indies, whose rulers had survived three centuries of resistance, collapsed in weeks. At Kalijati airfield in March 1942, Lieutenant-General Ter Poorten and Governor-General Tjarda van Starkenborgh surrendered the Indies unconditionally to Japan. One empire had replaced another at a signature — and everything Indonesians did for the next three years was done inside that fact.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
