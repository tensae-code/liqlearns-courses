# Indonesia Under Japanese Occupation

## Do it first

Make two lists: what the occupier promised, and what the occupier took. Compare their lengths. That comparison is this lesson.

The occupation chapters refuse the slogan and itemise the reality. Japan ruled through propaganda: posters on every wall and the Asia Raya newspapers preaching the Co-Prosperity Sphere. Behind the posters stood the camps — internment camps where Dutch women, among thousands of internees, were made to bow to the Emperor. The economy was occupation money, printed by the occupier and worth what he said it was. Women's labour was organised in the Fujinkai. And at the bottom of the system stood romusa — forced labour on works like the Selokan Mataram canal — under which Indonesians laboured and died in numbers the book does not soften. The transformation of government and the impact of Japanese rule, the chapters' own titles, mean this: a new tyranny, differently dressed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Occupation meant propaganda posters and Asia Raya newspapers, internment camps where Dutch women bowed to the Emperor, occupation money, Fujinkai women's labour, the Selokan Mataram works and romusa forced labour. Give one real example: Liberation was the poster; forced labour was the fact. The occupation's two faces — propaganda and romusa — are taught side by side. and name the lesson's key words: Asia Raya, Selokan Mataram. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
