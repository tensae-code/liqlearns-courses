# The Battle of Surabaya, 1945

## Do it first

Write the ultimatum as a note: hand over your weapons by morning. Then write the city's answer, as its youth and religious teachers framed it. That answer is this lesson.

Surabaya's battle grew from a death and an ultimatum. Brigadier Mallaby, the British commander in the city, was killed in the confused fighting of late October 1945. The British answer was an ultimatum: Surabaya must surrender its weapons. The city's answer was made by its youth and its ulama — the religious teachers whose call to defend the proclaimed Republic turned refusal into duty. On 10 November 1945 the British attacked with ships, planes and tanks, and Surabaya fought. The city could not win against that weight, and did not; but the stand fixed itself in the nation's memory as the revolution's purest proof of will. Indonesia keeps 10 November as Heroes' Day — the battle's date, not a victory's.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: After Brigadier Mallaby's death, Surabaya's youth and ulama refused the British ultimatum and fought the 10 November 1945 battle that became Heroes' Day. Give one real example: A city given an ultimatum — surrender your weapons — answered by fighting a modern army with what it had. The 10th of November is still Heroes' Day. and name the lesson's key words: Ultimatum, Heroes' Day. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
