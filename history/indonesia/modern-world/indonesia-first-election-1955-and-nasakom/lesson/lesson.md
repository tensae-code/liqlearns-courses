# The First Election, 1955, and Nasakom

## Do it first

Count a class vote on paper, by hand, into the evening. Then write what you do when the winners are four camps that distrust each other. That problem is this lesson.

The Grade 12 book gives the first election its human scene: ballot boxes counted into the night in Kemayoran, Jakarta, on 29 September 1955 — Indonesia's first general election, a whole people voting at last in the state it had proclaimed ten years before. Around the election the chapter sets the era's organising: the women's movement building its own national strength. But the count's result was the era's problem: the vote scattered among rival streams, no one of them able to govern alone. Sukarno's answer was a formula — Nasakom — binding the three great streams into one cord: Nasionalisme, Agama, Komunisme — nationalism, religion and communism. The formula held the streams together by naming them one; whether a formula could govern a polarised nation is the question the next chapters answer.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ballot boxes were counted into the night in Kemayoran on 29 September 1955 in Indonesia's first election, the women's movement organised — and Sukarno answered the fragmentation with Nasakom: nationalism, religion and communism in one formula. Give one real example: The first election proved Indonesians could vote a nation into a parliament. Its result — a parliament split among rivals — produced Sukarno's formula to hold them together. and name the lesson's key words: Nasakom, Fragmentation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
