# Riau and the Chinese Revolts

## Do it first

Imagine a rumour spreading through a frightened city quarter: 'they are coming to ship us away.' Write what fear does to a community in one week. That fear is this lesson.

The resistance atlas widens here beyond palaces. In Riau, the island people took up arms against the company on their own shores. And in Batavia itself, the company's own capital, the Chinese community rose in 1740. The spark was fear: a fire in the city was blamed on the Chinese, and word spread that the authorities meant to deport them — to ship the community away across the sea. Fearing deportation, the community revolted: the Geger Pacinan, the Chinese tumult. The company's capital discovered that the people it ruled included whole communities who, pushed far enough, would fight inside its own walls.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Riau's people took up arms against the company, and in 1740 Batavia's Chinese community — fearing deportation after a fire was blamed on them — revolted in the Geger Pacinan. Give one real example: Resistance was not only sultans and kingdoms: island peoples and a city's immigrant community also rose when the company's rule turned threatening. and name the lesson's key words: Geger Pacinan, Riau. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
