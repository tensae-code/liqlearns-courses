# The Banten War and the 1684 Treaty

## Do it first

Write a short scene: two claimants to one throne, and a powerful neighbour offering help to one of them. What should the neighbour's help cost? That price is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bab I.B

Banten, the pepper sultanate of Java's western end, was not conquered from the sea. It tore itself apart. From 1682 to 1684 the kingdom fought a war inside its own palace: Sultan Ageng, the old ruler, against his own son, Sultan Haji. The son looked for a strong friend and found the VOC. The company helped Haji win — and presented the bill. By the treaty of 17 April 1684, Haji handed the company the kingdom: Banten's trade and freedom of action passed under VOC control. The lesson the book draws is cold: a divided house does not need to be invaded. It can be collected, by treaty, from the winner it crowned.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bab 2.A.4 Perlawanan Banten

The Kurikulum 2013 atlas tells the same fall in its resistance order: Banten resisted company encroachment for years — and then succession war and treaty together delivered it to the VOC. Placed among the wars of resistance, Banten's story is the warning inside the atlas: the company did not only win battles. It waited at the elbow of every divided court, treaty in hand, and let Indonesians' own quarrels do the conquering.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Banten tore itself apart in the 1682–84 war between Sultan Ageng and his son Sultan Haji — and Haji's treaty with the VOC on 17 April 1684 handed the company the kingdom. Give one real example: A father and a son fought for one throne, and the company collected the kingdom as the price of helping the winner. and name the lesson's key words: Sultan Ageng, 1684 treaty. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
