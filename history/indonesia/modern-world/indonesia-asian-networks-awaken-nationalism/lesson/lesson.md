# Asian Networks Awaken Nationalism

## Do it first

Draw lines on a map from your town to three far places ideas could arrive from. Label each line with what travels on it. That web is this lesson.

The awakening chapter of the Grade 11 book is a map of wires. Hajj pilgrims in their batik sarongs sailed to Mecca and came home connected to the whole Muslim world. Nawawi al-Bantani, a Banten scholar settled in Mecca, wrote a Qur'an commentary that flowed back into the archipelago's schools — Indonesian scholarship speaking from Islam's centre. Beyond the Muslim world, Asia was rising everywhere at once: Gandhi in India, Sun Yat-sen in China, Rizal in the Philippines, each fighting the same empires in different tongues. And in the Netherlands itself, Indonesian students founded Perhimpunan Indonesia, organising in the coloniser's own capital. Wire by wire — pilgrimage, print, example, association — the archipelago was plugged into Asia's anti-colonial awakening, and its own nationalism drew current from all of them.

## Say it to your companion

Give one real example: Nationalism did not grow in one garden: it travelled the hajj routes, the book trade and the student ships — Indonesia's awakening was wired into all of Asia's. and name the lesson's key words: Nawawi al-Bantani, Perhimpunan Indonesia. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
