# Sukarno-Era Games and Mercusuar Projects

## Do it first

List what you would build first to make the world respect a new country, and what you would build first for its villages. If the lists fight over money, you have this lesson's tension.

Sukarno's Indonesia performed itself to the world. The Grade 12 book's chapter on the era's social, economic and cultural life leads with prestige built in concrete and sport: the 1962 Asian Games in Jakarta, with their great stadium; then, when the Olympic movement punished Indonesia, Sukarno's defiant answer — GANEFO, the Games of the New Emerging Forces, a rival games of his own invention. Over the capital rose the mercusuar projects, the 'lighthouse' monuments meant to beam the new nation's brilliance abroad. But the chapter refuses to stop at spectacle: in the same years, health and education policy reached down to ordinary villages — clinics and schools where the lighthouse beams did not need to reach. Prestige for the world, services for the village: the era held both, and the book teaches both.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Sukarno built prestige in concrete and sport — the 1962 Asian Games, his rival GANEFO games, and the 'mercusuar' lighthouse projects — while health and education policy reached ordinary villages. Give one real example: A new nation announced itself with stadiums and monuments the world could not ignore — and, in the same years, sent health and schooling down to the villages. and name the lesson's key words: GANEFO, Mercusuar projects. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
