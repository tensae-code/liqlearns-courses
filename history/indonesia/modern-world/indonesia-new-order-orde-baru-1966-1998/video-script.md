# Video script — The New Order: Orde Baru, 1966–1998

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The New Order fused parties into a three-colour politics, made the economy the commander of development through the Pelita plans, and enforced hegemony through social and cultural life — stability with a price the chapter itemises; the guide demands it be read not black-and-white, but as state strengthening and development set against weak policies, human-rights abuses and resistance.

The student book's New Order chapter describes the machine first. Politics was fused: the party system was pressed into a three-colour order — the government's vehicle and two licensed oppositions. Development was enthroned: the economy became the commander, marched forward in the Pelita, the five-year development plans that built roads, schools, dams and harvests across three decades. And the order's hegemony reached through social and cultural life — what could be said, printed, organised and remembered was supervised. The chapter's own balance is in its title's promise: stability, with a price the chapter itemises line by line.
The teacher guide sets the terms on which this era may be taught, and they are the same terms the student book uses: not black-and-white. The 1966–67 transition handed power from Sukarno to Suharto; the reading the guide demands sets state strengthening and real development on one side of the ledger, and on the other, weak policies, human-rights abuses, the resistance the regime provoked, and its end in crisis. Pupils are to evaluate the era from multiple perspectives — the New Order judged by evidence, in the country it made.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
