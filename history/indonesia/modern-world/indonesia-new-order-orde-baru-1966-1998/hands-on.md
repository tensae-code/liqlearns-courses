# Hands-on — The New Order: Orde Baru, 1966–1998

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Make a ledger with two columns for one long government: what it built, what it cost. Fill three lines each side from the lesson. That ledger is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The New Order fused parties into a three-colour politics, made the economy the commander of development through the Pelita plans, and enforced hegemony through social and cultural life — stability with a price the chapter itemises; the guide demands it be read not black-and-white, but as state strengthening and development set against weak policies, human-rights abuses and resistance. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
