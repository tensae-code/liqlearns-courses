# Sultan Agung Attacks Batavia

## Do it first

Stand at a city wall in your mind. Write what the attackers need to win, and what the defenders need. Then read whose needs ran out first. That weighing is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bab I.B

Sultan Agung of Mataram was the strongest Javanese ruler of his age, and he would not leave the Dutch company sitting in Batavia on his island's coast. Twice he sent Mataram's armies to besiege the VOC's capital, against Jan Pieterszoon Coen, the company's founder-governor. The sieges failed — Batavia's walls, ships and supplies held, and Mataram's long supply lines broke — but the attempt itself is the story: an inland rice empire measuring its full strength against a sea empire's fortress. The Grade 11 book adds how the war is remembered: the painter Sudjojono later reconstructed the siege on canvas, working from the archives — history rebuilt by an artist from evidence.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bab 2.A.3 Sultan Agung Versus J.P. Coen

The Kurikulum 2013 book titles its telling as a duel: Sultan Agung versus J.P. Coen. King against company-founder, Mataram against Batavia. Told that way, the sieges become the measure of two kinds of power — the great agrarian kingdom of the interior, and the armed trading company on the coast. The king could not take the city; the company could not march inland and take the kingdom. Java's future would be decided by other means, in later chapters — but the stand-off begins here, in front of Batavia's walls.
