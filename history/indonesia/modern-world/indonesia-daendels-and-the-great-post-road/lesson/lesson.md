# Daendels and the Great Post Road

## Do it first

Trace Java's north coast on a map from Anyer to Panarukan. Now write what it costs to build that road in a year, and who pays. That bill is this lesson.

In 1808 the Batavian Republic sent Herman Willem Daendels to hold Java against Britain, and Java got a whirlwind. Daendels arrived quoting the French Revolution — liberty, equality, a new order — and then ruled as a dictator, brooking no refusal. His monument is the Great Post Road, driven along Java's north coast from Anyer to Panarukan, a thousand kilometres of military highway built at frantic speed. The book names its price plainly: kerja rodi, forced labour, Javanese villagers conscripted to build it, in numbers and conditions that cost many their lives. The road still runs. So does the question the book leaves on it: what is a work of progress worth, when it is built on forced hands?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Governor-General Daendels (1808–1811), sent by the Batavian Republic to hold Java against Britain, quoted the French Revolution and ruled as a dictator — driving the Anyer–Panarukan Great Post Road out of the people by kerja rodi forced labour. Give one real example: A road a thousand kilometres long, built in a year by forced labour — the Great Post Road is Daendels' monument and his indictment in one. and name the lesson's key words: Kerja rodi, Great Post Road. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
