---

This lesson tells one story.
Governor-General Daendels (1808–1811), sent by the Batavian Republic to hold Java against Britain, quoted the French Revolution and ruled as a dictator — driving the Anyer–Panarukan Great Post Road out of the people by kerja rodi forced labour.

In 1808 the Batavian Republic sent Herman Willem Daendels to hold Java against Britain, and Java got a whirlwind. Daendels arrived quoting the French Revolution — liberty, equality, a new order — and then ruled as a dictator, brooking no refusal. His monument is the Great Post Road, driven along Java's north coast from Anyer to Panarukan, a thousand kilometres of military highway built at frantic speed. The book names its price plainly: kerja rodi, forced labour, Javanese villagers conscripted to build it, in numbers and conditions that cost many their lives. The road still runs. So does the question the book leaves on it: what is a work of progress worth, when it is built on forced hands?
