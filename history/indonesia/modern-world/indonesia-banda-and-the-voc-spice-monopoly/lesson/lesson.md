# Banda and the VOC Spice Monopoly

## Do it first

Imagine your village grows the only supply of something the world wants. Write what a fair trade would look like, then what a forced monopoly would look like. The difference is this lesson.

Follow the chain the book builds as a case study. Constantinople falls in 1453. Europe, cut off from its spices, hunts for sea routes around the world. The routes are found, the ships come east — and the chain ends in the Banda islands, the tiny volcanic specks where nutmeg and its red lace, mace, grew almost nowhere else on earth. There the Dutch East India Company, the VOC, forced the islanders to sell their nutmeg and mace to no one else. That is a monopoly with soldiers behind it: the growers kept the trees, but lost the right to choose their buyer. The spice that flavoured Europe's food was gathered under compulsion, at the far end of a chain that started with a fallen city.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The hunt for spices that began when Constantinople fell ends in Banda, where the VOC forced the islanders to sell their nutmeg and mace to no one else — a monopoly enforced on the islands that grew them. Give one real example: Nutmeg grew almost nowhere else on earth but Banda — and the company that seized the trade made selling it to anyone else a crime. and name the lesson's key words: VOC, Monopoly. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
