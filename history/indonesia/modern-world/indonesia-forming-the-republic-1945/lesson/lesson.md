# Forming the Republic, 1945

## Do it first

List what a brand-new state needs in its first week. Order your list. Then compare it with what the PPKI did, in the order it did it. That ordering is this lesson.

A proclamation declares a state; it does not yet run one. The Grade 12 book's first chapter follows the PPKI — the preparatory committee — through the days after 17 August 1945 as it assembled the Republic piece by piece. The committee wrote and adopted a constitution. It elected Sukarno president and Hatta vice-president. It built the working parts: ministries to govern, and regions — provinces — to govern across the archipelago. All of it was done in days, and under a strange shadow: the Japanese, defeated but not yet disarmed, still held the arms depots. The Republic's government was formed in the same streets where the old occupier still stood guard — a state assembled, as the chapter shows, out of paper, will and speed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the days after the Proclamation the PPKI wrote a constitution, elected Sukarno and Hatta, and built ministries and regions — a state assembled while the Japanese still held the arms depots. Give one real example: A constitution, a president, ministries, provinces — assembled in days, while the defeated occupier still guarded the guns. and name the lesson's key words: PPKI, Ministries. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
