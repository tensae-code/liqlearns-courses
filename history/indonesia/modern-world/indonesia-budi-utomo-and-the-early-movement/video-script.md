# Video script — Budi Utomo and the Early Movement

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Organisation replaced rebellion as the weapon: STOVIA students founded Budi Utomo, Sarekat Islam gathered the masses, and Tjipto, Douwes Dekker and Suwardi launched the Indische Partij — the movement organised in every key, from associations and unions to the PKI and Taman Siswa's schools.

In 1908 the weapon changed. Students of STOVIA, the colonial medical school, founded Budi Utomo — Utmost Endeavour — an association, not an army: members, meetings, dues, a programme. Around it the movement multiplied its forms. Sarekat Islam gathered the masses at gatherings like Kaliwungu, a movement of traders and believers counted in the hundreds of thousands. And Tjipto Mangunkusumo, Douwes Dekker and Suwardi Suryaningrat launched the Indische Partij, the first party to demand the Indies for all who lived there. The Grade 11 book's line is the chapter's thesis: organisation replaced rebellion as the weapon. What three centuries of wars could not do, the organised generation set out to do with cards, congresses and newspapers.
The Kurikulum 2013 book hears the movement in every key at once: early associations; religious organisations; youth and women's organisations; labour unions; the PKI, the communist party; and Taman Siswa's schools, educating outside the colonial system altogether. No single form carried the movement — it organised in every key, so that when one organisation was banned or broken, others played on. That breadth is why this generation's struggle outlasted every war in the atlas before it.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
