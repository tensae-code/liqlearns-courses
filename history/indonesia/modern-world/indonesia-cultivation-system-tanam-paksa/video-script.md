# Video script — The Cultivation System: Tanam Paksa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The Cultuurstelsel — Tanam Paksa — forced Javanese villages to grow sugar, coffee and tobacco for export; the book weighs its exploitation against the plantation towns, like Deli Serdang and Malang, that grew up around it.

After the Java War, the restored Dutch state built its engine: the Cultuurstelsel, the Cultivation System — in Indonesian memory, Tanam Paksa, forced planting. Javanese villages were required to devote land and labour to export crops — sugar, coffee, tobacco — delivered to the colonial state at fixed terms. The Grade 11 book weighs the system honestly on both scales. On one side, exploitation: villages bent to the state's crops, hunger where export fields displaced food. On the other, the changes it dragged in: plantation towns grew up around the working of the crops, like Deli Serdang and Malang, new towns with new economies. The weighing itself is the lesson — a system can build towns and still be a wrong.
The Kurikulum 2013 book is blunter about where the system stood: restored Dutch dominance rested on Tanam Paksa. It documents the burdens on Javanese villages as the colonial state's engine — the machine that made the colony pay. In the older book's architecture, the cultivation system is not an episode but a foundation: the decades of Dutch rule that follow, their wealth and their misery alike, run on forced planting.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
