# From Liberal to Guided Democracy, 1950–1966

## Do it first

Line up six dominoes and label them: parties, regions, army, economy, president, parties again. Tip the first in your mind. That falling line is this lesson.

This lesson stands on the Grade 12 teacher guide's overview of the years between the revolution and the New Order. The guide sets Indonesia inside the new Cold War constellation, and watches the young democracy polarise: parties and identities hardened against each other, cabinets rose and fell, and the imbalance between the centre and the regions broke into open rebellion — DI/TII's Islamic state, Sumatra's PRRI, Sulawesi's Permesta, and the RMS in the Moluccas. Liberal democracy gave way to Sukarno's Guided Democracy, an attempt to rule the polarisation from above. The guide's image is dominoes: each instability tipping the next, running in a line to the event of 30 September 1965, where the next lessons begin.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Indonesia inside the Cold War constellation polarised by party and identity; centre–region imbalance spawned DI/TII, PRRI/Permesta and the RMS — and the dominoes ran to the 30 September 1965 event. Give one real example: A parliament of many parties, regions in revolt, an army in politics: the guide teaches the 1950s and early 1960s as dominoes falling toward one night in 1965. and name the lesson's key words: Guided Democracy, Polarisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
