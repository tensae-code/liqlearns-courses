# Hands-on — The Spanish Flu, 1918

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write a one-page pamphlet to protect your street from a sickness going around: what to do, what not to believe. That pamphlet is this lesson's weapon.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The 1918 Spanish flu swept the Indies along the shipping routes, and the colonial state answered with pamphlets, newspaper campaigns against pandemic hoaxes, and mass cholera vaccination on the rubber estates. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
