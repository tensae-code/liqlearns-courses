# The Spanish Flu, 1918

## Do it first

Write a one-page pamphlet to protect your street from a sickness going around: what to do, what not to believe. That pamphlet is this lesson's weapon.

In 1918 the world's pandemic reached the Indies the way everything reached it: along the shipping routes, port by port. The Spanish flu swept the archipelago, and the Grade 11 book studies the colonial state's answer as a piece of history in itself. The state printed pamphlets to teach protection. It ran newspaper campaigns against pandemic hoaxes — false cures and wild rumours travelled as fast as the virus, and had to be fought in print. And it carried mass cholera vaccination to the rubber estates, where crowds of workers lived close together. The chapter's lesson is double: a pandemic tests a state's medicine, and its honesty. The flu chapter stands in the book at the end of the colonial period — a rehearsal, though no one knew it, for the crises that would finish the colonial state itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The 1918 Spanish flu swept the Indies along the shipping routes, and the colonial state answered with pamphlets, newspaper campaigns against pandemic hoaxes, and mass cholera vaccination on the rubber estates. Give one real example: A virus rode the same ships as the spices — and the colony fought it with paper: pamphlets, newspapers, and needles on the rubber estates. and name the lesson's key words: Spanish flu, Hoax. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
