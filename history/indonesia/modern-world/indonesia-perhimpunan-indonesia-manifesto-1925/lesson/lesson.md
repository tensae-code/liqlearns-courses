# Perhimpunan Indonesia and the 1925 Manifesto

## Do it first

Write two names for the same place: the name an outsider gave it, and the name its own people choose. Say what changes when the second name is used. That change is this lesson.

In the Netherlands, the coloniser's own capital, Indonesian students organised in Perhimpunan Indonesia — and in 1925 they issued a Political Manifesto that did one enormous thing with words. It named the nation. Not 'the Indies', the administrative label of the colony; not a list of islands and peoples; but Indonesia — one nation, to be freed as one. A manifesto is paper, but the paper carried the word home on the student ships, and the word reorganised the movement: every association, every congress after 1925 could now say what it was for in a single name. The Youth Pledge of 1928, three years later, pledges exactly this name.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Students in the Netherlands issued the 1925 Political Manifesto, naming Indonesia — not the Indies — as the nation to be freed, and carried the word home. Give one real example: One word did the work of an army: the students stopped saying 'the Indies', the coloniser's name, and named the nation Indonesia. and name the lesson's key words: Manifesto, Perhimpunan Indonesia. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
