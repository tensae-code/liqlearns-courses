# Video script — Colonial Education and the New Elites

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Colonial schools — STOVIA's medical students, the Kartini schools, Padang Panjang's Madrasah Diniyah Putri — were built to serve the colony, and instead produced the educated generation that would end it; the Volksraad opened as a limited council.

Every empire needs educated hands, and the colony built schools to grow its own: STOVIA, the medical school whose students fill the next chapters of this course; the Kartini schools, carrying girls' education in the name of Java's great letter-writer; and at Padang Panjang, the Madrasah Diniyah Putri, where Islamic schooling for girls took modern form. The schools were built to serve the colony — to staff its offices, clinics and classrooms. What they actually produced was a new elite: Indonesians educated enough to read the colony's own promises, measure its failures, and organise its ending. The same chapter notes the Volksraad, opened in Batavia as a limited people's council — a small door of representation that taught its members how much larger a door they needed.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
