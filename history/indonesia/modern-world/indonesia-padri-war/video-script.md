# Video script — The Padri War

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
In West Sumatra the Padri movement's religious war became a war against the Dutch: a civil war over faith drew the colonial army in, and Imam Bonjol's Padris ended fighting the Dutch instead.

In West Sumatra, the Padri movement — religious reformers demanding a stricter Islam — went to war with the defenders of the old customs. It began as a civil war inside Minangkabau society, faith against tradition. But one side called in the Dutch, and the war changed its nature: the religious war became a war against the Dutch themselves. The Grade 11 book notes how we see it: through the drawings of Justus Pieter de Veer, a Dutch officer-illustrator who fought in the war he sketched. The enemy's pencil preserved the Padris' war.
The Kurikulum 2013 book tells the turn plainly: West Sumatra's religious civil war draws the Dutch in, and Imam Bonjol's Padris end fighting the colonial army instead of their neighbours. Bonjol, the Padri leader, held the highlands for years. The atlas's lesson is about invitation: a foreign power asked into a local quarrel does not leave when the quarrel ends. It stays, and becomes the quarrel.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
