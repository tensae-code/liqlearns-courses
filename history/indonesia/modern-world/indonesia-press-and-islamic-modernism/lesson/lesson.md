# The Press and Islamic Modernism

## Do it first

Write a front page for an imaginary newspaper of your town, in your own language. What would you put first? That choosing is what the native press learned to do. This is the lesson.

Before there were parties, there was print. The Kurikulum 2013 book pairs two engines of change in one chapter. The first is the native press: newspapers in Malay, the language ordinary readers shared, carrying progress — news, argument, advertisement, indignation — into Malay-reading homes that no Dutch gazette had ever entered. A people that reads its own affairs in its own language is already halfway to organising them. The second engine is Islamic modernism: reform movements that reorganised faith for a new age, founding schools with new curricula, charities with new methods, and associations that taught Muslims to run modern institutions. Press and reform together built the awakening's infrastructure — the readers, the schools and the habits of association the political movement would inherit.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The native press carried progress into Malay-reading homes, while Islamic modernism and reform movements reorganised faith, schools and charity for a new age. Give one real example: Two printing-age forces moved together: newspapers that taught a people to read its own affairs, and a reformed faith that rebuilt its schools and charities. and name the lesson's key words: Native press, Islamic modernism. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
