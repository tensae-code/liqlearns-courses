# Aceh and Maluku Resist Portugal and the VOC

## Do it first

Put two dots on a map: Aceh in the far west, Maluku in the far east. Write one word under each for what grew there that empires wanted. That map is this lesson.

The resistance atlas of the Kurikulum 2013 book opens at the two ends of the archipelago. In the west, Aceh guarded the pepper coast: it fought the Portuguese first, and then the Dutch company that came after them, refusing for generations to let foreigners own the trade of its own shore. In the east, the Moluccas — the Spice Islands themselves — rose for their cloves and nutmeg, the treasures that grew nowhere else. Aceh versus Portugal and the VOC, Maluku taking up arms: these are the first two centuries of armed refusal, and the book sets them first for a reason. Before there was a nation to defend, there were already coasts that would not be owned.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Aceh fought the Portuguese and then the Dutch for the pepper coast, while the Moluccas rose for their cloves and nutmeg — the first two centuries of armed refusal. Give one real example: Pepper in the west, cloves and nutmeg in the east — at both ends of the archipelago, people fought the companies for the spices growing in their own soil. and name the lesson's key words: Aceh, Maluku. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
