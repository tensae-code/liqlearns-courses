# Exploration: Causes and Results

## Do it first

Think of something precious in your region. Write down who would want it, and what they might do to get it. That wanting — and what it causes — is this lesson.

The Kurikulum 2013 book opens its hunt with a name: the 'pearl of the East', the prize the West crossed oceans to seize. It names the motives without decoration — wealth, glory and gospel: gold and spices to take, fame and empire to win, and a faith to spread. Those three wants launched the voyages. But the book is careful about the word 'found': the Nusantara the Western ships reached was already trading, with Chinese ships in its harbours and its own sailors on every route. Exploration, in this telling, is not discovery of an empty place. It is the arrival of hunters at a busy crossroads — and everything in the chapters after this one follows from that arrival.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The West came east for wealth, glory and gospel — the three motives that launched the voyages — and 'found' a Nusantara that was already trading with Chinese ships; the pearl of the East became a prize to seize. Give one real example: The hunters called Nusantara the pearl of the East — but the pearl already had owners, sailors and trading partners when the hunters arrived. and name the lesson's key words: The three motives, Pearl of the East. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
