# Video script — The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Refusing both blocs under Bebas Aktif — free and active — Indonesia hosted the 1955 Asia-Africa Conference at Bandung and helped found the Non-Aligned Movement: the Cold War read from the equator.

The Cold War divided the world between two armed blocs, and every new nation was pressed to enlist. Indonesia's answer was a doctrine with a name: Bebas Aktif, free and active — free of both blocs, active in making peace. In 1955 the doctrine took a city's name: at Bandung, Indonesia hosted the Asia-Africa Conference, where the formerly colonised peoples of two continents met as principals, not as prizes. Out of that meeting's spirit grew the Non-Aligned Movement, which Indonesia helped found — a third position for the world's majority. The Grade 12 book files this lesson under the shared Cold War title, but its story is Indonesia's own: the Cold War read from the equator, by a nation that had just fought too hard for its independence to hand its foreign policy to anyone.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
