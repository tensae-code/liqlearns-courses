# Hands-on — The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write a reply to two powerful friends each demanding you join against the other. Your reply must refuse both and keep both friendships possible. That reply is this lesson's doctrine.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Refusing both blocs under Bebas Aktif — free and active — Indonesia hosted the 1955 Asia-Africa Conference at Bandung and helped found the Non-Aligned Movement: the Cold War read from the equator. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
