# The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

## Do it first

Write a reply to two powerful friends each demanding you join against the other. Your reply must refuse both and keep both friendships possible. That reply is this lesson's doctrine.

The Cold War divided the world between two armed blocs, and every new nation was pressed to enlist. Indonesia's answer was a doctrine with a name: Bebas Aktif, free and active — free of both blocs, active in making peace. In 1955 the doctrine took a city's name: at Bandung, Indonesia hosted the Asia-Africa Conference, where the formerly colonised peoples of two continents met as principals, not as prizes. Out of that meeting's spirit grew the Non-Aligned Movement, which Indonesia helped found — a third position for the world's majority. The Grade 12 book files this lesson under the shared Cold War title, but its story is Indonesia's own: the Cold War read from the equator, by a nation that had just fought too hard for its independence to hand its foreign policy to anyone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Refusing both blocs under Bebas Aktif — free and active — Indonesia hosted the 1955 Asia-Africa Conference at Bandung and helped found the Non-Aligned Movement: the Cold War read from the equator. Give one real example: Two superpowers demanded the world choose a side. At Bandung, the colonised world answered: we choose our own side. and name the lesson's key words: Bebas Aktif, Non-Aligned Movement. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
