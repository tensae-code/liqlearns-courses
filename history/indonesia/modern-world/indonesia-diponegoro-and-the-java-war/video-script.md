# Video script — Diponegoro and the Java War

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Prince Diponegoro's five-year Java War (1825–1830) was the last great princely resistance before the colonial state hardened — a guerrilla war that bled Java and the colonial treasury before his capture under a flag of negotiation, fixed forever in Raden Saleh's painting.

The method book meets the Java War through a painting. Raden Saleh's great canvas shows the capture of Prince Diponegoro, and the class reads it as a source: on the end of the Java War, and on the prince himself — a man raised outside the court who became its leader in war. A painting is not a photograph; it argues. Saleh paints the defeated prince standing proud among his captors, and the book teaches pupils to read that argument as evidence about how Indonesians remembered the war's end.
The Grade 11 survey closes the war in one line: Raden Saleh's canvas of Diponegoro's capture closes the five-year Java War, the last great princely resistance before the colonial state hardens. After 1830 there would be no more wars of princes on this scale in Java. The colonial state that emerged from the war was a harder, deeper state — and the chapters after this one, of cultivation and schools, are its works.
The Kurikulum 2013 atlas tells the war itself: the prince's five-year guerrilla war across Java, bleeding the island and the colonial treasury together, a war the Dutch could not finish by fighting. It was finished by talking: Diponegoro was captured under a flag of negotiation, seized at a meeting he had entered to parley. The atlas lets the method of the ending stand beside the war's scale — the empire's hardest Java war ended not with a battle, but with a broken truce.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
