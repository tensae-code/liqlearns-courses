# Hands-on — Diponegoro and the Java War

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at a painting of a captured prince, calm among his captors. Write what the painter wants you to feel, and what the captors wanted people to feel. The two feelings are this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Prince Diponegoro's five-year Java War (1825–1830) was the last great princely resistance before the colonial state hardened — a guerrilla war that bled Java and the colonial treasury before his capture under a flag of negotiation, fixed forever in Raden Saleh's painting. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
