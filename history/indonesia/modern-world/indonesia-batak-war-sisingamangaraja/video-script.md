# Video script — The Batak War: Sisingamangaraja

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Around Lake Toba, the priest-king Sisingamangaraja XII sustained Batak resistance against Dutch columns into the 20th century.

Around Lake Toba, the great lake in Sumatra's northern highlands, stood a ruler of a different kind: Sisingamangaraja XII, a priest-king, whose authority was sacred before it was military. When Dutch columns pushed into the Batak lands, he sustained the resistance — rallying the Batak people around the lake country, retreating, returning, refusing — and he kept it alive into the 20th century, decades after most of the atlas's wars had closed. The Batak War is the atlas's proof that resistance was not one generation's work. Around Toba, it passed into a new century still burning.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
