# The Batak War: Sisingamangaraja

## Do it first

Picture a lake inside an ancient volcano, with villages around it. Write how its priest-king could rally them all. That rallying is this lesson.

Around Lake Toba, the great lake in Sumatra's northern highlands, stood a ruler of a different kind: Sisingamangaraja XII, a priest-king, whose authority was sacred before it was military. When Dutch columns pushed into the Batak lands, he sustained the resistance — rallying the Batak people around the lake country, retreating, returning, refusing — and he kept it alive into the 20th century, decades after most of the atlas's wars had closed. The Batak War is the atlas's proof that resistance was not one generation's work. Around Toba, it passed into a new century still burning.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Around Lake Toba, the priest-king Sisingamangaraja XII sustained Batak resistance against Dutch columns into the 20th century. Give one real example: A priest-king in the highlands around a great lake kept a resistance alive into the age of steam and telegraphs. and name the lesson's key words: Priest-king, Lake Toba. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
