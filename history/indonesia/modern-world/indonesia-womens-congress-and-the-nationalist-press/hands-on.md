# Hands-on — The Women's Congress and the Nationalist Press

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the front page headline of a newspaper edited by someone the official papers ignore. Then write the agenda of their first congress. That voice is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The First Indonesian Women's Congress met in Yogyakarta on 22–25 December 1928, while Ruhana Kuddus edited Soenting Melayoe, Tirto Adhi Soerjo invented the native press, Rasuna Said rose toward parliament, and Max Havelaar still indicted the system. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
