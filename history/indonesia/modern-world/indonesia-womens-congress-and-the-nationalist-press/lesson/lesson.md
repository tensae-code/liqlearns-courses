# The Women's Congress and the Nationalist Press

## Do it first

Write the front page headline of a newspaper edited by someone the official papers ignore. Then write the agenda of their first congress. That voice is this lesson.

In December 1928, weeks after the youth pledged the nation, the First Indonesian Women's Congress met in Yogyakarta, from the 22nd to the 25th — women organising nationally in their own name. The Grade 11 book surrounds the congress with the movement's women of print and platform. Ruhana Kuddus edited Soenting Melayoe, a women's newspaper, in West Sumatra. Tirto Adhi Soerjo — the book honours him alongside — had invented the native press itself, the newspapers in Malay that taught a people to read its own affairs. Rasuna Said rose as an orator toward parliament. And over the whole system still hung Max Havelaar, the great novel that had indicted the cultivation system generations before: the book keeps it in the chapter because the indictment was still being answered.

Give one real example: Two months after the Youth Pledge, Indonesia's women held their own congress — the movement's other half, organised, publishing and speaking in its own voice. and name the lesson's key words: Soenting Melayoe, Rasuna Said. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
