# Pattimura's Revolt, 1817

## Do it first

Write what changes when an empire leaves and then comes back years later, expecting everything as it was. Who refuses first? That refusal is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bab I.B

When the Dutch returned to the Moluccas after the British interregnum, they found the islands unwilling. The rising of 1817 was led by Pattimura, an ex-sergeant — a man the colonial army itself had trained. In the Grade 11 book's telling, Pattimura leads the rising against the returning Dutch in the Moluccas, and pays the rising's price: he is executed at Fort Victoria in Ambon. The islands that had risen for their spices two centuries before rose again — this time against a returning master who expected obedience as his right.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bab 2.B.2 Pattimura Angkat Senjata

The Kurikulum 2013 atlas gives the rising its sharpest scene: Pattimura takes Fort Duurstede on the island of Saparua. A fort is the colonial state in stone — its flag, its guns, its gaol — and the ex-sergeant's men seized it from inside knowledge of how such places worked. From Saparua the rising led Maluku's refusal of 1817. It was crushed, as the atlas records without flinching; but a fort taken by the ruled from the rulers is never forgotten by either side.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the Moluccas, Pattimura — an ex-sergeant — led the 1817 rising against the returning Dutch, took Fort Duurstede on Saparua, and was executed at Fort Victoria in Ambon. Give one real example: A former soldier of the colonial army turned his training against it — Pattimura's rising is the Moluccas' answer to the Dutch return. and name the lesson's key words: Pattimura, Duurstede. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
