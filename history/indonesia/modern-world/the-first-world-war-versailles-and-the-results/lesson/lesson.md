# The First World War, Versailles and the Results

## Do it first

Hear a promise made to others that sounds like it should include you. Write the question you would ask its makers. That question is this lesson.

The Grade 11 book admits world events only where they change Indonesia's fate, and the Great War passes that test through its ending. At Versailles, the victors redrew Europe and spoke the language of nations' rights — new nations were promised, peoples were to decide their own futures. The book reads the settlement from Batavia: Indonesians heard the promise clearly, and measured it against their own condition. If nations in Europe deserved states of their own, why not the nation that had just named itself in the students' manifesto and the youth's pledge? Versailles fed Indonesian demands — demands the colonial order, built on the opposite principle, could not answer without ceasing to be itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Great War and the Versailles settlement are read from Batavia: new nations promised in Europe fed Indonesian demands the colonial order could not answer. Give one real example: Europe's war promised new nations for Europeans — and Indonesians, listening from Batavia, asked why the promise stopped at Europe's edge. and name the lesson's key words: Versailles, The Great War. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
