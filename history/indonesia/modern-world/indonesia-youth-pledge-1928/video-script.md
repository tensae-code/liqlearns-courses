# Video script — The Youth Pledge, 1928

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
At the Second Youth Congress in Jakarta in 1928, young delegates from rival regional associations pledged one motherland, one nation, one language — Indonesia.

In October 1928 the Second Youth Congress met in Jakarta. The delegates were young, and they came from rival regional associations — Jong Java, Jong Sumatra, and the rest, each proud of its own island first. What they did in that hall outgrew every rivalry: they pledged the Sumpah Pemuda, the Youth Pledge — one motherland, Indonesia; one nation, Indonesia; one language of unity, Indonesian. No army enforced it and no state yet existed to receive it. A generation simply promised, in public, to be one people — and the promise held. The Grade 11 book places the pledge at the climax of the national movement's chapter: organisation's finest hour, spoken by the young.
The Kurikulum 2013 book shows the road and the second front. The road runs through the congresses to the 1928 pledge and a modern nationalism; and that nationalism fought on two fronts at once — mass organisation outside the colonial institutions, and speeches inside the Volksraad, the limited council, where Indonesian members used the coloniser's own chamber as a platform. Outside and inside together, the movement pressed on until the colonial government's final years. The pledge was not the end of the struggle; it was the moment the struggle knew exactly what it was for.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
