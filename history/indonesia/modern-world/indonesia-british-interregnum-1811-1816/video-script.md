# Video script — The British Interregnum, 1811–1816

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Britain took Java in 1811, and Raffles's five-year interregnum interrupted Dutch rule before the colony was handed back in 1816.

Daendels had been sent to hold Java against Britain — and in 1811 Britain took it anyway. What followed was an interregnum, a between-rule: five years in which Java was governed by the British under Thomas Stamford Raffles, while the Netherlands itself was swallowed by Napoleon's Europe. Raffles reformed, surveyed, wrote Java's history and antiquities into a famous book — and then, in 1816, the colony was handed back to the Dutch under the settlements that ended the Napoleonic wars. The lesson inside the interruption is one Indonesians would meet again: their land could be transferred between empires by signatures in Europe, without a single Indonesian in the room. The returning Dutch of 1816 are the masters Pattimura would rise against a year later.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
