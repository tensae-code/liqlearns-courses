# Video script — The Krakatau Eruption, 1883

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
In 1883 Mount Krakatau erupted in one of the greatest catastrophes of the recorded age — and the class reads it through colonial records, learning how a single dated event anchors people, place and time.

The method book opens its whole course with this mountain. In 1883, Mount Krakatau, in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, erupted and collapsed in a catastrophe heard around the world, whose waves drowned the coasts nearby. The class does not start with the geology. It starts with the records: the colonial reports, the dated documents, the paper trail a single event leaves behind. Reading them, pupils learn the historian's basic move — take one event, fix its date, place it on the map, and find the people inside it. Krakatau is the anchor case for the whole book: people, place and time, held together by one dated explosion.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
