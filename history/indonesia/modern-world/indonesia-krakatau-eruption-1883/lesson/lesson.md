# The Krakatau Eruption, 1883

## Do it first

Choose the most exactly-dated event you know. Write its date, its place, and two people it touched. That anchoring — date, place, people — is this lesson.

The method book opens its whole course with this mountain. In 1883, Mount Krakatau, in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, erupted and collapsed in a catastrophe heard around the world, whose waves drowned the coasts nearby. The class does not start with the geology. It starts with the records: the colonial reports, the dated documents, the paper trail a single event leaves behind. Reading them, pupils learn the historian's basic move — take one event, fix its date, place it on the map, and find the people inside it. Krakatau is the anchor case for the whole book: people, place and time, held together by one dated explosion.
