# Hands-on — The Krakatau Eruption, 1883

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Choose the most exactly-dated event you know. Write its date, its place, and two people it touched. That anchoring — date, place, people — is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In 1883 Mount Krakatau erupted in one of the greatest catastrophes of the recorded age — and the class reads it through colonial records, learning how a single dated event anchors people, place and time. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
