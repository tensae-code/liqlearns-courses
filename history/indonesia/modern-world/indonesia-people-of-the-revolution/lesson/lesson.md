# The People of the Revolution

## Do it first

List three jobs a revolution needs besides soldiers. For each, write who did it in your own town's terms. That list is this lesson.

The Grade 12 book closes its revolution with the people the battle chapters leave out. Women ran the public kitchens that fed the fighters — an army marches on what those kitchens cooked — and women also stood on the front line itself. Artists and writers fought with the revolution's other weapons: the Generation of '45, the poets and authors who gave the struggle its language and its conscience while it was still being fought. And school students left their classrooms for the laskar pelajar, the student militias — children who decided their schooling could wait and their country could not. The chapter's argument is the level's completion: the Republic was defended by a whole people, and its history belongs to all of them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The revolution's other armies: women in public kitchens and on the front line, artists and writers of the Generation of '45, and school students who left class for the laskar pelajar. Give one real example: History books name generals. This chapter names the cooks, the poets and the schoolchildren who also made the revolution. and name the lesson's key words: Public kitchens, Laskar pelajar. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The revolution's other armies: women in public kitchens and on the front line, artists and writers of the Generation of '45, and school students who left class for the laskar pelajar.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
