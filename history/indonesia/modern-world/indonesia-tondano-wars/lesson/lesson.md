# The Tondano Wars

## Do it first

Think of a small town that refuses an order from a distant capital, again and again, for years. Write why it keeps refusing. That stubbornness is this lesson.

Not every war in the atlas is famous, and the book keeps this one on purpose. Around Lake Tondano in North Sulawesi, the Minahasa people fought the colonial state in a long war — little-told beside Java's great campaigns, but no shorter in courage or cost for the people who lived it. The Tondano wars stand in the atlas as proof of the pattern's reach: resistance was not a Javanese affair, nor only sultans' work. In the far north of Sulawesi, farming people around a lake took on the colonial state and kept fighting. The atlas restores their war to the national story, where it belongs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Minahasa people of Tondano, in North Sulawesi, fought the colonial state in a long, little-told war. Give one real example: Some wars fill chapters; Tondano's filled years — a small people's long fight that the big histories nearly forgot. and name the lesson's key words: Minahasa, Little-told war. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
