# Hands-on — The Road to the Proclamation, August 1945

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write a timetable of one decisive week, hour by hour, with two columns: what is collapsing, what is being built. That double column is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Japan's defeats mounted, Hiroshima lay in ruins, Tokyo permitted the Indonesian flag and anthem — and the draft proclamation was formulated at Admiral Maeda's house as the vacuum opened. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
