# Video script — The Road to the Proclamation, August 1945

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Japan's defeats mounted, Hiroshima lay in ruins, Tokyo permitted the Indonesian flag and anthem — and the draft proclamation was formulated at Admiral Maeda's house as the vacuum opened.

By August 1945 the occupation's master was losing the war. Japan's defeats mounted across the Pacific, and Hiroshima lay in ruins under the first atomic bomb. In Jakarta the signs turned: Tokyo now permitted what it had forbidden — the Indonesian flag could fly and the anthem be sung — concessions from an empire trying to leave friends behind. Then the surrender came, and a vacuum opened: Japan still held the arms, but its authority was finished, and the Dutch were not yet back. In that vacuum, in the house of Admiral Maeda, a Japanese officer who sheltered the nationalists, the draft proclamation was formulated — the short text that the next lesson reads aloud to the world.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Indonesia.
