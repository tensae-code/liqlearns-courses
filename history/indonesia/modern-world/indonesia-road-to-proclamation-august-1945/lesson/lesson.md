# The Road to the Proclamation, August 1945

## Do it first

Write a timetable of one decisive week, hour by hour, with two columns: what is collapsing, what is being built. That double column is this lesson.

By August 1945 the occupation's master was losing the war. Japan's defeats mounted across the Pacific, and Hiroshima lay in ruins under the first atomic bomb. In Jakarta the signs turned: Tokyo now permitted what it had forbidden — the Indonesian flag could fly and the anthem be sung — concessions from an empire trying to leave friends behind. Then the surrender came, and a vacuum opened: Japan still held the arms, but its authority was finished, and the Dutch were not yet back. In that vacuum, in the house of Admiral Maeda, a Japanese officer who sheltered the nationalists, the draft proclamation was formulated — the short text that the next lesson reads aloud to the world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Japan's defeats mounted, Hiroshima lay in ruins, Tokyo permitted the Indonesian flag and anthem — and the draft proclamation was formulated at Admiral Maeda's house as the vacuum opened. Give one real example: An empire was collapsing and a nation was being drafted in an admiral's dining room — August 1945 is the week the vacuum opened and Indonesians stepped into it. and name the lesson's key words: Vacuum, Maeda. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
