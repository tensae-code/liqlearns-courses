# Hands-on — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: From the 16th to the 19th centuries, ships sailed a triangle — European goods to Africa, kidnapped and purchased Africans carried across the perilous Middle Passage, and raw materials back to Europe. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Atlantic slave trade, triangle, Middle Passage, Africa, Europe, raw materials, 16th to 19th centuries.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
