# Video script — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From the 16th to the 19th centuries, ships sailed a triangle — European goods to Africa, kidnapped and purchased Africans carried across the perilous Middle Passage, and raw materials back to Europe.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

For three centuries, from the 1500s to the 1800s, Atlantic ships sailed the same grim triangle. First leg: European goods carried to Africa. Second leg — the Middle Passage: Africans, kidnapped and purchased, carried across the ocean in peril, to be enslaved in the Americas. Third leg: raw materials from the plantations carried back to Europe, and the triangle began again. The Bahamas' own slavery story, told in the next lessons, is one shore of this vast trade.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
