# About the History — Bahamas course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Social Science and Primary Social Studies books of the
Ministry of Education, Technical and Vocational Training, The
Bahamas. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for The Bahamas inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **four levels**, derived from the
progression of The Bahamas' own books as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps and First Peoples.** History
   begins as heritage: the timeline itself, the culture handed
   down (Junkanoo, the Brer and Anansi tales, sisal and straw,
   bush medicine, island festivals), surnames as evidence —
   then the first peoples: the Lucayans, and the Kongo Kingdom
   named as the people's African root.
2. **Level 2 — Arrival, Slavery and the Pirate Islands.**
   Columbus 1492 and the Lucayans' catastrophe; the slave
   trade's triangle; settlers and enslaved Africans; the Lord
   Proprietors 1629–1718; pirates, wreckers and Woodes Rogers;
   and the Loyalists and the plantation islands.
3. **Level 3 — Freedom, Booms and War Work.** Emancipation
   and the freed villages; the churches founded by the freed;
   Lincoln's blockade; Nassau's blockade-running and
   bootlegging booms; and The Contract's wartime workers.
4. **Level 4 — Majority Rule, Independence and the Nation
   Today.** The dated road 1942–1973; independence on 10 July
   1973 with its symbols, constitution, leaders and nation
   builders; tourism under Sir Stafford Sands; and Hurricane
   Dorian, 2019.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From the first people to the free nation:* "Start with what
  is handed down — Junkanoo, the tales, the remedies — and with
  the Lucayans, the first people of these islands. Then walk
  the hard road: Columbus, the slave ships, the settlers, the
  absentee owners, the pirates and the wreckers, the Loyalists
  and their plantations — until freedom comes to Adelaide and
  Fox Hill, Nassau grows rich twice on America's bans, and the
  long road through Burma Road and Black Tuesday reaches 10
  July 1973, when the flag of a sovereign Bahamas first rose."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

The Bahamas' books tell the national story as one continuous
people's story: Lucayans first, then Columbus, then English
settlers and enslaved Africans, then the freedom holidays —
Emancipation, Majority Rule, Independence — and the named
nation builders, with African-carried traditions (Junkanoo,
asue, Anansi and Brer tales) as the living thread that survives
slavery. Grade 5 turns to episodes — pirates, wreckers,
blockade runners and bootleggers as the booms that built the
islands — and Grade 6 finally tells the whole story as one
dated timeline, "Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973",
with the Majority Rule sequence as its spine. Even surnames
are taught as historical evidence of how each island was
settled. This course preserves that shape: the same story,
level by level.
