# History — Bahamas course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for The Bahamas inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** The Bahamas' books in hand teach the
past in the country's own progression: the Lower Primary books
(Grades 1 and 3) open with national identity and heritage — the
first leaders, Independence Day, Junkanoo and the tales handed
down — and tell the people's story in one sequence, Lucayans
first; the Grade 4 book runs the settler-and-nation sequence
(Lucayans, Adventurers, Loyalists, the enslaved, then leaders
and nation builders); the Grade 5 book turns to episodes —
pirates, wreckers, blockade runners and bootleggers, and
emancipation through the freed villages; and the Grade 6 book
tells the whole story as one dated timeline to 1973, with the
Majority Rule sequence as its spine. The ladder follows that
progression: heritage and first peoples first (Level 1), then
arrival, slavery and the pirate islands (Level 2), then freedom,
the booms and war work (Level 3), and last Majority Rule,
independence and the nation today (Level 4). The ladder is a
recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Social Science and Primary Social Studies books of the
Ministry of Education, Technical and Vocational Training, The
Bahamas — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list, the
combined-book contents map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps and First Peoples** — history begins as the child's own past and heritage — what a timeline is, the culture Bahamians hand down (Junkanoo, tales, crafts and bush medicine), surnames as evidence in the learner's own locality — then the islands' first peoples whole: the Lucayans, and the Kongo Kingdom the books name as the people's African root. 5 lessons, in titles: `first-steps/ (3 lessons), ancient-world/ (1 lesson), africa-to-1500/ (1 lesson)`.

2. **Arrival, Slavery and the Pirate Islands** — Columbus's 1492 landfall and the Lucayans' catastrophe, the Atlantic slave trade's triangle and Middle Passage, the English settlers and the enslaved Africans who reached the islands by many routes, the absentee Lord Proprietors (1629–1718), the pirates and wreckers and Woodes Rogers' clean-up, and the Loyalists who planted the plantation islands and built the colony's institutions. 6 lessons, in titles: `modern-world/ (5 lessons), africa-after-1500/ (1 lesson)`.

3. **Freedom, Booms and War Work** — emancipation and the freed villages and the long inequality after freedom, the churches the freed founded, America's Civil War blockade, the two booms America handed Nassau — blockade running and bootlegging — and The Contract that took Bahamian workers to wartime America. 5 lessons, in titles: `modern-world/ (5 lessons)`.

4. **Majority Rule, Independence and the Nation Today** — the dated road — Burma Road 1942, the PLP 1953, women's suffrage 1962, Black Tuesday 1965, Majority Rule 1967 — landing on independence on 10 July 1973, its symbols, constitution, leaders and nation builders; then the nation the books show today: tourism made year-round by Sir Stafford Sands, and Hurricane Dorian, 2019. 4 lessons, in titles: `modern-world/ (4 lessons)`.

**Totals: 20 lessons** (Level 1: 5, Level 2: 6, Level 3: 5,
Level 4: 4) — 70 placements stocked, all on student books.
