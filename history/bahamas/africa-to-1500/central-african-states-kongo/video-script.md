# Video script — Central African States: Kongo

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Today's Bahamian is said to descend from the Kongo Kingdom south of the Kongo River, and the two cultures still share one understanding — people relate to one another, children respect elders, and strangers receive unwavering hospitality.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

The Bahamian books trace the people's roots across the Atlantic. Today's Bahamian, they teach, descends from the Kongo Kingdom, the great state south of the Kongo River. And the descent is not only in blood: the two cultures still share one understanding of life — people relate to one another, children respect their elders, and a stranger at the door receives unwavering hospitality. An African kingdom's way of living crossed the ocean and is still recognisable in Bahamian homes.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
