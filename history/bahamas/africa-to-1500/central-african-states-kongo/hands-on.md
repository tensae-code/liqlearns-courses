# Hands-on — Central African States: Kongo

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Today's Bahamian is said to descend from the Kongo Kingdom south of the Kongo River, and the two cultures still share one understanding — people relate to one another, children respect elders, and strangers receive unwavering hospitality. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Kongo Kingdom, Kongo River, descent, respect for elders, hospitality.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
