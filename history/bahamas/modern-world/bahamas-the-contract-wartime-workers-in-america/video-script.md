# Video script — Bahamas: The Contract — Wartime Workers in America

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In the Second World War (1939–1945), American farms and factories emptied as men left for the troops, and under 'The Contract' Bahamian men and women left home to work those farms and factories and send their earnings back to their families.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

When the Second World War came (1939–1945), America's farms and factories emptied: their men had left for the troops, and the work still had to be done. The answer crossed the water. Under an agreement remembered simply as The Contract, Bahamian men and women left home to work those American farms and factories — and from their wages they sent earnings back to the families waiting in the islands. The Contract tied Bahamian households to the war's work, and its money home.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
