# Hands-on — Bahamas: The Contract — Wartime Workers in America

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: In the Second World War (1939–1945), American farms and factories emptied as men left for the troops, and under 'The Contract' Bahamian men and women left home to work those farms and factories and send their earnings back to their families. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: The Contract, Second World War, 1939-1945, farms, factories, earnings, families.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
