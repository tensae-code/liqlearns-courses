# Hands-on — Bahamas: Loyalists and the Plantation Islands

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: When America won independence in the 1780s, Loyalists who had stayed loyal to the English king fled to still-British Bahamas, planted cotton with enslaved labour, raked salt in the off-season, and built the colony's institutions — Christ Church Cathedral, schools, law and order, and John Wells's first newspaper. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Loyalists, American independence, cotton, enslaved labour, salt, Christ Church Cathedral, St Matthew's, John Wells, newspaper, Gazette.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
