# Bahamas: Loyalists and the Plantation Islands

## Do it first

Before any explanation, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already know or can guess about this — Bahamas: Loyalists and the Plantation Islands. One sentence is enough. This lesson will give you the full story: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Loyalists: the king's men who remade the islands

In the 1780s the American colonies won their independence — and the people who had stayed loyal to the English king had to go somewhere. They fled to the still-British Bahamas, and remade the islands: they planted cotton with enslaved labour, raked salt in the off-season, and built lasting things — Christ Church Cathedral and St. Matthew's church, the first schools, and a newspaper, started by John Wells.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold this lesson as a story with a turn — ask at each part: who is here, where are we, and what just changed?

## Sub lesson 2 — Early Connections: the Loyalists build the colony's institutions

The same building work made the colony's institutions: Loyalists from the United States built Christ Church Cathedral, established the first newspaper, the Gazette, and organised law and order in the community. A refugee wave became the colony's foundation layer.
