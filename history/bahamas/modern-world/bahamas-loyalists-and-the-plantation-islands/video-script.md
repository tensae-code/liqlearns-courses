# Video script — Bahamas: Loyalists and the Plantation Islands

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
When America won independence in the 1780s, Loyalists who had stayed loyal to the English king fled to still-British Bahamas, planted cotton with enslaved labour, raked salt in the off-season, and built the colony's institutions — Christ Church Cathedral, schools, law and order, and John Wells's first newspaper.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

In the 1780s the American colonies won their independence — and the people who had stayed loyal to the English king had to go somewhere. They fled to the still-British Bahamas, and remade the islands: they planted cotton with enslaved labour, raked salt in the off-season, and built lasting things — Christ Church Cathedral and St. Matthew's church, the first schools, and a newspaper, started by John Wells.

The same building work made the colony's institutions: Loyalists from the United States built Christ Church Cathedral, established the first newspaper, the Gazette, and organised law and order in the community. A refugee wave became the colony's foundation layer.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
