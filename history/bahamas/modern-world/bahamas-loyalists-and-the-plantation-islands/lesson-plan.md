# Lesson plan — Bahamas: Loyalists and the Plantation Islands

**Course:** History — Bahamas · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas: Loyalists and the Plantation Islands
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Arrival, Slavery and the Pirate Islands (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** When America won independence in the 1780s, Loyalists who had stayed loyal to the English king fled to still-British Bahamas, planted cotton with enslaved labour, raked salt in the off-season, and built the colony's institutions — Christ Church Cathedral, schools, law and order, and John Wells's first newspaper.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Arrival, Slavery and the Pirate Islands (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Loyalists were explorers → they were refugees of a revolution: loyal to the king, they fled America's independence and rebuilt their lives — and the colony — in The Bahamas.
- Go back to the one thing: When America won independence in the 1780s, Loyalists who had stayed loyal to the English king fled to still-British Bahamas, planted cotton with enslaved labour, raked salt in the off-season, and built the colony's institutions — Christ Church Cathedral, schools, law and order, and John Wells's first newspaper. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
