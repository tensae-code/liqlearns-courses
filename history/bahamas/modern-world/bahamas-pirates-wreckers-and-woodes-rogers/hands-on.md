# Hands-on — Bahamas: Pirates, Wreckers and Woodes Rogers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Pirates — Blackbeard, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, Calico Jack, Stede Bonnet, Henry Morgan and Charles Vane — infested The Bahamas, and wreckers lured ships onto reefs to strip them, until Woodes Rogers, first Royal Governor, pardoned some pirates and hanged the rest, cleared the islands out, and under him the first parliament met in 1729. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: pirates, Blackbeard, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, Calico Jack, Stede Bonnet, Henry Morgan, Charles Vane, wrecking, false lights, Woodes Rogers, parliament 1729.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
