# Bahamas: Pirates, Wreckers and Woodes Rogers

## Do it first

Before any explanation, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already know or can guess about this — Bahamas: Pirates, Wreckers and Woodes Rogers. One sentence is enough. This lesson will give you the full story: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pirates and Buccaneers: from boucan fires to Blackbeard

The pirate story begins with fire: shipwrecked English, French and Dutch hunters on Hispaniola cured meat over a slow fire called a boucan — until Spaniards chased them from the land to the sea, and the hunters became pirates. From there the Bahamas filled with famous names: Blackbeard, Anne Bonny, Mary Read, Calico Jack, Stede Bonnet, Henry Morgan and Charles Vane infested the islands — until Woodes Rogers pardoned those who surrendered, and hanged the rest.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold this lesson as a story with a turn — ask at each part: who is here, where are we, and what just changed?

## Sub lesson 2 — Wrecking: the false-light trade

A quieter, shadier trade waited on shore. From the mid-1600s wrecking was big business: wreckers lured ships onto cays and reefs — sometimes, it is said, with false lights — then stripped the goods and sold them. Many Bahamians made their living from the wrecks the reefs provided.

## Sub lesson 3 — Early Connections: pirates, Woodes Rogers and the first parliament

Give the clean-up its full weight. Pirates seized the islands in the 1600s and ruined their reputation, until Woodes Rogers, the first Royal Governor, cleared them out. Under his government the islands organised their first parliament in 1729 — John Colebrook its first Speaker, with 24 members. Order, at last, had an address.
