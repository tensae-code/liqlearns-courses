# Hands-on — Bahamas: English Settlers and Enslaved Africans

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: English settlers reached Eleuthera in 1648 and brought enslaved Africans to work plantations; Africans reached The Bahamas by many routes — direct from Africa, with the Loyalists, freed and settling, or freed by shipwreck — and Africans freed on the way to Cuba chose the islands, carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer stories with them. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Eleuthera, 1648, Eleutheran Adventurers, Captain Sayle, New Providence, enslaved Africans, freed Africans, Junkanoo, asue.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
