# Lesson plan — Bahamas: English Settlers and Enslaved Africans

**Course:** History — Bahamas · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas: English Settlers and Enslaved Africans
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Arrival, Slavery and the Pirate Islands (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** English settlers reached Eleuthera in 1648 and brought enslaved Africans to work plantations; Africans reached The Bahamas by many routes — direct from Africa, with the Loyalists, freed and settling, or freed by shipwreck — and Africans freed on the way to Cuba chose the islands, carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer stories with them.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Arrival, Slavery and the Pirate Islands (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bahamian History: Eleuthera settlers, enslaved Africans and the freed Africans who chose The Bahamas**
2. **Lucayans and Eleutheran Adventurers: the Adventurers settle Eleuthera and New Providence**
3. **Slavery: how enslaved and free Africans reached The Bahamas**
4. **Early Settlers: Adventurers, Loyalists and the enslaved**

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks enslaved Africans arrived by one route only → the books name several: direct from Africa, with the Loyalists from America, freed in America and settling, or freed by shipwreck.
- Go back to the one thing: English settlers reached Eleuthera in 1648 and brought enslaved Africans to work plantations; Africans reached The Bahamas by many routes — direct from Africa, with the Loyalists, freed and settling, or freed by shipwreck — and Africans freed on the way to Cuba chose the islands, carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer stories with them. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
