# Video script — Bahamas: Lord Proprietors, 1629 to 1718

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In 1629 Charles I granted The Bahamas and the Carolinas to Sir Robert Heath, and in 1670 Charles II handed the islands to the Lords Proprietors of Carolina — owners who wanted profit, showed little interest, and were refused their rents, until their rule ended in 1718 with Woodes Rogers.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

The islands were first ruled as property. In 1629 Charles I granted The Bahamas and the Carolinas to Sir Robert Heath. The Adventurers of that era even donated money to help build Harvard College across the ocean. Then in 1670 Charles II handed the islands to a new set of owners, the Lords Proprietors of Carolina. The Proprietors wanted profit from their distant property but showed little interest in it; the islanders refused to pay their rents. Absentee rule rotted away, and in 1718 it ended, when Woodes Rogers arrived to govern in earnest.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
