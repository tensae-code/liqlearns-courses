# Hands-on — Bahamas: Lord Proprietors, 1629 to 1718

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: In 1629 Charles I granted The Bahamas and the Carolinas to Sir Robert Heath, and in 1670 Charles II handed the islands to the Lords Proprietors of Carolina — owners who wanted profit, showed little interest, and were refused their rents, until their rule ended in 1718 with Woodes Rogers. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Lord Proprietors, Charles I, Sir Robert Heath, 1629, Charles II, 1670, Carolina, rents, Woodes Rogers, 1718, Harvard College.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
