# Lesson plan — Bahamas: Blockade Running and Bootlegging Booms

**Course:** History — Bahamas · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas: Blockade Running and Bootlegging Booms
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Freedom, Booms and War Work (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Twice, America's bans made Nassau rich: in 1861–1865 Nassau became the Confederacy's back door to Europe as blockade runners flourished, and from 1920 to 1933 America's liquor ban made Bahamian bootleggers rich — money that improved rich homes and tourist infrastructure while Over-the-Hill communities benefited far less.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Freedom, Booms and War Work (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Merges the two booms into one → separate them: blockade running is the 1860s Civil War trade in cotton and goods; bootlegging is the 1920–1933 liquor trade under Prohibition.
- Go back to the one thing: Twice, America's bans made Nassau rich: in 1861–1865 Nassau became the Confederacy's back door to Europe as blockade runners flourished, and from 1920 to 1933 America's liquor ban made Bahamian bootleggers rich — money that improved rich homes and tourist infrastructure while Over-the-Hill communities benefited far less. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
