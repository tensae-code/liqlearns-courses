# Hands-on — Bahamas: Blockade Running and Bootlegging Booms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Twice, America's bans made Nassau rich: in 1861–1865 Nassau became the Confederacy's back door to Europe as blockade runners flourished, and from 1920 to 1933 America's liquor ban made Bahamian bootleggers rich — money that improved rich homes and tourist infrastructure while Over-the-Hill communities benefited far less. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: blockade running, Nassau, Confederacy, bootlegging, Prohibition, 1920, 1933, Over-the-Hill, rum-running.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
