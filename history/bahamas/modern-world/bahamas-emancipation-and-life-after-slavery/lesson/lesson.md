# Bahamas: Emancipation and Life After Slavery

## Do it first

Before any explanation, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already know or can guess about this — Bahamas: Emancipation and Life After Slavery. One sentence is enough. This lesson will give you the full story: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Africans Freeds: emancipation and the freed villages

Freedom came in law in 1834 — and its news reached Bahamian slaves in 1835. What did freed people do first? They built. Settlements rose at Adelaide, Gambier and Fox Hill; some freed families won land from their former owners. But the old order held the heights: whites controlled business, voting required property, and schools stopped at the elementary level. So a door was forced open: Government High School was founded to give Black Bahamians the schooling beyond it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold this lesson as a story with a turn — ask at each part: who is here, where are we, and what just changed?

## Sub lesson 2 — Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 2: the slave-built communities

The same villages stand as the enslaved people's own foundation: the enslaved who worked and lived in The Bahamas established some of its oldest communities — Adelaide, Fox Hill and Bain Town — and freed slaves from America, too, chose these islands as their new home. The villages are emancipation written on the land.
