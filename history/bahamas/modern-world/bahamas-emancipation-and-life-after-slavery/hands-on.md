# Hands-on — Bahamas: Emancipation and Life After Slavery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Emancipation freed the enslaved — its news reached Bahamian slaves in 1835 — and freed people built settlements at Adelaide, Gambier and Fox Hill (and Bain Town and Adelaide among the slave-built communities); some won land from former owners, but whites controlled business, the vote needed property and schools stopped at elementary level, so Government High School was founded to give Black Bahamians more. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: emancipation, 1834, 1835, Adelaide, Gambier, Fox Hill, Bain Town, Government High School, vote, property.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
