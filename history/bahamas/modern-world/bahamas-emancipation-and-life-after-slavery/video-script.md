# Video script — Bahamas: Emancipation and Life After Slavery

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Emancipation freed the enslaved — its news reached Bahamian slaves in 1835 — and freed people built settlements at Adelaide, Gambier and Fox Hill (and Bain Town and Adelaide among the slave-built communities); some won land from former owners, but whites controlled business, the vote needed property and schools stopped at elementary level, so Government High School was founded to give Black Bahamians more.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

Freedom came in law in 1834 — and its news reached Bahamian slaves in 1835. What did freed people do first? They built. Settlements rose at Adelaide, Gambier and Fox Hill; some freed families won land from their former owners. But the old order held the heights: whites controlled business, voting required property, and schools stopped at the elementary level. So a door was forced open: Government High School was founded to give Black Bahamians the schooling beyond it.

The same villages stand as the enslaved people's own foundation: the enslaved who worked and lived in The Bahamas established some of its oldest communities — Adelaide, Fox Hill and Bain Town — and freed slaves from America, too, chose these islands as their new home. The villages are emancipation written on the land.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
