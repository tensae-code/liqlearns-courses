# Lesson plan — Bahamas: Emancipation and Life After Slavery

**Course:** History — Bahamas · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas: Emancipation and Life After Slavery
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Freedom, Booms and War Work (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Emancipation freed the enslaved — its news reached Bahamian slaves in 1835 — and freed people built settlements at Adelaide, Gambier and Fox Hill (and Bain Town and Adelaide among the slave-built communities); some won land from former owners, but whites controlled business, the vote needed property and schools stopped at elementary level, so Government High School was founded to give Black Bahamians more.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Freedom, Booms and War Work (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks emancipation made everything equal at once → the books are plain: freedom came, villages rose, but business, the property vote and schooling stayed unequal — which is why Government High School mattered.
- Go back to the one thing: Emancipation freed the enslaved — its news reached Bahamian slaves in 1835 — and freed people built settlements at Adelaide, Gambier and Fox Hill (and Bain Town and Adelaide among the slave-built communities); some won land from former owners, but whites controlled business, the vote needed property and schools stopped at elementary level, so Government High School was founded to give Black Bahamians more. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
