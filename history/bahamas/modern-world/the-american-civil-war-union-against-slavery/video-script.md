# Video script — The American Civil War: Union Against Slavery

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
President Abraham Lincoln blockaded the southern ports in America's Civil War (1861–1865): the plantation states had to export cotton to Europe and import guns they could not make, or lose the war against the North.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

North of The Bahamas, the United States tore itself in two. In the Civil War of 1861–1865, President Abraham Lincoln ordered a blockade of the southern ports: Union ships would seal the Confederacy's coast. The stakes were plain. The plantation states lived by exporting cotton to Europe — and they could not make the guns they needed; guns had to come in by sea. Seal the ports, and the South could neither sell its cotton nor arm itself, and would lose the war against the North. Everything in the next Bahamian lesson flows from that sealed coast.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
