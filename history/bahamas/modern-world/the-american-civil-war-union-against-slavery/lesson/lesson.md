# The American Civil War: Union Against Slavery

## Do it first

Before any explanation, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already know or can guess about this — The American Civil War: Union Against Slavery. One sentence is enough. This lesson will give you the full story: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## The story

North of The Bahamas, the United States tore itself in two. In the Civil War of 1861–1865, President Abraham Lincoln ordered a blockade of the southern ports: Union ships would seal the Confederacy's coast. The stakes were plain. The plantation states lived by exporting cotton to Europe — and they could not make the guns they needed; guns had to come in by sea. Seal the ports, and the South could neither sell its cotton nor arm itself, and would lose the war against the North. Everything in the next Bahamian lesson flows from that sealed coast.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold this lesson as a story with a turn — ask: who is here, where are we, and what changed?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: President Abraham Lincoln blockaded the southern ports in America's Civil War (1861–1865): the plantation states had to export cotton to Europe and import guns they could not make, or lose the war against the North. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: American Civil War, Lincoln, blockade, Confederacy, cotton, guns, North, South, 1861-1865. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
