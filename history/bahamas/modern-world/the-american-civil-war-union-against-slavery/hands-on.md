# Hands-on — The American Civil War: Union Against Slavery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: President Abraham Lincoln blockaded the southern ports in America's Civil War (1861–1865): the plantation states had to export cotton to Europe and import guns they could not make, or lose the war against the North. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: American Civil War, Lincoln, blockade, Confederacy, cotton, guns, North, South, 1861-1865.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
