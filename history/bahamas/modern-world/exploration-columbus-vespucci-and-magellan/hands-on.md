# Hands-on — Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: In 1492 Columbus landed in The Bahamas — three ships, a flag planted on a Bahamian shore — and the meeting of the old world and the new turned the Lucayans' world upside down: many were killed or enslaved, and soon very few Lucayans were left. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Columbus, 1492, landfall, San Salvador, flag, Lucayans, old world, new world.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
