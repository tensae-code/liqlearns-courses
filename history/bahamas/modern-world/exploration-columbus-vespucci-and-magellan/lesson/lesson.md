# Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

## Do it first

Before any explanation, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already know or can guess about this — Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan. One sentence is enough. This lesson will give you the full story: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bahamian History: Columbus lands in The Bahamas, 1492

In 1492 Columbus's ships reached The Bahamas — and for the Lucayans, the world turned upside down. The newcomers killed many and enslaved many; disease and violence did the rest, and soon very few Lucayans were left on the islands they had filled. The landfall that Europe celebrates is, in the Bahamian telling, first of all a Lucayan tragedy.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold this lesson as a story with a turn — ask at each part: who is here, where are we, and what just changed?

## Sub lesson 2 — Early Settlers: Columbus plants the flag, 1492

Picture the moment itself: three large ships land in 1492, and Columbus plants a long pole with a flag on the beach. With that act, the meeting of the old world and the new begins — and it begins on a Bahamian shore.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In 1492 Columbus landed in The Bahamas — three ships, a flag planted on a Bahamian shore — and the meeting of the old world and the new turned the Lucayans' world upside down: many were killed or enslaved, and soon very few Lucayans were left. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Columbus, 1492, landfall, San Salvador, flag, Lucayans, old world, new world. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
