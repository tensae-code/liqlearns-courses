# Video script — Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In 1492 Columbus landed in The Bahamas — three ships, a flag planted on a Bahamian shore — and the meeting of the old world and the new turned the Lucayans' world upside down: many were killed or enslaved, and soon very few Lucayans were left.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

In 1492 Columbus's ships reached The Bahamas — and for the Lucayans, the world turned upside down. The newcomers killed many and enslaved many; disease and violence did the rest, and soon very few Lucayans were left on the islands they had filled. The landfall that Europe celebrates is, in the Bahamian telling, first of all a Lucayan tragedy.

Picture the moment itself: three large ships land in 1492, and Columbus plants a long pole with a flag on the beach. With that act, the meeting of the old world and the new begins — and it begins on a Bahamian shore.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
