# Hands-on — Bahamas: Independence 1973

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: On 10 July 1973 The Bahamas stopped being ruled by Britain and became a sovereign nation — with its own flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms, a 1972 constitution as the basis of its law, Sir Milo Butler as first Governor General and Sir Lynden Pindling as first Prime Minister, and a roll of nation builders, Governors General and Prime Ministers who have led it since. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: independence, 10 July 1973, Sir Lynden Pindling, Sir Milo Butler, flag, anthem, March on Bahamaland, pledge, coat of arms, constitution 1972, Governors General, Prime Ministers, nation builders, Majority Rule Day, National Heroes Day.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
