# Lesson plan — Bahamas: Independence 1973

**Course:** History — Bahamas · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas: Independence 1973
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Majority Rule, Independence and the Nation Today (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** On 10 July 1973 The Bahamas stopped being ruled by Britain and became a sovereign nation — with its own flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms, a 1972 constitution as the basis of its law, Sir Milo Butler as first Governor General and Sir Lynden Pindling as first Prime Minister, and a roll of nation builders, Governors General and Prime Ministers who have led it since.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Majority Rule, Independence and the Nation Today (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Shrinks independence to one ceremony → the lesson is the whole settlement: the day itself, the symbols, the constitution, the leaders who followed, the holidays that remember, and the builders in every field.
- Go back to the one thing: On 10 July 1973 The Bahamas stopped being ruled by Britain and became a sovereign nation — with its own flag, anthem, pledge and coat of arms, a 1972 constitution as the basis of its law, Sir Milo Butler as first Governor General and Sir Lynden Pindling as first Prime Minister, and a roll of nation builders, Governors General and Prime Ministers who have led it since. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
