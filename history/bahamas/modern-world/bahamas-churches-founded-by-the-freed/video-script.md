# Video script — Bahamas: Churches Founded by the Freed

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Prince William, a freed slave, founded the Baptist church in The Bahamas, and freed Black people broke ground for Bethel Baptist on 1 August 1790; St Matthew's (1800–1802) and St Andrew's Kirk (1798–1810) rose nearby, and centuries later Rastafarianism grew after a Bob Marley concert in the late 1970s and Islam arrived in the early 1970s.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

Among the first things freed people built were churches of their own. Prince William, himself a freed slave, founded the Baptist church in The Bahamas, and on 1 August 1790 freed Black people broke the ground for Bethel Baptist. The older churches rose around them in the same years: St Matthew's in 1800–1802 and the Scottish St Andrew's Kirk in 1798–1810. The story does not stop there: centuries later, Rastafarianism grew in the islands after a Bob Marley concert in the late 1970s, and Islam arrived in the early 1970s — faith in The Bahamas keeps arriving and taking root.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
