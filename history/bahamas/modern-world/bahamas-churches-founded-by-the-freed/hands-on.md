# Hands-on — Bahamas: Churches Founded by the Freed

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Prince William, a freed slave, founded the Baptist church in The Bahamas, and freed Black people broke ground for Bethel Baptist on 1 August 1790; St Matthew's (1800–1802) and St Andrew's Kirk (1798–1810) rose nearby, and centuries later Rastafarianism grew after a Bob Marley concert in the late 1970s and Islam arrived in the early 1970s. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Prince William, Baptist, Bethel Baptist, 1 August 1790, St Matthew's, St Andrew's Kirk, Rastafarianism, Bob Marley, Islam.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
