# Video script — Bahamas: Tourism and Sir Stafford Sands

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Tourism grew from invalids seeking health and rich winter visitors at the Royal Victoria Hotel (1861) and Colonial Hotel (1900) into a year-round industry in the 1950s under Sir Stafford Sands, 'Father of Tourism', who headed the Development Board, developed Freeport with Wallace Groves, and pushed air conditioning, advertising, tax incentives, better air travel and legalised casinos, until research and training made tourism the number one industry by 1969–1979.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

Rich winter visitors once came only for the season, and went home when it ended. In the 1950s Sir Stafford Sands — remembered as the Father of Tourism — headed the Development Board and set out to change that: he developed Freeport with Wallace Groves, and pushed air conditioning, advertising, tax incentives, better air travel and legalised casinos. The work compounded: by 1969–1979, research and training had made tourism the number one industry of The Bahamas.

Start further back. The first tourists were invalids, seeking health in the sun; the Royal Victoria Hotel (1861) and the Colonial Hotel (1900) drew the rich winter visitors who followed. Even bootlegging helped the hotels before it helped the people: customs money from the liquor years dredged the harbour and brought water and electricity — for tourists — pointedly — not for ordinary Bahamians. Then, in the 1950s, Sir Stafford Sands made tourism a year-round business.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
