# Hands-on — Bahamas: Tourism and Sir Stafford Sands

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Tourism grew from invalids seeking health and rich winter visitors at the Royal Victoria Hotel (1861) and Colonial Hotel (1900) into a year-round industry in the 1950s under Sir Stafford Sands, 'Father of Tourism', who headed the Development Board, developed Freeport with Wallace Groves, and pushed air conditioning, advertising, tax incentives, better air travel and legalised casinos, until research and training made tourism the number one industry by 1969–1979. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: tourism, Sir Stafford Sands, Father of Tourism, Development Board, Freeport, Wallace Groves, Royal Victoria Hotel, Colonial Hotel, casinos.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
