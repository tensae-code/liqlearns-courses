# Bahamas: Tourism and Sir Stafford Sands

## Do it first

Before any explanation, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already know or can guess about this — Bahamas: Tourism and Sir Stafford Sands. One sentence is enough. This lesson will give you the full story: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tourism: Sir Stafford Sands builds a year-round industry

Rich winter visitors once came only for the season, and went home when it ended. In the 1950s Sir Stafford Sands — remembered as the Father of Tourism — headed the Development Board and set out to change that: he developed Freeport with Wallace Groves, and pushed air conditioning, advertising, tax incentives, better air travel and legalised casinos. The work compounded: by 1969–1979, research and training had made tourism the number one industry of The Bahamas.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold this lesson as a story with a turn — ask at each part: who is here, where are we, and what just changed?

## Sub lesson 2 — Tourism: from invalids to a year-round industry

Start further back. The first tourists were invalids, seeking health in the sun; the Royal Victoria Hotel (1861) and the Colonial Hotel (1900) drew the rich winter visitors who followed. Even bootlegging helped the hotels before it helped the people: customs money from the liquor years dredged the harbour and brought water and electricity — for tourists — pointedly — not for ordinary Bahamians. Then, in the 1950s, Sir Stafford Sands made tourism a year-round business.
