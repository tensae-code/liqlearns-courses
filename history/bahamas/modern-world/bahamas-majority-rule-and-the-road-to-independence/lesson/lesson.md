# Bahamas: Majority Rule and the Road to Independence

## Do it first

Before any explanation, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already know or can guess about this — Bahamas: Majority Rule and the Road to Independence. One sentence is enough. This lesson will give you the full story: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Majority Rule – Independence Part 1: the Burma Road Riot, 1942

The road begins with pay. In 1942 wages varied by sex and race — four shillings for men, two for women — and Bay Street merchants forced an American company building at Oakes Field to keep Bahamian wages unequal. The workers answered: they marched from Oakes Field to Bay Street, the Riot Act was read over them — and five days later they returned with a wage increase won. The Burma Road Riot proved protest could move the colony. Eleven years later, in 1953, the PLP was founded: the first political party to represent the Black majority.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold this lesson as a story with a turn — ask at each part: who is here, where are we, and what just changed?

## Sub lesson 2 — Majority Rule – Independence Part 2: suffrage, Black Tuesday, independence

The second telling follows the vote. Mary Ingraham and Georgianna Symonette led the Women's Suffrage Movement to victory in 1962, with Ruby Ann Cooper Darling the first woman to register. Then came Black Tuesday — 27 April 1965 — when Pindling threw the Speaker's mace out of the House window to the crowd below, and Butler followed with the hourglass: the House's power, the act declared, lies with the people. In 1969 the Premier became Prime Minister in title, and in 1973 independence arrived, Butler as Governor General and Pindling as Prime Minister.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 2: Burma Road on the timeline

Put the riot where it belongs on the line — first. On the road-to-independence line, the June 1942 Burma Road Riot is the first dated step: unequal pay by sex, race and nationality, the new Federation of Labour, the march on Bay Street, the Riot Act read — and a wage increase five days later. The road to 1973 starts on that road in 1942.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bahamian History Prior to Independence 1973 Part 3: 1953 to 1973, the new political era

The last stretch runs fast. From 1953 the PLP demanded one man one vote; in 1962 women won the vote; Black Tuesday in 1965 showed where the House's power truly lay; and in 1967 the PLP won the election — Majority Rule — and Pindling became the first Black Premier. The Bahamas was still a colony. Only in 1973 did independence make him Prime Minister of a sovereign nation.
