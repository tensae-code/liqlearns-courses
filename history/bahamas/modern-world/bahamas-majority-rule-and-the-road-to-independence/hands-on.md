# Hands-on — Bahamas: Majority Rule and the Road to Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: The road ran through protest to power: the Burma Road Riot of June 1942 won a wage increase in five days; the PLP was founded in 1953, the first party of the Black majority; women won the vote in 1962; on Black Tuesday, 27 April 1965, Pindling threw the Speaker's mace out of the House window; in 1967 the PLP won and Pindling became the first Black Premier, and in 1973 independence made him Prime Minister. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Burma Road Riot, 1942, PLP, 1953, women's suffrage, 1962, Mary Ingraham, Georgianna Symonette, Ruby Ann Cooper Darling, Black Tuesday, 1965, mace, Majority Rule, 1967, Pindling.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
