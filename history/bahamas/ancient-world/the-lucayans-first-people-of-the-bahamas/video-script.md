# Video script — The Lucayans: First People of The Bahamas

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Lucayans, a peaceful Arawak-speaking people who sailed from South America, were the first people of the islands — fishing, farming cassava, sweet potato and corn, living in huts, sleeping in hammocks, and leaving the hammock as a legacy the whole world still uses.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

Long before any European ship, a peaceful Arawak-speaking people sailed north from South America and made these islands home. The Lucayans settled by the sea, fished its waters and farmed cassava, sweet potato and corn. They lived in huts of branches and grass — and at night they slept in hammocks, slung above the ground.

Look at what the Lucayans left behind. Skilled sailors and fishermen, they lived in huts, slept in hammocks and lived off the land — and one of their gifts outlived them entirely: the hammock, a Lucayan legacy the whole world still uses today.

The Grade 5 telling calls them the first people to come to the islands: peaceful sailors and fishermen who lived in huts, slept in hammocks and lived off the land in harmony with nature. Before settlers, before ships from Europe, there was already a complete Bahamian way of life.

In the Grade 6 timeline the Lucayans stand at the head of a long line of peoples. After Columbus, group after group would find refuge on the islands — Adventurers, pirates, Loyalists, the enslaved — each changing the islands for better or worse, until an organised society under Woodes Rogers began the long road of ideas toward independence. The line starts here, with the first people.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
