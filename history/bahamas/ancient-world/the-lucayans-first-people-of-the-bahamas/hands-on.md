# Hands-on — The Lucayans: First People of The Bahamas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: The Lucayans, a peaceful Arawak-speaking people who sailed from South America, were the first people of the islands — fishing, farming cassava, sweet potato and corn, living in huts, sleeping in hammocks, and leaving the hammock as a legacy the whole world still uses. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: Lucayans, Arawak, South America, cassava, sweet potato, corn, hammock, huts.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
