# Lesson plan — The Lucayans: First People of The Bahamas

**Course:** History — Bahamas · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Lucayans: First People of The Bahamas
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Lucayans, a peaceful Arawak-speaking people who sailed from South America, were the first people of the islands — fishing, farming cassava, sweet potato and corn, living in huts, sleeping in hammocks, and leaving the hammock as a legacy the whole world still uses.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and First Peoples (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in The Bahamas, as the books themselves work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson told properly (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a Lucayan village by the sea, with branch-and-grass huts, a canoe on the shore and people fishing — the student points to the hut, the canoe and the hammock and says what each shows about Lucayan life
   - ADD GIF: a short GIF of a hammock swinging gently between two posts by a seaside hut — the student watches it swing and says which people gave the world the hammock

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the islands were empty before Columbus → the Lucayans were already there: settled, farming and fishing communities with their own language, homes and legacy.
- Go back to the one thing: The Lucayans, a peaceful Arawak-speaking people who sailed from South America, were the first people of the islands — fishing, farming cassava, sweet potato and corn, living in huts, sleeping in hammocks, and leaving the hammock as a legacy the whole world still uses. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
