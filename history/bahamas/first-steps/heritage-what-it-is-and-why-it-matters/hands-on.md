# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Culture is what a people hands down — stories, games, Junkanoo, crafts, bush medicine and festivals — and history is one of its main parts: it tells Bahamians where they have come from and where they are going. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: heritage, culture, Junkanoo, ring plays, Anansi, Brer Bookie, Brer Rabbie, bush medicine, sisal, straw, festivals, John Canoe.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
