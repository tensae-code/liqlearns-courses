# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

**Course:** History — Bahamas · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Culture is what a people hands down — stories, games, Junkanoo, crafts, bush medicine and festivals — and history is one of its main parts: it tells Bahamians where they have come from and where they are going.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and First Peoples (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Our Culture: the stories and games Bahamians hand down**
2. **It's Christmas Time: Junkanoo, the festival that carries Christmas**
3. **Bahamian Culture: traditions handed down**
4. **Celebrating Christmas: Junkanoo, the Christmas-season festival**
5. **Bahamian Culture: what identifies us as Bahamian**
6. **Junkanoo: a festival born in slavery days**
7. **Festivals Parts 1–2: each island's own festivals**
8. **Manufacturing Sisal and Straw: the old fibre crafts**
9. **Junkanoo: named for John Canoe**
10. **Cultural Myths & Practices: the stories a culture hands down**
11. **Bush Medicine for Common Ailments: knowledge from the bush**
12. **Cultural Practices Part 1: history is a main component of culture**
13. **Junkanoo: the celebration that survived**
14. **Cultural Connections Parts 1–2: comparing our culture with the world's**

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Culture is what a people hands down — stories, games, Junkanoo, crafts, bush medicine and festivals — and history is one of its main parts: it tells Bahamians where they have come from and where they are going.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in The Bahamas, as the books themselves work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson told properly (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks heritage is only old buildings → name the living parts too: tales retold, ring plays played, remedies remembered and Junkanoo danced each year — heritage lives in people, not only in places.
- Go back to the one thing: Culture is what a people hands down — stories, games, Junkanoo, crafts, bush medicine and festivals — and history is one of its main parts: it tells Bahamians where they have come from and where they are going. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
