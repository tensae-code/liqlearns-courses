# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
History tells what happened in the past, and a timeline — an outline of dates and stories in order — lets us place The Bahamas' story from the Lucayans and Columbus's 1492 landfall at San Salvador (Guanahani) onward.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

History is the telling of what happened in the past. The Bahamian books teach the tool for holding it: a timeline, an outline that sets dates and their stories in order. The Bahamian line begins with the Lucayans in the 1400s and reaches Columbus's landfall in 1492 at San Salvador, the island the Lucayans called Guanahani. Once events sit on a line, we can ask the historian's first questions — what came first, what followed, and what changed between them.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
