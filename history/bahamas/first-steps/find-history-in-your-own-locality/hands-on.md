# Hands-on — Find History in Your Own Locality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: A Bahamian surname is historical evidence: names such as Rolle on Exuma record which families settled which islands, and later Greek, Chinese and Haitian arrivals each left their names — and their stories — in the islands. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the story's turning point, and say each part aloud as you place it. Use the lesson's own key words: surnames, Rolle, Exuma, settlement, migration, Greeks, sponge industry, Chinese, Haitians.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place, a name, a day or a thing this lesson names, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
