# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A Bahamian surname is historical evidence: names such as Rolle on Exuma record which families settled which islands, and later Greek, Chinese and Haitian arrivals each left their names — and their stories — in the islands.

Look at the map. Here are the islands of The Bahamas, scattered in the Atlantic where the Caribbean begins — Nassau on New Providence, Eleuthera, Exuma, Abaco, Grand Bahama and the Family Islands around them.

Start with your own name. A Bahamian surname can tell which island a family comes from: Rolle is common on Exuma because a Loyalist named Rolle settled there in the late eighteenth century with the people he enslaved, and their descendants carry the name to this day. A name, read carefully, is a small piece of settlement history.

Widen the lens and the same lesson holds across the Caribbean: surnames match from island to island because enslaved people took their masters' English, French, Scottish and Irish names. Later peoples arrived for their own reasons — Greeks for the sponge industry, Chinese and Haitians fleeing politics and seeking a better life. Every name is a migration story waiting to be asked about.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
