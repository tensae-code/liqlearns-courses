# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

**Course:** History — Zambia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Zambia's heritage, in this lesson, is its freedom fighters: the book teaches children to demonstrate respect to the nationalists and freedom fighters — by role play and by the one-minute Salute Wall — because a country's heroes stay alive only if each new generation chooses to remember their names and sacrifices, and honouring them builds unity, identity and responsibility.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Zambia's heroes stay alive only if each new generation chooses to remember their names and sacrifices — and honouring them builds unity, identity and responsibility.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a person your own family or school remembers on purpose, and how you keep their name alive.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks honouring heroes is only for ceremonies → the book makes it classroom work: role play and the one-minute Salute Wall are things learners DO, so the remembering is practised, not just watched.
- Thinks heroes stay remembered by themselves → the book's own line is the opposite: a country's heroes stay alive only if each new generation chooses to remember their names and sacrifices.
- Go back to the one thing: Zambia's heritage, in this lesson, is its freedom fighters: the book teaches children to demonstrate respect to the nationalists and freedom fighters — by role play and by the one-minute Salute Wall — because a country's heroes stay alive only if each new generation chooses to remember their names and sacrifices, and honouring them builds unity, identity and responsibility. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
