# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand and say one hero's name out loud again, as the opening act taught. Hold that name in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and make a Salute Wall of your own: write the names from the lessons before — Kasanza, Mpezeni I, Mwata Kazembe, Kaunda, Mama Chikamoneka, Mama Kankasa, Mutembo, the Mposa Mabwe — in a row across the page, and under the row write the three things honouring builds: unity, identity, responsibility. Say each name aloud as you write it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for remembering in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time people honour someone on purpose — a name spoken, a minute kept, a story retold — note it: who was remembered, how, and what the lesson would say it builds. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
