# History — Zambia course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Zambia's
own — built from Zambia's *History Teaching Module — Form 2
Term 3* teacher guide (English). Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below
sit inside Level 1, in path order — what the learner proves,
step by step. The grade the book comes from lives only in
each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the module's: its topic numbers are
the order — the slave trade first (Topic 2.1.1, the earlier
Term 1 topic the module carries as its sample lesson plan),
then the two-stage resistance story (Topic 2.5: primary,
then secondary, then the places), then the nationalists
named and honoured (Topic 2.6.1), closing with the lesson
that teaches children to honour them. That ladder IS the
path order (recommended, never forced — a learner can jump
in higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — the slave trade, resistance and the road to
independence** (History Teaching Module — Form 2 Term 3,
teacher guide):

- `africa-after-1500/` — Level 1, first title. The Atlantic
  Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle (the trade's
  causes and impacts, the forms slavery took, and empathy
  for the enslaved, opened by the question "Can a human
  being own another?"); Zambia: Primary Resistance in
  Northern Rhodesia (the tax refusals of the Lunda under
  Kasanza, 1907, and the Gwembe Tonga, 1909; the Namwanga
  and Bisa refusing forced labour; the Ngoni under Mpezeni
  I, 1897, and Mwata Kazembe in arms; the defence of land,
  chiefs' authority and chitemene farming, and the
  independent churches); Zambia: Secondary Resistance and
  the Road to Independence (welfare associations, the
  Watchtower movement banned in 1935, the Copperbelt strikes
  of 1935 and 1940, Congress 1948 becoming the ANC 1951, the
  1952 London delegation, the fight against the 1953
  Federation, pass-burning, and UNIP's Cha Cha Cha campaign
  of 1961, to independence in 1964); Zambia: Liberation
  Struggle Sites (the Mulungushi Rock of Authority and the
  Mukuyu Tree at Kabwe, Chilenje House No. 394, Freedom
  House, Matero House No. 3144B, Bwacha House No. E1376, the
  Freedom Statue, and Isa ya Mwaamba at Choma); and Zambia:
  Nationalists of the Independence Struggle (the leading
  nationalists — Yamba, Lewanika, Nkumbula, Kaunda,
  Kapwepwe, Mama Chikamoneka, Mama Kankasa and Chona — and
  the other freedom fighters behind them, from Mutembo on
  the Freedom Statue to the Mposa Mabwe stone throwers of
  Kitwe).
- `first-steps/` — Level 1, second title. Heritage: What It
  Is and Why It Matters (demonstrating respect to
  nationalists and freedom fighters — role play and the
  one-minute Salute Wall — and why honouring them builds
  unity, identity and responsibility).
