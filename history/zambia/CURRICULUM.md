# History — Zambia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Zambia inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Zambia's placements in hand come
from one book with history in it — the Form 2 (Grade 8)
History Teaching Module for Term 3, a teacher guide for the
2024 History Syllabus — and its own topic numbers are the
progression the ladder follows. The module's sample lesson
plan carries the earlier Term 1 topic, Topic 2.1.1 (the
development of slavery and the slave trade), taught before
Term 3's topics in the syllabus year; then comes Topic 2.5,
Zambia's own two-stage resistance story — first local,
chief-led "primary" resistance, then organised "secondary"
resistance, closing with the places where the struggle was
planned; then Topic 2.6.1, the nationalists and freedom
fighters named and honoured, ending with the lesson that
teaches children to honour them. Zambia's own way, as filed,
is the ladder: its colonial past taught as a two-stage
resistance story, anchored in named liberation places and
named nationalists, including women, whom learners are taught
to honour by name. One book with placements, one level — the
ladder stops where the books in hand stop, not because Zambia
teaches nothing more: only Term 3 modules are held, and the
Grade 4 Social Studies Term 3 module in hand is record-only,
with no history at all (see ABOUT.md).

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own History Teaching Module — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full
book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Africa After 1500 → First Steps** — the slave trade
   first, as the module's own sample lesson plan teaches it
   (Topic 2.1.1, the earlier Term 1 topic: the trade's causes
   and impacts, the forms slavery took, and empathy for the
   enslaved, opened by the question "Can a human being own
   another?"); then Zambia's two-stage resistance story
   (Topic 2.5: primary resistance — the tax refusals, the
   labour refusals, the Ngoni and Mwata Kazembe in arms;
   secondary resistance — welfare associations, the
   Watchtower movement, the Copperbelt strikes, Congress and
   the ANC, the fight against the Federation, Cha Cha Cha,
   and independence in 1964; and the liberation struggle
   sites, the rock, the tree and the township houses); then
   the people (Topic 2.6.1: the leading nationalists and the
   other freedom fighters, in one lesson with two parts that
   build in order); and last the honouring itself, in the
   First Steps title (demonstrating respect — role play and
   the one-minute Salute Wall). 6 lessons, in two titles:
   `africa-after-1500/` (5 lessons), then `first-steps/`
   (1 lesson).

**Totals: 6 lessons** (Level 1: 6 — all from one teacher
guide; acceptance 0, labelled — see ABOUT.md).
