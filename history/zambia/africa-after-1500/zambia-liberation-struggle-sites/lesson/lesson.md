# Zambia: Liberation Struggle Sites

## Do it first

Close your eyes and picture an ordinary street: small houses, numbers on the doors. Now imagine that in one of those houses, men and women are planning the freedom of a whole country — and that people will remember that house number for ever. Say out loud: "Freedom has addresses." This lesson walks you to them.

## The story

Some history is kept in books. Zambia's book keeps this part in **places** — the sites of the liberation struggle, taught by name and, for the houses, by number, so that a learner can stand where the struggle stood.

Begin at **Kabwe**. There stands the **Mulungushi Rock of Authority** — a great rock where the nationalist movement gathered and spoke with one voice, a place of decision for the whole movement. Near it, the **Mukuyu Tree** at Kabwe, under whose shade meetings were held: a tree as a parliament, older than any building the movement could have hired.

Now come to **Lusaka**, and walk the townships. **Chilenje House No. 394** was **Kenneth Kaunda's home from 1960 to 1962** — the leader's own house, in the years when the final push was planned. **Freedom House** served the movement as its public face and headquarters in the capital. **Matero House No. 3144B** is remembered among the houses where the struggle was organised, meeting by meeting.

At **Bwacha House No. E1376** the book fixes a date as well as a door: there, on **8 March 1958**, **Kaunda was elected president of ZANC** — a township house holding the moment a movement chose its leader.

Two more places complete the map. The **Freedom Statue** stands as the struggle's monument in stone and bronze — the fighter breaking his chains, whom the next lesson names. And at **Choma**, **Isa ya Mwaamba** is kept among the places key to the struggle in the south.

That is the lesson's shape, and its point: a rock, a tree and ordinary township houses become **the map of a nation's freedom** — each place holding a meeting that moved Zambia toward **1964**. The houses look like any other houses. What makes them history is what was decided inside them.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: learn the places the way you would learn a walk: Kabwe first (rock and tree), then Lusaka house by house (394, Freedom House, 3144B, E1376), then the statue and Choma. A route is easier to remember than a list.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Zambia's liberation struggle is remembered in named places — the Mulungushi Rock of Authority and the Mukuyu Tree at Kabwe, Chilenje House No. 394 (Kaunda's home 1960–62), Freedom House and Matero House No. 3144B in Lusaka, Bwacha House No. E1376 (where Kaunda was elected ZANC president on 8 March 1958), the Freedom Statue, and Isa ya Mwaamba at Choma. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Mulungushi Rock, Mukuyu Tree, Chilenje House, Bwacha House, Freedom Statue. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: say "Freedom has addresses" out loud, and name one address from the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the walk — rock, tree, then the Lusaka houses by their numbers — and write beside each place what happened there.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week that your own community remembers for something that happened there. Note it.
