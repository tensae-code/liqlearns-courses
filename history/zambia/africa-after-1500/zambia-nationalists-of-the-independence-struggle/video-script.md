# Video script — Zambia: Nationalists of the Independence Struggle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. A struggle is people — and Zambia's book teaches its children the names.

Meet the front row. Dauti Yamba, the teacher. Godwin Mbikusita Lewanika, a king's son. Harry Mwaanga Nkumbula, the jailed party leader. Kenneth Kaunda and Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe. Mainza Chona. And two fearless women organisers: Julia Mulenga Nsofwa — Mama Chikamoneka — and Chibesa Kankasa — Mama Kankasa. One demand joins them all: majority rule, carried from the welfare societies to the presidency in 1964.

Now look behind them. John Zanco Mpundu Mutembo — you have seen him: he is the man on the Freedom Statue, and on the currency, breaking his chains. Moses Chinyama Luneta, Lewis Changufu, Nalumino Mundia, Reuben Kamanga, Sikota Wina, Justin Chimba, Grey Zulu, Aaron Milner — youth militants and union men.

And in Kitwe, the Mposa Mabwe — boys throwing stones at colonial power. The book honours them in the same breath as presidents.

Remember it this way: freedom had a front row and a back row, and Zambia teaches its children every name in both.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
