# Lesson plan — Zambia: Nationalists of the Independence Struggle

**Course:** History — Zambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zambia: Nationalists of the Independence Struggle
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Zambia's book teaches its independence struggle through named people in two companies: the leading nationalists — Dauti Yamba, Godwin Mbikusita Lewanika, Harry Mwaanga Nkumbula, Kenneth Kaunda, Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe, Julia Mulenga Nsofwa (Mama Chikamoneka), Chibesa Kankasa (Mama Kankasa) and Mainza Chona — who carried one demand, majority rule, from welfare societies to the presidency in 1964; and the other freedom fighters behind them — youth militants, union men and the Mposa Mabwe stone throwers of Kitwe — whose arrests and courage sped the British toward the exit.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The leading nationalists** — A teacher, a king's son, a jailed party leader and two fearless women organisers carry one demand — majority rule — from welfare societies to the presidency in 1964
2. **The other freedom fighters** — Behind the famous names stand youth militants, union men and stone-throwing boys whose arrests and courage sped the British toward the exit

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the independence struggle was carried by named people — leading nationalists above, and other freedom fighters behind them — whose one demand was majority rule.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.

## If the learner is stuck
- Learns only the presidents → the book's own shape is two companies: the leading nationalists, and the other freedom fighters behind them — and it honours the stone throwers of Kitwe in the same lesson as the presidency.
- Forgets the women → name them with their honoured names: Julia Mulenga Nsofwa, Mama Chikamoneka, and Chibesa Kankasa, Mama Kankasa — fearless women organisers in the front company of the struggle.
- Go back to the one thing: Zambia's book teaches its independence struggle through named people in two companies: the leading nationalists — Dauti Yamba, Godwin Mbikusita Lewanika, Harry Mwaanga Nkumbula, Kenneth Kaunda, Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe, Julia Mulenga Nsofwa (Mama Chikamoneka), Chibesa Kankasa (Mama Kankasa) and Mainza Chona — who carried one demand, majority rule, from welfare societies to the presidency in 1964; and the other freedom fighters behind them — youth militants, union men and the Mposa Mabwe stone throwers of Kitwe — whose arrests and courage sped the British toward the exit. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
