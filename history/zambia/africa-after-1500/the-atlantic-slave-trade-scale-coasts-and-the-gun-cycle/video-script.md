# Video script — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. It starts with a question a teacher writes on the board: can a human being own another?

Look at the map. Start on the coasts of Africa. People are seized from their homes — taken. They are carried onto ships and across the Atlantic Ocean — sold. And on the far shore they are set to work, year after year, with no pay at all.

Slavery took different forms, in different places, but the heart of it was the same: a person treated as property, as a thing owned by someone else.

Now think about what that taking did. To the people taken — everything. To the families and villages left behind — a wound that did not close. The book calls learners to feel with the enslaved people themselves: each one had a home, a name, a family, exactly as you do.

Remember it this way: one question judges the whole trade — can a human being own another? Zambia's book answers by teaching you to remember the people, not just the ships.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
