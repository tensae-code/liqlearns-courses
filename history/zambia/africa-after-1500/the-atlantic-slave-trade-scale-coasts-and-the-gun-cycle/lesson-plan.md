# Lesson plan — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

**Course:** History — Zambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Long before the resistance story, Africans were taken in the transatlantic slave trade — seized, sold and worked without pay — and Zambia's book opens the topic with one hard question, "Can a human being own another?", teaching the trade's causes and impacts, the forms slavery took, and empathy for the people who were enslaved.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence — it stands at the foot of Zambia's ladder, on nothing before it in this course (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the transatlantic slave trade took Africans who were sold and worked without pay, and the book opens it with the question "Can a human being own another?"
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a place where people are still treated as things to be used, and what the lesson's opening question says about it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks slavery was only hard work → the book's own words are stronger: people were taken, sold and worked without pay — treated as property, which is why the lesson opens by asking whether a human being can own another at all.
- Thinks the trade touched only faraway coasts → it is taught in Zambia's own book as the background every later topic stands on: the taking of Africa's people is where this course's story of foreign power over African lives begins.
- Go back to the one thing: Long before the resistance story, Africans were taken in the transatlantic slave trade — seized, sold and worked without pay — and Zambia's book opens the topic with one hard question, "Can a human being own another?", teaching the trade's causes and impacts, the forms slavery took, and empathy for the people who were enslaved. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
