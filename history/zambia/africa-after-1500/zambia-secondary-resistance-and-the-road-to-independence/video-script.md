# Video script — Zambia: Secondary Resistance and the Road to Independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. The village refusals grew up — into organisations that could shake a whole country.

Look at the map, and walk the years. In the 1920s, welfare associations appear in the towns: people organising to speak together. The Watchtower movement spreads its defiant message — until the government bans it in 1935.

Now go to the Copperbelt. The miners down tools — strikes in 1935, and again in 1940. Organised workers, stopping the colony's copper, all at once.

Then politics takes the road: the Northern Rhodesia African Congress in 1948, becoming the ANC in 1951. A delegation to London in 1952. And from 1953, the long fight against the Federation Britain imposed.

The last stops burn brighter: election boycott, passes set on fire, and in 1961 UNIP's Cha Cha Cha campaign sweeping the country. The colonial government concedes. In 1964, Northern Rhodesia becomes Zambia. Remember the road: it was walked, stop by stop — it was not handed over.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
