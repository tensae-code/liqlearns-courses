# Hands-on — Zambia: Secondary Resistance and the Road to Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the growth out loud again: village noes finding each other until a whole country says it together. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw the road to independence as a long line across the page. Mark the stops in order — 1920s welfare associations; 1935 Watchtower banned and the first Copperbelt strike; 1940 strike; 1948 Congress; 1951 ANC; 1952 London delegation; 1953 Federation fought; 1961 Cha Cha Cha — and end the road with a flag marked 1964. Say each stop aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see people organise — a meeting, a club, a campaign — note it: what they organised, and which stop on the lesson's road it resembles. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
