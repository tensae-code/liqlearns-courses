# Lesson plan — Zambia: Secondary Resistance and the Road to Independence

**Course:** History — Zambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zambia: Secondary Resistance and the Road to Independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Resistance in Northern Rhodesia grew from village refusals into organised, nationwide movements — Zambia's book calls it secondary resistance: welfare associations from the 1920s, the Watchtower movement (banned in 1935), the Copperbelt strikes of 1935 and 1940, the Northern Rhodesia African Congress of 1948 becoming the ANC in 1951, the 1952 London delegation and the fight against the 1953 Federation, election boycott and pass-burning, and UNIP's Cha Cha Cha campaign of 1961 — until the colonial government had to concede independence in 1964.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks independence was given as a gift → the book's road is a fight at every stop: associations, strikes, delegations, boycott and the Cha Cha Cha campaign pressed the colonial government until it had to concede independence in 1964.
- Confuses the two stages → primary resistance (the lesson before) was local and chief-led; secondary resistance is organised — associations, unions, congresses and parties acting across the whole country.
- Go back to the one thing: Resistance in Northern Rhodesia grew from village refusals into organised, nationwide movements — Zambia's book calls it secondary resistance: welfare associations from the 1920s, the Watchtower movement (banned in 1935), the Copperbelt strikes of 1935 and 1940, the Northern Rhodesia African Congress of 1948 becoming the ANC in 1951, the 1952 London delegation and the fight against the 1953 Federation, election boycott and pass-burning, and UNIP's Cha Cha Cha campaign of 1961 — until the colonial government had to concede independence in 1964. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
