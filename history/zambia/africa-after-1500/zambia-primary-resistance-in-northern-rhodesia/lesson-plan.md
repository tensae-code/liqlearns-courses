# Lesson plan — Zambia: Primary Resistance in Northern Rhodesia

**Course:** History — Zambia · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zambia: Primary Resistance in Northern Rhodesia
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Village by village in Northern Rhodesia, chiefs and farmers said no to colonial rule in its first stage — Zambia's book calls it primary resistance: refusing the tax (the Lunda under Kasanza at Mwinilunga in 1907, the Gwembe Tonga in 1909), refusing forced labour (the Namwanga and the Bisa), fighting with weapons (the Ngoni under Mpezeni I in 1897, Mwata Kazembe in the Luapula Valley), defending land, the chiefs' authority and chitemene farming, and building independent churches — and even in defeat, the refusal stayed alive.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Slave Trade, Resistance and the Road to Independence — it stands on the lesson before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: chiefs and farmers across Northern Rhodesia refused the tax, refused forced labour and fought for their land and their chiefs' authority — the first, local stage of resistance, which Zambia's book calls primary.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a time people in your own place said no together to something imposed on them, and what they refused.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks resistance began with political parties → Zambia's book teaches two stages: first this primary resistance — local, chief-led, village by village — and only later the organised, nationwide secondary resistance of the next lesson.
- Mixes up the refusals → sort them by what was refused: the tax (Lunda under Kasanza, 1907; Gwembe Tonga, 1909), forced labour (Namwanga and Bisa), and the armed fights (Ngoni under Mpezeni I, 1897; Mwata Kazembe in the Luapula Valley).
- Go back to the one thing: Village by village in Northern Rhodesia, chiefs and farmers said no to colonial rule in its first stage — Zambia's book calls it primary resistance: refusing the tax (the Lunda under Kasanza at Mwinilunga in 1907, the Gwembe Tonga in 1909), refusing forced labour (the Namwanga and the Bisa), fighting with weapons (the Ngoni under Mpezeni I in 1897, Mwata Kazembe in the Luapula Valley), defending land, the chiefs' authority and chitemene farming, and building independent churches — and even in defeat, the refusal stayed alive. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
