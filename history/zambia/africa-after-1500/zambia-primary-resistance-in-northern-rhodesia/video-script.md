# Video script — Zambia: Primary Resistance in Northern Rhodesia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Before there were parties, there were villages — and village by village, the chiefs and farmers of Northern Rhodesia said no.

Look at the map. At Mwinilunga, the Lunda under Chief Kasanza refuse the tax — that is 1907. In the Gwembe valley, the Tonga refuse it too, in 1909. The Namwanga and the Bisa refuse the forced labour draft: they will not be taken from their homes.

Now watch the fights. In 1897 the Ngoni rise under Mpezeni I. In the Luapula Valley, Mwata Kazembe defends his kingdom. What are they defending? Their land. Their chiefs' authority. Their chitemene farming — the way their fields have always been worked.

And quietly, another refusal grows: independent churches, where people worship under their own leaders. Zambia's book calls all of this primary resistance — the first stage, local and chief-led.

The fights were lost. But the book's point is this: even in defeat, the refusal stayed alive. Remember it that way — the no came first, and it never fully went away.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
