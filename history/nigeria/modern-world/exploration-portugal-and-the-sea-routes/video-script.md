# Video script — Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The first Europeans in Buganda — Speke, Grant and Stanley — came hunting the source of the Nile; Mutesa welcomed them because he wanted European guns against Egypt, and Stanley's letter home brought the missionaries.

The Nile's source was Europe's great geographical prize, and it drew explorers toward the lake kingdoms. Speke and Grant reached Buganda on that hunt, and after them came Stanley — the first Europeans the Kabaka's court received, arriving not by the caravan routes of the Arabs but as seekers after the river.

Kabaka Mutesa received them with calculation, not wonder. Egypt was expanding southward toward his kingdom, and Mutesa wanted European guns against Egypt. European visitors, in his eyes, were a possible counterweight — a source of weapons and of allies against the northern threat. The welcome was diplomacy, and both sides knew their own reasons.

Stanley's visit had a consequence neither guns nor geography: the letter he sent home from Buganda, praising the kingdom and calling for teachers of Christianity, was read across Britain. The missionaries answered it — and their arrival, told in the next lesson, split the court Mutesa had held together.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
