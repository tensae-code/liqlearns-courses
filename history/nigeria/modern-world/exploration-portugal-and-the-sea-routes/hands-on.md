# Hands-on — Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two parties meet, each wanting something the other has. The visitors want a river's secret and a road home to tell it. The king wants guns, because a dangerous neighbour is pressing from the north. Neither side is simply curious, and neither is simply generous. Watch what each welcome really means.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The first Europeans in Buganda — Speke, Grant and Stanley — came hunting the source of the Nile; Mutesa welcomed them because he wanted European guns against Egypt, and Stanley's letter home brought the missionaries. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
