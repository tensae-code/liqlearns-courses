# History — Nigeria course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Nigeria's
own — built from Nigeria's SS 2 *Africa and the Wider
World 2 — East, Central and Southern Africa since 1800*
student book (Longman Nigeria Plc, English, 1992), a course
that deliberately teaches other regions' histories for
their effect on Nigeria and Africa, by the comparative
method of its preface. Inside the course the organisation
is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below sit inside
Levels 1–3, in path order — what the learner proves, step
by step. The grade the book comes from lives only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: the comparative method
first, then the East African coast from the Swahili cities
to the sultanate's partition; then the two inland
case-studies, Buganda and Ethiopia; and last Southern
Africa, from its first peoples to union. That ladder IS
the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner can
jump in higher; the "start here instead" hints point down
this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — the method and the East African Coast**
(Preface, Ch. 1–3), in `first-steps/`, `africa-to-1500/`,
`ancient-world/` and `africa-after-1500/`:

- `first-steps/` — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold
  Together (the curriculum's own authors teach pupils to
  hold two states side by side — Dahomey against Buganda,
  Egypt against Zanzibar — to see how each answered the
  same century).
- `africa-to-1500/` — The East African Coast: The Swahili
  Cities (Bantu islanders intermarry with Arab and Persian
  settlers, and a new coastal people, the Waswahili, and
  their city-states are born).
- `ancient-world/` — Trade Routes That Linked the Old
  World (Arab sailors keep the monsoon secret for centuries
  and monopolise the dhow trade in ivory, gold and tortoise
  shell until others learn the winds).
- `africa-after-1500/` — The East African Coast: Portuguese
  Conquest and Rule (the Portuguese arrive for India-route
  ports, demand tribute, plunder Mombasa, Zanzibar and
  Pemba, and hold the coast as one province until Oman
  drives them out) · East Africa and the Omani Turn
  (Sayyid Said moves his capital across 3,200 km of ocean
  to Zanzibar and rebuilds Omani power on African soil;
  Zanzibar becomes the nerve centre where ivory, guns and
  cloves change hands) · The Indian Ocean and Trans-Saharan
  Slave Trades (Britain presses treaty after treaty on the
  sultans to end the East African slave trade the
  plantation economy depends on, while other powers look
  away) · Colonisation: The Partition and Fashoda (Germany
  seizes the mainland coast after a revolt against its
  company, Britain takes Zanzibar as a protectorate in
  1890, and the sultan is not even consulted).

**Level 2 — Buganda and Ethiopia** (Ch. 4–8), in
`africa-after-1500/`, `modern-world/` and
`africa-to-1500/`:

- `africa-after-1500/` — The Buganda Kingdom: Clans, Kabaka
  and Trade (forty totemic clans under one Kabaka build the
  strongest state on Lake Victoria; Arab traders reach
  Buganda by 1845 with guns and cloth, and Islam follows
  them) · Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior (CMS
  Protestants and French Catholics arrive a week apart,
  each tells Mutesa the other is not a proper Christian,
  and the court splits into rival religious parties) ·
  Buganda: Religious Wars and British Occupation (Mwanga
  kills Bishop Hannington, the parties fight three civil
  wars in 1888–1890, and Lugard's company army settles who
  rules; Baganda agents like Kakungulu then conquer and
  administer the east) · Ethiopia: The Era of the Princes
  (Zemene Mesafint) (emperors become puppets while
  regional lords rule Ethiopia in pieces, and the people
  wait for a prophesied Theodore) · Ethiopia: Tewodros II
  the Unifier (Kassa, the outlawed shifta whose widowed
  mother hawked medicine in Gondar, fights his way to the
  throne, ends the Era of the Princes, reforms church and
  army, and dies at Maqdala facing a British expedition) ·
  Ethiopia: Yohannes IV Against Egypt and Italy (Menelik's
  submission won by accord, Khedive Ismail's invasions
  crushed at Gundet and Gura, Italy taking Eritrea with
  British blessing, and death fighting the Mahdists in
  1889).
- `modern-world/` — Exploration: Portugal and the Sea
  Routes (Speke, Grant and Stanley, hunting the Nile's
  source, are welcomed by Mutesa, who wants European guns
  against Egypt — and Stanley's letter home brings the
  missionaries).
- `africa-to-1500/` — Ethiopia: Kingdoms and Faiths Before
  1800 (highland and lowland peoples, Aksum's Christianity,
  Islam's arrival and the Gondar emperors — the background
  to a country never colonised).

**Level 3 — Southern Africa: revolution, trek and union**
(Ch. 9–12), in `africa-to-1500/` and `africa-after-1500/`:

- `africa-to-1500/` — Southern Africa's First Peoples: San,
  Khoikhoi and Bantu (click-speaking San hunter-gatherers
  and Khoikhoi herders hold the land for centuries before
  Bantu farming chiefdoms spread across it).
- `africa-after-1500/` — The Northern Nguni Revolution
  Before Shaka (Dingiswayo of the Mthethwa and Zwide of
  the Ndwandwe build confederations and age-regiment
  armies; Zwide kills Dingiswayo in 1817, and the stage is
  set for Shaka) · Shaka and the Zulu Kingdom (the mocked
  illegitimate boy becomes Dingiswayo's commander, seizes
  the small Zulu chiefdom, and remakes war itself with the
  short stabbing spear, bare feet and age regiments) · The
  Mfecane: New States — Moshoeshoe, Mzilikazi and the
  Ngoni (Shaka's wars scatter peoples who build new
  kingdoms far away — Moshoeshoe's Lesotho, Mzilikazi's
  Ndebele, Soshangane's Gaza and the Ngoni — while emptied
  land invites the Boers in) · The Cape Colony and the
  Frontier Wars (Van Riebeeck's 1652 refreshment station
  grows into a settler colony whose trekboers fight a
  century of frontier wars with the Xhosa; Britain takes
  the Cape, lands 5,000 settlers in 1820, anglicises law
  and language and emancipates the slaves by 1835) · White
  Settlement and the Great Trek in South Africa (some
  6,000 Voortrekkers sell up and wagon north from 1835 to
  found free Boer states; the trek wars with Dingane and
  Mzilikazi; Natal, the Orange Free State and the
  Transvaal, on a racial order ranking every other race
  below Europeans) · South Africa: Diamonds, Gold and the
  Road to Union (Kimberley diamonds and Witwatersrand gold
  pull in Gujarati traders and Gandhi; two Anglo-Boer
  wars; independence surrendered in 1902 and South Africa
  united under Britain).
