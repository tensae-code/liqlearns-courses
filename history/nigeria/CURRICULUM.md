# History — Nigeria course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Nigeria inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Nigeria's read set in hand is one
book — its SS 2 (Grade 11) *Africa and the Wider World 2 —
East, Central and Southern Africa since 1800* student book
(Longman Nigeria Plc, 1992), which teaches other regions'
histories for their effect on Nigeria and Africa, in twelve
chapters plus a preface that sets the series' comparative
method. So the ladder follows the book's own order, grouped
the way its story divides: the method first, with the East
African coast the book opens on (Level 1, Preface and
Ch. 1–3); then the two inland case-studies the book builds
next — Buganda and Ethiopia (Level 2, Ch. 4–8); and last
Southern Africa, from its first peoples through revolution,
trek and union (Level 3, Ch. 9–12). The ladder stops where
the book in hand stops, not because Nigeria teaches nothing
more: eighteen further Nigeria titles stand in the record as
UNREAD, record-only (Books 1–18 in ABOUT.md), and the ladder
is provisional where they would sit.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own SS 2 *Africa and the Wider World 2* — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the book's. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **The Method and the East African Coast** — the course
   opens with the book's own method (the Preface): hold two
   states side by side — Dahomey against Buganda, Egypt
   against Zanzibar. Then the coast the method is first
   practised on: the Swahili cities born of Bantu, Arab and
   Persian intermarriage; the monsoon road and the Arab
   sailors' guarded secret; the Portuguese conquest that
   held the coast as one province; Sayyid Said's Omani turn
   — a capital moved 3,200 km to Zanzibar, nerve centre of
   ivory, guns and cloves; Britain's treaties squeezing the
   slave trade the plantations depended on; and the
   sultanate's own partition — Germany on the mainland,
   Britain's protectorate over Zanzibar in 1890, the sultan
   not consulted. 7 lessons, in four titles: `first-steps/`
   (1 lesson), `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson), `ancient-world/`
   (1 lesson) and `africa-after-1500/` (4 lessons).

2. **Buganda and Ethiopia** — the book's two inland
   case-studies. Buganda: forty totemic clans under one
   Kabaka, the strongest state on Lake Victoria; the
   Zanzibari Arabs arriving by 1845 with guns, cloth and
   Islam; Speke, Grant and Stanley hunting the Nile's
   source, welcomed by Mutesa who wanted guns against
   Egypt; the Protestant and Catholic missions arriving a
   week apart and splitting the court; Mwanga, the
   1888–1890 civil wars, Lugard's company army — and the
   Baganda themselves becoming the agents of occupation.
   Then Ethiopia, the never-colonised counterpoint: Aksum's
   Christianity, Islam's arrival and Gondar's castles; the
   Era of the Princes, when emperors were puppets; Kassa
   the shifta crowned Tewodros II, unifier and reformer,
   dead at Maqdala; and Yohannes IV — Menelik won by
   accord, Egypt crushed at Gundet and Gura, Eritrea lost
   to Italy, death fighting the Mahdists. 8 lessons, in
   three titles: `africa-after-1500/` (6 lessons),
   `modern-world/` (1 lesson) and `africa-to-1500/`
   (1 lesson).

3. **Southern Africa: Revolution, Trek and Union** — the
   first peoples first: click-speaking San, Khoikhoi
   herders, Bantu farming chiefdoms. Then the northern
   Nguni revolution before Shaka — Dingiswayo and Zwide,
   confederations and age regiments, 1817 — and Shaka
   himself: the short stabbing spear, bare feet, the
   kingdom built. The Mfecane scatters peoples who build
   Lesotho, the Ndebele, Gaza and the Ngoni states, and
   leaves the emptied land the Boers will read as vacant.
   At the Cape: Van Riebeeck's 1652 refreshment station, a
   century of frontier wars with the Xhosa, Britain's
   occupation, the 5,000 settlers of 1820 and the reforms
   to 1835. The answer is the Great Trek — 6,000
   Voortrekkers, wars with Dingane and Mzilikazi, and the
   republics of Natal, the Orange Free State and the
   Transvaal on a written racial order. The course closes
   on the mineral revolution: Kimberley diamonds,
   Witwatersrand gold, Gandhi's South African beginning,
   two Anglo-Boer wars, independence surrendered in 1902,
   and union under Britain. 7 lessons, in two titles:
   `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson) and `africa-after-1500/`
   (6 lessons).

**Totals: 22 lessons** (Level 1: 7 · Level 2: 8 · Level 3:
7 — 27 placements, all from the one SS 2 student book:
12 filed to existing lessons, 15 filed NEW LESSON NEEDED,
as filed).
