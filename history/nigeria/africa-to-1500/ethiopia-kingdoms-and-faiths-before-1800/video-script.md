# Video script — Ethiopia: Kingdoms and Faiths Before 1800

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before Theodore, Ethiopia already carried a long history — highland and lowland peoples, Aksum's ancient Christianity, Islam's arrival, and the emperors of Gondar — the background to a country that was never colonised.

Ethiopia's stage is two worlds in one country: the highlands — cool, mountainous, plough-farming — and the lowlands around them, home to pastoral peoples. Many peoples and languages shared this stage, and the book surveys them as the cast of the long history that follows.

Into the highlands came one of Africa's oldest Christian stories: the kingdom of Aksum adopted Christianity in ancient times, and the Ethiopian church grew from that root, with its own traditions, manuscripts and stone churches. Centuries later Islam arrived in the region, and Muslim communities and states lived alongside the Christian highland kingdom — two faiths sharing one land's history, in alliance and in war.

The survey closes with the emperors of Gondar, whose stone castles still stand as the highland kingdom's monument. This whole long background — peoples, faiths, kingdoms — is the inheritance of the country the book calls never colonised: when the scramble partitioned the continent, Ethiopia would meet it with a state and a story already a thousand years deep.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
