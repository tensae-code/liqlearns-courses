# Hands-on — Southern Africa's First Peoples: San, Khoikhoi and Bantu

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before the revolutions and kingdoms of the next lessons, learn who was already there. Three ways of life shared one land: hunters who gathered, herders who followed cattle, and farmers who cleared and planted. Ask of each: what does this way of life need from the land — and what happens when the needs collide?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Southern Africa's first peoples were the click-speaking San hunter-gatherers and the Khoikhoi herders, who held the land for centuries before Bantu farming chiefdoms spread across it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
