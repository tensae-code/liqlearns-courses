# Video script — The East African Coast: The Swahili Cities

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
On the East African coast, Bantu islanders and mainlanders intermarried with Arab and Persian settlers, and a new coastal people — the Waswahili — and their city-states were born.

The coast and its islands were Bantu country first — farmers and fishers whose languages and ways rooted the shore. Across the Indian Ocean came Arab and Persian settlers, traders who stayed, built stone houses and married into coastal families.

Out of that long intermarriage a new people was born: the Waswahili, the Swahili people — African in root, coastal in life, speaking a Bantu language enriched by the sea trade, and Muslim in faith. Their towns rose as city-states along the coast and on islands like Zanzibar and Pemba: each ruled itself, traded with the interior and the ocean, and answered to no single king.

Inside the towns the book distinguishes the Shirazi — families who claimed descent from Persian settlers of Shiraz and held pride of place — from the commoners, the town's working majority. The city-states were rich, quarrelsome and independent — which is why, when armed strangers later arrived by sea, the coast could not face them as one.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
