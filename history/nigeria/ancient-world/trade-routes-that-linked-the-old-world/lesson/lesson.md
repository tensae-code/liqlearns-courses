# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

## Do it first

Wind is a road if you know when it blows. On the Indian Ocean the winds reverse with the seasons — one way for half the year, back again for the other half. Ask: what is that knowledge worth to the sailors who hold it, and what happens when it leaks? Sail with the monsoon.

The Indian Ocean runs on the monsoon: seasonal winds that blow toward Africa for part of the year and reverse to carry ships home. A captain who knows the monsoon's calendar can ride out and ride back on schedule, year after year, linking Arabia, Persia, India and the East African coast in one trading world.

Arab sailors learned that calendar early — and kept it secret for centuries. Guarding the winds, they monopolised the dhow trade: their lateen-sailed boats carried the coast's ivory, gold and tortoise shell out, and brought cloth, beads and manufactured goods in. The secret was the monopoly; knowledge itself was the cargo that paid best.

Secrets of the sea do not keep forever. As others learned the winds — and as new sailors, above all the Portuguese, found their own way around Africa — the old monopoly broke. The trade routes that linked the old world stayed; the hands that held them changed.
