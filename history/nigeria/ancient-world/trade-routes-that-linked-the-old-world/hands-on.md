# Hands-on — Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Wind is a road if you know when it blows. On the Indian Ocean the winds reverse with the seasons — one way for half the year, back again for the other half. Ask: what is that knowledge worth to the sailors who hold it, and what happens when it leaks? Sail with the monsoon.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Arab sailors kept the secret of the monsoon winds for centuries and monopolised the dhow trade of the Indian Ocean — ivory, gold and tortoise shell — until others learned the winds. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
