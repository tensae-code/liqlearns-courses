# About the History — Nigeria course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own SS 2 *Africa and the Wider World 2*. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the book's.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Nigeria inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **three levels**, following the one
read book's own order — Preface and twelve chapters — grouped
the way its story divides, as filed:

1. **Level 1 — The Method and the East African Coast.** The
   series' comparative method (Preface), then the coast:
   Swahili cities, the monsoon trade, Portuguese conquest,
   the Omani turn, the slave-trade treaties, and the
   sultanate's partition (Ch. 1–3).
2. **Level 2 — Buganda and Ethiopia.** Buganda's clans and
   Kabaka, the Arabs, the explorers, the divided court, the
   religious wars and the occupation (Ch. 4–5); then
   Ethiopia before Theodore, the Era of the Princes,
   Tewodros II and Yohannes IV (Ch. 6–8).
3. **Level 3 — Southern Africa: Revolution, Trek and
   Union.** The first peoples, the northern Nguni revolution
   and Shaka, the Mfecane's new states, the Cape and the
   frontier wars, the Great Trek and the republics, and
   diamonds, gold and the road to union (Ch. 9–12).

All 22 lessons stand on a **student book** and count in the
acceptance reckoning below; no lesson in this course stands
on teacher-guide evidence.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Africa, compared:* "Begin with a method: hold two states
  side by side, as this book's own authors teach. Then walk
  the East African coast — Swahili cities born of three
  peoples, a sultan who moved his capital across an ocean,
  treaties against a trade his plantations needed, and a
  realm partitioned while its sultan was not consulted.
  Turn inland: a kingdom of forty clans divided by two
  missions in a single week, and an empire of puppet
  emperors reunified by an outlaw who took a prophecy for
  his name. End in the south: regiments of barefoot men
  with short spears, peoples scattered into new kingdoms,
  wagons rolling north out of an empire — and diamonds and
  gold deciding the end. This is Africa and the wider
  world, taught Nigeria's way: comparatively, and from
  Africa's side."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Book 19, the SS 2 student book** — Nigeria's SSS history
course deliberately teaches other regions' histories only
for their effect on Nigeria and Africa: the preface frames
the whole three-volume series as a decolonisation of
African history, built by the curriculum's own authors
(NERDC, Lagos 1985 / Kano 1986 workshops); Volume 2 makes
SS2 pupils compare regions side by side (Dahomey with
Buganda, Egypt with Zanzibar, the Great Trek as the road to
apartheid in Volume 3). Each chapter closes with
multiple-choice + essay Questions and a Further reading
list drawn heavily on the UNESCO General History of Africa.
*(Student book — Nigeria's own way, as filed.)*

**Books 1–18** — no way of the books can be stated: no copy
of any of them is held. They are Learn Africa Plc
approved-list catalogue titles (Primary 1–6 and JSS 1–3
Basic Social Studies, Nigerian History / History for
Primary Schools, History for JSS, and one combined Social
and Citizenship Studies title), print-only with no free
official PDF, as filed.
