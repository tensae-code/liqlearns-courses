# Hands-on — The Indian Ocean and Trans-Saharan Slave Trades

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
An economy can be built on a cruelty until the cruelty becomes its foundation: pull it out and the whole building shakes. Zanzibar's cloves grew on enslaved labour, and its caravans carried enslaved people as well as ivory. Now a foreign power arrives demanding signatures against that trade. Ask: what does a sultan sign, and what does he mean by signing?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Britain pressed treaty after treaty on the Omani sultans to end the East African slave trade — the trade the plantation economy depended on — while other powers looked away. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
