# Video script — The Indian Ocean and Trans-Saharan Slave Trades

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Britain pressed treaty after treaty on the Omani sultans to end the East African slave trade — the trade the plantation economy depended on — while other powers looked away.

The Omani empire's prosperity rested in part on enslaved people: the clove plantations of Zanzibar and Pemba were worked by enslaved labour, and the caravan trade carried captives to the coast alongside the ivory. The slave trade was not a side business of the system; it was one of its load-bearing walls.

Britain, now campaigning against slave trades worldwide, pressed the sultans of Zanzibar with treaty after treaty — each one narrowing the trade on paper: limits on where captives could be shipped, then wider bans. The sultans signed under pressure, balancing British warships and demands against an economy that depended on the very trade the treaties attacked.

Other European powers, the book notes, looked away — their own interests came first, and enforcement fell unevenly. So the treaties mark a long, contested squeeze rather than a single ending: signatures at Zanzibar, pressure from the sea, and a plantation economy resisting the loss of its foundation.
