# Hands-on — The Cape Colony and the Frontier Wars

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A vegetable garden for passing ships becomes a colony; the colony's farmers walk their herds inland; the inland is someone else's grazing ground. A century of wars follows from that one expanding edge. Then a new empire takes the garden, lands thousands of its own settlers, and rewrites the rules — including freedom for the enslaved. Follow the frontier, and then the reforms.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Cape began as Van Riebeeck's 1652 refreshment station and grew into a settler colony whose trekboers fought a century of frontier wars with the Xhosa; Britain then took the Cape, landed 5,000 settlers in 1820, and reformed law, language and slavery itself by 1835. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
