# Video script — The East African Coast: Portuguese Conquest and Rule

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The Portuguese came to the East African coast hunting ports for the India route, demanded tribute, plundered Mombasa, Zanzibar and Pemba, and held the coast as one province — until Oman drove them out.

Portugal's ships rounded Africa hunting the sea road to India, and the East African coast mattered to them as a chain of ports on that road — places to water, repair, trade and control. They did not come as the coast's old trading partners had come. They came with cannon, demanding tribute from towns that had never paid it.

Where towns resisted or bargained, the Portuguese plundered: Mombasa was sacked, and Zanzibar and Pemba felt the same treatment. Forts rose, garrisons stayed, and the separate city-states were held together under Portuguese power as one province of their eastern empire — the coast ruled from outside, for a route that led elsewhere.

The province lasted until a new sea power rose in Arabia itself. Oman, grown strong, crossed the ocean and drove the Portuguese out of the coast — the turn the next lesson follows, when the expellers of Portugal became the coast's new masters.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
