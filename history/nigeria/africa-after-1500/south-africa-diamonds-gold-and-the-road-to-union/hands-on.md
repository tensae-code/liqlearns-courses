# Hands-on — South Africa: Diamonds, Gold and the Road to Union

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stones and yellow metal, dug from two inland fields, did what no proclamation could: they made the Boer republics too valuable to leave alone. Money rushed in, workers rushed in, an empire's appetite sharpened — and within a generation the republics were gone and a union stood in their place. Follow the wealth to the war, and the war to 1902.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Kimberley diamonds and Witwatersrand gold transformed South Africa — pulling in Gujarati traders and Gandhi himself — until two Boer wars ended with the Boers surrendering independence in 1902 and South Africa uniting under Britain. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
