# Video script — Buganda: Religious Wars and British Occupation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Kabaka Mwanga's struggle with Buganda's religious parties broke into three civil wars in 1888–1890; Lugard's company army settled who ruled, and Baganda agents then became the agents of British occupation across Uganda.

Part 1 — Mwanga, the 1888–1890 civil wars, Lugard and the IBEAC

Mwanga succeeded Mutesa and found the religious parties stronger than the throne's old authority. His reign opened in blood: Bishop Hannington, approaching Buganda from the east, was killed on Mwanga's orders — a warning to the foreign faiths that became a martyrdom the missions never forgot.

Mwanga tried to balance Muslims against Christians and party against party, but the balance broke. Between 1888 and 1890 the parties fought three civil wars — the throne itself changing hands as factions won and lost the capital. Into that exhaustion came Captain Lugard with the army of the Imperial British East Africa Company, the IBEAC: a chartered company's small force, whose intervention settled which party — and whose protector — would rule Buganda.

Part 2 — The Baganda as agents of British occupation in Uganda

Occupation needed local hands, and Buganda supplied them. Apolo Kaggwa, the great Protestant chief, backed the British connection for thirty-seven years, making himself and his party the kingdom's governing force under the new order.

Beyond Buganda, Baganda agents carried the occupation outward: men like Kakungulu conquered and administered the eastern peoples in Britain's name, planting Buganda's model — and Buganda's agents — across what became Uganda. The reward was rank: Buganda rose above all its neighbours inside the protectorate, privileged for the part it had played in occupying the rest.
