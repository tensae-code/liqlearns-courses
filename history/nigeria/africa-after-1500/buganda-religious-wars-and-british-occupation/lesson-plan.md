# Lesson plan — Buganda: Religious Wars and British Occupation

**Course:** History — Nigeria · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Buganda: Religious Wars and British Occupation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Buganda and Ethiopia (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Kabaka Mwanga's struggle with Buganda's religious parties broke into three civil wars in 1888–1890; Lugard's company army settled who ruled, and Baganda agents then became the agents of British occupation across Uganda.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Buganda and Ethiopia — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Mwanga, the 1888–1890 civil wars, Lugard and the IBEAC** — Mwanga kills Bishop Hannington and tries to balance Muslims and Christians, the parties fight three civil wars, and Lugard's company army settles who rules.
2. **The Baganda as agents of British occupation in Uganda** — Protestant chief Apolo Kaggwa backs Britain for 37 years, Baganda agents like Kakungulu conquer and administer the east, and Buganda rises above all its neighbours.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Britain occupied Uganda with British soldiers alone → Lugard's force was a chartered company's army, and the occupation was carried across Uganda largely by Baganda agents like Kakungulu, under chiefs like Apolo Kaggwa.
- Go back to the one thing: Kabaka Mwanga's struggle with Buganda's religious parties broke into three civil wars in 1888–1890; Lugard's company army settled who ruled, and Baganda agents then became the agents of British occupation across Uganda. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
