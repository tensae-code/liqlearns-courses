# Hands-on — Ethiopia: Tewodros II the Unifier

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
His mother sold medicine in the market. He himself was an outlaw, a shifta, with a price on survival. Thirteen years after taking the throne he died an emperor on a mountain fortress, defying a British expedition. Trace how the outlaw became the unifier — and what his reforms cost.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Kassa — an outlawed shifta whose widowed mother hawked medicine in Gondar — fought his way to the throne as Tewodros II, ended the Era of the Princes, reformed church and army, and died at Maqdala in 1868 facing a British expedition. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
