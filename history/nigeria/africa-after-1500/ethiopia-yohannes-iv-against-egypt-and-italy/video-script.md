# Video script — Ethiopia: Yohannes IV Against Egypt and Italy

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Yohannes IV won Menelik's submission by accord rather than war, crushed Khedive Ismail's Egyptian invasions at Gundet and Gura, watched Italy take Eritrea with British blessing, and died in 1889 fighting the Mahdists.

Yohannes IV, emperor from 1872, inherited Tewodros's reunified but fragile Ethiopia. His most dangerous internal rival was Menelik, king of Shewa — and Yohannes's first achievement was not a battle: he won Menelik's submission by accord, an agreement that held the empire together without a civil war neither could afford.

The invasions came from Egypt. Khedive Ismail, expanding his empire southward, sent armies into Ethiopia — and Yohannes destroyed them at Gundet and at Gura. Egyptian expansion broke on Ethiopian resistance, and the empire's independence was proved in battle, not merely claimed.

The third danger arrived by sea and by diplomacy: Italy, with British blessing, took Eritrea on the coast — a loss Yohannes could protest but not reverse while other enemies pressed. In 1889, fighting the Mahdists who had risen in Sudan, Yohannes was killed in battle — an emperor who had settled the inside, beaten the northern invader, and died facing the next one.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
