# Video script — Shaka and the Zulu Kingdom

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Shaka — the mocked illegitimate boy who became Dingiswayo's commander — seized the small Zulu chiefdom and remade war itself: the short stabbing spear, bare feet, and age regiments.

Shaka was born the illegitimate son of a Zulu chief, mocked and driven into exile — the boy the chiefdom laughed at found his school in Dingiswayo's Mthethwa confederation, where he rose as a commander in the new warfare of age regiments. When Dingiswayo was killed, Shaka seized his own small Zulu chiefdom and began to build.

His military reforms were concrete and ruthless. The long throwing spear gave way to the short stabbing spear — war fought chest to chest, with a large shield, not from a safe distance. Sandals came off: bare feet, hardened by training, marched faster and farther. And the age-regiment system he had learned became the kingdom's skeleton — regiments quartered in military settlements, owing loyalty to the king, forbidden to marry until released.

The social reform matched the military one: conquered chiefdoms were absorbed into the Zulu kingdom, their young men drafted into its regiments, their identities re-forged as Zulu. A mocked exile's small chiefdom became, in a decade, the power of the region — the kingdom whose wars scatter the peoples of the next lesson across half a subcontinent.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
