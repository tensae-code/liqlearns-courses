# Hands-on — Shaka and the Zulu Kingdom

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
He began as the boy others mocked — illegitimate, exiled, small in every way the world measures. He ended remaking how a whole region fought, marched and lived. The tools were almost absurdly simple: a shorter spear, no sandals, men sorted by age. Ask how such small changes conquered — and what they cost the people inside them.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Shaka — the mocked illegitimate boy who became Dingiswayo's commander — seized the small Zulu chiefdom and remade war itself: the short stabbing spear, bare feet, and age regiments. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
