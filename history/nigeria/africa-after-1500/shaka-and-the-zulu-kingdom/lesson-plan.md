# Lesson plan — Shaka and the Zulu Kingdom

**Course:** History — Nigeria · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Shaka and the Zulu Kingdom
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Southern Africa: Revolution, Trek and Union (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Shaka — the mocked illegitimate boy who became Dingiswayo's commander — seized the small Zulu chiefdom and remade war itself: the short stabbing spear, bare feet, and age regiments.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Southern Africa: Revolution, Trek and Union — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: The Northern Nguni Revolution Before Shaka — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Shaka — the mocked illegitimate boy who became Dingiswayo's commander — seized the small Zulu chiefdom and remade war itself: the short stabbing spear, bare feet, and age regiments.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a team the learner knows that won not by better equipment but by changing how it trained and organised itself.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Shaka's power rested on guns or numbers → it rested on reforms anyone can list: the short stabbing spear, bare feet for speed, and age regiments loyal to the king — organisation, not firearms.
- Go back to the one thing: Shaka — the mocked illegitimate boy who became Dingiswayo's commander — seized the small Zulu chiefdom and remade war itself: the short stabbing spear, bare feet, and age regiments. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
