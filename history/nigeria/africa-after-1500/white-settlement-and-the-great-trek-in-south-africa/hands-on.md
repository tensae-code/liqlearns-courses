# Hands-on — White Settlement and the Great Trek in South Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Six thousand people sell their farms, load wagons, and walk out of an empire — not to another empire, but to found their own states beyond its reach. It is one of history's great deliberate migrations. Ask both questions the book asks: why did they go — and what order did they build where they landed?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From 1835 some 6,000 Voortrekkers sold up and wagonned north out of British rule to found free Boer states — fighting Dingane's Zulu and Mzilikazi's Ndebele on the way, and building Natal, the Orange Free State and the Transvaal on a racial order that ranked every other race below Europeans. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
