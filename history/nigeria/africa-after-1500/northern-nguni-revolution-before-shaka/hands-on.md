# Hands-on — The Northern Nguni Revolution Before Shaka

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Famous revolutions usually have a famous name attached. This one happened before the famous name — chiefdoms merging into confederations, young men regimented by age instead of clan, two great builders facing each other. When one kills the other in 1817, the stage stands set — and empty — for the man who learned from both.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before Shaka, the northern Nguni were already in revolution: Dingiswayo of the Mthethwa and Zwide of the Ndwandwe built confederations and age-regiment armies — and Zwide killed Dingiswayo in 1817, setting the stage for Shaka. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
