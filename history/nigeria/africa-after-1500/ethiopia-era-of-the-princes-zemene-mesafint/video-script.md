# Video script — Ethiopia: The Era of the Princes (Zemene Mesafint)

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
In the Era of the Princes — the Zemene Mesafint — Ethiopia's emperors became puppets while regional lords ruled the country in pieces, and the people waited for a prophesied Theodore to reunite them.

Ethiopia's society rested on its farming peoples, its church, its soldiers and its regional lords — and by the period this part of the book covers, the balance among them had broken. The emperors at Gondar kept the crown, the court and the title, but real power had drained to the great regional lords.

That age is the Zemene Mesafint, the Era of the Princes. Emperors became puppets — raised, ruled through and deposed by the lords who held the provinces. Ethiopia was one country in name and memory, and many countries in fact: each lord taxed, judged and made war from his own stronghold.

A land divided like that generates its own hope. Prophecy circulated that a king named Theodore would come to reunite Ethiopia and restore the empire's strength. The people waited for him — and the next lesson meets the unlikely man who took the prophecy as his own name and programme.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
