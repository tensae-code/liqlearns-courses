# Video script — East Africa and the Omani Turn

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Sayyid Said of Oman moved his capital across 3,200 km of ocean to Zanzibar and rebuilt Omani power on African soil, turning Zanzibar into the nerve centre where ivory, guns and cloves changed hands.

Part 1 — Sayyid Said and the rise of the Omani empire

The Busaidi dynasty had already done one great thing: under them, Oman itself expelled the Portuguese and became the Arabian sea power of the western Indian Ocean. Sayyid Said, its sultan, then made the move no ruler before him had made — he transferred his capital from Oman to Zanzibar, across some 3,200 km of ocean.

It was strategy, not exile. From Zanzibar, Said stood at the door of the East African interior and its trade, closer to the ivory and the caravan routes than any palace in Arabia could be. Omani power was rebuilt on African soil: an empire whose head sat on an African island and whose writ ran down the coast the Portuguese had once held.

Part 2 — Zanzibar as economic nerve centre: cloves, caravans and the interior trade

Power needs a market, and Said organised his. Foreigners were banned from the interior: the trade of the mainland would pass through hands he controlled. Arabs and Swahili became the middlemen — organising the caravans that walked inland and brought the interior's goods back to the coast.

So Zanzibar became the nerve centre of the whole system. Ivory came down the caravan routes to its market; guns went inland in exchange; and on Zanzibar and Pemba, clove plantations — worked by enslaved labour, the system's dark foundation — made the islands the spice heart of the trade. Everything changed hands at Zanzibar, and the sultan taxed the changing.
