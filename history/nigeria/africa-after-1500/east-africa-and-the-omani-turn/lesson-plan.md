# Lesson plan — East Africa and the Omani Turn

**Course:** History — Nigeria · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** East Africa and the Omani Turn
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Method and the East African Coast (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sayyid Said of Oman moved his capital across 3,200 km of ocean to Zanzibar and rebuilt Omani power on African soil, turning Zanzibar into the nerve centre where ivory, guns and cloves changed hands.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Method and the East African Coast — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sayyid Said and the rise of the Omani empire** — The Busaidi sultan who expelled the Portuguese from Oman moves his capital across 3,200 km of ocean to Zanzibar and rebuilds Omani power on African soil.
2. **Zanzibar as economic nerve centre: cloves, caravans and the interior trade** — Said bans foreigners from the interior, makes Arabs and Swahili the middlemen, and turns Zanzibar into the market where ivory, guns and cloves change hands.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: The East African Coast: Portuguese Conquest and Rule — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Sayyid Said of Oman moved his capital across 3,200 km of ocean to Zanzibar and rebuilt Omani power on African soil, turning Zanzibar into the nerve centre where ivory, guns and cloves changed hands.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a trader the learner knows who moved his shop to sit where all the goods must pass — and grew rich on the passing.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Said moved to Zanzibar because he had lost Oman → he moved from strength: Oman had expelled the Portuguese, and Zanzibar put him at the door of the interior trade he meant to control.
- Go back to the one thing: Sayyid Said of Oman moved his capital across 3,200 km of ocean to Zanzibar and rebuilt Omani power on African soil, turning Zanzibar into the nerve centre where ivory, guns and cloves changed hands. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
