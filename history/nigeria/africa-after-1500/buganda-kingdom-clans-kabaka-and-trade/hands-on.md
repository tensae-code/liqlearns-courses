# Hands-on — The Buganda Kingdom: Clans, Kabaka and Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A kingdom is not only a king. Under Buganda's king stood forty clans, each with its own totem, its own duties, its own marriage law — a whole people organised like interlocking rings. Picture the rings first. Then watch strangers arrive at the court with new goods and a new faith, and the rings begin to move.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Buganda, the strongest state on Lake Victoria, was built of some forty totemic clans under one Kabaka — and in 1845 Arab traders from Zanzibar reached it with guns and cloth, and Islam followed them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
