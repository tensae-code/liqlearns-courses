# Lesson plan — The Buganda Kingdom: Clans, Kabaka and Trade

**Course:** History — Nigeria · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** The Buganda Kingdom: Clans, Kabaka and Trade
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Buganda and Ethiopia (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Buganda, the strongest state on Lake Victoria, was built of some forty totemic clans under one Kabaka — and in 1845 Arab traders from Zanzibar reached it with guns and cloth, and Islam followed them.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Buganda and Ethiopia — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Buganda society, clans and government in the nineteenth century** — Forty totemic clans under one Kabaka build the strongest state on Lake Victoria, where no one may marry inside their own or their mother's clan — except the king.
2. **Buganda's neighbours and the coming of the Zanzibari Arabs** — Arab traders reach Buganda by 1845 with guns and cloth, Islam follows them, and the Kabaka's court starts to change from the inside.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Buganda, the strongest state on Lake Victoria, was built of some forty totemic clans under one Kabaka — and in 1845 Arab traders from Zanzibar reached it with guns and cloth, and Islam followed them.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a school or community the learner knows that is organised in groups with their own names and duties, all answering to one head.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Buganda's king ruled a formless crowd → the kingdom was built of some forty totemic clans under one Kabaka, with a marriage law that wove the clans together — and bound them to the throne.
- Go back to the one thing: Buganda, the strongest state on Lake Victoria, was built of some forty totemic clans under one Kabaka — and in 1845 Arab traders from Zanzibar reached it with guns and cloth, and Islam followed them. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
