# The Mfecane: New States — Moshoeshoe, Mzilikazi and the Ngoni

## Do it first

A stone dropped in a pond does not only move the water it touches. Shaka's wars scattered peoples hundreds of kilometres — and the scattered did not vanish: they built. Four new states rise in this lesson from the scattering. Trace each one's road, and notice what the emptied land behind them invited.

The wars of Shaka's rising kingdom set the region moving — the Mfecane, the scattering: peoples fleeing the Zulu power, fighting their own wars of survival as they went, and re-forming far from home. Its map is a map of roads taken under pressure.

On those roads, builders rose. Moshoeshoe gathered refugees on a mountain stronghold and built Lesotho — a kingdom made by diplomacy and shelter as much as by war. Mzilikazi, breaking from Shaka, led his people north to found the Ndebele kingdom. Soshangane built Gaza in the east, and the Ngoni carried their migration far north toward the lakes. Each new state was the Mfecane's child: born of flight, built by organisation.

And behind the scattering lay emptied land — districts depopulated by war and flight. That emptiness, real and exaggerated, became the Boers' invitation in the next lessons: trekking farmers read the thinned land as vacant, and moved into a landscape whose peoples had been scattered, not erased.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Mfecane — the scattering set off by Shaka's wars — drove peoples who built new kingdoms far away: Moshoeshoe's Lesotho, Mzilikazi's Ndebele, Soshangane's Gaza and the Ngoni — while emptied land invited the Boers in. Give one real example: families the learner knows who were scattered by trouble and built new lives — and new communities — in the places they landed, and name the lesson's key words: Mfecane, Moshoeshoe, Lesotho, Mzilikazi, Ndebele, Soshangane, Gaza, Ngoni, emptied land. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
