# Hands-on — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take two cups of different shapes and pour the same water into each. The water is the same; the shape it takes is not. History works like that: the same century pours into different states, and each state takes its own shape. This lesson teaches the course's own method — comparing states — before the stories begin.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Nigeria's history course teaches the past by holding two states side by side — Dahomey against Buganda, Egypt against Zanzibar — so the learner can see how each state answered the same century in its own way. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
