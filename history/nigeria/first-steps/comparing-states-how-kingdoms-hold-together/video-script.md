# Video script — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Nigeria's history course teaches the past by holding two states side by side — Dahomey against Buganda, Egypt against Zanzibar — so the learner can see how each state answered the same century in its own way.

This course was built by Nigeria's own curriculum authors to retell African history from Africa's side — a decolonisation of the story itself. Their chosen tool, set out in the book's preface, is comparison: place two states side by side and ask the same questions of each.

So Dahomey is read against Buganda, and Egypt against Zanzibar. Two kingdoms, two coasts, the same nineteenth-century pressures — trade, guns, faiths, empires at the door — and different answers. Where the answers match, the learner sees the century's force. Where they differ, the learner sees each people's own choices, leaders and land.

Hold this method through the whole course. Every story that follows — Zanzibar, Buganda, Ethiopia, the Zulu — can be laid beside another, and the laying-beside is itself the lesson: kingdoms hold together, or fail to, for reasons you can name.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Nigeria.
