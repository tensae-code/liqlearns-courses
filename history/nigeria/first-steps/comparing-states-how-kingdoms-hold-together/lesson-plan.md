# Lesson plan — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

**Course:** History — Nigeria · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Method and the East African Coast (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Nigeria's history course teaches the past by holding two states side by side — Dahomey against Buganda, Egypt against Zanzibar — so the learner can see how each state answered the same century in its own way.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Method and the East African Coast — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Nigeria's history course teaches the past by holding two states side by side — Dahomey against Buganda, Egypt against Zanzibar — so the learner can see how each state answered the same century in its own way.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: two towns the learner knows, asked the same question — how does each settle a dispute? — and the answers compared.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
