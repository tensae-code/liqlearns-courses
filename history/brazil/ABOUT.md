# About the History — Brazil course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 10 História student book. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Brazil inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **four levels**, following the
book's own four units as filed:

1. **Level 1 — Work: Labour Has a History.** Work compared
   across slave, feudal and capitalist societies; the
   construction of wage labour; Brazil and the United
   States ending slavery by different roads, to abolition
   in 1888 and through civil war; work in contemporary
   society.
2. **Level 2 — Cities: Industry Pulls People In.** The
   first cities; Brazil's explosive 20th-century urban
   growth; the industrial city's remaking of class, family
   and daily life and its lasting problems; Paraná's towns
   and the Port of Paranaguá.
3. **Level 3 — The State and Power.** The state born with
   the first civilisations (Greek, Roman and feudal
   forms); the formation of the national states; state
   power and violence — law or force against the state's
   own people; the imperialist state and its crisis.
4. **Level 4 — Domination and Resistance.** Greece and
   Rome from underneath (women, plebeians, slaves);
   feudal Europe's lowest answering back; the modern
   West resisted by colonised peoples, enslaved Africans
   and the new working classes; the labour movement born
   in the factory; contemporary Brazil's movements.

All fourteen lessons stand on a **student book** and count
in the acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Three relations, four movements:* "Start with work —
  the thing every society must organise — and watch it
  change: the slave, the feudal peasant, the wage worker,
  and the worker of your own street today. Then watch the
  cities swell as industry calls, until the story lands at
  home, on the dock at Paranaguá. Then meet the state: born
  with the first civilisations, grown to nation and empire
  — and ask when its force is law and when it is violence.
  And at the end, turn the whole story over and hear it
  from underneath: the women, the plebeians, the slaves,
  the peasants, the colonised, the factory hands — and the
  movements in your own streets, still answering. That is
  Brazil's book, and it is yours."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**História — Livro Didático Público — Ensino Médio
(student book)** — A public textbook written by serving
teachers, organised not by chronology but by three
relations — work, power and culture — so slavery, wage
labour, the state and resistance are each followed across
ancient, medieval and modern societies, always landing
back in Brazil and in Paraná itself. *(Student book.)*
