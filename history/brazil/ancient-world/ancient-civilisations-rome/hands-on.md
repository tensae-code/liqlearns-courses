# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Rome

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before there were states, there were villages that settled their own quarrels. Then something new appeared in the world. Say out loud: "Someone began to rule — and it never stopped." This lesson meets the state at its birth.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The state is born with the first civilisations and takes Greek, Roman and feudal forms — power organised first as city, empire and lordship. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
