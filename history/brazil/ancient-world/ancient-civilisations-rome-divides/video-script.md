# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Under Greek and Roman glory stand women without rights, plebeians fighting for a share of power, and slaves who revolt — resistance begins with the first states.

The last unit changes the camera angle. The Greek and Roman worlds of the earlier lessons are told here **from underneath** — from the people the glory stood on.

First, **women**: in both Greek and Roman societies, women lived **without rights** of their own — under the authority of fathers and husbands, shut out of the public life their cities boasted of. Half of each society stood outside its citizenship.

Then the **plebeians** of Rome: the common people, who farmed, fought in the armies — and were shut out of power by the patrician families above them. The plebeians did not accept it. They **fought for a share of power**, withdrawing, demanding, winning ground step by step across generations.

And deepest down, the **slaves**: owned outright, worked without mercy — and **revolting**. Slave revolts shook the ancient world, and though they were crushed, they prove the unit's opening claim: **resistance begins with the first states**. Wherever domination was built, the dominated answered — from the very beginning.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Brazil.
