# Video script — Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Feudal Europe's lowest — peasants, heretics, women, the ill — endured lordship and the Church, and answered with revolts and forbidden beliefs.

The camera stays underneath as the story moves into **feudal Europe** — the world of lordship met in the state unit, now seen from the fields and villages it ruled.

At the bottom stood the **peasants**: tied to the land, working the lord's fields beside their own, carrying the whole society on their labour. With them stood the **artisans** of the towns, and **women**, under lord and Church and household authority at once. Beyond them, the **heretics** — believers whose faith differed from the Church's teaching — and **the sick**, the ill and the different, pushed to society's edge.

Over them all stood two powers together: **lordship**, taking their labour and obedience, and the **Church**, commanding their belief. To endure feudal Europe from underneath was to be ruled in body and in soul.

And they **answered**. Peasants rose in **revolts** against their lords, again and again across the centuries. The forbidden beliefs themselves were an answer: **heresy** was resistance in the realm of the soul, a refusal to let the Church own even what a person thought about God. Domination in the medieval world, like the ancient, never stood unanswered.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Brazil.
