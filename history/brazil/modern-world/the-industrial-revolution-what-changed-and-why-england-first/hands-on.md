# Hands-on — The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A bell, a clock, a foreman, a machine that never tires — and workers who had never before been gathered in one place, together, all day. Say out loud: "Together, they could answer." This lesson tells how the labour movement was born.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Inside industrialisation, factory workers answered the new discipline with machine-breaking, unions and strikes — the labour movement was born in the factory itself. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
