# Video script — Brazil: State Power and Violence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The state claims a monopoly of force — and the chapter asks the hard question: when is that force law, and when is it violence against the state's own people?

Every state in this unit has rested, in the end, on **force**: soldiers, police, prisons. This chapter names the claim behind them. The state claims a **monopoly of force** — that it alone may use violence lawfully, and that everyone else who uses it is a criminal.

That claim has a good face. If only the state may use force, then neighbours cannot settle quarrels by arms, and the strong cannot simply take from the weak. Force under **law** — limited, judged, answerable — is what the state promises in return for its monopoly.

But the chapter asks the question the promise invites: **when is that force law, and when is it violence?** When the state's own force breaks its own limits — turned against the state's **own people**, beyond what law allows — the monopoly stops protecting and starts attacking. The same uniform can stand on either side of the line.

The lesson does not hand the pupil an answer to carry away; it hands them the **question**, and the line to look for: force bound by law, or force against the people. A citizen who can ask that question can judge any state — including their own.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Brazil.
