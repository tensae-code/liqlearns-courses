# Hands-on — Brazil: State Power and Violence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Only one body in the country may use force, says the state: itself. Say out loud: "Who watches the watcher?" This lesson asks when the state's force protects — and when it attacks the very people it claims to serve.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The state claims a monopoly of force — and the chapter asks the hard question: when is that force law, and when is it violence against the state's own people? Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
