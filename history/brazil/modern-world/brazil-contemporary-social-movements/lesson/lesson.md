# Brazil: Contemporary Social Movements

## Do it first

The last lesson of the book is not in the past. Look out of the window: marches, strikes, occupations, banners. Say out loud: "This unit ends in our own streets." This lesson meets the movements of the pupils' own lifetime.

The book closes where the pupils live: with the **social, political and cultural movements of contemporary society**. Its doorway is a slogan from **1968**, the year students and workers shook country after country: **"is it forbidden to forbid?"** — a young generation questioning every authority that ruled it.

The chapter walks the movements of the pupils' **own lifetime**, and names their peoples. **Students**, rising over their schools and their futures. **Workers**, in the unions and strikes the last lesson saw being born. **Women**, contesting the place assigned to them — the newest form of the oldest resistance in this unit. **Black movements**, answering the unfinished business of abolition — the freedom of 1888 that came without land or rights. And the **landless movements**, occupying unused land and demanding the land reform Brazil's countryside has waited on for generations.

Each movement **contests power**: each says that some part of how society is run — the school, the wage, the home, the colour line, the land — is not natural and not final, and can be changed by people acting together.

So the whole book lands here. Work, cities, the state, domination — and at the end, in the pupils' own streets, the answer the last unit has traced from the first states onward: people underneath, organising, resisting, and remaking their world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: From 1968's slogan "is it forbidden to forbid?" to Brazil's own movements, students, workers, women and Black and landless movements contest power in the pupils' own lifetime. Give one real example: a movement or march they have seen or heard of in their own town — and which of the lesson's peoples it belongs to, and what it contests, and name the lesson's key words: social movements, 1968, is it forbidden to forbid, students, workers, women, Black movements, landless movements, Brazil. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
