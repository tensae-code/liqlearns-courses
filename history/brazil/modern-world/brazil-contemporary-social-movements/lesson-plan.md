# Lesson plan — Brazil: Contemporary Social Movements

**Course:** History — Brazil · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Brazil: Contemporary Social Movements
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Domination and Resistance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From 1968's slogan "is it forbidden to forbid?" to Brazil's own movements, students, workers, women and Black and landless movements contest power in the pupils' own lifetime.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Domination and Resistance — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: From 1968's slogan "is it forbidden to forbid?" to Brazil's own movements, students, workers, women and Black and landless movements contest power in the pupils' own lifetime.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a movement or march they have seen or heard of in their own town — and which of the lesson's peoples it belongs to, and what it contests.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Brazil, as the book itself works.
