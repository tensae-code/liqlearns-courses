# Brazil: Paraná Urbanisation and the Port of Paranaguá

## Do it first

Every big story in this course so far happened somewhere else first. This one happens at home. Say out loud: "Our own state is in the world story." This lesson walks Paraná's towns — and stands on the dock at Paranaguá.

After two chapters of world cities, the book turns to **Paraná** itself — the pupils' own state. Paraná's towns, too, **industrialised**: workshops and factories rose, towns grew as people came in from the countryside, and the state's life reorganised itself around town and factory, as it had in the great cities of the earlier chapters.

But a farming state does not live by its factories alone. Paraná's wealth grew on its land — its **produce** — and produce must travel to be sold. So the chapter walks to the coast, to the **Port of Paranaguá**.

At Paranaguá, the state's produce was loaded onto ships and **shipped into the capitalist world market** — sold to buyers in other lands, at prices set by that market. The port is where Paraná's fields met the whole system this course has been studying: the world of wage labour, factories and world trade from Units I and II.

That is the chapter's point, and the unit's: the world story is not far away. It runs through the pupils' own state, down its roads to its own port, and out to sea.
