# Hands-on — Capitalism: Private Ownership, Factories and New Classes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name three kinds of work you saw this week — at home, in the street, in a shop. Now ask of each one: does the worker own the tools and the place, or does someone else? Hold that question. This lesson shows that the answer has changed across history, and that the change made the modern world.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Work itself has a history: people have laboured as slaves, as feudal workers and as wage workers, and capitalism begins when workers who own neither land nor tools must sell their labour for a wage — the world of work pupils still live in today. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
