# Lesson plan — Capitalism: Private Ownership, Factories and New Classes

**Course:** History — Brazil · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Capitalism: Private Ownership, Factories and New Classes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Work: Labour Has a History (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Work itself has a history: people have laboured as slaves, as feudal workers and as wage workers, and capitalism begins when workers who own neither land nor tools must sell their labour for a wage — the world of work pupils still live in today.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Work: Labour Has a History — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The concept of work / The world of work in different societies** — Work is compared across slave, feudal and capitalist societies, so pupils see labour itself has a history and is not one natural, unchanging thing
2. **The construction of wage labour** — Workers separated from land and tools are driven to sell their labour for a wage — the founding move of capitalism
3. **Work in contemporary society** — The wage-labour world built in the earlier chapters is brought up to the pupils' own day — precarious, informal and changing work in Brazil today

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Work itself has a history: people have laboured as slaves, as feudal workers and as wage workers, and capitalism begins when workers who own neither land nor tools must sell their labour for a wage — the world of work pupils still live in today.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a worker they know in Brazil whose job is informal or precarious — no contract, paid by the day — and say which of the lesson's three worlds of work it is closest to.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in Brazil, as the book itself works.
