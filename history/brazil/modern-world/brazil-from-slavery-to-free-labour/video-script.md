# Video script — Brazil: From Slavery to Free Labour

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Brazil and the United States ended slavery by different roads — Brazil by gradual laws to abolition in 1888, the United States through civil war — and both left the freed people with no land and no rights, to compete as wage workers.

**Brazil** was one of the great slave societies of the Americas, and it ended slavery slowly. The book tells it as a road of **gradual laws**, step by step over decades, until the final act: **abolition in 1888**. Brazil was the last country in the Americas to abolish slavery.

The **United States** took the other road. There, slavery ended through **civil war** — the slaveholding states fought to keep slavery, lost, and emancipation came by war and defeat, not by a slow staircase of laws.

But then the two stories meet again, and the meeting is bitter. In **both** countries, the freed people received **no land and no rights** to start their new lives. The formerly enslaved — who had built so much of both countries' wealth — were turned loose to **compete as wage workers**, with nothing, against everyone.

That is why this chapter sits inside the unit on work. Abolition ended ownership of people. It did not give the freed the land or tools that would have made their freedom full — so the story of slavery runs straight into the story of wage labour.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Brazil.
