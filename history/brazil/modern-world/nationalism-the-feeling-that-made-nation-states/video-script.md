# Video script — Nationalism: The Feeling That Made Nation-States

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Kings first centralised power into single states; then the idea of the nation turned subjects into citizens of national states.

The chapter starts with the **kings**. Across the early modern centuries, kings drew power toward themselves — breaking the independence of the feudal lords of the last lesson, gathering armies, taxes and law into one centre. Power was **centralised**, and the modern state took shape as one territory under one ruler.

But a state held together only by a king has a weakness: it belongs to the king, not to the people in it. The people are **subjects** — they obey, but the country is not theirs.

Then came the idea that changed the state's foundation: the **nation** — the idea that a people sharing a land, a language and a story form one whole, and that the state should be *their* state. Where that idea took hold, subjects were turned into **citizens**: members of a **national state**, with a claim to belong to it and a part in it.

That feeling — **nationalism**, the love and claim of the nation — made the map of the modern world. It built nation-states where empires and kingdoms had stood, and the chapters after this one show both its power and its dangers.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Brazil.
