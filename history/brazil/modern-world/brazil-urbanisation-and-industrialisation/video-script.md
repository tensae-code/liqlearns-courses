# Video script — Brazil: Urbanisation and Industrialisation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Industry pulled Brazil's country people into cities that exploded in the 20th century — growing faster than homes, water and work could follow.

The chapter begins far back, with the **first cities** in history — the first places where thousands lived together without farming their own food, fed by the countryside around them. A city, from the start, is a bargain: the countryside feeds it, and it gives back crafts, trade, government and ideas.

Then the story lands in **Brazil**, and in Brazil's **20th century** the bargain was struck at explosive speed. **Industry** rose in the cities, and industry needs hands. Factory by factory, the cities called, and **country people came** — leaving the land in vast numbers for wages, lights and the promise of a different life.

But the cities grew **faster than homes, water and work could follow**. The newcomers built what shelter they could at the edges; water, drains and steady jobs lagged behind the arriving crowds. Brazil became a country of huge cities in a few decades — a change other countries spread over centuries.

So the lesson holds two truths together: the move to the city was a real hope, chosen by millions; and the city they arrived in was not ready for them. Both truths are still written on Brazil's city maps today.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Brazil.
