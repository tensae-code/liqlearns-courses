# History — Brazil course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Brazil inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Brazil's book in hand is one —
the Grade 10 / Ensino Médio *História — Livro Didático
Público* student book (SEED-PR, 2006, 2nd edition) — and it
is not organised by chronology at all. It is a thematic
public textbook written by Paraná state school teachers,
organised by three relations — work, power and culture —
in four units, so slavery, wage labour, the state and
resistance are each followed across ancient, medieval and
modern societies, always landing back in Brazil and in
Paraná itself. So the ladder follows the book's own four
units in order: Work (Level 1, Unit I), Cities (Level 2,
Unit II), the State and Power (Level 3, Unit III), and
Domination and Resistance (Level 4, Unit IV). The ladder
stops where the book in hand stops, not because Brazil
teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 10 História student book — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Work: Labour Has a History** — the book's Unit I:
   work compared across slave, feudal and capitalist
   societies; the construction of wage labour, when workers
   separated from land and tools must sell their labour;
   Brazil and the United States ending slavery by different
   roads — gradual laws to abolition in 1888, and civil
   war — both leaving the freed landless to compete as wage
   workers; and work in contemporary society, precarious
   and informal. 2 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/`
   (2 lessons; the first carries 3 sub lessons).

2. **Cities: Industry Pulls People In** — the book's
   Unit II: from the first cities in history to Brazil's
   explosive 20th-century urban growth, industry pulling
   country people into cities that grew faster than homes,
   water and work could follow; the 19th-century industrial
   city remaking class, family and daily life, its crowding,
   pollution and segregation traced into today's cities;
   and the story landing at home — Paraná's towns
   industrialising and the Port of Paranaguá shipping the
   state's produce into the capitalist world market.
   3 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (3 lessons).

3. **The State and Power** — the book's Unit III: the
   state born with the first civilisations, in Greek,
   Roman and feudal forms; kings centralising power, then
   the idea of the nation turning subjects into citizens
   of national states; the state's claim to a monopoly of
   force, and the question of when that force is law and
   when it is violence against its own people; and the
   imperialist state — capitalist states carving the world
   into empires, whose rivalry brought the order to crisis
   and world war. 4 lessons, in two titles:
   `ancient-world/` (1 lesson) and `modern-world/`
   (3 lessons).

4. **Domination and Resistance** — the book's Unit IV,
   the whole story retold from underneath: women,
   plebeians and slaves resisting under Greek and Roman
   glory; feudal Europe's lowest — peasants, artisans,
   women, heretics and the sick — answering lordship and
   the Church with revolts and forbidden beliefs; colonised
   peoples, enslaved Africans and the new working classes
   resisting the modern West; factory workers answering
   the new discipline with machine-breaking, unions and
   strikes; and contemporary Brazil's own movements —
   students, workers, women, Black and landless movements
   contesting power in the pupils' own lifetime.
   5 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/` (2 lessons)
   and `modern-world/` (3 lessons).

**Totals: 14 lessons** (Level 1: 2 · Level 2: 3 · Level 3: 4
· Level 4: 5 — 16 placements, all from one student book).
