# History — Brazil course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Brazil's
own — built from Brazil's Grade 10 / Ensino Médio
*História — Livro Didático Público* student book
(SEED-PR, Portuguese). Inside the course the organisation
is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below sit inside
Levels 1–4, in path order — what the learner proves, step
by step. The grade the book comes from lives only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: not chronology but
three relations — work, power and culture — walked in the
book's four units: work first (labour has a history, from
slavery to the wage), then cities (industry pulls people
in), then the state and power, and last the whole story
retold from underneath, as domination and resistance. That
ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead"
hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — work: labour has a history** (História
SEED-PR, Unit I), in `modern-world/`:

- Capitalism: Private Ownership, Factories and New
  Classes (3 sub lessons — the concept of work and the
  world of work in different societies; the construction
  of wage labour; work in contemporary society) · Brazil:
  From Slavery to Free Labour (two slave societies end
  slavery differently — Brazil by gradual laws to
  abolition in 1888 without land or rights for the freed,
  the United States through civil war — and both leave
  the freed to compete as wage workers).

**Level 2 — cities: industry pulls people in** (Unit II),
in `modern-world/`:

- Brazil: Urbanisation and Industrialisation (from the
  first cities to Brazil's explosive 20th-century urban
  growth) · Effects of Industrialisation: Cities, Classes
  and Empires (the industrial city remakes class, family
  and daily life in the 1800s; crowding, pollution and
  segregation traced into today's cities) · Brazil:
  Paraná Urbanisation and the Port of Paranaguá (Paraná's
  towns industrialise; its port ships the state's produce
  into the capitalist world market).

**Level 3 — the state and power** (Unit III), in
`ancient-world/` and `modern-world/`:

- `ancient-world/` — Ancient Civilisations: Rome (the
  state born with the first civilisations, in Greek,
  Roman and feudal forms — city, empire and lordship).
- `modern-world/` — Nationalism: The Feeling That Made
  Nation-States (kings centralise power; the idea of the
  nation turns subjects into citizens) · Brazil: State
  Power and Violence (the state claims a monopoly of
  force; when is it law, and when violence against its
  own people?) · Imperialism and Its Crisis (capitalist
  states carve the world into empires; rivalry brings
  the order to crisis and world war).

**Level 4 — domination and resistance** (Unit IV), in
`ancient-world/` and `modern-world/`:

- `ancient-world/` — Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides
  (women, plebeians and slaves under Greek and Roman
  glory — resistance begins with the first states) ·
  Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism (peasants,
  artisans, women, heretics and the sick answering
  lordship and the Church with revolts and forbidden
  beliefs).
- `modern-world/` — Domination and Resistance in the
  Modern West (colonised peoples, enslaved Africans and
  the new working classes resist the modern West) · The
  Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England
  First (factory workers answer the new discipline with
  machine-breaking, unions and strikes — the labour
  movement is born) · Brazil: Contemporary Social
  Movements (from 1968's "is it forbidden to forbid?" to
  Brazil's students, workers, women and Black and
  landless movements).
