# Guatemala from Peace to the Present

## Do it first

Write the names of the presidents since the peace in order. Beside the list, write the two measures the book judges them all by: the hurricanes, and the unfinished promises of 1996. Judge the next president you hear of by the same two measures.

The book's last history chapter is the present itself. Álvaro Arzú, the president who signed the peace, also privatised the state firms — telephones, electricity — remaking the economy in the same years. Then came Portillo, Berger, Colom and Pérez Molina, and the book measures each by the same two scales. The first is nature: hurricanes Mitch, Stan and Agatha struck in turn, and each government was judged by how it answered flooded valleys and buried villages. The second scale is 1996 itself: the promises of the Peace Accords — on land, on indigenous rights, on justice — still unfinished, still waiting. Guatemala's story, the book ends by saying, is not over; its latest chapter is the reader's own time.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Since the peace, Arzú signed the accords and privatised state firms, and each successor — Portillo, Berger, Colom, Pérez Molina — has been measured against the hurricanes Mitch, Stan and Agatha and against the still-unfinished promises of 1996. Give one real example from your own life — hurricane season itself — Mitch, Stan and Agatha are the storms by which whole Guatemalan generations date their lives — and name the lesson's key words: Guatemala from Peace to the Present, Guatemala from 1996 to the present, the Peace Accords' promises. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
