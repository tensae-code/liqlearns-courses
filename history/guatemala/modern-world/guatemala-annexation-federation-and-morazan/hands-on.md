# Hands-on — Guatemala: Annexation, Federation and Morazán

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a federation: five small states inside one big outline. Write one thing the big outline should decide for all, and one thing each small state should keep for itself — the argument that broke Central America's Federation starts exactly there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Free of Spain, Central America was annexed to Iturbide's Mexico in 1822, declared absolute independence when he fell, abolished slavery in 1824 — and then built a Federation whose liberal reforms under Morazán and Gálvez broke against cholera and Rafael Carrera's Montaña rebellion in 1838. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
