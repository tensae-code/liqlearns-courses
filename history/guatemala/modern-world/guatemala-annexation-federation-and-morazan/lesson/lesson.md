# Guatemala: Annexation, Federation and Morazán

## Do it first

Draw a federation: five small states inside one big outline. Write one thing the big outline should decide for all, and one thing each small state should keep for itself — the argument that broke Central America's Federation starts exactly there.

Independence lasted months before the new provinces were annexed, in 1822, to Iturbide's Mexican empire. When Iturbide fell, Central America declared absolute independence — losing Chiapas and Soconusco to Mexico — and built itself anew as the Federal Republic of Central America. The young federation did one great thing early: in 1824 it abolished slavery. Under President Morazán and Guatemala's governor Gálvez, liberals pushed reform — education, law, the Church's privileges cut back. Then cholera struck, and frightened villagers were told the government had poisoned the wells. From the Montaña rose Rafael Carrera at the head of a rebellion of the fearful and the dispossessed — and in 1838 the Federation broke apart. Guatemala's separate story begins in its ruins.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Free of Spain, Central America was annexed to Iturbide's Mexico in 1822, declared absolute independence when he fell, abolished slavery in 1824 — and then built a Federation whose liberal reforms under Morazán and Gálvez broke against cholera and Rafael Carrera's Montaña rebellion in 1838. Give one real example from your own life — the abolition of slavery in 1824 — Central America ended it decades before many richer nations, a fact this lesson carries — and name the lesson's key words: Guatemala: Annexation, Federation and Morazán, Annexation to Mexico and the Federal Republic of Central America, Federation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
