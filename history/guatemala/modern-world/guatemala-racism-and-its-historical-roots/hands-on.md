# Hands-on — Guatemala: Racism and Its Historical Roots

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down one everyday phrase or act that treats a person as lesser because of their people or dress. Then write what the Velásquez Nimatuj case proved could be done about it: name it, document it, and carry it to law.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Racism in Guatemala is a relation of domination built across history — peoples told apart so their goods could be stripped — and it is still lived daily by the Maya peoples: in 2002 the anthropologist Irma Alicia Velásquez Nimatuj, in gala traditional dress, was turned away from a Guatemala City restaurant while her ladina companions were let in, and her case became a landmark. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
