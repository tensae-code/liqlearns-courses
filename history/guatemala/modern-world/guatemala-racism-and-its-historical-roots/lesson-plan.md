# Lesson plan — Guatemala: Racism and Its Historical Roots

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala: Racism and Its Historical Roots
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Reform, Revolution, War and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Racism in Guatemala is a relation of domination built across history — peoples told apart so their goods could be stripped — and it is still lived daily by the Maya peoples: in 2002 the anthropologist Irma Alicia Velásquez Nimatuj, in gala traditional dress, was turned away from a Guatemala City restaurant while her ladina companions were let in, and her case became a landmark.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Reform, Revolution, War and Peace (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The 2002 restaurant exclusion of Dr Irma Alicia Velásquez Nimatuj** — A Maya K'iche' anthropologist in gala traditional dress is turned away from a Guatemala City restaurant while ladina companions are let in; her case becomes a landmark in the national fight against racism.
2. **The content sheet "El racismo escolar"** — Racism is defined as a relation of domination built on telling peoples apart to strip their goods, its prejudices formed across history; in Guatemala the Maya peoples meet it daily — in schools, hospitals, buses and government — in everyday phrases the guide lists and names as racist.
