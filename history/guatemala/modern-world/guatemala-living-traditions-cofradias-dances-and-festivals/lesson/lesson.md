# Guatemala's Living Traditions: Cofradías, Dances and Festivals

## Do it first

Name one feast or dance in your town and write down what story it performs — whose saint, whose history, whose costume. If you do not know, ask an elder: that asking is this lesson's method.

Some history is not written; it is danced. In the colonial centuries, the Church organised the cofradías — brotherhoods charged with keeping each saint's feast: raising the money, carrying the image, feeding the town. The empire fell; the brotherhoods never stopped. Around their feasts, each people built its celebration: traditional dances in costume that act out old encounters and old stories, ladino festivities, carnival, the jaripeo riders, the mengalas of Amatitlán in their finery — and everywhere the Guatemalan corrido, the sung ballad that turns a person's deeds, a love or a disaster into verses a whole town can remember. To attend a Guatemalan feast is to watch its history performed by its own people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Guatemala's living traditions are its history performed: colonial cofradías — brotherhoods — still keep the saints' feasts alive, and each people turns its own past into dance, costume and the sung corrido ballads of its festivals, from carnival and jaripeo to the mengalas of Amatitlán. Give one real example from your own life — your own town's feria — the feast the cofradías still organise, exactly as this lesson describes — and name the lesson's key words: Guatemala's Living Traditions: Cofradías, Dances and Festivals, Cofradías, dances and festivities, cofradías. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
