# Hands-on — Guatemala's Living Traditions: Cofradías, Dances and Festivals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one feast or dance in your town and write down what story it performs — whose saint, whose history, whose costume. If you do not know, ask an elder: that asking is this lesson's method.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Guatemala's living traditions are its history performed: colonial cofradías — brotherhoods — still keep the saints' feasts alive, and each people turns its own past into dance, costume and the sung corrido ballads of its festivals, from carnival and jaripeo to the mengalas of Amatitlán. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
