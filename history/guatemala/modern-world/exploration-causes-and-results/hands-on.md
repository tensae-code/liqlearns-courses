# Hands-on — Exploration: Causes and Results

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing your family buys that comes from far away. Write down what would happen to its price if the only road it travels was blocked — that is Europe's problem in the 1400s.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Europe went looking for a new road east because its world had turned: Copernicus and Galileo moved the Sun to the centre, Gutenberg's press spread the new maps, and the Turks' capture of Constantinople blocked the old spice road — another way had to be found. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
