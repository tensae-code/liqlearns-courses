# Hands-on — Central America: Foreign Intervention and the Banana Companies

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down what your town would lose if it paid a foreign builder not in money but in land along the new road. That payment — land for rails — is the contract this lesson is about.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Foreign intervention came to Central America by contract: cash-poor republics paid a United States railway builder in land, the Soto-Keith contract became the model, and the United Fruit Company ended up holding whole regions — Izabal and Tiquisate — inside Guatemala. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
