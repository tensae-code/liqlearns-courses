# Hands-on — Guatemala's Four Peoples: Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the names of the four peoples of Guatemala. Beside each one, write one thing that people carries — a language, a place, a way of learning — that the others do not.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: One country, four peoples: the Maya, rooted before the conquest; the Garífuna, descended from Africans brought as slaves; the Xinka, traders of the south coast; and the Ladino, born of colonial mixing — each with its own way of knowing, and all recognised together by the Peace Accords. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
