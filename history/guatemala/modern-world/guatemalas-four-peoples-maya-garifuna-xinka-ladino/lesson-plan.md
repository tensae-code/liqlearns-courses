# Lesson plan — Guatemala's Four Peoples: Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala's Four Peoples: Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Conquest, Colony and Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** One country, four peoples: the Maya, rooted before the conquest; the Garífuna, descended from Africans brought as slaves; the Xinka, traders of the south coast; and the Ladino, born of colonial mixing — each with its own way of knowing, and all recognised together by the Peace Accords.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The four peoples, their cosmovisions and the linguistic map** — Four peoples share one country — Maya rooted before the conquest, Garífuna descended from Africans brought as slaves, Xinka traders of the south coast, Ladino born of colonial mixing — and the Peace Accords recognise them all.
2. **Four ways of knowing (second cycle of primary)** — Four peoples, four ways of knowing — Xinka learning centred on the Moon and community, Maya gratitude and the value of work (mitijul) handed down from the first four couples, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors, Ladino identity still being built.
3. **A society of many peoples (Grade 5 activities)** — A lottery game and a family tree show one classroom holding many peoples and 20+ languages, all descended from shared roots.
4. **Four ways of knowing (primero básico)** — Xinka learning is centred on the Moon and the community, with women forming the youngest children; Maya gratitude is traced to the first four couples of creation, and work done with diligence (mitijul) is what keeps violence away; Garífuna learning flows from the sea, the ancestors and the Hiyuruwa.
5. **Four ways of knowing (segundo básico)** — The same ancestral foundations are restated for older pupils — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors.
6. **Four ways of knowing (tercero básico)** — The same ancestral foundations are restated for the oldest básico pupils — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors.
