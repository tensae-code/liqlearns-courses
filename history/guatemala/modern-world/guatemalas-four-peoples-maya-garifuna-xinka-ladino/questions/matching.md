# Guatemala's Four Peoples: Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino — matching

Match each part with its meaning. Every item is marked with its sub lesson.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) The four peoples, their cosmovisions and the linguistic map — a)

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Four ways of knowing (second cycle of primary) — b)

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A society of many peoples (Grade 5 activities) — c)

**4.** (Sub lesson 4) Four ways of knowing (primero básico) — d)

**5.** (Sub lesson 5) Four ways of knowing (segundo básico) — e)

**6.** (Sub lesson 6) Four ways of knowing (tercero básico) — f)


Meanings:
a) Four peoples share one country — Maya rooted before the conquest, Garífuna descended from Africans brought as slaves, Xinka traders of the south coast, Ladino born of colonial mixing — and the Peace Accords recognise them all.
b) Four peoples, four ways of knowing — Xinka learning centred on the Moon and community, Maya gratitude and the value of work (mitijul) handed down from the first four couples, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors, Ladino identity still being built.
c) A lottery game and a family tree show one classroom holding many peoples and 20+ languages, all descended from shared roots.
d) Xinka learning is centred on the Moon and the community, with women forming the youngest children; Maya gratitude is traced to the first four couples of creation, and work done with diligence (mitijul) is what keeps violence away; Garífuna learning flows from the sea, the ancestors and the Hiyuruwa.
e) The same ancestral foundations are restated for older pupils — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors.
f) The same ancestral foundations are restated for the oldest básico pupils — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors.

**Answer key:** 1. a; 2. b; 3. c; 4. d; 5. e; 6. f
