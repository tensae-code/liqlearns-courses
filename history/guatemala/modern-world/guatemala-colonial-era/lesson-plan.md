# Lesson plan — Guatemala's Colonial Era

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala's Colonial Era
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Conquest, Colony and Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colonial Guatemala was built four times over: a capital founded at Iximche' in 1524 that fled revolt, drowning and earthquakes to its present valley; a society stacked in a pyramid held down by slavery, encomienda and the weekly repartimiento labour draft; and an economy of cacao, cotton and indigo whose cacao beans passed as coins until the mint of 1733.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The four foundings of Santiago de Guatemala** — Founded at Iximche' in 1524, the capital flees Kaqchikel revolt to Almolonga, is drowned by Volcán de Agua mud in 1541, rebuilt at Panchoy for 230 years, then the Santa Marta earthquakes of 1773 drive it to the Ermita valley as Nueva Guatemala.
2. **Colonial society: the pyramid and the labour drafts** — Captured indigenous people are enslaved and wash gold; the 1542 New Laws abolish slavery but tribute and the weekly repartimiento labour draft take its place under peninsulares, criollos, castas, indigenous and Black slaves.
3. **Colonial economy, money and learning** — Cacao, cotton and indigo pay the colony's way, cacao beans still pass as coins until the 1733 mint; friars teach reading to teach the faith, the first press arrives in 1660 and San Carlos University in 1676 — women may only attend primary school.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the capital was always where it stands → it moved four times in 250 years, driven by revolt, a volcanic mudslide and earthquakes; and thinks colonial labour was free → slavery was replaced on paper by tribute and the repartimiento draft, which took the labour anyway.
