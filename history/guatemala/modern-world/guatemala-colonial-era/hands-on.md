# Hands-on — Guatemala's Colonial Era

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the four places Santiago de Guatemala stood, in order, and beside each one the disaster or danger that drove it to move. A capital that moved four times is telling you its history — read it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Colonial Guatemala was built four times over: a capital founded at Iximche' in 1524 that fled revolt, drowning and earthquakes to its present valley; a society stacked in a pyramid held down by slavery, encomienda and the weekly repartimiento labour draft; and an economy of cacao, cotton and indigo whose cacao beans passed as coins until the mint of 1733. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
