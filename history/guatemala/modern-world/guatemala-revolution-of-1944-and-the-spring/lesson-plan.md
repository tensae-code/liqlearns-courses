# Lesson plan — Guatemala's Revolution of 1944 and the Spring

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala's Revolution of 1944 and the Spring
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Reform, Revolution, War and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** On 20 October 1944 students, teachers and young officers overthrew Ubico's successor and opened Guatemala's Spring: Arévalo built schools, a labour code and social security, and Arbenz's Decree 900 land reform touched United Fruit's land — until in 1954 the army, backed by the United States, overthrew him, and Castillo Armas and Ydígoras Fuentes closed the political space until it bred rebellion.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Revolution of 1944 and the governments of Arévalo and Arbenz (1945–1954)** — Students, teachers and young officers overthrow Ubico's successor on 20 October 1944; Arévalo builds schools, a labour code and social security; Arbenz's Decree 900 land reform touches United Fruit land — and in 1954 the army, backed by the United States, overthrows him.
2. **The governments of Castillo Armas and Ydígoras Fuentes** — Castillo Armas annuls the 1945 constitution, returns the expropriated land and bans unions before his 1957 assassination; under Ydígoras the closed political space breeds the MR-13 rebels of 1962, seed of the guerrilla.
