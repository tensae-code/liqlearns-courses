# Hands-on — Guatemala's Revolution of 1944 and the Spring

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the date 20 October 1944 and list who made that day: students, teachers, young officers. Then write what a 'spring' means after a long winter of dictatorship — the book's own metaphor for the decade that followed.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: On 20 October 1944 students, teachers and young officers overthrew Ubico's successor and opened Guatemala's Spring: Arévalo built schools, a labour code and social security, and Arbenz's Decree 900 land reform touched United Fruit's land — until in 1954 the army, backed by the United States, overthrew him, and Castillo Armas and Ydígoras Fuentes closed the political space until it bred rebellion. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
