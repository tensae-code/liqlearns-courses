# Guatemala's Revolution of 1944 and the Spring

## Do it first

Write the date 20 October 1944 and list who made that day: students, teachers, young officers. Then write what a 'spring' means after a long winter of dictatorship — the book's own metaphor for the decade that followed.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Revolution of 1944 and the governments of Arévalo and Arbenz (1945–1954)

The protests that felled Ubico did not stop with him. On 20 October 1944, students, teachers and young army officers rose together and overthrew his successor — the October Revolution. What followed is remembered as the Spring: ten years of open politics after decades of dictatorship. President Arévalo built schools across the country, gave workers a labour code and founded social security. His successor Arbenz went further: Decree 900, his land reform, broke up great idle estates to give land to the landless — and some of that land belonged to the United Fruit Company. In 1954 the army, backed by the United States, overthrew him. The Spring ended in a coup, and Guatemala's long winter began.

## Sub lesson 2 — The governments of Castillo Armas and Ydígoras Fuentes

The man the coup installed, Carlos Castillo Armas, undid the Spring piece by piece: the 1945 constitution annulled, the expropriated land returned, the unions banned. In 1957 he was assassinated. Under his successor Ydígoras Fuentes the political space stayed closed — no legal road left for change. And a closed space breeds its own answer: in 1962 young officers rose as the MR-13 rebels. They were defeated as soldiers, but their revolt was the seed of something larger — the guerrilla movements of the armed conflict that fills the next lesson.
