# Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

## Do it first

Draw the route you would sail if every road east from your town were blocked and you believed sailing west would reach the same place. Mark where you would actually arrive — that gap between belief and landfall is Columbus's story.

## Sub lesson 1 — The discovery of America: Columbus's four voyages

Columbus had a plan: sail west across the ocean and arrive in Asia from the other side. Portugal refused him. So he sold the plan to Isabel and Fernando of Spain — and on 12 October 1492 his lookout, Rodrigo de Triana, sighted land. It was not Asia, but Columbus never accepted that: through four voyages — the last tracing the coast from Honduras to Panama, close along Guatemala's own sea — he died still calling the lands he reached Asia. The man who changed the world's map never knew what he had found.

## Sub lesson 2 — Other discoveries and the Capitulación

Others saw clearer. Balboa crossed the narrow land and stood before a second ocean, the Pacific — proof this was no edge of Asia. Vespucci argued the point in writing: this was a new continent, and the mapmakers named it for him — America. Then the book shows the machine that turned discovery into conquest: the capitulación. A would-be conqueror signed a contract with the king: his own money and his own swords, spent at his own risk — and in return, land, titles and a share of the riches if he succeeded. Conquest, in this system, was a business venture with a royal signature.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Columbus, refused by Portugal, sold his westward plan to Isabel and Fernando and reached a land he died calling Asia; Balboa then reached the Pacific, Vespucci argued this was a new continent and lent it his name — America — and the capitulación system turned conquest into a signed business of money, swords, land and titles. Give one real example from your own life — the name America itself — spoken every day, it is Vespucci's first name carried on a continent — and name the lesson's key words: Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan, The discovery of America, Other discoveries and the Capitulación, America. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
