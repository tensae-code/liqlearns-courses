# Hands-on — Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw the route you would sail if every road east from your town were blocked and you believed sailing west would reach the same place. Mark where you would actually arrive — that gap between belief and landfall is Columbus's story.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Columbus, refused by Portugal, sold his westward plan to Isabel and Fernando and reached a land he died calling Asia; Balboa then reached the Pacific, Vespucci argued this was a new continent and lent it his name — America — and the capitulación system turned conquest into a signed business of money, swords, land and titles. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
