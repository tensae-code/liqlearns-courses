# Hands-on — Guatemala's Ancestral Authorities and Maya Justice

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a wrong done in your school or street — a theft, a fight. Write two endings: one where the wrongdoer is only punished, and one where the harm is repaired and balance restored. Which ending leaves the community whole?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Where the state court punishes, Guatemala's ancestral authorities gather the community to repair the harm and restore balance — Maya justice is restorative, not punitive, and the guide insists that violence or lynching is not Maya justice at all. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
