# Guatemala's Independence: Causes and Proclamation

## Do it first

Write two lists: things happening far away that could shake your town, and things wrong inside your town that make people want change. Independence needed both lists — check yours against the book's.

## Sub lesson 1 — The causes of independence

Empires fall from two directions at once, and the book separates them. From outside: the United States broke from Britain and stood as proof it could be done; the French Revolution proclaimed liberty, equality and fraternity and unmade a king; and Napoleon seized Spain itself, leaving the empire headless. From inside: the criollos, American-born Spaniards, were shut out of the high offices of their own land; Spain's trade monopoly strangled colonial merchants; and war taxes drained the kingdom for Spain's European wars. Shaken from outside, rotted from inside — the only question left was when, and who would move first.

## Sub lesson 2 — The independence of Central America

The decade before the Act was not quiet. In 1811 Delgado raised San Salvador in the first rising. In 1813 the Belén conspiracy was uncovered in the capital and its plotters punished. In 1820 Atanasio Tzul led an indigenous rebellion against the tribute itself. In the newspapers, liberals and conservatives argued the future in rival columns. And when Spain's crisis finally opened the door, it was a Honduran thinker in Guatemala City, José Cecilio del Valle, who drafted the words: the Act of Independence, signed on 15 September 1821. Central America was independent — by a pen, after a decade of risings had written the argument in deeds.
