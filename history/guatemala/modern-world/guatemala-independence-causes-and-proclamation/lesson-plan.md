# Lesson plan — Guatemala's Independence: Causes and Proclamation

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala's Independence: Causes and Proclamation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Conquest, Colony and Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Independence came from pressure outside and rot inside: the United States' independence, the French Revolution's liberty-equality-fraternity and Napoleon's seizure of Spain shook the empire, while criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes rotted it — until, after a decade of risings and conspiracies, José Cecilio del Valle drafted the Act signed on 15 September 1821.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Conquest, Colony and Independence (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The causes of independence** — US independence, the French Revolution's liberty-equality-fraternity and Napoleon's seizure of Spain shake the empire from outside; criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes rot it from inside.
2. **The independence of Central America** — Delgado raises San Salvador in 1811, the Belén conspiracy is uncovered in 1813, Atanasio Tzul rebels against tribute in 1820; liberals and conservatives argue in rival newspapers until José Cecilio del Valle drafts the Act signed on 15 September 1821.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks independence was granted from Spain in a single day → it was argued for a decade in rival newspapers, preceded by risings in 1811, 1813 and 1820, and drafted by a Central American, del Valle, when Spain's own crisis opened the door.
- Go back to the one thing: Independence came from pressure outside and rot inside: the United States' independence, the French Revolution's liberty-equality-fraternity and Napoleon's seizure of Spain shook the empire, while criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes rotted it — until, after a decade of risings and conspiracies, José Cecilio del Valle drafted the Act signed on 15 September 1821. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
