# Hands-on — Guatemala's Independence: Causes and Proclamation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write two lists: things happening far away that could shake your town, and things wrong inside your town that make people want change. Independence needed both lists — check yours against the book's.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Independence came from pressure outside and rot inside: the United States' independence, the French Revolution's liberty-equality-fraternity and Napoleon's seizure of Spain shook the empire, while criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes rotted it — until, after a decade of risings and conspiracies, José Cecilio del Valle drafted the Act signed on 15 September 1821. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
