# Hands-on — Belize and Guatemala's Territorial Claim

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find Belize on a map and shade the coast the logwood cutters spread over. Write one sentence for Guatemala's claim and one for Belize's answer — then write why both sides agreed in 2008 to let a court decide.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Belize began with English logwood cutters spreading over a coast Guatemala claims as its own; Belize won independence from Britain in 1981 without settling the claim, and in 2008 both countries accepted taking the dispute to a court — a quarrel of centuries handed to judges, not armies. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
