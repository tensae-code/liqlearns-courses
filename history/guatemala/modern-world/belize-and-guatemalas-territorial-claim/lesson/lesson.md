# Belize and Guatemala's Territorial Claim

## Do it first

Find Belize on a map and shade the coast the logwood cutters spread over. Write one sentence for Guatemala's claim and one for Belize's answer — then write why both sides agreed in 2008 to let a court decide.

On Guatemala's Caribbean coast stood forests of logwood, a tree whose heartwood made precious dyes. English cutters came for the wood and stayed, spreading their camps along the rivers of what is now Belize — on land Guatemala has always claimed as its own, inherited from Spain. The camps became a British colony. In 1981 Belize won independence from Britain — but independence settled nothing between Belize and Guatemala, whose claim stood as before. So the two countries chose the modern road: in 2008 both accepted taking the dispute to an international court, to be decided by law. A quarrel born in the age of logwood and empire awaits a judge's answer.
