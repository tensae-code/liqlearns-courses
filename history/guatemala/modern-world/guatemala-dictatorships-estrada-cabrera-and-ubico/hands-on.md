# Hands-on — Guatemala's Dictatorships: Estrada Cabrera and Ubico

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down one thing each dictator built that people could see — a palace, a road, a school festival — and one thing each took that people could feel. Dictatorships always show the first list and hide the second.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Two dictatorships bracket Guatemala's early twentieth century: Estrada Cabrera (1898–1920), self-styled protector of studious youth, ruling through eruption, earthquakes and flu until the Assembly declared him insane; and Ubico (1931–1944), who built palaces and roads on forced labour until the street protests of June 1944 brought him down. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
