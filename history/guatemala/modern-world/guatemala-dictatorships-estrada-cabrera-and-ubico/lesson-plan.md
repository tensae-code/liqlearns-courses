# Lesson plan — Guatemala's Dictatorships: Estrada Cabrera and Ubico

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala's Dictatorships: Estrada Cabrera and Ubico
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Reform, Revolution, War and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Two dictatorships bracket Guatemala's early twentieth century: Estrada Cabrera (1898–1920), self-styled protector of studious youth, ruling through eruption, earthquakes and flu until the Assembly declared him insane; and Ubico (1931–1944), who built palaces and roads on forced labour until the street protests of June 1944 brought him down.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Reform, Revolution, War and Peace (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks dictators fall only to armies → Estrada Cabrera was removed by his own Assembly declaring him insane, and Ubico by unarmed street protest — the book shows institutions and crowds, not generals, ending both rules.
- Go back to the one thing: Two dictatorships bracket Guatemala's early twentieth century: Estrada Cabrera (1898–1920), self-styled protector of studious youth, ruling through eruption, earthquakes and flu until the Assembly declared him insane; and Ubico (1931–1944), who built palaces and roads on forced labour until the street protests of June 1944 brought him down.
