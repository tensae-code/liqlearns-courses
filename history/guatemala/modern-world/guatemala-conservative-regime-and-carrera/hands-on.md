# Hands-on — Guatemala's Conservative Regime and Carrera

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down what a caudillo is in your own words: a leader whose power is himself. Then write one thing such a leader can do quickly that a parliament cannot — and one danger that comes with it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Rafael Carrera, the caudillo from the Montaña, ruled Guatemala for thirty conservative years: he founded the Republic of Guatemala in 1847, crushed the Los Altos separatists, and sent his troops to expel William Walker's filibusters from Nicaragua — a Guatemalan strongman defending all Central America. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
