# Guatemala's Conservative Regime and Carrera

## Do it first

Write down what a caudillo is in your own words: a leader whose power is himself. Then write one thing such a leader can do quickly that a parliament cannot — and one danger that comes with it.

Out of the Federation's ruins rose one man: Rafael Carrera, a caudillo from the Montaña whose rebellion had broken the liberals and who now ruled in their place. His thirty years — the conservative regime — reversed the liberal reforms and restored the Church, but Carrera was also a founder: in 1847 he proclaimed the Republic of Guatemala, a state of its own at last. When the western highlands tried to leave as the separate state of Los Altos, he crushed them and kept the country whole. And when the American filibuster William Walker seized Nicaragua with a private army, Carrera sent Guatemalan troops south to help expel him. The mountain caudillo had become, in his own way, Central America's defender.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rafael Carrera, the caudillo from the Montaña, ruled Guatemala for thirty conservative years: he founded the Republic of Guatemala in 1847, crushed the Los Altos separatists, and sent his troops to expel William Walker's filibusters from Nicaragua — a Guatemalan strongman defending all Central America. Give one real example from your own life — Los Altos and its capital Quetzaltenango — the separatist state Carrera crushed is still Guatemala's second city region today — and name the lesson's key words: Guatemala's Conservative Regime and Carrera, The conservative regime of the thirty years, Rafael Carrera. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
