# Hands-on — Guatemala's Conquest and Tecún Umán

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down the names of two groups in your own region that once failed to unite against a common danger. Then write what the Lienzo's weavers did that a Spanish letter could not do.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Guatemala's conquest came in two stages: Alvarado's army and smallpox advancing together against highland lords who failed to unite — Tecún Umán falling at El Pinar, Q'umarkaj burning, Santiago founded at Iximche' — then the betrayed Kaqchikel rising, and Jorge de Alvarado's second campaign, painted on cloth by his Quauhquechollan allies in the Lienzo de Quauhquechollan. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
