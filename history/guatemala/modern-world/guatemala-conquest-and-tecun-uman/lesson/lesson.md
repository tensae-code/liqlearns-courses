# Guatemala's Conquest and Tecún Umán

## Do it first

Write down the names of two groups in your own region that once failed to unite against a common danger. Then write what the Lienzo's weavers did that a Spanish letter could not do.

## Sub lesson 1 — The conquest of Guatemala, first stage

The highland lords were warned. Envoys from Moctezuma, the Mexica ruler already fallen, told them what was coming — but the K'iche', Kaqchikel and their neighbours, rivals for generations, still failed to unite. So Pedro de Alvarado advanced with two armies: his soldiers, and smallpox, the disease that raced ahead of them and emptied villages before a sword was drawn. At El Pinar the K'iche' captain Tecún Umán fell; Q'umarkaj, the K'iche' capital, burned. On the Kaqchikel capital Iximche', Alvarado founded Santiago — the first Spanish capital of Guatemala, planted in a conquered city.

## Sub lesson 2 — The second stage of the conquest

The conquest's second stage began in betrayal. The Kaqchikel had allied with the Spanish against their K'iche' rivals — and, betrayed as allies, they rose against the conquerors themselves. Jorge de Alvarado, Pedro's brother, then retraced the conquest route with allies of his own: warriors of Quauhquechollan. And here the book sets a source-work exercise, because those allies left a record no Spaniard wrote: the Lienzo de Quauhquechollan, a great cloth painting in which Quauhquechollan weavers painted the whole campaign — their campaign. Set beside Jorge's own letter, the lienzo lets pupils read the conquest in two hands: the conqueror's words, and his allies' pictures.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Guatemala's conquest came in two stages: Alvarado's army and smallpox advancing together against highland lords who failed to unite — Tecún Umán falling at El Pinar, Q'umarkaj burning, Santiago founded at Iximche' — then the betrayed Kaqchikel rising, and Jorge de Alvarado's second campaign, painted on cloth by his Quauhquechollan allies in the Lienzo de Quauhquechollan. Give one real example from your own life — Tecún Umán himself — Guatemala's national hero, whose fall at El Pinar every Guatemalan schoolchild learns — and name the lesson's key words: Guatemala's Conquest and Tecún Umán, The conquest of Guatemala, first stage, The second stage of the conquest, Tecún Umán. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
