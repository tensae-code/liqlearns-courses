# Central America's Armed Conflicts: El Salvador and Nicaragua

## Do it first

Write the three countries and their wars in a row — Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua — and under each, how its war ended: a peace accord, an election, a signed peace. The endings differ; that difference is the lesson.

Guatemala's war burned inside a burning region. In El Salvador, the FMLN guerrillas fought the state for twelve years, until both sides signed peace at Chapultepec in 1992. In Nicaragua the story ran the other way: in 1979 the Sandinistas toppled the Somoza family dynasty that had ruled for decades — then themselves fought a long war against the Contras, until in 1990 the exhausted country voted, and the Sandinistas lost the election to Violeta Chamorro and handed over power. Three countries, three wars of the same generation: one ended by election, one by accord — and Guatemala's, the longest, still had years to run.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Guatemala's war was not alone: El Salvador's FMLN war ended at Chapultepec in 1992, and Nicaragua's Sandinistas toppled the Somoza dynasty in 1979, fought the Contras, and lost the 1990 election to Violeta Chamorro — three neighbouring wars, three different endings. Give one real example from your own life — Chapultepec itself — the castle in Mexico City where El Salvador's peace was signed in 1992, four years before Guatemala's — and name the lesson's key words: Central America's Armed Conflicts: El Salvador and Nicaragua, Armed conflicts in Central America, Chapultepec. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
