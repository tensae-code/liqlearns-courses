# Guatemala's Liberal Reform of 1871

## Do it first

List three things that changed Guatemala fastest in the Reform — a railway, a telegraph, a school. Write which one you would have built first, and why. Barrios's Guatemala had to choose under pressure, exactly as you do.

In 1871 the liberals came back — with an army. García Granados and Justo Rufino Barrios invaded from Mexico and won on 30 June 1871, ending the conservative thirty years. Barrios, who soon ruled alone, rebuilt the country at a run. He stripped the Church of its lands and privileges, ordered free, secular schooling for every child, and dragged Guatemala into the modern age: railways across the valleys, the telegraph wire beside them, and coffee — planted on a vast scale as the nation's new wealth. But Barrios dreamed bigger than Guatemala: in 1885 he marched to reunite all Central America by force, and died in the attempt at Chalchuapa. The reformer fell; the reformed country — its rails, wires, schools and coffee — remained.
