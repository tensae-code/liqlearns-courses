# Hands-on — Guatemala's Liberal Reform of 1871

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three things that changed Guatemala fastest in the Reform — a railway, a telegraph, a school. Write which one you would have built first, and why. Barrios's Guatemala had to choose under pressure, exactly as you do.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Liberal Reform of 1871, won by García Granados and Justo Rufino Barrios invading from Mexico, rebuilt Guatemala in a generation: the Church stripped, free secular schooling ordered, railway, telegraph and coffee planted across the country — until Barrios died at Chalchuapa in 1885 trying to reunite Central America by force. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
