# History — Guatemala course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Guatemala inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Guatemala's book in hand teaches the
national story as one continuous timeline, unit by unit, and
the ladder follows that timeline's own eras as the Grade 4
student book presents them: the method first — history found in
your own locality, evidence weighed, heritage named (Level 1);
then the deep past — the first peoples, Mesoamerica and the
Maya, with the Postclassic kingdoms and one ancient-world sheet
from the básico guides (Level 2); then the making of the modern
country — the four peoples named, Europe's push outward,
conquest, colony, living traditions, independence, federation
and the conservative thirty years (Level 3); and last reform,
dictatorship, revolution, war and peace, closing with the
living questions the básico guides and the primary guide carry
— Maya justice and racism's historical roots (Level 4). The
four teacher guides do not make levels of their own: their
parts deepen the lessons where the story reaches them, and the
three lessons that stand on guides alone sit at the end of the
levels their topics belong to, labelled as evidence only. The
ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana books of the
Ministerio de Educación de Guatemala — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book contents
map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps → History Where You Live** — history begins
   at home: Tikal's two photographs and the question "how has
   my community changed in fifty years", the grandparents
   interviewed as the first historians; Tecún Umán weighed as
   evidence — a captain proven, a duel left open; and heritage
   named — the four peoples' art and feasts, the marimba, the
   Maya cosmovision round the family fire, and the values of
   three peoples set against violence. 3 lessons, in one
   title: `first-steps/` (3 lessons).

2. **Ancient World → First Peoples and the Maya** — hunters
   cross the Bering Strait and become farmers of maize, beans
   and chile; maize and nixtamal build Mesoamerica's shared
   culture; the Maya rise — Olmec neighbours and Preclassic
   farmers, the Classic city-states with their calendars and
   zero, and the twenty nawales still read today; the
   Postclassic highland kingdoms and their own books; and one
   sheet on Sparta and the ancient world's treatment of
   disability, from the segundo básico guide. 5 lessons, in
   one title: `ancient-world/` (5 lessons).

3. **Modern World → Conquest, Colony and Independence** —
   the four peoples named together, each with its own way of
   knowing; Europe's turn — new science, printed maps, a
   blocked spice road — and Columbus, Balboa, Vespucci and
   the capitulación; the conquest in two stages and the
   Lienzo de Quauhquechollan; the colonial era — a capital
   moved four times, the social pyramid and its labour
   drafts, cacao money and the first university; the living
   traditions of cofradías, dances and corridos;
   independence's causes and the Act of 15 September 1821;
   annexation, the Federation and Morazán; and Carrera's
   conservative thirty years. 9 lessons, in one title:
   `modern-world/` (9 lessons).

4. **Modern World → Reform, Revolution, War and Peace** —
   the Liberal Reform of 1871 and its rails, wires and
   coffee; Estrada Cabrera and Ubico; the banana companies'
   intervention by contract; Belize and the claim taken to a
   court; the Revolution of 1944 and the Spring, and the
   closure that bred rebellion; the internal armed conflict
   and Central America's wars; the peace process and the
   Firm and Lasting Peace of 29 December 1996; Guatemala
   from peace to the present; and the two living questions —
   the ancestral authorities and Maya justice, and racism
   and its historical roots. 11 lessons, in one title:
   `modern-world/` (11 lessons).

**Totals: 28 lessons** (Level 1: 3, Level 2: 5, Level 3: 9,
Level 4: 11) — 47 placements stocked: 25 lessons on the
Grade 4 student book (with teacher-guide parts as labelled
evidence inside four of them) and 3 lessons on teacher-guide
evidence only, acceptance 0, labelled.
