# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Where You Live (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A written history is built by weighing evidence: Tecún Umán is the test case — Alvarado's own letter never names him, an indigenous title does, and the book concludes the captain existed while his famous single duel stays an open question.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Where You Live (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: A written history is built by weighing evidence: Tecún Umán is the test case — Alvarado's own letter never names him, an indigenous title does, and the book concludes the captain existed while his famous single duel stays an open question.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a family story about a grandfather — told at home for years but written down nowhere — is evidence of exactly the kind this lesson teaches you to weigh.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
