# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Name one thing in your home or town that an older person made, kept or taught — a dish, a song, a craft, a feast day. Write who it came from and what story it carries.

## Sub lesson 1 — The art of the four peoples and the marimba

Heritage is not only stone. Each of Guatemala's four peoples tells its past through what it makes and celebrates. Maya hands shape ceramics and paint jícaras, the gourd cups decorated for generations. Garífuna festivities move to the drum-dance, carrying the memory of a people who crossed the sea. Xinka crafts keep the south coast's own designs alive, and every town's feria patronal gathers its people round its saint and its story. Above them all sounds the marimba — wooden keys over resonators, declared the national instrument in 1978 — one instrument carrying the shared story of four peoples.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Maya cosmovision: our way of understanding the world

The teacher guide reads the class a text on the Maya cosmovision — "our way of understanding the world." Its teaching is simple and deep: culture is the sacred inheritance of grandmothers and grandfathers. Around the family fire, the old ones pass down stories received over thousands of years, and those stories teach the young how to live — in balance with Mother Earth, taking care of everything that has life. Heritage here is not a thing in a museum. It is a way of living, handed from the fire to the child.

## Sub lesson 3 — The values of the Maya, Xinka and Garífuna cultures

In the primero básico guide, the values of three peoples are set side by side, each in its own words. The Maya sheets teach respect for the creative force, Tata Tiwix. The Xinka sheets teach care for life and community. The Garífuna sheets teach isiehabuni and aganbadi — love, and listen. Different peoples, different languages, one shared lesson: protect everything that has life. The guide sets these values against violence itself, because violence is what breaks the harmony between human beings and nature that all three peoples teach.

## Say it to your companion

Give one real example from your own life — the marimba heard at a town feast — an instrument declared national in 1978 that still carries every people's story at once — and name the lesson's key words: Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters, The art of the four peoples and the marimba, The Maya cosmovision, heritage. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
