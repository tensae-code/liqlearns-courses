# Hands-on — Find History in Your Own Locality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand in the oldest place you know near your home — a church, a market corner, an old tree. Write down one thing that must have looked different fifty years ago, and one question you would ask someone who was there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History begins where you live: a temple at Tikal, swallowed by jungle and restored, opens the question every child then asks of their own community — how has this place changed in fifty years — and the elders who lived it are the first historians. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
