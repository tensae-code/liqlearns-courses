# Lesson plan — America's First Peoples: Paleoindian and Archaic

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** America's First Peoples: Paleoindian and Archaic
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and the Maya (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The first Americans were hunters who crossed the ice-covered Bering Strait chasing mammoth and mastodon; in the Archaic period that follows, their descendants settle, plant maize, beans and chile, build villages and begin to trade.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and the Maya (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The first Americans were hunters who crossed the ice-covered Bering Strait chasing mammoth and mastodon; in the Archaic period that follows, their descendants settle, plant maize, beans and chile, build villages and begin to trade.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — maize, beans and chile on your own table — the three plants the Archaic settlers first planted are still the heart of the Guatemalan meal.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the first peoples arrived in an empty land already farming → the book's order is the point: first the crossing and the great hunts, then settling, planting and villages — farming is the second chapter, not the first.
- Go back to the one thing: The first Americans were hunters who crossed the ice-covered Bering Strait chasing mammoth and mastodon; in the Archaic period that follows, their descendants settle, plant maize, beans and chile, build villages and begin to trade. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
