# Hands-on — America's First Peoples: Paleoindian and Archaic

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine your family must walk to a completely new land where no one has ever lived, following the animals you hunt. Write down two things you would carry, and one thing you would have to learn to do differently once you stopped moving and settled.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The first Americans were hunters who crossed the ice-covered Bering Strait chasing mammoth and mastodon; in the Archaic period that follows, their descendants settle, plant maize, beans and chile, build villages and begin to trade. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
