# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Sparta and Athens Against Sparta

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a newborn baby in your own family or street. Write down who decides what happens to that child today, and one way your community cares for a child born with a disability.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: How the ancient world treated disability, as one history sheet in Guatemala's segundo básico guide tells it: in Sparta the council of elders judged each newborn, and babies who did not meet expectations were thrown from Mount Taygetus; the Catholic Church later banned infanticide, but treated disability as supernatural, sometimes with exorcisms. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
