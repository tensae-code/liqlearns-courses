# Ancient Civilisations: Sparta and Athens Against Sparta

## Do it first

Think of a newborn baby in your own family or street. Write down who decides what happens to that child today, and one way your community cares for a child born with a disability.

Inside its study of violence against people with disabilities, the segundo básico guide sets a history sheet: how did the ancient world treat a child born different? Its answer begins in Sparta, the soldier-city of Greece. There, no newborn simply went home: the council of elders judged each baby, and those who did not meet the city's expectations were thrown from Mount Taygetus. Centuries later the Catholic Church banned the killing of infants outright — a real turn — yet disability was still read as something supernatural, and sometimes answered with exorcisms. The sheet's point reaches the present: how a society treats its most vulnerable children is a measure of the society itself.
