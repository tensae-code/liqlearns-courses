# Guatemala's Postclassic Peoples and Their Books

## Do it first

Write the names of two peoples or towns near where you live that have ever been rivals and allies at different times. Then write one story your own people tells about how the world began.

When the Classic cities fell, the story moved to the highlands. There, kingdoms rose: the K'iche' at Q'umarkaj, the Kaqchikel at Iximche', the Mam at Saqulew, and around them the Itza', Lacandón, Poqomam, Poqomchi', Q'eqchi', Tz'utujil and Lenca peoples. These states fought and allied in turn — and the balance broke when the Kaqchikel, long under K'iche' power, split from the confederation and stood alone. Uniquely, these peoples tell us their story themselves: the Popol Wuj carries the K'iche' account of creation and their kings; the Anales de los Kaqchikeles records the Kaqchikel nation's own history; the Rabinal Achí preserves their drama of a captured warrior. Books written by the peoples themselves — that is the treasure of the Postclassic.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: After the Classic collapse, the highlands filled with kingdoms — K'iche' at Q'umarkaj, Kaqchikel at Iximche', Mam at Saqulew and their neighbours — fighting and allying until the Kaqchikel broke from the K'iche' confederation; and these peoples wrote their own account of creation and kings in the Popol Wuj, the Anales de los Kaqchikeles and the Rabinal Achí. Give one real example from your own life — the Popol Wuj — the K'iche' book of creation and kings, still read and honoured in Guatemala today — and name the lesson's key words: Guatemala's Postclassic Peoples and Their Books, The Postclassic period and its cultures, Popol Wuj. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
