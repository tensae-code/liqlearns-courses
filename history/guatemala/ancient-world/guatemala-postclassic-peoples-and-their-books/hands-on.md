# Hands-on — Guatemala's Postclassic Peoples and Their Books

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the names of two peoples or towns near where you live that have ever been rivals and allies at different times. Then write one story your own people tells about how the world began.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: After the Classic collapse, the highlands filled with kingdoms — K'iche' at Q'umarkaj, Kaqchikel at Iximche', Mam at Saqulew and their neighbours — fighting and allying until the Kaqchikel broke from the K'iche' confederation; and these peoples wrote their own account of creation and kings in the Popol Wuj, the Anales de los Kaqchikeles and the Rabinal Achí. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
