# Hands-on — The Maya

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a circle and divide it into twenty parts, one for each day-sign of the Maya sacred calendar. Write the name of one nawal you remember and what it means that a person is born under a sign of nature.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Maya story rises in three ages: Olmec neighbours and the first Maya farmers of the Preclassic, the great city-states of the Classic — Tikal, Uaxactún and Quiriguá under their ajau kings, counting time with two calendars and the zero — and, living on today, the twenty nawales of the sacred calendar that still shape how Maya people read their days. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
