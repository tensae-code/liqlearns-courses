# Lesson plan — The Maya

**Course:** History — Guatemala · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Maya
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and the Maya (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Maya story rises in three ages: Olmec neighbours and the first Maya farmers of the Preclassic, the great city-states of the Classic — Tikal, Uaxactún and Quiriguá under their ajau kings, counting time with two calendars and the zero — and, living on today, the twenty nawales of the sacred calendar that still shape how Maya people read their days.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Preclassic: Olmecs and early Maya** — The Olmec "mother culture" carves colossal stone heads while the first Maya settle, farm maize, start calendars and glyph writing, and trade jade and obsidian from Kaminal Juyú and Tak'alik A'baj.
2. **The Classic period (250–900)** — Maya city-states Tikal, Uaxactún and Quiriguá rise under ajau kings, count time with two interlocking calendars and the zero, carve their deeds on stelae — then the great cities fall.
3. **The Nawales (primero básico annex)** — Every person is born under a nawal, an energy of nature that shapes their character and gifts; the Ajq'ijab' (Maya priests) still read the days so people live in balance with the world.
4. **The Nawales (segundo básico annex)** — Every person is born under a nawal, an energy of nature that shapes their character and gifts; the Ajq'ijab' still read the days so people live in balance with the world.
5. **The Nawales (tercero básico annex)** — Every person is born under a nawal, an energy of nature that shapes their character and gifts; the Ajq'ijab' still read the days so people live in balance with the world.
