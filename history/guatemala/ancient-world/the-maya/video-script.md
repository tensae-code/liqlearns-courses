# Video script — The Maya

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Maya story rises in three ages: Olmec neighbours and the first Maya farmers of the Preclassic, the great city-states of the Classic — Tikal, Uaxactún and Quiriguá under their ajau kings, counting time with two calendars and the zero — and, living on today, the twenty nawales of the sacred calendar that still shape how Maya people read their days.

Before the great cities, there were the first farmers.

Then came the Classic age, from about 250 to 900.

The primero básico guide closes with an annex the whole básico series carries: Los Nawales, the twenty day-signs of the Maya sacred calendar — B'atz', E, Aj, I'x, Tz'ik'in, Ajmaq', No'j, Tijax, Kawoq, Ajpu, Imox, Iq', Aq'ab'al, K'at, Keme, Keej, Q'anil, Toj and the rest.

The segundo básico guide carries the same closing annex as its primero básico companion: the twenty nawales of the sacred calendar.

The tercero básico guide closes the series as the others do, with Los Nawales: the twenty day-signs, the nawal that marks each person's birth, and the Ajq'ijab' who still keep the count.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
