# Hands-on — Mesoamerica: Maize and the Shared Culture

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Hold a tortilla or think of the last one you ate. Write down what had to happen to wild grass before it could become that food — and one thing your town shares with towns far away, the way Mesoamerica's peoples shared theirs.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Mesoamerica is the shared world built on maize: a tiny wild cob became a people's staple once lime-cooking — nixtamal — unlocked its food value, and the peoples from Mexico to Costa Rica came to share pyramids, the ball game, calendars, glyphs and jade. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
