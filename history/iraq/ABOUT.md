# About the History — Iraq course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Iraq's own Social Studies and History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Iraq inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **seven levels**, derived from Iraq's own books' progression as filed (the placements file's book order is the teaching order, earliest books first), grouped by the eras of Iraq's own story as its books present them:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps at Home — Mesopotamia, the Islamic Cities and Iraq Province by Province.** Iraq's primary Social Studies books (Grades 4–6 as filed): history begins with the child's own yesterday, rises to ancient Mesopotamia and the Islamic cities, then walks all eighteen governorates one by one, each with its historical sketch. 12 lessons.
2. **Level 2 — The Ancient World Whole — Mesopotamia, Egypt, Arabia and the Neighbours.** The first intermediate book (Grade 7 as filed) becomes a straight ancient-history course: Mesopotamia first and longest, then the Nile, then pre-Islamic Arabia's kingdoms, then Persia, Greece and Rome. 16 lessons.
3. **Level 3 — Arabia Before Islam and the Prophet's Story.** The second intermediate book (Grade 8 as filed): Arabia's tribe, trade and faiths before Islam, then Mecca, then the Prophet's life in Mecca and Medina told in order. 4 lessons.
4. **Level 4 — Modern Iraq Is Founded — Ottoman Centuries to the United Nations.** The third intermediate book (Grade 9 as filed), Unit 2 'Modern and Contemporary History of Iraq': four Ottoman centuries, occupation, the 1920 Revolt as the founding act, monarchy, 1941 and the UN. 13 lessons.
5. **Level 5 — Arab-Islamic Civilisation as a System.** The literary track's first History book (Grade 10 as filed): not events but a civilisation studied as a system — concept, administration, courts, economy, cities, scholarship — and how it met the wider world and fell behind. 7 lessons.
6. **Level 6 — Europe and America, 1789–1945.** The literary track's second History book (Grade 11 as filed): a straight political narrative of the West — thinkers first, then revolutions, unifications, industrialisation, world wars and the world after 1945. 27 lessons.
7. **Level 7 — Iraq and the Arab World — Colonialism and Liberation.** The final literary-track book (Grade 12 as filed) returns home: Iraq in depth (Ottoman, war, monarchy, 1941–1958), then the whole Arab world under colonialism and in liberation. 16 lessons.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Iraq's story, as Iraq teaches it:* "Start with your own yesterday. Then stand in Mesopotamia, the land between the two rivers, where writing and law begin. Walk the Islamic cities — Basra, Kufa, Baghdad, Samarra — and then your own governorate. Climb to the ancient world whole, follow Arabia into Islam, watch modern Iraq founded in revolt, study its civilisation as a system, look outward to Europe and America, and come home to Iraq and the Arab world fighting free."
