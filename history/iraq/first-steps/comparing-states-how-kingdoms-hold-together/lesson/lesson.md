# Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Society organised under temple and ruler. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Society organised under temple and ruler

To survive nature's dangers people accept two authorities, the temple and the ruler, and communal life becomes a state.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this story, where it happens, and what changes because of it. That turn is the whole lesson.

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: to survive nature's dangers people accept two authorities, the temple and the ruler, and communal life becomes a state. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
