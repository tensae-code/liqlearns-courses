# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Iraq · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps at Home — Mesopotamia, the Islamic Cities and Iraq Province by Province (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the pupil becomes the historian, gathering photographs of antiquities and labelling each one in a personal notebook.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps at Home — Mesopotamia, the Islamic Cities and Iraq Province by Province — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Activity: collect pictures of historical remains and name them** — The pupil becomes the historian, gathering photographs of antiquities and labelling each one in a personal notebook.
2. **Historical sketch: Duhok governorate** — Mountain Duhok's old towns and citadels are set before the pupil as the landmarks of home to be found and recorded.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia's Gifts** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the pupil becomes the historian, gathering photographs of antiquities and labelling each one in a personal notebook.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Iraq and its neighbours you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
