# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia's Gifts

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Sumer's gifts: writing, ziggurats, laws and the lyre; Masterpieces of our old civilisation: Sumer, Akkad, Babylon, Assyria; The Iraqis were the first to invent writing and recording; The marks of civilisation: writing invented; Mesopotamia's sciences: writing, medicine, astronomy, time. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sumer's gifts: writing, ziggurats, laws and the lyre

Clay tablets and the Sumerian lyre show a people who turned wedge-shaped marks into records, music and law.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Masterpieces of our old civilisation: Sumer, Akkad, Babylon, Assyria

The Sumerian lyre and treasures, an Akkadian king's head, Babylon's tower and Assyria's winged bull parade past as proof in pictures.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Iraqis were the first to invent writing and recording

Temples need accounts, so Iraqis press clay tablets with a triangular reed pen and invent cuneiform, then roll cylinder seals that print their owner's name and picture.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — The marks of civilisation: writing invented

Pictorial writing turns daily acts into records, and with writing human history proper begins.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — Mesopotamia's sciences: writing, medicine, astronomy, time

Cuneiform spreads with the Akkadian language; doctors diagnose and prescribe; astronomers chart planets, split the day into twelve parts and the week into seven days.
