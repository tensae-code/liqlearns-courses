# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia's Gifts

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — an animated map, plain words: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Clay tablets and the sumerian lyre show a people who turned wedge-shaped marks into records, music and law.

On the map, follow this part: Sumer's gifts: writing, ziggurats, laws and the lyre. Clay tablets and the Sumerian lyre show a people who turned wedge-shaped marks into records, music and law.

On the map, follow this part: Masterpieces of our old civilisation: Sumer, Akkad, Babylon, Assyria. The Sumerian lyre and treasures, an Akkadian king's head, Babylon's tower and Assyria's winged bull parade past as proof in pictures.

On the map, follow this part: The Iraqis were the first to invent writing and recording. Temples need accounts, so Iraqis press clay tablets with a triangular reed pen and invent cuneiform, then roll cylinder seals that print their owner's name and picture.

On the map, follow this part: The marks of civilisation: writing invented. Pictorial writing turns daily acts into records, and with writing human history proper begins.

On the map, follow this part: Mesopotamia's sciences: writing, medicine, astronomy, time. Cuneiform spreads with the Akkadian language; doctors diagnose and prescribe; astronomers chart planets, split the day into twelve parts and the week into seven days.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Then go and do the lesson.
