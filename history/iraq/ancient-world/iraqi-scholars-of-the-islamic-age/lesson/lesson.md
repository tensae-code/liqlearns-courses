# Iraqi Scholars of the Islamic Age

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Iraqi scholars: grammar, chemistry, history and light. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Iraqi scholars: grammar, chemistry, history and light

Abu al-Aswad invents Arabic grammar in Basra; Jabir ibn Hayyan founds chemistry on experiment; the Mandaean al-Sabi writes on the astrolabe; al-Masudi travels and writes Meadows of Gold; Basra-born Ibn al-Haytham explains the eye and the rainbow; Shuhda of Baghdad teaches hadith to men and women.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this story, where it happens, and what changes because of it. That turn is the whole lesson.
