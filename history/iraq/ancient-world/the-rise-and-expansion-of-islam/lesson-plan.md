## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Islamic cities: Basra founded** — Utba ibn Ghazwan founds Basra in 14 AH as an administrative and military base for the eastern conquests; it becomes Iraq's only seaport at the head of the Gulf.
2. **Islamic cities: Kufa becomes a capital** — Old Kufa beside al-Hira becomes the seat of the fourth caliph Ali ibn Abi Talib in 36 AH and stays a major religious centre.
3. **Islamic cities: Baghdad, the Round City** — The Abbasid caliph Abu Jaafar al-Mansur builds round Baghdad, City of Peace, on the Tigris in 145 AH and makes it the Abbasid capital.
4. **Enrichment from our history: Imam Ali's covenant on equality** — Ali ibn Abi Talib writes to his governor Malik al-Ashtar that people are either your brother in religion or your equal in creation — a charter the book sets beside the 1948 Universal Declaration.
5. **Iraq, seat of the Islamic caliphate: Kufa capital 36–41 AH** — Ali makes Kufa his capital; the city of red sand by the Euphrates, with its great mosque, governs the caliphate for five years.
6. **Baghdad, seat of the Abbasid caliphate: the Round City** — Al-Mansur begins round Baghdad in 145 AH on the Tigris's west bank, ringed by two walls and four gates, finished with palace, mosque and treasury by 149 AH.
7. **Baghdad grows: al-Rusafa, schools and shrines** — Baghdad spills across the river into al-Rusafa with its markets, palaces and the Mustansiriyya school, gathers the shrines of Kadhimiyya and Abu Hanifa, and is chosen Arab Capital of Culture in 2013.
8. **Samarra, another Abbasid capital** — Al-Mu'tasim moves the capital to Samarra in 221 AH, builds palaces and the spiral Malwiyya minaret; eight Abbasid caliphs rule from it for some 58 years before the capital returns to Baghdad in 279 AH.
9. **Historical sketch: Baghdad governorate** — The capital's landmarks — the Abbasid palace world, the Mustansiriyya and the shrines — are listed as the pupil's own inheritance to name and count.
10. **Historical sketch: Anbar governorate** — The city of Anbar, built in the Sassanian age, gives the province its name; Ana appears already in Babylonian and Assyrian texts.
11. **Historical sketch: Wasit governorate** — Al-Hajjaj's garrison city of Wasit, set midway between Kufa and Basra, rises and is later abandoned, leaving its ruins as the province's name and lesson.
12. **Historical sketch: Karbala governorate** — At Karbala in 61 AH Husayn ibn Ali and al-Abbas fall in the Battle of al-Taff; their gold-domed shrines make the city, later rebuilt and served by Suleiman the Magnificent's canal under the Ottomans.
13. **Historical sketch: Najaf governorate** — Beside Kufa stand the Lakhmid palaces of al-Sadir and al-Khawarnaq; Najaf's hawza teaches jurisprudence, grammar, history and literature, ringed by churches and shrines.
14. **Historical sketch: Maysan governorate** — Marsh-edge Maysan carries its older Arabic and Islamic past under later Turkish names for its army camps and districts.
15. **Historical sketch: Basra governorate** — Basra, founded as Islam's first garrison city in Iraq, reaches the height of its civilisational prosperity as port and learning centre.
16. **Ch.4 Mecca before Islam: the city and the Kaaba** — Mecca, "house of the Lord" in the old Yemeni telling, sits dry and water-poor yet holy, drawing all Arabia to its sanctuary.
17. **Qusayy ibn Kilab gathers Quraysh** — In the early 5th century Qusayy, the Prophet's fifth ancestor, settles Quraysh in Mecca and takes the city's offices — hijaba, siqaya and rifada — into his house.
18. **Mecca's standing: Quraysh and the Hajj** — Quraysh guard the Hajj rites so strictly that all the tribes look on them with reverence, and reverence becomes trade and power.
19. **Ch.5 The Prophet in Mecca: birth and upbringing** — Muhammad is born an orphan in the Year of the Elephant (571 AD), raised after his grandfather's death by his uncle Abu Talib, who makes up a father's tenderness.
20. **The Black Stone and Hilf al-Fudul** — The young Muhammad settles the clans' quarrel over the Black Stone, and joins the Pact of the Virtuous sworn to take the wronged man's right from his wrongdoer.
21. **The call: secret then public, and persecution** — Commanded to preach openly, Muhammad refuses Quraysh's offers even were the sun put in his right hand and the moon in his left; the believers are mocked as sorcery and soothsaying.
22. **The Abyssinia migration and the boycott** — Persecuted Muslims sail to the Negus, who refuses Quraysh's gift-laden envoys; at home Banu Hashim endure three years' boycott in Abu Talib's ravine.
23. **Taif, the Aqaba pledges and the Hijra** — Rejected at Taif, Muhammad finds Yathrib's Aws and Khazraj at Aqaba; he leaves Mecca with Abu Bakr, hiding from Quraysh's trackers in Mount Thawr.
24. **Ch.6 The Prophet in Medina: a new society** — Yathrib becomes the City of the Prophet; mosque-building and the brothering of Emigrants and Helpers erase the ranks of colour and lineage.
25. **Badr, Uhud and the Trench** — Quraysh march with a thousand men to Badr though their caravan escaped; at Uhud victory turns, and at the Trench a confederacy besieges Medina in 5 AH.
26. **Hudaybiyya and Khaybar** — 1,400 Muslims set out for pilgrimage in 6 AH and accept a truce the book shows was no defeat; Khaybar's fortresses then open.
27. **Mu'ta, the conquest of Mecca, Hunayn and Tabuk** — At Mu'ta Muslims first face the Romans (8 AH); Mecca is taken the same year, Hunayn follows at once, and Tabuk (9 AH) is the Prophet's last expedition.
28. **The Farewell Pilgrimage and the Prophet's death** — In 10 AH Muhammad bids the people farewell on pilgrimage; in 11 AH (632 AD) he dies, the message delivered and Islam spread.
29. **Ch.6 The garrison cities (amsar)** — Basra, Kufa, Fustat and their sisters are founded as camps and grow into the civilisation's great cities.
30. **Ch.6 Cities beyond the amsar** — Older cities that Islam inherited and newer foundations alike are folded into one urban civilisation.
