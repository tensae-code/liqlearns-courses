## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Iraq's old cities: Ur and Hatra** — Ur rises in the south in the 3rd millennium BC, remembered as Abraham's birthplace, its ziggurat still standing; Hatra, city of the sun south-west of Mosul, keeps its temples and statues.
2. **Iraq, land of civilisations: the Sumerians** — In the south, Sumer invents cuneiform writing, raises ziggurats above the flood plain and writes laws to protect people's rights.
3. **The Babylonian civilisation and Hammurabi** — Hammurabi of Babylon carves his many laws on his famous stele, guaranteeing property, court access and freedom of movement; Babylon builds temples, hanging gardens and excels in astronomy and maths.
4. **The Assyrian civilisation** — Assyria in the north, named for the god Ashur, imposes law, builds a strong central state and army, and leaves Nineveh's walls and winged bulls.
5. **Mesopotamia, our homeland: the land's many names** — Iraq is Babylon, the land between the two rivers and the Sawad; Sumer names the south, Akkad the centre, Assyria the north, and the Tigris and Euphrates are called the gift that drew the first farmers.
6. **Iraq, cradle of laws and legislation: Hammurabi** — King Hammurabi carves 282 laws on black stone, standing humbly before Shamash the sun god, to spread justice, stop the strong oppressing the weak and punish thieves thirty-fold.
7. **Map of the oldest civilisations in Iraq (unit introduction)** — Before the tour begins, a map pins Iraq's oldest civilisations to the land the pupils are about to cross province by province.
8. **Historical sketch: Erbil governorate** — Erbil Citadel, inhabited for millennia and still standing over the modern city, heads a province full of remains that have survived to today.
9. **Historical sketch: Nineveh governorate** — Mosul, a great civilisational centre facing ancient Nineveh across the Tigris, keeps its Great Mosque and leaning minaret as its historical landmarks.
10. **Historical sketch: Kirkuk governorate** — Kirkuk's citadel and old quarters carry the province's long Assyrian-to-Islamic past into the present city.
11. **Historical sketch: Salah al-Din governorate** — Tell al-Sawwan, settled in the 6th millennium BC before history was written, opens a province that later holds Assyrian, Roman and Islamic cities and Tikrit.
12. **Historical sketch: Diyala governorate** — Diyala's people farm from the Ubaid age, some 6,000 years BC, and its old cities leave temples and palaces such as Tell Asmar.
13. **Historical sketch: Babylon governorate** — Babylon city and Kish anchor a province whose kings and monuments the pupil is asked to picture on a wall chart.
14. **Historical sketch: al-Diwaniyah (al-Qadisiyah) governorate** — Nippur (Nuffar), a Sumerian-Akkadian holy city and political hub of early Babylonian times around 2000 BC, yields thousands of clay tablets to the excavators.
15. **Historical sketch: Dhi Qar governorate** — Ur of the Chaldees, King Shulgi's palace and the ziggurat of Ur-Nammu, founder of Ur's Third Dynasty four thousand years ago, make Dhi Qar Sumer's home province.
16. **Historical sketch: al-Muthanna governorate** — Uruk (Warka) and its ziggurat are set as map-work: the pupil plots the province's historic cities on a blank map of Iraq.
17. **Ch.2 Mesopotamia: land and the conditions that raised it** — Mountains, wavy uplands, the flat alluvial plain and the western plateau are walked in turn, with fertile soil and river channels explaining the first farming villages.
18. **The Sumerians** — The south's oldest people found villages and cities and lay the foundation of civilisation in the land called Sumer.
19. **The Akkadians: Sargon founds the first empire** — Sargon of Akkad (2371–2316 BC) conquers the city-states and builds the ancient world's first empire, lasting a century and a half.
20. **The Old Babylonian age and Hammurabi's peak** — The land falls back into city-states, then Hammurabi raises Babylonian civilisation to its height; after his death foreigners press in and the Assyrians rise in the north.
21. **Political and social life in Mesopotamia** — City-states give way to unified rule; classes from king to slaves live under customs that harden into written law, and cotton — "tree wool" — joins the crops.
22. **The laws: the oldest written codes** — Ur-Nammu, Lipit-Ishtar and Hammurabi put law on stone, the feature the book says puts Iraq's civilisation first in the ancient world.
23. **Enrichment: Hammurabi's code read closely** — On a 2.25-metre stele the code orders death for the caught thief and protects widows and minors' property through the judge.
24. **Ch.1 The civilisations before: Mesopotamia, Egypt, the Levant and others** — A survey runs from Mesopotamia and Egypt through the Levant to the Arabs before Islam, setting the stage the new civilisation inherits.
