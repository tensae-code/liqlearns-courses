# Modern Iraq's Poets, Scholars and Artists

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Modern Iraq's poets, scholars and artists. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Modern Iraq's poets, scholars and artists

Al-Jawahiri sings to the Tigris from exile; al-Sayyab, Nazik al-Malaika, Sargon Boulus and the Kurdish poet Goran voice their homeland; Mandaean physicist Abdul Jabbar Abdullah becomes Baghdad University's first president after 1958; sculptor Jawad Saleem raises the Freedom Monument in Tahrir Square.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this story, where it happens, and what changes because of it. That turn is the whole lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: al-Jawahiri sings to the Tigris from exile; al-Sayyab, Nazik al-Malaika. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Modern Iraq's Poets, Scholars and Artists. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
