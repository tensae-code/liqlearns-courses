# Video script — Kushan empire

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Kushan empire.

The Rabatak inscription recites the Kushan royal line — Kujula Kadphises, Vima Takto, Vima Kadphises, Kanishka — and names the empire's furthest cities.

The Kushans rule their vast empire from two political centres, Peshawar and Begram, and Begram's hoard shows the trade of three worlds meeting.

Hadda, ten kilometres south-west of Jalalabad, is remembered by travellers and pilgrims as one of ancient Afghanistan's great cultural centres, rich in temples and sculpture.

Bamiyan, registered as world heritage in 2003, roots its painted caves and colossal Buddhas in the Kushan age, when it was the empire's house of faith.

The Kushans raise the land's greatest ancient empire, its line recited in the Rabatak inscription.

Begram shares imperial rule with Peshawar and hoards the trade goods of three worlds.

Hadda by Jalalabad flourishes as the empire's cultural heart.

Bamiyan's painted caves and colossal Buddhas make it the empire's house of faith.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
