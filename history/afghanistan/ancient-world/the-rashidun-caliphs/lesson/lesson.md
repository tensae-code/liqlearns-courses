# The Rashidun Caliphs

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "The Rashidun Caliphs" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 6, Lesson 46: The caliphate of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (11–13 AH)

At Saqifa the companions choose Abu Bakr; he holds the community together through the zakat refusals and gathers the Qur'an's scattered pages.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lesson 47: The caliphate of Umar (13–24 AH)

Umar, the second caliph, builds the administration and justice of a fast-growing Islamic state.

## Sub lesson 3 — Lesson 48: The caliphate of Uthman (24–36 AH)

Under the third caliph the Qur'an is fixed in one standard copy, and discontent gathers around his rule.

## Sub lesson 4 — Lesson 49: The caliphate of Ali (36–40 AH)

Ali, the fourth caliph, rules through civil strife until his martyrdom closes the Rashidun age.

## Sub lesson 5 — Lesson 50: A brief review of the Rashidun caliphs' period

Pupils sum up the era from Abu Bakr's election in 11 AH to Ali's martyrdom — thirty years that set Islam's pattern of rule.

## Sub lesson 6 — Lesson 28: Do you know how Abu Bakr's caliphate began?

Abdullah "Abu Bakr", named al-Siddiq by the Prophet, is raised to the caliphate; the false prophet Musaylima is killed in his time and his followers return to Islam.

## Sub lesson 7 — Lesson 29: What do you know of Umar al-Faruq's caliphate?

On Abu Bakr's death the caliphate passes to Umar ibn al-Khattab, under whom new garrison cities such as Basra are founded.

## Sub lesson 8 — Lesson 30: Do you know the events of Uthman's caliphate?

Uthman ibn Affan, converted by Abu Bakr's invitation and famed for generosity, holds the caliphate in the years when the Qur'an is gathered into one book.

## Sub lesson 9 — Lesson 31: How did Ali's caliphate pass?

Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Prophet's cousin and son-in-law, rules from Kufa mosque's city through the community's first civil strife.

## Sub lesson 10 — Lesson 38: The Rashidun Caliphs (Abu Bakr)

The first caliph holds the community together and gathers the Qur'an.

## Sub lesson 11 — Lesson 39: Hazrat Umar Faruq

The second caliph builds the administration of a growing state.

## Sub lesson 12 — Lesson 40: Hazrat Uthman

The third caliph fixes the Qur'an in one standard copy.

## Sub lesson 13 — Lesson 41: Hazrat Ali

The fourth caliph rules from Kufa through civil strife.

## Sub lesson 14 — Lesson 42: A brief look at the brilliant age of the Rashidun Caliphs

The thirty years of the rightly-guided caliphs are summed up as Islam's golden pattern of rule.
