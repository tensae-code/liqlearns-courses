# Lesson plan — Kingdoms, Empires and Other African States

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Kingdoms, Empires and Other African States
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient Africa, Its Kingdoms and Empires (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** African peoples built kingdoms, empires and other states — creating them, organising their political, administrative, social and economic life — and their courts and cities produced learning and art that outlived the rulers.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Ancient Africa, Its Kingdoms and Empires — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Building and Organising Kingdoms and Empires** — African peoples built states: kingdoms, empires and other formations, each created deliberately and then organised — political life under a ruler and a court, administrative life through officials who carried decisions outward, social life ordering the peoples inside, and economic life feeding and funding the whole..
2. **Learning, Art and Culture in the Kingdoms and Empires** — The organised state made a second harvest possible.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: How Africa's Regions Traded with Each Other — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: African peoples built kingdoms, empires and other states — creating them, organising their political, administrative, social and economic life — and their courts and cities produced learning and art that outlived the rulers.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a school or club that only works because someone organises it — roles, rules and shared tasks — the same building work, at a small scale.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.
