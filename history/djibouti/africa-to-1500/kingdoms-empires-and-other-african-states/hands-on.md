# Hands-on — Kingdoms, Empires and Other African States

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A crowd is not yet a state. What has to be added — a ruler? laws? officials? shared work? — before a people becomes a kingdom? Name two things. This lesson shows how African peoples built such states, and what their courts and cities made that lasted longer than any ruler.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: African peoples built kingdoms, empires and other states — creating them, organising their political, administrative, social and economic life — and their courts and cities produced learning and art that outlived the rulers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
