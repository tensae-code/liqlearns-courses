# Hands-on — Daily Life in Ancient Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before kings and empires, there is an ordinary day. Picture one in an ancient African town: waking in a built home, eating with family, dressing for the day. Which of those ordinary things tells you most about how those people lived? Hold your answer. This lesson walks through that ordinary day, part by part.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Daily life in ancient Africa in four parts: the homes and towns people built, the family every child first belongs to, the food that decided how societies thrived, and the dress, ornaments and art that carried each civilisation's identity. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
