# Lesson plan — Daily Life in Ancient Africa

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Daily Life in Ancient Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient Africa, Its Kingdoms and Empires (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Daily life in ancient Africa in four parts: the homes and towns people built, the family every child first belongs to, the food that decided how societies thrived, and the dress, ornaments and art that carried each civilisation's identity.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Ancient Africa, Its Kingdoms and Empires — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Homes and Towns** — Ancient Africans built their lives: homes raised to shelter families, and in time towns where many homes stood together.
2. **The Family** — Inside those homes stands the family — the first community every child belongs to and learns from.
3. **Food** — What ancient Africans grew, hunted and cooked decided how their societies thrived.
4. **Dress, Ornaments and Art** — Last, the body itself carries the civilisation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: From Stone Tools to Farming — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Daily life in ancient Africa in four parts: the homes and towns people built, the family every child first belongs to, the food that decided how societies thrived, and the dress, ornaments and art that carried each civilisation's identity.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: the learner's own ordinary day — home, family meals, clothes chosen — read the same way: what each part shows about how their own community lives.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.
