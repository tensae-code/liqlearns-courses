# Lesson plan — How Africa's Regions Traded with Each Other

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** How Africa's Regions Traded with Each Other
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient Africa, Its Kingdoms and Empires (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's regions were tied together by trade: the Sahara, which seems to divide the continent, became a road, the great rivers grew civilisations on their banks, and trade circuits stitched the great empires into one working world.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Ancient Africa, Its Kingdoms and Empires — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Sahara, Barrier and Road; the Great Rivers** — The Sahara looks like a wall — the greatest desert on earth, set across the north of Africa.
2. **Trade Circuits of the Great Empires** — Inside the great empires, trade ran in circuits — routes that looped town to town and region to region, carrying goods out and bringing goods back.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: Daily Life in Ancient Africa — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Africa's regions were tied together by trade: the Sahara, which seems to divide the continent, became a road, the great rivers grew civilisations on their banks, and trade circuits stitched the great empires into one working world.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a market town in the learner's own region where goods from far places arrive by road — the town lives because the road connects it, not despite the distance.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Sahara only divided Africa → the desert became a road: caravans crossed it, and trade stitched the regions and empires together.
- Go back to the one thing: Africa's regions were tied together by trade: the Sahara, which seems to divide the continent, became a road, the great rivers grew civilisations on their banks, and trade circuits stitched the great empires into one working world. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
