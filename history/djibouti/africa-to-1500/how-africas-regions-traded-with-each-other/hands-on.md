# Hands-on — How Africa's Regions Traded with Each Other

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at a map of Africa. The great desert sits like a wall across the north. Would you call it a barrier — or could it be a road? And the great rivers: what grows along a river that cannot grow away from one? Hold both questions. This lesson answers them with trade.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Africa's regions were tied together by trade: the Sahara, which seems to divide the continent, became a road, the great rivers grew civilisations on their banks, and trade circuits stitched the great empires into one working world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
