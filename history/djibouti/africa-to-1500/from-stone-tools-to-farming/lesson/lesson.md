# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Pick up a stone. With your bare hands, try to imagine cutting, scraping or digging with it — then imagine the same stone chipped to a sharp edge. What new things become possible? Hold that difference. This lesson is about the first African societies and the tools that remade their lives.

The first African societies did not only survive — they **made things**. Above all they shaped **stone tools**: stones chipped and edged until a hand could cut, scrape, dig and shape with them. A tool is an idea made solid, and each new tool widened what a community could do.

Around their tools, the first societies built a **way of life** — how they got their food, how they sheltered, how they worked together and divided the tasks of the day. Read the tools and you read the life: that is why this lesson pairs the tool-kit and the way of life in one breath. Before there were towns or kings, there were hands, stones, and a life organised around what the hands had learned to make.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The first African societies shaped stone tools and built a way of life around them — tools changed what hands could do, and life changed with them. Give one real example: a home where one good tool — a knife, a hoe — changes what the whole family can cook, build or plant in a day, and name the lesson's key words: stone tools, first societies, way of life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
