# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pick up a stone. With your bare hands, try to imagine cutting, scraping or digging with it — then imagine the same stone chipped to a sharp edge. What new things become possible? Hold that difference. This lesson is about the first African societies and the tools that remade their lives.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The first African societies shaped stone tools and built a way of life around them — tools changed what hands could do, and life changed with them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
