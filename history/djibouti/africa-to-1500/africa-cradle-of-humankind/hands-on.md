# Hands-on — Africa, Cradle of Humankind

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask the people at home: where does our family story begin? Every family story has a first place. Now make the question as big as it can be: where does the story of all human beings begin? This lesson gives the answer the fossils give — and it is the continent under your feet.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The first fossils show humankind beginning in Africa: the human story starts on this continent and spreads out from it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
