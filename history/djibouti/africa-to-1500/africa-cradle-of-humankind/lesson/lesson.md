# Africa, Cradle of Humankind

## Do it first

Ask the people at home: where does our family story begin? Every family story has a first place. Now make the question as big as it can be: where does the story of all human beings begin? This lesson gives the answer the fossils give — and it is the continent under your feet.

The oldest traces of human beings — the **first fossils** — are found in **Africa**. Bones and footprints preserved in the earth show the long **evolution of humankind** beginning on this continent: Africa is where the human story starts.

From that African beginning, people **spread out** — across the continent first, and in time beyond it, until human beings lived in every part of the world. Every person alive, on every continent, stands at the far end of a journey that began in Africa. That is why the book places this lesson at the front of everything: the cradle of humankind is Africa, and the first civilisations grow from that same ground.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The first fossils show humankind beginning in Africa: the human story starts on this continent and spreads out from it. Give one real example: a family that can name the village where its oldest known ancestor lived — the place every later branch of the family spread out from, and name the lesson's key words: fossils, humankind, Africa, beginnings, spread. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
