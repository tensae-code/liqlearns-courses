# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and Africa's Beginnings (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is the ordered story of past events: children learn what history is, and learn to place events in order on a timeline.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and Africa's Beginnings — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course. Nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History is the ordered story of past events: children learn what history is, and learn to place events in order on a timeline.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a grandparent telling the family's story in order — first the old home, then the move, then the children growing — so a young listener can follow what came first and what followed.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a long timeline drawn on a classroom wall, with small drawings of events pinned along it in order — the student points along the line and says which event came first, which next, and which last.
   - ADD GIF: a short GIF of event cards sliding along a timeline into their right order, one by one — the student watches and says why the story breaks if two cards swap places.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing and gives a real example (lesson/lesson.md).

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks history is a pile of old facts → history is a story: events placed in order, so that what came before explains what came after.
- Go back to the one thing: History is the ordered story of past events: children learn what history is, and learn to place events in order on a timeline. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
