# What History Is

## Do it first

Think of three things that happened in your own life — when you were small, last year, and this week. Say them in a jumbled order, then say them in the right order. Notice how the story only makes sense when the events stand in their order. Hold that feeling. This lesson names what you just did: history.

Every course of stories needs a first lesson, and this book begins by naming its subject. **History is the ordered story of past events** — not a heap of old things, but events told in the order they happened, so that one event can explain the next.

The tool of that order is the **timeline**: a line along which events are placed, earlier ones to one side, later ones to the other. A child who can place an event on the line — before this, after that — has taken the first step of the historian: turning the past from a blur into a story that can be followed, checked and retold.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History is the ordered story of past events: children learn what history is, and learn to place events in order on a timeline. Give one real example: a grandparent telling the family's story in order — first the old home, then the move, then the children growing — so a young listener can follow what came first and what followed, and name the lesson's key words: history, timeline, past events, order. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
