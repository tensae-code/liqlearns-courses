# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of three things that happened in your own life — when you were small, last year, and this week. Say them in a jumbled order, then say them in the right order. Notice how the story only makes sense when the events stand in their order. Hold that feeling. This lesson names what you just did: history.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is the ordered story of past events: children learn what history is, and learn to place events in order on a timeline. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
