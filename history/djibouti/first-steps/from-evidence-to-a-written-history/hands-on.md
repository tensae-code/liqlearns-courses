# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Hold up one old thing from your home — a photograph, a tool, a letter. Ask it three questions out loud: Who made you? When? What can you tell me about the people who used you? Notice: the thing does not speak until you question it. This lesson is about that questioning — how sources become a written history.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written history is built from sources: the class hunts for sources, questions them, and turns the evidence into Africa's written past. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
