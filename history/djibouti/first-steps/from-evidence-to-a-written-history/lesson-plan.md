# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and Africa's Beginnings (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A written history is built from sources: the class hunts for sources, questions them, and turns the evidence into Africa's written past.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and Africa's Beginnings — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: What History Is — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: A written history is built from sources: the class hunts for sources, questions them, and turns the evidence into Africa's written past.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: an elder questioned carefully about the old days — what they saw themselves, what they were told — while the listener writes down only what the answers can support.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a source can simply be copied → a source must be questioned first: who made it, when, and what it can and cannot prove.
- Go back to the one thing: A written history is built from sources: the class hunts for sources, questions them, and turns the evidence into Africa's written past. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
