# History — Djibouti course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Djibouti inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Djibouti's book in hand is one —
and it is not a Djibouti national book: it is the UNESCO
General History of Africa (HGA) pedagogical-guide prototype
for ages 10–12 (French, teacher guide), filed in the library
under Djibouti. Its placements are pan-African accordingly:
history taught entirely as Africa's own story, organised in
9 learning units (UA) that all feed one base competence —
arguing for continental integration from Africa's past. So
the ladder follows the guide's own unit order, grouped the
way the continent's story divides: the historian's craft and
Africa's beginnings first (Level 1, UA1), then ancient
Africa — daily life, trade and the kingdoms and empires
(Level 2, UA2–UA3), then slavery, colonisation and the
resistance to both (Level 3, UA4–UA6), and last
independence, unity and Africa's future (Level 4, UA7–UA9).
The ladder stops where the guide in hand stops, not because
Djibouti teaches nothing more: Djibouti's own primary
history/social-studies book is still missing, as filed —
a library debt named in ABOUT.md.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — here, the
UNESCO HGA pedagogical guide filed under Djibouti — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps and Africa's Beginnings** — the guide's
   UA1: what history is (the ordered story of past events,
   told on a timeline); from evidence to a written history
   (sources hunted, questioned, and turned into Africa's
   written past); Africa, cradle of humankind (the first
   fossils); and from stone tools to farming (the first
   societies' tools and the way of life built around them).
   4 lessons, in two titles: `first-steps/` (2 lessons) and
   `africa-to-1500/` (2 lessons).

2. **Ancient Africa, Its Kingdoms and Empires** — UA2–UA3:
   daily life in ancient Africa in four sub lessons (homes
   and towns, the family, food, dress, ornaments and art);
   how Africa's regions traded with each other (the Sahara
   as barrier and road, the great rivers, and the empires'
   trade circuits, in two sub lessons); and kingdoms,
   empires and other African states (their organisation,
   and the learning and art that outlived the rulers, in
   two sub lessons). 3 lessons, in one title:
   `africa-to-1500/` (3 lessons).

3. **Slavery, Colonisation and Resistance** — UA4–UA6:
   slavery before the Atlantic trade (captivity and
   dependence inside the continent); the Indian Ocean and
   trans-Saharan slave trades; the Atlantic slave trade;
   resistance to slavery and the slave trades (slavery
   named a crime against humanity); Africa's internal
   transformations 1500–1900 (Africa's own forces, in two
   sub lessons); explorers and missionaries mapping the
   interior; the Berlin Conference; direct and indirect
   rule (and apartheid); African resistance to
   colonisation; and liberation movements. 10 lessons, in
   one title: `africa-after-1500/` (10 lessons).

4. **Independence, Unity and Africa's Future** — UA7–UA9:
   decolonisation, colony by colony, by negotiation or by
   struggle; neocolonialism (the flag changes, control
   stays); African unity — the founding of the OAU (with
   the obstacles to unity, in two sub lessons); after the
   flag (environment, population, health, debt,
   globalisation); the African Renaissance; gender and
   Africa's future (no unity while half the people are held
   back); and the African diasporas, ancient and modern.
   7 lessons, in one title: `africa-after-1500/`
   (7 lessons).

**Totals: 24 lessons** (Level 1: 4 · Level 2: 3 · Level 3:
10 · Level 4: 7 — 31 placements, all from one teacher
guide, evidence only, acceptance 0).
