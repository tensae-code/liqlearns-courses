# Hands-on — Resistance to Slavery and the Slave Trades

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
In the last three lessons, people were taken. Ask the question those lessons leave hanging: did they go quietly? Think of every step of a captive's road — capture, march, coast, ship, arrival — and ask at each step: what could a person do to resist? This lesson answers: they resisted at every step.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The enslaved resisted at every step — resistance to slavery and the slave trades runs through the whole story — and the lesson names slavery what it is: a crime against humanity. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
