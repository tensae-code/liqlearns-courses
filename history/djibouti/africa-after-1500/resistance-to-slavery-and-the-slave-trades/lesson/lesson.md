# Resistance to Slavery and the Slave Trades

## Do it first

In the last three lessons, people were taken. Ask the question those lessons leave hanging: did they go quietly? Think of every step of a captive's road — capture, march, coast, ship, arrival — and ask at each step: what could a person do to resist? This lesson answers: they resisted at every step.

This lesson restores the other half of the story. The enslaved **resisted at every step**: resisting capture, escaping on the march and from the coasts, rising against the trades that hunted them. Resistance was not rare or late — it runs from the first moment of the first capture to the last of the trades.

And the book gives the whole system its true name: **slavery, a crime against humanity**. That naming matters. It says the wrong was not only in cruel masters or hard journeys; the thing itself — human beings held, sold and worked as property — was a crime, and those who fought it, at whatever step and at whatever cost, stood on the side of humanity itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The enslaved resisted at every step — resistance to slavery and the slave trades runs through the whole story — and the lesson names slavery what it is: a crime against humanity. Give one real example: a person who refuses, escapes, fights back or holds onto their language and name under a system built to erase them — resistance takes many forms, all of them human, and name the lesson's key words: resistance, slavery, crime against humanity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
