# After the Flag: Debt, Drought and the Integration Answer

## Do it first

The celebration ends. The flag is up. Now the new state opens its account books, looks at its rain gauges, its clinics, its growing towns — and finds a desk full of hard problems no anthem can solve. This lesson names those problems honestly, as the inheritance of independence.

Independence was a beginning, not an ending. This lesson lists the **new challenges** the book sets before the continent's children: the **environment** under pressure, **population** growing and moving, **health** to be secured for all, **debt** owed to outside lenders, and **globalisation** binding Africa's fortunes to a world economy it does not command.

The book's framing is sober and bracing: independent Africa faces **new dominations and new problems** — and they are problems **its children must solve**. No empire will solve them; that is what independence means. The lesson hands the desk of files to the learner's own generation, deliberately.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Independent Africa faces new challenges — environment, population, health, debt and globalisation — new dominations and new problems that its own children must solve. Give one real example: a family that finally gets its own house and, with the keys, its first repair bills, debts and decisions — freedom and the problems of freedom arriving together, and name the lesson's key words: new challenges, environment, population, health, debt, globalisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
