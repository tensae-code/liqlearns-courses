# The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

## Do it first

Count slowly to ten. Now imagine that counting, person by person, not to ten but to millions — each number a person taken from a home, a family, a language. That is the scale of this lesson's story. Hold the weight of the number. This lesson tells what the Atlantic trade did to two continents.

The **Atlantic slave trade** is the largest of the trades in this course, and the lesson does not soften its scale: it **tore millions of people from Africa**, carried across the ocean in ships, over centuries. Its causes lay in the demand for labour in the **Americas**; its machinery ran through the coasts where captives were gathered and embarked.

Its **consequences reshaped two continents**. Africa lost people — above all the young and strong — and societies bent around the raiding, the wars and the fear the trade fed. The Americas were transformed the other way: built in large part on the stolen labour of the Africans carried there, who also carried their languages, skills and cultures into new worlds. One trade, two continents remade — neither was ever the same again.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Atlantic slave trade tore millions of people from Africa and reshaped two continents — Africa that lost them, and the Americas that were built on their stolen labour. Give one real example: a town that loses its young people year after year — who is left to farm, to teach the children, to defend it — the loss compounds beyond the people taken, and name the lesson's key words: Atlantic slave trade, millions, Africa, Americas, causes, consequences. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
