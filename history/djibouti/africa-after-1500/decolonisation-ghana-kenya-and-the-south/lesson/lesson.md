# Decolonisation: Ghana, Kenya and the South

## Do it first

Picture the colonial map of Africa — every land coloured with an empire's colour. Now picture the colours changing, land by land, year by year, to flags of their own. Some changes will be signed at tables. Others will be fought for. This lesson tells the great unmaking: decolonisation.

This is the lesson the whole level turns on: **the liberation of the continent**. Colony by colony, the colonial map was undone and **Africa won its flags back** — new states raising their own colours where the empires' colours had flown.

The roads differed, and the book names both: **peaceful independences**, won by negotiation, pressure and politics; and **violent independences**, won through hard struggle where the coloniser would not let go. Ghana's road, Kenya's road and the long road of the south are among the stories this process holds. Different roads, one destination — a continent of states governing themselves, facing the new questions of the lessons that follow.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Colony by colony, Africa won its flags back — the liberation of the continent came by negotiation in some lands and by hard struggle in others, until the colonial map was undone. Give one real example: a row of houses on one street regaining their own keys one by one — some by agreement with the landlord, some only after a long fight — until the whole street holds its own keys, and name the lesson's key words: decolonisation, independence, negotiation, struggle, liberation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
