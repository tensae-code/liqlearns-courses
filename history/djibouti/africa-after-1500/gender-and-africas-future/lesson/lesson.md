# Gender and Africa's Future

## Do it first

A team takes the field with half its players told to sit out — not for lack of skill, but by an old rule. Can that team be whole? Can it win? Now make the team a continent trying to unite. This lesson asks the question the guide insists on: what about the half who are women?

This lesson joins two things that are often kept apart: **the question of gender** and **the unity of the continent**. The guide's argument is direct: a continent **cannot unite while half its people are held back** — and half of Africa's people are its women.

So the full place of women — in education, in work, in public life and in the decisions of their countries — is not a side topic added to the unity project; it is a condition of it. A unity built by only half the people would be half a unity. The book sets the question before its young readers as part of Africa's future itself: the continent rises whole, or it does not rise.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The guide argues that the continent cannot unite while half its people are held back: the question of gender — the full place of women — belongs to Africa's unity and its future. Give one real example: a school that finally opens its science prizes to every pupil and discovers talent it had been wasting — the whole school rises when the held-back half is freed, and name the lesson's key words: gender, women, unity, future. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
