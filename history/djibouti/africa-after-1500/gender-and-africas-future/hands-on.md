# Hands-on — Gender and Africa's Future

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A team takes the field with half its players told to sit out — not for lack of skill, but by an old rule. Can that team be whole? Can it win? Now make the team a continent trying to unite. This lesson asks the question the guide insists on: what about the half who are women?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The guide argues that the continent cannot unite while half its people are held back: the question of gender — the full place of women — belongs to Africa's unity and its future. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
