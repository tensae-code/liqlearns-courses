# The African Renaissance

## Do it first

A great house has fallen into disrepair — but its foundations are sound and its old plans are still in a drawer. What would a renaissance of that house mean? Not copying the neighbour's house. This lesson takes that word — renaissance, rebirth — and gives it to a continent.

After the challenges, the book sets a hope with a name: the **African Renaissance** — **a call for Africa to renew itself**. A renaissance is a rebirth: a civilisation returning to strength by drawing on what is deepest in itself.

Its **foundations**, in the book's telling, are Africa's **own heritage and confidence** — the long story this whole course has told, from the first fossils to the kingdoms' art, claimed again as the ground of a new building. Its **challenges and stakes** are equally plain: the renewal must answer the real problems of the last lesson, not merely celebrate the past. Heritage as fuel, not as a museum — that is the Renaissance the book calls its children to build.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The African Renaissance is a call for the continent to renew itself — to rebuild its strength and confidence from its own heritage, facing its challenges with its own resources of culture and memory. Give one real example: a craftsman who relearns his grandfather's techniques, not from nostalgia but because they work — renewing his trade from his own heritage, and name the lesson's key words: African Renaissance, renewal, heritage, confidence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
