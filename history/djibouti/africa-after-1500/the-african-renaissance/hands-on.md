# Hands-on — The African Renaissance

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A great house has fallen into disrepair — but its foundations are sound and its old plans are still in a drawer. What would a renaissance of that house mean? Not copying the neighbour's house. This lesson takes that word — renaissance, rebirth — and gives it to a continent.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The African Renaissance is a call for the continent to renew itself — to rebuild its strength and confidence from its own heritage, facing its challenges with its own resources of culture and memory. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
