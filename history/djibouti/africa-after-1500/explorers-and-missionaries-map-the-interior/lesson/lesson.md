# Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior

## Do it first

A stranger knocks at a town's gate. He says he has come to see the land, to teach, to trade. The town lets him in. Years later, soldiers arrive who have read his maps and his letters. Was the first knock innocent? This lesson tells how Europe's presence began — and what followed behind it.

Europe's presence in Africa took **forms**, in an order the lesson insists on. First came the **travellers, missionaries, explorers and traders**: voyages of discovery, missions carrying a new faith, exploration of the interior, and commerce along the coasts and rivers. These were the faces Africa first knew.

But the lesson refuses to stop at the first faces. Behind the travellers and missions came **settlement** — and behind settlement, conquest. The maps the explorers drew, the reports the missionaries sent and the trading posts the merchants planted became the knowledge and the footholds from which the taking of the land was organised. First the knock at the gate; then, in the next lessons, the seizure of the house.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Europe's presence in Africa came in stages: travellers, missionaries, explorers and traders arrived first — and settlement and conquest followed behind them. Give one real example: a visitor welcomed to survey a family's land for a school, whose drawings are later used by others to claim the land — the welcome and the use made of it are two different things, and name the lesson's key words: explorers, missionaries, travellers, trade, European presence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
