# The Indian Ocean and Trans-Saharan Slave Trades

## Do it first

The Atlantic is not the only ocean in this story, and ships are not the only carriers. Picture two older roads out of Africa: a caravan line crossing the desert northward, and sailing boats leaving the east coast. Who is walking in those lines, against their will? This lesson follows those two trades.

Two great trades carried enslaved Africans away long before and long beside the Atlantic one. The **trans-Saharan trade** moved captives north on **caravans** across the desert; the **Indian Ocean trade** moved them east on **dhows** — the sailing boats of that ocean — from Africa's eastern coast. Both ran for **centuries**.

The lesson weighs their **causes and consequences** together: demand in the lands beyond desert and ocean pulled the trades onward, supply was fed by war and raiding within Africa, and the consequences fell on the continent — people drained away, communities living under the threat of capture, and routes of ordinary trade shadowed by the trade in human beings.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: For centuries, caravans across the Sahara and dhows across the Indian Ocean carried enslaved Africans out of the continent — the trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean slave trades, their causes and their consequences. Give one real example: two roads leaving one town in opposite directions, each carrying people away who will never return — the town is changed by both roads, not only the famous one, and name the lesson's key words: trans-Saharan trade, Indian Ocean trade, caravans, dhows, enslaved. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
