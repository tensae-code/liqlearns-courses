# African Resistance to Colonisation

## Do it first

A stranger arrives with a paper and says: sign, and your land is ours. Some sign under pressure. What happens next in town after town across a continent? This lesson answers: people fought — from the very first treaties onward. The conquest was never a walk into empty, silent land.

The conquest of Africa was **fought**, not simply received. From the **first treaties** onward — the papers pressed on rulers as the opening move of taking — African peoples organised **resistance movements** against the conquest: refusing, fighting, and contesting the seizure of their lands and their rule.

Place this lesson beside the Berlin lesson and the story corrects itself. The powers wrote their rules in Europe, but on African ground every advance met people defending their own country. Resistance did not wait for the colonial systems to settle in; it began with the conquest itself, and its memory feeds directly into the liberation movements of the next lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: African peoples fought the colonial conquest from the first treaties onward — resistance was not an afterthought to colonisation; it began with the conquest itself. Give one real example: a neighbourhood that organises against an unjust eviction from the first notice, not years later — resistance beginning with the threat itself, and name the lesson's key words: resistance, conquest, treaties. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
