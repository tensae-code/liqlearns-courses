# Colonisation: The Berlin Conference

## Do it first

Imagine the owners of your town's land being decided in a meeting in a far capital — a meeting to which nobody from your town was invited — where the deciders draw up rules for who may take which street. That is the shape of this lesson. The town is a continent. The meeting is Berlin.

At the **Berlin Conference**, the **European powers** sat down together and did something extraordinary in its coldness: they **fixed the rules for occupying Africa**. Rival empires that might have fought each other over the continent instead agreed on the terms of taking it — how claims would be made, recognised and held.

Notice where the meeting happened and who was not there. The rules for Africa's land were written in **Europe**, at a table with no Africans seated at it. Yet those paper rules governed what followed on the ground: the rush of occupation, the drawing of boundaries, and the colonial systems of the next lesson. Berlin is where the conquest was given its rulebook.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: European powers met in Berlin and wrote the rules for taking African land — a conference about Africa, held in Europe, that set the terms of the occupation to come. Give one real example: a school timetable written by people who never asked the pupils — the rules are real and will be enforced, which is exactly why it matters who wrote them, and name the lesson's key words: Berlin Conference, occupation rules, partition. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
