# Neocolonialism

## Do it first

The flag over the government house changes. The governor sails home. Has the control ended? Look at who still owns the mines, sets the prices, lends the money. This lesson gives a name to control that stays after the flag changes — and teaches you to look for it.

The lesson begins with a definition. **Neocolonialism** is the continuation of foreign control after formal independence: the flag changes, the anthem is sung — and yet the decisive levers of the country's life remain in outside hands.

Its **practices** work without governors: control through money lent and owed, through trade terms set elsewhere, through companies and influence that steer a young state's choices. Its **consequences** are the lesson's warning — independence that is real in law can be narrowed in fact, and a people must learn to see control that no longer wears a uniform. Naming it is the first defence against it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Neocolonialism is control without the flag: after independence, foreign control found new ways to stay — through money, trade and influence rather than governors. Give one real example: a shop whose old owner 'sold' it but still supplies all its goods, sets its prices and holds its debt — the sign changed; the control did not, and name the lesson's key words: neocolonialism, independence, control, influence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
