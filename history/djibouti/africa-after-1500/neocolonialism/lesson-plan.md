# Lesson plan — Neocolonialism

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Neocolonialism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Independence, Unity and Africa's Future (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Neocolonialism is control without the flag: after independence, foreign control found new ways to stay — through money, trade and influence rather than governors.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Independence, Unity and Africa's Future — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Neocolonialism is control without the flag: after independence, foreign control found new ways to stay — through money, trade and influence rather than governors.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a shop whose old owner 'sold' it but still supplies all its goods, sets its prices and holds its debt — the sign changed; the control did not.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks independence ended foreign control completely → the flag changed, but control found new ways to stay; that staying is neocolonialism.
- Go back to the one thing: Neocolonialism is control without the flag: after independence, foreign control found new ways to stay — through money, trade and influence rather than governors. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
