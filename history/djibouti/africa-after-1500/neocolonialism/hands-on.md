# Hands-on — Neocolonialism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The flag over the government house changes. The governor sails home. Has the control ended? Look at who still owns the mines, sets the prices, lends the money. This lesson gives a name to control that stays after the flag changes — and teaches you to look for it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Neocolonialism is control without the flag: after independence, foreign control found new ways to stay — through money, trade and influence rather than governors. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
