# Lesson plan — African Unity: The Founding of the OAU

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** African Unity: The Founding of the OAU
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Independence, Unity and Africa's Future (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The new states tried to stand together: pan-Africanism inspired the founding of the OAU and later the African Union, with regional integrations beside them — while borders, rivalries and outside interests kept pulling the unity project back.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Independence, Unity and Africa's Future — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pan-Africanism, the OAU, the African Union and Regional Integrations** — Behind the founding stands an idea older than the states themselves: pan-Africanism — the conviction that African peoples share one fate and should stand together.
2. **The Obstacles to African Unity** — The book is honest about the drag on the project.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: Neocolonialism — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The new states tried to stand together: pan-Africanism inspired the founding of the OAU and later the African Union, with regional integrations beside them — while borders, rivalries and outside interests kept pulling the unity project back.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: small shops on one street forming an association to bargain together — stronger united, yet each shopkeeper still guards his own till; unity and rivalry in one picture.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.
