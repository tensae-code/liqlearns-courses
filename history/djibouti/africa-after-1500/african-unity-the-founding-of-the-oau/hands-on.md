# Hands-on — African Unity: The Founding of the OAU

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Thirty separate households on one street each lock their own door. What can they not do alone that they could do together — against fire, against thieves, in bargaining with the town? The new African states asked the same question at the scale of a continent. This lesson tells what they built — and what kept pulling it back.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The new states tried to stand together: pan-Africanism inspired the founding of the OAU and later the African Union, with regional integrations beside them — while borders, rivalries and outside interests kept pulling the unity project back. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
