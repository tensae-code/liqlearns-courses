# Video script — African Diasporas, Ancient and Modern

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Africans scattered across the world — by slavery, by migration, by exile — form diasporas ancient, modern and contemporary: they keep building the modern world, stay tied to home, and their departure by brain drain is a loss Africa counts.

The course closes by following the Africans who left. The **diasporas** — Africans living scattered outside the continent — are **ancient, modern and contemporary**: communities formed across the centuries, first and most terribly by the slave trades, and in later times by migration and exile.

Two truths close the book's story. First, the diasporas' **contributions**: scattered Africans **keep building the modern world** — its labour, arts, sciences and struggles — and they **stay tied to home**, sending back money, ideas and loyalty. Second, the cost the book names plainly: the **brain drain**, the departure of the trained and talented, is a loss the continent counts even as it honours its children abroad. The story of Africa, the course ends by saying, is also written wherever Africans are.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school.
