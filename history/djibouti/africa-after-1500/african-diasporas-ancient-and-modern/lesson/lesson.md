# African Diasporas, Ancient and Modern

## Do it first

Seeds blown from a tree land in other soils and grow there. Are they still part of the tree's story? Africans live on every continent — some carried by force long ago, some gone in our own time for work and study. This final lesson follows them, and asks what ties them to home.

The course closes by following the Africans who left. The **diasporas** — Africans living scattered outside the continent — are **ancient, modern and contemporary**: communities formed across the centuries, first and most terribly by the slave trades, and in later times by migration and exile.

Two truths close the book's story. First, the diasporas' **contributions**: scattered Africans **keep building the modern world** — its labour, arts, sciences and struggles — and they **stay tied to home**, sending back money, ideas and loyalty. Second, the cost the book names plainly: the **brain drain**, the departure of the trained and talented, is a loss the continent counts even as it honours its children abroad. The story of Africa, the course ends by saying, is also written wherever Africans are.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Africans scattered across the world — by slavery, by migration, by exile — form diasporas ancient, modern and contemporary: they keep building the modern world, stay tied to home, and their departure by brain drain is a loss Africa counts. Give one real example: a family member working abroad who sends money home, calls on feast days and plans to return — gone in body, tied in loyalty; the family counts both the help and the absence, and name the lesson's key words: diasporas, slavery, migration, exile, brain drain, contributions. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
