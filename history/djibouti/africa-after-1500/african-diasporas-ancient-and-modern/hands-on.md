# Hands-on — African Diasporas, Ancient and Modern

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Seeds blown from a tree land in other soils and grow there. Are they still part of the tree's story? Africans live on every continent — some carried by force long ago, some gone in our own time for work and study. This final lesson follows them, and asks what ties them to home.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Africans scattered across the world — by slavery, by migration, by exile — form diasporas ancient, modern and contemporary: they keep building the modern world, stay tied to home, and their departure by brain drain is a loss Africa counts. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
