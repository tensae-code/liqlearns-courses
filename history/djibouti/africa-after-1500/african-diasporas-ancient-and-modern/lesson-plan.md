# Lesson plan — African Diasporas, Ancient and Modern

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** African Diasporas, Ancient and Modern
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Independence, Unity and Africa's Future (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africans scattered across the world — by slavery, by migration, by exile — form diasporas ancient, modern and contemporary: they keep building the modern world, stay tied to home, and their departure by brain drain is a loss Africa counts.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Independence, Unity and Africa's Future — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: Gender and Africa's Future — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Africans scattered across the world — by slavery, by migration, by exile — form diasporas ancient, modern and contemporary: they keep building the modern world, stay tied to home, and their departure by brain drain is a loss Africa counts.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a family member working abroad who sends money home, calls on feast days and plans to return — gone in body, tied in loyalty; the family counts both the help and the absence.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: an animated-map style picture of the world with lines running from Africa to the Americas, Europe and beyond, and thinner lines running back — remittances, visits, ideas — the student traces one line out and one line back and says what travels on each.
   - ADD GIF: a short GIF of points of light spreading from Africa across a world map over the centuries, then fine threads linking the far points back home — the student watches and says what the threads stand for.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing and gives a real example (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Africans abroad are lost to Africa's story → the diasporas keep building the modern world and stay tied to home; the book counts their contributions and the brain drain alike.
- Go back to the one thing: Africans scattered across the world — by slavery, by migration, by exile — form diasporas ancient, modern and contemporary: they keep building the modern world, stay tied to home, and their departure by brain drain is a loss Africa counts. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
