# Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule

## Do it first

Two head teachers run two schools differently: one manages every classroom herself; the other rules through the class prefects. The styles differ. Who is in charge in both schools? Hold that question. This lesson compares the colonial systems — and gives the same answer for all of them.

Once the land was taken, each empire had to **rule** it, and the empires chose different **systems**. Under **direct rule**, the coloniser's own officials governed. Under **indirect rule**, the coloniser governed through local rulers kept under its command. South Africa's system hardened into **apartheid** — rule by forced separation of peoples.

The lesson's judgement is plain, and it is the point of the comparison: **each empire rules differently, but every system puts the coloniser on top**. The differences matter — they shaped daily life differently in different colonies — but no arrangement of colonial rule made the colonised the masters of their own country. That is what 'colonial system' means, in all its forms.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The colonial empires ruled in different systems — direct rule, indirect rule, and apartheid — but every system, however it was arranged, put the coloniser on top. Give one real example: two managers running shops in different styles — one deciding everything, one working through supervisors — while the ownership, and the profit, stay in the same hands, and name the lesson's key words: direct rule, indirect rule, apartheid, colonial systems. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
