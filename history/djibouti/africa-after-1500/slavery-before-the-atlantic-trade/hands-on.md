# Hands-on — Slavery Before the Atlantic Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
When you hear the word slavery in Africa's story, your mind jumps to ships crossing the Atlantic. Push that picture aside for one lesson. Ask instead: what forms of captivity and dependence existed inside the continent before those ships came? This lesson answers honestly — the older story comes first.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before the Atlantic ships, captivity and dependence already existed inside Africa in several forms — internal slavery, captivity, dependence and servitude were part of the continent's older story. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
