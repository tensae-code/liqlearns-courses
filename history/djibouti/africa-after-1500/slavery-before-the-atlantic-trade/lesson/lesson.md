# Slavery Before the Atlantic Trade

## Do it first

When you hear the word slavery in Africa's story, your mind jumps to ships crossing the Atlantic. Push that picture aside for one lesson. Ask instead: what forms of captivity and dependence existed inside the continent before those ships came? This lesson answers honestly — the older story comes first.

This lesson stands **before the ships**. Long before the Atlantic trade, African societies already knew **forms of unfreedom**: **internal slavery**, **captivity** — prisoners taken in war — and wider conditions of **dependence and servitude**, where a person's labour and movement belonged, in different degrees, to another.

The forms were not one thing, and the lesson keeps them distinct: a captive's fate, a dependent's place in a household, a servant's obligations — each had its own rules and its own harshness. Naming them honestly matters for everything after: when the ocean trades arrive in the next lessons, they will grow on ground where captivity already existed — and they will change its scale and nature beyond recognition.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Before the Atlantic ships, captivity and dependence already existed inside Africa in several forms — internal slavery, captivity, dependence and servitude were part of the continent's older story. Give one real example: the difference between a servant bound to a household, a prisoner taken in war, and a person sold across an ocean — three different conditions that must not be blurred into one, and name the lesson's key words: internal slavery, captivity, dependence, servitude. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
