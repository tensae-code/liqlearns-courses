# Lesson plan — Africa's Internal Transformations, 1500–1900

**Course:** History — Djibouti · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Africa's Internal Transformations, 1500–1900
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Slavery, Colonisation and Resistance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Between 1500 and 1900 Africa's societies kept changing from their own internal dynamics — the great socio-political formations of those centuries rose from Africa's own forces, not from European triggers.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Slavery, Colonisation and Resistance — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order: Resistance to Slavery and the Slave Trades — recommended first (the soft hint: "if you know this one, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first"), never forced. A learner can land on this lesson directly and learn it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Between 1500 and 1900 Africa's societies kept changing from their own internal dynamics — the great socio-political formations of those centuries rose from Africa's own forces, not from European triggers.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a town that rebuilds itself after a fire by its own carpenters' decisions — outsiders may trade at its market, but the rebuilding plan is the town's own.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Africa only changed when Europeans acted → the guide insists Africa's own forces drove these transformations; Europeans did not trigger them.
- Go back to the one thing: Between 1500 and 1900 Africa's societies kept changing from their own internal dynamics — the great socio-political formations of those centuries rose from Africa's own forces, not from European triggers. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
