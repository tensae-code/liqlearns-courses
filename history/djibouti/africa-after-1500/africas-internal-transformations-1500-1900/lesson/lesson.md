# Africa's Internal Transformations, 1500–1900

## Do it first

A river changes its course. Do you explain it by the sea it flows to — or by the land, the rain and the river's own waters? Between 1500 and 1900, African societies changed greatly. This lesson asks where the force of that change came from — and the book's answer may surprise you.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Great Socio-Political Formations, 1500–1900

The centuries from **1500 to 1900** were not a waiting room in Africa's story. Across the continent, societies kept **changing**: new **great socio-political formations** — states and societies on a large scale — rose, grew, and transformed the regions around them.

Read those four centuries as African history in motion: peoples reorganising, powers rising and answering new conditions, ways of life remade. The continent of 1900 was not the continent of 1500, and the difference was made, in large part, on the continent itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Africa's Own Forces: the Endogenous Causes

The guide is blunt about the cause. These transformations had **endogenous causes** — causes from inside. **Europeans did not trigger these changes; Africa's own forces did**: its peoples' decisions, rivalries, ideas, trade and ambitions drove the remaking of its societies.

Hold that sentence against a lazy habit of storytelling, in which Africa only moves when pushed from outside. This lesson pushes back: between 1500 and 1900 the pushing, building and breaking were overwhelmingly Africa's own work — the ground on which the later, violent arrival of colonial rule would fall.
