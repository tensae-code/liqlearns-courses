# Liberation Movements

## Do it first

One person refusing is brave. A whole people organised — with leaders, plans and a single aim spoken aloud — is something else. What is that aim, when the thing being fought is colonial rule itself? This lesson names it, and tells how resistance grew into it.

Resistance **grew into organisation**. The fights against conquest — local, scattered, heroic — matured over the colonial years into **liberation movements**: organised movements of whole peoples, with leadership, programmes and discipline.

And a liberation movement is known by its aim, spoken plainly: **independence** — the end of colonial rule and the recovery of the country's government by its own people. Where the first resistance said 'not this treaty, not this seizure', the movements said 'not this rule at all'. That growth — from refusal to a movement with one aim — is the bridge between the conquest lessons and the independences that open the next level.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Resistance grew up: scattered fights against conquest became organised liberation movements — movements with one declared aim, independence. Give one real example: neighbours who first protested separately, then formed one association with elected leaders and one written demand — the same anger, organised, and name the lesson's key words: liberation movements, independence, organisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
