# Video script — Why Europe Colonised: Motives and the Civilizing Mission

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Europeans colonised Rwanda for what they could take and where they could sell: wolfram, gold and coltan, fertile soil and markets for their goods, and to spread Christianity — a hunt driven by the Industrial Revolution at home.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Why did Europeans want Rwanda? The book answers plainly. Under its hills lay wolfram, gold and coltan. Its soil was fertile. Its people were a market where European goods could be sold. And the missionaries wanted to spread Christianity across the country. Behind it all stood the Industrial Revolution: Europe's new factories were hungry — for raw materials to feed them and markets to sell what they made — and that hunger drove the hunt for colonies.

Remember it this way: Europeans colonised Rwanda for what they could take and where they could sell: wolfram, gold and coltan, fertile soil and markets for their goods, and to spread Christianity.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
