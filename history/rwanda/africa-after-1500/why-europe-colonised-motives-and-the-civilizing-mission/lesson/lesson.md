# Why Europe Colonised: Motives and the Civilizing Mission

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Why did Europeans want Rwanda? The book answers plainly. Under its hills lay wolfram, gold and coltan. Its soil was fertile. Its people were a market where European goods could be sold. And the missionaries wanted to spread Christianity across the country. Behind it all stood the Industrial Revolution: Europe's new factories were hungry — for raw materials to feed them and markets to sell what they made — and that hunger drove the hunt for colonies.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Sort the motives into three baskets: take (minerals, soil), sell (markets), and spread (Christianity). Every motive in the lesson fits one basket.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Europeans colonised Rwanda for what they could take and where they could sell: wolfram, gold and coltan, fertile soil and markets for their goods, and to spread Christianity — a hunt driven by the Industrial Revolution at home. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
