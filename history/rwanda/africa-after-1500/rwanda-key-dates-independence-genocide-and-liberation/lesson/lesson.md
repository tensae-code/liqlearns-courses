# Rwanda: Key Dates — Independence, Genocide and Liberation

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The youngest historians in Rwanda learn their country's story as three dates on the timeline line. First, 1962: the country gains independence. Then 1994, the heaviest year: the Genocide against the Tutsi — and then, on 4 July of that same year, Liberation. And 2016: Rwanda hosts the African Union summit, the continent meeting on Rwandan soil. Past, marked in years a child can place: freedom, catastrophe and rescue, and a country hosting Africa.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: 1962 — free. 1994 — genocide, then liberation on 4 July. 2016 — the summit. Say the three dates aloud once and the timeline holds them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Three dates carry modern Rwanda on the timeline: 1962, independence; 1994, the Genocide against the Tutsi and then Liberation on 4 July; and 2016, when Rwanda hosted the African Union summit. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
