# Video script — Rwanda: Liberation War 1990–1994

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Liberation War began on 1 October 1990, when the RPF attacked from Uganda to end exile and discrimination: Rwigema fell at the start, Kagame took command, the Arusha Accords were signed in 1993 — and in 1994 the RPF stopped the genocide and liberated Kigali on 4 July.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

On 1 October 1990 the RPF attacked from Uganda. Its fighters were the children of exile, and their aim was to end exile and discrimination and come home. The war's first days took its commander: Rwigema fell, and Kagame took command. Fighting and talking went together — in 1993 the Arusha Accords were signed. Then came April 1994 and the genocide, and the RPF fought through it, stopped it, and liberated Kigali on 4 July 1994.

The teacher's guide teaches this war by story and evidence: the teacher narrates a short Liberation War story, shows photographs and a film clip, and learners must then explain it themselves — how the injustices, divisionism and corruption of the two Republics drove exiles to fight their way home. The war is taught as the answer to a question the two Republics left open.

Remember it this way: The Liberation War began on 1 October 1990, when the RPF attacked from Uganda to end exile and discrimination: Rwigema fell at the start, Kagame took command, the Arusha Accords were signed in 1993.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
