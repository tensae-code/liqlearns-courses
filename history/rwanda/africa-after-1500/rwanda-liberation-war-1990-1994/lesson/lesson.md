# Rwanda: Liberation War 1990–1994

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 11.3: The Liberation War

On 1 October 1990 the RPF attacked from Uganda. Its fighters were the children of exile, and their aim was to end exile and discrimination and come home. The war's first days took its commander: Rwigema fell, and Kagame took command. Fighting and talking went together — in 1993 the Arusha Accords were signed. Then came April 1994 and the genocide, and the RPF fought through it, stopped it, and liberated Kigali on 4 July 1994.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: 1990 attack, commander falls, Kagame commands, 1993 Accords, 4 July 1994 Kigali. Five beats — the war in one breath.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 11 (guide): Teaching post-independent Rwanda

The teacher's guide teaches this war by story and evidence: the teacher narrates a short Liberation War story, shows photographs and a film clip, and learners must then explain it themselves — how the injustices, divisionism and corruption of the two Republics drove exiles to fight their way home. The war is taught as the answer to a question the two Republics left open.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Liberation War began on 1 October 1990, when the RPF attacked from Uganda to end exile and discrimination: Rwigema fell at the start, Kagame took command, the Arusha Accords were signed in 1993 — and in 1994 the RPF stopped the genocide and liberated Kigali on 4 July. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
