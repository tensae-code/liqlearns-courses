# Rwanda: First Republic 1962–1973

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Independence did not arrive quietly. After the violence of 1959, a referendum in 1961 abolished the monarchy itself, and the republic that followed was Kayibanda's: his PARMEHUTU party ruled alone, one party over the whole country. The republic imposed a 9% quota on Tutsi and drove Tutsi out of schools. A republic that began by ending kings ended by closing doors — and on 5 July 1973 the army's Habyarimana overthrew Kayibanda, and the First Republic fell.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: First Republic in one line: referendum ends the kings, one party rules, a 9% quota shuts doors, a general ends it in 1973.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The First Republic was born from the 1959 violence and the 1961 referendum that abolished the monarchy: Kayibanda's PARMEHUTU republic ruled as one party, imposed a 9% Tutsi quota and drove Tutsi from schools — until Habyarimana overthrew Kayibanda on 5 July 1973. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
