# Video script — Why Some Africans Collaborated

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Not every African resisted: some peoples, weakened by disease, rinderpest and conflict — like the Maasai and the Banyankole — or hoping for goods, protection or missionary favour, chose to work with the Europeans.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

The book asks an honest question: why did some Africans work with the Europeans instead of fighting them? Part of the answer is weakness. The Maasai and the Banyankole had been struck by disease, by rinderpest that killed their cattle, and by conflict — a people counting its dead herds cannot easily count its spears. And part of the answer is hope: goods, protection against enemies, or the favour of the missionaries. Collaboration, the lesson shows, was often survival wearing another name.

Remember it this way: Not every African resisted: some peoples, weakened by disease, rinderpest and conflict.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
