# Rwanda: Road to Independence

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 10.6: Berlin, German rule and Rudahigwa's reforms

The road begins in a European conference room: at Berlin in 1884–85, Rwanda was handed to Germany, which ruled through the king. Then a new king changed the country's course. Rudahigwa abolished the old burdens — Ubuhake, the cattle-clientship, and Uburetwa, forced labour — opposed the tribal identity cards the Belgians had imposed, and in 1954 demanded independence outright. He died in Bujumbura in 1959, with the demand still ringing.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Walk the road in five stops: Berlin, rule through the king, Rudahigwa's reforms, the parties, 1 July 1962. Five stops, whole road.

## Sub lesson 2 — Units 10.6–10.7: Parties and independence

After Rudahigwa, the future was fought over by parties: MSM, APROSOMA, UNAR, RADER and MDR-PARMEHUTU each promised a different Rwanda. In November 1959 chaos broke out and Tutsi fled the country. The fight ended at the ballot and the flag: on 1 July 1962 Rwanda became independent, with Grégoire Kayibanda as its first President.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 10 Lessons 9–10: Road to independence and independence (guide)

The teacher's guide sequences the whole road for the classroom: Rudahigwa first, then the five parties and the reasons each was formed, and the road ending where it must — at independence on 1 July 1962. The guide's order is the lesson's order: reformer, parties, flag.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rwanda's road ran from Berlin's gift of the country to Germany, through rule through the king, to King Rudahigwa — who abolished Ubuhake and Uburetwa, opposed the tribal identity cards and demanded independence in 1954 — then through five parties and the violence of 1959, to independence on 1 July 1962 under Grégoire Kayibanda. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
