# Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 10.5: Explorers and missionaries in Rwanda

Before the administrators came the travellers. Baumann and von Götzen explored Rwanda's interior. Then came the missions: the White Fathers, under Bishop Hirth, founded the Save mission in 1900, and CMS Protestants followed. The missions built schools and hospitals — real gifts — but they also undermined Rwandan culture, and their stations paved the road that colonial rule would travel.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Explorers drew the maps, missionaries built the stations — and the colonial rulers walked in behind both. That order is the lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 13.1: Explorers in East Africa

The wider East African story begins at sea: in 1498 Vasco da Gama sailed seeking the sea route to India. Centuries later Speke reached Lake Victoria and found the Nile's source. Livingstone travelled fighting the slave trade, and Stanley crossed the continent to find him. Explorer by explorer, their maps opened the interior — and colonisation followed the maps.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 13.2: Missionaries in East Africa

The missionaries came to stay. Krapf reached Rabai in 1844; Mackay worked in Uganda. Their missions brought literacy, hospitals, churches and new crops, and they helped abolish the slave trade. East Africa's schools and clinics begin in these stations — and so, the books hold both truths together, did the road in for foreign rule.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Explorers and missionaries opened the interior before the rulers came: Baumann, von Götzen, da Gama, Speke and Livingstone mapped and travelled it, and missions — the White Fathers at Save in 1900, Krapf, Mackay and the CMS — brought schools, hospitals and literacy, helped end the slave trade, and at the same time undermined local culture and paved the way for colonialism. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
