# Video script — Explorers and Missionaries Map the Interior

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Explorers and missionaries opened the interior before the rulers came: Baumann, von Götzen, da Gama, Speke and Livingstone mapped and travelled it, and missions — the White Fathers at Save in 1900, Krapf, Mackay and the CMS — brought schools, hospitals and literacy, helped end the slave trade, and at the same time undermined local culture and paved the way for colonialism.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Before the administrators came the travellers. Baumann and von Götzen explored Rwanda's interior. Then came the missions: the White Fathers, under Bishop Hirth, founded the Save mission in 1900, and CMS Protestants followed. The missions built schools and hospitals — real gifts — but they also undermined Rwandan culture, and their stations paved the road that colonial rule would travel.

The wider East African story begins at sea: in 1498 Vasco da Gama sailed seeking the sea route to India. Centuries later Speke reached Lake Victoria and found the Nile's source. Livingstone travelled fighting the slave trade, and Stanley crossed the continent to find him. Explorer by explorer, their maps opened the interior — and colonisation followed the maps.

The missionaries came to stay. Krapf reached Rabai in 1844; Mackay worked in Uganda. Their missions brought literacy, hospitals, churches and new crops, and they helped abolish the slave trade. East Africa's schools and clinics begin in these stations — and so, the books hold both truths together, did the road in for foreign rule.

Remember it this way: Explorers and missionaries opened the interior before the rulers came: Baumann, von Götzen, da Gama, Speke and Livingstone mapped and travelled it, and missions.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
