# Video script — Colonisation: Its Effects on Africa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Colonisers like Carl Peters for Germany and Lugard for Britain planted colonial rule in Africa, and it cut both ways: administration, roads, western schooling and medicine, and new nations — bought with foreign rule itself, while the foreigners met wild animals, disease, hostile peoples and language barriers.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Two names stand for the planting of colonial rule: Carl Peters, who worked for Germany, and Lugard, who worked for Britain. What they planted changed Africa for good and ill. It brought administration, roads, western schooling and medicine, and it drew the new nations on the map. And the foreigners themselves paid prices they had not expected — wild animals, disease, hostile peoples who fought them, and language barriers at every station. Colonialism's effects, the book insists, are a ledger with entries on both sides.

Remember it this way: Colonisers like Carl Peters for Germany and Lugard for Britain planted colonial rule in Africa, and it cut both ways: administration, roads, western schooling and medicine, and new nations.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
