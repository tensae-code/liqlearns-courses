# Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The teacher's guide sets out the forms plainly. A colony is ruled outright by the foreign power; a protectorate keeps a local ruler under foreign control. Rwanda knew both hands. Germany ruled indirectly — through the king, leaving the throne standing while real power leaned on it. Belgium ruled directly: its officials governed themselves, and the king's power was stripped away. Same country, two systems — and the difference decided how Rwandans experienced colonial rule.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Indirect = through the king. Direct = over the king. One word each, and the whole lesson is held.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Colonial powers ruled in two forms — the colony and the protectorate — and Rwanda felt both hands: Germany ruled indirectly, through the king, while Belgium ruled directly and stripped the king's power. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
