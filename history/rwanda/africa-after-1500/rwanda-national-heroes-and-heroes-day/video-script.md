# Video script — Rwanda: National Heroes and Heroes Day

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Rwanda's heroes are honoured for what they did: they ended the genocide, freed the country from bad rule and promoted unity — Rudahigwa demanding independence, Rwagasana fighting divisionism, Sister Félicité Niyitegeka refusing to abandon Tutsi families, and, in the guide's matching, Rwigema and Uwilingiyimana beside them.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

What did the heroes do? The book answers with deeds: heroes ended the genocide, freed the country from bad rule, and promoted unity where division had ruled. Rudahigwa stands for the demand for independence. Rwagasana stands against divisionism. And Sister Félicité Niyitegeka stands for a refusal: when killers came, she would not abandon the Tutsi families sheltering with her. Heroes Day exists so these deeds are named out loud, every year.

The teacher's guide turns honour into an exercise: its answers pair each hero with what they did — Rwigema, Rudahigwa, Rwagasana, Uwilingiyimana and Sister Félicité, name matched to deed. A hero you cannot match to a deed, the guide implies, is a hero you do not yet know.

Remember it this way: Rwanda's heroes are honoured for what they did: they ended the genocide, freed the country from bad rule and promoted unity.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
