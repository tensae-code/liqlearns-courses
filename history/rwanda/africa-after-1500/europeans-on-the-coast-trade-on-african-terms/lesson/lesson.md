# Europeans on the Coast: Trade on African Terms

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Colonial rule in Rwanda did not begin with an army. It began with a company. In 1897 Carl Peters founded the German East Africa Company in Rwanda, and its men traded — minerals and skins bought and sold. Trade looked peaceful, but it planted a foreign power inside the country: after the traders came the administrators, and the company town became the colony's door.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Traders first, rulers after. When a lesson asks how colonial rule began in Rwanda, start with the company, not the flag.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In Rwanda the traders came before the rulers: Carl Peters founded the German East Africa Company in 1897, trading minerals and skins — and the administrators followed the traders in. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
