# Rwanda: 1994 Genocide Against the Tutsi

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 12.1–12.2: Meaning and causes of the Genocide

The book begins with causes, because the genocide was not a sudden madness. Colonial rule turned Rwanda's social classes into fixed ethnic groups and stamped them on identity cards. After independence came divisionism, poor leadership and impunity — killers never punished — and around the president the Akazu circle, the Hutu Power movement, the Kangura newspaper and RTLM radio poured out hatred year after year. In April 1994 the built hatred exploded.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: This lesson has a before, a during and an after. Before: hatred built. During: planned killing. After: unity, healing, refusal of denial. Hold the three and you hold the lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Units 12.3–12.4: Planning, execution and how it was stopped

The killing was planned: the Akazu prepared a 'final solution' — lists of 'traitors' to be killed, machetes imported in bulk, and the Interahamwe and Impuzamugambi militia trained to do it. Prime Minister Uwilingiyimana was murdered at the start. The genocide was stopped the only way it could be: the RPF defeated the perpetrators, and on 19 July 1994 a Government of National Unity was formed over a ruined country.

## Sub lesson 3 — Units 12.6–12.8: Ideology, denial, prevention and consequences

The lesson ends facing forward. Genocide ideology and denial are crimes, because the lie is how the killing tries to live on. Prevention means unity, healing, and reconciliation through the Abunzi mediators. And the consequences are counted in people: widows, orphans, the displaced — and a ruined economy the survivors had to rebuild from nothing.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 12 (guide): Teaching the Genocide unit

The teacher's guide sequences the whole unit as evidence work: meaning, causes, planning, execution, the Holocaust comparison, ideology and denial, prevention and consequences — with memorial-site field visits as core evidence work, where learners stand where it happened and read the proof themselves.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi was built before it exploded: colonial rule hardened social classes into ethnic groups with identity cards, and divisionism, poor leadership, impunity, the Akazu, Hutu Power, Kangura and RTLM built the hatred — then came the planned killing, lists and militia, the murder of Prime Minister Uwilingiyimana, and the RPF's victory and Government of National Unity on 19 July 1994; what remains is to refuse the ideology and denial, and to heal through unity and the Abunzi. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
