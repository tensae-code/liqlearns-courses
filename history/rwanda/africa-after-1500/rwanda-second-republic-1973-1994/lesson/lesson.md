# Rwanda: Second Republic 1973–1994

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Habyarimana's republic began by closing the country's politics: the constitution was suspended, parties were banned, and the MRND ruled as a one-party state. For years that was the whole system. Then pressure — inside and outside — forced the door: in 1991 parties were allowed again. Three transitional governments followed, led in turn by Nsanzimana, Nsengiyaremye and Uwilingiyimana, each trying to carry a divided country toward a settlement it never safely reached.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Second Republic: one party (MRND) for most of it, then the door cracks in 1991 — three transitional governments walk through it. Learn the three names in order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Habyarimana's Second Republic suspended the constitution, banned parties and ruled through the MRND one-party state — until pressure forced parties open in 1991, and three transitional governments under Nsanzimana, Nsengiyaremye and Uwilingiyimana tried to carry the country through. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
