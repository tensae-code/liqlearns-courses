# Video script — Rwanda Resistance: King Yuhi V Musinga

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
King Yuhi V Musinga refused Belgium's direct rule and the loss of his power — so the Belgians and the Catholic Church exiled him, to Kamembe and then Moba in 1931, and he died in exile in 1944; his fall taught Rwandans what resistance cost, and how the Belgians divided Rwandans to rule them.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

When Belgium took direct rule, King Yuhi V Musinga refused. He would not accept the loss of his power or govern as Belgium's servant. The answer of the Belgians — backed by the Catholic Church — was exile: in 1931 the king was driven out, first to Kamembe, then further, to Moba. He never came home. Musinga died in exile in 1944, a king who had said no and paid for it with everything.

The teacher's guide walks pupils through the resistance lesson by lesson: why Rwandans resisted — the king's power lowered, and Rwandans divided against each other by the Belgians — and what Musinga's exile cost the country. Resistance here is taught as a question, not just a story: what does a people lose when its king is taken, and what does it keep?

Remember it this way: King Yuhi V Musinga refused Belgium's direct rule and the loss of his power.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
