# Rwanda Resistance: King Yuhi V Musinga

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 10.3: Reaction of Rwandans to colonial rule

When Belgium took direct rule, King Yuhi V Musinga refused. He would not accept the loss of his power or govern as Belgium's servant. The answer of the Belgians — backed by the Catholic Church — was exile: in 1931 the king was driven out, first to Kamembe, then further, to Moba. He never came home. Musinga died in exile in 1944, a king who had said no and paid for it with everything.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Musinga's story is the price tag of saying no. Remember the two exile places in order: Kamembe, then Moba.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 10 Lessons 3–4: Resistance and its impact (guide)

The teacher's guide walks pupils through the resistance lesson by lesson: why Rwandans resisted — the king's power lowered, and Rwandans divided against each other by the Belgians — and what Musinga's exile cost the country. Resistance here is taught as a question, not just a story: what does a people lose when its king is taken, and what does it keep?

## Say it to your companion
