# About the History — Rwanda course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Rwanda's own
Rwanda Education Board (REB) Social Studies books. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Rwanda inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **four levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: Finding History Where You Live.**
   What history is, finding it by asking at home, at school
   and in the Sector, and dating the past on the timeline.
2. **Level 2 — Peoples and the Coast: East Africa Before the
   Europeans Ruled.** The Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite migrations
   and the Swahili coast towns built by trade.
3. **Level 3 — Colonisation, Resistance and the Road to
   Independence.** Why Europe came, explorers, missionaries
   and traders, direct and indirect rule, Musinga's refusal
   and exile, collaboration, Rudahigwa's reforms, the parties
   and 1 July 1962.
4. **Level 4 — Independent Rwanda: Heroes, Republics,
   Liberation and Genocide.** Heritage and Heroes Day,
   Rwanda's key dates, the First and Second Republics, the
   Liberation War, the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi, and
   the comparison with the Holocaust and other genocides.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Rwanda's story:* "Start where you stand: history is what
  happened in time past, and you find it by asking — parents,
  the head teacher, the elders of your Sector — and setting
  it on the timeline. Then look wider: East Africa filled
  with peoples — Bantu farmers, Nilotes down the Nile,
  herders from Ethiopia — and its coast towns grew rich on
  trade. Then the hard turn: Germany, then Belgium, ruled a
  kingdom of proud kings; Musinga refused and was exiled;
  Rudahigwa reformed and demanded freedom; and on 1 July
  1962 Rwanda stood independent. Last, the story Rwanda tells
  whole: its heroes, its two Republics, the Liberation War —
  and 1994, the Genocide against the Tutsi, stopped by
  Rwandans themselves, remembered so its ideology is refused
  forever."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Primary 2 (Books 1–2):** History enters only as the child's own place and school — ask parents and the head teacher, then judge what in a place's past was good or bad. The guide adds: the teacher must know Rwanda's history, especially of the school's locality; pupils retell family/local history to classmates and learn to avoid bad history and aim to be heroes.
- **Primary 3 (Book 3):** History widens from village to Sector (Umurenge), then becomes a drawn timeline — past/present/future — anchored on Rwanda's own dates: 1962, 1994, 2016.
- **Primary 5 (Books 5–6):** Colonial history is told through Rwanda's own kings — Musinga resists and is exiled, Rudahigwa reforms and demands independence — before the parties and 1 July 1962. The guide labels Unit 10 TOPIC AREA: HISTORY and teaches it in 13 lessons with flowcharts and guided discussion, aiming to develop nationalism and patriotism.
- **Primary 6 (Books 7–8):** The final primary year is Rwanda's own modern story told whole — the two Republics, the Liberation War, and a full unit on the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi, compared with the Holocaust, Cambodia and Bosnia. The guide teaches History by narration and evidence — the Liberation War lesson opens with the teacher narrating a short story, then photographs and a film clip, then group discussion and short written notes.
