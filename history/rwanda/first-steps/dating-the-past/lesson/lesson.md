# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 9.2: The timeline (umurongo ndangagihe)

A boy hears stories but cannot order them — which came first, which came after? He is taught the timeline, the umurongo ndangagihe: a line with three parts. Behind us is the past, where finished events belong. In the middle stands today. Ahead of us is the time to come. Once an event is placed on the line, the story lands in its right time and stops floating.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Draw one line on paper. Put today in the middle. Everything you remember goes left; everything you hope for goes right.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 9.3: Using the timeline

Then the class uses the line itself. Pupils place their own birth year on it, and their parents' birth years, and read off what each marked year means: who came first, how long ago things happened, what stands between a year and today. The timeline stops being a picture and becomes a tool the child can work with.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A timeline — umurongo ndangagihe — puts every event in its right time: it has three parts, the past, today, and the time to come, and you read a life or a country's story by placing its years along the line. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
