# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A timeline — umurongo ndangagihe — puts every event in its right time: it has three parts, the past, today, and the time to come, and you read a life or a country's story by placing its years along the line.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

A boy hears stories but cannot order them — which came first, which came after? He is taught the timeline, the umurongo ndangagihe: a line with three parts. Behind us is the past, where finished events belong. In the middle stands today. Ahead of us is the time to come. Once an event is placed on the line, the story lands in its right time and stops floating.

Then the class uses the line itself. Pupils place their own birth year on it, and their parents' birth years, and read off what each marked year means: who came first, how long ago things happened, what stands between a year and today. The timeline stops being a picture and becomes a tool the child can work with.

Remember it this way: A timeline.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
