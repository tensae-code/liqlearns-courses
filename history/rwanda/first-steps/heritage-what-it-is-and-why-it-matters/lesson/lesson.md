# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Rwanda keeps its heroes the way other countries keep treasure — sorted, named and honoured on a fixed day. The heroes stand in three companies: Imanzi, Imena and Ingenzi. Every 1 February the country remembers them together. In their ranks stand Rwigema, who led the liberation, and the Unknown Soldier, who stands for every fighter whose name was lost. And there are schoolchildren among them: the Nyange school students, who in 1997 were ordered to split themselves by ethnic group, refused — and were killed for refusing. Heritage, this lesson teaches, is courage remembered on purpose.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Heroes Day is 1 February. When you hear a hero's name, ask the lesson's question: which company do they stand in — Imanzi, Imena or Ingenzi?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A country's heritage of courage has names and ranks: Rwanda sorts its heroes into Imanzi, Imena and Ingenzi, remembers them every 1 February, and counts among them Rwigema, the Unknown Soldier, and the Nyange school students who in 1997 refused to split by ethnic group and were killed. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
