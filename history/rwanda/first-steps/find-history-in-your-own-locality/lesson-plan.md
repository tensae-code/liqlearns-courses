# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Rwanda · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: Finding History Where You Live (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History lives where you live: you find it by asking the people who were there — parents at home, the head teacher at school, elders and leaders in the Sector — what made your place known, and then telling it back.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: Finding History Where You Live (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 9.1: Important things that marked the history of where you live** — The children begin at home.
2. **Unit 9.2: Important things that marked the history of your school** — Next the class visits the head teacher with questions ready: When was our school built? Who built it? Who led it first? What trophies has it won? Each answer is a piece of the school's own past, and the pupils collect them the way historians collect evidence — by asking, listening and remembering..
3. **Unit 9 guidance: Interviewing leaders about local and school history** — The teacher's guidance turns the asking into a method: pupils are sent to school leaders and village leaders to ask what marked their school and their village, and then they must explain what they heard back to the whole class.
4. **Unit 9.1: History of our Sector** — A year later the circle widens from village to Sector — the Umurenge.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History lives where you live: you find it by asking the people who were there — parents at home, the head teacher at school, elders and leaders in the Sector — what made your place known, and then telling it back.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Rwanda's books themselves work, telling the past as stories to be asked about, placed and retold.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: History lives where you live: you find it by asking the people who were there — parents at home, the head teacher at school, elders and leaders in the Sector — what made your place known, and then telling it back. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
