# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Rwanda · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: Finding History Where You Live (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is what happened in time past — and knowing it shows where you come from and where you are going; the past holds both good things to copy, like heroes and festivals, and bad things to avoid, like wars and famine.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: Finding History Where You Live (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 9: What history means** — A class sits down to its first history lesson and learns the word itself: history is what happened in time past — the things people did before today.
2. **Unit 9 guidance: Defining history and why it matters** — The teacher's guidance deepens the same lesson: pupils are led to say in their own words that history is what happened in past time, and then to say why it matters.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course. Nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History is what happened in time past — and knowing it shows where you come from and where you are going; the past holds both good things to copy, like heroes and festivals, and bad things to avoid, like wars and famine.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Rwanda's books themselves work, telling the past as stories to be asked about, placed and retold.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: History is what happened in time past — and knowing it shows where you come from and where you are going; the past holds both good things to copy, like heroes and festivals, and bad things to avoid, like wars and famine. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
