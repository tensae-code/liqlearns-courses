# The Holocaust and Other Genocides

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The Rwandan classroom looks outward, to measure its own catastrophe against the world's. Rwanda's genocide lasted about 100 days, killed with machetes at close range, and was stopped by Rwandans themselves — the RPF. Beside it stands the Holocaust: Hitler's organised killing of over 6 million Jews. Beside that, Pol Pot's Cambodia, where a government turned on its own people, and Bosnia, where massacre returned to Europe. Different countries, different weapons — the same crime, and the same question: how does a people let it happen, and how is it stopped?

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Comparison is not competition. Each genocide in this lesson answers: who was targeted, how, and who stopped it — Rwanda's answer to the last is Rwandans themselves.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rwanda's pupils compare genocides to understand their own: Rwanda's 100 days, killing by machete, stopped by Rwandans themselves — set beside Hitler's killing of over 6 million Jews, Pol Pot's Cambodia, and Bosnia. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
