# Video script — East African Coast: The Swahili Cities

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Traders made the East African coast a meeting place of worlds: Arab traders settled and founded Mombasa and Lamu, bringing Islam and new goods; Indians brought the rupee; the Portuguese built Fort Jesus and brought maize and cassava.

Look at the map. We are in Rwanda — and, where the story widens, in East Africa around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Sailors and traders crossed the ocean and settled the East African coast. Arab traders founded towns like Mombasa and Lamu, and brought Islam, cowrie shells for money, cloves and rice. Traders from India brought the rupee that people used in trade. Then the Portuguese came and built Fort Jesus to guard Mombasa — and in their ships came new foods, maize and cassava, that would change what East Africa eats forever. The coast became Swahili: African towns speaking to Arabia, India and Europe across the water.

Remember it this way: Traders made the East African coast a meeting place of worlds: Arab traders settled and founded Mombasa and Lamu, bringing Islam and new goods; Indians brought the rupee; the Portuguese built Fort Jesus and brought maize and cassava..

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
