# East African Coast: The Swahili Cities

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Sailors and traders crossed the ocean and settled the East African coast. Arab traders founded towns like Mombasa and Lamu, and brought Islam, cowrie shells for money, cloves and rice. Traders from India brought the rupee that people used in trade. Then the Portuguese came and built Fort Jesus to guard Mombasa — and in their ships came new foods, maize and cassava, that would change what East Africa eats forever. The coast became Swahili: African towns speaking to Arabia, India and Europe across the water.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Look at a map of the Indian Ocean. The coast towns face the sea because their first streets were built by people who arrived by boat.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Traders made the East African coast a meeting place of worlds: Arab traders settled and founded Mombasa and Lamu, bringing Islam and new goods; Indians brought the rupee; the Portuguese built Fort Jesus and brought maize and cassava. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
