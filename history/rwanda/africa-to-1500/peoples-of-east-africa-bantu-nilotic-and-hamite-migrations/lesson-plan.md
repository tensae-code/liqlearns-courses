# Lesson plan — Peoples of East Africa: Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite Migrations

**Course:** History — Rwanda · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Peoples of East Africa: Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite Migrations
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Peoples and the Coast: East Africa Before the Europeans Ruled (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** East Africa was peopled by migrations: Bantu farmers, Nilotes who came down the Nile, and Hamite and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists from Ethiopia settled side by side — and people still move today, for trade, work and safety, as immigrants in one place and emigrants from another.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Peoples and the Coast: East Africa Before the Europeans Ruled (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 9.1: Major ethnic groups of East Africa** — Long before today's borders, peoples moved into East Africa and settled side by side.
2. **Unit 9.2: Immigration, emigration and why people move** — The moving never stopped.
3. **Unit 9 (guide): Teaching East Africa's peoples** — The teacher's guide turns the migrations into an inquiry: lesson plans sequence the four language peoples in order, and learners are sent to research other Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite groups for themselves, in library and internet sources.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

- Go back to the one thing: East Africa was peopled by migrations: Bantu farmers, Nilotes who came down the Nile, and Hamite and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists from Ethiopia settled side by side — and people still move today, for trade, work and safety, as immigrants in one place and emigrants from another.
