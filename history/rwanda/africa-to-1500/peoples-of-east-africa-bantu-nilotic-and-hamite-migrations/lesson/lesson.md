# Peoples of East Africa: Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite Migrations

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 9.1: Major ethnic groups of East Africa

Long before today's borders, peoples moved into East Africa and settled side by side. The Bantu came as farmers, planting and building settled villages. The Nilotes travelled down the Nile valley from the north. The Hamites and the Nilo-Hamite pastoralists came from Ethiopia's direction, herding cattle across the grasslands. Farmers and herders, river people and highland people — East Africa's map of peoples was drawn by these journeys.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Every family has a moving story. Ask where your grandparents lived before here — that is migration, the same force that peopled East Africa.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 9.2: Immigration, emigration and why people move

The moving never stopped. People move for trade, to find work, and to reach safety — and every move has two names. Leave a country and you are an emigrant from it; arrive in another and you are an immigrant in it. Both places are changed by the move: the one left behind, and the one that receives new hands, new skills and new stories.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 9 (guide): Teaching East Africa's peoples

The teacher's guide turns the migrations into an inquiry: lesson plans sequence the four language peoples in order, and learners are sent to research other Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite groups for themselves, in library and internet sources. The peoples of East Africa are not only to be memorised — they are to be hunted down and proven, group by group.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: East Africa was peopled by migrations: Bantu farmers, Nilotes who came down the Nile, and Hamite and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists from Ethiopia settled side by side — and people still move today, for trade, work and safety, as immigrants in one place and emigrants from another. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
