# History — Iceland course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Iceland's
own — built from Iceland's two History teacher guides,
*Víkingaöld* (the Viking Age, 800–1050) and *Lýðræði og
tækni* (Democracy and Technology — the nineteenth century),
both in Icelandic. Inside the course the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: the titles below sit inside Levels
1–7, in path order — what the learner proves, step by step.
The grades the books come from live only in each lesson's
sources.

The path's own logic is the books': the craft first, twice —
on the younger book's ground (an excavated grave, a
class-built timeline) and again deeper in the older book
(judging sources, fact from opinion) — with the Viking Age
itself between the two turns of the craft; then the old
society and the revolutions that shook it, machines,
emigration and the new ideas, nations and empires with the
empire question turned on Iceland itself, and last
Iceland's own peaceful road out — Jón Sigurðsson, the
Fjallkona, 1918's sovereignty and its flu, the republic of
1944, and the vote widened to women. That ladder IS the
path order (recommended, never forced — a learner can jump
in higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — first steps: reading Iceland's ground**
(Víkingaöld teacher guide, its opening and closing parts):

- `first-steps/` — Level 1 and Level 3, the craft titles.
  Who Studies Prehistory (the archaeologist reads a real
  pagan grave — the Fjallkona's — as evidence) · Dating
  the Past (the age ends, and the class builds its
  timeline from the Viking Age to today).

**Level 2 — the Viking Age and the Norse World**
(Víkingaöld teacher guide, its middle):

- `ancient-world/` — Level 2, one great lesson in six sub
  lessons. The Viking Age and the Norse World (origins and
  the ships · what a Viking was · raids and voyages from
  Russia to America · Eiríks saga rauða · Norse society
  and the Althing at Þingvellir · homes and daily life in
  the turf hall).

**Level 3 — first steps again, deeper: judging the sources**
(Lýðræði og tækni teacher guide, its opening worksheets),
in `first-steps/`:

- From Evidence to a Written History (who made this
  source, when, and why) · Fact or Opinion (pairs sort
  statements and defend the borderline).

**Levels 4–7 — the nineteenth century and Iceland's road**
(Lýðræði og tækni teacher guide), in `modern-world/`, with
the empire lessons in `africa-after-1500/`:

- `modern-world/` — Levels 4–7. Iceland's Old Society
  (tenants, servants, the vistarband, the 1703 census) ·
  American Independence · The French Revolution Breaks
  Out, 1789 · Napoleon: Reformer, Conqueror, Fallen (his
  wars cut Iceland off) · Jørgen Jørgensen's Coup in
  Iceland, 1809 · The Industrial Revolution: What Changed
  and Why England First · Iceland's Late Industrialisation
  (fish, trawlers, new towns) · Icelandic Emigration to
  North America (Sigurður's choice) · Liberalism and
  Socialism (the strike role-play) · Nationalism: The
  Feeling That Made Nation-States · Italy Unifies: Cavour
  and the Patriots · Germany Unifies: Bismarck, Blood and
  Iron · Was Iceland a Colony? · Iceland's Road to
  Independence (Jón Sigurðsson and the Fjallkona; the
  republic of 1944) · The 1918 Spanish Flu · Women Win
  the Vote.
- `africa-after-1500/` — Level 6, the empire lessons.
  Why Europe Colonised: Motives and the "Civilizing
  Mission" (profit, duty and destiny, weighed by the
  pupils) · The Scramble and Berlin, 1884–85 (the race,
  Rhodes, and the rules written without Africans).
