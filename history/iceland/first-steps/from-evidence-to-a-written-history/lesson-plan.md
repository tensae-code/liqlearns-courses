# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Iceland · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — First Steps Again, Deeper: Judging the Sources (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before any history is written, its sources must be judged: who made this source, when, and why — a source that is not questioned can make the written history lie.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — First Steps Again, Deeper: Judging the Sources — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

- Stands in path order after **The Viking Age and the Norse World** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: before any history is written, its sources must be judged: who made this source, when, and why — a source that is not questioned can make the written history lie.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the Iceland they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Iceland and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks an old source must be true just because it is old → Age proves nothing by itself: a source was made by someone, at some time, for some reason — judge those three first, then let the source speak.
- Go back to the one thing: before any history is written, its sources must be judged: who made this source, when, and why — a source that is not questioned can make the written history lie. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
