# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Someone hands you an old letter about a fight in your town. Before you believe a word of it, write down three questions you would ask about the letter itself — who wrote it, when, and what they wanted. This lesson teaches exactly those questions: the first worksheet of Iceland's older history book is not a story at all, but how to judge one.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: before any history is written, its sources must be judged: who made this source, when, and why — a source that is not questioned can make the written history lie. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
