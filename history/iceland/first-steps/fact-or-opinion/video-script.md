# Video script — Fact or Opinion

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A statement of fact can be checked, but an opinion is what someone thinks — and a reader of history must be able to sort the one from the other, especially where the borderline is defended.

The worksheet's title is an order: Þjálfaðu hugann — train your mind. Its exercise is simple to say and hard to do: pairs of pupils take statements and sort them into staðreynd eða skoðun — fact or opinion.

A fact is a statement that can be checked against evidence: the assembly met at Þingvellir; the flu came in 1918. An opinion is what someone thinks or judges: the assembly was glorious; the king was wicked. Books of history carry both, often in the same sentence, and an untrained reader swallows both the same way.

The learning lives at the borderline. Some statements dress opinion as fact, or hide a judgement inside a measurement, and the pairs must defend their sorting of those. Defending the borderline is the skill: it is exactly what a citizen does later with speeches, newspapers and promises.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
