# Hands-on — Fact or Opinion

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Here are two sentences: 'The assembly met at Þingvellir.' 'The assembly was the finest gathering in the world.' Write down which one you could check, and which one is what somebody thinks. Iceland's pupils do this in pairs — and must defend the hard ones. This lesson is that sorting.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a statement of fact can be checked, but an opinion is what someone thinks — and a reader of history must be able to sort the one from the other, especially where the borderline is defended. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
