# Hands-on — Dating the Past

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a long line across a page. At the left write 'the Viking Age' and at the right write 'today'. Now place three things on it: the first ships reaching Iceland, the end of the Viking Age, and your own birth. This lesson is about that line — and why the Viking Age closes where it does.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the past is put in order on a timeline — and Iceland's own class builds one that runs from the Viking Age all the way to their own day, so every story can be hung in its place. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
