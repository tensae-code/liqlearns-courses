# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine you find an old object buried in the soil — a brooch, a bead, a bone. Nobody wrote down whose it was. Write down three things the object itself could still tell you: who might have owned it, how they lived, and what they believed. This lesson is about the people who learn to read exactly that.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the people who study the ages before writing are archaeologists, and they read the past from things left in the ground — as Iceland's children do with a real pagan grave, the grave of the Fjallkona. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
