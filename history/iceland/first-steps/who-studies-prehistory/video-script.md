# Video script — Who Studies Prehistory

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The people who study the ages before writing are archaeologists, and they read the past from things left in the ground — as Iceland's children do with a real pagan grave, the grave of the Fjallkona.

Long before anyone in Iceland wrote books, people lived, worked, believed and died — and were buried. Their ages left no written words behind. So who can study them? The archaeologist: the person who reads the past from the things left in the ground.

Iceland's own book opens with a real find: a woman's pagan grave, dug up and examined piece by piece. In the grave lie the things buried with her — the finds that earned her the name the Fjallkona, the Mountain Woman. Nothing in the grave is labelled, yet everything in it speaks: her ornaments say she was dressed with care, the grave goods say her people believed the dead travelled on, and the burial itself says she mattered to the living.

That is the archaeologist's method, and the book makes children practise it like a field trip: look first, touch carefully, and let the objects be the evidence. A written page tells you what its writer chose to say; a grave tells you what a life actually held. Before any written history can begin, this is how the silent ages are studied — one find at a time.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
