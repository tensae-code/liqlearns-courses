# Lesson plan — Iceland's Road to Independence

**Course:** History — Iceland · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Iceland's Road to Independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7 — Iceland's Road to the Republic (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Iceland walked out of Danish rule by peaceful steps: Jón Sigurðsson led the independence struggle with pen and argument, the Fjallkona became the nation's face — and the road ended with home rule, sovereignty in 1918, and the republic proclaimed at Þingvellir in 1944.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 7 — Iceland's Road to the Republic — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Jón Sigurðsson and the Fjallkona** — the struggle had a leader, and the worksheets name the question about him: **Var Jón Sigurðsson frelsishetja?** — was Jón Sigurðsson a freedom hero? His weapons were the pen and the argument: from Copenhagen he edited, wrote and organised, proving from Iceland's old laws and history that Iceland was a nation with rights Denmark itself had once recognised.
2. **Home Rule, Sovereignty and the Republic of 1944** — the chapter Þjóðfrelsi og lýðræði — national freedom and democracy — walks the road's end: **Leiðin til lýðveldis**, the way to the republic.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks independence must always be won by war → Iceland's road is the book's counter-example: a peaceful struggle of argument, law and patience — Jón Sigurðsson never fired a shot — ending in a negotiated sovereignty and a republic proclaimed by law.
- Go back to the one thing: Iceland walked out of Danish rule by peaceful steps: Jón Sigurðsson led the independence struggle with pen and argument, the Fjallkona became the nation's face — and the road ended with home rule, sovereignty in 1918, and the republic proclaimed at Þingvellir in 1944. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
