# Hands-on — Iceland's Road to Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
No army, no barricades: one small nation talked, argued and voted its way out of an empire's shadow over a hundred years. Write down what weapons such a struggle uses instead of swords. This lesson walks Iceland's road — with its hero, its symbol, and its destination on the field of the old Althing.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Iceland walked out of Danish rule by peaceful steps: Jón Sigurðsson led the independence struggle with pen and argument, the Fjallkona became the nation's face — and the road ended with home rule, sovereignty in 1918, and the republic proclaimed at Þingvellir in 1944. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
