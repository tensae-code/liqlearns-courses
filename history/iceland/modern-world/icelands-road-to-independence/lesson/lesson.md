# Iceland's Road to Independence

## Do it first

No army, no barricades: one small nation talked, argued and voted its way out of an empire's shadow over a hundred years. Write down what weapons such a struggle uses instead of swords. This lesson walks Iceland's road — with its hero, its symbol, and its destination on the field of the old Althing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Jón Sigurðsson and the Fjallkona

The struggle had a leader, and the worksheets name the question about him: **Var Jón Sigurðsson frelsishetja?** — was Jón Sigurðsson a freedom hero? His weapons were the pen and the argument: from Copenhagen he edited, wrote and organised, proving from Iceland's old laws and history that Iceland was a nation with rights Denmark itself had once recognised.

Under his leadership the struggle stayed **peaceful and legal**: the Althing was restored as Iceland's assembly, free trade won, a constitution granted — each step argued, none seized. Beside the leader stands the symbol: the **Fjallkona**, the Mountain Woman — the same figure whose pagan grave opened this course — now drawn as the nation herself, Iceland personified, the face on the struggle's banners and poems.

## Sub lesson 2 — Home Rule, Sovereignty and the Republic of 1944

The chapter Þjóðfrelsi og lýðræði — national freedom and democracy — walks the road's end: **Leiðin til lýðveldis**, the way to the republic. Step by negotiated step: **home rule**, with Icelanders governing their own affairs from Reykjavík; then **sovereignty in 1918**, Iceland a free state in personal union with the Danish king; and at last the union itself dissolved by agreement.

In **1944**, with Denmark under German occupation and Iceland's own vote overwhelming, the **republic was proclaimed at Þingvellir** — on the very field where the allsherjargoði had opened the medieval Althing a thousand years before. The course's circle closes there: the grave, the assembly, the struggle and the republic all stand on one ground, and the nation's own way of teaching its history makes sure every Icelander knows it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Iceland walked out of Danish rule by peaceful steps: Jón Sigurðsson led the independence struggle with pen and argument, the Fjallkona became the nation's face — and the road ended with home rule, sovereignty in 1918, and the republic proclaimed at Þingvellir in 1944. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Jón Sigurðsson, Fjallkona, home rule, sovereignty 1918, republic 1944, Þingvellir. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
