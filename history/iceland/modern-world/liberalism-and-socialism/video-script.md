# Video script — Liberalism and Socialism

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The factory age bred two great answering ideas — liberalism, trusting the free individual and the free market, and socialism, demanding that workers join together; Iceland's pupils learn them by playing factory hands who must decide whether to found a union and strike.

The industrial revolution created a new world of owners and hands, and the century argued about how that world should be ruled. Two great answers faced each other. Liberalism trusted the free individual: free trade, free contracts, a state that keeps out of the way — let each person rise by their own effort. Socialism answered that a lone worker against an owner is not free at all: only joined together, owning and deciding in common, could working people hold real power.

The book does not leave these as definitions to copy. Its worksheets stage a factory role-play: pupils play factory hands at the century's start — the wages, the hours, the hunger — and then face the organiser's question in the second worksheet's title: “Viltu fara í verkfall?” — do you want to go on strike?

To strike or not, to found a union or not: the class must decide, in role, with the risks real inside the game — the sack, the empty table, the hope of winning better terms together. That is how Iceland teaches the -isms: not as labels, but as the choices the factory age forced on the people who lived it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
