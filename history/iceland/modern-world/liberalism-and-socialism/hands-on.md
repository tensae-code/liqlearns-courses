# Hands-on — Liberalism and Socialism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You work twelve hours at a machine. The owner sets your wage, your hours, your rent. Write down two different answers people of the time gave to one question: who should hold power over working life? This lesson names those answers — liberalism and socialism — and then makes you live one of them.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the factory age bred two great answering ideas — liberalism, trusting the free individual and the free market, and socialism, demanding that workers join together; Iceland's pupils learn them by playing factory hands who must decide whether to found a union and strike. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
