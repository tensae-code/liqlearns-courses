# Video script — Women Win the Vote

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The democracy Iceland built was unfinished while half its people could not vote: the book closes by making the class debate, in period voices, whether women may vote and hold office — the question the century answered yes.

The book's last worksheet is a question, and it is staged as a fight of words: Eiga konur að fá að gegna embættum? — should women be allowed to hold office? The class role-plays the debate in period voices: the arguments as they were actually made, for and against, in the years when the question was open.

Behind the worksheet stands the whole arc of the course's democracy story. The revolutions promised the rights of man; the factories raised the question of who counts as a citizen; Iceland's own road built a state by argument. Yet through all of it, women — half the nation — stood outside the vote and outside office, their claim answered for generations with custom dressed as nature.

The debate the pupils replay is the one the century settled: Icelandic women won the vote and the right to stand for office, among the earliest peoples in the world to grant it, and the democracy the republic inherited was doubled by it. The course ends here on purpose: the last lesson of Iceland's nineteenth century is the widening of 'the people' itself — the argument every earlier lesson was quietly about.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
