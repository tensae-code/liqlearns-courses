# Hands-on — Women Win the Vote

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Half the adults in the room may speak; the other half must stay silent, though the decision binds them too. Write down what you would say if you were called to argue — for either side — in the year 1910. Iceland's book ends its nineteenth century with exactly that debate, played in period voices.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the democracy Iceland built was unfinished while half its people could not vote: the book closes by making the class debate, in period voices, whether women may vote and hold office — the question the century answered yes. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
