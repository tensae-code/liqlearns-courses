# Video script — Jørgen Jørgensen's Coup in Iceland, 1809

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In the summer of 1809, with Iceland cut off by war, the adventurer Jørgen Jørgensen seized power, proclaimed Iceland independent — and was removed again within months.

Napoleon's wars had cut Iceland off from Denmark; the Danish governor had been taken prisoner by a British warship's party, and authority in Reykjavík stood empty. Into that emptiness, in the summer of 1809, stepped Jørgen Jørgensen — a Danish adventurer who arrived with a British trading expedition and stayed to rule.

His valdarán — his coup — was swift. Jørgensen arrested the remaining Danish officials, took power in his own hands, and proclaimed Iceland independent, promising land, free trade and a new order. For a few weeks the island had a self-declared ruler who styled himself its protector.

It could not last. He had no army of his own, no recognition abroad, and a British warship's captain soon judged the adventure a nuisance: Jørgensen was removed within months and carried away, and Danish rule resumed as if the summer had been a dream. The worksheet leaves pupils with the puzzle the story deserves: a proclamation of independence that meant everything and nothing — the word spoken a century too early, by the wrong man, for his own reasons.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
