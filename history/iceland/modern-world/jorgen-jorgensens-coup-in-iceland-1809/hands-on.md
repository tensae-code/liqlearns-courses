# Hands-on — Jørgen Jørgensen's Coup in Iceland, 1809

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The governor is gone, the war has cut the sea-road, and a foreign ship anchors in the harbour with armed men aboard. Write down who is in charge of your country this morning. In Reykjavík in 1809 the honest answer, for one strange summer, was: a Danish adventurer named Jørgen Jørgensen.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: in the summer of 1809, with Iceland cut off by war, the adventurer Jørgen Jørgensen seized power, proclaimed Iceland independent — and was removed again within months. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
