# Video script — Germany Unifies: Bismarck, Blood and Iron

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Germany's many states were unified under Prussia by Bismarck, who said the great questions of the day would be decided by blood and iron — and in 1871 an emperor was proclaimed in the palace of Versailles.

Germany, like Italy, was a patchwork — dozens of states, with two giants inside it, Austria and Prussia, contesting the lead. Prussia's minister Bismarck took the unification into his own hands and announced his method in advance: the great questions of the day are decided not by speeches and votes, but by blood and iron.

He proved it with engineered wars, each one gathering more German states under Prussia: Austria pushed out of German affairs, then France — the hereditary rival — defeated and occupied. Worksheet 17 lets pupils read the victor's own words: Kaiser Wilhelm's 1871 speech.

The climax was staged for maximum sting: in 1871, in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles — the palace of France's kings, in conquered France — the German princes proclaimed their emperor. A new great power stood in Europe's centre, unified by war, and every neighbour, and every small nation watching, recalculated what the age of nationalism could do.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
