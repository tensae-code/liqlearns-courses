# Hands-on — Germany Unifies: Bismarck, Blood and Iron

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Italy's stitching was half diplomacy, half volunteers. Germany's unifier announced a harder recipe in advance: 'blood and iron.' Write down what you think he meant. This lesson follows Bismarck as he makes good on the words — and ends in a conquered enemy's palace.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Germany's many states were unified under Prussia by Bismarck, who said the great questions of the day would be decided by blood and iron — and in 1871 an emperor was proclaimed in the palace of Versailles. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
