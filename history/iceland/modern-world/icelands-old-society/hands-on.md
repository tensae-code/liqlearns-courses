# Hands-on — Iceland's Old Society

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine your whole life fits in one room of someone else's farmhouse, and the law says you may not leave the farm that feeds you. Write down what you would own, and what you would not. This lesson opens Iceland's old society — the world the nineteenth century had to change.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: in old Iceland most people owned nothing: tenant farmers worked other men's land, servants were bound to farms by the vistarband, and the census of 1703 counted them all, name by name. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
