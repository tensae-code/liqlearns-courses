# Video script — The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Machines, factories and fast-growing populations remade work itself — beginning in England — and even children's work was swallowed by the factory system.

The book's long chapter carries a double title — Fólksfjölgun og tækni: population growth and technology. The two belong together. More people were surviving and multiplying than ever before, and new machines — steam-driven, iron-built — could do the work of many hands at once.

It began in England, where coal, capital, colonies and inventors met, and it changed where work lived. Spinning and weaving left the cottage for the factory: a building of machines owned by one man, worked by many who owned nothing but their labour, at hours the machine set. Towns swelled around the mills as country people poured in for wages.

The book insists on the whole picture, including children's work: small hands were cheap, and children tended machines for long days in the new factories. The industrial revolution remade wealth and poverty together — goods cheaper and more plentiful than ever, and a new working class living by the factory bell. Every later chapter of the century — Iceland's included — is this revolution arriving somewhere new.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
