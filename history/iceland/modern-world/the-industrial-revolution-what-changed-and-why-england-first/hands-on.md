# Hands-on — The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a shirt. In the old world, one family grew, spun, wove and sewed it at home. Now imagine a building where a hundred machines make shirts all day, run by people who own none of them. Write down three things that change when work moves from the home to that building. This lesson is that move — the industrial revolution.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: machines, factories and fast-growing populations remade work itself — beginning in England — and even children's work was swallowed by the factory system. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
