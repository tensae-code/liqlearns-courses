# Hands-on — Nationalism: The Feeling That Made Nation-States

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a song in your own language that makes a crowd stand a little straighter. Write down what that feeling is, and what it could build if a whole people felt it at once. This lesson names it — nationalism — and follows it as it remakes Europe's map.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: nationalism — the feeling that a people is a nation and needs its own state — redrew the map of Europe in the nineteenth century; and the book makes pupils weigh it honestly: a feeling that can build a state can also turn on its neighbours. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
