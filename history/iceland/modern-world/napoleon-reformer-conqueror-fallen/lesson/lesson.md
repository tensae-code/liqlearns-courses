# Napoleon: Reformer, Conqueror, Fallen

## Do it first

Find Iceland on a map, then find France. Between them lie thousands of kilometres of sea and several kingdoms. Write down how a war started in France could possibly reach a farm in Iceland. This lesson follows Napoleon's wars north — all the way to Iceland's shore.

Out of the French Revolution rose **Napoleon** — reformer at home, conqueror abroad. His armies carried the revolution's legal reforms across Europe at sword-point and knocked thrones down like skittles; his wars lasted a generation and no corner of the continent stood outside them.

The book's own chapter turns the map north: **Napóleónsstyrjaldir á Norðurlöndunum** — Napoleon's wars in the Nordic countries. Denmark, Iceland's ruling power, was pulled into the wars against Britain; Danish shipping was attacked, trade strangled, and the sea-road between Copenhagen and Iceland — the road Iceland's food, goods and government travelled — was **cut**.

For Iceland the great war was not a battle but an absence: ships that did not come, officials who could not be reached, a colony left suddenly alone with itself. That absence is the doorway to the next lesson — the summer of 1809, when a stranger seized the empty seat of power in Reykjavík.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Napoleon's wars redrew Europe — and reached even the far North, cutting Iceland off from Denmark and setting the stage for the strangest summer in Iceland's history. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Napoleon, Nordic countries, Denmark, cut off, wars. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
