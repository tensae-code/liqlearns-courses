# Video script — American Independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Britain's American colonies rebelled against their king and invented something new — a democratic republic, built on the claim that government rests on the people's consent.

The book's chapter shouts its title: Bylting! — Revolution! Across the Atlantic, Britain's American colonies had grown rich and confident under a king who ruled them from London, taxed them, and gave them no voice in the Parliament that taxed them.

The quarrel sharpened into a principle: taxation and law without representation were tyranny. The colonies rebelled, fought a war against the greatest power of the age — and won their independence.

Then came the invention. Instead of a new king, the Americans built a democratic republic: a state whose written rules put the people's consent at the foundation and divided power so no one ruler could gather it all. For Iceland's pupils, the point of the story is the idea itself — that a people can found a state on consent — an idea their own country would one day use.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
