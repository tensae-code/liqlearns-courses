# Hands-on — American Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A king across the ocean taxes you, and you have no seat in the hall where the tax was decided. Write down what you would demand first. The American colonists demanded, rebelled, and then built a new kind of state — this lesson tells how, as Iceland's pupils learn it under the heading 'Bylting!' — Revolution!

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Britain's American colonies rebelled against their king and invented something new — a democratic republic, built on the claim that government rests on the people's consent. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
