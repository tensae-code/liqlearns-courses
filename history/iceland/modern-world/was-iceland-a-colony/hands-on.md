# Hands-on — Was Iceland a Colony?

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You have just studied how Europe seized Africa and called it duty and destiny. Now turn around: your own country was ruled for centuries from Copenhagen, its trade monopolised, its king an ocean away. Write down your verdict — colony, or not? — and one reason. Then open the lesson, where Iceland's own pupils must argue the same question.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: after studying empire abroad, Iceland's pupils turn the definition homeward: was Iceland itself a colony of Denmark? — the question is the lesson, and the class must argue it from the evidence of their own history. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
