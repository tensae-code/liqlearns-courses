# Hands-on — Iceland's Late Industrialisation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
England's revolution ran on coal mines and cotton mills. Iceland has no coal and grows no cotton. Write down what Iceland has in plenty that machines could be turned upon. The answer fills this lesson: the sea, and the fish in it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the industrial revolution came late to Iceland — not through coal and cotton, but through fish: steam trawlers, processing and new towns rising around the harbours. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
