# Iceland's Late Industrialisation

## Do it first

England's revolution ran on coal mines and cotton mills. Iceland has no coal and grows no cotton. Write down what Iceland has in plenty that machines could be turned upon. The answer fills this lesson: the sea, and the fish in it.

The chapter's title admits the delay: **Síðbúin atvinnubylting á Íslandi** — Iceland's late-coming industrial revolution. While England's mills had run for generations, Iceland remained the old society of turf farms, tenants and servants this course has already met.

When the change came, it came **through fish**. Steam **trawlers** could catch on a scale sail and oar never matched; the catch needed hands ashore — gutting, salting, drying, packing — and those hands gathered where the ships landed. **New towns** rose around the harbours, and Icelanders left the farms for wages, streets and shops for the first time in the country's history.

Late, then, but transformative: within a generation the centre of Icelandic life moved from the isolated farm to the fishing town, and the old vistarband world began to dissolve. The next lesson shows the other road Icelanders took when the old world squeezed them — across the ocean.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the industrial revolution came late to Iceland — not through coal and cotton, but through fish: steam trawlers, processing and new towns rising around the harbours. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
