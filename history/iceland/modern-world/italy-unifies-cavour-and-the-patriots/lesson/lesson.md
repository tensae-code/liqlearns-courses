# Italy Unifies: Cavour and the Patriots

## Do it first

Look at a map of Italy before 1860: not one country but many — a king here, a duke there, a foreign emperor ruling the north. Write down what it would take to make them one. This lesson tells how it was done, by a minister's cunning and a patriot's red-shirted thousand.

The nationalism of the last lesson here becomes a deed. The Italian peninsula was divided among kingdoms, dukedoms, the Pope's states and provinces ruled from Vienna — 'Italy' named a place, not a country.

Two kinds of men unified it. **Cavour**, minister of the one free Italian kingdom, Piedmont-Sardinia, worked by statecraft: alliances, diplomacy and well-chosen wars to expel Austria and gather the north. **Garibaldi** and his patriots worked by fire: volunteers in red shirts who landed in Sicily and swept the south for the nation, then handed their conquest to the king.

Statecraft and sword together **stitched Italy's fragments into one kingdom**. For Iceland's pupils the lesson is the pattern of the age: a feeling (nationalism), a plan (Cavour's), and men willing to fight (Garibaldi's) — the recipe that was remaking Europe, and that small nations everywhere studied with close attention.
