# Hands-on — Italy Unifies: Cavour and the Patriots

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at a map of Italy before 1860: not one country but many — a king here, a duke there, a foreign emperor ruling the north. Write down what it would take to make them one. This lesson tells how it was done, by a minister's cunning and a patriot's red-shirted thousand.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Italy, long a patchwork of kingdoms, dukedoms and foreign-ruled provinces, was stitched into one kingdom by Cavour's statecraft and Garibaldi's volunteers — nationalism with a flag and an army. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
