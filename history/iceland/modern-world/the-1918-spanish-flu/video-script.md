# Video script — The 1918 Spanish Flu

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In 1918, the year of sovereignty, the Spanish flu reached Iceland and killed in a few weeks more Icelanders than any war had ever touched — a nation's triumph and its mourning in the same year.

The worksheet's title is two words: Spænska veikin — the Spanish sickness. In 1918 a new influenza swept a world exhausted by war, and in the autumn it reached Iceland by ship, as everything reached Iceland.

It moved with terrible speed. Within weeks the sickness was in the towns and the farms; the young and strong fell hardest, households were struck whole, and the dead numbered in the hundreds — killed in weeks what war never touched, for Iceland had sat out the Great War entirely and lost more people to the flu than many belligerents lost soldiers from small nations.

The book sets the flu in the very year of sovereignty: the flags of 1918 flew over a country also digging graves. That pairing is why the lesson stands where it does in the course — a nation's proudest year and its saddest season are the same year, and history that keeps only the flags tells half the story.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
