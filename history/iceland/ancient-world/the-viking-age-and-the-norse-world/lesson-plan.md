# Lesson plan — The Viking Age and the Norse World

**Course:** History — Iceland · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Viking Age and the Norse World
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Viking Age and the Norse World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** between about 800 and 1050 the Norse people of the North — Iceland's own ancestors — built ships that opened the whole northern world: they raided, traded and settled from Russia to America, governed themselves at the Althing, and told their story in the sagas.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The Viking Age and the Norse World — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Origins and the Ships** — the story starts with a people of the North — the **Norse** — farmers and fishers on hard coasts, and with the tool that changed everything for them: the **ship**.
2. **What a Viking Was** — the age has a name — the **Viking Age, about 800 to 1050** — but the book stops to correct the picture the name paints.
3. **Raids, Voyages and Finding the Way** — still, the raiding and voyaging reached astonishingly far.
4. **Erik the Red and the Seeress (Eiríks saga rauða)** — iceland kept its past in stories — the **sagas** — and the book reads one in the original tradition: *Eiríks saga rauða*, the Saga of Erik the Red.
5. **Norse Society and the Althing** — how did a people with no king in Iceland govern themselves? Through the **assembly**.
6. **Homes and Daily Life** — step inside the **turf hall**: thick grass-roofed walls against the cold, a long fire down the middle, benches along the sides.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks 'Viking' was the name of a whole people who were all raiders → The book is plain: 'to go a-viking' was a job some men did in the season; the people were the Norse — farmers, traders, settlers and lawmakers, most of whom never lifted a raiding axe.
- Go back to the one thing: between about 800 and 1050 the Norse people of the North — Iceland's own ancestors — built ships that opened the whole northern world: they raided, traded and settled from Russia to America, governed themselves at the Althing, and told their story in the sagas.
