# Hands-on — The Viking Age and the Norse World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand at the shore in your mind and look north and west: open sea, no maps, no compass. Write down how you would find your way to a shore you have never seen. Then open this lesson — the Norse sailors did exactly that, and their world is where Iceland begins.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: between about 800 and 1050 the Norse people of the North — Iceland's own ancestors — built ships that opened the whole northern world: they raided, traded and settled from Russia to America, governed themselves at the Althing, and told their story in the sagas. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
