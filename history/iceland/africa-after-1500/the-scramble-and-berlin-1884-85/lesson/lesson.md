# The Scramble and Berlin, 1884–85

## Do it first

A continent is being divided on a map by men who have never seen most of it, in a city thousands of kilometres away. Write down who is NOT in the room. This lesson tells the Scramble for Africa — and the Berlin Conference that wrote its rules.

The chapter is named a race: **Kapphlaupið um Afríku** — the Scramble for Africa. Once the first powers planted flags on the coasts, every rival feared being shut out, and the grabbing became frantic: expeditions, treaties signed with bewildered chiefs, flags run up river after river.

The race's great grabber has a name in the book: **Cecil Rhodes**, who dreamed of a British Africa 'from the Cape to Cairo' and seized the land that bore his name, Rhodesia. Men like Rhodes were the race's runners; the powers behind them were its owners.

And the race had a rulebook, written at the **Berlin Conference of 1884–85**: the powers agreed that a claim counted when it was **effectively occupied** and the others notified — no African ruler sat at that table. Africa's straight-line borders, and a century of consequences, descend from that room. The next lesson turns the conference's own word — colony — homeward, onto Iceland itself.
