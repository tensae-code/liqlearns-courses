# Hands-on — The Scramble and Berlin, 1884–85

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A continent is being divided on a map by men who have never seen most of it, in a city thousands of kilometres away. Write down who is NOT in the room. This lesson tells the Scramble for Africa — and the Berlin Conference that wrote its rules.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Europe's powers raced to seize Africa — the Scramble — and at Berlin in 1884–85 they set the rules of the race among themselves, with men like Cecil Rhodes grabbing for a continent no African had offered them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
