# Why Europe Colonised: Motives and the “Civilizing Mission”

## Do it first

A speaker claims his country has the right to rule yours: it will bring trade, schools, and 'civilisation'. Write down two questions you would ask him. Iceland's book puts exactly this case to its pupils — Europe's arguments for empire, laid out to be weighed, not swallowed.

The chapter's title is blunt: **Evrópumenn leggja heiminn undir sig** — Europeans lay the world under themselves. In the late nineteenth century Europe's powers seized most of Africa and much of Asia, and they did not call it theft. They called it three things.

First, **profit**: colonies meant raw materials, captive markets and land. Second, **duty**: the claimed burden of carrying law, medicine, schools and the Christian faith to peoples deemed not yet 'civilised' — the **civilizing mission**. Third, **destiny**: the belief that ruling other peoples was the natural fate and proof of a great nation. Profit, duty and destiny — the arguments marched together, each covering the others.

Worksheet 22 hands the arguments to the pupils to **weigh**. Who profits, in fact? Whose civilisation is being judged, and by whom? What would the ruled say if the worksheet were theirs? The book's Icelanders — themselves ruled from Copenhagen, as the coming lessons ask outright — are being trained to hear an empire's self-praise with both ears open.
