# History — Iceland course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Iceland inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Iceland's set in hand is two
teacher guides, and the ladder follows their own order —
earliest book first. The first book (the Viking Age guide)
opens and closes with the historian's craft practised on
Iceland's own ground — an excavated grave at the front, a
class-built timeline at the back — and gives its whole middle
to the Viking Age itself (Level 1, then Level 2). The second
book (Democracy and Technology — the nineteenth century)
again opens with the craft, one step deeper — judging sources,
sorting fact from opinion (Level 3) — then walks its century
in the book's own order: the old society and the age of
revolutions (Level 4), machines, emigration and the new ideas
(Level 5), nations and empires, ending with the empire
question turned on Iceland itself (Level 6), and Iceland's
own road out — Jón Sigurðsson, the Fjallkona, sovereignty in
1918, the republic of 1944, the flu that shared that year,
and the widening of the people itself in women's vote
(Level 7). The road-to-independence lesson stands where its
second part lands, at the course's close. The ladder stops
where the books in hand stop, not because Iceland teaches
nothing more. Every lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence
only — the acceptance reckoning in ABOUT.md says so plainly.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Víkingaöld and Lýðræði og tækni books — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: Reading Iceland's Ground** — before any
   Icelandic story, the craft itself on Iceland's own soil:
   who studies prehistory (the archaeologist, and the
   excavated pagan grave of the Fjallkona), and dating the
   past (the Viking Age's end, and the class's own Tímalína
   timeline from 800 to today). 2 lessons, in one title:
   `first-steps/` (2 lessons).

2. **The Viking Age and the Norse World** — the younger
   book's whole middle as one great lesson in six parts:
   origins and the ships that opened the world; what a
   Viking was (a job, not a people); raids and voyages from
   Russia to America, steered by sun, birds and memory;
   Eiríks saga rauða — Erik the Red and the seeress
   Þorbjörg; Norse society and the Althing at Þingvellir;
   and homes and daily life in the turf hall. 1 lesson with
   6 sub lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (1 lesson).

3. **First Steps Again, Deeper: Judging the Sources** — the
   older book opens with the craft at a higher turn: from
   evidence to a written history (who made this source,
   when, and why), and fact or opinion (pairs sorting
   statements and defending the borderline). 2 lessons, in
   one title: `first-steps/` (2 lessons).

4. **Old Iceland and the Age of Revolutions** — the world
   the nineteenth century inherited and the shocks that
   struck it: Iceland's old society (tenant farmers,
   servants under the vistarband, the census of 1703);
   American independence; the French Revolution of 1789;
   Napoleon — whose wars cut Iceland off from Denmark; and
   Jørgen Jørgensen's coup of 1809 in the empty seat of
   power. 5 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/`
   (5 lessons).

5. **Machines, Emigration and the New Ideas** — the
   industrial revolution (what changed, and why England
   first — children's work included); Iceland's late
   industrialisation through fish, steam trawlers and new
   towns; Icelandic emigration to North America, weighed
   through Sigurður of Skagafjörður; and liberalism and
   socialism, learned by factory role-play — "Viltu fara í
   verkfall?", do you want to strike? 4 lessons, in one
   title: `modern-world/` (4 lessons).

6. **Nations and Empires** — nationalism, the feeling that
   made nation-states (weighed positive or negative);
   Italy unifies under Cavour and Garibaldi; Germany
   unifies under Bismarck, by blood and iron; why Europe
   colonised — profit, duty and destiny weighed by the
   pupils themselves; the Scramble and Berlin 1884–85;
   and the lens turned homeward: was Iceland a colony?
   6 lessons, in two titles: `modern-world/` (4 lessons)
   and `africa-after-1500/` (2 lessons).

7. **Iceland's Road to the Republic** — the book's close,
   and the course's: Iceland's road to independence in two
   parts (Jón Sigurðsson and the Fjallkona; home rule,
   sovereignty in 1918, the republic at Þingvellir in
   1944); the 1918 Spanish flu, which shares the
   sovereignty year; and women win the vote — the book's
   last worksheet a role-play debate on whether women may
   hold office. 3 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/`
   (3 lessons).

**Totals: 23 lessons** (Level 1: 2 · Level 2: 1 · Level 3: 2 ·
Level 4: 5 · Level 5: 4 · Level 6: 6 · Level 7: 3 — 29
placements, all from two teacher guides, evidence only,
acceptance 0).
