# What history is

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "What history is" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Anteprima: Il concetto di Medioevo; Periodizzare; Medioevo e feudalesimo; Oltre l'eurocentrismo

The course opens by questioning its own frame — 'Middle Ages' is a label invented later, periodising is a choice, and the story must not be told from Europe alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — Anteprima: Il '700; La periodizzazione; La modernità politica europea

The volume opens by defending its own frame — a 'long' 1700s from 1660 to the revolutions — teaching periodisation itself as a historian's choice, not a fact of nature.

## Sub lesson 3 — Anteprima: La guerra come evento periodizzante: il secolo breve

The volume opens by defending its frame — Hobsbawm's "short twentieth century" (1914–1991) against the "long nineteenth" — teaching periodisation itself as a historian's choice before a single event is told.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of What history is as Italy's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
