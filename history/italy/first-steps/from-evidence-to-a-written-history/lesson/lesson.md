# From evidence to a written history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From evidence to a written history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Documenti and Laboratorio storia strands in every unit

Each unit closes its chapters with primary documents — an agrarian contract, Magna Carta, Las Casas, the Bill of Rights — to be read, questioned and turned into written history.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Fare storia dossiers and Guida allo studio / Sviluppare le competenze strands throughout

Over 180 numbered documents — from Magna Carta to the Putney debates — are set as the student's own evidence, each with study guides teaching how to read, compare and write from them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Documenti and 'Un documento, un diritto' strands in every unit

The Declaration of Independence, the Rights of Man, Olympe de Gouges, the Gettysburg Address and the Berlin Act are set as the student's own evidence, each tied to a right still in force.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Fare storia dossiers throughout (178 numbered pieces)

From Galileo's two planes of science to Gramsci's missed agrarian revolution, every module closes its argument in documents the student must weigh — the Laterza method in its purest form.

## Sub lesson 5 — Documenti, Storiografia, Laboratorio storia and Storici a confronto strands in every unit

Fischer against Ferguson on Germany's war guilt, Corner against Ben-Ghiat on fascist modernity, Dian against Parsi on the post-Western order — the student watches historians argue the century from its evidence, 1900 to Ukraine.

## Sub lesson 6 — The Fare storia dossiers throughout (207 numbered pieces in four dossier modules)

From Clark on whether 1914 could have gone otherwise and the London Pact, through Arendt on totalitarianism, Levi and Höss on the Shoah, the Bandung manifesto and King's America, to Darnton at the Berlin Wall and Piketty on inequality — the Laterza method in its fullest form.

## Say it to your companion

Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
