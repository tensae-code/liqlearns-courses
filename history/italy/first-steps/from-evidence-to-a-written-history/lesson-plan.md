# Lesson plan — From evidence to a written history

**Course:** History — Italy · **Title:** First steps · **Lesson:** From evidence to a written history
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Europe's revival after the year 1000 to the 1600s — the first telling (Barbero, Vol. 1): communes and crusades, crisis and plague, signorie, Renaissance, the voyages, Reformation and the century of crisis (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of From evidence to a written history as Italy's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Europe's revival after the year 1000 to the 1600s — the first telling (Barbero, Vol. 1): communes and crusades, crisis and plague, signorie, Renaissance, the voyages, Reformation and the century of crisis — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Documenti and Laboratorio storia strands in every unit** — each unit closes its chapters with primary documents — an agrarian contract, Magna Carta, Las Casas, the Bill of Rights — to be read, questioned and turned into written history
2. **§2 The Fare storia dossiers and Guida allo studio / Sviluppare le competenze strands throughout** — over 180 numbered documents — from Magna Carta to the Putney debates — are set as the student's own evidence, each with study guides teaching how to read, compare and write from them
3. **§3 Documenti and 'Un documento, un diritto' strands in every unit** — the Declaration of Independence, the Rights of Man, Olympe de Gouges, the Gettysburg Address and the Berlin Act are set as the student's own evidence, each tied to a right still in force
4. **§4 The Fare storia dossiers throughout (178 numbered pieces)** — from Galileo's two planes of science to Gramsci's missed agrarian revolution, every module closes its argument in documents the student must weigh — the Laterza method in its purest form
5. **§5 Documenti, Storiografia, Laboratorio storia and Storici a confronto strands in every unit** — Fischer against Ferguson on Germany's war guilt, Corner against Ben-Ghiat on fascist modernity, Dian against Parsi on the post-Western order — the student watches historians argue the century from its evidence, 1900 to Ukraine
6. **§6 The Fare storia dossiers throughout (207 numbered pieces in four dossier modules)** — from Clark on whether 1914 could have gone otherwise and the London Pact, through Arendt on totalitarianism, Levi and Höss on the Shoah, the Bandung manifesto and King's America, to Darnton at the Berlin Wall and Piketty on inequality — the Laterza method in its fullest form

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
