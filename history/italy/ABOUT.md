# History — Italy course — ABOUT

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Italy's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How the course is organised

One course for Italy, organised by levels, never grades (see CURRICULUM.md). Lessons sit under title folders named for the placements' own units (`first-steps/`, `ancient-world/`, `modern-world/`, `africa-after-1500/`). Every placement in the placements file stocks into exactly one lesson, with exactly one provenance row in that lesson's `lesson/sources.md`. Placements at `liqlearns-courses-work/expansion-history-2026-11-15/italy-placements.md` — 424 placements in total, the count of lines containing `story beats:` in the file; the lines govern where a book header's printed tally disagrees.

Records as filed: 6 records / 6 distinct books / read via grab.py (release assets). All 6 books are student books in Italian from Italy's liceo courses — two rival three-volume courses covering the same school years in parallel: the Barbero course (Zanichelli, 2024) and the Giardina course (Editori Laterza, 2015; 2016 reprints). Books are numbered 1–6 in this course, in filing order.

## The way of the books

Each book's own way is carried in its entry above. The arc they make together: two rival liceo courses tell the same three school years twice. The Barbero course (Books 1, 3, 5) builds each unit on *nuclei fondanti* — core nuclei — with primary documents, *Storici a confronto* debates in which paired historians argue the same question, *Laboratorio storia* and CLIL English closings in every unit, and Italy's own story given centre stage: the communes and the Renaissance in the first volume, the Risorgimento in two full chapters and liberal Italy's crises told from inside in the second, and in the third a run from 1900 to the war in Ukraine that ends by arguing about the present itself. The Giardina course (Books 2, 4, 6) organises the same centuries in *moduli* built around documents — numbered DOC dossiers, *Fare storia* strands, *Eventi* and *Personaggi* boxes, a *Guida allo studio* and competence exercises — so the student works as a historian from sources first and narrative second: 180-plus numbered documents in the first volume, 178 in the second, and 207 numbered documents and historiography pieces in four dossier modules in the third. Periodisation itself is taught as a historian's choice in both courses — Giardina's volumes open by questioning their own frames ('Middle Ages' as a label invented later, the 'long' 1700s from 1660, Hobsbawm's 'short twentieth century' 1914–1991). Where both courses teach the same story, both tellings are stocked into the one lesson, in filing order.
