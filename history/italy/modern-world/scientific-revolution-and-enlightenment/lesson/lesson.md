# Scientific revolution and enlightenment

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Scientific revolution and enlightenment" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unità 6, Cap. 18 §4 The Scientific Revolution

Galileo's telescope and his clash with Cardinal Bellarmino (Storiografia) and Newton's Principia (in the Documents) remake the picture of the universe against the Church's resistance.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unità 6, Cap. 18 §5 A new political thought

Locke argues power rests on consent and toleration (Document: Letter on toleration), giving the coming revolutions their language.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unità 1, Cap. 2 §§1–2 The hinges of the Enlightenment; Culture in the age of the Lumi

Reason, toleration and the Encyclopédie — Kant, Voltaire, Diderot and Rousseau arm a generation with the idea that society can be remade by thought.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unità 1, Cap. 2 §3 The Enlightenment, a movement open to the world

Scientific voyages, salons and freemasonry carry the Lumi beyond Europe's courts, and the course asks whether the Enlightenment's science also fathered modern racism (Storici a confronto).

## Sub lesson 5 — Modulo 1, Cap. 3 §§1–3 A cultural revolution: the new science; Galileo's trial (Eventi); A new political thought

Science separates its plane from religion's (Document: Galileo), and political thought follows — the century learns to argue from evidence in both worlds.

## Sub lesson 6 — Modulo 1, Cap. 3 §§4–7 The Enlightenment: its hinges and diffusion; The French illuminists; The new sciences and the Enlightenment in Europe; The Italian illuminists

Voltaire (Personaggi), the philosophes and Italy's own reformers carry reason into salons, lodges and academies across the continent.
