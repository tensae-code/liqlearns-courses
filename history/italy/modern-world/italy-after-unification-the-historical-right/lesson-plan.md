# Lesson plan — Italy after unification the historical right

**Course:** History — Italy · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Italy after unification the historical right
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The 1700s and 1800s — the first telling (Barbero, Vol. 2): Enlightenment and enlightened reform, industry, the American and French revolutions, Napoleon, Restoration, 1848, the Risorgimento and liberal Italy to 1900 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Italy after unification the historical right as Italy's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The 1700s and 1800s — the first telling (Barbero, Vol. 2): Enlightenment and enlightened reform, industry, the American and French revolutions, Napoleon, Restoration, 1848, the Risorgimento and liberal Italy to 1900 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unità 4, Cap. 14 §§5–6 The first years of united Italy: the political, economic and social situation; The annexation of Veneto and Rome as capital** — the Historical Right balances the budget and fights brigantaggio in the South (Storici a confronto: was it a civil war?); Venice (1866) and Rome (1870) complete the map, and the Southern question opens
2. **§2 Modulo 4, Cap. 14 The first years of united Italy (demography, economy and society; Governing united Italy; The revolts against unity and brigantaggio; The economy and fiscal policy; The conquest of Veneto and the taking of Rome)** — a poor, divided kingdom taxes flour and fights brigands (Fare storia: Lupo on southern brigandage) until Porta Pia (Eventi, 1870) makes Rome the capital
