# Medieval world the ottomans

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Medieval world the ottomans" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unità 4, Cap. 11 §1 The fall of the Byzantine empire and the rise of Ottoman power

Mehmed II's cannon breach Constantinople's walls in 1453; the Venetian Senate's report (Document) records Christian Europe's shock as an empire of a thousand years ends.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unità 4, Cap. 11 §2 The age of Suleiman the Magnificent

Suleiman takes Belgrade, Rhodes and Hungary and besieges Vienna; the Ottoman state becomes the Mediterranean's other superpower.

## Sub lesson 3 — Il declino dell'Impero bizantino (in the Mediterranean chapters)

Byzantium shrinks between crusaders and Turks while Christian–Muslim trade and war share one sea.

## Sub lesson 4 — Il Mediterraneo nel '400; I poteri nell'Impero ottomano; Il dominio turco nel Mediterraneo

After 1453 the Ottoman system — sultan, divan, timars — rules the eastern Mediterranean and presses on Italy's own coasts.

## Sub lesson 5 — L'espansione turca; DOC dossiers on the fall of Constantinople (Tedaldi; a Turkish chronicle; Piccolomini)

1453 is told three ways at once — a Christian witness, a Turkish chronicle and an Italian humanist — the course's method in one event.

## Sub lesson 6 — Modulo 2, Cap. 4 §1 The decline of the Ottoman Empire and the Safavid parabola; The siege of Vienna (Eventi)

After 1683 the Ottoman tide ebbs and Persia's Safavid arc closes; Busbecq's Letters from Turkey (Fare storia) let the empire describe itself.
