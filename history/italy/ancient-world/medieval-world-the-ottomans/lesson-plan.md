# Lesson plan — Medieval world the ottomans

**Course:** History — Italy · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Medieval world the ottomans
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Europe's revival after the year 1000 to the 1600s — the first telling (Barbero, Vol. 1): communes and crusades, crisis and plague, signorie, Renaissance, the voyages, Reformation and the century of crisis (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Medieval world the ottomans as Italy's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Europe's revival after the year 1000 to the 1600s — the first telling (Barbero, Vol. 1): communes and crusades, crisis and plague, signorie, Renaissance, the voyages, Reformation and the century of crisis — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Unità 4, Cap. 11 §1 The fall of the Byzantine empire and the rise of Ottoman power** — Mehmed II's cannon breach Constantinople's walls in 1453; the Venetian Senate's report (Document) records Christian Europe's shock as an empire of a thousand years ends
2. **§2 Unità 4, Cap. 11 §2 The age of Suleiman the Magnificent** — Suleiman takes Belgrade, Rhodes and Hungary and besieges Vienna; the Ottoman state becomes the Mediterranean's other superpower
3. **§3 Il declino dell'Impero bizantino (in the Mediterranean chapters)** — Byzantium shrinks between crusaders and Turks while Christian–Muslim trade and war share one sea
4. **§4 Il Mediterraneo nel '400; I poteri nell'Impero ottomano; Il dominio turco nel Mediterraneo** — after 1453 the Ottoman system — sultan, divan, timars — rules the eastern Mediterranean and presses on Italy's own coasts
5. **§5 L'espansione turca; DOC dossiers on the fall of Constantinople (Tedaldi; a Turkish chronicle; Piccolomini)** — 1453 is told three ways at once — a Christian witness, a Turkish chronicle and an Italian humanist — the course's method in one event
6. **§6 Modulo 2, Cap. 4 §1 The decline of the Ottoman Empire and the Safavid parabola; The siege of Vienna (Eventi)** — after 1683 the Ottoman tide ebbs and Persia's Safavid arc closes; Busbecq's Letters from Turkey (Fare storia) let the empire describe itself

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
