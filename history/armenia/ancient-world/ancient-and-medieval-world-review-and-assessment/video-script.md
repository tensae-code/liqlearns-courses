# Video script — Ancient and medieval world review and assessment

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Ancient and medieval world review and assessment.

pupils prove the whole Ancient East arc in a written test whose every answer carries a printed points value.

pupils prove the Greek arc — polis, Sparta, Athens, Persia wars, Alexander — in a rubric-scored written test.

pupils prove the Roman arc — kings, republic, conquests, Caesar, Augustus, fall — in a rubric-scored written test.

pupils prove the medieval arc — Byzantium, Islam, feudal Europe, Mongols, Ottomans, China, Japan, America — in a rubric-scored written test.

pupils prove the whole course arc in a final rubric-scored written test.

hour-by-hour guidance walks Egypt, Mesopotamia, the Hittites, India, China and Achaemenid Iran, closing with the Theme 1 summary lesson.

teaching notes stress why Athens became Greek culture's centre and how the summary lesson (Lesson 31) generalises the whole ancient world.

guidance centres Rome's fall and the Great Migration, Byzantium's thousand-year survival, Rus', the Crusades and the pre-Columbian Americas.

the Arab Caliphate, Mongols, China, India, Japan, the Ottomans and Iran are taught as one eastern arc, then two closing lessons summarise the whole course.

each theme ends with a designed summary lesson that tests the story arc, not just dates.

each block closes a chapter by testing whether pupils can argue the chapter's challenge-and-answer arc.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
