# Lesson plan — Ancient and medieval world review and assessment

**Course:** History — Armenia · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient and medieval world review and assessment
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Ancient and medieval world review and assessment as Armenia's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§12 Repeat / summarise Theme 1: scored test on the Ancient East** — pupils prove the whole Ancient East arc in a written test whose every answer carries a printed points value
2. **§22 Repeat / summarise Theme 2 (Greece part): scored test on ancient Greece** — pupils prove the Greek arc — polis, Sparta, Athens, Persia wars, Alexander — in a rubric-scored written test
3. **§32 Repeat / summarise Theme 2 (Rome part): scored test on ancient Rome** — pupils prove the Roman arc — kings, republic, conquests, Caesar, Augustus, fall — in a rubric-scored written test
4. **§48 Repeat / summarise Theme 3: scored test on medieval civilisations** — pupils prove the medieval arc — Byzantium, Islam, feudal Europe, Mongols, Ottomans, China, Japan, America — in a rubric-scored written test
5. **§58 Repeat / summarise Theme 4: scored test on the changing 1500s** — pupils prove the whole course arc in a final rubric-scored written test
6. **Methodical advice and sample planning for Theme 1, the Ancient East (Lessons 3–15)** — hour-by-hour guidance walks Egypt, Mesopotamia, the Hittites, India, China and Achaemenid Iran, closing with the Theme 1 summary lesson
7. **Methodical advice for Theme 2, Greece and Rome (Lessons 16–31)** — teaching notes stress why Athens became Greek culture's centre and how the summary lesson (Lesson 31) generalises the whole ancient world
8. **Methodical advice for Theme 3, medieval civilisations of the West and America (Lessons 32–52)** — guidance centres Rome's fall and the Great Migration, Byzantium's thousand-year survival, Rus', the Crusades and the pre-Columbian Americas
9. **Methodical advice for Theme 4, the medieval East, and the final summaries (Lessons 53–68)** — the Arab Caliphate, Mongols, China, India, Japan, the Ottomans and Iran are taught as one eastern arc, then two closing lessons summarise the whole course
10. **The guide's theme summary and assessment blocks** — each theme ends with a designed summary lesson that tests the story arc, not just dates
11. **The guide's chapter summary and assessment blocks** — each block closes a chapter by testing whether pupils can argue the chapter's challenge-and-answer arc

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
