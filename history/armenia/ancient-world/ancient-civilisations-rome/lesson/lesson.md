## Sub lesson 1 — §23 The beginning of the Roman civilisation

Etruscans, Latins and Samnites share Italy, Romulus founds Rome in 753 BCE, kings rule until the last is expelled and the republic is proclaimed in 510 BCE.

## Sub lesson 2 — §24 The Roman republic's social and political order

patricians and plebeians fight a two-century duel, plebeians win tribunes and the Twelve Tables, and the Senate, consuls and citizen legions run the republic.

## Sub lesson 3 — §25 Rome takes the Mediterranean

Rome survives Pyrrhus, outlasts Hannibal in the Punic Wars, breaks Macedonia and Antiochus III, and by 133 BCE rules the Mediterranean shore.

## Sub lesson 4 — §26 The republic in crisis

slave-worked latifundia ruin small farmers, the Gracchi brothers pay with their lives for land reform, Spartacus' slave revolt shakes Italy, and Sulla marches on Rome.

## Sub lesson 5 — §27 The struggle for power in Rome

Caesar, Pompey and Crassus share power, Caesar conquers Gaul, crosses the Rubicon, rules as dictator and falls to daggers in 44 BCE.

## Sub lesson 6 — §28 The founding of the empire in Rome

Octavian beats Antony at Actium in 30 BCE and, as Augustus, keeps republican forms while holding an emperor's power for over forty years.

## Sub lesson 7 — Theme 3 §5: the stages of Roman society; the formation of the "Roman world"

a Tiber village becomes a republic, then an empire, and Roman law and roads bind three continents into one world.

## Sub lesson 8 — Thematic plan, Theme 3: the antique civilisations (14 hours; plan table verified)

Greece's polis, Hellenism and Rome's rise to a world empire form the classical core of the course.
