# Lesson plan — Ancient civilisations rome

**Course:** History — Armenia · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient civilisations rome
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Ancient civilisations rome as Armenia's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§23 The beginning of the Roman civilisation** — Etruscans, Latins and Samnites share Italy, Romulus founds Rome in 753 BCE, kings rule until the last is expelled and the republic is proclaimed in 510 BCE
2. **§24 The Roman republic's social and political order** — patricians and plebeians fight a two-century duel, plebeians win tribunes and the Twelve Tables, and the Senate, consuls and citizen legions run the republic
3. **§25 Rome takes the Mediterranean** — Rome survives Pyrrhus, outlasts Hannibal in the Punic Wars, breaks Macedonia and Antiochus III, and by 133 BCE rules the Mediterranean shore
4. **§26 The republic in crisis** — slave-worked latifundia ruin small farmers, the Gracchi brothers pay with their lives for land reform, Spartacus' slave revolt shakes Italy, and Sulla marches on Rome
5. **§27 The struggle for power in Rome** — Caesar, Pompey and Crassus share power, Caesar conquers Gaul, crosses the Rubicon, rules as dictator and falls to daggers in 44 BCE
6. **§28 The founding of the empire in Rome** — Octavian beats Antony at Actium in 30 BCE and, as Augustus, keeps republican forms while holding an emperor's power for over forty years
7. **Theme 3 §5: the stages of Roman society; the formation of the "Roman world"** — a Tiber village becomes a republic, then an empire, and Roman law and roads bind three continents into one world
8. **Thematic plan, Theme 3: the antique civilisations (14 hours; plan table verified)** — Greece's polis, Hellenism and Rome's rise to a world empire form the classical core of the course
