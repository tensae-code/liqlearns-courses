# History — Armenia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Armenia inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Armenia's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** Armenia's books teach two stories side by side — world history and Armenia's own history — in the placements files' own teaching order, earliest books first. The first books open with how to study history, run the ancient world whole (first civilisations, Greece, Rome, the medieval world to the 1500s) and, in parallel, Armenia from the Highland's first peoples through Urartu, the Yervanduni, Artashesian and Arshakuni kingdoms, Avarayr and Mashtots to the restored Bagratuni kingdom. The next books run the "New Times" (the 1500s to 1900) while Armenia's own book answers its stateless centuries — conquest, Cilicia, division and liberation plans. The next books run the newest age whole (1914 to today) while Armenia's own book tells its twentieth century as one story — genocide, a first republic won and lost, seventy Soviet years, Karabakh and a third republic. The next world book then re-reads the ancient and medieval worlds by civilisation and what each left us, and the final world book drops chronology for six cross-cutting themes traced to today. The ladder follows that progression: each level is that turn of the books, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. Seven filed books are record-only with no History parts and carry no placements, so they make no level — they are named in ABOUT.md as filed. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
