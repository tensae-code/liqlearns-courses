# Fact or opinion

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Fact or opinion" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — §§4.3–4.4 Carrying out a study project; studying material with media-literacy skills

pupils learn to test a claim against its source before believing or repeating it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Preface: how history is formed — why distinguish facts from interpretations

the same news is told differently on different channels, so pupils learn to split the hard fact (a battle's date, a treaty) from the teller's interpretation before the course begins.

## Sub lesson 3 — §1: forming history — separating facts from interpretations in the classroom

the same event reaches pupils through different tellers, and the guide trains teachers to make pupils split the checkable fact from the teller's judgement.

## Sub lesson 4 — Appendix: glossary of terms and concepts

the closing glossary trains the exact vocabulary — conflict, cooperation, genocide, integration — in which the whole course has taught pupils to argue.
