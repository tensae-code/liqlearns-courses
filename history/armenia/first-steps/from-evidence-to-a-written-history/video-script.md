# Video script — From evidence to a written history

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: From evidence to a written history.

pupils learn to interrogate the book's source boxes, maps and timelines — evidence first, then the written history built from it.

the teacher is shown how each chapter's Xenophon, Strabo or Khorenatsi extract becomes class evidence from which pupils build their own account.

"Voices from the past" source boxes, glossaries and 3D monastery models teach pupils to rebuild events from testimony.

teachers turn chronicle extracts and travellers' accounts into the evidence from which pupils rebuild a divided Armenia.

letters, photographs, documents and objects disagree with each other, and the historian's craft is to question them, choose between viewpoints and build the account.

pupils' written histories are judged on how they handle evidence — sources chosen, compared and cited — not on retelling alone.

pupils interrogate ancient authors on Athens' restored democracy and Rome's wars, learning to squeeze evidence from a text before believing it.

teachers are given worked source material for the thematic course, keeping evidence-work inside a year otherwise organised by concepts.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
