# From evidence to a written history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From evidence to a written history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — How to work with this textbook

pupils learn to interrogate the book's source boxes, maps and timelines — evidence first, then the written history built from it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The guide's method: participatory lessons built on sources

the teacher is shown how each chapter's Xenophon, Strabo or Khorenatsi extract becomes class evidence from which pupils build their own account.

## Sub lesson 3 — Introduction: how to work with this textbook

"Voices from the past" source boxes, glossaries and 3D monastery models teach pupils to rebuild events from testimony.

## Sub lesson 4 — The guide's method for the medieval and early-modern centuries

teachers turn chronicle extracts and travellers' accounts into the evidence from which pupils rebuild a divided Armenia.

## Sub lesson 5 — Part Two §3: working with primary sources

letters, photographs, documents and objects disagree with each other, and the historian's craft is to question them, choose between viewpoints and build the account.

## Sub lesson 6 — §7: expected learning outcomes and assessing source-based work

pupils' written histories are judged on how they handle evidence — sources chosen, compared and cited — not on retelling alone.

## Sub lesson 7 — Part Three §2: historical sources, worked texts on Athens and Rome with questions

pupils interrogate ancient authors on Athens' restored democracy and Rome's wars, learning to squeeze evidence from a text before believing it.

## Sub lesson 8 — Part Three §2: historical sources

teachers are given worked source material for the thematic course, keeping evidence-work inside a year otherwise organised by concepts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of From evidence to a written history as Armenia's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
