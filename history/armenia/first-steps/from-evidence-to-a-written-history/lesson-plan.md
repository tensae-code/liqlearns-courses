# Lesson plan — From evidence to a written history

**Course:** History — Armenia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From evidence to a written history
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of From evidence to a written history as Armenia's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How to work with this textbook** — pupils learn to interrogate the book's source boxes, maps and timelines — evidence first, then the written history built from it
2. **The guide's method: participatory lessons built on sources** — the teacher is shown how each chapter's Xenophon, Strabo or Khorenatsi extract becomes class evidence from which pupils build their own account
3. **Introduction: how to work with this textbook** — "Voices from the past" source boxes, glossaries and 3D monastery models teach pupils to rebuild events from testimony
4. **The guide's method for the medieval and early-modern centuries** — teachers turn chronicle extracts and travellers' accounts into the evidence from which pupils rebuild a divided Armenia
5. **Part Two §3: working with primary sources** — letters, photographs, documents and objects disagree with each other, and the historian's craft is to question them, choose between viewpoints and build the account
6. **§7: expected learning outcomes and assessing source-based work** — pupils' written histories are judged on how they handle evidence — sources chosen, compared and cited — not on retelling alone
7. **Part Three §2: historical sources, worked texts on Athens and Rome with questions** — pupils interrogate ancient authors on Athens' restored democracy and Rome's wars, learning to squeeze evidence from a text before believing it
8. **Part Three §2: historical sources** — teachers are given worked source material for the thematic course, keeping evidence-work inside a year otherwise organised by concepts

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
