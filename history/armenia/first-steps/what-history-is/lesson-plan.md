# Lesson plan — What history is

**Course:** History — Armenia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What history is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of What history is as Armenia's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The first books: how to study history, the ancient world whole, and Armenia from the Highland's first peoples to the Bagratuni kingdom — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Introduction: why and how to study history** — the book opens by asking what history is, why the past matters today, and how a pupil's own family story joins the nation's
2. **Thematic planning and introductory lessons (Lessons 1–2)** — the teacher opens the year by introducing the textbook and the ancient world itself — what this history is and how the class will travel it
3. **The guide's subject concept: Social Science as the study of society in its development** — the course opens by telling teachers that society can only be understood by following how it changed — its groups, values and institutions over time
4. **Subject concept and outcome standards for the New Times course** — the teacher first frames what kind of history this is — how societies change — before any event is taught
5. **The guide's framing of Social Science as a subject that follows society through time** — teachers are told the subject's thread is society itself — its values, rights and institutions as they form and change
6. **Lesson 1: introduction, the programme and how the textbook is built** — pupils open a new course by learning how the book itself is organised and what questions the year will ask
7. **§1: historical thinking skills and methods** — before any dates, pupils are taught what a historian actually does — ask questions of the past, weigh evidence and build an account
8. **Theme 1 §1: the need to study history** — a pupil asks why the past matters and meets history as the memory that explains the present, opening with Abu Simbel's rescued temples
9. **Part One §1: the aims of teaching "World History" in high school** — the subject exists to widen the pupil's worldview and form a responsible citizen who can read change in human societies
10. **Introduction: six big themes for the final year** — pupils are told the year's history runs from the ancient world to today, but sorted by questions — how peoples cooperate, clash, fight and create — not by dates
11. **Part One §2: what history is** — history is named the memory of a person, a nation and all humanity, and world history shows the road from the first civilisations to global interaction

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
