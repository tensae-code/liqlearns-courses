# Video script — What history is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: What history is.

the book opens by asking what history is, why the past matters today, and how a pupil's own family story joins the nation's.

the teacher opens the year by introducing the textbook and the ancient world itself — what this history is and how the class will travel it.

the course opens by telling teachers that society can only be understood by following how it changed — its groups, values and institutions over time.

the teacher first frames what kind of history this is — how societies change — before any event is taught.

teachers are told the subject's thread is society itself — its values, rights and institutions as they form and change.

pupils open a new course by learning how the book itself is organised and what questions the year will ask.

before any dates, pupils are taught what a historian actually does — ask questions of the past, weigh evidence and build an account.

a pupil asks why the past matters and meets history as the memory that explains the present, opening with Abu Simbel's rescued temples.

the subject exists to widen the pupil's worldview and form a responsible citizen who can read change in human societies.

pupils are told the year's history runs from the ancient world to today, but sorted by questions — how peoples cooperate, clash, fight and create — not by dates.

history is named the memory of a person, a nation and all humanity, and world history shows the road from the first civilisations to global interaction.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
