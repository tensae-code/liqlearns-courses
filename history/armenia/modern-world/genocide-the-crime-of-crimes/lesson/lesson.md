# Genocide the crime of crimes

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Genocide the crime of crimes" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theme 4 §15: genocide

the Young Turks' destruction of the Armenians in 1915 — the Mets Yeghern — becomes the case from which Raphael Lemkin builds the very word "genocide" and the 1948 UN Convention, yet Rwanda and Darfur show prevention still failing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Programme Theme 4: Problems of all mankind

genocide heads the theme's content list, ahead of terrorism, trafficking, cybercrime and environmental problems.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Genocide the crime of crimes as Armenia's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
