# Assessment early modern and modern world history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Assessment early modern and modern world history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Summary I: Themes 1–2

a designed review gathers discoveries, revolutions and industrialisation into one arc.

## Sub lesson 2 — Summary II: Themes 3–4

the closing review ties the century's transformations to the beliefs that drove them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Thematic planning tables for Themes 1–2 (civilisations enter the New Times; industrial society takes the lead)

every section is planned with its key concepts and the textbook pages that carry them, from the Discoveries to Japan's Asian path.

## Sub lesson 4 — Thematic planning and assessment design for Themes 3–4

the year's second half is planned as one arc — Napoleon's transformations to the ideologies that promised better worlds — with its summary assessments.

## Sub lesson 5 — Theme 1 summary: the civilisational crisis and its resolution

a designed review binds the alliance system, the national problem, the war and 1917 into one crisis story.

## Sub lesson 6 — Theme 2 summary: the new world order, 1919–1939

the review asks why an order built to end war produced a worse one in twenty years.

## Sub lesson 7 — Theme 3 summary: the world in WWII and the Cold War

the review closes the bipolar age, from total war to the two-camp peace.

## Sub lesson 8 — Theme 4 summary: the world at the end of the 1900s and start of the 2000s

the review closes the century with the bipolar order gone and a new one unformed.

## Sub lesson 9 — Theme 5 summary and the chronology of important events

a final review and one long dated timeline close the three-year world-history course.
