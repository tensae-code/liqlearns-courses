# Lesson plan — Assessment early modern and modern world history

**Course:** History — Armenia · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Assessment early modern and modern world history
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — New Times and Armenia's stateless centuries: the modern world begins, and Armenia answers conquest, division and liberation plans (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Assessment early modern and modern world history as Armenia's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — New Times and Armenia's stateless centuries: the modern world begins, and Armenia answers conquest, division and liberation plans — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Summary I: Themes 1–2** — a designed review gathers discoveries, revolutions and industrialisation into one arc
2. **Summary II: Themes 3–4** — the closing review ties the century's transformations to the beliefs that drove them
3. **Thematic planning tables for Themes 1–2 (civilisations enter the New Times; industrial society takes the lead)** — every section is planned with its key concepts and the textbook pages that carry them, from the Discoveries to Japan's Asian path
4. **Thematic planning and assessment design for Themes 3–4** — the year's second half is planned as one arc — Napoleon's transformations to the ideologies that promised better worlds — with its summary assessments
5. **Theme 1 summary: the civilisational crisis and its resolution** — a designed review binds the alliance system, the national problem, the war and 1917 into one crisis story
6. **Theme 2 summary: the new world order, 1919–1939** — the review asks why an order built to end war produced a worse one in twenty years
7. **Theme 3 summary: the world in WWII and the Cold War** — the review closes the bipolar age, from total war to the two-camp peace
8. **Theme 4 summary: the world at the end of the 1900s and start of the 2000s** — the review closes the century with the bipolar order gone and a new one unformed
9. **Theme 5 summary and the chronology of important events** — a final review and one long dated timeline close the three-year world-history course

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
