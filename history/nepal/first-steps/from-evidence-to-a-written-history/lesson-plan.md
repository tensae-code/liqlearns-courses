# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Nepal · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps in History (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** We know the past from evidence — inscriptions, coins, old documents, stories, pictures and objects — and a written history is built by hunting that evidence, sorting it and keeping it safe.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps in History (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Historical Objects of Our Community** — History is a record of past events we did not see — so how can we know it? By its objects.
2. **Our Historical Objects** — What counts as a historical object? The law itself answers: under the Ancient Monuments Preservation Act 2013 BS, manuscripts, genealogies, inscriptions, copperplates, coins, statues, temples, chaityas and rest houses more than a hundred years old are protected things.
3. **turning research into a class history** — Three groups, three kingdom sets, one classroom wall.
4. **Historical Sources** — Nepal's past is sorted into its evidence.
5. **Events from Pictures, Video and Documents** — The modern story is sourced from things you can see.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: We know the past from evidence — inscriptions, coins, old documents, stories, pictures and objects — and a written history is built by hunting that evidence, sorting it and keeping it safe.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Nepal's books themselves work, telling the past as stories and tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to its turn: who was there, where it happened, and what changed. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: We know the past from evidence — inscriptions, coins, old documents, stories, pictures and objects — and a written history is built by hunting that evidence, sorting it and keeping it safe. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
