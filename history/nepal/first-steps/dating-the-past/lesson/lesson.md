# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Dating the Past. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — My Birthday

Meet Rita Chaudhary of Dang. Rita can say exactly when her own story began: she was born on 7 Fagun 2062 BS, and she tells her class the day, the month and the year. That is the first move of history — fixing a life to a date. Now do what Rita's class does: write your own birth day, date, month and year, and then the same record for two of your friends. Your past has its first page.
## Sub lesson 2 — My Friend's Birthday

Radha wants to know about her friend Ramesh, so she asks his mother the historian's questions: when was he born, and where? The answer comes back full and dated — Wednesday 14 Magh 2054 BS, at Kalaiya in Bara. Radha writes it carefully in her notebook. A birthday is not fully known until it has a place as well as a date. Now tabulate five friends: for each one, the birth date and the birth place.
## Sub lesson 3 — dividing Nepal's history into periods

Before the stories, the map of time itself. The teacher pins up a chart: ancient, medieval and modern Nepal, with their dates and their marker events — the start of Nepal Sambat, Prithvi Narayan's conquest of the valley. For the ancient dynasties, written sources are scarce, so the teacher tells them as a story, a katha, the way such pasts have always travelled. Periods are shelves: every later story in this course sits on one of them.
## Sub lesson 4 — Timeline Building

For the modern unit, the guide's method is the timeline itself. For every period — the 2007 BS revolution, the democratic decade, 2017 to 2046 BS, and 2046 BS to today — pupils build dated timeline models and event charts. History here is learned as an ordered chain of dated turning points: nothing floats, everything has its date and its place in the chain.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History starts with a date: your own birthday, written with its day, month and year, is the first dated record of the past — and timelines and periods are how all later dates are kept in order. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
