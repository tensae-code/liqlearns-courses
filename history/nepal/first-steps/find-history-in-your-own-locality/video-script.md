# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The past is not far away: your family's generations, your neighbours' changed lives and jobs, and your own village's story are history you can find, ask about and chart where you live.

Watch the map and the pictures as the story moves. Sunita Thapa's family carries its past in three generations. Chhewang's grandparents lived in Humla, one of Nepal's remotest districts, and their world was hard: no road, no school, no health post, and goods so dear because they came by plane and mule. Tenzing left his village of Chokde in Nuwakot and settled at Trishuli Bazaar, and his life changed its clothes and its food: dhindo gave way to market food, and the old dress — docha, bakkhu, pangden — gave way to foreign-style clothes. In Banjaria, Bara, work itself has a history. Dhaulya of Doti goes to look at his own district's past with his feet. Every neighbour in Nepalgunj arrived from somewhere. A community's history is also written by its good people. How did your village get its name? One hill village tells its story that way — the name first, then what can be seen from it: the snow peak, the temple, the rivers, the Kaligandaki and the Rupse falls that make the place known to strangers. Bibek Khadgi traces his own lineage as an identity record. Pushpa's project looks for the historical personalities of her own community, and finds them close to home. Lay two pictures of your community side by side: then, and now. Society itself has changed shape within living memory. The guide sends the class out of the classroom. The book ends its history unit at the pupil's own doorstep.

Remember it this way: The past is not far away: your family's generations, your neighbours' changed lives and jobs, and your own village's story are history you can find, ask about and chart where you live.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
