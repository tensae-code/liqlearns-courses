# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Nepal · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps in History (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The past is not far away: your family's generations, your neighbours' changed lives and jobs, and your own village's story are history you can find, ask about and chart where you live.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps in History (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **My Ancestors** — Sunita Thapa's family carries its past in three generations.
2. **Friend's Family Then and Now** — Chhewang's grandparents lived in Humla, one of Nepal's remotest districts, and their world was hard: no road, no school, no health post, and goods so dear because they came by plane and mule.
3. **My Neighbours Then and Now** — Tenzing left his village of Chokde in Nuwakot and settled at Trishuli Bazaar, and his life changed its clothes and its food: dhindo gave way to market food, and the old dress — docha, bakkhu, pangden — gave way to foreign-style clothes.
4. **My Neighbour's Occupation Before and Now** — In Banjaria, Bara, work itself has a history.
5. **History of My Village** — Dhaulya of Doti goes to look at his own district's past with his feet.
6. **The Past of My Family and Neighbours** — Every neighbour in Nepalgunj arrived from somewhere.
7. **The Good People of Our Community** — A community's history is also written by its good people.
8. **My Delightful Village** — How did your village get its name? One hill village tells its story that way — the name first, then what can be seen from it: the snow peak, the temple, the rivers, the Kaligandaki and the Rupse falls that make the place known to strangers.
9. **My Lineage, My Identity** — Bibek Khadgi traces his own lineage as an identity record.
10. **Historical Personalities of Our Community** — Pushpa's project looks for the historical personalities of her own community, and finds them close to home.
11. **The Past and Present of Our Community** — Lay two pictures of your community side by side: then, and now.
12. **Social Structure and Lifestyle, Past and Present** — Society itself has changed shape within living memory.
13. **Identifying and Preserving Local Historical Sites** — The guide sends the class out of the classroom.
14. **Identifying and Preserving the Historical Sites of the Local Area** — The book ends its history unit at the pupil's own doorstep.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The past is not far away: your family's generations, your neighbours' changed lives and jobs, and your own village's story are history you can find, ask about and chart where you live.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Nepal's books themselves work, telling the past as stories and tasks.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's story told properly (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of a child talking with a grandparent outside a village house, with an old fort on the hill behind them — the student looks at the picture and names the people and the place in it
   - ADD GIF: a short GIF of a short GIF of a village scene changing from long ago to today — a dirt track becoming a road, candles becoming solar lights — the student watches the movement and says what changed
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing and gives a real example (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to its turn: who was there, where it happened, and what changed. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: The past is not far away: your family's generations, your neighbours' changed lives and jobs, and your own village's story are history you can find, ask about and chart where you live. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
