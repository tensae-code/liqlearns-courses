# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our National Symbols

Some things belong to every Nepali at once. The flag is one: two triangles joined, with the moon and the sun on crimson. With it stand the cow, the danfe bird and the laligurans flower, and the crimson colour itself. These are the national symbols — signs every child in the country should be able to recognise and name as their own shared heritage.
## Sub lesson 2 — Our Historical Heritages

Temples, chaityas, durbars, manuscripts and copper plates: the things our ancestors made are not theirs any more — they are the nation's property, held by all of us. Some are so precious the whole world protects them: Lumbini, Boudha, Changunarayan, Swayambhu, Pashupatinath and the three Durbar Squares stand on the World Heritage list. Heritage is a trust. What the ancestors left, this generation must hand on unbroken.
## Sub lesson 3 — Our Ancestors

In Lamidanda, Khotang, Sombahadur remembers his grandfather Kaladhar — not for battles, but for service. Kaladhar brought the road, dug the irrigation canal and built the pati-pauwa rest places where travellers shelter. His grandmother Devimaya served the poor and the helpless. This is a different kind of ancestor-worship: honour the ones who built and served, and let their work set the standard for yours.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Heritage is what the people before us made and left — symbols, temples, old objects and ancestors' good work — and it belongs to everyone, so everyone must protect it. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
