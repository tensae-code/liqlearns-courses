# The Anglo-Nepalese War and the Treaty of Sugauli

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — The Anglo-Nepalese War and the Treaty of Sugauli. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

Why did Nepal fight the East India Company in 1814? Border quarrels over Butwal and Sheoraj, and the Nawab's pressure, pushed the two powers into war. The lesson weighs the whole account: why Nepal fought, how the fronts went, and what the Treaty of Sugauli cost — a third of the land — against what it saved: Nepal itself, unconquered. A treaty can be a defeat and a rescue in the same signature.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Border quarrels pushed Nepal into war with the East India Company in 1814; the Treaty of Sugauli ended it in 1816 — Nepal lost a third of its land, but kept its independence. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
