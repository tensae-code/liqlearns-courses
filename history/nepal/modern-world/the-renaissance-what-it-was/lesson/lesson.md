# The Renaissance: What It Was

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — The Renaissance: What It Was. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

Pictures open the lesson: Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael, Dante, Erasmus. The class charts what the Renaissance means — a rebirth — and what caused it in medieval Europe. Then the changes it brought: to art, to literature, and to science, where Gilbert, Galileo, Newton, Kepler and Copernicus remade the understanding of the world itself. When people start asking old questions in new ways, a whole age can turn.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Renaissance was Europe's great rebirth of art, literature and science: Leonardo, Michelangelo and Raphael painted and built, and thinkers from Dante and Erasmus to Copernicus and Galileo changed how people saw the world. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: state the lesson's one thing aloud, in your own words.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the people, the place, the turn, and what came of it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
