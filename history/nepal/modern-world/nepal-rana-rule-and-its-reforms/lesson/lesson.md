# Rana Rule and Its Reforms

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Rana Rule and Its Reforms. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rana Rule — Political, Social and Economic Condition

In 1903 BS Jung Bahadur seized power and did something new: he made the prime ministership hereditary in his family, and Rana rule lasted 104 years. Weigh it whole. The dark side: no rights for the people, caste evils, sati and slavery, and luxury imports for the rulers. The reforms: the Muluki Ain law code, Bir Hospital, a library; Chandra Shumsher abolished sati in 1977 BS and slavery in 1981 BS, and founded Trichandra College in 1975 BS. A hard rule can still leave real buildings and real laws behind.
## Sub lesson 2 — The Rise of Rana Rule and the Causes of Its End; Rana Rule's Economic and Social Effects

Jang Bahadur seizes power in the Kot massacre and founds a hereditary prime-ministership that lasts 104 years. The lessons trace the whole Rana order: its rigid class ladder, its few reforms, its heavy extraction from the land and people. And then its end — caused not by one blow but by many: education spreading, martyrs remembered, parties organising, and the revolution of 2007 BS. Systems fall when the ideas holding them up fall first.
## Sub lesson 3 — State Power after Unification

After the unifier, power moves through Shah kings and strong ministers. Bhimsen Thapa holds a long prime-ministership; court struggles sharpen behind the throne; and the sequence ends in Jang Bahadur's Kot massacre and hereditary Rana rule. State power after unification is a relay race run with a drawn sword — each holder passing power to the next by struggle, until one family takes it for a century.
## Sub lesson 4 — The Modern Period — Economy, Law, Culture and Art Skill

The modern centuries are surveyed whole to close the course. An agrarian economy and its slow change. The Muluki Ain, the legal code that ordered the kingdom's law. And the culture and art of the Shah and Rana centuries — the buildings, crafts and customs those years left behind. Economy, law, culture, art: a civilisation is the sum of all four, and the survey ends by adding them up.
