# Nepal's Martyrs and the People's Movements

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Nepal's Martyrs and the People's Movements. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our Brave Martyrs

For 104 years the Rana family ruled Nepal, and opposing them could cost your life. Shukraraj, Gangalal, Dharmabhakta and Dasharath Chand were killed for standing against Rana rule — the four martyrs. They were not the last: martyrs fell again under the thirty-year Panchayat, and again in the Second People's Movement of 2062–63 BS, which restored the people's rights as Loktantra. Each generation's freedom was paid for by named people.
## Sub lesson 2 — Search of Martyrs and Respect

On Magh 16, at a martyrs' programme, Shanti lays a garland for Sagar Singh, who was martyred in Lalitpur in 2046 BS. The honouring has its places and its first name: Lakhan Thapa is remembered as Nepal's first martyr, and the Shahid Gate in Kathmandu honours the famous four. But every district has its own martyrs, known and unknown. The lesson ends as a research task: search newspapers, ask elders, and find the martyrs of your own district.
## Sub lesson 3 — Our Brave Martyrs — Introduction and Contribution

The martyrs are taught with pictures, charts and documentary film, so their faces are known and not only their names. First stands Lakhan Thapa, the first martyr. Then the four of 1997 BS: Shukraraj Shastri, Dharmabhakta Mathema, Gangalal Shrestha and Dasharath Chand, executed for opposing the Ranas. A martyr's contribution is measured by what their death made possible for everyone who came after.
