# The First World War, 1914 to 1918

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — The First World War, 1914 to 1918. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

Europe pulls itself into war. Secret alliances divide it into the Triple Alliance and the Triple Entente; fierce nationalism and militarism arm the camps; imperial rivalry sharpens every quarrel; propaganda heats the crowds; and the Balkans stand as the tinderbox. In July 1914 Austria-Hungary attacks Serbia, and the alliances fire one after another. Four years later the Treaty of Versailles, 1919, closes the war — and the results lesson counts the cost and studies the new map.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Secret alliances, fierce nationalism, militarism and imperial rivalry pulled Europe into war in July 1914; four years of fighting ended with the Treaty of Versailles in 1919, a huge cost and a new map. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
