# Nepal's Unification and the Anglo-Nepalese War Heroes

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Nepal's Unification and the Anglo-Nepalese War Heroes. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our Heroes and Heroines

When the British attacked, Nepal's defenders became its first national heroes. Bal Bhadra Kunwar held the fort of Nalapani, with women and children fighting beside the soldiers. Amar Singh Thapa guarded the western forts. Bhakti Thapa, seventy years old, died defending Malaun. And behind them stands Queen Rajendra Laxmi, who ruled eight years and annexed small states to finish the unification Prithvi Narayan Shah had begun. Remember their names: they held a young country together.
## Sub lesson 2 — Our National Heroes — Prithvi Narayan, Balabhadra and Amar Singh

In a dream, the unifier explains himself. Prithvi Narayan Shah raises one finger: all Nepalis as one — that was the idea of unification. Balabhadra tells how he held Nalapani on little food and water and would not surrender. Amar Singh, offered British gold and rank, refuses them as a traitor's price. A country is made by an idea, defended by endurance, and kept by refusing to sell it.
## Sub lesson 3 — The Unification of Nepal — People's Participation

A class research report tells unification as the people's work, not the king's alone. Astrologer Kulananda Dhakal's counsel fixed the hour of the Nuwakot attack. Commander Kalu Pande fell at Kirtipur. And ordinary citizen Bise Nagarchi raised the war fund from 12,000 Gorkha households, while youths of sixteen to thirty marched. Kings plan campaigns; peoples pay for them, time them and fight them.
## Sub lesson 4 — Rajendra Laxmi and Bahadur Shah in the Unification

Prithvi Narayan died, and the work passed to two regents who quarrelled. His widowed daughter-in-law Rajendra Laxmi ruled as regent and pushed the armies west. She quarrelled with her brother-in-law Bahadur Shah and had him exiled. When she died in 1842 BS, Bahadur Shah returned as regent and carried unification to the Mahakali river and beyond. The kingdom grew even while its court fought itself.
## Sub lesson 5 — The Nepal–British War

Trade ambition turned to war. In November 1814 the East India Company went to war with Nepal. The fighting made legends even in defeat: Balbhadra at Nalapani, Bhakti Thapa at Deuthal, Ranveer Singh at Jitgadhi. But Colonel Ochterlony broke the west, and in March 1816 the Sugauli Treaty ended the war. Nepal lost a third of its land — and kept its freedom, which no treaty took away.
## Sub lesson 6 — National Luminary Prithvi Narayan Shah

He was born at Gorkha on 27 Poush 1779 BS and crowned at twenty, with a vow: weld the scattered states into one Nepal. By 1826 BS the foundation was laid. On his deathbed he left the Divya Upadesh, advice that still teaches: Nepal is a garden of four varnas and thirty-six castes; it is a yam between two stones; take no bribes and give none; and let the people's chosen man be Kaji. The unifier's last lesson was about how to keep what he had made.
## Sub lesson 7 — Our National Luminaries (later figures)

The gallery continues into the later ages. Ram Shah of Gorkha, famed for justice reforms. Prithvi Narayan Shah, the unifier. Bhimsen Thapa, the first prime minister. The war heroes Amar Singh Thapa and Balbhadra Kunwar. And Bhanubhakta Acharya, who gave Nepal its Ramayana in Nepali. Sword, law, statecraft and verse: the later gallery holds the makers of the modern country.
## Sub lesson 8 — The Unification of Nepal — Causes and Effects

The unification campaign is told fort by fort. Nuwakot is won, lost and retaken. Kirtipur falls only at the third assault, and Kalu Pande falls with its taking. Makwanpur is taken; the valley is blockaded; and Kathmandu is seized during Indrajatra, while Jayaprakash Malla feasts. Lalitpur and Bhaktapur follow. Causes below, effects after — until the scattered kingdoms stand as one Nepal, and nothing in the hills is ever separate again.
## Sub lesson 9 — The Contribution of the Heroes in the Nepal–British War

The war's heroes are honoured one by one. Amar Singh Thapa in the west. Balbhadra Kunwar at Nalapani. Bhakti Thapa at Malaun. Their stands did something no victory parade could: they made the British respect Nepali arms even in defeat. A country that loses land but keeps its honour has kept the thing it will rebuild from.
## Sub lesson 10 — The Modern Period

The modern age opens in 1825 BS with Prithvi Narayan Shah. The valley is conquered; the kingdom expands east and west; and the Anglo-Nepalese war tests the new state almost at its birth. Modern Nepal begins not in peace but in making and defending — the pattern of its first century is set in this one lesson.
