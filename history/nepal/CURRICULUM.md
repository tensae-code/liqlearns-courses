# History — Nepal course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Nepal inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in ABOUT.md and in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Nepal's own books give the ladder
their shape. The earliest books teach the past as the child's
own — a birthday, a family, a village — before any national
story (Level 1). The middle books run the national story in
the country's own period division, ancient (before 936 BS),
medieval (936–1825 BS) and modern (after 1825 BS), exactly as
the Grade 7 and Grade 12 books periodise it: ancient Nepal
and its old-world comparisons (Level 2), medieval Nepal
(Level 3), and modern Nepal with the modern world around it
(Level 4). The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced
gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Nepal's own
Social Studies books and teacher guides — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps in History** — Nepal's books begin history with the child: a birthday dated in full, three generations of family, neighbours then and now, the symbols and heritage everyone shares, what the past teaches, and the evidence it is known from. Five lessons that give the learner the tools every later level uses. 5 lessons, in title folders: `first-steps/` (5 lessons).

2. **Level 2 — Ancient Nepal and the Old World** — The story widens to the land itself: the valley lake and the country's name, the first dynasties, Mithila and the Shakya republic on the Terai, the Licchavi golden age, the ancient luminaries — and, for comparison, the old civilisations of Greece, Rome and the Indus. 10 lessons, in title folders: `ancient-world/` (10 lessons).

3. **Level 3 — Medieval Nepal** — The medieval centuries: the Khas kingdom of Sinja, from whose speech Nepali grows; the Malla valley split into three rival, art-rich kingdoms; and the Baise, Chaubise and Sen small kingdoms beyond the valley. 3 lessons, in title folders: `ancient-world/` (3 lessons).

4. **Level 4 — Modern Nepal and the Modern World** — The modern story whole: unification and its heroes, the British war and Sugauli, the first prime minister, Rana rule and the martyrs who opposed it, the democratic leaders, and the long road from the Rana fall through Panchayat, both people's movements and civil war to the federal republic — with the Renaissance, the voyages and the two World Wars, each with Nepal's place in it. 14 lessons, in title folders: `modern-world/` (14 lessons).

**Totals: 32 lessons** (Level 1: 5 — Level 2: 10 — Level 3: 3 — Level 4: 14), stocking all 84 placements filed for Nepal.
