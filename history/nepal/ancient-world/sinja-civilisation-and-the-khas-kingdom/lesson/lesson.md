# Sinja Civilisation and the Khas Kingdom

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Sinja Civilisation and the Khas Kingdom. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

Pupils meet this civilisation through a documentary film: Sinja, in Jumla, capital of the Khas kingdom founded by Nagaraj, come from Tibet's Ngari. Its kings — Nagraj, Krachalla, Ashok Challa who built Kankre Bihar at Surkhet, Jitari, Ripu Malla — stretch the kingdom wide, until around 1450 BS it breaks into the Baise and Chaubise states. Its deepest legacy is a language: from Khas speech grows Nepali, and its first inscription still stands at Dullu.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: At Sinja in Jumla the Khas kings built a great hill kingdom — and from its Khas speech the Nepali language itself grew, with its first inscription standing at Dullu. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
