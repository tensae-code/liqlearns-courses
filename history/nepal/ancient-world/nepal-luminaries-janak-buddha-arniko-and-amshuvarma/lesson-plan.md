# Lesson plan — Nepal's Luminaries: Janak, Buddha, Arniko and Amshuvarma

**Course:** History — Nepal · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Nepal's Luminaries: Janak, Buddha, Arniko and Amshuvarma
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient Nepal and the Old World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Nepal remembers its ancient great ones as lights: Janak the wise king, Buddha the teacher of truth and non-violence born at Lumbini, Arniko who carried pagoda art to China, Sankhadhar who freed the poor from debt, and Amshuvarma the reforming ruler.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Ancient Nepal and the Old World (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Our National Heroes — Janak, Sita, Buddha, Arniko, Sankhadhar and Amshuvarma** — Nepal's oldest gallery of great ones opens in Mithila: King Janak, who made his kingdom flourish, and his daughter Sita, who married Ram.
2. **National Luminary Gautam Buddha** — He was born at Lumbini on Vaishakh Purnima in 563 BCE, son of Suddhodana and Mayadevi — a prince.
3. **National Luminary Arniko** — In 1245 CE a boy sculptor named Balbahu is born in Nepal — the boy the world will call Arniko.
4. **Our National Luminaries (ancient and medieval figures)** — The gallery of ancient and medieval luminaries opens in Mithila with King Janak and his daughter Sita.

- Loses the thread of the story → go back to its turn: who was there, where it happened, and what changed. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: Nepal remembers its ancient great ones as lights: Janak the wise king, Buddha the teacher of truth and non-violence born at Lumbini, Arniko who carried pagoda art to China, Sankhadhar who freed the poor from debt, and Amshuvarma the reforming ruler.
