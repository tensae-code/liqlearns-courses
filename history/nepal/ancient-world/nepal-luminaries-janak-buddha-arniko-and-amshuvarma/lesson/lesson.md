# Nepal's Luminaries: Janak, Buddha, Arniko and Amshuvarma

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Nepal's Luminaries: Janak, Buddha, Arniko and Amshuvarma. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our National Heroes — Janak, Sita, Buddha, Arniko, Sankhadhar and Amshuvarma

Nepal's oldest gallery of great ones opens in Mithila: King Janak, who made his kingdom flourish, and his daughter Sita, who married Ram. Then Buddha, born at Lumbini, who preached truth and non-violence and is honoured as the Light of Asia. Then Arniko, the architect who carried pagoda building to Tibet and China; Sankhadhar, who paid off the debts of the poor and began the Nepal Sambat era; and the Lichchhavi king Amshuvarma, who reformed society. Kings, a teacher, an artist and a debt-payer: greatness wears many clothes.
## Sub lesson 2 — National Luminary Gautam Buddha

He was born at Lumbini on Vaishakh Purnima in 563 BCE, son of Suddhodana and Mayadevi — a prince. Then he saw four sights that changed everything: an old man, a sick man, a corpse and an ascetic. He left the palace, sought the answer, and won enlightenment under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya. His teaching — truth, non-violence, the middle way — spread across the world. Lumbini is now a World Heritage site, and UNESCO marks his birthday as a day of peace.
## Sub lesson 3 — National Luminary Arniko

In 1245 CE a boy sculptor named Balbahu is born in Nepal — the boy the world will call Arniko. The king chooses him for his skill. When China asks Nepal for master craftsmen, Arniko leads eighty artists north, builds the great chaitya — the White Stupa of Miaoying Temple — and is made chief of the imperial craftsmen. Nepali pagoda art spreads so far that China calls Nepal the land of sculpture. A teenager's hands carried a country's name across Asia.
## Sub lesson 4 — Our National Luminaries (ancient and medieval figures)

The gallery of ancient and medieval luminaries opens in Mithila with King Janak and his daughter Sita. Then Gautam Buddha, Light of Asia, born at Lumbini. Then Amshuvarma, the Licchavi minister who balanced Tibet and India against each other and raised the Kailashkut palace. And Arniko of Patan, the boy-genius artist who led eighty craftsmen to Tibet and China and carried Nepali art to the world. Wisdom, teaching, statecraft and art — the old ages had all four.
