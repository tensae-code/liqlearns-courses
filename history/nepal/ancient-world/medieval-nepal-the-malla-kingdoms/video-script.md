# Video script — Medieval Nepal: The Malla Kingdoms

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
For some 900 medieval years the Malla kings ruled the valley — after Yaksha Malla's death his sons split it into Kantipur, Patan and Bhaktapur, rival kingdoms rich in art, coins, festivals and trade with Tibet.

Watch the map and the pictures as the story moves. Nepal's own books divide its past into three ages: ancient — Gopal, Mahispal, Kirat, Lichchhavi; medieval, from 936 BS, the start of Nepal Sambat, for some 900 years; and modern, after 1825 BS. The three valley kingdoms lived as rivals who sometimes allied and sometimes invaded, calling in Sen or Gorkha armies when it suited them. After the Licchavis come the Mallas. Medieval life is reconstructed whole, not only its kings.

Remember it this way: For some 900 medieval years the Malla kings ruled the valley — after Yaksha Malla's death his sons split it into Kantipur, Patan and Bhaktapur, rival kingdoms rich in art, coins, festivals and trade with Tibet.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
