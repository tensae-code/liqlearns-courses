# Medieval Nepal: The Malla Kingdoms

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Medieval Nepal

Nepal's own books divide its past into three ages: ancient — Gopal, Mahispal, Kirat, Lichchhavi; medieval, from 936 BS, the start of Nepal Sambat, for some 900 years; and modern, after 1825 BS. In the medieval age the country splits: a Khas kingdom, Tirhut, and Nepalmandal. The Mallas enter the valley from Kusinagar; Aridev Malla takes the name 'Malla' from a wrestling bout. And when Yaksha Malla dies in 1538 BS, his sons divide the valley into three: Kantipur, Patan and Bhaktapur.
## Sub lesson 2 — Medieval Nepal — Political, Social and Economic Condition

The three valley kingdoms lived as rivals who sometimes allied and sometimes invaded, calling in Sen or Gorkha armies when it suited them. Society ran on caste and occupation; sati and child marriage stood beside festivals like Indra Jatra. Malla courts kept learned pandits and monastery schools, coined silver money, and grew rich on trade with Tibet. Splendour and hardship in the same streets: that is the medieval valley whole.
## Sub lesson 3 — The Medieval Political Condition

After the Licchavis come the Mallas. The political map of the medieval valley is drawn whole: Yaksha Malla's sons divide it into three rival kingdoms. Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur — three courts, three palaces, one small valley. The lesson maps that order before judging it: division made rivalry, and rivalry, as the next lesson shows, also made art.
## Sub lesson 4 — The Medieval Economic, Social and Cultural Condition

Medieval life is reconstructed whole, not only its kings. Land and how it was held; trade and what it carried; a society ordered by caste; its festivals filling the calendar; and above it all the pagoda art and craft that made the valley famous far beyond its hills. To know an age, count its harvests and its holidays as well as its battles.
