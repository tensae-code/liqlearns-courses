# The Licchavi Age

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — The Licchavi Age. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

For centuries the Licchavis rule the valley, and they leave their record in the most durable form there is: inscriptions. From the stones we read Manadeva's victories and Amshuvarma's statecraft between Tibet and India. Temples rise, coins circulate, and art and administration reach a golden age. A dynasty that carves its story in stone is still speaking a thousand years later.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Licchavis ruled the valley for centuries and left their story carved in stone: Manadeva's victories, Amshuvarma's clever balance between Tibet and India, temples, coins and fine art. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
