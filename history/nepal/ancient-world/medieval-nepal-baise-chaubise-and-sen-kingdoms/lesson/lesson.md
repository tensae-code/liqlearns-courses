## Sub lesson 1 — Medieval Nepal — Baise, Chaubise and Sen Kingdoms

Class groups take this lesson as researchers: to the school library, and back with presentations. West of the Kali Gandaki stand the Baise states; in the Gandak region, the Chaubise — both born as the Sinja and valley kingdoms weakened and local chiefs crowned themselves (Kaski's Sinja chiefs ruled some 300 years). The Sen kingdom begins with a refugee line: Jog Sen's descendant Rudra Sen makes Palpa a state in 1550 BS; Mukunda Sen divides it among his sons, and its branches — Makwanpur, Chaudandi, Vijaypur — spread east. Small kingdoms are not a footnote; for centuries they were the map.
## Sub lesson 2 — The Rise and Fall of the Small Kingdoms in the Medieval Period

Beyond the valley, the small kingdoms rise and fall. The Baise and Chaubise principalities and the Sen kingdoms of the hills and Terai grow, compete and weaken one another. Draw the map and you see a country in pieces — dozens of flags over one people's land. That divided map is exactly what unification would later gather into one Nepal.
