# Ancient Civilisations: Greece

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Ancient Civilisations: Greece. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

Sail west across the old maps to Greece. With atlas, wall maps and pictures of Homer and Herodotus, the class locates the land and meets its civilisation: its cities, its art, its thought. The question is the legacy — what did Greek art, Greek thinking and Greek city life hand down to the world that followed? Some gifts travel thousands of years and are still being used.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ancient Greece gave the later world city life, art and big thinking — a civilisation Nepali pupils meet on the map of Europe, with Homer's poems and Herodotus, the teller of histories. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: state the lesson's one thing aloud, in your own words.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the people, the place, the turn, and what came of it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
