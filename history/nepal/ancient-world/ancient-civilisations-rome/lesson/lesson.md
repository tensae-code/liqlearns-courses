# Ancient Civilisations: Rome

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Ancient Civilisations: Rome. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

Rome begins with a pictured founder: Romulus, and the city that carries his name. The class places Rome on the map of ancient Europe, then charts its legacy — how Roman law, Roman building and the Roman Empire shaped the world that came after. Roads, laws and arches: an empire can fall and still govern the future through what it built and wrote.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rome grew from Romulus's city into a great empire, and its law, building and roads shaped the world that came after it. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: state the lesson's one thing aloud, in your own words.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the people, the place, the turn, and what came of it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
