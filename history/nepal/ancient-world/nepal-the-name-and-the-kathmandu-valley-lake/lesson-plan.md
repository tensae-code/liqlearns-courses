# Lesson plan — Nepal: The Name and the Kathmandu Valley Lake

**Course:** History — Nepal · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Nepal: The Name and the Kathmandu Valley Lake
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient Nepal and the Old World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Long ago the Kathmandu Valley was a great lake; the story tells how its water drained away so people could settle — and the country's name, Nepal, carries rival stories that must be weighed against each other.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Ancient Nepal and the Old World (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How It Was Named Nepal** — Why is the country called Nepal? The stories compete, and no single one wins easily.
2. **The Origin of the Word Nepal** — The teacher opens the valley's oldest story as a performance: long ago, the Kathmandu Valley was one great lake.
3. **The Meaning and Origin of the Word Nepal** — The oldest lesson returns with harder questions.
4. **The Ancient History of Nepal** — The final survey begins before history itself.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Long ago the Kathmandu Valley was a great lake; the story tells how its water drained away so people could settle — and the country's name, Nepal, carries rival stories that must be weighed against each other.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Nepal's books themselves work, telling the past as stories and tasks.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's story told properly (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of a great lake filling a valley between green hills, with a narrow gorge cut at one end and water rushing out — the student looks at the picture and names the people and the place in it
   - ADD GIF: a short GIF of a short GIF of lake water draining through a gorge until fields and the first houses appear — the student watches the movement and says what changed
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing and gives a real example (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to its turn: who was there, where it happened, and what changed. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: Long ago the Kathmandu Valley was a great lake; the story tells how its water drained away so people could settle — and the country's name, Nepal, carries rival stories that must be weighed against each other. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
