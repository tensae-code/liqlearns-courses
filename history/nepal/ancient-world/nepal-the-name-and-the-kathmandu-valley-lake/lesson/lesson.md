# Nepal: The Name and the Kathmandu Valley Lake

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Nepal: The Name and the Kathmandu Valley Lake. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — How It Was Named Nepal

Why is the country called Nepal? The stories compete, and no single one wins easily. One says Krishna, or the sage Manjushree, drained the valley's great lake so the land could be lived in. Another gives the name to a herdsman, Nippa, or to the sage Ne who protected the land. Others trace it through the Newars, the Kirant word 'Nepar', the Newari sense of 'middle country', or the Tibetan 'house of wool'. For centuries the name belonged to the valley; only with unification in 1825 BS did 'Nepal' become the whole country.
## Sub lesson 2 — The Origin of the Word Nepal

The teacher opens the valley's oldest story as a performance: long ago, the Kathmandu Valley was one great lake. The monk Manjushree came from China, cut the gorge at Chobhar, and let the water out — and only then could people settle the drained land. Pupils act the part and feel the story in their bodies. Then the harder work begins: weighing the rival bases — religious, historical and linguistic — for the name 'Nepal' itself.
## Sub lesson 3 — The Meaning and Origin of the Word Nepal

The oldest lesson returns with harder questions. Pupils weigh every basis offered for the name 'Nepal': the sage 'Ne'; the Kiranti language; Tibetan; Newari; Lepcha; and Sanskrit literature. No basis is accepted just because it is old or beautiful — each must carry its proof. And the method turns homeward: what is the naming basis of your own settlement? Every place name is a small unsolved history.
## Sub lesson 4 — The Ancient History of Nepal

The final survey begins before history itself. The land is explained first: thirteen crore years ago a sea, Tethys, covered it; the Himalaya rose; and the Kathmandu valley lay under a great lake whose waters drained away. Then the name: rival proofs weighed — geographical, mythological, historical. The word 'Nepal' is first written about 500–600 CE, and the Tistung inscription of 569 BS is its first use. Only then does the Gopal age begin. Land first, name second, kings third.
