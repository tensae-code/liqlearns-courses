# Kathmandu Valley Civilisation: From Lake to Licchavi

## Do it first

Before you read anything, do this: say out loud what you already think this lesson is about, from its name alone — Kathmandu Valley Civilisation: From Lake to Licchavi. Name one person, one place, or one thing you expect to meet in it. Keep your guess in mind; the story will test it.

The valley's civilisation starts under water. The lake is Nagdaha; the sage plants the lotus; Manjushree cuts the Chobhar gorge and the water drains away. On the freed land the dynasties follow in order — Gopal, Mahispal, Kirat. The class charts the valley's ancient social condition against its condition now: same bowl of hills, two utterly different worlds. A civilisation is what people build on land that was once a lake.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Kathmandu Valley's civilisation begins with its lake: the lotus story and the draining of the water, then herding dynasties in turn, until the valley becomes a settled, farming, worshipping land. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood where the same kind of thing happens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the people, the place, the turn, and what came of it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
