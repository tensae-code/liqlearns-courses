# About the History — Suriname course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Suriname's own *Wij en ons verleden* (We and Our Past) books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Suriname inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four levels**, following the *Wij en ons verleden* series' own progression (see CURRICULUM.md for the derivation): Level 1 — first steps and Suriname in stories (14 lessons); Level 2 — the national survey: first peoples to independence (11 lessons); Level 3 — the national story rebuilt, theme by theme (6 lessons); Level 4 — modern national life: crisis, war, development and the republic (9 lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The arc whole:* "First, what history is — learned from a stone axe in a school garden, from a grandmother's games and from a museum — and Suriname's own symbols, its peoples' stories: Sampie, Ja-Ja Dandé, Matzeliger, Sophie Redmond, Mata Gauri. Then the whole national line in order: the first peoples, Columbus and El Dorado, the Dutch colony, the plantations and the slave trade, emancipation and the contract workers, resistance, crisis, war, and independence in 1975. Then the same story rebuilt theme by theme, and at the top modern Suriname: the unions, the schools, the dam at Brokopondo, boss in our own house, the coup and the road back to democracy."
