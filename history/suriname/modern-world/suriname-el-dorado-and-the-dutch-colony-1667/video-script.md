# Video script — Suriname: El Dorado and the Dutch Colony, 1667

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Spaniards chase the golden king El Dorado by Lake Parima and leave the "Wild Coast" disappointed; around 1650 Willoughby's English plant tobacco and sugar and build Fort Willoughby, until Abraham Crijnssen's Zeeland fleet takes Suriname in 1667 and the fort becomes Fort Zeelandia. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Columbus coasts Venezuela on his third voyage but never lands in Suriname; later Spaniards pushing up the Amazon are shot at with poisoned arrows and chase golden legends along the Wild Coast. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
