# Suriname: Plantations and Slavery

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Hoofdstuk 3: Plantages en slaven

Plantations are laid out along the rivers; Indigenous people are first forced to work, then enslaved Africans are shipped in, sold in Paramaribo and driven by overseers while planters grow rich. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Thema 4 Les 1: De aanleg van plantages

Plantations are drained and laid out along the lower rivers and creeks with sluices and polders; today's place names (Beekhuizen, Berlijn, Totness) still remember them. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Thema 4 Les 2: Plantage Meerzorg

One plantation studied whole: Meerzorg in Commewijne; by 1712 there are some 200 plantations, Cassard's French attack exposes weak defences, and Fort Nieuw Amsterdam is built in answer. ## Sub lesson 4 — Thema 4 Les 3: Hoe was het werk verdeeld op een plantage?

A plantation is a whole organisation under its owner and director; enslaved field gangs do the planting, tending and harvesting under the basya's watch while others look on at punishments.
