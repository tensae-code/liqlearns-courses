# Suriname: The Maroons and Ja-Ja Dandé

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Suriname: The Maroons and Ja-Ja Dandé** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Les 5: Ja-Ja Dandé, een moedige vrouw

Ja-Ja Dandé, priestess daughter of Maroon leader Ayoko, warns her forest people of soldiers, heals with herbs and persuades the Maroons to make peace, even letting the whites hold church in Santea. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Les 5: Ja-Ja Dandé (teacher guidance)

Teachers frame Ja-Ja Dandé as respect for elders' knowledge and for people who laid foundations the young now build on. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
