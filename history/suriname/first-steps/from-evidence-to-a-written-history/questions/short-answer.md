# From Evidence to a Written History — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Les 3: Susan vindt iets”?
**Answer:** Digging the school garden, Susan turns up a shining flat stone; her teacher identifies a stone axe made by Indigenous people 500+ years ago, before iron tools. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Les 3: Susan vindt iets (teacher guidance)” end?
**Answer:** Teachers use found shards and old bottles in dug soil to confront pupils directly with remains of past communities, then place the stone axe on the timeline. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
