# History — Suriname course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Suriname inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Suriname's books in hand are the *Wij en ons verleden* (We and Our Past) series — the Grade 5 story book, the older Grade 6 survey volume, and the new-edition Grade 7 and Grade 8 books, each with its teacher guide where filed — together with two Grade 3 OJW books that carry no history content and stand record-only, as filed. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the series' own progression: each level below is one book's pass of the national story, re-expressed as a level — first the story book for the youngest readers, then the straight chronological survey, then the same story rebuilt theme by theme, and last modern national life in themes. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Suriname's own *Wij en ons verleden* books of the Ministerie van Onderwijs, Wetenschap en Cultuur (MinOWC) and the earlier MINOV — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps and Suriname in Stories** — the Grade 5 *Wij en ons verleden* story book and its teacher guide — history first as short read-aloud stories about Surinamese people (the anthem and flag, a found stone axe, Sampie, Ja-Ja Dandé, the escape to freedom, Matzeliger, Sophie Redmond, Mata Gauri, the drowned town, the cutter voyage, Pak Saleh's wayang), opening with what history is and closing with oral history and the museum — 14 lessons.

2. **The National Survey: First Peoples to Independence** — the older Grade 6 survey volume (2004 revised edition) — one straight chronological national line: the first peoples (Arawak and Carib), Columbus and El Dorado, Crijnssen and the Dutch colony of 1667, plantations and the Atlantic slave trade, emancipation and contract labour, bauxite and new livelihoods, resistance from Kaikoesie to Boni and Mariënburg, the crisis and Doedel and Anton de Kom, the Second World War at home, and independence in 1975 — 11 lessons.

3. **The National Story Rebuilt, Theme by Theme** — the new-edition Grade 7 book and its teacher guide — the national story rebuilt in themes: how we know the first peoples, the Maya, Teotihuacán and the Inca, the Portuguese sea routes and Columbus's results, and immigration making one plural society of many peoples — 6 lessons.

4. **Modern National Life: Crisis, War, Development and the Republic** — the new-edition Grade 8 book and its teacher guide — modern national life in themes: the Great Depression, the unions, the history of schooling itself, cultures under colonial rule, the war years, development aid and the Brokopondo dam, how the colony was governed, autonomy — “boss in our own house” — independence, and the 1980 coup, the interior war and the return to democracy — 9 lessons.

**Totals: 40 lessons** (Level 1: 14 · Level 2: 11 · Level 3: 6 · Level 4: 9 — 131 placements stocked; all 40 lessons stand on student books, so every lesson is taught in 1 country (Suriname); the three teacher guides' 60 placements stock as labelled evidence inside the same lessons; all 40 lessons carry sub lessons).
