# North Africa: Fatimids, Mamluks and the Funj — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.**  The Fatimids claim the caliphate from North Africa, conquer Egypt, found Cairo and its Al-Azhar mosque-university in 972, and rule the richest trade crossroads of the Mediterranean until Salahuddin ends their line.
**Answer: True**

**2.**  The part “Chapter 3: the Fatimid Dynasty (909–1171)” is mainly about this: Osman I's little Anatolian beylik grows for six centuries — Mehmed II takes Constantinople in 1453, Selim I takes Egypt and the Hijaz in 1517, Suleiman besieges Vienna; the Janissaries rise and rot; the empire shrinks through Karlowitz (1699) and Greek independence (1821) until the Turkish Republic replaces it in 1923.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: The Fatimids claim the caliphate from North Africa, conquer Egypt, found Cairo and its Al-Azhar mosque-university in 972, and rule the richest trade crossroads of the Mediterranean until Salahuddin ends their line.
