# Hands-on — Ancient Egypt: What Egypt Gave

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Ancient Egypt: What Egypt Gave — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Society stands as a pyramid drawn on the page — pharaoh, vizier and officials, nobles and priests, scribes and soldiers, artisans and merchants, peasants and slaves; below the pharaoh-god, a world of many gods, hieroglyphic writing, and pyramids whose three inner chambers guard the king's road to the afterlife. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
