# About the History — Pakistan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Pakistan's own Punjab Curriculum and Textbook Board student books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Pakistan inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Legends and religious-history stories are told as the books tell them, as plot summaries in our own words.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the books' own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps and the First Civilisations.** Heritage and the historian's craft first, exactly as Pakistan's youngest books open them — what history is, how the past is dated and known from evidence, and the past you can hold in a museum — then the first civilisations in the books' own order (Mesopotamia, Egypt, Mehrgarh and the Indus in Pakistan's own soil, and China), closing with two long stories that begin in this age: how messages learned to fly, and how money grew from barter to banknotes.
2. **Level 2 — The Ancient World: Persia, Greece, Rome and India's Empires.** The empires that follow the first civilisations — Persia of Cyrus and Darius, Greece in its city-states and its games, Rome from village to divided empire, Alexander's march to the Indus, and India ordered by the Aryans, the Mauryas, the Kushans of Gandhara and the Gupta golden age.
3. **Level 3 — The Rise of Islam and the Medieval Worlds.** Islam's rise told as civilisation history — the Prophet's life and the rightly-guided caliphs, Islam's entry into Sindh in 712 where Pakistan's own story begins, Baghdad's golden age, Seljuks, Fatimids, Salahuddin, Ottomans, Safavids and the Crusades — beside medieval Europe's own arc: feudalism, the Vikings, Magna Carta, and the Black Death that ends the Middle Ages.
4. **Level 4 — India: Sultanate, Mughals and the British Advance.** The Subcontinent's own long arc — the Delhi Sultanate's five dynasties, Babar at Panipat, Sher Shah's five-year system, Akbar, the marble age of Jahangir and Shah Jahan, Aurangzeb and the long decline — then revival before 1857, the Company's advance from Plassey, Mysore's resistance, the War of Independence of 1857, and the reform movements that answer it.
5. **Level 5 — Europe Transformed and the World Wars.** Europe remade — the Renaissance and its great figures, the voyages of exploration and Portugal's sea road, the Reformation, the Enlightenment, revolution in France and Napoleon's arc, and industry in its two stages — then the two world wars: their causes, courses and results, and the divided, rebuilding world after 1945.
6. **Level 6 — The Pakistan Movement and Pakistan Since 1947.** Pakistan's national story as its books make it the spine — the movement from Congress and the Muslim League through the Lahore Resolution to 14 August 1947, told province by province, founder by founder, with women, students and minorities carrying it too — then the new state: refugees and state-building, Kashmir, three constitutions, Ayub Khan and the 1965 war, and 1971 and after.

All 69 of the 69 lessons stand on **student books** and count in the acceptance reckoning below; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *One land where the world's first cities and a new nation's founding stand in the same school story, told in its own books' order:* "Begin with the past you can hold — old coins, stamps and a museum visit — and learn how historians date and prove what happened. Stand in Mohenjo-daro and Mehrgarh, in Babylon and on the Nile. March with Cyrus and Alexander, sit in Athens and Rome, and watch Greece meet the Buddha in Gandhara. Follow Islam from Makkah to Sindh, sit in Baghdad's House of Wisdom, and hold Jerusalem with Salahuddin. Cross India with the Sultans and the Mughals, watch a trading company become a ruler, and rise in 1857. See Europe reborn, industrial and at war with itself twice — then come home: the Lahore Resolution, the founders, 14 August 1947, and the hard, hopeful building of Pakistan. That is Pakistan's own story, from its own school books, and it is yours."
