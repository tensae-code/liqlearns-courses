# Babar and Panipat: The Founding of the Mughal Empire — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.**  Zahir-ud-Din Babar (1483–1530), heir of Timur and Genghis Khan, loses Samarkand and Ferghana and turns to India; at Panipat in 1526 his small organised army, cannon and tulughma tactics destroy Ibrahim Lodhi's host; Khanwa (1527) breaks the Rajputs; his Babarnama remains the empire's founding diary.
**Answer: True**

**2.**  The part “Chapter 4: Babar, the first Mughal ruler” is mainly about this: Jahangir (1605–1627) rules through art and justice while his queen Noor Jahan (1577–1645) — poet, patron and power behind the throne — shapes the court; his son Shah Jahan turns the empire's wealth into marble: the Taj Mahal, Delhi's Jama Masjid, Lahore Fort and the Shalimar Gardens.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: Zahir-ud-Din Babar (1483–1530), heir of Timur and Genghis Khan, loses Samarkand and Ferghana and turns to India; at Panipat in 1526 his small organised army, cannon and tulughma tactics destroy Ibrahim Lodhi's host; Khanwa (1527) breaks the Rajputs; his Babarnama remains the empire's founding diary.
