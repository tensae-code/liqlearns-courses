# Hands-on — Aurangzeb, the Marathas and the Long Mughal Decline

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Aurangzeb, the Marathas and the Long Mughal Decline — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Aurangzeb stretches the empire to its greatest size and breaks it doing so — endless Deccan wars against Shivaji's Marathas (1630–1680), revolts from Punjab to the south, and after his death a parade of weak emperors until the last, Bahadur Shah Zafar, is exiled to Rangoon after 1857 and dies there in 1862. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
