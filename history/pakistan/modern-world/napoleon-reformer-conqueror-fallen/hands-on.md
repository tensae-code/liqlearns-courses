# Hands-on — Napoleon: Reformer, Conqueror, Fallen

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Napoleon: Reformer, Conqueror, Fallen — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The chapter's closing timeline carries Napoleon from the Revolution's general to the empire that reorganised Europe and the defeat that ended him — the arc the book uses to close the modern period's political story. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
