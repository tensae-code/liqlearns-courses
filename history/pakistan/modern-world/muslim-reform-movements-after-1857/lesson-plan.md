## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 4: Historical Personalities — Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (1817–1898)** — After the catastrophe of 1857, Sir Syed argues the Muslims of India must learn the new knowledge or be left behind — he opens schools at Muradabad and Ghazipur, founds the Scientific Society in 1863, raises the college at Aligarh that becomes a university, and plants the two-nation idea from which Pakistan grows.
2. **Ch.2 Reform Movements: after 1857 Muslims are blamed as rebels, lose jobs and land, fall behind in education as Persian gives way to English** — A defeated community is punished and shut out, and its leaders must decide whether to fight the British or learn from them.
3. **Ch.2: Sir Syed Ahmed Khan argues Muslims were not alone in 1857, urges reconciliation with the British and modern education, and presents the Two-Nation Theory** — Sir Syed tells Muslims to stop mourning the Mughals, take English education, and recognise Hindus and Muslims as two nations.
4. **Ch.2: the Aligarh movement builds schools — Muhammadan Educational Conference 1886, Anjuman-i-Himayat-i-Islam and Islamia College Lahore, Sindh Madressa-tul-Islam Karachi, Islamia College Peshawar** — Reform becomes bricks and classrooms, as community-funded colleges carry a generation of Muslims into modern professions.
5. **Ch.2: Darul-Uloom Deoband (1866) and Nadva-tul-Ulema, Lucknow (1894) answer the same crisis from the religious side, training scholars and teaching trades** — While Aligarh looks West, Deoband guards the faith, and the two answers to 1857 shape Muslim India together.
