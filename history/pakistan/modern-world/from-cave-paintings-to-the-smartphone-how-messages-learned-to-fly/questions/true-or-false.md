# From Cave Paintings to the Smartphone: How Messages Learned to Fly — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   The first messages are paintings on cave walls; then come letters and the post card, Marconi's wireless of 1901, Baird's television (Pakistan's own TV starts in 1964), the telephone in the pocket and Babbage's computer — every generation's message travels faster and farther than the last.
**Answer: True**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   The part “Chapter 2 (Culture): Means of Communication” is mainly about this: On the Nile, the river Herodotus called Egypt's gift, farmers grow rich on the flood; pharaohs raise the pyramids of Giza and the Sphinx, priests embalm mummies for the life after death, and the kingdom lasts three thousand years.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: The Class 4 story continues into the age of mass media — newspapers and magazines, radio and television, then the computer, the internet and social media shrink the world into what the book, quoting Marshall McLuhan, calls a global village.
