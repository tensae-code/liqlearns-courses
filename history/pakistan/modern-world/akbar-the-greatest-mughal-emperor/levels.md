# Akbar: The Greatest Mughal Emperor — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Crowned at fourteen after the Second Battle of Panipat (1556), Akbar welds northern India into one empire, abolishes the jizya, marries Rajput princesses, debates all faiths in the Ibadat Khana and proclaims Din-e-Ilahi in 1582 — the book names him the greatest Mughal emperor. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
