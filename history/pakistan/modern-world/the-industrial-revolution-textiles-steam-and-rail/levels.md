# The Industrial Revolution: Textiles, Steam and Rail — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — England, 1760–1840: steam is tamed step by step — Savery's pump of 1698, Newcomen's engine of 1712 draining mines, Watt's engine of 1776 — then steam takes to rails (the 1829 locomotive trials) and water (Fulton's Clermont of 1807, Trevithick's first railway engine of 1804). — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
