## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 4: the contribution of the provinces and regions in the creation of Pakistan** — After the failed war of 1857 the Muslims of India organise; the Lahore Resolution of 23 March 1940 demands a separate homeland; and each province plays its part — Punjab passes the resolution's spirit at the Minar-e-Pakistan's ground, Sindh, the land where Islam first entered with Muhammad bin Qasim in 712 and which the book calls Bab-ul-Islam, becomes the first province to vote for Pakistan in 1943, Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa join by their own decisions and referendum, until 14 August 1947.
2. **Chapter 4: the role of minorities in the creation and development of Pakistan** — The new country is not built by Muslims alone — minority citizens, from the Christians, Hindus, Sikhs and Parsis who chose Pakistan to servants of the sick like Dr Ruth Pfau, share in its making, and the chapter closes with the heroes decorated with the Nishan-e-Haider.
3. **Chapter 4: Historical Personalities — the organisers and the name-giver** — Begum Rana Liaquat Ali Khan builds the women's movement and founds APWA in 1949; Begum Jahan Ara Shahnawaz organises the Punjab women's Muslim League from 1937; Chaudhry Rahmat Ali, a student at Cambridge, coins the very name "Pakistan" in his 1933 pamphlet Now or Never; and Sir Abdullah Haroon carries the demand in Sindh, whose assembly votes to join Pakistan on 26 June 1947.
4. **Chapter 4: the role of the provinces and regions** — The map is completed piece by piece — Punjab, the movement's centre under leaders like Sir Mian Muhammad Shafi, is divided by the 3 June 1947 plan; Khyber Pakhtunkhwa votes in its referendum; Balochistan's Shahi Jirga and Quetta municipality decide for Pakistan; Mirza Hasan Khan's Gilgit scouts raise Pakistan's flag in Gilgit-Baltistan in November 1947; and Sardar Ibrahim's Azad Jammu and Kashmir joins its future to Pakistan.
5. **Ch.4 Political Awakening: A. O. Hume founds the Indian National Congress (1885) and its early demands grow** — A retired British official creates India's first national political platform, which Muslims soon find speaks mainly for Hindus.
6. **Ch.4: the Partition of Bengal (1905) delights Muslims, angers Hindus and is annulled in 1911; the Simla Deputation (1906) wins the promise of separate electorates** — Bengal's division shows Muslims the power of a Muslim-majority province, and Agha Khan's deputation turns that lesson into a constitutional demand.
7. **Ch.4: the All India Muslim League is founded at Dhaka (1906) under Nawab Salimullah and Viqar-ul-Mulk to guard Muslim rights** — Muslims decide they need a party of their own, and the League that will demand Pakistan is born.
8. **Ch.4: the Lucknow Pact (1916) unites Congress and League on one reform scheme, Congress accepting separate electorates** — For once Hindu and Muslim leaders sign the same paper, the high point of cooperation before the split.
9. **Ch.4: the Khilafat Movement (1919) mobilises Muslims to save the Ottoman Caliphate, then fades when the British refuse and Turkey itself abolishes the office** — Indian Muslims march for a caliph in Istanbul, and the movement collapses when its cause disappears.
10. **Ch.4: the Nehru Report (1928) rejects separate electorates and weightage; the Muslim League reacts and Jinnah's counter-demands harden the two sides** — A Congress-written constitution leaves Muslims unprotected, and the League learns to bargain as a separate nation.
11. **Ch.4: Iqbal's Allahabad Address (1930) proposes a separate Muslim state in the north-west; the Round Table Conferences (1930–32) fail and the Communal Award (1932) follows** — A poet-philosopher names the destination while London conferences prove Hindus and Muslims cannot agree on the road.
12. **Ch.4: the Government of India Act 1935 brings provincial autonomy; the 1937 elections and Congress ministries, documented in the Pirpur Report, convince Muslims of Hindu rule** — Two years of Congress government become the League's best recruiting argument for a separate homeland.
13. **Ch.4: the Lahore Resolution (March 1940), moved by A. K. Fazlul Huq with Jinnah presiding, demands independent Muslim-majority states** — At Minto Park the League stops asking for safeguards and demands a country of its own.
14. **Ch.4: women and students carry the movement — Fatima Jinnah, Begum Salma Tassaduq Hussain, Lady Nusrat Haroon, the Muslim Girls Federation and student activists** — Purdah-era women and college students become organisers and couriers of a national movement.
15. **Ch.4: the Cripps Proposals (1942) are rejected by both sides, Gandhi launches Quit India, and Direct Action Day (1946) turns Calcutta violent** — Wartime offers satisfy nobody, and politics moves from conference tables to the street.
16. **Ch.4: Mountbatten arrives, the Independence Act passes on 18 July 1947, power is transferred, Jinnah becomes Governor-General and Liaquat Ali Khan Prime Minister; minorities also play their role in Pakistan's establishment** — An empire ends at midnight and a new state begins with a refugee crisis already at its door.
