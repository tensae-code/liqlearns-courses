# Hands-on — The Founders: Jinnah, Iqbal and Fatima Jinnah

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Founders: Jinnah, Iqbal and Fatima Jinnah — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Three lives carry the founding — Muhammad Ali Jinnah, born in Karachi in 1876, who joins the Muslim League in 1913, presides over the 1940 Lahore session and becomes the new state's first Governor-General before his death in 1948; Allama Iqbal, born in Sialkot in 1877, whose 1930 Allahabad address first dreams the separate homeland aloud; and Fatima Jinnah, the Quaid's sister, a dentist who stands beside the movement and is remembered as Madar-e-Millat, Mother of the Nation. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
