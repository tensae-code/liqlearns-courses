# The Founders: Jinnah, Iqbal and Fatima Jinnah — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Three lives carry the founding — Muhammad Ali Jinnah, born in Karachi in 1876, who joins the Muslim League in 1913, presides over the 1940 Lahore session and becomes the new state's first Governor-General before his death in 1948; Allama Iqbal, born in Sialkot in 1877, whose 1930 Allahabad address first dreams the separate homeland aloud; and Fatima Jinnah, the Quaid's sister, a dentist who stands beside the movement and is remembered as Madar-e-Millat, Mother of the Nation. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
