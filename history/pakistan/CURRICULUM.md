# History — Pakistan course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Pakistan inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Pakistan's books in hand are the Punjab Curriculum and Textbook Board's Social Studies student books for Classes 4–5 and History student books for Classes 6–8, all under the Single National Curriculum (2020 for Classes 4–5, 2022 for Classes 6–8). The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the eras as Pakistan's own books present them: heritage and the historian's craft with the first civilisations, as the youngest books open them (Level 1); the ancient world's empires — Persia, Greece, Rome and India's own (Level 2); the rise of Islam and the medieval worlds, Muslim and European (Level 3); India from the Delhi Sultanate through the Mughals to the British advance and the reform movements (Level 4); Europe transformed and the two world wars (Level 5); and Pakistan's own national story — the Movement and the state since 1947 — as the spine the oldest book builds to (Level 6). The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Pakistan's own Punjab Curriculum and Textbook Board student books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps and the First Civilisations** — heritage and the historian's craft first, exactly as Pakistan's youngest books open them — what history is, how the past is dated and known from evidence, and the past you can hold in a museum — then the first civilisations in the books' own order (Mesopotamia, Egypt, Mehrgarh and the Indus in Pakistan's own soil, and China), closing with two long stories that begin in this age: how messages learned to fly, and how money grew from barter to banknotes — 14 lessons.

2. **The Ancient World: Persia, Greece, Rome and India's Empires** — the empires that follow the first civilisations — Persia of Cyrus and Darius, Greece in its city-states and its games, Rome from village to divided empire, Alexander's march to the Indus, and India ordered by the Aryans, the Mauryas, the Kushans of Gandhara and the Gupta golden age — 10 lessons.

3. **The Rise of Islam and the Medieval Worlds** — Islam's rise told as civilisation history — the Prophet's life and the rightly-guided caliphs, Islam's entry into Sindh in 712 where Pakistan's own story begins, Baghdad's golden age, Seljuks, Fatimids, Salahuddin, Ottomans, Safavids and the Crusades — beside medieval Europe's own arc: feudalism, the Vikings, Magna Carta, and the Black Death that ends the Middle Ages — 13 lessons.

4. **India: Sultanate, Mughals and the British Advance** — the Subcontinent's own long arc — the Delhi Sultanate's five dynasties, Babar at Panipat, Sher Shah's five-year system, Akbar, the marble age of Jahangir and Shah Jahan, Aurangzeb and the long decline — then revival before 1857, the Company's advance from Plassey, Mysore's resistance, the War of Independence of 1857, and the reform movements that answer it — 11 lessons.

5. **Europe Transformed and the World Wars** — Europe remade — the Renaissance and its great figures, the voyages of exploration and Portugal's sea road, the Reformation, the Enlightenment, revolution in France and Napoleon's arc, and industry in its two stages — then the two world wars: their causes, courses and results, and the divided, rebuilding world after 1945 — 15 lessons.

6. **The Pakistan Movement and Pakistan Since 1947** — Pakistan's national story as its books make it the spine — the movement from Congress and the Muslim League through the Lahore Resolution to 14 August 1947, told province by province, founder by founder, with women, students and minorities carrying it too — then the new state: refugees and state-building, Kashmir, three constitutions, Ayub Khan and the 1965 war, and 1971 and after — 6 lessons.

**Totals: 69 lessons** (Level 1: 14 · Level 2: 10 · Level 3: 13 · Level 4: 11 · Level 5: 15 · Level 6: 6 — 108 placements stocked, all from student books; 18 lessons carry sub lessons; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only — every lesson stands on a student book, as filed in ABOUT.md).
