# Hands-on — The Rise and Expansion of Islam

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Rise and Expansion of Islam — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Muhammad, born in Makkah in 571, orphaned young, known as Al-Sadiq and Al-Amin, marries Khadija and in 610 receives the first revelation in Cave Hira; a decade of persecution, the boycott and the Year of Sorrow follow; the migration to Abyssinia in 615 and the Hijra to Madinah in 622 — with Abu Bakr, hiding in Cave Thawr — open the Hijri era; then Badr (624), Uhud (625), the Trench (627), Hudaybiyyah (628), the peaceful conquest of Makkah (630), and the Farewell Pilgrimage and his death in 632. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
