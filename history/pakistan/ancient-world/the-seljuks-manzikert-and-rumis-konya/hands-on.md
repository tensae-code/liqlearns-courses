# Hands-on — The Seljuks: Manzikert and Rumi's Konya

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Seljuks: Manzikert and Rumi's Konya — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Tughril Beg's Turks take Baghdad and become Islam's sword-arm; Alp Arslan crushes Byzantium at Manzikert in 1071 and opens Anatolia; Malik Shah and his vizier Nizam-ul-Mulk hold the empire together; and in Seljuk Konya the poet Rumi (1207–1273) sings — the dynasty's other conquest. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
