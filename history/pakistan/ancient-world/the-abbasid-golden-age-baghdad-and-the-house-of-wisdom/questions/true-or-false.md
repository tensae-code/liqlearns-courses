# The Abbasid Golden Age: Baghdad and the House of Wisdom — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.**  The Abbasids move the capital to Al-Mansur's new round city of Baghdad (762); under Haroon-al-Rashid (786–809) the Bait-ul-Hikmah — House of Wisdom — gathers the learning of Greece, Persia and India; Al-Kindi philosophises, Ibn Sina writes the Canon of Medicine; and in 1258 the Mongols drown the golden age in the Tigris.
**Answer: True**

**2.**  The part “Chapter 2: the Abbasid Dynasty (750–1258)” is mainly about this: Salahuddin ibn Ayyub, a Kurdish soldier from Tikrit, unites Egypt and Syria, retakes Jerusalem in 1187, and faces Richard the Lionheart in the Third Crusade — the book's sultans list runs his house to 1260.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: The Abbasids move the capital to Al-Mansur's new round city of Baghdad (762); under Haroon-al-Rashid (786–809) the Bait-ul-Hikmah — House of Wisdom — gathers the learning of Greece, Persia and India; Al-Kindi philosophises, Ibn Sina writes the Canon of Medicine; and in 1258 the Mongols drown the golden age in the Tigris.
