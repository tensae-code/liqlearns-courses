# Hands-on — Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: When Rome's western empire falls, Germanic tribes carve Europe into little kingdoms; the Pope crowns Charlemagne and calls his realm a Holy Roman Empire; land becomes the only wealth, and society stacks into kings, lords, knights and serfs — the feudal pyramid the chapter draws in full, with daily life, manorial agriculture, trade, guild schools and inventions (spinning wheel, compass, printing press, gunpowder) as its texture. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
