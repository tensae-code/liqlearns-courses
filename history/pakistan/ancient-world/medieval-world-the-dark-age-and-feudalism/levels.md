# Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — When Rome's western empire falls, Germanic tribes carve Europe into little kingdoms; the Pope crowns Charlemagne and calls his realm a Holy Roman Empire; land becomes the only wealth, and society stacks into kings, lords, knights and serfs — the feudal pyramid the chapter draws in full, with daily life, manorial agriculture, trade, guild schools and inventions (spinning wheel, compass, printing press, gunpowder) as its texture. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
