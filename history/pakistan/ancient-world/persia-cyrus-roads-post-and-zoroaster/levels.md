# Persia: Cyrus, Roads, Post and Zoroaster — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Cyrus founds the Achaemenid Empire in 550 BCE and takes Babylon in 539; Cambyses adds Egypt in 525; Darius the Great organises twenty satrapies, builds the Royal Road from Sardis to Susa and a postal relay to hold it together; Zoroaster's faith burns underneath it all — until Alexander ends the empire in 331 BCE. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
