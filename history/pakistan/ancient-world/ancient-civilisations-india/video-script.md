# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: India


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

In the learners' own soil, the Indus Valley people build planned cities like Mohenjo-daro around 2500 BCE, stamp their goods with animal seals and trade far away; the cities fade, Aryan newcomers arrive around 1500 BCE, and the buried cities sleep until excavators uncover them in 1921–22.

In the learners' own soil, the Indus Valley people build planned cities like Mohenjo-daro around 2500 BCE, stamp their goods with animal seals and trade far away; the cities fade, Aryan newcomers arrive around 1500 BCE, and the buried cities sleep until excavators uncover them in 1921–22.

From 3300 to 1300 BCE the Indus cities — Mohenjo-daro and Harappa first among them — plan their streets, baths and drains, farm the floodplain, carve animal seals in a logo-syllabic script no one has yet read, trade with Mesopotamia, and then, for reasons still argued (river shifts, floods, drying), fade away.

Chandragupta Maurya founds India's first great empire in 321 BCE; his grandson Ashoka (268–232 BCE), sickened by the slaughter at Kalinga, adopts Buddhism, carves his edicts on pillars — one becomes modern India's emblem — and sends the faith across Asia; within fifty years of his death the empire is gone (185 BCE).


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Pakistan.
