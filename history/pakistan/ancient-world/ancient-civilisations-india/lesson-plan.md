**Course:** History — Pakistan · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient Civilisations: India

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 4: Early Civilisations 3 — the Indus Valley** — In the learners' own soil, the Indus Valley people build planned cities like Mohenjo-daro around 2500 BCE, stamp their goods with animal seals and trade far away; the cities fade, Aryan newcomers arrive around 1500 BCE, and the buried cities sleep until excavators uncover them in 1921–22.
2. **Chapter 1: the Indus Valley civilisation** — From 3300 to 1300 BCE the Indus cities — Mohenjo-daro and Harappa first among them — plan their streets, baths and drains, farm the floodplain, carve animal seals in a logo-syllabic script no one has yet read, trade with Mesopotamia, and then, for reasons still argued (river shifts, floods, drying), fade away.
3. **Chapter 3: the Mauryan Empire** — Chandragupta Maurya founds India's first great empire in 321 BCE; his grandson Ashoka (268–232 BCE), sickened by the slaughter at Kalinga, adopts Buddhism, carves his edicts on pillars — one becomes modern India's emblem — and sends the faith across Asia; within fifty years of his death the empire is gone (185 BCE).
