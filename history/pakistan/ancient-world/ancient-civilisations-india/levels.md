## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 4: Early Civilisations 3 — the Indus Valley
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: In the learners' own soil, the Indus Valley people build planned cities like Mohenjo-daro around 2500 BCE, stamp their goods with animal seals and trade far away; the cities fade, Aryan newcomers arrive around 1500 BCE, and the buried cities sleep until excavators uncover them in 1921–22. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 1: the Indus Valley civilisation
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: From 3300 to 1300 BCE the Indus cities — Mohenjo-daro and Harappa first among them — plan their streets, baths and drains, farm the floodplain, carve animal seals in a logo-syllabic script no one has yet read, trade with Mesopotamia, and then, for reasons still argued (river shifts, floods, drying), fade away. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 3: the Mauryan Empire
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Chandragupta Maurya founds India's first great empire in 321 BCE; his grandson Ashoka (268–232 BCE), sickened by the slaughter at Kalinga, adopts Buddhism, carves his edicts on pillars — one becomes modern India's emblem — and sends the faith across Asia; within fifty years of his death the empire is gone (185 BCE). — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
