# Hands-on — From Barter to Banknotes: The Story of Money

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — From Barter to Banknotes: The Story of Money — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Money has a biography — first barter, swapping goods for goods; then metal coins, struck in China around 1000 BCE and in Lydia around 500 BCE, with leather notes in China by 118 BCE; then paper money in China around 806 CE, spreading until notes, and finally plastic cards, carry the world's trade. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
