# Lesson plan — From Barter to Banknotes: The Story of Money

**Course:** History — Pakistan · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** From Barter to Banknotes: The Story of Money
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps and the First Civilisations (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Money has a biography — first barter, swapping goods for goods; then metal coins, struck in China around 1000 BCE and in Lydia around 500 BCE, with leather notes in China by 118 BCE; then paper money in China around 806 CE, spreading until notes, and finally plastic cards, carry the world's trade.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps and the First Civilisations (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 6 (Economics): the Evolution of Money** — Money has a biography — first barter, swapping goods for goods; then metal coins, struck in China around 1000 BCE and in Lydia around 500 BCE, with leather notes in China by 118 BCE; then paper money in China around 806 CE, spreading until notes, and finally plastic cards, carry the world's trade.
2. **Chapter 6 (Economics): the history of money in the Subcontinent** — The same story on home ground — punch-marked coins in the ancient kingdoms, new coinages after the Arab arrival of 712, Sher Shah Suri's silver rupiya that still gives the rupee its name, British coins stamped with the monarch's face after 1857, and in 1948 a new state's own State Bank.
