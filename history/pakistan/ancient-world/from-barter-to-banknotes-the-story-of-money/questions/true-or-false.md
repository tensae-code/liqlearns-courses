# From Barter to Banknotes: The Story of Money — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   Money has a biography — first barter, swapping goods for goods; then metal coins, struck in China around 1000 BCE and in Lydia around 500 BCE, with leather notes in China by 118 BCE; then paper money in China around 806 CE, spreading until notes, and finally plastic cards, carry the world's trade.
**Answer: True**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   The part “Chapter 6 (Economics): the history of money in the Subcontinent” is mainly about this: Society stands as a pyramid drawn on the page — pharaoh, vizier and officials, nobles and priests, scribes and soldiers, artisans and merchants, peasants and slaves; below the pharaoh-god, a world of many gods, hieroglyphic writing, and pyramids whose three inner chambers guard the king's road to the afterlife.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: The same story on home ground — punch-marked coins in the ancient kingdoms, new coinages after the Arab arrival of 712, Sher Shah Suri's silver rupiya that still gives the rupee its name, British coins stamped with the monarch's face after 1857, and in 1948 a new state's own State Bank.
