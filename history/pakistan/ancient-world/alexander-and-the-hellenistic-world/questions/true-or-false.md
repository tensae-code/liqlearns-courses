# Alexander and the Hellenistic World — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.**  Alexander (356–323 BCE), king of Macedon at twenty, destroys the Persian Empire, and in 327 BCE turns toward India — the book asks why he wanted India at all: its wealth, and the Greek belief that the world's edge lay just beyond the Indus.
**Answer: True**

**2.**  The part “Chapter 2: Alexander the Great” is mainly about this: Muhammad, born in Makkah in 571, orphaned young, known as Al-Sadiq and Al-Amin, marries Khadija and in 610 receives the first revelation in Cave Hira; a decade of persecution, the boycott and the Year of Sorrow follow; the migration to Abyssinia in 615 and the Hijra to Madinah in 622 — with Abu Bakr, hiding in Cave Thawr — open the Hijri era; then Badr (624), Uhud (625), the Trench (627), Hudaybiyyah (628), the peaceful conquest of Makkah (630), and the Farewell Pilgrimage and his death in 632.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: Alexander (356–323 BCE), king of Macedon at twenty, destroys the Persian Empire, and in 327 BCE turns toward India — the book asks why he wanted India at all: its wealth, and the Greek belief that the world's edge lay just beyond the Indus.
