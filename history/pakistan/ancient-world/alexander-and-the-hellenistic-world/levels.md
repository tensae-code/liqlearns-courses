# Alexander and the Hellenistic World — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Alexander (356–323 BCE), king of Macedon at twenty, destroys the Persian Empire, and in 327 BCE turns toward India — the book asks why he wanted India at all: its wealth, and the Greek belief that the world's edge lay just beyond the Indus. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
