# Gandhara: Where Greece Met the Buddha — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   In Pakistan's own north-west, around Peshawar, Swat and Taxila, a kingdom stands where empires collide — Persian satraps, Alexander's march of 327–326 BCE, Ashoka turning it Buddhist and raising stupas, Kanishka spreading the faith, and Greek sculptors carving the Buddha's face — until the White Huns burn it down around 500 CE.
**Answer: True**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   The part “Chapter 3: the Kushan Empire” is mainly about this: Before any civilisation, the book teaches how the past is known at all — primary sources (diaries, birth certificates, interviews, government records, newspapers) versus secondary sources (research articles, textbooks, encyclopaedias), and what each is used for.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: The Kushans, Central Asian nomads turned kings — Kadphises I, Vima Kadphises with his gold coins, and Kanishka (127–151 CE), whose empire stitches the Silk Road from China to Rome; under them Gandhara's Buddhism travels to China and Sanskrit learning flourishes.
