# The Delhi Sultanate: Five Dynasties — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.**  Mahmood of Ghazni raids India seventeen times (1000–1027), but Shahab-ud-Din Ghori plants rule — Tarain 1192 — and his slave-general Qutb-ud-Din Aibak founds the Sultanate in 1206; five dynasties follow — Mamluk (Iltutmish the real organiser, Razia the queen, Balban), Khilji (Ala-ud-Din's market reforms and Mongol-proof armies), Tughlaq (Firoz Shah's canals and employment bureau), Sayyid, and Lodhi, until Babar ends it at Panipat.
**Answer: True**

**2.**  The part “Chapter 4: the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)” is mainly about this: Crowned at fourteen after the Second Battle of Panipat (1556), Akbar welds northern India into one empire, abolishes the jizya, marries Rajput princesses, debates all faiths in the Ibadat Khana and proclaims Din-e-Ilahi in 1582 — the book names him the greatest Mughal emperor.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: Mahmood of Ghazni raids India seventeen times (1000–1027), but Shahab-ud-Din Ghori plants rule — Tarain 1192 — and his slave-general Qutb-ud-Din Aibak founds the Sultanate in 1206; five dynasties follow — Mamluk (Iltutmish the real organiser, Razia the queen, Balban), Khilji (Ala-ud-Din's market reforms and Mongol-proof armies), Tughlaq (Firoz Shah's canals and employment bureau), Sayyid, and Lodhi, until Babar ends it at Panipat.
