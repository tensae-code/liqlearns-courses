# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sharks, turtles and whales in Pacific culture
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the sacred Tongan shark-noosing ritual for the goddess Hina, turtle taboos that let only chiefs and priests eat turtle at ceremonies, and whales told of across the Pacific as guardians and ancestors — the animals of science are also ancestors and guardians in the people's stories, and losing them would erase identity — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional uses of the reef and ocean — Tuvalu's own practices
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tuvalu's own practices — women and children gathering shellfish on Nukufetau, men diving for pearl shells for fish hooks, gogo seabirds caught on moonlit windy nights with pulou nets of coconut fibre — fishing as a community craft governed by custom, closed seasons and sharing, before subsistence gave way to imports and taking more than is needed — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — People and the natural environment — knowledge passed down
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pacific people passed their special understanding of the environment and its natural resources down through the generations — in stories, poems, legends and songs, and by elders showing the young — knowing when to plant, fish and spare the land as an inheritance carried in memory — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Case study 1 — taro in Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu: Tuvalu's pulaka
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tuvalu's pulaka — the giant swamp taro brought by travellers from Kiribati, grown in great compost pits below the water table, its pit-making knowledge passed down through generations, its leaves wrapping food and its corm cooked as taufagogo in an earth oven — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Case study 2 — the amazing coconut tree
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the coconut palm gives food, drink, oil, fibre and shelter; in Tuvalu its sap is tapped for traditional red toddy, and Niue's name breaks down as Niu (coconut) + e (here) — one tree feeds, houses and names a people — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Notes for use of Book 3 — the lesson on inheritance (teacher-guide evidence)
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the teacher guide has teachers discuss how knowledge is passed down through generations, send pupils home to ask an elder about traditional use of natural resources, and invite an elder into the classroom — a lesson about a tree becomes a lesson about inheritance (teacher-guide evidence) — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Notes for teachers in Tuvalu — safeguarding, story and song (teacher-guide evidence)
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tuvaluans are urged to re-learn the ancestors' way of safeguarding resources — closing a lagoon such as Namo to all use until it is healthy again — and to keep Tuvaluan values alive through verbal storytelling and fakaseasea/fatele song (teacher-guide evidence) — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
