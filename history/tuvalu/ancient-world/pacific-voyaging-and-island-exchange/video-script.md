# Video script — Pacific Voyaging and Island Exchange

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. The sea did not divide the Pacific islands. The sea joined them.

Look at the map. Dot after dot of islands rises out of one enormous ocean: Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue, Rotuma, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Futuna and Uvea. From far away they look alone. They were not.

Long ago, these islands were part of one large exchange community — with no borders. And the way the community worked was movement: people voyaged — regularly, on purpose — from island to island, finding their way by stars and legends. Every crossing carried something: wealth, skills, goods. What one island had, another island could receive. What one people knew how to do, another people could learn.

That is the turn in this story: the water between the islands was not a wall. It was the road the community travelled on. The reader that tells this story gives it a title that says it all: "The Ocean in our blood."

Remember it this way: one sea, many homes — and the voyaging made them one community.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
