# History — Tuvalu course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Tuvalu's
own — built from the regional USP/UNESCO Education for
Sustainable Development readers carried in Tuvalu's record
(Reader 1 *People and the Sea*, Reader 3 *People and the
Natural Environment*, and their Teacher Guide; English).
Inside the course the organisation is by **levels, not
grades**: the titles below sit inside the levels, in path
order — what the learner proves, step by step. The grade
the books come from lives only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the readers': Reader 1 opens with
the voyaging past, then the sea's heritage; Reader 3 turns
landward to the heritage of the pulaka pits and the coconut
tree; the Teacher Guide closes Level 1 by sending the
learner to an elder in their own locality and by its
Tuvalu section on safeguarding resources; and Level 2 takes
the modern turn the same Reader 1 tells — the coming of
European whaling. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — the Pacific past through the sea and the land**
(ESD Readers 1 and 3, student books, with the ESD Teacher
Guide as labelled evidence):

- `ancient-world/` — Level 1, first title. Pacific
  Voyaging and Island Exchange ("The Ocean in our blood":
  Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue, Rotuma, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Futuna
  and Uvea as one large exchange community with no borders,
  its peoples voyaging by stars and legends between islands
  and circulating wealth, skills and goods — one sea
  joining many homes).
- `first-steps/` — Level 1, second title. Heritage: What
  It Is and Why It Matters (sharks, turtles and whales in
  Pacific culture — Hina's shark-noosing, the turtle taboos,
  whales as guardians and ancestors; Tuvalu's own
  traditional uses of the reef and ocean — Nukufetau
  shellfish gathering, pearl-shell fish hooks, gogo birds
  taken with pulou nets; environmental knowledge passed
  down through the generations; Tuvalu's pulaka, the giant
  swamp taro in its compost pits; the amazing coconut tree
  — red toddy in Tuvalu and Niue's own name-story; the
  teacher guide's lesson on inheritance; and the teacher
  guide's Tuvalu section — the closed Namo lagoon, and
  values kept alive in storytelling and fatele song); and
  Find History in Your Own Locality (the teacher guide's
  method: pupils talk to an elder about sacred and
  traditional practices, list traditional fishing practices
  and the fish that were caught, and find a legend from
  their own culture about the turtle, whale or shark — the
  oldest person in the village becomes the textbook).

**Level 2 — the modern world reaches the Pacific**
(ESD Reader 1, student book):

- `modern-world/` — Level 2, only title. European Whaling
  in the Pacific (Pacific people used only stranded whales,
  taking teeth for chiefs' necklaces, until North American
  whaling fleets reached Tonga in the 1890s and hunted
  mothers and calves — near extinction in one century of
  commercial hunting, then slow recovery under legal
  protection).
