# History — Tuvalu course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Tuvalu inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Tuvalu has no national book of its
own in this set — the Pacific past is taught regionally,
through the sea and the land, in the USP/UNESCO Education
for Sustainable Development readers Tuvalu's record carries
(the same set is also shelved under Nauru and Niue — see
ABOUT.md). The readers' own order is the ladder. Reader 1,
*People and the Sea*, opens with the voyaging past — one
large exchange community of islands with no borders, its
peoples voyaging by stars and legends — and then turns to
heritage: the sea animals of Pacific culture, Tuvalu's own
traditional fishing and bird-catching on its nine coral
islands, and, inside the same reader, the modern turn —
European whaling in the Pacific. Reader 3, *People and the
Natural Environment*, is the landward half: farming
knowledge handed down through the generations, above all
Tuvalu's pulaka pits and the coconut tree. The Teacher
Guide closes the set by sending the learner out of the book
altogether — to an elder in their own locality — and by its
Tuvalu-specific section: the closed Namo lagoon, and
Tuvaluan values kept alive through storytelling and fatele
song. The ladder stops where the books in hand stop, not
because Tuvalu teaches nothing more: Tuvalu's shelf holds
no Tuvalu national history or social-studies book at all
(see ABOUT.md's honesty notes).

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — the regional
USP/UNESCO ESD readers carried in Tuvalu's record — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Ancient World → First Steps** — the Pacific past as
   Tuvalu's readers tell it: first the voyaging and island
   exchange that made one sea join many homes; then heritage
   itself, in seven parts that build in order (the sharks,
   turtles and whales of Pacific culture; Tuvalu's own
   traditional uses of the reef and ocean; environmental
   knowledge passed down through the generations; Tuvalu's
   pulaka; the coconut tree that feeds, houses and names a
   people; the teacher guide's lesson on inheritance; and
   the teacher guide's Tuvalu section — the closed lagoon,
   the story and the song); and last the method that finds
   history at home — the elder as the textbook.
   3 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/` (1 lesson),
   then `first-steps/` (2 lessons).
2. **Modern World** — the modern turn inside Reader 1: the
   whales the islands had lived beside safely for centuries,
   using only stranded whales, meet North American whaling
   fleets in the 1890s — hunted, mothers and calves too,
   nearly to extinction in one century, then slowly
   recovering under legal protection. 1 lesson, in one
   title: `modern-world/` (1 lesson).

**Totals: 4 lessons** (Level 1: 3 — 2 lessons on student
books, 1 of them with two teacher-guide parts named as
evidence; 1 lesson on teacher-guide evidence only,
acceptance 0, labelled. Level 2: 1 lesson on a student
book — see ABOUT.md).
