# Video script — European Whaling in the Pacific

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. The Pacific lived beside the whale safely for centuries — and then, in one century, the whale was nearly gone.

Begin with the old way. When a whale stranded — when the sea itself gave one up on a beach — the islanders used it, and took its teeth for chiefs' necklaces. That was all. No fleets, no hunting at sea. The animals the Heritage lesson met as guardians and ancestors swam free, and the people took only what the sea delivered.

Then the turn. In the 1890s, North American whaling fleets reached Tonga. These were commercial hunters: whales were cargo now, hunted at sea for profit — and the fleets hunted mothers and calves too, striking the next generation along with this one.

Count the speed of it. An animal that had lived beside the islands safely for centuries was driven near extinction in one century of commercial hunting. That is the wound this story tells.

And the story does not end in the wound. Under legal protection, the whales slowly recover — the sea's great guardians coming back, generation by generation, because the hunting was stopped by law.

Remember it this way: only stranded whales for centuries; the fleets for one century; protection, and the slow return.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
