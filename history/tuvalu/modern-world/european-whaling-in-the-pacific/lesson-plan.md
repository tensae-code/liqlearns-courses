# Lesson plan — European Whaling in the Pacific

**Course:** History — Tuvalu · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** European Whaling in the Pacific
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Modern World Reaches the Pacific (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** For centuries Pacific people used only stranded whales, taking teeth for chiefs' necklaces — until North American whaling fleets reached Tonga in the 1890s and hunted the whales commercially, mothers and calves too, driving an animal the islands had lived beside safely for centuries near extinction in one century, until legal protection let it slowly recover.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The Modern World Reaches the Pacific — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the islands used only stranded whales for centuries; commercial fleets hunting mothers and calves nearly emptied the sea of whales in one century; protection let them slowly recover.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — one living thing people nearby once took gently and now must protect, or the reverse.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Pacific people hunted whales the way the fleets did → the book's own line is the opposite: the islands used only stranded whales — whales the sea itself gave up — and took teeth for chiefs' necklaces.
- Thinks the whales vanished slowly over ages → the book's turn is the speed: centuries of living safely beside the islands, then near extinction in one century of commercial hunting.
- Go back to the one thing: only stranded whales for centuries — then the fleets, mothers and calves hunted — near extinction in one century — then protection and slow recovery. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
