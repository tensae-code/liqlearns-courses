# Hands-on — European Whaling in the Pacific

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw your two seas again, as the opening act taught — the crowded old sea and the emptied hunted sea — and say the lesson's line aloud: centuries safe, one century hunted, then protection.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw the story as a line of three pictures: a stranded whale on a beach with a chief's necklace beside it; a fleet's boats closing on a mother and calf; and a mother and calf swimming free in an empty protected sea. Under each picture write one line: what the people took, and what it cost or saved. Say each line aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for the lesson's pattern in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time something living is taken gently, or taken hard, or protected by a rule, note it: what was taken, how, and what the lesson would say comes of that way of taking. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
