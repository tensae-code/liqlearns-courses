# Video script — Ancient Libya

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

A green land of elephants and giraffes dries into the Sahara; dinosaur bones at Nalut, giraffe carvings in the Acacus and pottery at Murzuq are the evidence its first peoples left. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Tehenu, Temehu, Libu, Meshwesh and Nasamones face Egypt across the desert, while the Garamantes build Garama in Fezzan, drive war chariots, run the Sahara caravans to the Three Cities and defy Rome. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Hereditary chiefs rule with tribal councils and unite under one leader against Rome; a Libyan script of thirty letters is carved in columns; the great god rides a bull carrying the sun disc, and caravans have traded into Africa since about 1520 BCE. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Libya.
