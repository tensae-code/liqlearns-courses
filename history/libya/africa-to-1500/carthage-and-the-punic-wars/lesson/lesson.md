# Carthage and the Punic Wars

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Carthage and the Punic Wars** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit (North Africa): the Phoenicians and the founding of Carthage

Tyrian settlers found Carthage on the Tunisian coast and the trading city grows into the western Mediterranean's sea power. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit (North Africa): civilisational features in North Africa

Carthaginian farmers write farming treatises, its ports and fleets run the sea trade, and Libyan, Punic and Greek cities share one coast. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Phoenicians in North Africa

Dido flees Tyre and founds Carthage in 814 BCE; its empire runs from Sirte to Spain until the Punic Wars from 264 BCE end with Carthage destroyed in 146 BCE — in Libya the Phoenicians plant Oea, Leptis and Sabratha. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 4 — Features of Carthaginian civilisation

Elected kings checked by 300 elders and a court of 104 judges; ships to Egypt and Greece, caravans to Garama, the dam on Wadi Kaam, new crops and six-storey houses. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
