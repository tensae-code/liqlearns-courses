# From Stone Tools to Farming — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** What is the heart of the story in “Part 2: a brief look at the prehistoric ages”?
a) Stone-age hunters chip tools in the Old, Middle and New Stone Ages, tame animals and learn farming, then metals replace stone and settled civilisation becomes possible.  b) Chosen wali in 1711 amid chaos, Ahmad Karamanli ends the revolts, founds Libya's navy and its Tripoli shipyard, secures the desert caravan trade and builds his great mosque — his family rules for over a century.  c) In Medina the Prophet organises a state — administration, covenants, an army — fights Badr, Uhud and the Trench, makes the Hudaybiyya truce, then enters Mecca in conquest and dies leaving Arabia united.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Stone-age hunters chip tools in the Old, Middle and New Stone Ages, tame animals and learn farming, then metals replace stone and settled civilisation becomes possible.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** What is the heart of the story in “How human communities developed before history”?
a) Old Stone Age hunters learn fire, settle by water, farm and grind grain, until pottery, copper and the first villages end the long prehistory.  b) Tyrian settlers found Carthage on the Tunisian coast and the trading city grows into the western Mediterranean's sea power.  c) Arab traders and settlers carry Islam down the East African coast and the Swahili cities grow on the monsoon trade.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Old Stone Age hunters learn fire, settle by water, farm and grind grain, until pottery, copper and the first villages end the long prehistory.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
