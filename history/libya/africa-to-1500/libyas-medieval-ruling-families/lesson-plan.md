## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 5: ruling families inside Libyan lands in the medieval era** — Inside what is now Libya, local families and dynasties govern the coast and the oases between the great empires, keeping Libyan towns alive between Fatimids, Zirids and Hafsids.
2. **The Aghlabids** — Harun al-Rashid appoints Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab over Ifriqiya in 800; his line builds al-Abbasiyya and a great fleet, takes Sicily in 827 under the judge Asad ibn al-Furat, and makes the Maghreb flourish — until the Fatimids end the state in 909.
3. **The Fatimids** — The Fatimid call rises in Ifriqiya, destroys the Aghlabids, then takes Egypt and founds Cairo, ruling the southern Mediterranean.
4. **The Fatimid state** — Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi founds the Fatimid state in North Africa in 909 and builds Mahdia; al-Mu'izz makes Tripoli's port his naval base for the Sicily jihad, Jawhar takes Egypt in 969 and founds Cairo and al-Azhar — then the Banu Hilal invasion ruins Ifriqiya and the state fades away in 1171.
