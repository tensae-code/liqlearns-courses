## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 5: ruling families inside Libyan lands in the medieval era

Inside what is now Libya, local families and dynasties govern the coast and the oases between the great empires, keeping Libyan towns alive between Fatimids, Zirids and Hafsids. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Aghlabids

Harun al-Rashid appoints Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab over Ifriqiya in 800; his line builds al-Abbasiyya and a great fleet, takes Sicily in 827 under the judge Asad ibn al-Furat, and makes the Maghreb flourish — until the Fatimids end the state in 909. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Fatimids

The Fatimid call rises in Ifriqiya, destroys the Aghlabids, then takes Egypt and founds Cairo, ruling the southern Mediterranean. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Fatimid state

Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi founds the Fatimid state in North Africa in 909 and builds Mahdia; al-Mu'izz makes Tripoli's port his naval base for the Sicily jihad, Jawhar takes Egypt in 969 and founds Cairo and al-Azhar — then the Banu Hilal invasion ruins Ifriqiya and the state fades away in 1171. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
