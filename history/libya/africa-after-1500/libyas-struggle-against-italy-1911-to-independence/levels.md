# Libya's Struggle against Italy, 1911 to Independence — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 5: the national movements in the Arab world — Libya
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: From the first battles of 1911 at Abu Meliana and al-Shatt, Libyans resist Italy for twenty years through desert war and Italian detention camps until independence comes after the second war — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why Italy invaded, and how it prepared
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Cheated of Tunisia in 1881, Italy wins Europe's consent, then creeps in peacefully — Banco di Roma branches in Tripoli and Benghazi, schools, clinics, doctors — before an ultimatum over the Edirne ship affair gives Istanbul 24 hours; war is declared on 29 September 1911 — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — The invasion and the Treaty of Ouchy
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The fleet shells Tripoli on 3 October 1911 against barely 7,000 Ottoman troops, and the garrison refuses Istanbul's surrender order; at Ouchy on 18 October 1912 the Sultan withdraws — but his firman grants Libyans self-rule, so Libyans reject the treaty and fight on: Derna's Friday battle in May 1913, Janduba, and Mahruqa in Fezzan — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — After the war: the Tripolitanian Republic
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: At Misrata on 16 November 1918 the leaders — Baruni, al-Murayyid, al-Suwayhili, Bilkhayr — proclaim the Tripolitanian Republic; the 1919 Suani Ben Yadam peace wins a Fundamental Law of rights, and at Gharyan in 1920 the leaders resolve that only unity under one elected leader can save the country — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — The jihad resumes: Graziani and Omar Mukhtar
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Fascist Italy tears up the agreements in 1922 and Graziani grinds through the Western Mountain — Khalifa ibn Askar is hanged after a show trial — while in the Green Mountain Omar Mukhtar, Senussi sheikh since 1902 and veteran of the Chad fighting, takes command in 1923 and defeats the Italians at Bir Bilal and Marsa Brega — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — The liberation of Libya
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: In Cairo in 1940 the exiles form a provisional government and a Libyan army that grows to 14,000 men; after El Alamein on 23 October 1942 Montgomery's advance frees Benghazi on 20 November and enters Tripoli on 23 January 1943 — Italian rule is over — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Military administration and the partition plan
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Britain rules Cyrenaica and Tripolitania separately and France holds Fezzan; the Bevin–Sforza plan of 1948 to split Libya among the three powers leaks, the streets explode in protest, a four-power committee hears one demand everywhere — independence — and the Arab League carries Libya's case to the United Nations — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Independence
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: On 21 November 1949 the UN votes Libya independent by 1 January 1952; the Committee of Sixty writes a constitution of 213 articles and chooses a federal monarchy under King Idris; at ten in the morning on 24 December 1951 independence is proclaimed at al-Manar Palace in Benghazi — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Italy prepares and invades Libya
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Beaten to Tunisia in 1881, Italy prepares Libya quietly — Banco di Roma branches, Italian schools, study missions — wins Britain's and France's consent, then shells Tripoli and lands on 5 October 1911 against barely 7,000 Ottoman troops, meeting a coast-wide resistance of named battles from Abu Meliana and al-Shatt to al-Hawari — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Ouchy rejected, and the victory of al-Qardabiya
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Libyans refuse the 1912 Treaty of Ouchy that abandons them to Italy; unity hardens, and at al-Qardabiya on 28 April 1915 Safi al-Din al-Senussi, Ramadan al-Suwayhili, Salih al-Atyush and Ahmad Sayf al-Nasr destroy Colonel Miani's column — Italy falls back on the defensive — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 11 — The Ajdabiya government and the Tripolitanian Republic
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Idris al-Senussi governs Cyrenaica from Ajdabiya and wins the Akrama agreement of 1917 — prisoners exchanged, Senussi zawiyas reopened, seized property restored — while the western leaders proclaim the Tripolitanian Republic on 18 November 1918, the Arab homeland's first republic, with its governing council, shura council and scholars' assembly — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 12 — The resistance resumes: Omar Mukhtar
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Italy breaks its word after the Gharyan conference pledges allegiance to Idris in 1922 and storms Misrata; in the east, after Idris's exile, Omar Mukhtar — born in 1862 at al-Butnan, a desert sheikh who had memorised the Quran — leads the renewed jihad from Bir Bilal and Brega, and the struggle runs to 1931 — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 13 — The Fascist era in Libya
Sub lesson 13 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Fascist rule wars on the Libyan personality itself — gatherings and clubs banned, education strangled, Libya cut off from the Islamic world and its history distorted, fertile land seized by force for Italian settlers — and the resistance answers by moving abroad, to committees in Damascus, Egypt and the Levant — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 14 — Liberation and independence
Sub lesson 14 questions from the type files. Pass mark: At Alexandria in October 1939 the exiles rally to Idris, and the Cairo conference of 9 August 1940 raises the Libyan Arab Forces — 14,000 men who advance with the Allies from Egypt — and after the years of military administration and the United Nations road, the independent Libyan Kingdom is proclaimed — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
