# Abbasid Caliphate: Baghdad Golden Age — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** What is the heart of the story in “Chapter 4: the rise of the Abbasid state”?
a) A secret then open call topples the Umayyads, al-Mansur builds Baghdad and becomes the state's true founder, and the caliphate turns eastward and patronises civilisation.  b) A kind climate, open land where peoples can meet and trade, enough food, and safety from attack — where these gather, villages grow into civilisations.  c) Genghis Khan unites the steppe; Hulagu burns Baghdad's libraries and kills the caliph in 1258; Sultan Qutuz executes the Mongol envoys, and at Ain Jalut in 1260 Baybars breaks the myth of Mongol invincibility.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) A secret then open call topples the Umayyads, al-Mansur builds Baghdad and becomes the state's true founder, and the caliphate turns eastward and patronises civilisation.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** What is the heart of the story in “Chapter 4: the second Abbasid era, retreat and collapse”?
a) Weak caliphs, foreign guards who seize power, an over-wide state and failing revenues break the caliphate into pieces.  b) One faith and one state unite the Arab tribes, and a new era of Muslim history begins.  c) The fleet shells Tripoli on 3 October 1911 against barely 7,000 Ottoman troops, and the garrison refuses Istanbul's surrender order; at Ouchy on 18 October 1912 the Sultan withdraws — but his firman grants Libyans self-rule, so Libyans reject the treaty and fight on: Derna's Friday battle in May 1913, Janduba, and Mahruqa in Fezzan.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Weak caliphs, foreign guards who seize power, an over-wide state and failing revenues break the caliphate into pieces.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
