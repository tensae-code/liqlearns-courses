# Umayyad Caliphate: From Damascus to Cordoba — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** What is the heart of the story in “Chapter 3: the Umayyad state in the Levant”?
a) Muawiya founds a hereditary caliphate at Damascus, builds its administration and fleets, fights Byzantium for a century, and the dynasty falls when its Arab tribal base splits.  b) Amorites, Canaanites and Aramaeans build rival kingdoms and city-states across Syria and Palestine, trading and fighting between Egypt and Mesopotamia.  c) The Fatimid call rises in Ifriqiya, destroys the Aghlabids, then takes Egypt and founds Cairo, ruling the southern Mediterranean.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Muawiya founds a hereditary caliphate at Damascus, builds its administration and fleets, fights Byzantium for a century, and the dynasty falls when its Arab tribal base splits.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** What is the heart of the story in “The Umayyad state”?
a) Muawiya turns the caliphate into a hereditary kingship at Damascus; Karbala and Ibn al-Zubayr's revolt shake it; Abd al-Malik Arabises the diwans and strikes the first Islamic dinar, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz briefly restores the Rashidun way.  b) The caliphate moves to Baghdad and the Islamic state takes its classical shape — viziers, diwans, judges and a world city of learning.  c) On 21 November 1949 the UN votes Libya independent by 1 January 1952; the Committee of Sixty writes a constitution of 213 articles and chooses a federal monarchy under King Idris; at ten in the morning on 24 December 1951 independence is proclaimed at al-Manar Palace in Benghazi.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Muawiya turns the caliphate into a hereditary kingship at Damascus; Karbala and Ibn al-Zubayr's revolt shake it; Abd al-Malik Arabises the diwans and strikes the first Islamic dinar, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz briefly restores the Rashidun way.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
