# Umayyad Caliphate: From Damascus to Cordoba

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Umayyad Caliphate: From Damascus to Cordoba** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 3: the Umayyad state in the Levant

Muawiya founds a hereditary caliphate at Damascus, builds its administration and fleets, fights Byzantium for a century, and the dynasty falls when its Arab tribal base splits. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Umayyad state

Muawiya turns the caliphate into a hereditary kingship at Damascus; Karbala and Ibn al-Zubayr's revolt shake it; Abd al-Malik Arabises the diwans and strikes the first Islamic dinar, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz briefly restores the Rashidun way. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Expansion under the Umayyads

Sind is secured under al-Walid, armies cross the Oxus toward China, frontier forts face Byzantium in summer and winter campaigns — and Constantinople holds out, to fall only in 1453. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Umayyad state and its expansion

Uqba ibn Nafi founds Kairouan in 670 and the conquest runs west to the Atlantic under Musa ibn Nusayr; Tariq ibn Ziyad crosses in 711 and gives Gibraltar his name, while eastward the armies reach Sind and the borders of China. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
