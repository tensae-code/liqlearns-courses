# Vandals and Byzantines in North Africa — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** What is the heart of the story in “The Vandals in Libya”?
a) Genseric's Vandals cross from Spain in 429, take Carthage in 439 and western Libya in 455, and tear down Rome's walls at Tripoli, Leptis and Sabratha — until Libyan tribes defeat them in the battles of 527–533.  b) The desert tribes of Najd accept kings from Yemen's choice and Hujr ibn Amr founds Kinda, the one kingdom of central Arabia before Islam.  c) Faith in knowledge drives translation, medicine, mathematics, astronomy and great cities from Baghdad to Cordoba.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Genseric's Vandals cross from Spain in 429, take Carthage in 439 and western Libya in 455, and tear down Rome's walls at Tripoli, Leptis and Sabratha — until Libyan tribes defeat them in the battles of 527–533.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** What is the heart of the story in “The Byzantines in Libya”?
a) Belisarius lands in 533 with 35,000 men and 600 ships, captures King Gelimer and restores North Africa to Justinian; forts rise along the coast manned by Libyan soldiers, but corrupt clergy and quarrelling sects hollow the empire out.  b) The Fatimids rise in Ifriqiya, conquer Egypt and found Cairo, then leave the Zirids holding the Maghreb coast as their deputies.  c) Arabia slowly dries, and from about 3500 BCE Semitic tribes wave out toward Palestine, Sinai, the Nile Delta and North Africa, mixing with the peoples they find.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Belisarius lands in 533 with 35,000 men and 600 ships, captures King Gelimer and restores North Africa to Justinian; forts rise along the coast manned by Libyan soldiers, but corrupt clergy and quarrelling sects hollow the empire out.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
