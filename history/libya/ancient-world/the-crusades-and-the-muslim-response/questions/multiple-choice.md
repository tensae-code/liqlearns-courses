# The Crusades and the Muslim Response — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** What is the heart of the story in “Chapter 4: the Islamic world's jihad against the Crusaders”?
a) Crusader states seize the Levant coast, then Zengi, Nur al-Din and Saladin reunite the Muslims and retake Jerusalem.  b) Italy breaks its word after the Gharyan conference pledges allegiance to Idris in 1922 and storms Misrata; in the east, after Idris's exile, Omar Mukhtar — born in 1862 at al-Butnan, a desert sheikh who had memorised the Quran — leads the renewed jihad from Bir Bilal and Brega, and the struggle runs to 1931.  c) The past is cut into ages and eras and dated by centuries and by before/after turning points, so a Libyan, an Egyptian and a Greek event can be set on one timeline.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Crusader states seize the Levant coast, then Zengi, Nur al-Din and Saladin reunite the Muslims and retake Jerusalem.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** What is the heart of the story in “The Ayyubids”?
a) Saladin founds the Ayyubid state, unites Egypt and the Levant, and turns the Muslim world toward recovering Jerusalem.  b) Reforming walis govern the province — Ali Rida Pasha applies modern laws, trims the foreigners' privileges and builds Tripoli's clock tower; Ahmad Rasim Pasha, the longest in office, founds the strangers' hospital at Bab al-Bahr.  c) In Medina the Prophet organises a state — administration, covenants, an army — fights Badr, Uhud and the Trench, makes the Hudaybiyya truce, then enters Mecca in conquest and dies leaving Arabia united.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Saladin founds the Ayyubid state, unites Egypt and the Levant, and turns the Muslim world toward recovering Jerusalem.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
