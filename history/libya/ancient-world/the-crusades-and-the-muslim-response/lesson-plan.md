## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 4: the Islamic world's jihad against the Crusaders** — Crusader states seize the Levant coast, then Zengi, Nur al-Din and Saladin reunite the Muslims and retake Jerusalem.
2. **The Ayyubids** — Saladin founds the Ayyubid state, unites Egypt and the Levant, and turns the Muslim world toward recovering Jerusalem.
3. **The Crusader invasion** — Western armies carve Crusader states from the Levant's coast while the Muslim world is divided.
4. **Saladin and the power of unity** — Saladin's victory shows the book's moral — divided, the Muslims fell; united under one leadership, they defeat the West.
5. **The Ayyubid state** — As Fatimid viziers quarrel and call in the Crusaders, Nur al-Din sends Shirkuh and his nephew Saladin to Egypt; Saladin ends the Fatimid state in 1171 and unites Egypt with the Levant.
6. **The Crusades against the Arab homeland** — Europe's kings march for Jerusalem and the trade roads while Baghdad is weak and Egypt decays; Crusader kingdoms plant themselves at Jerusalem and Tripoli — until Saladin unites the Muslims and crushes them at Hattin in 1187, freeing Jerusalem.
7. **The Mamluks expel the Crusaders** — Baybars rebuilds the army and the fleet and takes Jaffa and Antioch; the pressure never lets up until the last Crusaders are expelled in 1291 — a danger that had lasted since 1097.
