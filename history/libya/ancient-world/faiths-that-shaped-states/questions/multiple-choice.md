# Faiths That Shaped States — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** What is the heart of the story in “Chapter 1: features of Islamic life in the Prophet's era”?
a) The new faith rebuilds politics, society, the economy, learning, building and war around one community, and the chapter ends by drawing lessons from the Prophet's life.  b) A green land of elephants and giraffes dries into the Sahara; dinosaur bones at Nalut, giraffe carvings in the Acacus and pottery at Murzuq are the evidence its first peoples left.  c) Cedar ships steered by the pole star carry purple dye and glass to Cadiz and Carthage, and the 22-letter alphabet they invent travels via Greece and Rome into Europe's letters.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) The new faith rebuilds politics, society, the economy, learning, building and war around one community, and the chapter ends by drawing lessons from the Prophet's life.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** What is the heart of the story in “The effect of the call”?
a) One faith and one state unite the Arab tribes, and a new era of Muslim history begins.  b) After the Almohads fall in 1269 the Maghreb fragments and Spain strikes the coast: 120 ships bombard Tripoli in July 1510, two thousand are killed and the mosques looted — an occupation that lasts until the Ottomans come in 1551.  c) The caliphate moves to Baghdad and the Islamic state takes its classical shape — viziers, diwans, judges and a world city of learning.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) One faith and one state unite the Arab tribes, and a new era of Muslim history begins.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
