# Mongol Invasion and the Mamluk Response — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) ** What is the heart of the story in “Chapter 4: the Mongol invasion and the Islamic response”?
a) Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow.  b) Uqba ibn Nafi founds Kairouan in 670 and the conquest runs west to the Atlantic under Musa ibn Nusayr; Tariq ibn Ziyad crosses in 711 and gives Gibraltar his name, while eastward the armies reach Sind and the borders of China.  c) Beaten to Tunisia in 1881, Italy prepares Libya quietly — Banco di Roma branches, Italian schools, study missions — wins Britain's and France's consent, then shells Tripoli and lands on 5 October 1911 against barely 7,000 Ottoman troops, meeting a coast-wide resistance of named battles from Abu Meliana and al-Shatt to al-Hawari.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) ** What is the heart of the story in “Chapter 6: Mongol rule in Iraq”?
a) After Baghdad's fall, Mongol Ilkhans rule Iraq while the rest of the Arab world lives under other masters until the Ottomans come.  b) England's revolution, France's 1789 revolution and Italy's unification teach Europe's peoples that nations should rule themselves — an idea that will travel to the Arab world.  c) Libya becomes an Ottoman province in 1551; walis such as the Saqizlis raise mosques, schools and fortresses — though the era's later years slide into corruption and disorder as the empire weakens after Suleiman.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) After Baghdad's fall, Mongol Ilkhans rule Iraq while the rest of the Arab world lives under other masters until the Ottomans come.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
