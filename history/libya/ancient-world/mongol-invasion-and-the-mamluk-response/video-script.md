# Video script — Mongol Invasion and the Mamluk Response

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Mongol armies sweep the east and sack Baghdad, until the Mamluks of Egypt stop them and the Islamic world survives its darkest blow. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

After Baghdad's fall, Mongol Ilkhans rule Iraq while the rest of the Arab world lives under other masters until the Ottomans come. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Genghis Khan unites the steppe; Hulagu burns Baghdad's libraries and kills the caliph in 1258; Sultan Qutuz executes the Mongol envoys, and at Ain Jalut in 1260 Baybars breaks the myth of Mongol invincibility. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

The Mongols sack Baghdad in 1258 and march on the Levant and Egypt; the Mamluks destroy their army at Ain Jalut in 1260 and clear them from the Levant. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Libya.
