# Video script — The Rise and Expansion of Islam

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Muhammad preaches first in secret then openly in Mecca, Quraysh persecute the believers, and the weakest find refuge with the just king of Abyssinia before the migration to Yathrib turns a persecuted band into a community. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

In Medina the Prophet organises a state — administration, covenants, an army — fights Badr, Uhud and the Trench, makes the Hudaybiyya truce, then enters Mecca in conquest and dies leaving Arabia united. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Abu Bakr holds Arabia together, Umar organises the conquering state, Uthman standardises the Qur'an, and Ali's caliphate ends in the first great split. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Driven by faith and pressure at home, Muslim armies defeat both Persia and Byzantium, and the book asks why the two great empires fell so fast. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Umayyad armies carry Islam to the Atlantic in the west and to Sindh and Transoxiana in the east, the widest reach of the early conquests. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Libya.
