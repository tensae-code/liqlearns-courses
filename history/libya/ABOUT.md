# About the History — Libya course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Libya's own Centre for Educational Curricula and Educational Research books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Libya inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the story as Libya's own books present it (see CURRICULUM.md for the derivation): Level 1 — first steps and the ancient world (30 lessons); Level 2 — arabia before islam and the rise of islam (7 lessons); Level 3 — the caliphates and islamic civilisation (7 lessons); Level 4 — the maghreb, al-andalus and islam in africa (10 lessons); Level 5 — ottoman libya and the karamanlis (5 lessons); Level 6 — europe transformed, the world wars and libya's struggle to independence (17 lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The arc whole:* "First, what history itself is — how the past is dated and what makes a civilisation — then the ancient Arab world whole: Mesopotamia, the Levant, Egypt, ancient Libya with the Garamantes and Cyrenaica, Carthage, and Arabia south, centre and north, closing with what the ancient Arab world gave civilisation. Then Arabia before Islam and the rise of Islam: the call in Mecca, the state in Medina, the Rashidun expansion. Then the caliphates — Umayyad, Abbasid — how they were run, the civilisation they built, and the Crusades and Mongols answered. Then the Maghreb and al-Andalus: Fatimids, Libya's own ruling families, Almoravids and Almohads, Granada's fall, and Islam's road south of the Sahara. Then Ottoman Libya and the Karamanli state, its treaties and its war with America. And last Europe transformed — Renaissance, revolutions, industry — the world wars, and Libya's own twenty-year struggle against Italy, from 1911 to independence on 24 December 1951, with Palestine's open wound closing the course."
