# Hands-on — The Bangladesh Liberation War 1971 in Our Locality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask at home, gently and with permission, what your own locality remembers of 1971 — a place, a name, a story. Write down only what you are freely told. This lesson is about why that remembering matters.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Liberation War of 1971 is first met not as a date but as a place: the killing field in the children's own village, where 46 local men and women were killed, and the story of Azad, captured by the Pakistani forces, whose mother never stopped waiting — one martyr standing for thousands unknown. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
