# The Bangladesh Liberation War 1971 in Our Locality

## Do it first

Ask at home, gently and with permission, what your own locality remembers of 1971 — a place, a name, a story. Write down only what you are freely told. This lesson is about why that remembering matters.

The class's inquiry into its own locality reaches the village's 1971 killing field — the ground where 46 local men and women were killed in the Liberation War. There the pupils hear the story of Azad, a young man captured by the Pakistani forces, whose mother never stopped waiting for him. Azad's story is one among thousands of unknown martyrs; told carefully, one name can stand for all the names a village lost.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Liberation War of 1971 is first met not as a date but as a place: the killing field in the children's own village, where 46 local men and women were killed, and the story of Azad, captured by the Pakistani forces, whose mother never stopped waiting — one martyr standing for thousands unknown. Give one real example from your own life — a memorial, a graveyard corner or an elder's story in your own locality — the war's history stands in places like these — and name the lesson's key words: The Bangladesh Liberation War 1971 in Our Locality, Muktijuddha in Our Locality, killing field, Azad. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
