# Hands-on — Nationalism: The Feeling That Made Nation-States

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down what makes your country one country in your own words: a language, a flag, a shared story? You have just touched the feeling this lesson studies — the feeling that built the modern map.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Nation-states rise in England, Spain, France, Russia and Prussia; the French Revolution of 1789 defines the idea through Renan — and nationalism then multiplies independent states across the world, Bengal's own included. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
