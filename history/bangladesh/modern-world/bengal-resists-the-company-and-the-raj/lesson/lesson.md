# Bengal Resists the Company and the Raj

## Do it first

Write two columns: 'fought with arms' and 'fought with ideas'. Before reading, put any resistance you already know of into a column. Bengal used both columns for two centuries — this lesson fills them in.

British rule in Bengal is resisted from its first century to its last. Fakir Majnu Shah's bands harry the Company; indigo ryots rise against the planters who force the blue dye on their fields; Titumir raises his standard in 1831; and in 1857 the sepoys turn their muskets on the Raj. Beside the fighters stand the thinkers — Rammohan Roy, Vidyasagar and Derozio fighting with ideas, reforming and arguing Bengal awake. Arms and ideas together press on, until in 1947 the British are forced to leave.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Bengal resists British rule with arms and with ideas: Fakir Majnu Shah's bands, the indigo ryots, Titumir's 1831 rising and the sepoys of 1857 fight the Company and the Raj, while Rammohan Roy, Vidyasagar and Derozio fight with ideas — until the British are forced to leave in 1947. Give one real example from your own life — a school argument won by a well-made point — ideas as weapons is half of Bengal's resistance story — and name the lesson's key words: Bengal Resists the Company and the Raj, Fakir Majnu Shah, indigo ryots, Titumir 1831. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
