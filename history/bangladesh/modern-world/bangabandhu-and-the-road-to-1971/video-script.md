# Video script — Bangabandhu and the Road to 1971

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Sheikh Mujibur Rahman — Bangabandhu — fights the Urdu-only policy from 1948, is expelled and jailed, hunger-strikes in prison in February 1952, then leads the 6-Point movement of 1966, the mass uprising of 1969 and the 1970 election victory, declaring on 7 March that 'the struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation' — a road rooted in Bengal's thousand-year humanitarian and secular spirit, opening when Dhaka's students first defied Pakistan's language policy.

Mujib knew Bengal from its waterways, travelling them to reach its people — and the people made him Bangabandhu, Friend of Bengal.

Onto that road steps a young man from Tungipara, born in 1920.

What drove him? Class Nine answers with the man himself: a love for all people regardless of language, religion or caste, and a rule of nation before self.

The road opens in insult.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
