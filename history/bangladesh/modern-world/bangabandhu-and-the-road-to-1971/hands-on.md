# Hands-on — Bangabandhu and the Road to 1971

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the years 1948, 1952, 1966, 1969, 1970 and 1971 in a row. This lesson fills each one with a step on a single road. Before you read on, write beside each year what you think might have happened — then check yourself against the story.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Sheikh Mujibur Rahman — Bangabandhu — fights the Urdu-only policy from 1948, is expelled and jailed, hunger-strikes in prison in February 1952, then leads the 6-Point movement of 1966, the mass uprising of 1969 and the 1970 election victory, declaring on 7 March that 'the struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation' — a road rooted in Bengal's thousand-year humanitarian and secular spirit, opening when Dhaka's students first defied Pakistan's language policy. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
