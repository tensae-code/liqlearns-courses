# Hands-on — The Emergence of Bangladesh 1971

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw one long road on a page. Along it, mark the stops you already know of Bengal's past — old kingdoms, foreign rulers, 1947, 1971. This lesson walks that whole road in order, so keep your drawing: you will add to it all through this course.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bangladesh emerges at the end of a journey of millennia — from the ancient janapadas through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British extraction, and the 1947 partition that made East Pakistan a colony of the West — until Bangabandhu's movement wins independence in 1971, the war's spirit is carried into rebuilding, and the land's name grows from Banga to Bangladesh. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
