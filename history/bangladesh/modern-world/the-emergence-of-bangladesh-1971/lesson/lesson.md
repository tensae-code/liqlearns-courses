# The Emergence of Bangladesh 1971

## Do it first

Draw one long road on a page. Along it, mark the stops you already know of Bengal's past — old kingdoms, foreign rulers, 1947, 1971. This lesson walks that whole road in order, so keep your drawing: you will add to it all through this course.

## Sub lesson 1 — The journey of millennia

Class Six closes with the whole road in one view. From the ancient janapadas, Bengal passes under Pala and Sena kings, Muslim sultans and Mughal emperors; then comes British extraction and the indigo fields, and in 1947 a partition that makes the east a colony of the west inside Pakistan. Against that long subjection rises Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's movement — and in 1971 the journey of millennia arrives: independent Bangladesh.

## Sub lesson 2 — The long arc

Class Seven walks the same road asking what the land itself was called. The Banga janapada grows through Bangal and Bangala into Bengal; Deva, Chandra, Pala and Sena dynasties rule it, Mauryas and Guptas reach into it, Sultanate and Mughal empires hold it; then come Portuguese, Dutch, French and English, and finally, from 1947, Pakistani exploitation. Every age leaves its layer — and the arc still ends in 1971.

## Sub lesson 3 — The spirit, the homecoming, the rebuilding

The war won, its spirit had to be carried home. Freed from a Pakistani prison by international pressure, Bangabandhu lands in independent Bangladesh on 10 January 1972. The spirit he brings is the war's own: freedom of language, culture and destiny, and an end to exploitation. It turns practical at once — students farming, building, and teaching literacy across the country, rebuilding with their own hands.

## Sub lesson 4 — From Banga to Bangladesh, from Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu

Class Nine binds the land's name to its leader's. The name grows with the people's story — from Banga to Bangladesh — while the people's friend, Bangabandhu, becomes a symbol of oppressed peoples everywhere. The world answers in kind: on 23 May 1973 he receives the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace, honoured as Bishwabandhu, Friend of the World.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Bangladesh emerges at the end of a journey of millennia — from the ancient janapadas through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British extraction, and the 1947 partition that made East Pakistan a colony of the West — until Bangabandhu's movement wins independence in 1971, the war's spirit is carried into rebuilding, and the land's name grows from Banga to Bangladesh. Give one real example from your own life — your country's flag flying over your school — this lesson tells the thousand-year road that flag stands at the end of — and name the lesson's key words: The Emergence of Bangladesh 1971, janapadas, Pala, Sena. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
