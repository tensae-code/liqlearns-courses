# Lesson plan — The Emergence of Bangladesh 1971

**Course:** History — Bangladesh · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Emergence of Bangladesh 1971
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bangladesh emerges at the end of a journey of millennia — from the ancient janapadas through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British extraction, and the 1947 partition that made East Pakistan a colony of the West — until Bangabandhu's movement wins independence in 1971, the war's spirit is carried into rebuilding, and the land's name grows from Banga to Bangladesh.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The journey of millennia** — From the ancient janapadas through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British extraction and the 1947 partition, to Bangabandhu's movement and independence in 1971.
2. **The long arc** — Banga janapada becomes Bangal, Bangala, Bengal under Deva, Chandra, Pala and Sena dynasties, Mauryas, Guptas, Sultanate and Mughals, then Europeans, and finally Pakistani exploitation from 1947 — all ending in 1971.
3. **The spirit, the homecoming, the rebuilding** — Bangabandhu lands in independent Bangladesh on 10 January 1972 and turns the war's spirit into students farming, building and teaching literacy.
4. **From Banga to Bangladesh, from Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu** — The land's name grows from Banga to Bangladesh as Bangabandhu becomes a symbol of all oppressed peoples and receives the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace on 23 May 1973.
