# Hands-on — Foreign Friends of the Liberation War

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List the ways news of a faraway event reaches you today. In 1971 the list was shorter and more dangerous. This lesson names the people who carried one country's war to the world — and what it cost and what it won.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bangladesh's Liberation War was carried to the world by foreign friends: Simon Dring's cables from a Dhaka hotel, the BBC and Akashvani, India's shelter for about one crore refugees under Indira Gandhi, Soviet diplomatic backing, Senator Edward Kennedy's camp visits, and George Harrison singing for Bangladesh. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
