# Foreign Friends of the Liberation War

## Do it first

List the ways news of a faraway event reaches you today. In 1971 the list was shorter and more dangerous. This lesson names the people who carried one country's war to the world — and what it cost and what it won.

## Sub lesson 1 — The journalists

When the Pakistan army struck on 25 March 1971, foreign reporters were expelled from Dhaka. One did not leave: 26-year-old Simon Dring hid in the Hotel Intercontinental, then slipped out to see the city and cabled the massacre to The Daily Telegraph. The BBC and Akashvani carried the war to the world from outside. Because journalists stayed, hid and broadcast, the killing could not be kept a secret.

## Sub lesson 2 — Refugees, diplomacy and song

The war drove about one crore refugees into India, which sheltered, fed and spoke for them under Indira Gandhi. The Soviet Union backed Bangladesh diplomatically; US Senator Edward Kennedy walked the refugee camps and carried their story home. And in New York, George Harrison sang for Bangladesh before the world. Shelter, diplomacy and song — three kinds of friendship, all of them counted in 1971.

Give one real example from your own life — a news report about a faraway crisis on a phone at home — somebody, somewhere, chose to carry that story; this lesson honours the carriers of 1971 — and name the lesson's key words: Foreign Friends of the Liberation War, Simon Dring, Daily Telegraph, BBC. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
