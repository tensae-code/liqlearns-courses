# Lesson plan — Foreign Friends of the Liberation War

**Course:** History — Bangladesh · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Foreign Friends of the Liberation War
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — History Through Work and Trade: Bengal's Economy and the Road to 1971 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bangladesh's Liberation War was carried to the world by foreign friends: Simon Dring's cables from a Dhaka hotel, the BBC and Akashvani, India's shelter for about one crore refugees under Indira Gandhi, Soviet diplomatic backing, Senator Edward Kennedy's camp visits, and George Harrison singing for Bangladesh.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — History Through Work and Trade: Bengal's Economy and the Road to 1971 (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The journalists** — 26-year-old Simon Dring hides in Dhaka's Hotel Intercontinental after foreign reporters are expelled on 25 March 1971 and cables the massacre to The Daily Telegraph.
2. **Refugees, diplomacy and song** — About one crore refugees flood into India, which shelters and speaks for them under Indira Gandhi; the Soviet Union backs Bangladesh, Edward Kennedy visits the camps, and George Harrison sings for Bangladesh.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Bangladesh's Liberation War was carried to the world by foreign friends: Simon Dring's cables from a Dhaka hotel, the BBC and Akashvani, India's shelter for about one crore refugees under Indira Gandhi, Soviet diplomatic backing, Senator Edward Kennedy's camp visits, and George Harrison singing for Bangladesh.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a news report about a faraway crisis on a phone at home — somebody, somewhere, chose to carry that story; this lesson honours the carriers of 1971.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Bangladesh fought entirely alone and unheard → journalists risked themselves to cable the massacre out, India sheltered a crore of refugees, and a Beatle sang the country's name to the world.
- Go back to the one thing: Bangladesh's Liberation War was carried to the world by foreign friends: Simon Dring's cables from a Dhaka hotel, the BBC and Akashvani, India's shelter for about one crore refugees under Indira Gandhi, Soviet diplomatic backing, Senator Edward Kennedy's camp visits, and George Harrison singing for Bangladesh.
