# Hands-on — British Rule in Bengal: Two Verdicts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Read two sentences about the same school day — one from a teacher's report, one from a pupil's diary. They will not match. This lesson puts two verdicts on one rule side by side and teaches you to weigh them, not just pick the louder one.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: British rule in Bengal carries two verdicts side by side: a 1911 British weekly boasts of the peace and security given to India, while economist Angus Maddison counts India's share of world wealth collapsing to about 3 percent by the time the British left. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
