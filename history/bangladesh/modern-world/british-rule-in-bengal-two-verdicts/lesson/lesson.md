# British Rule in Bengal: Two Verdicts

## Do it first

Read two sentences about the same school day — one from a teacher's report, one from a pupil's diary. They will not match. This lesson puts two verdicts on one rule side by side and teaches you to weigh them, not just pick the louder one.

The Class Eight book ends its Bengal survey by putting two verdicts on British rule face to face. In 1911 a British weekly boasts of the peace and security Britain has given India — the ruler's verdict on itself. Against it stands the economist Angus Maddison's arithmetic: India's share of the world's wealth collapsed under British rule to about 3 percent by the time the British left. Boast and count cannot both be the whole story; the lesson leaves the weighing to the learner.
