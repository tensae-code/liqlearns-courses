# Hands-on — Bengal Sultanate, Mughals and the Baro Bhuiyans

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Count the rivers you would have to cross to walk across Bengal. An empire ruling from dry land had to cross every one of them, against men who knew every channel. This lesson is about the kings who made Bengal independent — and the river lords who kept it stubborn.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah founds an independent Bengal Sultanate — 'Shah-i Bangalah' — and centuries later, though Akbar's armies kill Daud Karrani in 1576, the Baro Bhuiyan zamindars under Isa Khan keep resisting Mughal subadars river by river. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
