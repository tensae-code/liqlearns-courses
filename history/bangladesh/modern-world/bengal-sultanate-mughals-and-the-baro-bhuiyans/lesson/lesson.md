# Bengal Sultanate, Mughals and the Baro Bhuiyans

## Do it first

Count the rivers you would have to cross to walk across Bengal. An empire ruling from dry land had to cross every one of them, against men who knew every channel. This lesson is about the kings who made Bengal independent — and the river lords who kept it stubborn.

Bengal becomes a sultanate of its own when Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah founds an independent Bengal Sultanate and takes the title 'Shah-i Bangalah' — King of Bengal. Centuries later the Mughal empire reaches for the prize: Akbar's armies kill the last sultan, Daud Karrani, in 1576. But Bengal does not simply submit. The Baro Bhuiyan zamindars, the twelve river lords under Isa Khan, keep resisting the Mughal subadars, fighting river by river through the delta they know and the empire does not.
