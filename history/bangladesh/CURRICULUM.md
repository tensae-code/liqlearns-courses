# History — Bangladesh course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Bangladesh inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Bangladesh's books in hand are four
"History and Social Science" student books of the National
Curriculum 2022 (each in English and Bangla parallel editions,
placed once), and each book reads the whole past through its own
lens — that lens is the ladder. The first book teaches history
by **inquiry**: Khushi Apa's class investigates its own locality,
interviews elders under strict consent rules, and only then
widens out to Bengal, South Asia and the world (Level 1). The
second book re-reads the past through **economics** — how people
earned, ate, traded and were exploited (Level 2). The third
turns to **social and cultural history** — language, faith,
food, festivals and art (Level 3). The fourth is the
**political** year — Bengal's path from janapada to republic set
beside the world's political forms (Level 4). A lesson sits at
the level of the book where it first appears; later books'
parts stocked into the same lesson deepen it there. The ladder
is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB) books —
except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book contents
map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps / Ancient World / Africa to 1500 / Modern
   World → History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the
   Deep Past** — what history is, how evidence becomes a
   written history, dating the past, and finding history in
   your own locality under the elders-interview rules; who
   studies prehistory; Bengal the river land and its old names,
   its first city at Mahasthangarh; stone tools to farming and
   the first cities; the peoples of South Asia; and the
   Liberation War met first as a killing field in the
   children's own village, closing with the emergence of
   Bangladesh at the end of a journey of millennia.
   12 lessons, in four titles: `first-steps/` (5 lessons),
   `ancient-world/` (4 lessons), `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson),
   `modern-world/` (2 lessons).

2. **Ancient World / Modern World → History Through Work and
   Trade: Bengal's Economy and the Road to 1971** — Bengal's
   famed textiles, its boatbuilding and its ports and coins in
   a trade reaching the Mediterranean, Arabia, Africa and
   Southeast Asia; then Bangabandhu and the road to 1971, and
   the foreign friends who carried the Liberation War to the
   world. 3 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/` (1 lesson),
   `modern-world/` (2 lessons).

3. **First Steps / Ancient World / Africa to 1500 / Modern
   World → History Through Society and Culture: How People
   Lived** — historiography (deciphering scripts and writing
   cultural history); Bengal's festivals, faith, food and
   building diversifying century by century; the two verdicts
   on British rule; and Egypt, Mesopotamia and Greece read
   through society and culture rather than kings and battles.
   6 lessons, in four titles: `first-steps/` (1 lesson),
   `ancient-world/` (3 lessons), `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson),
   `modern-world/` (1 lesson).

4. **Ancient World / Modern World → History Through Power and
   Politics: Rulers, Resistance and the Nation-State** —
   Sparta against Athens and the fall of the city-states;
   nationalism and the nation-state; Bengal's political path —
   Mauryas and Guptas, Shashanka and the Palas, the independent
   Sultanate, the Mughals and the Baro Bhuiyans, and two
   centuries of resistance to the Company and the Raj.
   5 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/` (2 lessons),
   `modern-world/` (3 lessons).

**Totals: 26 lessons** (Level 1: 12, Level 2: 3, Level 3: 6,
Level 4: 5) — 46 placements stocked, every one on a student
book.
