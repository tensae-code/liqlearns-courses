# Hands-on — Bengal: A River Land and Its Old Names

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw the land you live in from memory: its rivers, its low and high ground, the sea if it is near. Then write the oldest name you know for your own locality. Bengal's story starts exactly there — a land and its names.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bengal is a river land ringed by the Himalayas, hills and the Bay of Bengal, known across time as Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela; its people settled beside the great rivers, farmed the flood-fed soil, lived with flood, storm and tiger — and grew from Austric, Dravidian and Arya peoples mixing into one diverse society. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
