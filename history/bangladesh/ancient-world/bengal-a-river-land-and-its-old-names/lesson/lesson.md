# Bengal: A River Land and Its Old Names

## Do it first

Draw the land you live in from memory: its rivers, its low and high ground, the sea if it is near. Then write the oldest name you know for your own locality. Bengal's story starts exactly there — a land and its names.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bengal as a region

Look at the land first, the book says, before any king. Bengal is a river land ringed by the Himalayas, by hills, and by the Bay of Bengal. Across the centuries it was known by many names — Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela — a family of old lands inside one natural region. Rulers drew and redrew state borders across it, but those shifting borders never matched the land's own natural ones.

## Sub lesson 2 — Landforms shape settlement

The land then shapes the people. Settlers clear the forest and plant themselves beside the great rivers — the Ganges, the Padma, the Brahmaputra and the Meghna — farming soil made rich by flood-water. But the rivers' gifts have a price, paid every year: flood, storm, and the tiger in the forest edge. To live in Bengal has always meant taking the gift and the danger together.

## Sub lesson 3 — The peoples who made Bengal's culture

Who, then, are Bengal's people? The Class Eight book answers with a procession. Austric-speaking peoples came, then Dravidian, then the Arya language peoples — each entering Bengal in turn, each mixing with those already there. Out of that long mixing came the diverse foundation of Bengali society: its language, its customs, its way of life, built by many peoples becoming one people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Bengal is a river land ringed by the Himalayas, hills and the Bay of Bengal, known across time as Banga, Pundra, Gauda, Radha, Samatata and Harikela; its people settled beside the great rivers, farmed the flood-fed soil, lived with flood, storm and tiger — and grew from Austric, Dravidian and Arya peoples mixing into one diverse society. Give one real example from your own life — the river nearest your home — it has watered farms, carried boats and flooded fields for everyone who ever lived beside it — and name the lesson's key words: Bengal: A River Land and Its Old Names, Banga, Pundra, Gauda. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
