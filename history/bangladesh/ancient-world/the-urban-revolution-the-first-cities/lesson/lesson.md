# The Urban Revolution: The First Cities

## Do it first

Name the biggest settlement near your home. Write three things it has that a village does not: perhaps writing on signs, a market that never moves, people whose only job is making or ruling. Those are the marks of Childe's urban revolution.

Childe's second revolution is the Urban Revolution: farming's surplus raises something the world had never seen — the city, with writing, science and art inside its walls. The chapter tours the worlds the rivers and seas made possible: the Indus cities, Egypt on the Nile, Mesopotamia between its two rivers, the cities of China, and the Greek city world by the sea. Where water carried food and trade, cities rose.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Gordon Childe's second revolution raises the first cities — with writing, science and art — and the river-valley civilisations of the Indus, Egypt, Mesopotamia and China, and the Greek city world, show what rivers and seas made possible. Give one real example from your own life — the nearest town's market and signboards — writing, trade and special jobs in one place are the city's ancient signature — and name the lesson's key words: The Urban Revolution: The First Cities, Urban Revolution, Gordon Childe, river-valley civilisations. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
