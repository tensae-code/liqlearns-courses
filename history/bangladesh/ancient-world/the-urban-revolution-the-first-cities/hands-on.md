# Hands-on — The Urban Revolution: The First Cities

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name the biggest settlement near your home. Write three things it has that a village does not: perhaps writing on signs, a market that never moves, people whose only job is making or ruling. Those are the marks of Childe's urban revolution.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Gordon Childe's second revolution raises the first cities — with writing, science and art — and the river-valley civilisations of the Indus, Egypt, Mesopotamia and China, and the Greek city world, show what rivers and seas made possible. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
