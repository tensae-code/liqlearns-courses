# Bengal's Textiles, Boats and Ports

## Do it first

Look at the cloth you are wearing and at the nearest boat, rickshaw or vehicle you can see. Both descend from Bengal's two old industries. Write one sentence on what fine cloth and good boats together let a river people do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Industry: textiles and boats

Bengal worked as beautifully as it farmed. Its fine textiles were famous far beyond its rivers — the 9th-century Arab merchant Sulaiman praised them, and Europeans were still coveting them into the 1600s. And a river people needs boats: Bengal's boatbuilding industry, forged by its waterways, shaped the vessels that carried its goods and its people. Cloth and boats together made Bengal a maker, not only a grower.

## Sub lesson 2 — Trade, commerce and money

Making led to trading, and trading grew its tools. Bengal moved from barter to money — punch-marked coins, then the coins of Gupta, Gauda and Harikela rulers. Through the ports of Tamralipti, Chandraketugarh and Samandar, its goods sailed to the Mediterranean, Arabia, Africa and Southeast Asia. Two thousand years ago, this river land was already woven into the trade of half the world.
