# Hands-on — Bengal's Textiles, Boats and Ports

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at the cloth you are wearing and at the nearest boat, rickshaw or vehicle you can see. Both descend from Bengal's two old industries. Write one sentence on what fine cloth and good boats together let a river people do.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bengal's famed fine textiles — praised by the 9th-century Arab merchant Sulaiman and coveted by Europeans into the 1600s — and its river-forged boatbuilding clothed and carried a trading people who moved from barter to punch-marked coins and traded through Tamralipti, Chandraketugarh and Samandar with the Mediterranean, Arabia, Africa and Southeast Asia 2,000 years ago. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
