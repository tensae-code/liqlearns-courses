# Ancient Civilisations: India

## Do it first

List the languages spoken in your own locality — at home, at school, in the market. Bengal's larger neighbour learned long ago what your list shows: many peoples can make one shared history.

South Asia was never peopled once. Over the ages, peoples of many languages and cultures entered the subcontinent — by its passes, its rivers and its coasts. They struggled with one another, mixed with one another, and settled, until their separate stories braided into one shared, diverse history. That braiding, the book teaches, is the real beginning of the region Bengal belongs to.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Peoples of many languages and cultures entered South Asia over the ages, and through struggle, mixing and settling they made one shared, diverse history — the subcontinent's past belongs to all of them together. Give one real example from your own life — the many languages heard in one market day — difference living inside one shared place is South Asia's oldest pattern — and name the lesson's key words: Ancient Civilisations: India, peoples of South Asia, South Asia, diverse history. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
