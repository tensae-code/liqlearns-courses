# Hands-on — Ancient Bengal: Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find the oldest human-made thing in your locality — a mound, a pond, an old wall, a ruin. Stand at it and write what it proves: that people lived, worked and organised themselves here long before you.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bengal's first settlements grow into its first city at Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh (Pundranagar) on the Karatoya, where Bengal's oldest Brahmi stone inscription (c. 300 BCE), granary rice 3,500 years old, crop lists and copper-plate land grants prove a rich farming land. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
