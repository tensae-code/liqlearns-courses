# Ancient Bengal: Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh

## Do it first

Find the oldest human-made thing in your locality — a mound, a pond, an old wall, a ruin. Stand at it and write what it proves: that people lived, worked and organised themselves here long before you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pandu Rajar Dhibi and Mahasthangarh

At Pandu Rajar Dhibi, the diggers found a mound of the copper-and-stone age — a settlement from Bengal's deep past. And on the Karatoya river stands Mahasthangarh, the walled city the ancients called Pundranagar, holding Bengal's oldest Brahmi stone inscription, from about 300 BCE. Together the two sites tell one story: villages in Bengal were already growing into Bengal's first city three thousand years ago.

## Sub lesson 2 — The agricultural economy

The Class Seven book returns to the same ground and asks what its people ate and grew. The evidence answers richly: rice 3,500 years old, kept from a granary near Pandu Rajar Dhibi; the Mahasthan Brahmi inscription's lists of crops; and copper-plate land grants naming mango, betel, coconut and cotton. Long before any modern state, Bengal was already a rich farming land, and its fields were being granted, taxed and recorded.
