# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Sparta and Athens Against Sparta

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine two schools on one street: one trains only soldiers, the other lets its pupils vote on its rules. Write one strength and one weakness of each. Sparta and Athens are those two schools grown into cities — and this lesson tells what happened when they fought.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Sparta tests its newborns and raises seven-year-olds as soldiers; Athens invents democracy; their thirty-year Peloponnesian War exhausts both — and Philip of Macedon's rising power swallows the Greek world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
