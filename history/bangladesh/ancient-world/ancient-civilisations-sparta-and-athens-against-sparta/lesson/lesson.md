# Ancient Civilisations: Sparta and Athens Against Sparta

## Do it first

Imagine two schools on one street: one trains only soldiers, the other lets its pupils vote on its rules. Write one strength and one weakness of each. Sparta and Athens are those two schools grown into cities — and this lesson tells what happened when they fought.

Two cities, two answers to how people should be ruled. Sparta tests its newborns for strength and takes its boys at seven to raise as soldiers — the whole state a barracks. Athens, across the hills, invents democracy, rule by the citizens' vote. When the two collide in the thirty-year Peloponnesian War, both exhaust themselves — and the Greek world they weakened is swallowed by the rising power of Philip of Macedon.

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Sparta tests its newborns and raises seven-year-olds as soldiers; Athens invents democracy; their thirty-year Peloponnesian War exhausts both — and Philip of Macedon's rising power swallows the Greek world. Give one real example from your own life — a vote taken in your own class or family — Athens invented that way of deciding, and Sparta is its standing opposite — and name the lesson's key words: Ancient Civilisations: Sparta and Athens Against Sparta, Sparta, Athens, democracy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
