# Hands-on — Bengal's Political History: From the Mauryas to the Palas

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw Bengal as a house with a door. First, great emperors from outside hold the keys; then a king of Bengal's own takes them. This lesson tells who held the keys first, and who took them back.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Maurya and Gupta emperors reach into Bengal — Samudra Gupta captures Banga and Pundra — and as Gupta power fades, Shashanka of Karnasubarna becomes Bengal's first great independent king, with the Pala dynasty following him. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
