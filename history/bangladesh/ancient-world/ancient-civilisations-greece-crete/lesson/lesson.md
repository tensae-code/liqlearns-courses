# Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

## Do it first

Think of one story you know with a god or hero in it, and one question you have asked that has no easy answer. Myths answer the first way, philosophy the second — Greece gave the world both, and this lesson shows the unequal society they grew in.

Greece, read through society and culture, is a study in contrast. A privileged class rules a divided society — the few above, the many below, as in Egypt and Mesopotamia. But out of this unequal world come the gods and myths everyone still retells, and thinkers whose questions found mathematics, philosophy and science. The gifts outlast the order that produced them, and belong now to everyone after.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ancient Greece is a divided society ruled by a privileged class — yet its gods, myths and thinkers shape mathematics, philosophy and science for everyone who comes after. Give one real example from your own life — a classroom debate — asking 'why?' in public, as Greek thinkers did, is a habit this lesson's world invented — and name the lesson's key words: Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete, ancient Greece, privileged class, gods. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
