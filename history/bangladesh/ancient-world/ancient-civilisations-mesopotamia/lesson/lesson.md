# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

## Do it first

Name the two rivers of your own land that matter most, and one rule your locality lives by that is written down somewhere. Mesopotamia's cities grew the same way — water first, then written law to hold the city together.

Between two great rivers, the Sumerians and then the Babylonians raise city civilisations of a new kind. Society arranges itself in classes; the city keeps its own laws, worships its own gods, and builds its own learning. Read socially, as Class Eight reads it, Mesopotamia is the proof that the city is not just walls — it is an order, written down and believed in, that lets thousands of strangers live as one people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between two rivers, the Sumerian and Babylonian city civilisations build a class society with its own laws, gods and learning — Mesopotamia orders life by written rule at the dawn of cities. Give one real example from your own life — a written rule that orders your own school day — law as a city's memory and backbone begins in worlds like this one — and name the lesson's key words: Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia, Mesopotamia, Sumerian, Babylonian. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
