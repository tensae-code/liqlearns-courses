# About the History — Bangladesh course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
"History and Social Science" books of the National Curriculum
2022 (National Curriculum and Textbook Board, NCTB, Dhaka). The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Bangladesh inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they
prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four levels**, derived from the
books' own lenses, in the books' order:

1. **Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and
   the Deep Past.** History is learned by inquiry through one
   continuing classroom story — teacher Khushi Apa and her
   Class Six pupils investigate their own locality, interview
   elders under strict consent rules, and only then widen out
   to Bengal, South Asia and the world; the Liberation War is
   first met as a killing field in the children's own village,
   not as a date.
2. **Level 2 — History Through Work and Trade: Bengal's
   Economy and the Road to 1971.** Class Seven re-reads the
   whole past through one lens — economics: how people earned,
   ate, traded and were exploited, from stone tools to colonial
   extraction, before returning to Bangabandhu and to the
   foreigners who helped in 1971.
3. **Level 3 — History Through Society and Culture: How People
   Lived.** Class Eight turns to social and cultural history —
   how ordinary people's language, faith, food, festivals and
   art change over centuries — and reads even Egypt,
   Mesopotamia and Greece through society and culture rather
   than kings and battles.
4. **Level 4 — History Through Power and Politics: Rulers,
   Resistance and the Nation-State.** Class Nine is the
   political-history year — Bengal's political path is traced
   janapada to republic, world political forms (pharaohs,
   Sparta, Athens, nation-states) are set beside it, and
   Bangabandhu is studied as the bearer of Bengal's
   thousand-year humanitarian and secular spirit, honoured by
   the World Peace Council as 'Bishwabandhu', Friend of the
   World.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Ask first, then widen:* "Start where you stand: interview
  your elders, question every source, date what you find. Then
  walk out — to Bengal's rivers and first cities, to its
  textiles and ports, to its festivals and temples, to its
  kings and resisters — until the road reaches 1971, and a new
  country stands at the end of a journey of millennia."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

Bangladesh's own way, across the four books: history is
learned by inquiry through one continuing classroom story —
Khushi Apa's class investigates its own locality first, under
strict rules for interviewing elders, and each later class
re-reads the whole past through a new lens: economics in
Class Seven, society and culture in Class Eight, politics in
Class Nine. The Liberation War is never a bare date: it is a
killing field in the children's own village, a mother waiting
for Azad, refugees and foreign friends, and a spirit carried
home into rebuilding. *(Student books.)*
