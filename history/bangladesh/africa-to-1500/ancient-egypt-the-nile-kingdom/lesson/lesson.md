# Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

## Do it first

Draw a pyramid. Divide it into three layers and write who you think stood at the top, the middle and the bottom of Egypt's society. This lesson fills your drawing with the real names and the real order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ancient Egypt: society and culture

The Class Eight book reads Egypt through its society. Three classes stand under the god-king Pharaohs: the court above, and below, landless farmers and prisoners sold into labour. Yet the picture has its surprises — women like Nefertiti stand near the throne itself. And this ordered, unequal world gives everyone after it great gifts: writing, religion and mathematics all carry Egypt's mark.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Pharaohs

Class Nine reads the same land through its power. Egypt's kings, from Menes to Tutankhamun, rule as descendants of the sun god Re — owning, in the belief of the time, all wealth and all lives. Death does not end the reign: mummified and laid in pyramids, the king goes on ruling after death, while priests preach his glory to the living. Pharaoh is not merely a ruler; he is a god with a state.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ancient Egypt is a three-class society under god-king Pharaohs — from Menes to Tutankhamun, rulers as descendants of the sun god Re, owning all wealth and lives, mummified in pyramids to rule after death — with landless farmers and sold prisoners below, women like Nefertiti near the throne, and a people who give the world writing, religion and mathematics. Give one real example from your own life — any ranked order you know — a school, a team, a workplace — Egypt's three classes are the ancient shape of the same question: who stands where, and why — and name the lesson's key words: Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom, Pharaoh, Nefertiti, Menes. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
