# Lesson plan — From Stone Tools to Farming

**Course:** History — Bangladesh · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From Stone Tools to Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From the first stone tools 3.3 million years ago, cave life and hunting give way to copper worked alongside stone — and then to Gordon Childe's 'Neolithic Revolution', when people stopped wandering for food, farmed, reared animals and settled for good, reaching Bengal about seven thousand years ago.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Stone Age to Copper-Stone Age** — From the first stone tools 3.3 million years ago, cave life and hunting give way to copper worked alongside stone.
2. **The Agricultural Revolution** — In Gordon Childe's 'Neolithic Revolution' people stop wandering for food, farm, rear animals and settle permanently, reaching Bengal about seven thousand years ago.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: From the first stone tools 3.3 million years ago, cave life and hunting give way to copper worked alongside stone — and then to Gordon Childe's 'Neolithic Revolution', when people stopped wandering for food, farmed, reared animals and settled for good, reaching Bengal about seven thousand years ago.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — the rice or vegetables on your own plate — somebody's ancestors first chose to plant rather than wander, and your meal descends from that choice.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks farming was always there → for most of human time there was no farming at all; settling to farm was a revolution, and a late one.
- Go back to the one thing: From the first stone tools 3.3 million years ago, cave life and hunting give way to copper worked alongside stone — and then to Gordon Childe's 'Neolithic Revolution', when people stopped wandering for food, farmed, reared animals and settled for good, reaching Bengal about seven thousand years ago. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
