# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

List everything your family ate yesterday, and beside each item write where it came from: hunted, gathered, grown, or bought. Almost everything will say 'grown' — this lesson tells how that changed everything.

## Sub lesson 1 — Stone Age to Copper-Stone Age

The human story of tools begins 3.3 million years ago with the first shaped stone. For ages beyond counting, people lived in caves and hunted, their whole technology held in the hand. Then, slowly, a new material joined the old: copper, worked alongside stone in the Copper-Stone Age — the first metal, and the first hint that the old way of life would not last forever.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Agricultural Revolution

The scholar Gordon Childe named what happened next a revolution — the Neolithic Revolution. People stopped wandering in search of food. They farmed the land, reared animals and settled permanently in one place. Everything later — villages, cities, states — stands on that turn. And it reached Bengal too, about seven thousand years ago, when the first farmers took up the river land's rich soil.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: From the first stone tools 3.3 million years ago, cave life and hunting give way to copper worked alongside stone — and then to Gordon Childe's 'Neolithic Revolution', when people stopped wandering for food, farmed, reared animals and settled for good, reaching Bengal about seven thousand years ago. Give one real example from your own life — the rice or vegetables on your own plate — somebody's ancestors first chose to plant rather than wander, and your meal descends from that choice — and name the lesson's key words: From Stone Tools to Farming, Stone Age, Copper-Stone Age, Neolithic Revolution. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
