# Hands-on — Dating the Past

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page. Mark today at the right end. Now place these years on it: 1971, 1952, 1920, 1757. Under each, write which century it belongs to. You have built the frame every history in this course hangs on.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The past is placed in order with centuries and decades on either side of the BC/AD line; economies and eras can be periodised too — but the lines between ancient, medieval and modern are human choices, and Bengal's own facts show where the labels fail. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
