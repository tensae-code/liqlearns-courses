# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Draw a line across a page. Mark today at the right end. Now place these years on it: 1971, 1952, 1920, 1757. Under each, write which century it belongs to. You have built the frame every history in this course hangs on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Centuries, decades, BC and AD

Before any story can be told in order, the class learns the measuring frame. Years gather into decades of ten and centuries of a hundred, and the whole line is split by the BC/AD divide. On that frame the class pins the dates that matter most to them: the Battle of Plassey in 1757, Bangabandhu's birth in 1920, the Language Movement of 1952 and the Liberation War of 1971 — each placed by its century and its decade, on the AD side of the line.

## Sub lesson 2 — Periodisation in economic history

The working past can be divided too. Economic history periodises the human economy from hunting-and-gathering through the Stone, Copper, Iron and Bronze ages, then on to farming, cities and worldwide trade. Against that long frame the book sets a dated chronology for the subcontinent, for Bengal and for Bangladesh, so a learner can see where their own land stands inside the whole human story of work.

## Sub lesson 3 — The problem of dividing eras

In Class Nine the frame itself is put on trial. Dividing the past into ancient, medieval and modern eras looks tidy, but it is a human invention — and Bengal's facts expose the seams. From the seventh century onward, the book shows moments where one region's 'medieval' is another's turning point, and the label fails the facts. The lesson is not to drop the eras, but to hold them knowingly: they are tools, not truths.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The past is placed in order with centuries and decades on either side of the BC/AD line; economies and eras can be periodised too — but the lines between ancient, medieval and modern are human choices, and Bengal's own facts show where the labels fail. Give one real example from your own life — a family photograph dated by an elder's memory — placing it by decade and century is this lesson's skill at home — and name the lesson's key words: Dating the Past, centuries, decades, BC. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
