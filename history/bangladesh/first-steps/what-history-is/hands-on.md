# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down three things that happened in your family's past — a move, a harvest, a wedding, a hard year. Beside each one, write how you know it: who told you, what object or paper proves it. You have just done the first act of a historian.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is the logical, source-based account of how people lived, farmed, built kingdoms and reached the present — and a people without it has no identity; read through work and trade, it becomes economic history, the story of how people earned, ate and were governed. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
