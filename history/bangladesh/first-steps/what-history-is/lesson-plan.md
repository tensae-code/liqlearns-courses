# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Bangladesh · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is the logical, source-based account of how people lived, farmed, built kingdoms and reached the present — and a people without it has no identity; read through work and trade, it becomes economic history, the story of how people earned, ate and were governed.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What history is** — History is the logical, source-based account of how people lived, farmed, built kingdoms and reached the present.
2. **What economic history is** — Geography and economics drive history hardest, and economic history rebuilds past farming, industry and trade.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson on the path — nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History is the logical, source-based account of how people lived, farmed, built kingdoms and reached the present — and a people without it has no identity; read through work and trade, it becomes economic history, the story of how people earned, ate and were governed.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — your own family's past — the stories elders tell are your first history, and this lesson teaches you to ask what stands behind each one.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
