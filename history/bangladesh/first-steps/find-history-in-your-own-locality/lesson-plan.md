# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Bangladesh · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History begins where you live: pupil teams interview elders about their own village — with permission, confidentiality and no pressure to answer — and plan real inquiries, like the class investigation of what happened in their locality in 1971.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Explore the history of our neighbourhood** — Pupil teams interview 4–5 elders about their own village, under rules of permission, confidentiality and no pressure to answer.
2. **The inquiry project** — The class plans its own investigation of 1971 in its locality — interviewing elders and checking the local library.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History begins where you live: pupil teams interview elders about their own village — with permission, confidentiality and no pressure to answer — and plan real inquiries, like the class investigation of what happened in their locality in 1971.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — an elder in your own street — interviewed under the book's rules, their memory becomes your locality's history.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
