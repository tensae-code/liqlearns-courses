# Hands-on — Find History in Your Own Locality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Choose one elder in your family or street. Write three questions about how your locality used to be — and before the questions, write the promise you will make first: that you ask with permission, and that anything private stays private.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History begins where you live: pupil teams interview elders about their own village — with permission, confidentiality and no pressure to answer — and plan real inquiries, like the class investigation of what happened in their locality in 1971. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
