# Lesson plan — Historiography: The History of History Writing

**Course:** History — Bangladesh · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Historiography: The History of History Writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — History Through Society and Culture: How People Lived (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History itself has a history: scripts change century by century and must be deciphered before old texts speak, historians assemble the decoded sources — with reference works like Banglapedia — into written cultural history, and their methods and era schemes are human inventions argued over in print.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — History Through Society and Culture: How People Lived (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Deciphering and writing cultural history** — Scripts change century by century and must be decoded before old texts speak; the historian assembles deciphered sources, and reference works like Banglapedia, into a written cultural history.
2. **Methods of learning history** — Historians' methods and era schemes are human inventions argued over in print, in Dhaka and Kolkata newspapers as much as in books.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History itself has a history: scripts change century by century and must be deciphered before old texts speak, historians assemble the decoded sources — with reference works like Banglapedia — into written cultural history, and their methods and era schemes are human inventions argued over in print.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a grandparent's old letter in fading handwriting — reading the past always begins with making its writing speak again.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
