# Hands-on — Historiography: The History of History Writing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one sentence and copy it in the oldest-looking handwriting you can manage. Give it to a companion: can they read it easily? Multiply that difficulty by a thousand years, and you have this lesson's first problem — deciphering.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History itself has a history: scripts change century by century and must be deciphered before old texts speak, historians assemble the decoded sources — with reference works like Banglapedia — into written cultural history, and their methods and era schemes are human inventions argued over in print. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
