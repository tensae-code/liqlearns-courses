# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pretend you find an old coin in the soil near your home. Write down three questions you would ask it before believing anything it seems to say: Where were you made? Who used you? How did you get here? That questioning is where written history begins.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written history is built from sources that are questioned first: a lost ball can lead to a ruined building, and coins, inscriptions, travellers' accounts, manuscripts and buildings each testify — differently — about the past. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
