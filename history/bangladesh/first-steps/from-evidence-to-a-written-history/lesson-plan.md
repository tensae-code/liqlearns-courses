# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Bangladesh · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A written history is built from sources that are questioned first: a lost ball can lead to a ruined building, and coins, inscriptions, travellers' accounts, manuscripts and buildings each testify — differently — about the past.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — History by Inquiry: Your Locality, Bengal and the Deep Past (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The ruined building in the jungle** — A lost cricket ball leads the class to a mound with broken pottery, coins and palm-leaf manuscripts, each a source to be interrogated.
2. **Sources of economic history** — Coins, inscriptions, travellers' accounts and artefacts each testify about past economic life, and their differing characters decide what can be claimed.
3. **Two kinds of sources** — Literary sources like the Charyapada and archaeological sources like inscriptions, copperplates, terracotta, coins and buildings must each be questioned.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: A written history is built from sources that are questioned first: a lost ball can lead to a ruined building, and coins, inscriptions, travellers' accounts, manuscripts and buildings each testify — differently — about the past.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a coin found in a field near home — questioned properly, it can testify about trade, rulers and daily life.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
