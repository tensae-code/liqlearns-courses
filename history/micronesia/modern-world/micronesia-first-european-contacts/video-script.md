# Video script — Micronesia's First European Contacts

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. The outside world did not arrive in Micronesia all at once. It arrived island by island, face to face — and the islands kept the records.

A storm blows Portuguese sailors under Dioga da Rocha to Ulithi. Face to face, two worlds, neither expecting the other. Spanish ships follow, puzzling both sides. And when ships demand provisions, welcome can turn — hostile in Chuuk Lagoon, hostile at Pulap, musket fire in the Marshalls.

Now go small. Kapingamarangi remembers its arrivals one by one: Tongohiti. The Wareai traders of the 1800s. Castaways from Kiribati. The Moduroo people in the 1870s. Each name kept, in order.

Nukuoro logs canoes and ships the same way: Fiji, Kiribati, the Marshalls in 1870, Onotoa, Yap, Sonsorol, Namoluk — and among them, two European sightings, 1806 and 1840. In the atoll's own book, Europe is two lines in a long register.

Remember it this way: contact was a hundred first meetings — and the islands wrote them down. Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
