# Lesson plan — Micronesia's First European Contacts

**Course:** History — Micronesia · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Micronesia's First European Contacts
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Modern World I: Contact, Missions and Trade (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** First contact in Micronesia came island by island and face to face: storm-blown Portuguese in Ulithi, puzzling Spanish visits, welcomes that turned hostile when ships demanded provisions — and on the outer atolls, a slower roll of arrivals, each one remembered and logged in the islands' own records.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Modern World I: Contact, Missions and Trade — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 3, Portuguese in Ulithi, Spaniards in Yap, musket fire in the Marshalls** — Storm-blown Portuguese under Dioga da Rocha meet islanders face to face, Spanish visits puzzle both sides, and welcomes turn hostile in Chuuk Lagoon and Pulap as ships demand provisions.
2. **Chapter 8, Before the Foreigners Came** — The first outsiders arrive one by one — the man Tongohiti, the Wareai traders of the 1800s, castaways from Kiribati, and the Moduroo people in the 1870s — each arrival remembered in the atoll's own record.
3. **Chapter 3, the canoes and ships that came** — Arrivals are logged one by one — money and canoes from Fiji, Kiribati, the Marshalls (1870), Onotoa, Yap, Sonsorol and Namoluk, a European sighting in 1806 and another in 1840 — an atoll keeping its own contact register.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
