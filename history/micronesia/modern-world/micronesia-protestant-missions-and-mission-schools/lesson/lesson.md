# Micronesia's Protestant Missions and Mission Schools

## Do it first

A new teaching arrives in a community that has lost almost everyone it had. Before you read, think: what does a community in that condition need from a new faith — comfort, order, leaders, schools? Then read how the Protestant missions actually grew in Micronesia: carried, in the end, by islanders.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 7, The Foundations of Christianity

In the 1850s, American Protestant missionaries land in Kosrae and Pohnpei — the foundations of Christianity in Micronesia's own islands. The chiefs are not bystanders: the Nahnken of Kitti, a paramount chief of Pohnpei, weighs the new faith, and chiefly judgement decides where the teaching may take root.

Then the story changes hands. Islander missionaries — converts become carriers — take the faith onward: to the Marshalls, to the Mortlocks, to Chuuk. The mission's map of Micronesia is drawn less by the foreigners who landed first than by the islanders who paddled and sailed it outward.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 7, mission schools and the language of school

The missions built schools, and the schools changed the islands' tongues and leadership. Mission schools trained islander teachers and pastors — the faith's future carried in island hands — and English church-and-school words entered the island languages, where many remain.

Literacy was power. Native Christians, able to read and teach, grew too strong for the last stands made against them: the school had armed the new faith with its own people's voices. What began as a foreigners' landing ends, in this chapter, as an island institution.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 6, American Missionaries in Micronesia

The government book tells Kosrae's landing from the island's side. The American missionaries reach a Kosrae shattered: its population fallen ninety percent in fifty years, its old religion seeming — to its own people — to have failed them.

Into that collapse, the church does double work. It rebuilds Kosraean leadership and faith together: new belief and new leaders rising in the same generation, each holding the other up. Read beside the first telling, the point is plain: on Kosrae the mission did not only convert. It re-founded a people's order after catastrophe.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: American Protestant missionaries landed in Kosrae and Pohnpei in the 1850s, chiefs such as the Nahnken of Kitti weighed the faith, islander missionaries carried it to the Marshalls, the Mortlocks and Chuuk, mission schools trained islander teachers and pastors and brought English church-and-school words into the languages — and on Kosrae, down ninety percent in fifty years, the church rebuilt leadership and faith together. Give one real example of a teaching spreading because local people carried it, and name the lesson's key words: missionary, Nahnken of Kitti, mission schools, literacy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
