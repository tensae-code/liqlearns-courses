# Video script — Micronesia in the Japanese Mandate Years

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Japan's mandate transformed Micronesia more deeply than any rule before it — and hollowed out the chiefs while it built.

1914: Japan seizes Germany's islands and rules them as a League of Nations Mandate. Towns rise. Industries open. A cash economy spreads. Immigrants pour in — Okinawan, Japanese — until islanders are a minority in their own land.

Children pass through kogakko schools toward civil-service and trade jobs. Katsuobushi and phosphate sail out to the Empire. Young leaders feel the excitement of a wider world — the book does not deny it.

But sit where the chiefs sit: in 'yes-yes councils', summoned to agree, given no real place. Kapingamarangi dates the era 1914 to 1945 — ending, like the others, when war arrives. Remember it this way: transformation above, hollowing below. Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
