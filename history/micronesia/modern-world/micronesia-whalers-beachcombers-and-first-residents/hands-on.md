# Hands-on — Micronesia's Whalers, Beachcombers and First Residents

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A port town grows up around ships that stay for weeks — sailors ashore, traders dealing, some men never sailing away again. Before you read, list what such a season brings a small island: the good, the dangerous, the lasting. Then read the whaling age in the eastern Carolines.

## The lesson in your hands and on your feet
Take paper and a pencil and draw one anchorage in the whaling age with everything in its place: ships, drying racks, a chief's house, a beachcomber, and the village. Label who gains and who pays. Then walk to the nearest harbour, dock or landing in your place and list what a visiting vessel still needs from the shore today — water, food, repairs, rest — the whaler's list in modern form.
