# Micronesia's Whalers, Beachcombers and First Residents

## Do it first

A port town grows up around ships that stay for weeks — sailors ashore, traders dealing, some men never sailing away again. Before you read, list what such a season brings a small island: the good, the dangerous, the lasting. Then read the whaling age in the eastern Carolines.

The whaling fleets found the eastern Carolines, and the anchorages crowded: whalers taking on water and food, beche-de-mer traders gathering the sea slug for the China market. The ships brought trade — and a new kind of resident. Deserting sailors jumped ship and stayed, becoming beachcombers: foreigners who lived ashore under the chiefs, inside island society, on island terms.

The age cut both ways, and the book does not hide the cutting. The crews of the Waverly and the Harriet were massacred — meetings of worlds ending in killing. And quieter than any massacre, disease arrived with the ships: the pox cut island populations, emptying villages the ships had only visited. The first residents stayed; so did the losses.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: whalers and beche-de-mer traders crowded the eastern Carolines, deserting sailors became beachcombers living under chiefs, the Waverly and Harriet crews were massacred, and the pox cut island populations. Give one real example of visitors leaving lasting change behind, and name the lesson's key words: whalers, beche-de-mer, beachcomber, the pox. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: sort your good/dangerous/lasting list against the lesson's real arrivals and costs.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a crowded anchorage — whalers, traders, one beachcomber under a chief — with the age's costs named beside it.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and this week ask an elder about the first foreigner who stayed in your place, if any. Note the story.
