# The Marianas and Carolines: First Catholic Missions

## Do it first

A guest arrives in your village bringing a new teaching. At first there is welcome. Then the guest's guards begin giving orders. Trace, before you read, the exact point where welcome could turn. Then read how the first Catholic missions in the Marianas walked that line.

The first mission comes to Guam. Fr. Sanvitores plants the Catholic faith there — the first church of Spain's Pacific — and the islands the Spanish had called the Ladrones are renamed the Marianas, a name that itself proclaims the mission's purpose.

But the mission does not stand alone. Spanish soldiers stand behind it, and welcome turns to conflict: the islanders who first received the priests resist the power that arrives with them, and Guam's mission years become years of struggle. A generation on, the story moves south: Fr. Cantova carries word of the faith into the Carolines, taking the mission story from the Marianas toward Micronesia's own islands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Fr. Sanvitores planted the first Catholic mission on Guam, the Ladrones became the Marianas, Spanish soldiers turned welcome into conflict, and Fr. Cantova later carried the faith into the Carolines. Give one real example of a welcome turning when power arrives with a guest, and name the lesson's key words: mission, Marianas, Sanvitores, Cantova. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: mark the point where welcome turned on Guam, and say what arrived alongside the teaching.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Guam's mission, the renaming, the conflict, and Cantova's journey south, as one timeline.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and this week find the oldest church or mission site near you and note when its story begins.
