# Micronesia's Traditional Leadership Under Change

## Do it first

A chief's son becomes a leader — but not only because of his father. His power also comes from a church office and from land a foreign reform handed him. Before you read, think: when the grounds of authority change, is the tradition still itself? Then read how Micronesia's government book traces leadership under change.

Set the three traditional models beside the foreign centuries and watch the ground move. Warfare ends — one whole arena of chiefly power gone. Populations collapse — the people over whom authority runs are suddenly few. Churches arrive, and German land reforms redraw who holds the land itself.

Authority migrates to the new grounds. The book's case is Henry Nanpei: his power comes from church office and from reform-granted land — not only from his father, the Nahnken. The chiefly line continues, but it now stands on church and deed as well as descent. Tradition, in this telling, is not a statue. It is a house rebuilt on new footings while the family still lives in it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: ended warfare, collapsed populations, churches and German land reforms moved the grounds of chiefly authority, so Henry Nanpei's power came from church office and reform-granted land as well as from his Nahnken father. Give one real example of a role whose grounds have changed in your own community, and name the lesson's key words: change, church office, land reform, Nanpei. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: list the old grounds of a chief's power and the new grounds Nanpei stood on.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a chiefly house on two sets of footings — descent on one side, church office and reform land on the other.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and this week ask an elder how one local leader's authority today differs from the old way. Note the answer.
