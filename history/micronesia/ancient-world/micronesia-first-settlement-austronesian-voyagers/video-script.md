# Video script — Micronesia's First Settlement: The Austronesian Voyagers

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Micronesia was not found by accident. It was settled — by voyagers who knew exactly what they were doing.

Begin in southeast China, about five thousand years before Christ. Villages grow crowded. Austronesian families take to the sea — Taiwan first, then island by island through the Philippines, then along two paths into Micronesia. Long before them, older peoples had walked the Sunda and Sahul land bridges, when the sea was low. Two waves people the Pacific.

How did the second wave cross open ocean? They sang the map. Star songs — the paths of the stars carried in memory and sung at sea. Their outrigger canoes tacked against the wind, zigzag to a shore no one aboard had seen. And they carried a beginning in the hull: adzes, coir rope, pigs, chickens, seed crops — a whole subsistence life, packed.

And the far atolls still open their own histories with this story. Kapingamarangi's reader proves it three ways — archaeology, linguistics, DNA — and names the Lapita voyagers reaching the outliers from Tuvalu. Mwoakilloa retells it in Mwoakillese. Nukuoro retells it in Nukuoroan.

Remember it this way: the horizon was the road, the stars were the chart, and the canoe carried a world. Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
