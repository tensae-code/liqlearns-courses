# Lesson plan — Micronesia's First Settlement: The Austronesian Voyagers

**Course:** History — Micronesia · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Micronesia's First Settlement: The Austronesian Voyagers
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient World: The First Settlement (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Micronesia was settled by voyagers: Austronesian families who left crowded villages in southeast China for Taiwan about 5,000 BC and travelled island by island — singing their star paths, tacking outrigger canoes against the wind, carrying adzes, rope, pigs, chickens and seed crops — along two paths into the islands, where the atoll readers tell the same two-wave story in the islands' own languages.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 2, Centuries of Migration and the two paths of settlement** — Crowded villages push Austronesian families out of southeast China to Taiwan about 5,000 BC, then island by island through the Philippines and along two paths into Micronesia, while older peoples had already crossed the Sunda and Sahul land bridges.
2. **Chapter 2, Traveling Beyond the Horizon: the outrigger, star songs and the tool kit** — Navigators sing star paths instead of writing them, tack against the wind in outrigger canoes, and land with adzes, coir rope, pigs, chickens and seed crops to build a subsistence life on each new island.
3. **Chapter 1, The Origin of the People of Kapingamarangi** — Archaeology, linguistics and DNA together trace two waves into the Pacific — the first across the Sunda and Sahul land bridges some 50,000 years ago, the second the Austronesian Lapita voyagers from Taiwan who reach the Polynesian outliers, Kapingamarangi among them, from Tuvalu.
4. **Chapter 1, The Beginning of Mwoakilloa** — The same two-wave settlement science as the sister readers — first peoples crossing to Sahul, then Austronesian voyagers spreading through the Pacific — is retold in Mwoakillese for Mwoakilloa's own children.
5. **Chapter 1, How the First People Came to Nukuoro** — The two waves into the Pacific are retold in Nukuoroan — the first peoples of the ice-age land bridges, then the Austronesian voyagers whose descendants settle the Polynesian outlier atolls.
