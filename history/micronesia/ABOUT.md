# About the History — Micronesia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — the
Federated States of Micronesia's own Department of
Education books and the Pohnpei Studies readers. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons
are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Micronesia inside the History
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's
place is found by what they prove, and each level stands
on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never
a forced gate. The school grade each book comes from
appears only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance. History is taught as stories: who, where,
what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **five levels**, following the
books' own eras: (1) First Steps — how history is pieced
from evidence, and how Micronesian communities held
together in their three home-grown leadership models,
with the atolls' own chiefly orders beside them; (2) the
first settlement by the Austronesian voyagers, told in
the national book and in all three atoll readers; (3)
contact, missions and trade — Europe's race, first
meetings, both mission waves, the China trade, the
whalers and the copra age; (4) the four foreign rulers
and the war — Spain, Germany and Sokehs, Japan's mandate,
the Pacific war and its island memory, and traditional
leadership changing underneath; (5) the American time —
Trust Territory, the leap into the modern world, the
Congress of Micronesia's road, and the independent FSM.
All 20 lessons stand on **student books** and count in
the acceptance reckoning below; this country's set, as
filed, contains no teacher guides at all.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The whole course:* "First, how we know: the past
  survives in pieces — letters, journals, reports, songs
  and elders' legends — and Micronesia pieces them into
  its family album; and how the islands held together —
  Chuuk's clan chiefs, Pohnpei's paired paramount chiefs,
  Yap's woven nets. Then the voyagers: star songs,
  outriggers, two paths into the islands. Then the world
  arrives — Columbus's race, first meetings, missions,
  traders, whalers, copra — and then the rulers, one
  after another: Spain, Germany and Sokehs's answer,
  Japan's mandate, the war in the lagoons, America and
  the Trust Territory. And at the end, the octopus comes
  back together: the Congress, the constitution, and in
  1979 a president of the islands' own — an independent
  nation of islands."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Over Seas and Time (Grade 9)** — one national story
told island by island in strict time order: settlement,
contact, visitors, then four foreign rulers in turn
(Spain, Germany, Japan, America) — every chapter opening
in a lived scene (a boy watching a strange ship, a
Chuukese man weeping at a flag) and closing with
vocabulary, understanding, critical-thinking and
community-research tasks. *(Student book.)*

**Micronesian Government (Grade 11)** — government
taught as a story that starts before foreigners: three
home-grown leadership models (Chuuk, Pohnpei, Yap)
compared state by state, then power followed through
missionaries, German and Japanese rulers, and the
islanders' own congresses to independence. Its History
units (One–Three) are placed here; its Unit Four is
present-day Civics, named above and not placed per
scope, as filed (verdict PARTIAL for History scope).
*(Student book.)*

**The three Pohnpei Studies readers (Kapingamarangi,
Mwoakilloa, Nukuoro)** — each atoll writes its own
history in its own language: the shared science of
settlement first, then its own founding traditions and
chiefly order, then — Kapingamarangi in full — the
foreign eras, Spanish, German, Japanese and American,
each in its own dated section. Mwoakilloa's history is a
succession of eighteen Nahmwarki; Nukuoro's is a roll of
arrivals at a meeting-place atoll. *(Student readers.)*
