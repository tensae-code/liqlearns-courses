# History — Micronesia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Micronesia (the Federated States of
Micronesia) inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have
proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The
school grade each book comes from appears only in each
lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Micronesia's own books tell one
national story island by island in strict time order — the
Grade 9 *Over Seas and Time* walks it chapter by chapter,
the Grade 11 *Micronesian Government* retells the same
centuries as a story of leadership, and the three Pohnpei
Studies atoll readers (Kapingamarangi, Mwoakilloa, Nukuoro)
tell their own islands' shares of it in their own
languages. The ladder follows that story's own eras as the
books present them: the method and the old order first —
how history is pieced from evidence, and how Micronesian
communities held together before any foreign flag
(Level 1); then the first settlement, the Austronesian
voyagers (Level 2); then contact, missions and trade —
Europe's race, the first meetings, the Catholic and
Protestant missions, the China trade, the whalers and the
copra stores (Level 3); then the four foreign rulers and
the war — Spain, Germany and the Sokehs Rebellion, Japan's
mandate, the Pacific war on the islands, and traditional
leadership itself changing under it all (Level 4); and
last the American time — the Trust Territory, the leap
into the modern world, the Congress of Micronesia's road
to self-government, and the independent FSM (Level 5).
The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Micronesia's
own FSM Department of Education and Pohnpei Studies books
— except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the
people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list, the
combined-book contents map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps → How History Is Found and How
   Communities Hold Together** — the past survives in
   pieces (letters, journals, reports, songs, chants and
   elders' legends — Utamatua, Mwoakilloa's 1,700-year
   reckoning, Vave), and the historian pieces them into a
   people's family album; then the old order itself:
   Chuuk's loose clan chiefs, Pohnpei's stacked chiefdoms
   under the Nahnmwarki and Nahnken, Yap's ranked village
   nets, and the atolls' ariki, Nahmwarki successions and
   aligi — everywhere power balanced, checked by the
   people's voice. 2 lessons, in one title: `first-steps/`
   (2 lessons).

2. **Ancient World → The First Settlement** — crowded
   villages in southeast China send Austronesian families
   to Taiwan about 5,000 BC and island by island along two
   paths into Micronesia, navigators singing star paths in
   tacking outriggers, landing with adzes, rope, pigs,
   chickens and seed crops — the same two-wave story the
   Kapingamarangi, Mwoakilloa and Nukuoro readers open
   with, in their own languages. 1 lesson, in one title:
   `ancient-world/` (1 lesson).

3. **Modern World → Contact, Missions and Trade** —
   Europe's race for the Indies (Columbus, Tordesillas,
   the Manila Galleon road of 1565); first contacts island
   by island (Rocha in Ulithi, welcomes turning hostile in
   Chuuk Lagoon and Pulap, the atolls' own arrival
   registers); Sanvitores and the Catholic missions;
   the China trade and Lütke's scientific voyages; the
   whalers, beche-de-mer traders and beachcombers, with
   the Waverly and Harriet massacres and the pox; the
   Protestant missions of the 1850s, carried onward by
   islander missionaries and mission schools; and copra —
   the coconut, the companies, and foreign law following
   trade in behind warships and the 1870 treaty. 7
   lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (7 lessons).

4. **Modern World → Four Foreign Rulers and the War** —
   Spain, first coloniser (Yap and Pohnpei, the attack at
   three months, Reverend Doane, the 1899 sale at the
   empire's edge); Germany, bought in 1899 for just over
   four million dollars (district offices, reforms, roads
   built with Yap's chiefs — and Sokehs's rebellion of
   1910 answered with soldiers in 1911); Japan's mandate
   (towns, kogakko schools, immigrants, islanders a
   minority at home, chiefs in yes-yes councils); the
   Pacific war — lifeline, battlefield, relief — and the
   war that stayed in elders' stories, rusted cannons and
   bunkers; and traditional leadership under change, in
   Henry Nanpei's church office and reform land. 6
   lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (6 lessons).

5. **Modern World → The American Time and
   Independence** — the US Navy's rebuilding and
   repatriation, the UN Trust Territory from 1947, and
   islanders building municipalities, magistrates and
   district congresses inside it (Kapingamarangi's
   Baalangi); the leap of the 1960s–70s — money, radios,
   schools, hospitals, roads, airfields, Peace Corps,
   Micronesianization; the Congress of Micronesia's road
   — Amaraich's octopus, years of pressing America, the
   UN appeal, the Marshalls and Palau going their own
   way, four districts' constitution; and the independent
   FSM — Nakayama's 1979 inauguration, the Compact of
   Free Association, the UN seat, November 3, and a
   diaspora bound home by remittances. 4 lessons, in one
   title: `modern-world/` (4 lessons).

**Totals: 20 lessons** (Level 1: 2, Level 2: 1, Level 3:
7, Level 4: 6, Level 5: 4) — 51 placements stocked, every
lesson on student books (see ABOUT.md).

## Where the build stands

- **Course frame — BUILT** (this CURRICULUM, TITLES.md,
  ABOUT.md, and the folder README entry).
- **Levels 1–5 — BUILT**: 20 lessons, 10 questions each;
  the course's 51 placements stock the 20 lessons. Every
  lesson stands on **student books** — the Grade 9 and
  Grade 11 national textbooks and the three Pohnpei
  Studies atoll readers — so every lesson counts in the
  acceptance reckoning (taught in 1 country, Micronesia);
  there are no teacher guides and no UNREAD books in this
  country's set as filed. The course is complete as far
  as the books in hand reach: *Micronesian Government*'s
  Unit Four (present-day Civics) and *Over Seas and
  Time*'s geology Chapter 1 and Appendix are named in
  ABOUT.md's contents map and feed no history lesson, as
  filed.
