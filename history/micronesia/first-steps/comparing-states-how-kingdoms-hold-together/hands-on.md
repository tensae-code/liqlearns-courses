# Hands-on — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a meeting you have seen — a family, a class, a village — where a decision had to be made. Who spoke? Who decided? Could the others say no? Hold that picture. This lesson compares how Micronesia's own communities organised power long before any foreign government arrived: three models, three different ways of holding together, one shared rule — power is balanced, never one person's alone.

## The lesson in your hands and on your feet
Take paper and a pencil and draw the three models as three shapes: circles side by side for Chuuk, a ladder for Pohnpei, a net for Yap. Under each, write who can say no to the chief. Then act it: with two or three companions, hold one decision three times — once deciding by one person's command, once by majority vote, once by talking until everyone can stand behind it — and note which way felt like the books' way, and why.
