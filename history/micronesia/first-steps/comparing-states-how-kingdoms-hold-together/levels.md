# Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 1, Traditional Leadership Across Micronesia
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: across Chuuk, Pohnpei and Yap, traditional leaders share power through meetings and consensus rather than majority vote, and the people's voice checks the chief in every state — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 2, The Chuuk Model
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Chuuk's clan and lineage chiefs hold little power over their own people and none over other chiefs — a loose model shared by many outer islands — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 3, The Pohnpei Model
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pohnpei stacks section chiefs under two paramount chiefs in each of five chiefdoms — Nahnmwarki and Nahnken — while Kosrae once ran a tighter two-paramount-chief island — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Chapter 4, The Yap Model
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Yap binds villages into ranked nets whose councils make nearly every important decision — the most tightly woven of the three models — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Chapter 5, Themes in Micronesian Traditional Leadership
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: comparing the three models shows power shared three different ways — by clan, by stacked chiefdoms, by village nets — yet everywhere balanced between leaders and people — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Chapter 3, The Ariki and the People of the Land
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Kapingamarangi orders itself under its ariki (chiefs), whose authority is bound to the land-holding people — a small atoll's own model of how a community holds together — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Chapter 4, The Histories of the Nahmwarki
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: eighteen paramount chiefs are chronicled in order — Lakaidak, Lajahpwoa, Woajeidipw and on to Sahn Johnson — a chiefly succession that is itself the political history of the atoll — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Chapter 2, How the People Lived Together
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Nukuoro's order is built on its aligi (chiefs) — Takotonga and Hodooligi named and chronicled — and on the customs that bind households to the chiefly line — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
