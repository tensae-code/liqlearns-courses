# Lesson plan — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

**Course:** History — Micronesia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Found and How Communities Hold Together (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Micronesia's peoples held themselves together in three home-grown ways — Chuuk's loose clan chiefs, Pohnpei's stacked chiefdoms under two paramount chiefs, and Yap's tightly woven village nets — and in every model power was shared and balanced, checked by meetings, consensus and the people's voice, never held by one person alone.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 1, Traditional Leadership Across Micronesia** — Across Chuuk, Pohnpei and Yap, traditional leaders share power through meetings and consensus rather than majority vote, and the people's voice checks the chief in every state.
2. **Chapter 2, The Chuuk Model** — Chuuk's clan and lineage chiefs hold little power over their own people and none over other chiefs — a loose model shared by many outer islands.
3. **Chapter 3, The Pohnpei Model** — Pohnpei stacks section chiefs under two paramount chiefs in each of five chiefdoms — Nahnmwarki and Nahnken — while Kosrae once ran a tighter two-paramount-chief island.
4. **Chapter 4, The Yap Model** — Yap binds villages into ranked nets whose councils make nearly every important decision — the most tightly woven of the three models.
5. **Chapter 5, Themes in Micronesian Traditional Leadership** — Comparing the three models shows power shared three different ways — by clan, by stacked chiefdoms, by village nets — yet everywhere balanced between leaders and people.
6. **Chapter 3, The Ariki and the People of the Land** — Kapingamarangi orders itself under its ariki (chiefs), whose authority is bound to the land-holding people — a small atoll's own model of how a community holds together.
7. **Chapter 4, The Histories of the Nahmwarki** — Eighteen paramount chiefs are chronicled in order — Lakaidak, Lajahpwoa, Woajeidipw and on to Sahn Johnson — a chiefly succession that is itself the political history of the atoll.
8. **Chapter 2, How the People Lived Together** — Nukuoro's order is built on its aligi (chiefs) — Takotonga and Hodooligi named and chronicled — and on the customs that bind households to the chiefly line.
