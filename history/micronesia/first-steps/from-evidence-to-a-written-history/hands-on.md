# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask an older person in your family about one thing that happened before you were born — a voyage, a storm, a chief, a ship's arrival. Listen for two kinds of piece: what they saw themselves, and what was told to them. Hold both in mind. This lesson is about where a written history comes from: pieces like those, gathered, checked and pieced together.

## The lesson in your hands and on your feet
Take paper and a pencil and make your own evidence page: on the left, draw or list three paper pieces (a letter, a journal, a report); on the right, three memory pieces (a song, a chant, an elder's legend). Draw lines joining any pieces that could check each other, and under each line write what the checking would show. Then go out with your feet: walk to one elder in your family or village, ask one question about the past, and write down the answer word for word as your own first piece of evidence.
