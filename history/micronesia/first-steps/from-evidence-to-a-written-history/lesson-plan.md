# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Micronesia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Found and How Communities Hold Together (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The past does not survive whole: it survives in pieces — letters, journals, reports, songs, chants and elders' told traditions — and a written history is made when someone selects those pieces, checks them against each other, and pieces them into the story of a people's own past.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How History Is Found and How Communities Hold Together — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Foreword and Introduction: what history is** — The past survives only in pieces — missionary letters, whaling journals, government reports — and the historian selects and pieces them into the story of a people's own family album.
2. **Chapter 2, oral legends of the settlement of Pohnpei and Chuuk and cultural similarities across Micronesia** — Island songs, chants and elder-told settlement legends are checked against language families and shared tools and customs to trace who came from where.
3. **Chapter 2, The Story of Utamatua** — The founding tradition is told as the community keeps it — a spared castaway's son Utamatua, taught navigation by his father, becomes the ancestor from whom Kapingamarangi traces itself.
4. **Chapters 2–3, the origin traditions and the beginning of Mwoakilloa** — The atoll's beginnings survive as told tradition, reaching back some 1,700 years in the community's own reckoning, and the reader sets that oral record down as the island's first history.
5. **Chapter 3, First Things: the coming of Vave** — The traditions name Vave, the bringer who reaches Nukuoro and reshapes its life, and preserve the sequence of his deeds and death as the island's foundational oral record.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, teaching Micronesia's past as its own islands' story.
