# Hands-on — The Renaissance: Its Great Figures

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A scholar in Rotterdam laughs at folly in elegant Latin and half of Europe reads him. A sculptor in Rome lies on his back under a chapel ceiling, painting the creation of the world. Between them, Europe's mind is being remade. This lesson meets the great figures of the Renaissance.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Erasmus, Michelangelo and a new figure — the artist — remake Europe's mind: humanists recover the ancient world in books, and artists step out of anonymity to sign their work and be celebrated as creators. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
