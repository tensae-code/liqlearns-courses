# Lesson plan — The Renaissance: Its Great Figures

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Renaissance: Its Great Figures
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and the Reformations (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Erasmus, Michelangelo and a new figure — the artist — remake Europe's mind: humanists recover the ancient world in books, and artists step out of anonymity to sign their work and be celebrated as creators.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and the Reformations — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Erasmus, Michelangelo and a new figure — the artist — remake Europe's mind: humanists recover the ancient world in books, and artists step out of anonymity to sign their work and be celebrated as creators.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a young musician in the learner's town who signs her own songs and is known by her name, not as 'someone's backing singer' — a person becoming an author of her own work.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Renaissance was only about painting → it was a whole movement of mind: humanist scholars like Erasmus recovered ancient texts and criticised their own age in print, while artists gained a new status — named, signed and celebrated as creators.
- Go back to the one thing: Erasmus, Michelangelo and a new figure — the artist — remake Europe's mind: humanists recover the ancient world in books, and artists step out of anonymity to sign their work and be celebrated as creators. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
