# Hands-on — Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
In 1492 a ship reaches an island. Its captain writes home that he has 'discovered' a new world. But people are standing on the beach watching him arrive — people whose families have lived there for thousands of years. So who discovered what? This lesson puts the word itself on trial.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Europeans 'discover' lands that other peoples already call home — and France's book puts the very word 'discovery' on trial, asking what it hides and whom it erases. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
