# Lesson plan — Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and the Reformations (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Europeans 'discover' lands that other peoples already call home — and France's book puts the very word 'discovery' on trial, asking what it hides and whom it erases.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and the Reformations — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Europeans 'discover' lands that other peoples already call home — and France's book puts the very word 'discovery' on trial, asking what it hides and whom it erases.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: arriving at a friend's house for the first time and announcing you have 'discovered' it — your friend, who lives there, hears the word very differently.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks questioning the word 'discovery' means the voyages did not matter → the voyages changed the world utterly; the book's point is about language: from the shore where people already lived, 1492 was an arrival, an encounter — and soon an invasion — not a discovery of empty land.
- Go back to the one thing: Europeans 'discover' lands that other peoples already call home — and France's book puts the very word 'discovery' on trial, asking what it hides and whom it erases. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
