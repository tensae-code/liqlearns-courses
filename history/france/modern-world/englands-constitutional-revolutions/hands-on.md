# Hands-on — England's Constitutional Revolutions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Across the Channel, a neighbouring kingdom does the unthinkable: it puts its own king on trial and cuts off his head. Then, a generation later, it writes laws that tie every future king's hands. France watched all of it — fascinated and horrified. This lesson crosses the Channel.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: England tries its king, executes him, and ends by binding the crown itself with habeas corpus and a Bill of Rights — and Voltaire holds English liberty up to shame France, while the book asks how free England really was. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
