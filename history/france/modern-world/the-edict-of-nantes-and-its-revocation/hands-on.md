# Hands-on — The Edict of Nantes and Its Revocation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A king who changed his own religion to win his crown signs a law letting two religions live in one kingdom. Eighty-seven years later his great-grandson tears the law up. This lesson holds the two signatures side by side.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Henri IV's Edict of Nantes in 1598 grants France's Protestants peace and a protected place in the kingdom; in 1685 Louis XIV revokes it — one edict given, one edict torn up, and religious unity chosen over toleration. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
