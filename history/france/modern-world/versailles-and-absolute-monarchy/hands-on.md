# Hands-on — Versailles and Absolute Monarchy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A king builds the most splendid palace in Europe and orders his nobles to come and live in it — to dance, to bow, to wait in corridors for a glance. It looks like luxury. It is actually a trap of gold. This lesson walks into Versailles.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Louis XIV moves the court to Versailles and governs by himself — the palace is not decoration but an instrument: the great nobles live where the king can watch them, and all power radiates from one man. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
