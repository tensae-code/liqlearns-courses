# Lesson plan — Versailles and Absolute Monarchy

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Louis XIV moves the court to Versailles and governs by himself — the palace is not decoration but an instrument: the great nobles live where the king can watch them, and all power radiates from one man.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a head teacher who moves every club, prize and announcement into one hall where she always sits — soon nothing in the school happens except around her chair.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Versailles was just a beautiful palace → it was a tool of government: by keeping the great nobles at court, competing for favour instead of raising armies in their provinces, Louis XIV turned a dangerous aristocracy into courtiers.
- Go back to the one thing: Louis XIV moves the court to Versailles and governs by himself — the palace is not decoration but an instrument: the great nobles live where the king can watch them, and all power radiates from one man. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
