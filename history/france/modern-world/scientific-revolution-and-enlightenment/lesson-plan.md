# Lesson plan — Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Enlightenment, Science and the Cracking Old Regime (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From Galileo's telescope to the Paris salons, a new way of knowing — observe, measure, reason, publish — spreads through society; and Émilie du Châtelet translates Newton and does physics the academies would not let her sign.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — Enlightenment, Science and the Cracking Old Regime — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Enlightenment was only a few famous philosophers → France's book teaches it as a social spread of the scientific spirit: instruments, academies, salons, translations and applied inventions carrying the new way of knowing through society — with women like du Châtelet doing central work from its margins.
- Go back to the one thing: From Galileo's telescope to the Paris salons, a new way of knowing — observe, measure, reason, publish — spreads through society; and Émilie du Châtelet translates Newton and does physics the academies would not let her sign. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
