# Hands-on — Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
An old man who has looked through a telescope is made to kneel and take back what he saw. A woman at a country château translates the greatest physics book in the world — and cannot put her standing behind it as a man could. Between Galileo and Émilie du Châtelet, this lesson tells how a new way of knowing fought its way into the world.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From Galileo's telescope to the Paris salons, a new way of knowing — observe, measure, reason, publish — spreads through society; and Émilie du Châtelet translates Newton and does physics the academies would not let her sign. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
