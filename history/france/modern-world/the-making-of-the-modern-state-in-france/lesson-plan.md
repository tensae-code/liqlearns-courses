# Lesson plan — The Making of the Modern State in France

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Making of the Modern State in France
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The Making of the State: France and the British World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** François Ier's Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts imposes French in law and administration, and a century later Colbert builds the king's finances, factories and fleet into one state machine — step by step, the modern French state takes shape.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The Making of the State: France and the British World — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: François Ier's Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts imposes French in law and administration, and a century later Colbert builds the king's finances, factories and fleet into one state machine — step by step, the modern French state takes shape.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a school that decides every notice, record and rule will be written in one language everyone can read — suddenly the office can run the whole school, and everyone can check what was decided.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the modern state appeared all at once with one king → France's book shows it built in steps across reigns: language and law unified under François Ier, then finances, industry and navy organised as one machine under Colbert for Louis XIV.
- Go back to the one thing: François Ier's Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts imposes French in law and administration, and a century later Colbert builds the king's finances, factories and fleet into one state machine — step by step, the modern French state takes shape. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
