# Hands-on — The Making of the Modern State in France

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A king signs an ordinance about paperwork — every judgement, every contract, every register in the kingdom must now be written in French. Boring? It is one of the most powerful acts a state can perform. This lesson watches France's modern state being built, signature by signature and ledger by ledger.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: François Ier's Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts imposes French in law and administration, and a century later Colbert builds the king's finances, factories and fleet into one state machine — step by step, the modern French state takes shape. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
