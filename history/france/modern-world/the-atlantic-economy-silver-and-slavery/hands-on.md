# Hands-on — The Atlantic Economy: Silver and Slavery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two ships cross the Atlantic in opposite directions. Eastbound: a treasure ship heavy with American silver. Westbound: a ship carrying enslaved Africans in its hold. Between those two ships lies a whole world economy — and this lesson's hard truth.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: American silver floods Europe and remakes its economy, while enslaved Africans are shipped across the Atlantic to mine and plant — and France's own ports, Nantes and Bordeaux, grow rich on that same Atlantic trade. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
