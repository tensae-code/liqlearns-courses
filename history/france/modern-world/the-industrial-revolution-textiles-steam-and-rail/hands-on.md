# Hands-on — The Industrial Revolution: Textiles, Steam and Rail

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A kettle boils, its lid rattling. For a thousand years that was just dinner. Then men of the scientific century looked at the rattling lid and saw the strongest servant humanity had ever had. This lesson meets the steam engine.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The steam engine turns Enlightenment science into industrial power: heat and pressure, studied by the new science, become the machine that will remake work, towns and travel. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
