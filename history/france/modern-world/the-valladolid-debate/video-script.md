# Video script — The Valladolid Debate

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
In 1550, Spain stops to debate whether the peoples of America have souls and rights: Las Casas defends them as fully human, Sepúlveda argues they are natural slaves — and the conscience of an empire is put on trial.

By 1550, reports of the conquest's cruelty had reached Spain itself, and the emperor Charles V ordered something extraordinary: the conquests were paused while learned men debated their justice at Valladolid. The question before the judges cut to the root — what are the peoples of America? Do they have souls? Can they own property, rule themselves, and be converted only by persuasion — or are they, as Aristotle's phrase ran, slaves by nature, fit to be ruled and made to work?

For the second view stood Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda, who argued the peoples of America were inferior beings whose wars and sacrifices proved they needed Spanish masters. Against him stood Bartolomé de Las Casas, the Dominican who had lived in America and spent his life denouncing its destruction: the peoples of America, he argued, are fully human, rational and free; their lands are theirs; faith can be preached, never imposed by the sword. The judges never gave a clean verdict and the conquest rolled on — but the debate remains: the moment an empire's own conscience was argued out loud, and found the conquest wanting.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in France.
