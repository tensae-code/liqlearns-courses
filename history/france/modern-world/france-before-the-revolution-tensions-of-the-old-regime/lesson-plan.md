# Lesson plan — France Before the Revolution: Tensions of the Old Regime

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** France Before the Revolution: Tensions of the Old Regime
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Enlightenment, Science and the Cracking Old Regime (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Tax revolts like the Va-nu-pieds, the rich and poor of Paris side by side, and salons where a new public argues about everything — the Old Regime in the decades before 1789 is cracking under its own weight.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — Enlightenment, Science and the Cracking Old Regime — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The revolt of the Va-nu-pieds; rich and poor in Paris** — Tax revolts and Parisian poverty show an old regime cracking under its own weight.
2. **A salon in the eighteenth century; changes and tensions of the Old Regime** — In the salons a new public argues about everything the old order forbids.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Tax revolts like the Va-nu-pieds, the rich and poor of Paris side by side, and salons where a new public argues about everything — the Old Regime in the decades before 1789 is cracking under its own weight.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: an old bridge everyone still uses: the paint is fine, the view is famous — but the cracks in the pillars widen every year, and the engineers' reports are discussed in every café in town.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
