# Hands-on — American Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The colonists quote England's own laws back at England: no taxation without representation. When the mother country refuses, they do what England did in 1688 — and go one step further: no king at all. This lesson follows Washington and the birth of the American republic.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Britain's American colonists take Britain's own ideas of liberty, rebel against its crown, and under Washington found a republic — the English model crossing the Atlantic and coming back transformed. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
