# Video script — American Independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Britain's American colonists take Britain's own ideas of liberty, rebel against its crown, and under Washington found a republic — the English model crossing the Atlantic and coming back transformed.

The English model did not stay in England. In Britain's thirteen American colonies, settlers raised on English law and English ideas measured their mother country by its own standard — and found it wanting. When London taxed the colonies without their consent, the colonists answered with England's own cry: no taxation without representation. Protests became boycotts, boycotts became war in 1775, and in 1776 the colonies declared independence, grounding it in universal words: all men are created equal, with rights no government may take away.

Winning that war took a leader who could hold a fragile cause together: George Washington, commander of the Continental Army, who kept his outmatched forces alive through defeats and winters until French help and American endurance turned the war, and Britain recognised the United States in 1783. Then came the second founding: a constitution in 1787 making not a monarchy limited by law, but a republic — power from the people, a president instead of a king, Washington its first holder. France's book closes its British-world chapter here: the English model, carried across the Atlantic, came back as the American model — and French readers, soon to make their own revolution, studied both.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in France.
