# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Rome

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Rome has torn itself apart in civil war for a hundred years. Generals fight generals, and everyone is tired of it. Then one young man wins — and does something clever: he takes all the power, but calls himself only the 'first citizen'. This lesson is his story.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Augustus ends Rome's civil wars and founds the principate — one man ruling as 'first citizen' — and Rome stamps its order on the whole Mediterranean world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
