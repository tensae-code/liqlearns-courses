# Ancient Civilisations: Rome

## Do it first

Rome has torn itself apart in civil war for a hundred years. Generals fight generals, and everyone is tired of it. Then one young man wins — and does something clever: he takes all the power, but calls himself only the 'first citizen'. This lesson is his story.

Rome grew from a city on the Tiber into the master of Italy, then of the whole Mediterranean — the Roman footprint: roads, law, cities and one order from Spain to Syria. But the Republic that conquered the sea could not govern its own generals, and the last century of the Republic drowned in civil wars. Out of those wars stepped Octavian, who defeated all his rivals and stood alone by 27 years before our era.

Alone, he took a new name — Augustus — and founded the principate: he held the real power, over the armies and the provinces, but presented himself as princeps, the first citizen, restoring the Republic rather than replacing it. The Senate still met, the old offices still existed — and one man decided. Under Augustus the empire found its shape and its peace, and Rome's order was stamped on the whole Mediterranean world for centuries.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Augustus ends Rome's civil wars and founds the principate — one man ruling as 'first citizen' — and Rome stamps its order on the whole Mediterranean world. Give one real example: a class captain who is chosen to keep order and slowly becomes the one who decides everything — while still insisting he is 'just one of the class', and name the lesson's key words: Augustus, principate, princeps, Rome, empire, Mediterranean order. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
