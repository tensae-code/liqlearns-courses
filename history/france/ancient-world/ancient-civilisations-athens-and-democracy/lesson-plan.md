# Lesson plan — Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Mediterranean in Antiquity (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pericles leads Athens at the height of its democracy, and the city that rules itself also becomes the head of an empire over other Greek cities.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Mediterranean in Antiquity — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Pericles leads Athens at the height of its democracy, and the city that rules itself also becomes the head of an empire over other Greek cities.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a school meeting where the pupils themselves vote on a class rule — and then notice how the confident speakers steer the vote, just as Pericles steered Athens.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks democracy in Athens meant everyone could vote → women, enslaved people and foreigners were shut out; the citizens who voted were free Athenian men only, and Athens' wealth rested partly on the cities it dominated.
- Go back to the one thing: Pericles leads Athens at the height of its democracy, and the city that rules itself also becomes the head of an empire over other Greek cities. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
