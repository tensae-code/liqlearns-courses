# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
An emperor dreams before a battle — so the story is told — and paints a new sign on his soldiers' shields. He wins. Then he does two enormous things: he favours a once-persecuted religion, and he builds a whole new capital at the other end of his empire. Meet Constantine.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Constantine moves Rome's capital east to Constantinople and turns the empire toward Christianity, and the one Roman world begins its long division into East and West. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
