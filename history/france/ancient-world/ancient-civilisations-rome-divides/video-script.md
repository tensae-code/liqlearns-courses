# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Constantine moves Rome's capital east to Constantinople and turns the empire toward Christianity, and the one Roman world begins its long division into East and West.

Three centuries after Augustus, the emperor Constantine made two decisions that changed the Roman world forever. First, he turned the empire toward Christianity: the faith that earlier emperors had persecuted gained his favour and protection, its bishops became men of influence, and over time the empire of the old gods became a Christian empire. Second, he built a new capital in the east — Constantinople, on the straits between Europe and Asia, closer to the rich eastern provinces and the threatened frontiers.

A capital is not just a palace: where the emperor sits, power, money and law gather. With the court's centre of gravity in the east, the empire's two halves — Latin West and Greek East — drifted further apart in language, wealth and fate. Constantine did not sign a division, but he set the turn from which the division came: the West would fall, the East would live on for a thousand years as the Byzantine world of the next level.
