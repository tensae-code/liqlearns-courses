# Lesson plan — Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Mediterranean in Antiquity (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Constantine moves Rome's capital east to Constantinople and turns the empire toward Christianity, and the one Roman world begins its long division into East and West.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Constantine moves Rome's capital east to Constantinople and turns the empire toward Christianity, and the one Roman world begins its long division into East and West.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a family that moves its home to a new town while keeping the old house — after a generation, the two households live differently and the split becomes real.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Constantine split the empire in two at once by a single law → he moved the capital and favoured Christianity; the lasting East–West division grew over the following generations from that eastern turn.
- Go back to the one thing: Constantine moves Rome's capital east to Constantinople and turns the empire toward Christianity, and the one Roman world begins its long division into East and West. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
