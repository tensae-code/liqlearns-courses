# Hands-on — Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A ship leaves a Muslim port carrying spices and silk. It docks in a Christian city, where a merchant sells half the cargo to a trader bound for Constantinople. Enemies on land, partners on the water. This lesson follows the goods — and the ideas — that crossed the sea.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The three worlds of the medieval Mediterranean trade, borrow and fight across the same water — the sea divides the powers, but its routes link them into one exchanging world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
