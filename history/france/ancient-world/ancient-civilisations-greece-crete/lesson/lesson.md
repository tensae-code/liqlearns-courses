# Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

## Do it first

Sail out from Greece in an old wooden ship and keep the coast in sight. At every good harbour — in Italy, in Sicily, on the shores of France and Spain and across to Asia — you find the same thing: a Greek city, speaking Greek, building Greek temples. This lesson follows that footprint.

The Greeks lived in city-states — small, proud, independent cities — and when a city grew crowded or hungry for trade, it sent out ships to found a new city on a distant shore. From the coasts of Italy and Sicily to the shore where Marseille now stands in France, and eastward to Asia Minor, Greek cities came to ring the whole Mediterranean like, in the old image, frogs around a pond.

Wherever they settled, the Greeks left a footprint that outlasted their ships: the Greek language, spoken and written around the sea; Greek art and temples, whose columns still stand; and the Greek idea of the polis — a city run by its citizens. Crete and the islands were stepping stones of that world. Long after the city-states themselves fell, the footprint remained, and Rome — the subject of the next lessons — would walk in it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Greek cities spread all around the Mediterranean Sea and leave their language, their art and their way of running a city as a footprint on every shore they touch. Give one real example: a word from the learner's own language that travelled from somewhere else — like Greek words for theatre, school and democracy that still travel the world today, and name the lesson's key words: Greek cities, colonies, Mediterranean, language, art, polis, footprint. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
