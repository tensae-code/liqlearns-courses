# Medieval World: Byzantium and Justinian

## Do it first

Stand on a hill above the Mediterranean in the year 1000 and look three ways. East: a Roman empire still standing, Greek and Christian, ruled from Constantinople. South and east: a vast Muslim world of caliphs and scholars. West: Christian kingdoms and an emperor of their own. Three powers, one sea. This lesson sets them face to face.

When France's book crosses into the medieval Mediterranean, it asks first about the forms of power — and finds three of them facing each other around the same water. In the east stands the Byzantine Empire: the Roman Empire still alive, Greek in language, Christian in faith, ruled by an emperor in Constantinople who saw himself as God's deputy on earth. Its great early emperor Justinian had gathered Roman law into one code and raised the vast dome of Hagia Sophia; his empire guarded the sea's eastern shore for centuries.

Across the southern and eastern shores stands the Muslim world, born in Arabia in the seventh century and grown with astonishing speed into caliphates stretching from Spain to Persia — a world of great cities, scholars and merchants, ruled in the name of Islam. And in the west stands the Christian West of kings and popes, poorer and more divided, where Charlemagne's heirs and the Church of Rome claimed their own universal order. Three powers, three faiths' orders, one sea between them: that triangle is the stage for everything in this level — the trade, the borrowing and the wars.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Around the medieval Mediterranean, three powers face each other across one sea — the Byzantine Empire, the Muslim world and the Christian West — and each claims to hold the true order of the world. Give one real example: three strong families on one street, each sure its own way of running a house is the right one — trading with each other, borrowing from each other, and quarrelling at the fences, and name the lesson's key words: Byzantium, Justinian, Constantinople, Muslim world, Christian West, three powers. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
