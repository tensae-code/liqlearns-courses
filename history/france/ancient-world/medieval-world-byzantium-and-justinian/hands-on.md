# Hands-on — Medieval World: Byzantium and Justinian

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand on a hill above the Mediterranean in the year 1000 and look three ways. East: a Roman empire still standing, Greek and Christian, ruled from Constantinople. South and east: a vast Muslim world of caliphs and scholars. West: Christian kingdoms and an emperor of their own. Three powers, one sea. This lesson sets them face to face.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Around the medieval Mediterranean, three powers face each other across one sea — the Byzantine Empire, the Muslim world and the Christian West — and each claims to hold the true order of the world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
