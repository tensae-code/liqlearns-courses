**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and the Reformations (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** An Aztec drawing of the sixteenth century shows an empire at the moment it meets the men who will destroy it — Tenochtitlan, a great city on a lake, seen through its own people's eyes.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: An Aztec drawing of the sixteenth century shows an empire at the moment it meets the men who will destroy it — Tenochtitlan, a great city on a lake, seen through its own people's eyes.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a photograph of your own town taken the year before it changed completely — everything in it is ordinary to the people inside it, and precious because of what came next.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Aztecs were a small tribe the Spanish stumbled on → they were an empire: Tenochtitlan, their capital on Lake Texcoco, was one of the largest cities in the world, ruling millions through tribute and war.
- Go back to the one thing: An Aztec drawing of the sixteenth century shows an empire at the moment it meets the men who will destroy it — Tenochtitlan, a great city on a lake, seen through its own people's eyes. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
