# Video script — The Aztecs: Tenochtitlan

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
An Aztec drawing of the sixteenth century shows an empire at the moment it meets the men who will destroy it — Tenochtitlan, a great city on a lake, seen through its own people's eyes.

France's book studies the notion of empire through a single image: an Aztec drawing of the sixteenth century. Behind the drawing stands Tenochtitlan, the Aztec capital — a city built on an island in Lake Texcoco, crossed by canals and causeways, crowned by the great temple pyramid, and home to more people than almost any city in Europe. From it, Aztec emperors ruled an empire of millions, held together by war, tribute and fear, rich in gold, feathers and food from floating gardens.

The drawing was made in the very years when that world met its destroyers: Cortés and the Spanish had landed in 1519, and within two years Tenochtitlan fell, besieged, starved and ruined. The drawing matters because it is the empire's own testimony — how Aztec artists saw their order, their ruler and their gods — set beside the conquerors' accounts. An empire, this lesson shows, is not only armies and tribute; it is also a picture of the world, and the Aztecs' picture survives even though their state did not.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in France.
