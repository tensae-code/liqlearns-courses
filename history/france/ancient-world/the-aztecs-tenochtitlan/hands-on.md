# Hands-on — The Aztecs: Tenochtitlan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at a picture drawn by Aztec artists: their emperor, their city on the lake, their gods and warriors — and at the edge of the page, strangers in steel. This lesson reads that drawing, and meets the empire it shows, at the last moment of its world.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: An Aztec drawing of the sixteenth century shows an empire at the moment it meets the men who will destroy it — Tenochtitlan, a great city on a lake, seen through its own people's eyes. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
