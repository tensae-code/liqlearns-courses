## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Bernard of Clairvaux preaches a crusade, and knights sail east to carve out Latin states in the Levant — Christian kingdoms planted on a foreign shore, always fragile, always needing the sea.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a neighbourhood team that wins a ground far from home and then has to keep it, season after season, with every player having to travel a long way to help.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the Latin states were solid, lasting kingdoms → they were fragile coastal states, short of men, dependent on reinforcements and supplies arriving by sea, and they lasted only as long as that lifeline held.
- Go back to the one thing: Bernard of Clairvaux preaches a crusade, and knights sail east to carve out Latin states in the Levant — Christian kingdoms planted on a foreign shore, always fragile, always needing the sea. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
