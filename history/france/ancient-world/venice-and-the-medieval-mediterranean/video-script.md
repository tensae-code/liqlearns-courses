# Video script — Venice and the Medieval Mediterranean

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Venice, a city built on water in a lagoon, grows rich and powerful by commanding the sea's trade routes — its ships, its merchants and its diplomacy make it the Mediterranean's great middleman.

Venice began as a refuge: people fleeing invaders on the mainland settled the muddy islands of a lagoon, building on forests of wooden piles driven into the water. A city with no fields and no roads had only one way to live — the sea. Venetians became sailors, shipbuilders and merchants, and their lagoon, safe from armies, became the hinge between the Byzantine East and the Christian West.

From that hinge, Venice commanded routes: its fleets carried crusaders and cargo alike, its merchants won trading privileges in Constantinople and the ports of the Levant, and Eastern luxuries — spices, silk, glass materials — passed through Venetian warehouses toward all Europe. The city on water grew rich as the sea's middleman, proof of this level's lesson: in the medieval Mediterranean, power belonged to whoever commanded the routes across the water.
