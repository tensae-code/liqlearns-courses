# Lesson plan — Venice and the Medieval Mediterranean

**Course:** History — France · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Venice and the Medieval Mediterranean
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Medieval Mediterranean: Three Worlds Around One Sea (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Venice, a city built on water in a lagoon, grows rich and powerful by commanding the sea's trade routes — its ships, its merchants and its diplomacy make it the Mediterranean's great middleman.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The Medieval Mediterranean: Three Worlds Around One Sea — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Venice, a city built on water in a lagoon, grows rich and powerful by commanding the sea's trade routes — its ships, its merchants and its diplomacy make it the Mediterranean's great middleman.
2. Give one real example of it from their own life: a market stall placed exactly where every road in town crosses — the stallholder grows rich not by making things but by standing where all trade must pass.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks Venice grew rich from farming rich land → Venice had almost no farmland; its wealth came from the sea — ships, trade routes, and carrying other people's goods between East and West.
- Go back to the one thing: Venice, a city built on water in a lagoon, grows rich and powerful by commanding the sea's trade routes — its ships, its merchants and its diplomacy make it the Mediterranean's great middleman. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
