# About the History — France course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Seconde *Histoire 2de*. The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the book's.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for France inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **five levels**, following the one
read book's own order — its four themes, with the first
theme's two chapters taken as two levels, as filed:

1. **Level 1 — The Mediterranean in Antiquity.** Athens and
   Pericles, the Greek footprint, Augustus's principate, and
   Constantine's eastern, Christian turn (Thème 1, La
   Méditerranée antique).
2. **Level 2 — The Medieval Mediterranean: Three Worlds
   Around One Sea.** The three powers, their exchanges and
   conflicts, Venice, and the Crusades' Latin East (Thème 1,
   La Méditerranée médiévale).
3. **Level 3 — The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and
   the Reformations.** The 'discovery' on trial, the Aztecs,
   the silver-and-slavery world-economy, Valladolid,
   Erasmus and Michelangelo, Luther and the break with
   Erasmus (Thème 2).
4. **Level 4 — The Making of the State: France and the
   British World.** Villers-Cotterêts and Colbert, the Edict
   of Nantes given and revoked, Versailles — and England's
   constitutional revolutions, Voltaire's praise nuanced,
   and American independence (Thème 3).
5. **Level 5 — Enlightenment, Science and the Cracking Old
   Regime.** The scientific spirit's diffusion, Émilie du
   Châtelet, the steam engine — then the Va-nu-pieds, rich
   and poor in Paris, and the salons (Thème 4).

All 22 lessons stand on a **student book** and count in the
acceptance reckoning below; no lesson in this course stands
on teacher-guide evidence.

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Book 2, the Seconde student manual** — France's own way,
as filed: the Seconde year is built as four themes that keep
returning to France's own story inside the world's — the
Mediterranean inheritance, the Atlantic opening, the making
of the French and English states, and the tensions that will
explode in 1789 — taught through document studies,
interactive quizzes and "question problématisée" essays
rather than narrative chapters. This course preserves that
shape: its levels are the book's themes, its lessons follow

France's own way, as filed.)*
