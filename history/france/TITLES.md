# History — France course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is France's
own — built from France's Seconde *Histoire 2de — Manuel
Libre d'Histoire, Seconde* (Les Manuels Libres, French,
COMPLETE), a year built as four themes that keep returning
to France's own story inside the world's — the Mediterranean
inheritance, the Atlantic opening, the making of the French
and English states, and the tensions that will explode in
1789. Inside the course the organisation is by **levels,
not grades**: the titles below sit inside Levels 1–5, in
path order — what the learner proves, step by step. The
grade the book comes from lives only in each lesson's
sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: the ancient Mediterranean
first, then the medieval sea of three worlds; then the
Atlantic opening with the Renaissance and the Reformations;
then the making of the state, in France and in the British
world; and last the Enlightenment, the sciences and the
cracking Old Regime. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — The Mediterranean in Antiquity** (Thème 1, La Méditerranée antique), in `ancient-world/`:

- Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy (Pericles leads Athens at its democratic height and turns a city into an empire) · Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete (Greek cities ring the Mediterranean and leave language, art and politics as their footprint) · Ancient Civilisations: Rome (Augustus founds the principate and Rome stamps its order on the whole sea) · Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides (Constantine moves the capital east and turns the empire toward Christianity)

**Level 2 — The Medieval Mediterranean: Three Worlds Around One Sea** (Thème 1, La Méditerranée médiévale), in `ancient-world/`:

- Medieval World: Byzantium and Justinian (three powers face each other around one sea — Byzantine empire, Muslim world, Christian West) · Trade Routes That Linked the Old World (the three worlds trade, borrow and fight across the same water) · Venice and the Medieval Mediterranean (a city built on water grows rich commanding the sea's trade routes) · The Crusades and the Latin East (Bernard preaches a crusade and knights sail east to found fragile Latin states)

**Level 3 — The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and the Reformations** (Thème 2), in `modern-world/` and `ancient-world/`:

- Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan (Europeans 'discover' lands other peoples already call home, and the word itself is put on trial) · The Aztecs: Tenochtitlan (an Aztec drawing shows an empire meeting the men who will destroy it) · The Atlantic Economy: Silver and Slavery (American silver floods Europe while enslaved Africans are shipped to mine and plant — and Nantes and Bordeaux grow rich on the same trade (two parts, filed under Thème 2 and Thème 4, stocked in file order)) · The Valladolid Debate (in 1550 Spain debates whether the peoples of America have souls and rights — Las Casas against Sepúlveda) · The Renaissance: Its Great Figures (Erasmus, Michelangelo and a new figure — the artist — remake Europe's mind) · The Reformation: Luther and Protestantism (Luther's theses crack the Western Church — on ground the humanists prepared, before Erasmus and Luther break over free will (two parts))

**Level 4 — The Making of the State: France and the British World** (Thème 3), in `modern-world/`:

- The Making of the Modern State in France (François Ier's ordinance imposes French in law — and Colbert builds finances, factories and fleet into one state machine (two parts)) · The Edict of Nantes and Its Revocation (Henri IV grants Protestants peace in 1598; Louis XIV tears it up in 1685) · Versailles and Absolute Monarchy (Louis XIV moves the court to Versailles and rules France from one palace) · England's Constitutional Revolutions (England tries its king, then binds the crown with habeas corpus and a bill of rights — and Voltaire praises English liberty to shame France, while the book asks how free England really was (two parts)) · American Independence (Britain's colonists take its own ideas, rebel, and Washington founds a republic)

**Level 5 — Enlightenment, Science and the Cracking Old Regime** (Thème 4), in `modern-world/`:

- Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment (from Galileo's telescope to the salons a new way of knowing spreads — and Émilie du Châtelet translates Newton from the academies' margins (two parts)) · The Industrial Revolution: Textiles, Steam and Rail (steam turns Enlightenment science into industrial power) · France Before the Revolution: Tensions of the Old Regime (tax revolts and Parisian poverty show an old regime cracking — and in the salons a new public argues about everything it forbids (two parts))
