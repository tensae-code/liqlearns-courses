# History — France course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for France inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** France's read set in hand is one
book — its Grade 10 (Seconde) *Histoire 2de — Manuel Libre
d'Histoire, Seconde* (Les Manuels Libres, French, COMPLETE),
an HTML manual built as four themes that keep returning to
France's own story inside the world's. So the ladder follows
the book's own order — the placements file's book order is
the teaching order — grouped the way its story divides: its
first theme is itself two chapters, so it makes two levels
(the ancient Mediterranean, then the medieval Mediterranean
of three worlds); then one level per theme — the Atlantic
opening with the Renaissance and the Reformations; the
making of the state in France and the British world; and
last the Enlightenment, the sciences and the tensions that
will explode in 1789. The ladder stops where the book in
hand stops: the manual's year ends on the eve of the
Revolution, and the record set's only other item, the
*Géographie 2de GT* manual, is RECORD ONLY — pure Geography,
not placed (see ABOUT.md).

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Seconde *Histoire 2de* — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the book's. See ABOUT.md for the full
book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **The Mediterranean in Antiquity** — France's book opens
   on the sea its own south coast faces. Athens first:
   Pericles leads the democracy at its height while the city
   turns its league into an empire. Then the Greek footprint
   — cities ringing the whole Mediterranean, leaving
   language, art and the citizen-run city on every shore.
   Then Rome: Augustus founds the principate and stamps
   Rome's order on the whole sea; and Constantine turns the
   empire east and Christian, setting its long division in
   motion. 4 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/`
   (4 lessons).

2. **The Medieval Mediterranean: Three Worlds Around One
   Sea** — the same sea, a thousand years on, with three
   powers facing each other across it: the Byzantine Empire
   of Justinian's line, the Muslim world, and the Christian
   West. The three worlds trade, borrow and fight across the
   same water; Venice grows rich commanding the routes; and
   Bernard of Clairvaux preaches a crusade that plants the
   fragile Latin states on the Levant coast. 4 lessons, in
   one title: `ancient-world/` (4 lessons).

3. **The Atlantic Opening, the Renaissance and the
   Reformations** — the sea of the story widens to an ocean.
   The 'discoveries' are put on trial as a word; an Aztec
   drawing shows Tenochtitlan's empire meeting its
   destroyers; American silver builds a world-economy on
   enslaved African labour (a lesson whose second part,
   France's own ports, is filed by the book under its fourth
   theme and stocked here with its first, in file order);
   Spain debates the humanity of America's peoples at
   Valladolid; Erasmus and Michelangelo remake Europe's
   mind; and Luther's theses crack the Western Church, on
   ground the humanists had prepared. 6 lessons, in two
   titles: `modern-world/` (5 lessons) and `ancient-world/`
   (1 lesson — the Aztecs, as filed).

4. **The Making of the State: France and the British
   World** — the book turns home. François Ier's Ordinance
   of Villers-Cotterêts imposes French in law and the modern
   state takes shape; Henri IV's Edict of Nantes grants the
   Protestants peace, and Louis XIV tears it up; Colbert
   builds finances, factories and fleet into one machine;
   and Louis XIV governs by himself from Versailles. Across
   the Channel, the counter-model: England tries its king
   and binds the crown with habeas corpus and a Bill of
   Rights — Voltaire's praise, and the book's nuance — and
   Britain's colonists carry the model across the Atlantic
   and found a republic under Washington. 5 lessons, in one
   title: `modern-world/` (5 lessons).

5. **Enlightenment, Science and the Cracking Old Regime** —
   the last theme doubles back on France itself. The
   scientific spirit spreads from Galileo's telescope
   through academies and salons; Émilie du Châtelet
   translates Newton from the academies' margins; and the
   steam engine turns the new science into industrial power.
   Then the tensions: the barefoot Va-nu-pieds against the
   salt tax, rich and poor in one Paris street, and the
   salons where a new public argues about everything the old
   order forbids. The course ends where the book ends — on
   the eve of 1789, with the cracks showing. 3 lessons, in
   one title: `modern-world/` (3 lessons).

**Totals: 22 lessons** (Level 1: 4 · Level 2: 4 · Level 3:
6 · Level 4: 5 · Level 5: 3 — 28 placements, all from the
one Seconde student book: 15 filed to existing lessons, 13
filed NEW LESSON NEEDED, as filed).
