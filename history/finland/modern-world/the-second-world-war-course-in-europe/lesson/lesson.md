# The Second World War Course in Europe

## Do it first

Write the 'separate war' claim in one sentence, then the strongest single piece of photographic evidence against it from this lesson.

Finland has a comforting sentence about its war: it was a separate war, fought beside Germany but not with her. This lesson's book does something brave with that sentence — it sets the birthday photographs against it. Hitler received as an honoured guest at the Marshal's birthday, the courtesies, the closeness: read as evidence, the photographs press hard on the claim, and the claim cracks. The lesson belongs to Finland's wider Second World War course in Europe because it teaches the national story the way historians must: a country's own favourite sentence, tested against its own archive.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Finland long called its Continuation War a separate war apart from Germany's — and its own schoolbook sets the birthday-visit photographs against that claim, and the claim cracks. Give one real example: The courtesies of Hitler's 1942 visit to Mannerheim's birthday are read as evidence: a 'separate war' does not receive such guests in such a way. and name the lesson's key words: Separate war, Evidence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
