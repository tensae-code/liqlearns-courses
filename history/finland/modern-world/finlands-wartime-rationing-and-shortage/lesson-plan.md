# Lesson plan — Finland's Wartime Rationing and Shortage

**Course:** History — Finland · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Finland's Wartime Rationing and Shortage

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **introduction: the Ministry of Public Provision and the ration-card system** — The system comes first.
2. **Task 1, Rantasalmi letter 1942** — From Rantasalmi in 1942, a pregnant evacuee mother of three writes: her husband is at the front, and her family's cards — the B, G2 and M2 classes — do not feed them.
3. **Task 2, Ivalo letter 1942** — From Ivalo in Lapland in 1942 comes an accusation.
4. **Task 3, Tohmajärvi letter 1943** — From Tohmajärvi in 1943, the problem is potatoes and shoes.
5. **Task 4, Pietarsaari letter 1943** — From Pietarsaari in 1943, the workers' complaint is about timing.
6. **Task 5, Helsinki letter 1942** — From Helsinki in 1942 comes the letter that jokes.
7. **Task 6, Kauhava letter 1942** — From Kauhava in 1942, the last letter is a smallholder wife's reckoning.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks rationing is just queues → It is a whole system of fairness argued case by case: card classes, appeals, delivery quotas — and letters that test whether the rules are just.
