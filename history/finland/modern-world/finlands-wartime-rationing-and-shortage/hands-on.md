# Hands-on — Finland's Wartime Rationing and Shortage

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Plan one day of meals for a family using only rationed amounts: write the menu, then the one item whose shortage would hurt most, and why.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: When foreign trade stops and men and fields are lost to the war, a new ministry rations sugar and coffee first, then nearly all food and clothes by ration cards classed by heaviness of work — and ordinary families argue their cases by letter. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
