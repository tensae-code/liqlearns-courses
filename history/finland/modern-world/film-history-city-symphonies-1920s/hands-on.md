# Hands-on — Film History: City Symphonies, 1920s

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Plan a one-day symphony of your own street: list five shots from dawn to dark that would make your street the hero of a film.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In the 1920s filmmakers turn the city itself into the hero: Strand, Ruttmann and Vertov film Manhattan, Berlin and a man with a movie camera as visual music. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
