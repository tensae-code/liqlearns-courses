# Video script — Film History: City Symphonies, 1920s

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
In the 1920s filmmakers turn the city itself into the hero: Strand, Ruttmann and Vertov film Manhattan, Berlin and a man with a movie camera as visual music.

Some 1920s filmmakers give the hero's part to a city. Paul Strand films Manhattan's towers and crowds; Walter Ruttmann sets Berlin's day to a rhythm, a symphony of the big city from dawn trains to midnight lights; and Dziga Vertov's Man with a Movie Camera follows his own cameraman through a Soviet city that wakes, works and plays — the camera itself dancing inside the film. No single character carries these films. Streets, trams, machines and crowds play together like instruments, and the city performs its own music.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
