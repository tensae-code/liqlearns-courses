# Film History: City Symphonies, 1920s

## Do it first

Plan a one-day symphony of your own street: list five shots from dawn to dark that would make your street the hero of a film.

Some 1920s filmmakers give the hero's part to a city. Paul Strand films Manhattan's towers and crowds; Walter Ruttmann sets Berlin's day to a rhythm, a symphony of the big city from dawn trains to midnight lights; and Dziga Vertov's Man with a Movie Camera follows his own cameraman through a Soviet city that wakes, works and plays — the camera itself dancing inside the film. No single character carries these films. Streets, trams, machines and crowds play together like instruments, and the city performs its own music.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the 1920s filmmakers turn the city itself into the hero: Strand, Ruttmann and Vertov film Manhattan, Berlin and a man with a movie camera as visual music. Give one real example: Vertov's cameraman rides above the waking city, filming it filming itself — the city performs its own symphony from dawn to night. and name the lesson's key words: City symphony, Vertov. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
