# Video script — Film History: The Birth of Cinema — Lumière, 1895

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. ---

This lesson tells one story.
On 28 December 1895 the Lumière brothers light their cinematograph in a Paris café: workers leave a factory, a train seems to rush at the audience — and framing, the choice of what the picture holds, is born.

On 28 December 1895, in a Paris café, the Lumière brothers light their cinematograph — camera, printer and projector in one wooden box — and cinema is born in public. The first films are minutes of real life: workers streaming out of the Lumière factory; a train rushing into a station so directly that the audience flinches. But the book's deeper point is quieter: from this first show, someone must choose what the picture holds and what it leaves out. Framing — rajaus, representation — is born with the train, and every film since is made inside that choice.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
