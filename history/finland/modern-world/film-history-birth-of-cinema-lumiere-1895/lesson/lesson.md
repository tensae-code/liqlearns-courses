# Film History: The Birth of Cinema — Lumière, 1895

## Do it first

Frame a scene in your home with your hands: choose what is inside the frame and what you leave out. Write why your choice changes the story.

On 28 December 1895, in a Paris café, the Lumière brothers light their cinematograph — camera, printer and projector in one wooden box — and cinema is born in public. The first films are minutes of real life: workers streaming out of the Lumière factory; a train rushing into a station so directly that the audience flinches. But the book's deeper point is quieter: from this first show, someone must choose what the picture holds and what it leaves out. Framing — rajaus, representation — is born with the train, and every film since is made inside that choice.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: On 28 December 1895 the Lumière brothers light their cinematograph in a Paris café: workers leave a factory, a train seems to rush at the audience — and framing, the choice of what the picture holds, is born. Give one real example: The arriving train makes café viewers flinch — a flat picture has learned to charge straight at its audience. and name the lesson's key words: Cinematograph, Framing. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
