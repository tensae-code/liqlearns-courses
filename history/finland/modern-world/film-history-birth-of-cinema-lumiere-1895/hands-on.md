# Hands-on — Film History: The Birth of Cinema — Lumière, 1895

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Frame a scene in your home with your hands: choose what is inside the frame and what you leave out. Write why your choice changes the story.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: On 28 December 1895 the Lumière brothers light their cinematograph in a Paris café: workers leave a factory, a train seems to rush at the audience — and framing, the choice of what the picture holds, is born. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
