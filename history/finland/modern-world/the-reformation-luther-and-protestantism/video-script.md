# Video script — The Reformation: Luther and Protestantism

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The Reformation splits the North's church: Olaus Magnus draws his richly pictured map in exile in Italy hoping the Pope will win the newly Lutheran Nordic kingdoms back.

Why is the map so full of pictures? Because it is an argument. Olaus Magnus drew his North in exile in Italy, after the Reformation had made Sweden Lutheran and driven out its Catholic archbishop — Olaus's own brother Johannes, the last Catholic to hold that seat. The richly pictured map is an advertisement for the lost Catholic North, made to move the Pope: win these kingdoms back, restore the archbishop. Every reindeer, sea monster and Lappish hunter on the sheet is doing persuasion's work. The Reformation, in this lesson, is not only sermons and kings — it is a map drawn as a plea.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
