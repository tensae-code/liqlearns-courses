# The Reformation: Luther and Protestantism

## Do it first

Write one sentence for each side: why a Lutheran king might banish a Catholic archbishop, and why that archbishop might answer with a map.

Why is the map so full of pictures? Because it is an argument. Olaus Magnus drew his North in exile in Italy, after the Reformation had made Sweden Lutheran and driven out its Catholic archbishop — Olaus's own brother Johannes, the last Catholic to hold that seat. The richly pictured map is an advertisement for the lost Catholic North, made to move the Pope: win these kingdoms back, restore the archbishop. Every reindeer, sea monster and Lappish hunter on the sheet is doing persuasion's work. The Reformation, in this lesson, is not only sermons and kings — it is a map drawn as a plea.

## Say it to your companion

Give one real example: The map itself is the mission: every picture of northern life is an advertisement meant to move the Pope to restore Sweden's last Catholic archbishop. and name the lesson's key words: Reformation, Olaus Magnus. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
