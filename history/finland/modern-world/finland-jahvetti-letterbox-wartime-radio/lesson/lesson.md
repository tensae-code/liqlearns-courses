# Finland's Jahvetti Letterbox: Wartime Radio

## Do it first

Write the letter you would have sent Jahvetti in 1943: one trouble, plainly told, in half a page. Then write the two-sentence answer the programme owed you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Johdanto: the Jahvetin kirjelaatikko programme and its archive

The archive first. Through Finland's war years, ordinary people in trouble write letters — not to a newspaper, but to a radio programme: Jahvetin kirjelaatikko, Jahvetti's letterbox. Some 100,000 letters arrive over the war, and more than 70,000 of them survive, filling eighteen shelf-metres in the National Archives. For pupils they are anonymised — names protected — and offered whole: to be read, heard and questioned. It is the home front's own handwriting, preserved by accident of a radio show, and it is one of Finland's great sources for the war.

## Sub lesson 2 — Jahvetin kirjelaatikko (the programme, 1940–1945)

The voice that answers is Jahvetti — beloved, familiar, trusted — and secretly a performance: behind the name stands teacher and writer Yrjö Kilpeläinen. Twice a week the programme answers citizens on air. But the office behind it belongs to the State Information Office, and it has a second task: to sound the nation's mood from the letters and to fight rumours and Soviet propaganda. The book calls it a one-man front in Finland's spiritual national defence — a radio voice doing the work of morale itself.
