# Hands-on — Finland's Jahvetti Letterbox: Wartime Radio

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the letter you would have sent Jahvetti in 1943: one trouble, plainly told, in half a page. Then write the two-sentence answer the programme owed you.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Through the war years some 100,000 Finns write their troubles to a radio letterbox; the beloved voice Jahvetti — secretly teacher and writer Yrjö Kilpeläinen — answers on air, while over 70,000 letters survive in the National Archives. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
