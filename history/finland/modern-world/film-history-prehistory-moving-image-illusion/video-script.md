# Video script — Film History: Prehistory — The Moving-Image Illusion

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before film exists, inventors make still pictures seem to move: thaumatropes, phenakistoscopes and zoetropes spin, and Reynaud's praxinoscope theatre projects the illusion for an audience (1825–1895).

Cinema's story starts half a century before cinema. Between 1825 and 1895, inventors across Europe play with a discovery about the eye: show it still pictures fast enough, in the right order, and it sees movement that is not there. They spin thaumatropes — a bird on one side of a card, a cage on the other, one picture when it twirls. They build phenakistoscopes and zoetropes, drums and discs whose slits let dancing figures come alive. And Émile Reynaud goes furthest: his praxinoscope theatre projects the moving illusion for a paying audience. The pictures have learned to move; only the photograph has not yet joined them.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
