# Hands-on — Film History: Prehistory — The Moving-Image Illusion

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a bird on one side of a card and a cage on the other. Spin the card fast on two strings and watch them join — write what your eye did.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before film exists, inventors make still pictures seem to move: thaumatropes, phenakistoscopes and zoetropes spin, and Reynaud's praxinoscope theatre projects the illusion for an audience (1825–1895). Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
