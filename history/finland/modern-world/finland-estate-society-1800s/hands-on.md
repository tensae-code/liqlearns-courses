# Hands-on — Finland's Estate Society in the 1800s

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw the seven rungs of the 1800s ladder from noble to landless lodger. Write your own family's place if you had lived then, and one thing that place would have decided for you.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In 1800s Finland your birth estate decides your life before you can walk: nobles, clergy, burghers and peasants stand ranked above crofters, servants and the landless — and pupils learn it by living one role. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
