# Film History: Méliès and the Attraction Cinema

## Do it first

Film or act a tiny disappearance: stop, move one object while nothing records, start again. Write what the stop trick did to the scene.

Georges Méliès is a stage magician who buys a camera — and then the camera jams. When the film runs again, the street has jumped: a bus has become a hearse, people have vanished and appeared. Another man would curse the machine; Méliès builds an art on the accident. His studio becomes a magic box where the stop trick makes anything transform, appear or fly — and in 1902 his travellers are shot to the Moon in a rocket, landing in its eye. This is attraction cinema: the film does not pretend to be life; it performs wonders and dares you not to stare.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Stage magician Georges Méliès turns the camera into a magic box: a jammed film teaches him the stop trick, and in 1902 he sails his travellers to the Moon. Give one real example: A camera jam of one second shows a bus become a hearse — Méliès sees the accident and builds a career of wonders on it. and name the lesson's key words: Stop trick, Attraction cinema. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
