# Film History: German Expressionism, 1920s

## Do it first

Draw your room twice: once as it is, once as it would look if the drawing had to show fear. Write which lines you bent, and why.

In post-war Germany, filmmakers paint fear. The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari unfolds in streets that lean at impossible angles, with shadows painted onto the walls — the world drawn as a disturbed mind sees it. Murnau's Nosferatu stretches a vampire's shadow across a wall until the shadow is the horror. Lang's Metropolis raises a shining machine-city over the workers' depths below. The book's word for the craft is mise-en-scène: everything placed inside the picture — sets, light, shadow, costume — arranged until a character's inner state becomes the picture itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Expressionist film paints a character's inner fear onto the picture itself: distorted sets and hard shadow carry Caligari, Nosferatu and Metropolis. Give one real example: In Caligari the streets lean and the shadows are painted on — the world is drawn the way a frightened mind feels it. and name the lesson's key words: Expressionism, Mise-en-scène. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
