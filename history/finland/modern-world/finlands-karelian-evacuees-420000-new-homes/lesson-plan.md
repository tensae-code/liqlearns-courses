# Lesson plan — Finland's Karelian Evacuees: 420,000 New Homes

**Course:** History — Finland · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Finland's Karelian Evacuees: 420,000 New Homes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Finland at War: Letters, Rationing and the Home Front (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Finland cedes about a tenth of its land, and 420,000 Karelians and others must rebuild their lives elsewhere: a quick-settlement law shares out land, but housing is desperate and welcome is uneven — told through the evacuees' own letters.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — Finland at War: Letters, Rationing and the Home Front (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Teema 2 background: 420,000 people leave home** — The background is a wound in the map.
2. **Tehtävä 1 (yläkoulu): letter from Säkylä, 26 Feb 1941** — The first letter is a soldier's.
3. **Tehtävä 2 (yläkoulu): letter from Rovaniemi, 27 Feb 1941** — The second letter asks about the gift houses.
4. **Tehtävä 3 (yläkoulu): letter from Suonenjoki, 20 Sep 1941** — The third letter is about rent.
5. **Tehtävä 4 (lukio): letter from Oulu, 21 Apr 1941** — The fourth letter comes from Oulu, 21 April 1941, and is set for the older pupils.
6. **Tehtävä 5 (lukio): letters from Säkylä (1941) and Reposaari, 17 Oct 1944, with the Laukko crofter eviction of 1906–07 as comparison** — The fifth task holds two letters — from Säkylä in 1941 and Reposaari on 17 October 1944 — and sets a mirror beside them: the Laukko crofter evictions of 1906–07, when crofter families were bundled out into the frost a generation earlier.
7. **Tehtävä 6 (lukio): a 1944 letter and Jahvetti's office's reply (Kauhava letter)** — The last letter is a wife's.
8. **introduction: cession and resettlement** — The same book, filed again in the second Finland filing, opens with the cession itself: a tenth of Finland's land given up, 420,000 people on the road, the rapid-resettlement law of 1940 buying land for the displaced — and housing still desperate, friction still following the newcomers into their new villages.
9. **Task 1, Säkylä letter 1941** — In this filing the Säkylä letter is read again, and its sting is named: locals call the Karelians by the old ethnic slur for Russians — the very enemy the Karelian veteran fought.
10. **Task 2, Rovaniemi letter 1941** — The Rovaniemi letter, in this filing, sharpens its question about the Swedish gift houses: fairy-tale-expensive, the writer calls them.
11. **Task 3, Suonenjoki letter 1941** — The Suonenjoki letter, in this filing, adds the mechanism: the landlord blames his cession tax for the raised rent on the single stove-room, and no rent board exists anywhere to appeal to.
12. **Task 4, Oulu letter 1941** — The Oulu letter, in this filing, gives the figures their full edge: the reservist from Pitkäranta is refused an advance of 3,000 marks against his 34,000-mark compensation claim.
13. **Task 5, Säkylä repeated and Reposaari 1944 letters** — The fifth task, in this filing, joins the Säkylä letter repeated with the Reposaari letter of 1944: a family dumped into a kitchen corner while rooms stand empty.
14. **Task 6, 1944 letter and reply** — The sixth task, in this filing, returns to the 1944 wife's plea and the office's refusal, now read with the summer's offensive burning behind it: as the front fights to hold, leave is refused, and the resettlement story is shown from inside the women's war — the evacuation carried, in large part, by wives holding new homes together alone.
15. **Task 3, the evacuee photographs** — The evacuee photographs close the lesson.
