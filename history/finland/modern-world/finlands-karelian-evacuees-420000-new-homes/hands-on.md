# Hands-on — Finland's Karelian Evacuees: 420,000 New Homes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine your family must leave home with one day to pack. Write the five things you would carry, and the first question you would ask the strangers whose village receives you.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Finland cedes about a tenth of its land, and 420,000 Karelians and others must rebuild their lives elsewhere: a quick-settlement law shares out land, but housing is desperate and welcome is uneven — told through the evacuees' own letters. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
