# Hands-on — Film History: Soviet Montage, 1920s

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw one neutral face three times. Beside each, draw a different scene — a meal, a storm, a gift — and write what the face now seems to feel each time.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Montage means 1+1 is more than 2: Kuleshov shows one face reading as hunger, grief or desire beside different shots, and Eisenstein's Battleship Potemkin turns cutting into meaning itself. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
