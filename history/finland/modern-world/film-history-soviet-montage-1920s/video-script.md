---

This lesson tells one story.
Montage means 1+1 is more than 2: Kuleshov shows one face reading as hunger, grief or desire beside different shots, and Eisenstein's Battleship Potemkin turns cutting into meaning itself.

In the young Soviet Union, film stock is scarce and theory is bold. Lev Kuleshov runs the experiment that names the age: he cuts one actor's one expressionless face against a bowl of soup, a coffin, a playing child — and audiences praise the actor's hunger, grief and tenderness. The face never changed; the meaning was made by the join. Sergei Eisenstein drives the principle into Battleship Potemkin, where cutting itself becomes the argument — montage, in the book's formula, where one plus one is more than two.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
