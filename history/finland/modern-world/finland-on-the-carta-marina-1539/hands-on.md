# Hands-on — Finland on the Carta Marina, 1539

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a quick map of your own neighbourhood from memory, then check it against a real map. Write one thing you drew wrong, and why.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Finland first steps onto Europe's maps as Finlandia: Olaus Magnus spends twelve years drawing the North, and his Carta Marina of 1539 stays in use for a hundred years. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
