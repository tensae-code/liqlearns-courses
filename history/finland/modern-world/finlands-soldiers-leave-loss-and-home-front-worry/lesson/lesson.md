# Finland's Soldiers: Leave, Loss and Home-Front Worry

## Do it first

Write the field-post letter a soldier sends home in one page: what he may say, what he cannot, and the one sentence that carries the most.

## Sub lesson 1 — reading the letters through women's experience

The teacher sheet for the whole Jahvetti set begins by naming who actually writes the letters: most of them are wives and mothers. Their pens carry the home front's war — the worry, the shortages, the waiting — and reading the letters through women's experience is the way into every theme that follows. The front has its historians; the home front has its letter-writers, and they are mostly women.

## Sub lesson 2 — introduction: who served, where, and the field post

The introduction sets the scale. Hundreds of thousands of Finns serve in the war years, but only about a third of them fight at the front: a man's age and trade decide his post — front line, labour company, transport, defence. Between every post and home runs one thread: the field post, letters carried both ways. And the cost is counted at the end: about 95,000 Finns die. Every letter in this lesson is written inside those numbers.

## Sub lesson 3 — Task 1, Oulu letter 1944

From Oulu in 1944, a mother writes who has already buried two sons. A third son's brother has fallen, and she begs that the surviving son be let home for the hero's funeral. Leave is refused. The letter holds grief and arithmetic together: how many sons does one family owe? The office's answer holds the army's arithmetic instead — and pupils must weigh both.

## Sub lesson 4 — Task 2, Hämeenlinna letter Dec 1944

From Hämeenlinna in December 1944, parents protest a stranger turn: the war with the Soviet Union is over by armistice, yet the young men born in 1925 still fight in the north — now against the Germans, in the Lapland War — while their letters and parcels vanish on the way. The parents' war has changed enemy without ending, and their sons' silence from the north is the loudest part of the letter.

## Sub lesson 5 — Task 3, Kotala letter 1942

From Kotala in 1942, the argument is about usefulness. A wounded farmer, classed B2, idles in a labour company, fit for neither full service nor release — while at home his wife and three small children run the farm alone. Release him where he is useful, the letter argues: a farm feeding a family is war work too, and the army is wasting the man it cannot use.

## Sub lesson 6 — Task 4, Perniö letter Oct 1944

From Perniö in October 1944, a wife charges unfairness in the going home itself. Shirkers, she writes, and artillerymen were demobilised and rested first, while the front infantry — her husband among them — got nothing. Demobilisation has an order, and the letter insists the order is unjust: those who bore the worst are sent home last.

## Sub lesson 7 — Task 5, Inkeroinen letter 1944 with Jahvetti's reply printed

From Inkeroinen in 1944, a soldier's granted funeral leave is cancelled — and this time Jahvetti's reply is printed with the letter. The office answers carefully: only the man's commander can judge a total leave ban. Pupils read both sides of the exchange, the plea and the refusal, and see how the office must answer grief with jurisdiction — kindly, and without power to grant.

## Sub lesson 8 — Task 6, Lappeenranta letter c.1941

The last letter, from Lappeenranta around 1941, is a veteran's protest. A man who served in 1918 writes against the sentences given to soldiers who return late from leave — six to seven months in prison — when broken transport, not desertion, made them late. Punishment, he argues, must know the difference between a shirker and a man the roads failed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Hundreds of thousands serve but only a third fight at the front; age and trade decide a man's post, letters are the only link home — and about 95,000 Finns die, while families argue leave, loss and fairness by letter. Give one real example: A mother in Oulu who has buried two sons begs that the third be let home for his brother's hero's funeral — and leave is refused. and name the lesson's key words: Field post, Leave. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
