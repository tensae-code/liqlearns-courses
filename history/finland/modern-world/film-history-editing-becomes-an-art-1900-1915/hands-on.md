# Hands-on — Film History: Editing Becomes an Art, 1900–1915

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Tell a two-shot story with two drawings: shot one inside a tunnel, shot two outside it. Show them in order and write what the viewer joins together.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Between 1900 and 1915 cutting joins the pieces into film's own grammar: Smith cuts a kiss in a tunnel to a passing train, Porter stages the Great Train Robbery, and Griffith builds the language. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
