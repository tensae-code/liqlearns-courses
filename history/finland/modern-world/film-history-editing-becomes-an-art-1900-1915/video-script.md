# Video script — Film History: Editing Becomes an Art, 1900–1915

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. ---

This lesson tells one story.
Between 1900 and 1915 cutting joins the pieces into film's own grammar: Smith cuts a kiss in a tunnel to a passing train, Porter stages the Great Train Robbery, and Griffith builds the language.

The next invention is invisible: the cut. Around 1900, G. A. Smith films a kiss inside a railway carriage in a tunnel — and cuts it against the train rushing past outside, joining two separate shots into one event the viewer experiences whole. Edwin S. Porter stages The Great Train Robbery, moving the story across places no stage could hold. And D. W. Griffith takes the join furthest, weaving shots into a grammar — close and far, cause and effect, chase and rescue — until film stops being photographed theatre and becomes a language of its own.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
