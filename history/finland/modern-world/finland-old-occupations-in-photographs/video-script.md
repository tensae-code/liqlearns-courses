# Video script — Finland's Old Occupations in Photographs

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Finland's work has changed beyond recognition: museum photographs from the 1910s to 1977 show lift girls, lamp cleaners, shoeblack boys and log-floaters in trades that have vanished or transformed.

Seventeen photographs, taken between the 1910s and 1977, hold a Finland of work that has vanished or changed beyond recognition. Lift girls ride the elevators of the Stockmann department store in 1930. A gas-lamp cleaner climbs his ladder in 1912, in the last years before electric light ends his trade. A shoeblack boy works the station in 1952, the Olympic summer. Log-floaters ride the Vantaa river, walking the timber downstream. None of these jobs can be applied for today — but in the national photo archive they are still at work, and the pupil's task is to read them as the record of a changing country.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
