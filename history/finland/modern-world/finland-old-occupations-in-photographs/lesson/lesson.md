# Finland's Old Occupations in Photographs

## Do it first

Ask someone at home about a job in your family that no longer exists, or has wholly changed. Write the job's name and the one tool that tells its story.

Seventeen photographs, taken between the 1910s and 1977, hold a Finland of work that has vanished or changed beyond recognition. Lift girls ride the elevators of the Stockmann department store in 1930. A gas-lamp cleaner climbs his ladder in 1912, in the last years before electric light ends his trade. A shoeblack boy works the station in 1952, the Olympic summer. Log-floaters ride the Vantaa river, walking the timber downstream. None of these jobs can be applied for today — but in the national photo archive they are still at work, and the pupil's task is to read them as the record of a changing country.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Finland's work has changed beyond recognition: museum photographs from the 1910s to 1977 show lift girls, lamp cleaners, shoeblack boys and log-floaters in trades that have vanished or transformed. Give one real example: Log-floaters ride the Vantaa river's timber in one photograph, while a shoeblack boy works Helsinki station in Olympic year 1952 in another — two vanished worlds in one set. and name the lesson's key words: Occupation, Photo archive. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself
