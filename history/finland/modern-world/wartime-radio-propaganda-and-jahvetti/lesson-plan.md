# Lesson plan — Wartime Radio, Propaganda and Jahvetti

**Course:** History — Finland · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Wartime Radio, Propaganda and Jahvetti
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Finland at War: Letters, Rationing and the Home Front (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** One radio channel and communal listening make the voice a weapon: the state builds Jahvetti to hear and steer the nation's mood, while Soviet agitator Armas Äikiä shouts over the Lahti wavelength — and pupils judge the steering for themselves.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — Finland at War: Letters, Rationing and the Home Front (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Task 4 mood-regulation question** — The service package ends with a question aimed at Jahvetti himself.
2. **introduction: radio in 1940s Finland and the letter-box programme** — The media package opens with the medium itself.
3. **Task 1, dugout letter 1942** — The front answers back.
4. **Task 2, radio speech 4 July 1944 (part)** — On 4 July 1944, at the evacuation summer's height, Jahvetti's radio speech preaches two duties: do your own duty, and give the displaced a fair welcome on their new shores.
5. **Task 3, radio speech 7 March 1944 (part)** — The speech of 7 March 1944 makes a smaller, stranger ask: battery radios.
6. **Task 4, the 4 July 1944 speech in full (upper secondary)** — For the older pupils, the 4 July 1944 speech is read whole.
7. **Task 5, Humppila letter Oct 1944** — The last letter is a listener's.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
