# Hands-on — Wartime Radio, Propaganda and Jahvetti

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three ways a radio voice in 1944 could steer a nation's mood, then write which of them you can spot in today's social media.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: One radio channel and communal listening make the voice a weapon: the state builds Jahvetti to hear and steer the nation's mood, while Soviet agitator Armas Äikiä shouts over the Lahti wavelength — and pupils judge the steering for themselves. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
