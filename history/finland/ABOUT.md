# About the History — Finland course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own open learning materials (Finna Luokkahuone / Finna.fi,
the National Archives of Finland, AOE.fi, the National
Museum and the Valve Film School, in Finnish and Swedish).
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Finland inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
Legends and religious-history stories are told as the books
tell them, as plot summaries in our own words.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the books'
own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: Finding History Where You Live.**
   The discovery expedition, the evidence craft stocked from
   both filings, the mystery-object method, and fact against
   rumour.
2. **Level 2 — Finland Before Writing: The Mystery Objects.**
   Stone, bronze and iron read from nine National Museum
   objects.
3. **Level 3 — Finland Enters the Map and the Estate
   Order.** The Carta Marina, the Reformation read off the
   map, and the estate module lived from inside.
4. **Level 4 — Work and Leaving: Photographs of a Changing
   Finland.** Old occupations in the photo archive, and
   emigration across the oceans.
5. **Level 5 — The Camera's Century: A History of Film.**
   The Valve Film School's time journey, era by era.
6. **Level 6 — Finland at War: Letters, Rationing and the
   Home Front.** The Jahvetti archive whole, and the
   Continuation War in the SA-kuva photographs.

All 26 of the 26 lessons stand on **student books** and count
in the acceptance reckoning below; teacher-guide placements
ride as named evidence rows in 4 of them, and no lesson
stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *One land, told through its things and its letters:*
  "Begin by hunting the past in your own street, and learn
  to question a photograph before you believe it. Then hold
  Finland's oldest story in your hands — a clay elk, a
  chewed lump of pitch, a Roman cauldron in a burial pond.
  Watch Finland step onto Europe's maps as Finlandia, and
  live one rung of its estate ladder in the 1800s. Read its
  vanished trades in the photo archive and cross the ocean
  with its emigrants. Ride the camera's century from the
  first spinning discs to Potemkin. And end with Finland at
  war — in the letters its own people wrote to a radio
  voice, and in the photographs its own army took. That is
  Finland's own story, from its own school books, and it is
  yours."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **The Finna Luokkahuone sets (Books 4–7, 14–21)** — the
  national photo archive is the textbook: real museum and
  SA-kuva photographs, questioned first and believed after,
  with historical empathy as the second, disciplined aim.
- **The Jahvetti archive (Books 8–13)** — Finland's war is
  taught from the home front's own handwriting: real
  anonymised letters to a trusted radio voice, each with
  source questions that climb from the letter's facts to
  analysis to the pupil's own judgement; the archive itself,
  and the ethics of reading private letters, are part of the
  lesson. The letters printed inside the packages are filed
  as plot summaries only.
- **Book 1 (Aikamatka elokuvaan)** — film history as a time
  journey the pupil makes with their hands: an era's story,
  its films, a workshop in its style, and an analysis round.
