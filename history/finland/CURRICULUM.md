# History — Finland course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Finland inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Finland's books in hand are open
learning materials from Finna Luokkahuone / Finna.fi, the
National Archives, AOE.fi and the Valve Film School — primary
discovery material and a poster first (the earliest records),
then the National Museum's prehistory objects and the Carta
Marina set, then the Grade 7 module and archive sets. The two
placements files' book order is the teaching order, earliest
books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the eras as
Finland's own books present them: the historian's craft first,
learned on photographs, maps and letters (Level 1); Finland
before writing, read from nine mystery objects (Level 2);
Finland entering Europe's maps and the estate order its
children must learn to live inside (Level 3); work and leaving,
told through the photo archive (Level 4); the camera's own
century, the history of film (Level 5); and Finland at war,
told by the home front's own letters and the army's own
photographs (Level 6). The ladder is a recommended path, never
a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own open learning materials (Finna Luokkahuone, the National
Archives, AOE.fi, the National Museum and the Valve Film
School) — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: Finding History Where You Live** — history begins as a hunt in your own neighbourhood and a question put to a photograph: the discovery expedition and the workplace visit, the whole craft of evidence to a written history stocked from twelve parts across both filings, the nine mystery objects as the archaeologist's first exercise, and fact against rumour in wartime 4 lessons.

2. **Finland Before Writing: The Mystery Objects** — ten thousand years before writing, read from things you can hold: Stone Age clay, chewed pitch and an offering gouge; bronze display and a Roman cauldron in a burial pond; and the Viking Age's locks, keys and a 7-gram ear spoon 3 lessons.

3. **Finland Enters the Map and the Estate Order** — Finlandia appears on Olaus Magnus's Carta Marina of 1539, the map itself read as a Reformation argument, and the 1800s estate ladder learned by living one rung of it — names researched, a character built, interviewed, and writing home in role 3 lessons.

4. **Work and Leaving: Photographs of a Changing Finland** — seventeen archive photographs of trades that have vanished or changed beyond recognition, and the great emigration across the oceans rebuilt from single photographs and lived through empathy exercises 2 lessons.

5. **The Camera's Century: A History of Film** — the film school's own time journey: the moving-image illusion, the Lumière café of 1895, Méliès's magic, editing's grammar, Soviet montage, the city symphonies and German Expressionism 7 lessons.

6. **Finland at War: Letters, Rationing and the Home Front** — the war as the home front wrote it: the Jahvetti letterbox and its 100,000 letters, rationing argued letter by letter, the 420,000 Karelian evacuees, soldiers' leave and loss, radio as a weapon of mood, the Continuation War in the army's own photographs, and Finland's 'separate war' claim tested against them 7 lessons.

**Totals: 26 lessons** (Level 1: 4 · Level 2: 3 · Level 3: 3 · Level 4: 2 · Level 5: 7 · Level 6: 7 — 96 placements stocked: the student books and student decks of both filings, with teacher guides and teacher sheets stocked as named evidence; 14 lessons carry sub lessons; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only — every lesson carries at least one student-book placement, as filed in ABOUT.md).
