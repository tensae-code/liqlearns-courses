# Hands-on — Finland's Prehistory: Bronze and Iron Age Objects

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find one metal object in your home that is kept for show rather than use. Write why its material makes it special, the way bronze was special then.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: When metal arrives, objects change their meaning: bronze becomes luxury and display, a fire-stone guards the harvest, and a Roman-made cauldron ends its journey in a burial pond. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
