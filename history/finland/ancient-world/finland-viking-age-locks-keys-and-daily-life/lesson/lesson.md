# Finland's Viking Age: Locks, Keys and Daily Life

## Do it first

Count the keys you or your family carry today. Write what each one protects, and what that says about what your age values.

## Sub lesson 1 — Esine 4: Rauta-avain (iron key, Viking Age c. 800–1050)

The iron key, from the Viking Age around 800–1050, announces a new idea: property that stays locked. For the first time, valuables are kept behind locks as a matter of course, and someone must carry the key. A key is a small thing, but it changes daily life — stores can be left, wealth can wait, and trust is replaced by iron.

## Sub lesson 2 — Esine 6: Lukkoja ja avaimia (Iron Age / Viking Age locks and a padlock from Hämeenlinna, c. 800–1050)

The locks and padlock from Hämeenlinna complete the picture. Padlocks with loops for chains guard Viking Age stores: the chain runs through the loop, the lock closes, and the goods sleep safely. Security has become a thing you carry in your hand — and the pupil who has carried a school locker key all year already lives inside this invention.

## Sub lesson 3 — Esine 5: Korvalusikka (ear spoon, Viking Age c. 800–1050)

The smallest object tells the most intimate story. An ear spoon of the Viking Age, fifty-nine millimetres long and seven grams in weight, was made for cleaning ears. Daily hygiene, a thousand years ago, had its own fine instruments — and the story continues: the wax such spoons gathered was later prized in folk medicine. Nothing in the past is too small to be evidence; care of the body leaves its tools behind like everything else.
