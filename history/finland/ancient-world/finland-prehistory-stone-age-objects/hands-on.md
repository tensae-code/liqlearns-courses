# Hands-on — Finland's Prehistory: Stone Age Objects

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Chew a piece of gum for one minute, then look at the tooth marks you leave. Write what a finder in a thousand years could learn about you from them alone.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Finland's Stone Age left no words, but its things remain: a clay elk of unknown meaning, birch pitch still holding tooth marks, and a giant stone gouge sunk unused in rapids. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
