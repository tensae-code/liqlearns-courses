# Video script — Finland's Prehistory: Stone Age Objects

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Finland's Stone Age left no words, but its things remain: a clay elk of unknown meaning, birch pitch still holding tooth marks, and a giant stone gouge sunk unused in rapids.

The first mystery object is a small elk shaped from clay in the Neolithic, the late Stone Age. An elk mattered: it was food, clothing and survival in animal form. But why shape one in clay, small enough to hold? Its meaning is still unknown — perhaps a charm, a toy, a god, a maker's mark. The object teaches the honest edge of prehistory: some questions the evidence answers, and some it leaves open, and the archaeologist must say which is which.
The second object is smaller still: a lump of birch pitch, chewed like gum in the Stone Age. Birch pitch was chewed to clean the teeth, and this lump kept the marks of its chewer's teeth pressed into it thousands of years ago. Of all the objects, this one is the most personal — a person who left no name, no grave goods, no words, is met here almost face to face, in the print of their own teeth.
The third object is a giant: a chisel-like gouge of green schist, nearly half a metre long, found at Kiuruvesi. Its stone had been carried hundreds of kilometres to reach Finland's lakes. Yet the great tool was apparently never used — it was sunk in rapids, most likely as an offering. A thing too valuable to use, given to the water: the Stone Age, the object says, had wealth, trade roads and religion, long before it had writing.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
