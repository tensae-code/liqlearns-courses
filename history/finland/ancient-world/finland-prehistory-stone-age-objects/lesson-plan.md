# Lesson plan — Finland's Prehistory: Stone Age Objects

**Course:** History — Finland · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Finland's Prehistory: Stone Age Objects
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Finland Before Writing: The Mystery Objects (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Finland's Stone Age left no words, but its things remain: a clay elk of unknown meaning, birch pitch still holding tooth marks, and a giant stone gouge sunk unused in rapids.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Finland Before Writing: The Mystery Objects (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Esine 1: Savi-idoli-hirvi (clay elk idol, Neolithic)** — The first mystery object is a small elk shaped from clay in the Neolithic, the late Stone Age.
2. **Esine 7: Purupihka (birch-pitch chewing gum, Stone Age)** — The second object is smaller still: a lump of birch pitch, chewed like gum in the Stone Age.
3. **Esine 8: Korutaltta (giant green-schist gouge, Neolithic, found at Kiuruvesi)** — The third object is a giant: a chisel-like gouge of green schist, nearly half a metre long, found at Kiuruvesi.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Finland's Stone Age left no words, but its things remain: a clay elk of unknown meaning, birch pitch still holding tooth marks, and a giant stone gouge sunk unused in rapids.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: A lump of birch pitch chewed thousands of years ago still carries the chewer's tooth marks — a Stone Age person met, almost face to face.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a small chewed lump is rubbish → It is a document: the pitch was chewed to clean teeth, and its tooth marks are the signature of a person who left no other.
- Go back to the one thing: Finland's Stone Age left no words, but its things remain: a clay elk of unknown meaning, birch pitch still holding tooth marks, and a giant stone gouge sunk unused in rapids. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
