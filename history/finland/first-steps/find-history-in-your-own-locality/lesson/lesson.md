# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Think of one place near your home that is older than you — a building, a street corner, a workplace. Write one thing that happened there before you were born, and how you could check it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Löytöretket: historian opiskelua koulun lähiympäristössä (Seppo game + green screen discovery day)

A class has finished studying a period of history, and now it must find that same past again — outside the classroom, in its own neighbourhood. The pupils play a location game on their phones, following clues through the school's surroundings, and keep an expedition diary as they go. The day ends in front of a green screen, where each pupil steps photographically inside the world just studied — onto the deck of Columbus's Santa Maria, among the explorers. The lesson's turn is simple and strong: the past is not locked in a book; it is standing in your own street, waiting to be found again.

## Sub lesson 2 — Part 3: workplace visit or museum visit with interview questions

The hunt for history can also start from a job. The class leaves school to meet a living holder of an old trade, interview questions ready, and asks the worker how the trade was done and how it has changed. If no holder can be met, the class walks into a museum instead and finds the trade's traces among its objects. Either way the evidence is first-hand: a voice that remembers, or a thing that survives — and the pupils' questions are what turn a visit into history.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History is not only far away: a finished period of the past can be found again in your own neighbourhood — in its streets, workplaces and museums — and you can step inside it. Give one real example: A class that has studied explorers goes out, phone clues in hand, and photographs itself by green screen aboard Columbus's Santa Maria — the past found on its own streets. and name the lesson's key words: Local history, Discovery expedition. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: History is not only far away: a finished period of the past can be found again in your own neighbourhood — in its streets, workplaces and museums — and you can step inside it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
