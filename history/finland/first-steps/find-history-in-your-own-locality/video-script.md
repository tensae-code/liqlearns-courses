# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History is not only far away: a finished period of the past can be found again in your own neighbourhood — in its streets, workplaces and museums — and you can step inside it.

A class has finished studying a period of history, and now it must find that same past again — outside the classroom, in its own neighbourhood. The pupils play a location game on their phones, following clues through the school's surroundings, and keep an expedition diary as they go. The day ends in front of a green screen, where each pupil steps photographically inside the world just studied — onto the deck of Columbus's Santa Maria, among the explorers. The lesson's turn is simple and strong: the past is not locked in a book; it is standing in your own street, waiting to be found again.
The hunt for history can also start from a job. The class leaves school to meet a living holder of an old trade, interview questions ready, and asks the worker how the trade was done and how it has changed. If no holder can be met, the class walks into a museum instead and finds the trade's traces among its objects. Either way the evidence is first-hand: a voice that remembers, or a thing that survives — and the pupils' questions are what turn a visit into history.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
