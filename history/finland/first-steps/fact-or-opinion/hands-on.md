# Hands-on — Fact or Opinion

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down one rumour you have heard this year, then write what you would need to see before you could call it a fact.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In wartime, a rumour is not a fact: Jahvetti's speech teaches the nation to name rumour-mongers' tales for what they are and to check before repeating. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
