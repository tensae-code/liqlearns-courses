# Video script — Who Studies Prehistory?

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Where there are no writings, objects must be read instead: archaeologists — and pupils thinking like them — guess what a silent thing was for, then test the guess against the answers.

Nine objects lie before the class, and none of them comes with a label. They are real things from Finland's prehistory — the thousands of years before writing reached the North — held by the National Museum. Working in pairs, pupils must do what archaeologists do: look hard at each silent object, guess what it was for, and defend the guess. Only afterwards are the museum's answers revealed, object by object, each answer opening the object's period and story. With no writings to read, thinking itself becomes the source — and a guess you can defend is the first step of the science.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Finland.
