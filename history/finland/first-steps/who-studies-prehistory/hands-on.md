# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory?

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pick up one object in your home whose use a stranger might not guess. Write your best guess at its story: who used it, for what, and what clue gave it away.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Where there are no writings, objects must be read instead: archaeologists — and pupils thinking like them — guess what a silent thing was for, then test the guess against the answers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
