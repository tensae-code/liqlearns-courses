# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take any old photograph or letter you can find at home. Write three questions you would ask it before believing it: who made it, when, and what it leaves out.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A photograph, a map or a letter does not speak by itself: you question it — who made it, when, why, and what it cannot show — and only then build a written history out of it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
