# History — Ukraine course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Ukraine's
own — built from its Grade 7 *History of Ukraine* student
book (Orion, Kyiv, 2024), which tells one national story,
reign by reign, from the Slavs' settlement to the end of the
15th century, teaching the medieval Rus state as Ukraine's
own first state, "Rus-Ukraine". Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below
sit inside Levels 1–5, in path order — what the learner
proves, step by step. The grade the book comes from lives
only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: first the origins — the
Slavs, Kyiv's founding and the birth of the state — then the
golden age of baptism and law, the breaking apart, the
western kingdom and the Mongol storm, and last the lands
living inside new states while their culture holds, closing
on the "gates of Europe". That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — origins: slavs, kyiv and the birth of rus-ukraine**, in `ancient-world/`:

- Ukraine Enters the Middle Ages (the Middle Ages on Ukrainian lands begin from an older world — steppe horsemen, Greek cities on the coast — and the Slavs, from whose farming communities the whole story continues) · The Slavs Settle and Kyiv Is Founded (Slavic tribes spread across Eastern Europe, farmed, traded and joined into tribal unions — and on the Dnipro hills Kyi, Shchek, Khoryv and their sister Lybid founded Kyiv, the city everything after this grows from) · Rus-Ukraine: A State Is Born (in the ninth century the East Slavic tribes were united under one prince at Kyiv, and a loose tribal world became a state — Rus-Ukraine) · Rus-Ukraine under Oleh and Ihor (Oleh seized Kyiv in 882, subdued the neighbouring tribes and won trade treaties from Byzantium; Ihor kept the tribute rounds going until the Derevlians, squeezed once too often, killed him in 945) · Rus-Ukraine under Olha and Sviatoslav (Princess Olha avenged Ihor, then reformed tribute itself — fixed dues and collection points — and was baptised in Constantinople; her son Sviatoslav the Brave smashed Khazaria and died in a Pecheneg ambush in 972)

**Level 2 — the golden age: volodymyr and yaroslav**, in `ancient-world/`:

- Volodymyr the Great Baptises Rus-Ukraine (Volodymyr won the throne in a brothers' war, ringed the steppe frontier with fortress towns, and in 988 baptised Rus in the Dnipro — churches, schools and a tithe followed, and the young state entered Christian Europe) · Yaroslav the Wise and the Ruska Pravda (Yaroslav beat his brothers and the Pechenegs, raised St Sophia in Kyiv, married his children into Europe's royal houses — and gave Rus its first written law code, the Ruska Pravda, replacing blood vengeance with fines) · How Rus-Ukraine Was Ruled and Lived (the golden-age state day to day: prince, boyars, druzhyna and viche at the top; free smerds, dependent zakupy and kholopy below; towns of craftsmen and merchants and villages of farmers) · The Culture of Rus-Ukraine: The Golden Age (the golden age in culture: byliny epics sung about Volodymyr's heroes, the first schools and libraries, chronicle writing beginning, and St Sophia's mosaics and the Desiatynna church making Kyiv a city of stone and books)

**Level 3 — fragmentation: many princes, one land**, in `ancient-world/`:

- Rus-Ukraine Breaks Apart: The Yaroslavychi (Yaroslav's testament divided Rus among his sons, and within years the brothers were at war with each other while the Polovtsians raided a land whose princes no longer stood together) · Volodymyr Monomakh Holds Rus Together (the Liubech congress of 1097 failed to stop the feuds, but Volodymyr Monomakh — campaigner against the Polovtsians and author of the Instruction — reunited most of Rus under Kyiv once more, and his son Mstyslav held it together until 1132) · Fragmented Rus: Kyiv, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav (after Mstyslav the unity was gone for good — Kyiv was fought over and even sacked by a Rus prince in 1169, while Chernihiv and Pereiaslav went their own ways on the steppe frontier) · Halych and Volyn Rise (in the west Yaroslav Osmomysl made Halych rich on salt and the Dnister trade while Volyn grew beside it — and all the while Pechenegs, Torks and Polovtsians rode the steppe and Crimea changed hands beyond the mountains) · The Culture of Rus-Ukraine: The Age of Fragmentation (even as the state splintered, its culture flowered — Nestor's chronicle, the Tale of Ihor's Campaign, graffiti scratched by ordinary Kyivans on St Sophia's walls, schools, medicine and white-stone churches in every principality)

**Level 4 — halych-volyn and the mongol storm**, in `ancient-world/`:

- Roman Mstyslavych Founds the Halych-Volyn State (Roman Mstyslavych, prince of Volyn, took Halych in 1199 and united the two western lands into the strongest state in Rus — then died in Poland in 1205, leaving infant sons and circling boyars and kings) · The Mongols Invade Rus-Ukraine (the Rus princes and Polovtsians fell together at the Kalka in 1223; Batu returned in 1237–1241, Kyiv was stormed and burned in December 1240, and the Ukrainian lands were folded into the Golden Horde's tribute system) · Danylo, King of Rus (Roman's son Danylo fought boyars, Poles, Hungarians and Mongols to rebuild his father's state, founded Kholm and Lviv, bowed to Batu at Sarai to buy time — and in 1253 accepted a royal crown from the Pope's legate, becoming king of Rus) · The Halych-Volyn State after Danylo (Danylo's son Lev held Galicia from his capital at Lviv and his grandson Yurii I took the royal title again — but by 1340 the Romanovych line died out and Poland and Lithuania divided the inheritance) · The Culture of the Halych-Volyn Land (under Mongol shadow the west kept the Rus inheritance alive — the Halych-Volyn Chronicle, church schools and writing, stone fortresses and painted icons in the land that preserved a Ukrainian state tradition)

**Level 5 — the late middle ages: ukrainian lands in new states**, in `ancient-world/`:

- Ukrainian Lands in the Lithuanian State (after the Romanovych line ended in 1340, Lithuania gathered most Ukrainian lands — Algirdas took Kyiv after Blue Waters in 1362 — and for a century the lands kept their laws, faith and Rus language inside a state whose princes ruled 'without changing the old ways') · Ukrainian Lands under Hungary, Moldova and Muscovy (the borderlands went other ways — Transcarpathia under Hungary since the Magyar crossing of the Carpathians and Prince Laborets' doomed defence of Ungvar, Northern Bukovina inside Moldavia, and the north-eastern borderlands drawn toward Moscow) · Crimea and the Northern Black Sea in the Late Middle Ages (Genoese trading cities on the coast, the Greek principality of Theodoro in the mountains, and from the 1440s the Crimean Khanate of the Girays — a new steppe power that would face Ukraine for three centuries) · Ukrainian Towns, Crafts and Magdeburg Law (three-field farming spread, guild crafts grew — Lviv's armourers were famous by 1394 — trade roads linked the lands to Europe and the Black Sea, and town after town won Magdeburg self-government) · Ukrainian Culture in the 14th and 15th Centuries (with no state of their own, the culture held — church and monastery schools, students like Yurii Drohobych reaching Bologna and Kraków and becoming rector at Bologna, fortress and church building from Lutsk to Kamianets) · Ukraine in Medieval Europe: The Course Closes (the course closes by testing Serhii Plokhy's image of Ukraine as 'the gates of Europe' against everything the year has covered — a journey through medieval Ukraine from Kyiv's founding to the end of the 15th century)
