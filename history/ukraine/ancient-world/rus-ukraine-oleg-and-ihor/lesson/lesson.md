# Rus-Ukraine under Oleh and Ihor

## Do it first

Two rulers, two ends. One dies with treaties in his hand; the other is killed by the very people who pay him. Before reading, guess: what makes tribute fair, and what makes it deadly? Keep your answer for the end of the lesson.

In 882 Oleh came down the Dnipro and seized Kyiv, killing its rulers Askold and Dir. From Kyiv he subdued the neighbouring tribes one by one, pulling them into the new state and its tribute.

Then he turned his boats south. Rus fleets sailed on Constantinople, the rich capital of Byzantium, until the empire signed trade treaties: Rus merchants could come, trade and be protected. The young state had forced the greatest empire of its world to deal with it as a partner.

Oleh's successor Ihor kept the system running — above all the tribute rounds, the yearly circuit collecting goods from the subject tribes. In 945 he came to the Derevlians and, having taken tribute once, demanded it again. Squeezed once too often, the Derevlians rose and killed him. The state survived its prince; the lesson about tribute did not go unlearned — his widow would teach it next.
