# Video script — Crimea and the Northern Black Sea in the Late Middle Ages

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Genoese trading cities on the coast, the greek principality of theodoro in the mountains, and from the 1440s the crimean khanate of the girays — a new steppe power that would face ukraine for three centuries.

The northern Black Sea coast in the late Middle Ages was a meeting of three worlds. On the shore stood the Genoese trading cities — Caffa the greatest — walled ports where Italian merchants bought and sold the goods of the whole steppe and sea: grain, furs, wax, and, darkly, captives.

In Crimea's mountains held out Theodoro, a small Greek principality, Orthodox and stubborn, ruling its fortresses and villages in the old Byzantine tradition long after Constantinople's power had shrunk.

And on the steppe behind them a new power rose: from the 1440s the Crimean Khanate of the Giray dynasty, heirs of the Golden Horde in the peninsula, builders of Bakhchisarai, raiders and traders both. The Khanate would face Ukraine — raiding it, trading with it, allying with its Cossacks and fighting them — for three centuries to come. The late-medieval board is complete: every power of the next age is now on it.
