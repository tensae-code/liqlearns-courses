# Volodymyr the Great Baptises Rus-Ukraine

## Do it first

One year, one river, one whole people walking into the water. Before reading, list what a ruler might gain by giving his whole land one faith. Then check your list against what actually followed the baptism.

Volodymyr came to the throne through a brothers' war, and he ruled like a man who meant the state to hold. Along the steppe frontier he raised a ring of fortress towns, settled with defenders, so the Pecheneg raids would break on walls instead of villages.

Then came the act that names his reign. In 988 Rus was baptised: Kyiv's people went down into the Dnipro, and Christianity became the faith of the state. Churches rose, the first schools opened for the children of the leading families, and a tithe — a tenth of the prince's income — was set aside to sustain the church.

The turn is bigger than one ceremony. Baptised Rus entered Christian Europe as a recognised member of its family of states: its princes could marry into Europe's royal houses, its church connected it to Constantinople and to the written, bookish world. The state born in the ninth century now had a faith, a law-in-waiting and a place on the map of Christendom.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Volodymyr won the throne in a brothers' war, ringed the steppe frontier with fortress towns, and in 988 baptised Rus in the Dnipro — churches, schools and a tithe followed, and the young state entered Christian Europe. Give one real example from the story as taught, and name the lesson's key words: Volodymyr the Great, 988, baptism, Dnipro, tithe, fortress towns, Christian Europe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
