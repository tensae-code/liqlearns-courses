# Hands-on — Fragmented Rus: Kyiv, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A capital so prized that princes fight to hold it — and one prince burns it instead. Before reading, answer: what does it do to a land when its greatest city becomes a prize of war?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: after Mstyslav the unity was gone for good — Kyiv was fought over and even sacked by a Rus prince in 1169, while Chernihiv and Pereiaslav went their own ways on the steppe frontier. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
