# Video script — Fragmented Rus: Kyiv, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
After mstyslav the unity was gone for good — kyiv was fought over and even sacked by a rus prince in 1169, while chernihiv and pereiaslav went their own ways on the steppe frontier.

After Mstyslav's death in 1132, the reunion ended for good. Rus was now a family of principalities, and the richest prize, Kyiv, changed hands again and again as princes fought for the senior throne.

The bitterest day came in 1169, when Andrei Bogoliubsky, prince of the north-eastern land, sent an army that took and sacked Kyiv itself — a Rus prince plundering the mother city of Rus, and then not even staying to rule it. The centre no longer held because no one any longer believed in the centre.

Chernihiv and Pereiaslav, the other two great lands of the south, went their own ways: Chernihiv rich and often at odds with Kyiv, Pereiaslav the frontier shield on the steppe edge, first to meet every Polovtsian raid. Three neighbouring principalities, one language, one faith, one chronicle tradition — and no single state. That divided land is what the next storms will strike.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Ukraine.
