# The Culture of Rus-Ukraine: The Age of Fragmentation

## Do it first

A state breaks; its songs get better. Before reading, guess why hard times might make a people write more, not less. Then find the Kyivans who wrote on a church wall — what did ordinary people want to say?

The state splintered; the culture flowered. In Kyiv's Caves Monastery the monk Nestor shaped the great chronicle — the Tale of Bygone Years — gathering the whole story of Rus from its beginnings, the very source from which the founding stories in this course are told.

The age's most famous work sings of failure: the Tale of Ihor's Campaign tells how Prince Ihor of Novhorod-Siverskyi rode against the Polovtsians in 1185, was defeated and captured — and turns the defeat into a plea for the princes to unite. Literature itself had become the land's conscience.

And writing was no longer only for monks and princes. On the walls of St Sophia, ordinary Kyivans scratched graffiti — prayers, names, notes of deals and deaths — in their own hands. Schools taught, healers practised medicine, and every principality raised its white-stone churches. Divided in power, Rus in these centuries was one in word, faith and art.
