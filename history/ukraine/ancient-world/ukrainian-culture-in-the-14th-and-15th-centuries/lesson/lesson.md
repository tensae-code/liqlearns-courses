# Ukrainian Culture in the 14th and 15th Centuries

## Do it first

A people with no state sends a son to run a famous university abroad. Before reading, answer: what carries a culture when there is no state to carry it? Collect the carriers as you read.

The Ukrainian lands in the 14th and 15th centuries had no state of their own. The culture held anyway. Its first carriers were the church and monastery schools, where boys learned letters, singing and the services, and where books were copied and kept.

Its scholars travelled. Yurii Drohobych, from the town of Drohobych, studied and taught at Kraków and Bologna — and became rector of Bologna's university, one of Europe's oldest, a son of a stateless land running a seat of world learning. Others followed the same roads to Prague, Kraków and the Italian universities, and brought the learning home.

And the land built. Fortresses and churches rose from Lutsk in the north to Kamianets in the south — stone statements that the culture was not merely surviving but creating. No state, yet schools, scholars and stone: the inheritance the Halych-Volyn land had kept was still alive in new hands.
