# Ukraine Enters the Middle Ages

## Do it first

Draw a line across a page and mark it 'the Middle Ages'. Before the line, write three names you meet in this lesson: Cimmerians, Scythians, Greeks. After the line, write one word: Slavs. This lesson is about what stands on each side of that line.

The course begins by asking a simple question: when do the Middle Ages start on Ukrainian lands, and what was here before them? Periodising — cutting the past into ages — is the first tool, and this lesson picks it up before any prince is named.

Look back first. The southern steppe was horseman country: Cimmerians, then Scythians, then Sarmatians rode it in turn, buried their kings in great mounds and traded with the world. On the Black Sea coast stood Greek poleis — cities like Olbia and Chersonesus — where ships, grain, wine and Greek speech met the steppe.

Then the turn: the Slavs. Farming communities spreading across the river valleys and forest edges, growing grain, keeping bees and cattle, and organising themselves in tribes. Everything in this course grows from those communities — the cities, the princes and the state all start here.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the Middle Ages on Ukrainian lands begin from an older world — steppe horsemen, Greek cities on the coast — and the Slavs, from whose farming communities the whole story continues. Give one real example from the story as taught, and name the lesson's key words: Middle Ages, Cimmerians, Scythians, Sarmatians, Olbia, Chersonesus, Slavs. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
