# Hands-on — Danylo, King of Rus

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A child driven from his father's throne grows up to wear a crown no Rus prince had worn. Before reading, list what stands between the child and the crown. Then count how many of them Danylo actually faced.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Roman's son Danylo fought boyars, Poles, Hungarians and Mongols to rebuild his father's state, founded Kholm and Lviv, bowed to Batu at Sarai to buy time — and in 1253 accepted a royal crown from the Pope's legate, becoming king of Rus. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
