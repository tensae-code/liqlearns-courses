# The Slavs Settle and Kyiv Is Founded

## Do it first

Tell the founding story as the chronicle tells it: three brothers and a sister on green hills above a great river. Then ask: why would people choose those hills? List three reasons a river hill is a good place for a city.

The Slavs did not stay in one small homeland. In the great settlement they spread across Eastern Europe — clearing forest, ploughing fields, raising cattle, working iron and trading along the rivers. Their life was organised in tribes, and the tribes joined into tribal unions, each with its own elders and its own land.

On the high hills above the Dnipro, where the river could be watched and crossed, a settlement grew that would outgrow all the others. The chronicle tells its founding as a family story: three brothers — Kyi, Shchek and Khoryv — and their sister Lybid settled on the hills, and the city took the eldest brother's name: Kyiv.

It is told as a founding story, and that is how this lesson keeps it — the plot the chronicle gives. What is certain is the place: whoever held those hills held the river road between the forest and the steppe, and Kyiv grows from that crossing point into the head of a state.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Slavic tribes spread across Eastern Europe, farmed, traded and joined into tribal unions — and on the Dnipro hills Kyi, Shchek, Khoryv and their sister Lybid founded Kyiv, the city everything after this grows from. Give one real example from the story as taught, and name the lesson's key words: Kyi, Shchek, Khoryv, Lybid, Kyiv, Dnipro, tribal unions. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
