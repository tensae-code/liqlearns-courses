# Video script — Volodymyr Monomakh Holds Rus Together

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The liubech congress of 1097 failed to stop the feuds, but volodymyr monomakh — campaigner against the polovtsians and author of the instruction — reunited most of rus under kyiv once more, and his son mstyslav held it together until 1132.

In 1097 the princes met at Liubech to end the feuds: each would hold his father's land, and they would stand together against the Polovtsians. The congress is famous — and it failed. The blinding of a prince days after the oaths showed how thin the promises were.

The man who did the work instead was Volodymyr Monomakh. A tireless campaigner, he led united Rus armies deep into the steppe and broke the Polovtsian pressure for a generation. Called to Kyiv in 1113, he ruled as the land's reuniter — most of Rus obeyed one prince again.

Monomakh also wrote. His Instruction to his children — part memoir, part rule of life: do not be lazy, judge justly, care for the weak, keep your oath — became the most famous princely text of the age. His son Mstyslav the Great held the reunited land together after him, until his death in 1132. After Mstyslav, no one could.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Ukraine.
