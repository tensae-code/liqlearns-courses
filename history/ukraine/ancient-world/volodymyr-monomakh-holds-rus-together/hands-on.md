# Hands-on — Volodymyr Monomakh Holds Rus Together

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A meeting of princes promises peace — and fails. One prince then does with campaigns and a famous book of advice what the meeting could not. As you read, collect Monomakh's tools: how many are swords, and how many are words?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the Liubech congress of 1097 failed to stop the feuds, but Volodymyr Monomakh — campaigner against the Polovtsians and author of the Instruction — reunited most of Rus under Kyiv once more, and his son Mstyslav held it together until 1132. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
