# Video script — How Rus-Ukraine Was Ruled and Lived

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The golden-age state day to day: prince, boyars, druzhyna and viche at the top; free smerds, dependent zakupy and kholopy below; towns of craftsmen and merchants and villages of farmers.

Who ruled, and who did the work? At the top stood the prince, ruling with his boyars — the great landowners who advised him — and his druzhyna, the armed retinue that fought his wars and ran his errands of power. In the towns the viche, the assembly of free townsmen, could roar its approval or its anger, and princes ignored it at their peril.

Below the court stood the farming world. The smerds were free peasants who worked their own land and paid their dues. The zakupy stood lower: men who had taken a loan — a 'kupa' — and worked it off on the lender's land, free in law but bound until the debt cleared. At the bottom were the kholopy, wholly unfree, owned like property.

Between court and village stood the towns: craftsmen in their workshops — smiths, potters, jewellers, armourers — and merchants whose goods moved down the Dnipro to Constantinople and across to Europe. That is the golden-age state as it actually worked on an ordinary day: a ladder of ranks, a countryside of farms, and towns humming with making and trading.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Ukraine.
