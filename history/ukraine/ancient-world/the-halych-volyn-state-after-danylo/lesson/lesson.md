# The Halych-Volyn State after Danylo

## Do it first

A state built by two strong men meets its hardest test: ordinary heirs, then no heirs at all. Before reading, answer: what keeps a state alive when its founding line ends?

Danylo's son Lev moved the capital to Lviv, the city his father had founded and named for him, and held Galicia against all comers — Mongols, Hungarians, Poles — keeping the kingdom a power in the region. His grandson Yurii I took the royal title again, styling himself king of Rus, and under him the state still minted, traded and treated with Europe.

But a state carried by one family lives and dies with that family. The later Romanovychi left fewer and weaker heirs, boyar factions pulled at the throne, and in 1340 the last prince of the line, Yurii II, died — by poison, the chronicles say — with no heir of the house to follow.

The inheritance was divided by stronger neighbours: Poland's king Casimir III took Halych, and Lithuania gathered Volyn. The Romanovych state — the last state of old Rus on Ukrainian lands — ended not with a sack but with a succession. Its memory, its cities and its royal tradition passed into the keeping of the lands themselves.
