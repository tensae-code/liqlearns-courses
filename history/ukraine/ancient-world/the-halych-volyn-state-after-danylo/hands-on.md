# Hands-on — The Halych-Volyn State after Danylo

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A state built by two strong men meets its hardest test: ordinary heirs, then no heirs at all. Before reading, answer: what keeps a state alive when its founding line ends?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Danylo's son Lev held Galicia from his capital at Lviv and his grandson Yurii I took the royal title again — but by 1340 the Romanovych line died out and Poland and Lithuania divided the inheritance. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
