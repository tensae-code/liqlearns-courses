# Rus-Ukraine under Olha and Sviatoslav

## Do it first

A widow takes power. First she punishes the men who killed her husband; then she changes the rule that caused the killing. As you read, keep two lists: what Olha did in anger, and what she did in wisdom. Which list is longer?

Ihor's widow Olha took power for her young son, and her first acts were vengeance on the Derevlians who had killed him — the chronicle tells them as stern, cunning tales. But vengeance was not her lasting work.

Her lasting work was a reform. The tribute that killed Ihor was arbitrary: a prince took what he could, when he came. Olha fixed it — set dues, the 'uroky', that each land owed, and fixed collection points, the 'pohosty', where they were delivered. Tribute became a system instead of a seizure. Then she travelled to Constantinople and was baptised, opening Rus to the Christian world a generation before the official baptism.

Her son Sviatoslav the Brave was a warrior of another kind: he smashed the Khazar khaganate, warred in the Balkans against Byzantium and Bulgaria, and lived in camp with his men. In 972, coming home up the Dnipro, Pechenegs ambushed him at the rapids and killed him. The reformer and the warrior, mother and son, leave Rus stronger in law and wider in fame — and still ringed by the steppe.
