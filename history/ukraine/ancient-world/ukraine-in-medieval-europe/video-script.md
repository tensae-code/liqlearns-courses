# Video script — Ukraine in Medieval Europe: The Course Closes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The course closes by testing serhii plokhy's image of ukraine as 'the gates of europe' against everything the year has covered — a journey through medieval ukraine from kyiv's founding to the end of the 15th century.

The course closes the way the book closes: by generalising — drawing the whole year into one tested idea. The historian Serhii Plokhy's image names Ukraine 'the gates of Europe': the land where Europe meets the steppe, where its peoples, faiths, goods and armies have passed back and forth for a thousand years.

Test the image against the course. Gates: the Dnipro road and the Greek ports; baptism bringing Rus into Christian Europe; Yaroslav's marriages binding Kyiv to Europe's royal houses; Danylo's crown from Rome; Magdeburg charters and Drohobych at Bologna. Gates swinging the other way: Polovtsian and Mongol storms through the same opening, tribute to Sarai, captives through Caffa. A gate, the course concludes, is not a wall and not an open field — it is the place where two worlds meet, trade, marry, fight and change each other. That meeting place is Ukraine's medieval story.

The book ends with doing, not just judging: a web-quest journey through medieval Ukraine and a 'historical philately' exhibition — the whole age told in stamps, from Kyiv's founding to the end of the 15th century. Know the past, the book's motto runs, make sense of the present, forecast the future. The course ends where its learners live: in the present the gates still stand in.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Ukraine.
