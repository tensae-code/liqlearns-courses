# Hands-on — Roman Mstyslavych Founds the Halych-Volyn State

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two lands, one prince, six years. Before reading, mark what a unifier must do in year one — and what happens to a new state when its maker dies with babies in the cradle.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Roman Mstyslavych, prince of Volyn, took Halych in 1199 and united the two western lands into the strongest state in Rus — then died in Poland in 1205, leaving infant sons and circling boyars and kings. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
