# Roman Mstyslavych Founds the Halych-Volyn State

## Do it first

Two lands, one prince, six years. Before reading, mark what a unifier must do in year one — and what happens to a new state when its maker dies with babies in the cradle.

In 1199 Roman Mstyslavych, prince of Volyn, took Halych and united the two western lands under one hand. The result — the Volyn-Halych principality, the state of the Romanovychi, Roman's line — was at once the strongest state in Rus: rich on salt and trade, respected abroad, its prince courted as an ally by kings and feared by the steppe.

Roman ruled hard and built fast, curbing the great boyars who had made and unmade Halych's princes for decades. For six years the west stood as one power while the Dnipro lands stayed divided.

Then, in 1205, Roman died on campaign in Poland. His sons Danylo and Vasylko were small children. At once the boyars rose, foreign kings — Poland and Hungary — reached for the inheritance, and the strongest state in Rus became a prize fought over for a generation. What Roman built would have to be built again — by his son.
