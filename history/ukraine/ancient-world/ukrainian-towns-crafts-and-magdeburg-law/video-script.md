# Video script — Ukrainian Towns, Crafts and Magdeburg Law

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Three-field farming spread, guild crafts grew — lviv's armourers were famous by 1394 — trade roads linked the lands to europe and the black sea, and town after town won magdeburg self-government.

The countryside changed first: three-field farming — winter crop, spring crop, and a field resting fallow in turn — spread across the Ukrainian lands in the 14th and 15th centuries, feeding more people from the same soil.

The towns changed faster. Crafts organised into guilds — masters, journeymen and apprentices under strict rules of quality — and some towns' work became famous far away: Lviv's armourers were renowned by 1394. Trade roads carried the lands' grain, wax, honey, furs and salt west into Europe and south to the Black Sea, and carried cloth, metalwork and wine back.

And town after town won the prize that crowned the change: Magdeburg law — a charter of self-government, named for the German city whose law it copied, giving the town its own elected council and court, free of the local lord's officials. A society of peasants, craftsmen, merchants and self-governing burghers: Ukraine's lands inside other states were quietly becoming something new.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Ukraine.
