# Hands-on — The Mongols Invade Rus-Ukraine

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A warning comes in 1223 — and the land goes back to quarrelling. Seventeen years later the storm returns. Before reading, answer: what should the princes have done with those seventeen years?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the Rus princes and Polovtsians fell together at the Kalka in 1223; Batu returned in 1237–1241, Kyiv was stormed and burned in December 1240, and the Ukrainian lands were folded into the Golden Horde's tribute system. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
