# History — Ukraine course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Ukraine inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Ukraine's set in hand is one book —
its Grade 7 *History of Ukraine* student book (Orion, Kyiv,
2024), which tells one national story in five sections, reign
by reign, from the Slavs' settlement to the end of the 15th
century: the medieval Rus state taught as Ukraine's own first
state, "Rus-Ukraine". So the ladder follows the book's own
five sections, in its own order: origins and the birth of the
state (Level 1, §§1–5), the golden age of Volodymyr and
Yaroslav (Level 2, §§7–11), fragmentation (Level 3, §§13–17),
the Halych-Volyn state and the Mongol storm (Level 4,
§§19–23), and the late Middle Ages, when the lands live inside
Lithuania and its neighbours and the course closes on the
"gates of Europe" (Level 5, §§25–29 and the course
generalisation §31). The book's six generalisation paragraphs
(§§6, 12, 18, 24, 30, §31) are review lessons for the sections
they close and are filed with those sections; §31 is stocked
as the closing lesson of Level 5. The ladder stops where the
book in hand stops, not because Ukraine teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 7 *History of Ukraine* — except the **Tip boxes**:
a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips
are ours; the lessons are the book's. See ABOUT.md for the
full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Origins: Slavs, Kyiv and the Birth of Rus-Ukraine** — the course opens by periodising the Middle Ages and looking back at ancient Ukraine — Cimmerians, Scythians and Sarmatians in the steppe, Greek poleis like Olbia and Chersonesus on the coast — then the Slavs spread, farm and unite in tribal unions, Kyi and his brothers found Kyiv, the tribes are joined into the state of Rus-Ukraine, Oleh takes Kyiv and wins Byzantium's trade treaties, Ihor dies for a doubled tribute, and Olha reforms the tribute and is baptised, leaving the warrior Sviatoslav to smash Khazaria and fall at the rapids. 5 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (5 lessons).

2. **The Golden Age: Volodymyr and Yaroslav** — Volodymyr wins the throne, rings the steppe with fortress towns and baptises Rus in the Dnipro in 988; Yaroslav the Wise beats the Pechenegs, raises St Sophia, marries his children across Europe and gives Rus the Ruska Pravda, its first written law; then the state as it was lived — prince, boyars, druzhyna and viche above, smerds, zakupy and kholopy below, towns of craftsmen and merchants — and its golden-age culture of byliny, schools, libraries, chronicles and stone churches. 4 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (4 lessons).

3. **Fragmentation: Many Princes, One Land** — Yaroslav's testament divides Rus among the Yaroslavychi and the brothers fall to war while the Polovtsians raid; Monomakh's campaigns and his Instruction reunite the land once more and Mstyslav holds it to 1132; then the unity ends for good — Kyiv fought over and sacked by a Rus prince in 1169, Chernihiv and Pereiaslav going their own ways — while in the west Halych grows rich on salt and the Dnister under Osmomysl, and the splintered land's culture flowers in Nestor's chronicle, the Tale of Ihor's Campaign and the graffiti of ordinary Kyivans. 5 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (5 lessons).

4. **Halych-Volyn and the Mongol Storm** — Roman Mstyslavych unites Volyn and Halych in 1199 into Rus's strongest state and dies in 1205 leaving infants; the Mongols fall at the Kalka in 1223 as a warning, return under Batu, and burn Kyiv in December 1240, folding the lands into the Golden Horde; Roman's son Danylo rebuilds the state, founds Kholm and Lviv, bows at Sarai to buy time and is crowned king of Rus in 1253; his son Lev and grandson Yurii I hold the kingdom until the line dies out in 1340 — while the land's chronicle, schools, fortresses and icons keep the Rus inheritance alive under the Mongol shadow. 5 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (5 lessons).

5. **The Late Middle Ages: Ukrainian Lands in New States** — Lithuania gathers most Ukrainian lands — Algirdas takes Kyiv after Blue Waters in 1362 — and rules for a century 'without changing the old ways', the lands keeping their laws, faith and Rus language; the borderlands go other ways (Transcarpathia under Hungary with Laborets' doomed Ungvar, Northern Bukovina in Moldavia, the north-east drawn toward Moscow); Crimea holds Genoese ports, Greek Theodoro and from the 1440s the Giray Khanate; towns win Magdeburg self-government and guild crafts flourish; the stateless culture holds through church schools and Drohobych's rectorship at Bologna; and the course closes by testing Plokhy's 'gates of Europe' against the whole story. 6 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (6 lessons).

**Totals: 25 lessons** (Level 1: 5 · Level 2: 4 · Level 3: 5 · Level 4: 5 · Level 5: 6 — 25 placements, all from the one Grade 7 student book, all NEW LESSON NEEDED as filed: Ukraine's own medieval national history, which no existing lesson covered).
