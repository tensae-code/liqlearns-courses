# About the History — Ukraine course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 7 *History of Ukraine*. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the book's.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Ukraine inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **five levels**, following the
book's own five sections, in its own order, as filed:

1. **Level 1 — Origins: Slavs, Kyiv and the Birth of
   Rus-Ukraine.** The ancient lands and the Slavs' settlement,
   Kyiv's founding, the state's birth, Oleh and Ihor, Olha's
   reform and Sviatoslav (§§1–5).
2. **Level 2 — The Golden Age: Volodymyr and Yaroslav.**
   The baptism of 988, the Ruska Pravda, the state as it was
   ruled and lived, and golden-age culture (§§7–11).
3. **Level 3 — Fragmentation: Many Princes, One Land.**
   The Yaroslavychi, Monomakh's reunion and its end, the
   three southern principalities, Halych and Volyn rising,
   and the age's flowering culture (§§13–17).
4. **Level 4 — Halych-Volyn and the Mongol Storm.** Roman's
   united western state, the Mongol invasion and Kyiv's fall
   in 1240, Danylo crowned king of Rus, his successors, and
   the culture that kept the inheritance (§§19–23).
5. **Level 5 — The Late Middle Ages: Ukrainian Lands in New
   States.** Lithuania and the borderlands, Crimea and the
   Khanate, towns and Magdeburg law, the stateless culture
   holding, and the course's closing generalisation
   (§§25–29, §31).

All 25 lessons stand on a **student book** and count in the
acceptance reckoning below; no lesson in this course stands
on teacher-guide evidence.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Rus-Ukraine:* "Begin on green hills above a great river,
  where three brothers and their sister found Kyiv. Watch a
  loose world of tribes become a state; watch it baptised in
  the Dnipro and given a written law; watch it break apart
  among brothers, burn under the Mongols, and rise again in
  the west under a crowned king. Then walk the centuries when
  the land had no state of its own — and its schools, towns
  and scholars held the story anyway — to the gates where
  Europe and the steppe have always met. This is Ukraine's
  own first state, told by Ukraine's own book."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Book 1, the Grade 7 student book** — One national story
told reign by reign from the book's own chronicle sources —
every paragraph opens with a question, works a primary source
(the Povist / chronicle excerpts, Ruska Pravda, Monomakh's
Instruction), and closes with "Знаю минуле — осмислюю
сьогодення — прогнозую майбутнє" (I know the past, I make
sense of the present, I forecast the future) tasks, so the
medieval Rus state is taught as Ukraine's own first state,
"Rus-Ukraine", not as a neighbour's prehistory. *(Student
book — Ukraine's own way, as filed.)*
