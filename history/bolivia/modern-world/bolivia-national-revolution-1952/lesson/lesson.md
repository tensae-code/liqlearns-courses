# Bolivia: The National Revolution 1952

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Bolivia: The National Revolution 1952** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — La Revolución Nacional de 1952

On 9–10 April 1952 an armed mobilisation of soldiers and worker leaders defeats the army; worker control (control obrero) follows in the mines, and the Agrarian Reform remakes the countryside, though spontaneously and insufficiently. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — El Voto Universal y el MNR

The revolution's deepest change is universal suffrage, first exercised in the 1956 elections, while the MNR's two wings pull the National Revolution into internal conflict. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Año 6: La Revolución Nacional

Teacher-compendium unit, same arc as the student text. Hold the whole part in one picture: who is acting, where, and what they are trying to do. Then watch for the turn — the moment their world stops being what it was and becomes something new.

## Sub lesson 4 — Año 6: Reorientación de la Revolución Nacional

The National Revolution is reoriented — stabilisation, the eastern development turn and the road to 1964. Tell it back step by step, in order, and the part explains itself: a world as it was, a turn that breaks or builds it, and a world afterwards that carries the mark of that turn.
