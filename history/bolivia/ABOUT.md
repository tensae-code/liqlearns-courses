# About the History — Bolivia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Bolivia's own Ministerio de Educación Ciencias Sociales learning texts. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Bolivia inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the six-year arc of Bolivia's own Ciencias Sociales books (see CURRICULUM.md for the derivation): Level 1 — first steps, human beginnings and the first civilisations (16 lessons); Level 2 — Abya Yala whole, then the colonial world (15 lessons); Level 3 — the medieval world, new ideas and independence (13 lessons, teacher-guide evidence only, as filed); Level 4 — Bolivia founded: republic, land, silver and the sea (10 lessons); Level 5 — the world in crisis and Bolivia toward 1952 (13 lessons, teacher-guide evidence only, as filed); Level 6 — world war, Cold War and Bolivia's revolution to today (15 lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The arc whole:* "First, what history is and how the first peoples and civilisations rise — ending on the Altiplano's own Chiripa, Tiwanaku and Aymara señoríos. Then Abya Yala whole — Inca, Moxos, Guaraní — and the colonial world that breaks it, to the great rebellions. Then the long road through the medieval world and new ideas to independence, Bolivia's founding and its nineteenth century of land, silver, rubber and the lost sea, the crisis century and the Chaco, and at the top 1952, dictatorship, the Gas War and democratic recovery."
