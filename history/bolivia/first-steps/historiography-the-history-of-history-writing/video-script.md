# Video script — Historiography: The History of History Writing


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

Students weigh the theses for and against Spain's "Black Legend", reading Bartolomé de las Casas (1542) against its deniers. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Revisionist historians re-argue the independence — students learn the national story itself is debated. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

Traditional-positivist history is set against critical, reflexive history — the method unit of the fifth year. Hold the whole part in one picture: who is acting, where, and what they are trying to do. Then watch for the turn — the moment their world stops being what it was and becomes something new.


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Bolivia.
