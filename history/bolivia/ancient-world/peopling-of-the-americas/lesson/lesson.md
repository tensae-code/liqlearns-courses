# The Peopling of the Americas

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **The Peopling of the Americas** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Poblamiento de América

The first Americans arrive over the Bering land bridge, and perhaps also by coastal sea routes, peopling a new continent. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Año 1: Poblamiento del Abya Yala

The peopling of Abya Yala — the Americas under their indigenous name — opens America's own history. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The first Americans arrive over the Bering land bridge, and perhaps also by coastal sea routes, peopling a new continent. Give one real example: The peopling of Abya Yala — the Americas under their indigenous name — opens America's own history. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: compare your first map and line with the story you now know, and fix what was wrong.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The first Americans arrive over the Bering land bridge, and perhaps also by coastal sea routes, peopling a new continent.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
