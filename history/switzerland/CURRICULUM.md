# History — Switzerland course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Switzerland inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Switzerland's set in hand is one
teaching package in three parallel language editions (German
original; Italian and Romansh translations), all from the canton
of Graubünden: a slim pupil reader, *Sorge oder Zwang?* ("Care
or Coercion?"), that tells five true life-stories from the
canton, and a teacher companion at each of two levels —
Sekundarstufe I (the reader's own level) and Sekundarstufe II
(the same five fates re-entered at research depth, with a
deepening and extra documents per case). So the ladder follows
the package's own order: the method first — how this history is
read, from evidence, telling fact from opinion (Level 1, the
companions' introduction and the reader's source pages) — then
the five fates themselves, each told first as the reader tells
it, then deepened by the companions' background and documents
(Level 2, the reader's five cases with both companions) — and
last remembrance, the canton's own signs that these lives are
not forgotten (Level 3, the Sek II companion's closing
teaching). The ladder stops where the books in hand stop, not
because Switzerland teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Graubünden teaching package *Sorge oder Zwang?* — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: How This History Is Read** — the method of
   the whole package: a schoolbook built like a public history
   magazine, where the pupil steps into the victims' place,
   hears authorities and bystanders too, works the original
   sources and reaches their own judgement; a written history
   built from the little that survives (the Albin family's four
   documents; what a memoir can and cannot prove); and fact
   told from opinion, where reports that blamed a girl's
   wandering on her "blood" decided a court case, and a home's
   glowing facade hid exhaustion and humiliation. 3 lessons,
   in one title: `first-steps/` (3 lessons).

2. **Care or Coercion: The Five Fates** — the reader's five
   cases, each with its companions' background and deepening:
   the overview of the compulsory welfare measures (the poor
   sorted "worthy" and "unworthy", the 1912 Civil Code, the
   peak before the AHV 1948, the rights turn of the 1970s);
   the Albin family, dissolved 1949–1953 against Canova's
   appeal; Uschi Waser, taken at seven months by the "Children
   of the Road" scheme that took 586 Yenish children; Cornelia
   Studer and the "Gott hilft" home at Zizers, praised by its
   own 1955 inspection; Florian Branger, administratively
   detained without a court until his world shrank to walls;
   and Ruedi Hofer, hired out to over thirty farms, closing on
   whether amends can be made — the 2017 law and the payment
   named a "solidarity contribution", not compensation.
   6 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (6 lessons).

3. **Remembering: Signs of Remembrance** — heritage as a
   country's public memory of its own wrongdoing: the place of
   remembrance above Chur (2018), the Rätisches Museum's
   special exhibition, the special stamp that funds emergency
   aid, and the schoolbook itself made as an act of
   remembrance. 1 lesson, in one title: `first-steps/`
   (1 lesson).

**Totals: 10 lessons** (Level 1: 3 · Level 2: 6 · Level 3: 1
— 25 placements, from the German editions of one pupil reader
and its two teacher companions; the Italian and Romansh
parallel editions are placed once under the German editions,
as filed).
