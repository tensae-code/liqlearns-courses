# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing your town keeps on purpose — a monument, a museum shelf, a name on a wall. Write down who it is kept for. This lesson is about a canton deciding, in stone, paper and print, to remember the people its own offices once wronged.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Heritage is what a country chooses to remember in public: Graubünden opened a place of remembrance above Chur in 2018, the Rätisches Museum mounted a special exhibition, a special stamp funded emergency aid — and this schoolbook itself was made as an act of remembrance, so that the victims of the welfare measures are not forgotten twice. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
