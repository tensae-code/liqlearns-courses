# Video script — What History Is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History, in this course, is read the way this schoolbook was built: like a public history magazine — you step into the victims' place, you also hear the authorities and the bystanders, you work the original sources yourself, and in the end you must reach your own judgement.

This course opens with a lesson about itself. The Swiss schoolbook behind it was not built like an ordinary textbook. It was built like a public history magazine — the kind of thing adults read — because its makers wanted pupils to do real historical work, not to memorise a finished story.

The method has four moves. First, step into the place of the people it happened to: the children taken away, the parents judged, the man locked up. Second, listen also to the other voices — the officials who signed the orders and the neighbours who watched and said nothing — because a judgement that hears only one side is not yet a judgement. Third, work the original sources yourself: the reports, letters and files are printed in the book as photographs, so your hands are on the same paper the historians used. Fourth — and this is the turn — reach your own judgement. The book asks its question in its very title, care or coercion, and refuses to answer it for you.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Switzerland.
