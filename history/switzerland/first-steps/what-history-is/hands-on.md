# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take any old family photograph or letter you know of. Write down three voices that could speak about it: the person it happened to, the person who decided it, and a person who only watched. This lesson is about reading history with all three voices — and judging for yourself.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History, in this course, is read the way this schoolbook was built: like a public history magazine — you step into the victims' place, you also hear the authorities and the bystanders, you work the original sources yourself, and in the end you must reach your own judgement. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
