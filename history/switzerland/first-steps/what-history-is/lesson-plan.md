# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Switzerland · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How This History Is Read (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History, in this course, is read the way this schoolbook was built: like a public history magazine — you step into the victims' place, you also hear the authorities and the bystanders, you work the original sources yourself, and in the end you must reach your own judgement.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How This History Is Read — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. 2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the book itself works.

- Expects the book to hand down a finished verdict → this book deliberately ends each case on an open judgement question; the reader's own judgement is the work, not a failure of the book.
- Go back to the one thing: History, in this course, is read the way this schoolbook was built: like a public history magazine — you step into the victims' place, you also hear the authorities and the bystanders, you work the original sources yourself, and in the end you must reach your own judgement. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
