# Video script — Fact or Opinion

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Words written as fact can carry opinion inside them: home reports praised Ursula Waser as a nice girl, then blamed her wandering on her 'blood' — and those same judgements, treated as fact, decided a court case against her twenty years later; a home's glowing reports and a survivor's memoir can describe the same house in opposite ways.

The reports on Ursula Waser begin kindly. She is, the visitors write, a nice girl. Then comes the 'but': her restlessness, her running, her wandering are blamed on her 'blood' — on the travelling people she was born to. It is written in the same neat hand, on the same official paper, as if it were a measured fact like her height.

It was not a fact. It was a judgement — an opinion about a whole people — dressed in a report's clothing. And judgements written down do not stay on paper. Twenty years later, when Ursula stood in court against the stepfather who had abused her, the court weighed those old reports — and believed them over her. The lesson is sharp: before you trust any written claim, ask which part could actually be checked, and which part is somebody's judgement wearing a fact's coat.

The children's home 'Gott hilft' at Zizers had two faces. Its donor newsletter told supporters of a happy house. Inspectors came, looked, and wrote glowing reports. That was the facade — the face the home showed the world.

Behind it, Cornelia Studer's memoir tells of exhaustion and humiliation: children working before dawn, hunger used as punishment, adults who were never reported. Both accounts are written down. Both claim to describe the same house. In 2016 the question was put openly, and four people drew four different balances: Cornelia herself, her daughter, a researcher, and the home's own present-day director. Fact or opinion is not a game here — it is how a whole country decides what really happened inside its own walls.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Switzerland.
