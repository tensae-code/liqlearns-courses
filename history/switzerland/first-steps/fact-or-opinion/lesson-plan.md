# Lesson plan — Fact or Opinion

**Course:** History — Switzerland · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Fact or Opinion
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How This History Is Read (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Words written as fact can carry opinion inside them: home reports praised Ursula Waser as a nice girl, then blamed her wandering on her 'blood' — and those same judgements, treated as fact, decided a court case against her twenty years later; a home's glowing reports and a survivor's memoir can describe the same house in opposite ways.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How This History Is Read — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **'A nice girl, but…' — judgements that decided a court case** — Home reports praise Ursula as a nice girl, then blame her wandering on her 'blood', and those same judgements, written as fact, decide a court case against her twenty years later.
2. **Facade and what stands behind it** — The home's donor newsletter and glowing inspection reports describe a happy house while Cornelia's memoir describes exhaustion and humiliation, and in 2016 the victim, her daughter, a researcher and the home's own director each draw a different balance.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks anything in an official report is a fact → a report can state a checkable fact (22 moves) and hide a judgement ('her blood') in the same sentence; only the checkable part is fact.
- Go back to the one thing: Words written as fact can carry opinion inside them: home reports praised Ursula Waser as a nice girl, then blamed her wandering on her 'blood' — and those same judgements, treated as fact, decided a court case against her twenty years later; a home's glowing reports and a survivor's memoir can describe the same house in opposite ways.
