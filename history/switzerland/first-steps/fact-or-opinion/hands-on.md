# Hands-on — Fact or Opinion

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Read these two sentences: 'The girl was moved twenty-two times.' 'The girl wandered because of her blood.' Write down which one is a fact you could check, and which one is a judgement dressed as a fact. This lesson is about telling them apart — because courts once failed to.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Words written as fact can carry opinion inside them: home reports praised Ursula Waser as a nice girl, then blamed her wandering on her 'blood' — and those same judgements, treated as fact, decided a court case against her twenty years later; a home's glowing reports and a survivor's memoir can describe the same house in opposite ways. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
