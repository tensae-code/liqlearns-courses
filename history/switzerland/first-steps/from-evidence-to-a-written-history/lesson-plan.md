# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Switzerland · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How This History Is Read (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A written history is built from what survives: for the Albin family, two home-visit reports, a psychiatric report and a lawyer's appeal are all that is left — and a person's own memoir must be judged just as carefully, because lived and merely heard episodes merge in memory while the official files tell yet another story.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How This History Is Read — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Albin family in four documents** — Two welfare visitors' home-visit reports, a psychiatric report and lawyer Gaudenz Canova's appeal are all that survives of the family — and the cantonal government still approves the dissolution in 1953.
2. **What a memoir can and cannot prove** — Cornelia Studer opens her memoir by admitting her lived and her merely heard episodes have merged; the official files differ sharply from both, and pupils must judge what such a witness account can and cannot prove.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats a memoir as either all true or all lies → a witness account is evidence to be weighed: memory merges lived and heard episodes, and the files differ from both; the historian judges what each can prove.
