# From Evidence to a Written History — depth

This lesson has sub lessons; each is proved in order, the one before opening the next. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — A written history is built from what survives: for the Albin family, two home-visit reports, a psychiatric report and a lawyer's appeal are all that is left — and a person's own memoir must be judged just as carefully, because lived and merely heard episodes merge in memory while the official files tell yet another story. — in their own words, give one real example from the story as taught, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new home, school or street situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
