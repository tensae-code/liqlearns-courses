# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine a whole family's life had to be told from four pieces of paper. Write down what four papers about your own family would show — and what they would leave out. This lesson is about building a true story from the little that survives.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written history is built from what survives: for the Albin family, two home-visit reports, a psychiatric report and a lawyer's appeal are all that is left — and a person's own memoir must be judged just as carefully, because lived and merely heard episodes merge in memory while the official files tell yet another story. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
