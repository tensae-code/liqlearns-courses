# About the History — Switzerland course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Graubünden teaching package *Sorge oder Zwang?* ("Care or
Coercion?"). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in
a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Switzerland inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **three levels**, following the
package's own order, as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How This History Is Read.** The
   method of the whole package (a schoolbook built like a
   public history magazine; every voice heard; the reader's
   own judgement); a written history built from the little
   that survives, and a memoir weighed as evidence; and fact
   told from opinion in the reports that followed two of the
   five children.
2. **Level 2 — Care or Coercion: The Five Fates.** The
   overview of the compulsory welfare measures, then the
   reader's five cases in its own order — the Albin family,
   Uschi Waser, Cornelia Studer, Florian Branger, Ruedi
   Hofer — each told first as the reader tells it, then
   deepened by the Sek I companion's background and the Sek II
   companion's documents.
3. **Level 3 — Remembering: Signs of Remembrance.** Heritage
   as the canton's public memory of its own wrongdoing: the
   place of remembrance, the museum exhibition, the stamp,
   and the schoolbook itself.

Eight lessons stand on a **student book** and count in the
acceptance reckoning below; two lessons stand on
teacher-guide evidence only, acceptance 0, labelled.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Care, or coercion?:* "Begin by learning how this history
  asks to be read: every voice heard, the original files in
  your own hands, and the judgement left to you. Then meet
  the five fates of one canton. A family of eight children,
  taken apart piece by piece. A Yenish baby, moved
  twenty-two times by a scheme that took 586 children. A
  girl behind the praised facade of a children's home. A
  sailor locked away by an office, without a court. A boy
  hired out to thirty farms, asking whether amends can ever
  be made. And at the end, a canton that builds a place of
  remembrance above its capital — and puts this book in its
  children's hands as the remembering itself. Care, or
  coercion? The books ask. You judge."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**The whole set** — History is taught as five true
life-stories from one canton (Graubünden/Grischun/Grigioni).
A slim pupil reader tells each fate as a story with facsimile
original sources, and a teacher companion at each level
(Sekundarstufe I ≈ Grade 7, Sekundarstufe II ≈ Grade 10)
supplies the background, the worksheets and — at Sek II —
extra documents and a deepening question per case. The
question over the whole set is in its title: care, or
coercion? *(The set's own way, as filed.)*

**Book 1, the reader (student book)** — Five representative
fates stand for over 100,000 people hit by compulsory welfare
measures; each story is told first, then continued by the
pupil from photographed original files, and each ends on an
open judgement question, not a verdict. *(Student book.)*

**Book 4, the Sek I companion (teacher guide)** — The
companion turns each fate into a jigsaw-class
(Gruppenpuzzle): groups master one case from background
briefings plus worksheets and teach it to the class, judged
against Lehrplan 21 competences (Swiss history of the 20th
century, children's and human rights). *(Teacher guide —
evidence.)*

**Book 7, the Sek II companion (teacher guide)** — At the
upper level the same five fates are re-entered at research
depth — pupils weigh a lawyer's appeal against a government's
rejection word for word, debate whether a welfare scheme was
cultural genocide, and judge a reparation law against the
popular initiative that forced it. *(Teacher guide —
evidence.)*
