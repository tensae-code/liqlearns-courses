# Hands-on — Cornelia Studer and the 'Gott Hilft' Children's Home

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A home's name means 'God helps'. Write down what a seven-year-old would expect behind such a name — then what you would check before believing it. This lesson walks through the gate at Zizers with Cornelia, and then reads the inspectors' own report.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Seven-year-old Cornelia Studer and her brother were sent to the 'Gott hilft' home at Zizers 'for rest and order' and found eight years of pre-dawn labour, beatings, hunger punishments and unreported abuse — inside a self-financing home network, founded in 1916, that ran on children's farm labour and still won top marks from cantonal inspectors in 1955. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
