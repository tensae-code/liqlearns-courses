# Hands-on — Switzerland's Compulsory Welfare Measures: An Overview

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down the question in this course's title — care, or coercion? — and one sentence on what each word would look like if the state came to a poor family's door. This lesson tells how a whole system could call itself care while it did coercion's work.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: For over a century Switzerland's welfare authorities could take children from poor families, lock people up without a court and hire children out as labour — the era grew from a poor law that fought the poor instead of poverty, peaked before social insurance, and ended only with the post-war boom, the 1948 social insurance, women's suffrage, 1968 and the 1974 human-rights convention. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
