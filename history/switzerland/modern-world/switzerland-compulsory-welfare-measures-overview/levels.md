# Switzerland's Compulsory Welfare Measures: An Overview — depth

This lesson has sub lessons; each is proved in order, the one before opening the next. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — For over a century Switzerland's welfare authorities could take children from poor families, lock people up without a court and hire children out as labour — the era grew from a poor law that fought the poor instead of poverty, peaked before social insurance, and ended only with the post-war boom, the 1948 social insurance, women's suffrage, 1968 and the 1974 human-rights convention. — in their own words, give one real example from the story as taught, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new home, school or street situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
