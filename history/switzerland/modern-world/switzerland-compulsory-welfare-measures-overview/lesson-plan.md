# Lesson plan — Switzerland's Compulsory Welfare Measures: An Overview

**Course:** History — Switzerland · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Switzerland's Compulsory Welfare Measures: An Overview
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Care or Coercion: The Five Fates (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** For over a century Switzerland's welfare authorities could take children from poor families, lock people up without a court and hire children out as labour — the era grew from a poor law that fought the poor instead of poverty, peaked before social insurance, and ended only with the post-war boom, the 1948 social insurance, women's suffrage, 1968 and the 1974 human-rights convention.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How the era began and ended** — Switzerland stops starving but stays bitterly poor, officials split the poor into 'worthy' and 'unworthy', the fight turns from poverty onto the poor themselves, and only the post-war boom, social insurance and the 1974 human-rights convention end the era.
2. **The roots: poor law, the 1912 Civil Code, and the rights turn** — An 1857 Grisons poor ordinance already locks up the 'work-shy', the 1912 Civil Code hands guardianship offices wide preventive powers, measures peak before social insurance (AHV 1948), and women's suffrage, 1968 and the 1974 human-rights convention finally break the system.
