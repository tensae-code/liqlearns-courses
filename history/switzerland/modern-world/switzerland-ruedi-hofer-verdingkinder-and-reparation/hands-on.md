# Hands-on — Ruedi Hofer, the Hired-Out Children, and Whether Amends Can Be Made

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down what money cannot give back. Keep the list: this lesson ends the five fates with the man who drew a scythe strapped to his ruined hand, and with a country's attempt — a law, a fund, an apology, a stamp — to answer him.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ruedi Hofer was passed to over 30 farm places as a Verdingkind — a child hired out as labour — lost half a hand at eight in an unexplained explosion, was exploited and abused, and never got a steady job; he stands for over 100,000 hired-out Swiss children, and his case asks the course's closing question: can such a wrong ever be made good — the state answers with a 2017 law and a payment it deliberately calls a 'solidarity contribution', not compensation. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
