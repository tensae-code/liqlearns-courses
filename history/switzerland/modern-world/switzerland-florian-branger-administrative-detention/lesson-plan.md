## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Home from the sea, into the walls** — Sailor Florian Branger comes home to Chur, collects small convictions, and the guardianship authority answers not with trials but with ever longer administrative stays in the Realta work institution, prison and psychiatric clinic until his world shrinks to walls.
2. **Guilty — to blame himself — or partly to blame?** — Branger's own handwritten life story, a court judgement and a clinic doctor pleading for his accident pension face Realta's refusal to take him back and its defence of chaining inmates who run away.
3. **Fürstenau and Realta: detention without a court** — From 1840 Graubünden locks the 'dissolute' and 'work-shy' into Fürstenau, then Realta, by office decision without a court, in foot-chains into the 1930s; some ten years of Branger's life are detention on top of his short prison sentences, and about 80% of the detained are men judged against male work norms.
4. **Realta from inside, Loosli 1938, and the cemetery** — Realta's 1917 statutes name the 'dissolute' it may hold, a 1932 newspaper looks inside, ex-inmate Carl Albert Loosli in 1938 calls Swiss administrative detention the country's own concentration camps, and in 2016 archaeologists read 103 inmates' skeletons from the institution cemetery.
