# Hands-on — Florian Branger and Administrative Detention

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down the difference between a prison sentence a court gives and a lock-up an office orders. Keep the paper in front of you: this lesson is about a man whose world shrank to walls by the second kind — and about the writer who called it Switzerland's own concentration camps.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Sailor Florian Branger came home to Chur and collected small convictions; the guardianship authority answered not with trials but with ever longer administrative stays — in the Realta work institution, prison and a psychiatric clinic — under a system that, from 1840, locked the 'dissolute' and 'work-shy' into institutions by office decision, without a court, some ten years of his life in detention on top of his sentences. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
