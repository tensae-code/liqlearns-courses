# Lesson plan — The Albin Family Dissolved

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Eight children and no family any more** — Josef and Sophia Albin have eight children in twelve years of extreme poverty, the authorities declare them incapable, take the children away in stages 1949–1953 and block all contact, and both parents die without the family ever reuniting.
2. **Measured against the model family** — The 1912 code makes the husband head of the family and 'neglect' a stretchable word, the Albins are measured against that model and fail it, while other poor families got beds, furniture and a 'family rescue' instead of dissolution.
3. **The appeal, answered point by point** — Lawyer Gaudenz Canova appeals in full against taking the Albins' last four children, the Grisons government answers point by point and rejects him, and pupils must reconstruct 1947–1953 from both files and deliver their own verdict.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
