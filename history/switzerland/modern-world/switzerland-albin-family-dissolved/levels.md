# The Albin Family Dissolved — depth

This lesson has sub lessons; each is proved in order, the one before opening the next. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Josef and Sophia Albin had eight children in twelve years of extreme poverty; the authorities declared the parents incapable, took the children away in stages between 1949 and 1953, blocked all contact — and both parents died without the family ever reuniting, judged against a family model other poor families were helped to meet instead. — in their own words, give one real example from the story as taught, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new home, school or street situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
