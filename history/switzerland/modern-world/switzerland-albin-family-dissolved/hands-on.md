# Hands-on — The Albin Family Dissolved

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Count eight children. Now imagine an office deciding, child by child over four years, that none of them may stay. Write down what you think the office believed it was doing. This lesson tells the Albin family's story — and then puts the office's own law on the table.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Josef and Sophia Albin had eight children in twelve years of extreme poverty; the authorities declared the parents incapable, took the children away in stages between 1949 and 1953, blocked all contact — and both parents died without the family ever reuniting, judged against a family model other poor families were helped to meet instead. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
