# Hands-on — Uschi Waser and the Yenish Children of the Road

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the number 22. That is how many times one girl was moved in thirteen years. Beside it write the number 586 — the children taken by the scheme that took her. This lesson tells her story, and the scheme's.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ursula 'Uschi' Waser, a Yenish child, was taken by police at seven months for Pro Juventute's 'Children of the Road' scheme — a scheme that took 586 Yenish children between 1926 and 1973 to erase a travelling culture — was moved 22 times in 13 years, was not believed in court against her abusive stepfather, and only as an adult read the files that had betrayed her. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
