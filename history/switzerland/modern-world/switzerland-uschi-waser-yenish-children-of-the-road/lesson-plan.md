# Lesson plan — Uschi Waser and the Yenish Children of the Road

**Course:** History — Switzerland · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Uschi Waser and the Yenish Children of the Road
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Care or Coercion: The Five Fates (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ursula 'Uschi' Waser, a Yenish child, was taken by police at seven months for Pro Juventute's 'Children of the Road' scheme — a scheme that took 586 Yenish children between 1926 and 1973 to erase a travelling culture — was moved 22 times in 13 years, was not believed in court against her abusive stepfather, and only as an adult read the files that had betrayed her.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Taken at seven months, moved twenty-two times** — Baby Ursula, a Yenish child, is taken by police at seven months for Pro Juventute's 'Children of the Road' scheme, is moved 22 times in 13 years, is not believed in court against her abusive stepfather, and only as an adult reads the files that betrayed her.
2. **The scheme: 586 children, 1926–1973** — Alfred Siegfried's scheme takes 586 Yenish children (294 from Graubünden) to erase a travelling culture, parents appeal in vain, journalist Hans Caprez's 1972 articles force the scheme to close, and federal president and Pro Juventute apologise in 1986/87.
3. **The scheme in its own words** — Siegfried writes in 1943 that the travelling people's family bond must be torn apart, his office files Ursula's whole childhood on green index cards, and pupils weigh that record against Nazi persecution of the Sinti and Roma and against the charge of cultural genocide.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
