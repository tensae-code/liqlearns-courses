# Uschi Waser and the Yenish Children of the Road — depth

This lesson has sub lessons; each is proved in order, the one before opening the next. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Ursula 'Uschi' Waser, a Yenish child, was taken by police at seven months for Pro Juventute's 'Children of the Road' scheme — a scheme that took 586 Yenish children between 1926 and 1973 to erase a travelling culture — was moved 22 times in 13 years, was not believed in court against her abusive stepfather, and only as an adult read the files that had betrayed her. — in their own words, give one real example from the story as taught, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new home, school or street situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
