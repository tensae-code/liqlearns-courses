# History — Morocco course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Morocco inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Morocco's book in hand is one —
the Grade 5 *Al-Moufid in Social Studies* teacher guide — and
its own History component is the ladder. The guide's own
programme table runs Grade 4 "the recent past", Grade 5
"Morocco in the ancient age", Grade 6 "prehistory and the
historical eras", and Grade 5 walks a child in 12 lessons
from what a historian is, through prehistoric and Amazigh
Morocco, to the dynasty sequence Idrisid → Almoravid →
Almohad → Marinid → Saadian → Alaouite, each dynasty studied
through one standing monument (Qarawiyyin, the Almoravid
Dome, a madrasa, El Badi Palace, the kasbahs). So the ladder
is: the historian's craft first (Level 1, the guide's History
Lessons 1–2, three lessons as filed), ancient Morocco
(Level 2, Lessons 3–6), the dynasties (Level 3, Lessons
7–10), and the sharifian dynasties that close the guide's
line (Level 4, Lessons 11–12). The ladder stops where the
book in hand stops, not because Morocco teaches nothing
more. Every lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only —
the acceptance reckoning in ABOUT.md says so plainly.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Al-Moufid Social Studies book — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book contents
map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: The Historian's Craft** — before any
   Moroccan story, the craft itself, as the guide's first
   two History lessons teach it: what history is and what a
   historian does with the tools of the trade; the evidence —
   the witnesses of the past (شواهد الماضي) — found and
   questioned on the road from evidence to a written
   history; and dating the past, the line that separates
   prehistory from the historical eras, read with
   development, simultaneity and succession. 3 lessons, in
   one title: `first-steps/` (3 lessons).

2. **Ancient Morocco** — the land before and at the meeting
   with the sea powers: prehistoric Morocco, the first
   Moroccans hunting, sheltering and surviving, known from
   their remains; the Amazigh civilisation — its own system
   of rule, daily life and script; the Phoenician and
   Carthaginian meeting, Hanno's voyage and Lixus; and Roman
   Morocco, the occupation, the exploitation, and Aedemon's
   revolt. 4 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/`
   (3 lessons) and `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson).

3. **The Dynasties of Morocco** — the dynasty sequence as
   the guide walks it, one monument each: the Idrisids, the
   coming of Islam, Fez and the Qarawiyyin; the Almoravids
   uniting Morocco, Marrakech and the Almoravid Dome; the
   Almohads uniting the Islamic West from Tripoli to
   Seville; and the Marinids, remembered for creativity —
   the madrasas of Fez and the Mawlid still celebrated.
   4 lessons, in one title: `ancient-world/` (4 lessons).

4. **The Sharifian Dynasties** — the guide's close: the
   Saadians, sharifs from the south, and the economic
   golden age of sugar (the Chichaoua works) and El Badi
   Palace; and the Alaouites from Tafilalt — Moulay Rashid
   unifying the country, Moulay Ismail building the state,
   the kasbahs as its model in stone. 2 lessons, in one
   title: `modern-world/` (2 lessons).

**Totals: 13 lessons** (Level 1: 3 · Level 2: 4 · Level 3: 4 ·
Level 4: 2 — 13 placements, all from one teacher guide,
evidence only, acceptance 0).
