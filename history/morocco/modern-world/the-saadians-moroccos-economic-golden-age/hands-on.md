# Hands-on — The Saadians: Morocco's Economic Golden Age

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine a country that discovers it can grow something the whole world wants to buy. The fields fill, the works smoke, the ships queue — and money pours in. What should the rulers do with such wealth? Build, of course. But build what? The Saadians' answer still has a name: El Badi — the Incomparable.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: sharifs from the south reunite Morocco, grow rich on sugar and gold, and spend it on a palace of marble and onyx — the Saadian age is the economic one: the sugar works of Chichaoua, and El Badi Palace as its monument. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
