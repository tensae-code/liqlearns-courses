# Video script — The Saadians: Morocco's Economic Golden Age

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Sharifs from the south reunite Morocco, grow rich on sugar and gold, and spend it on a palace of marble and onyx — the Saadian age is the economic one: the sugar works of Chichaoua, and El Badi Palace as its monument.

From the **south** of Morocco rise the **Saadians** — sharifs, descendants of the Prophet, like the Idrisids long before them. The lesson follows their state's **founding and stages** as they reunite a divided Morocco under one rule again.

But the book's chosen word for the Saadians is **economic prosperity**. At the centre of the lesson stands an ambitious economic project studied in depth: the **sugar industry** — sugar grown and worked in Morocco, with the **Chichaoua works** as the case in hand, turning the land's cane into wealth the world would pay for, alongside the gold of the age.

Wealth that size gets spent in stone. The dynasty's monument study is **El Badi Palace** in Marrakech — a palace of marble and onyx, built to show exactly how rich the Saadian age had become. Its name means the Incomparable, and it was meant to be believed.

So the Saadian chapter is the golden, economic one: sugar works smoking, gold flowing, and a palace that spent the age's wealth on wonder.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
