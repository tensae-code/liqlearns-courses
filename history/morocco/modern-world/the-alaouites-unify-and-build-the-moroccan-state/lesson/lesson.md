# The Alaouites Unify and Build the Moroccan State

## Do it first

End the course where it began: with a map. Morocco lies divided — region against region, no single rule holding it. Somebody has to travel that map, town by town, and make it one country again. Then, once it is one, somebody has to build the state that holds it. Two rulers, two jobs: that is the last lesson.

From **Tafilalt**, in Morocco's south-east, comes the last family in the line: the **Alaouites**, sharifs like the Saadians before them. The book gives their state two jobs in its very title — **unifying the country** and **building the state**.

The first job belongs to **Moulay Rashid**. The lesson follows his efforts to unite the country **on a map** — the historian's map again, as with Rome's occupation long before, but this time the map fills in with one Moroccan rule, region by region, until the broken country is stitched back together.

The second job belongs to **Moulay Ismail**: building the state — and building it, in part, literally. Under Moulay Ismail rises the age's **military architecture**, with the **kasbahs** as the model: fortress-towns planted across the land, walls that hold the state together in stone as its laws hold it together on paper.

And there the book's line stops: from the first hunters in the soil, through the Amazigh, the sailors, Rome, and dynasty after dynasty, to a Morocco united and built into a state — one unbroken national line, exactly as Morocco's own programme tells it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: another sharifian family from Tafilalt stitches a broken Morocco back together and walls it with fortress-towns — Moulay Rashid unites the country, and Moulay Ismail builds the state, with the kasbahs as its model in stone. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Alaouites, Tafilalt, Moulay Rashid, Moulay Ismail, kasbahs, unifying, building the state. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
