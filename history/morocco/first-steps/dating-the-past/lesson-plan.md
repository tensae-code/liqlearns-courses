# Lesson plan — Dating the Past

**Course:** History — Morocco · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a line drawn on the page separates the ages before writing from the ages with it — prehistory from history — and everything Moroccan hangs on that line in order, using development, simultaneity and succession.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

- Stands in path order after **From Evidence to a Written History** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a line drawn on the page separates the ages before writing from the ages with it — prehistory from history — and everything Moroccan hangs on that line in order, using development, simultaneity and succession.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the Morocco they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Morocco and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

- Go back to the one thing: a line drawn on the page separates the ages before writing from the ages with it — prehistory from history — and everything Moroccan hangs on that line in order, using development, simultaneity and succession. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
