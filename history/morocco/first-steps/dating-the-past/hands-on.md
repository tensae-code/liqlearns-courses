# Hands-on — Dating the Past

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a long line across a page. At the left write 'long, long ago' and at the right write 'today'. Now try to place three things on it: the first hunters in Morocco, the building of a great mosque-university in Fez, and your own birth. You have just built the historian's timeline — now this lesson draws its most important mark on that line.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a line drawn on the page separates the ages before writing from the ages with it — prehistory from history — and everything Moroccan hangs on that line in order, using development, simultaneity and succession. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
