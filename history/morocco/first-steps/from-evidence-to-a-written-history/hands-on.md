# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Hide a small object somewhere in the room while your companion looks away. Then give only clues — where it might be, what it is near. Watch: your companion cannot see the object, but from the clues they can work out where it is. The past is like that hidden object: gone, but it left clues behind.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the past survives in traces — the evidence, the witnesses of the past (شواهد الماضي) — and the historian's first job is to find them and question them before any history can be written. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
