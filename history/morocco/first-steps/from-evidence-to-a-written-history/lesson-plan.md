# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Morocco · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the past survives in traces — the evidence, the witnesses of the past (شواهد الماضي) — and the historian's first job is to find them and question them before any history can be written.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **What History Is** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the past survives in traces — the evidence, the witnesses of the past (شواهد الماضي) — and the historian's first job is to find them and question them before any history can be written.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the Morocco they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Morocco and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a written history comes first and the evidence afterwards → the order is the other way: find the witnesses, question them, and only then write.
- Go back to the one thing: the past survives in traces — the evidence, the witnesses of the past (شواهد الماضي) — and the historian's first job is to find them and question them before any history can be written. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
