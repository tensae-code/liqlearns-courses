# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before you read anything, look around the room and find three old things — a worn coin, an old photograph, a building stone, a grandparent's tool. For each one ask: who used this, and what could it tell me about the people who came before me? You have just picked up the historian's first tools: your eyes and your questions.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: history is a craft practised by people with tools — before any Moroccan story, a learner meets the historian: what history means, what a historian does, and the tools of the trade; history is not a list of kings. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
