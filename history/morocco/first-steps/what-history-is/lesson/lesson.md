# What History Is

## Do it first

Before you read anything, look around the room and find three old things — a worn coin, an old photograph, a building stone, a grandparent's tool. For each one ask: who used this, and what could it tell me about the people who came before me? You have just picked up the historian's first tools: your eyes and your questions.

Every story in this course — the first hunters, the Amazigh kingdoms, the great dynasties — was built by somebody. That somebody is the **historian**, and history is the historian's craft: finding out what really happened in the past, and telling it truly.

A historian is not a person who memorises a list of kings. A historian is a worker with **tools**. Some tools are in the hands: ways of digging, measuring, photographing and preserving what the past left behind. Some tools are in the head: questions. Who made this? When? Why? How do we know?

The Moroccan book this course follows puts this lesson first, before any Moroccan story at all — and that order is the teaching. First learn what the craft is. Then every story that follows can be asked the historian's question: how do we know this happened?

So history means the studied, tested story of the human past — built by people, with tools, from what the past left behind.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: history is a craft practised by people with tools — before any Moroccan story, a learner meets the historian: what history means, what a historian does, and the tools of the trade; history is not a list of kings. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: history, historian, tools of the trade, craft, the past. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
