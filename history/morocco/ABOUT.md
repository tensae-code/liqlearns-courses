# About the History — Morocco course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Al-Moufid Social Studies book for Grade 5. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Morocco inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **four levels**, following the
book's own History component as filed:

1. **Level 1 — first steps: the historian's craft.** Before
   any Moroccan story: what history is and what a historian
   does, the evidence/witnesses of the past (شواهد الماضي),
   and dating the past — prehistory against the historical
   eras on a timeline.
2. **Level 2 — ancient Morocco.** Prehistoric Morocco and
   the first Moroccans; the Amazigh civilisation; the
   Phoenician and Carthaginian meeting (Hanno, Lixus); and
   Roman occupation, exploitation and Aedemon's resistance.
3. **Level 3 — the dynasties of Morocco.** The Idrisids and
   the coming of Islam (Fez, the Qarawiyyin); the Almoravids
   uniting Morocco (Marrakech, the Almoravid Dome); the
   Almohads uniting the Islamic West; the Marinids and
   their creativity (the madrasas, the Mawlid).
4. **Level 4 — the sharifian dynasties.** The Saadians'
   economic golden age (sugar, the Chichaoua works, El Badi
   Palace); the Alaouites unifying the country (Moulay
   Rashid) and building the state (Moulay Ismail, the
   kasbahs).

All thirteen lessons stand on a **teacher guide** and count
nowhere in the acceptance reckoning below — they are
evidence only, labelled as such in every lesson.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *First the craft, then the land, then the line of
  dynasties:* "First, learn the historian's craft — the
  tools, the witnesses of the past, the line where writing
  begins. Then meet the first Moroccans in the soil, the
  Amazigh with their own rule and their own letters, the
  sailors from Tyre and Carthage, and Rome — and Aedemon
  rising against it. Then walk the line of dynasties:
  Idrisid, Almoravid, Almohad, Marinid, each remembered by
  one standing monument; the Saadians rich on sugar, and
  the Alaouites stitching Morocco back together and walling
  it with kasbahs."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Al-Moufid in Social Studies, Grade 5 (teacher guide)** —
Moroccan History is one unbroken national line — the
guide's own programme table runs Grade 4 "the recent past",
Grade 5 "Morocco in the ancient age", Grade 6 "prehistory
and the historical eras" — and Grade 5 walks a child from
what a historian is, through prehistoric and Amazigh
Morocco, to the dynasty sequence Idrisid → Almoravid →
Almohad → Marinid → Saadian → Alaouite, each dynasty
studied through one standing monument (Qarawiyyin, the
Almoravid Dome, a madrasa, El Badi Palace, the kasbahs).
*(Teacher guide.)*
