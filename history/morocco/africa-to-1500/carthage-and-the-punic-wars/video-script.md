# Video script — Carthage and the Punic Wars

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Sailors from Tyre and Carthage meet Amazigh Morocco, and both sides leave changed — a Phoenician trading centre on Moroccan ground, Hanno's voyage down Africa's west coast, and Lixus, the first city beyond the Pillars of Hercules.

Across the sea stood **Tyre**, home of the Phoenicians, the great sailors and traders of the ancient world, and its daughter city **Carthage** on Africa's northern shore. Their ships came to Morocco, and the meeting is studied the historian's way: a **Phoenician trading centre** on Moroccan ground, read from a picture and a plan — where the ships landed, where goods were stored, how the place was laid out.

The book calls what happened **mutual civilisational influence**: influence running both ways. The newcomers brought their goods, their ways and their letters; the Amazigh brought theirs. Both sides leave the meeting changed — that is what 'mutual' means.

Then the story takes to the sea. King **Hanno** the Carthaginian sails down Africa's **west coast**, past Morocco and beyond, on one of the famous voyages of the ancient world. And the Carthaginians found **Lixus** — named in the book as the first city beyond the **Pillars of Hercules**, the strait where the Mediterranean opens into the great ocean.

The lesson's filed name carries the wider story too — Carthage and the Punic Wars, the struggle that would later shake the whole western Mediterranean. It begins here, in trade, voyages and a city planted on Moroccan ground.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
