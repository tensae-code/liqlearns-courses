# Video script — Amazigh Civilisation of Ancient Morocco

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before any foreigner landed, the Amazigh governed themselves, built, dressed, ate and wrote in their own letters — a whole civilisation of their own.

Before any ship from across the sea landed, Morocco's own people — the **Amazigh** — already had a civilisation. This lesson reads it the historian's way, from a text and a diagram: first, their **system of rule** — the Amazigh governed themselves, with their own order, not as a crowd without leaders.

Then the lesson walks through **daily life**. Their **housing** — the homes they built for their land and weather. Their **clothing** — what they wore. Their **food** — what they ate, and here the book counts Morocco's **Jewish popular dishes** as part of the heritage on the table: the land's peoples and their dishes belong to one shared table.

And the Amazigh **wrote**. They had a script of their own — their own letters, in which a people can keep its words. A people with its own rule, its own homes, dress and food, and its own letters is not the edge of somebody else's story.

So when the sailors of the next lesson land on this coast, remember who they meet: not an empty shore, but the Amazigh civilisation of ancient Morocco.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
