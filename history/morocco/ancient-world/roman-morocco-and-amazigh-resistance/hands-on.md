# Hands-on — Roman Morocco and Amazigh Resistance

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a rough map of Morocco. Colour the coast and the rich lowlands one colour, and the mountains another. Now ask: if a great empire across the sea wanted this land's food and wealth, which colour would it hold tight, and where would its rule run thin? Your map is about to become this lesson's map.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Rome takes the coast and the grain, and an Amazigh freedman named Aedemon raises the mountains against the empire — occupation read on a map, exploitation named, and resistance with a name and a face. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
