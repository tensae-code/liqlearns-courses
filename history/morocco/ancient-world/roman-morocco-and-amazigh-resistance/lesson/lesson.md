# Roman Morocco and Amazigh Resistance

## Do it first

Draw a rough map of Morocco. Colour the coast and the rich lowlands one colour, and the mountains another. Now ask: if a great empire across the sea wanted this land's food and wealth, which colour would it hold tight, and where would its rule run thin? Your map is about to become this lesson's map.

The sailors came to trade. **Rome** came to rule. This lesson opens with the **map of the Roman occupation**: which parts of Morocco the empire held — the coast and the productive land it could reach and tax — and where its grip ended.

Then the book names what occupation meant: Rome **exploited** Morocco. The land's produce — above all its grain and wealth — was drawn off for the empire. Occupation is not just soldiers on a map; it is a land made to feed somebody else's power.

But the story does not end with the map. The Amazigh answered. The book gives the answer a model case and a name: **Aedemon**, an Amazigh freedman — a man who had himself been held in another's power — raised a **revolt** against Rome, and the mountains became the strong ground of resistance.

Aedemon's revolt did not end the occupation, but it fixed the pattern this course will see again and again in Morocco's story: an outside power takes the land, and Morocco's own people rise against it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rome takes the coast and the grain, and an Amazigh freedman named Aedemon raises the mountains against the empire — occupation read on a map, exploitation named, and resistance with a name and a face. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Roman occupation, exploitation, Aedemon, revolt, resistance, mountains. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
