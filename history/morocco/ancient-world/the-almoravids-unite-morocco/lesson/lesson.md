# The Almoravids Unite Morocco

## Do it first

Picture riders coming out of the Sahara, their faces veiled against the sand, carrying a strict idea of how people should live and pray. They are few, but they move as one. What happens when such a movement reaches a land of divided cities and rival rulers? Morocco is about to be made one.

Out of the **Sahara** came the **Almoravids** — a movement of reformers, veiled riders bound together by a strict religious idea and hard desert discipline. Where Morocco was divided, they brought one rule.

The lesson follows their work in two moves. First, **unifying the country**: the Almoravids take Morocco piece by piece and make it one state. Second, the **extension of the state**: the new power does not stop at Morocco's edge — it founds **Marrakech**, the city that will be its capital, and pushes on into **Spain**, ruling an empire that straddles the strait.

Their monument, the one the book stops to study, stands in Marrakech: the **Almoravid Dome — Qubbat al-Barudiyyin** — a small, beautiful domed building, all that survives above ground of the Almoravids' own palace age in their capital.

The dynasty fell, as dynasties do. But the shape it gave Morocco — one country, unified, with Marrakech at its heart — outlasted it, and the next dynasty will inherit both.
