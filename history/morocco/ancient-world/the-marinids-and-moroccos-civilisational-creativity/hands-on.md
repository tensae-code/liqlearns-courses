# Hands-on — The Marinids and Morocco's Civilisational Creativity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Some rulers are remembered for the lands they took. This dynasty is remembered for what it built and what it celebrated. Imagine walking into a college courtyard where every wall is carved, tiled and inlaid — built not for a palace, but for students. Who builds like that, and why? That question is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Zenata horsemen become sultans who fill Fez with colleges so beautiful the dynasty is remembered in tile and cedar — Marinid creativity lives in the madrasas, and in the Mawlid celebration still kept today. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
