# Video script — The Marinids and Morocco's Civilisational Creativity

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Zenata horsemen become sultans who fill Fez with colleges so beautiful the dynasty is remembered in tile and cedar — Marinid creativity lives in the madrasas, and in the Mawlid celebration still kept today.

The **Marinids** — Zenata horsemen who became sultans — take Morocco after the Almohad empire fades. The lesson follows their state's **founding and stages**, but the book's chosen word for this dynasty is not conquest. It is **creativity** — civilisational creativity.

Its proof stands in **Fez**: the Marinid **madrasas**, colleges for learning, built with a richness of carved plaster, tile and cedar that makes them among the most beautiful buildings in Morocco. A dynasty that spends its wealth on colleges is telling you what it values: learning, housed beautifully.

And the Marinids leave something else — not a building but a **tradition still alive**: the celebration of the Prophet's Birthday, the **Mawlid**, kept in Morocco from Marinid times to today. Buildings can fall; a celebration the people keep, century after century, does not.

So the Marinid chapter of Morocco's line is the creative one: tile and cedar in Fez, and a feast day still on the calendar.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
