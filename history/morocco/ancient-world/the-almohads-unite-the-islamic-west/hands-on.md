# Hands-on — The Almohads Unite the Islamic West

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The Almoravids came from the desert. The movement that replaces them comes from the mountains. Imagine a preacher in a high mountain valley gathering followers around one strict creed — one idea about God and rule — until the valley cannot hold them. Where do they go? Down, toward the cities, and then across the whole west.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a mountain preacher's movement swallows the Almoravids' empire and rules from Tripoli to Seville under one creed — the Almohads unite the whole Islamic West, and their architecture carries the mark of the empire. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
