# Prehistoric Morocco: The First Moroccans

## Do it first

Go outside and pick up one stone. Now imagine you have no knife, no pot, no shop and no house — only stones like this one, animals to hunt, and a cave or a shelter to find before dark. Everything you would need to live, you would have to make or find. Hold that thought: it is the whole of this lesson.

Long before Morocco had a name, people already lived on this land. We know them the prehistory way — not from anything they wrote, for they wrote nothing, but from what they left in the soil: their **remains**, their bones, and the tools their hands shaped.

From those remains a way of life can be read. The first Moroccans **hunted** for food. They found **shelter** against the weather and the wild animals. They made what they needed from the world around them, and they survived — generation after generation — in a land with no farms, no cities and no kings.

Their story matters because it is the true beginning of Morocco's line. Every later story in this course — the Amazigh kingdoms, the sailors from across the sea, the dynasties — stands on ground these first people already walked.

The historian reads them the way the last lessons taught: the witnesses are bones and tools, and questioned carefully, even a stone can tell how a person lived.

Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: prehistoric Morocco, first Moroccans, remains, hunting, shelter. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
