# Video script — Liberation and Independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The paigc under amílcar cabral first tried negotiation with portugal and, refused, launched the armed struggle in guinea — and after portugal's carnation revolution of 25 april 1974, cabo verde's independence was proclaimed on 5 july 1975, in unity with guinea-bissau.

The resistance you met in the revolts never vanished — it has **remote roots**, running back through the islands' and Guinea's long refusal of colonial rule. In the 1900s that resistance took organised form: the **PAIGC**, the party of African independence for Guinea and Cabo Verde, led by **Amílcar Cabral** — the same Cabral who called the people's culture its own wealth.

Cabral's road did not begin with weapons. The PAIGC first tried **negotiation** with **Portugal**: a peaceful path to independence, asked for at the table. Portugal **refused**.

Refused, the party changed its road. The PAIGC launched the **armed struggle** — in **Guinea**, on the mainland, where the fight for the two peoples' shared freedom was waged with arms. The islands and Guinea were bound in one struggle and one party: that is why Cabo Verde's independence story runs through Guinea's war.

The war was ended not on the battlefield alone but in Portugal itself. On **25 April 1974**, the **Carnation Revolution** overturned Portugal's government — and with it, the colonial war came to its end. The road the PAIGC had walked — negotiation refused, then armed struggle — now opened onto its goal.

On **5 July 1975**, **Cabo Verde's independence was proclaimed**. The islands that Portuguese sailors had found empty five centuries before, peopled by force and by hope, stood as a free country at last.

And note the form of it, as the book teaches: independence was proclaimed **in unity with Guinea-Bissau** — the two peoples who had struggled together under one party entered freedom together. What that unity became, and how the new state was built, is the next lesson.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, on your island.
