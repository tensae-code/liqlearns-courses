# Lesson plan — Liberation and Independence

**Course:** History — Cabo Verde · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Liberation and Independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Liberation and the Two Republics (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the PAIGC under Amílcar Cabral first tried negotiation with Portugal and, refused, launched the armed struggle in Guinea — and after Portugal's Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, Cabo Verde's independence was proclaimed on 5 July 1975, in unity with Guinea-Bissau.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Liberation and the Two Republics — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Decolonisation of Cabo Verde — the National Liberation Struggle** — After remote roots of resistance, the PAIGC under Amílcar Cabral first tries negotiation with Portugal, then, refused, launches the armed struggle in Guinea.
2. **Decolonisation and the Proclamation of Independence** — Portugal's Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974 ends the war, and on 5 July 1975 Cabo Verde's independence is proclaimed, in unity with Guinea-Bissau.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **The End of the Slave Society** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the PAIGC under Amílcar Cabral first tried negotiation with Portugal and, refused, launched the armed struggle in Guinea — and after Portugal's Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, Cabo Verde's independence was proclaimed on 5 July 1975, in unity with Guinea-Bissau.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or island — or, for this book's lessons, from the Cabo Verde they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Cabo Verde and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks independence was simply given → the PAIGC asked by negotiation first, was refused, and fought an armed struggle; independence came at the end of that road.
- Go back to the one thing: the PAIGC under Amílcar Cabral first tried negotiation with Portugal and, refused, launched the armed struggle in Guinea — and after Portugal's Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, Cabo Verde's independence was proclaimed on 5 July 1975, in unity with Guinea-Bissau. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
