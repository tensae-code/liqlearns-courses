# Hands-on — Popular Revolts in the Islands

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name three things that would make ordinary people angry enough to rise together: a tax they cannot pay, empty stomachs, being treated badly by those in power. Say them out loud. This lesson tells how the poor of Cabo Verde rose, again and again, for exactly those reasons.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: again and again the poor of the islands rose — Ribeira Grande 1811, the Engenhos 1822, Monte Agarro 1835, Achada Falcão 1841, Casa Martins 1847, Ribeirão Manuel 1910 — against taxes, hunger and abuse. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, at the harbour. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
