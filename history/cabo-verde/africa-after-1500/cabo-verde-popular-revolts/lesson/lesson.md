# Popular Revolts in the Islands

## Do it first

Name three things that would make ordinary people angry enough to rise together: a tax they cannot pay, empty stomachs, being treated badly by those in power. Say them out loud. This lesson tells how the poor of Cabo Verde rose, again and again, for exactly those reasons.

The islands' story is not only ports and routes. It is also the story of the **poor** — and of what they did when life was pressed too hard. Again and again across a hundred years, the poor of the islands **rose**.
The book names them one by one, and the names matter. **Ribeira Grande, 1811.** The **Engenhos, 1822.** **Monte Agarro, 1835.** **Achada Falcão, 1841.** **Casa Martins, 1847.** And still, a century after the first, **Ribeirão Manuel, 1910.** Six risings, in different places, in different generations — grandparents rose, and their grandchildren rose after them.
What were they against? The book gives the same three enemies: **taxes** laid on people who had almost nothing, **hunger** in dry years on dry islands, and **abuse** — the harsh treatment of the poor by those with power. These revolts did not win the islands a new order; they were put down. But they are kept in the islands' history as proof of something: the poor were never silent, and the memory of rising runs under everything that comes later — including the liberation struggle you will meet in Level 3.
