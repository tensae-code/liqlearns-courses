# Discovery and Settlement of Cabo Verde

## Do it first

Stand at a harbour or look at a map of the Atlantic. Point to the islands of Cabo Verde, far out at sea off West Africa. Now say out loud: "When the first ships reached these islands, nobody lived there at all." This lesson tells how empty islands were found — and how people then came to live on them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Discovery of the islands of Cabo Verde

It was the age of **European expansion**: Portuguese sailors were pushing down the coast of Africa and out into the Atlantic, looking for new routes and new lands. On one of these voyages they reached a group of islands far out at sea — the islands of **Cabo Verde**.
And here is the strange thing: **nobody lived there**. No towns, no farms, no people at all — the islands were **uninhabited**. That is what makes Cabo Verde's story unlike almost any other: its human story does not begin with a people already there. It begins with empty land and arriving ships.
The book itself stops and asks a sharp question: who really **"discovered"** these islands? A discovery usually means finding something unknown — but can you "discover" land that no one lived on, land that was simply there, waiting? Keep the question open. It is the first question of Cabo Verde's history, and the book wants you to think about it, not just repeat the word.

## Sub lesson 2 — Occupation and peopling of the islands

Finding empty islands is one thing. **Living on them** is another. The islands are dry, water is scarce, and everything — people, seeds, animals — had to arrive by ship. Peopling them was slow, hard work.
The first islands to be peopled were **Santiago** and **Fogo**. The people who came were **Europeans** and **Africans** — and many of the Africans were brought **enslaved**, against their will, to work the new land. From Santiago and Fogo, settlement spread to the **remaining islands**, one by one.
What did the settlers do to live? They **farmed** — cotton and maize became the islands' crops. They **raised cattle**. They **fished** the rich seas around them. And they **traded**, because an island that cannot grow everything must exchange with the world. Out of this hard beginning — ships, dry land, forced and free labour side by side — the peopling of Cabo Verde began.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: in the age of European expansion, Portuguese sailors found the islands of Cabo Verde with nobody living on them — and Europeans and Africans, many of them enslaved, then peopled Santiago and Fogo first, and the remaining islands after. Give one real example from your own home, school or island, and name the lesson's key words: uninhabited islands, discovery, Santiago, Fogo, peopling, enslaved Africans. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
