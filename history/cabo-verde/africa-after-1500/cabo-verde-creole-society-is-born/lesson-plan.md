# Lesson plan — A Creole Society Is Born

**Course:** History — Cabo Verde · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** A Creole Society Is Born
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Past of the Islands (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on the islands, peoples and cultures from Europe and Africa met and made something new — a creole society, creole in its language, food, music and custom — and that creole culture is the Cabo Verdean people's own wealth.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The Past of the Islands — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Origin and evolution of Cabo Verdean society and culture** — Peoples and cultures from Europe and Africa meet on the islands and a new society is born — creole in language, food, music and custom, ordered in a strict social structure.
2. **Cabo Verdean culture** — The creole culture — language, oral traditions, typical dishes, religious festivals, music and dance, literature, crafts and housing — presented, in Cabral's words, as the people's own wealth to defend and develop.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Discovery and Settlement of Cabo Verde** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: on the islands, peoples and cultures from Europe and Africa met and made something new — a creole society, creole in its language, food, music and custom — and that creole culture is the Cabo Verdean people's own wealth.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or island — or, for this book's lessons, from the Cabo Verde they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Cabo Verde and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
