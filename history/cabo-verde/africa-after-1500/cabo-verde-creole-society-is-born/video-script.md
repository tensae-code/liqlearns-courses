# Video script — A Creole Society Is Born

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
On the islands, peoples and cultures from europe and africa met and made something new — a creole society, creole in its language, food, music and custom — and that creole culture is the cabo verdean people's own wealth.

The settlers who peopled the islands came from two worlds: **Europe** and **Africa**. They did not simply live side by side. On small islands, far from both homelands, their peoples and cultures **met** — and out of the meeting, something **new** was born: a **creole society**.

You can hear it and taste it. The society spoke a new **language**, born from the meeting. It cooked new **food**, danced to new **music**, and kept new **customs** — none of them purely European, none of them purely African, all of them Cabo Verdean. This is the birth of the Cabo Verdean people as a people: not a copy of somewhere else, but a creation of the islands themselves.

But the new society was not equal. It was ordered in a **strict social structure** — some commanded and owned, others served and were enslaved. Hold both truths together, the way the book does: a new culture was being born, inside a hard and unequal order.

The second book opens the creole culture up, part by part, so you can see what the meeting of peoples actually made. There is the **language** itself. There are the **oral traditions** — the stories carried by mouth from old to young. There are the **typical dishes**, the **religious festivals**, the **music and dance**, the **literature**, the **crafts**, and the **housing** — the way people build their homes on these islands.

And the book gives this culture its defender's words. **Amílcar Cabral** — the leader you will meet again in the liberation struggle — called this culture the people's **own wealth**: something the Cabo Verdean people made themselves, to **defend and develop**. Culture here is not decoration. It is the proof that a people exists, and the treasure it carries into its future.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, on your island.
