# Decline and Porto Grande

## Do it first

Think of a shop on a road that the traffic no longer uses — the road moves, and the shop fades. Say out loud: "A port lives from the routes that call at it." This lesson tells how the islands' first great port faded — and how a new one rose on a different island.

A port is only as alive as the routes that call at it. The old sailing routes that had made **Ribeira Grande** great slowly **faded** — ships found other paths, other stops. And as the routes went, the city **decayed**: its trade shrank, its importance drained away, and the islands entered a long time of **abandonment and neglect**.
Then the ships themselves changed. **Steam** replaced sail, and a steam ship crossing the Atlantic needs one thing above all: **coal**, taken on mid-voyage. On the island of **São Vicente** lay the harbour of **Mindelo** — **Porto Grande**, a great natural port in exactly the right place. Steamers crowded in to coal there, and Porto Grande became one of the Atlantic's great **coaling ports**.
So the islands' centre of gravity moved. **São Vicente took Santiago's place**: the new age's port rose on a new island, while the old city that sail had made sat quiet. One lesson runs through both halves of this story: Cabo Verde's fortune has always turned on the routes of the sea — and on being ready when the routes change.

Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
