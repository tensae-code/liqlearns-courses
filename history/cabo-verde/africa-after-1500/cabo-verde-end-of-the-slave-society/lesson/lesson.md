# The End of the Slave Society

## Do it first

Remember how the islands were peopled: many Africans were brought enslaved, and the creole society grew up inside a strict order with slavery at its base. Say out loud: "One day, that order ended." This lesson tells what changed when slavery was abolished — and how the islands had to remake themselves without it.

From the peopling of Santiago and Fogo onward, the islands' society had rested on **slavery**: enslaved Africans farmed, built, served and laboured, and the strict social structure you met in the creole society's birth had slavery at its base.
Then, in the course of the 1800s, **slavery was abolished**. The slave-owning society — a whole way of ordering life, work and rank around owning people — came to its **end**. No law can remake a society by itself, though. What followed was the real work of the lesson: in the **second half of the 1800s**, Cabo Verdean **society, culture and economy were remade without slavery**.
Think about what "remade" means, piece by piece. **Society**: people who had been property stood as persons, and the old ranks had to be re-thought. **Culture**: the creole culture kept growing, now carried by free people. **Economy**: work that had been forced had to be organised in new ways, on dry islands that still had to farm, fish and trade to live. The end of the slave society is not one date — it is the long remaking that closed the islands' old order and opened the road to the century of struggle that follows.
