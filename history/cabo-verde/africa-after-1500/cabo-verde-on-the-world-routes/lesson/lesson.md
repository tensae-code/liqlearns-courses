# Cabo Verde on the World Routes

## Do it first

Find Cabo Verde on a map of the Atlantic, between Europe, Africa and America. Put your finger on the sailing routes that pass it. Say out loud: "A ship crossing this ocean needs a place to stop — and these islands are that place." This lesson tells what that position did for the islands' first great city.

Look at the map again. The islands sit in the **Atlantic** where the great sailing routes cross — between Europe, the African coast and the lands across the ocean. For ships on long voyages, such a position is gold: a place to stop, take on water and food, repair, and trade. The islands became a **strategic stopping point** on the **international routes**.
One city rose on that trade: **Ribeira Grande**, on the island of **Santiago**. It grew fast — not from what the dry islands produced, but from its position. Ships called there, and Ribeira Grande traded with the **nearby West African coast**, close across the water. Wealth and people flowed through its port, and the town became a city while the other islands were still being settled.
And at the centre of the rising city stood the **Church**. In Ribeira Grande the Church was not at the edge of life but at its middle — its buildings, its feasts, its authority woven into the city's days. When you picture the islands' first great city, picture it whole: a harbour full of ships, a market trading with the African coast, and the Church at its centre.
