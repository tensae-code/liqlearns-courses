# History — Cabo Verde course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Cabo Verde inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Cabo Verde's books in hand are two —
the Grade 5 *História e Geografia de Cabo Verde* student book
and the Grade 6 *Caderno Experimental* student workbook — and
their own progression, as filed, is the ladder. The child's
first History subject is the past of her own islands only: the
Grade 5 book opens with the discipline itself (Tema 0 — what
History is), then tells the islands' past theme by theme, "The
Past of the Islands" 1–8: discovery, peopling, the birth of a
creole society, the world routes, decline and Porto Grande,
the popular revolts, and the end of the slave society. The
Grade 6 workbook continues the story where Grade 5 stopped —
creole culture held up as the people's wealth, then Cabral's
liberation struggle, independence, and the First and Second
Republics. So the ladder is: method first (Level 1), the past
of the islands as the Grade 5 book tells it (Level 2), and
liberation and the two Republics as the Grade 6 workbook
closes it (Level 3). One lesson of Level 2 — A Creole Society
Is Born — carries both books: its first part is the Grade 5
book's theme 3 and its second part is the workbook's Tema 2 §9,
in file order. The ladder stops where the books in hand stop,
not because Cabo Verde teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own História e Geografia de Cabo Verde books — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book contents
map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: What History Is** — the discipline itself,
   as the Grade 5 book presents it before any island story:
   History as the science that studies people in time and
   space, working from maps, old texts, photographs, monuments
   and oral tradition, to understand Cabo Verde's present
   through its past. 1 lesson, in one title: `first-steps/`
   (1 lesson).

2. **The Past of the Islands** — the Grade 5 book's own theme
   sequence, in order: the discovery of the uninhabited
   islands and their peopling (Santiago and Fogo first); the
   birth of a creole society, deepened by the Grade 6
   workbook's presentation of creole culture as the people's
   own wealth; the archipelago on the international routes,
   with Ribeira Grande rising on the coast trade; decline and
   Porto Grande — the old city decaying as the routes faded,
   Mindelo's coaling port rising with steam; the popular
   revolts of the poor, 1811–1910; and the end of the
   slave-owning society, with society, culture and economy
   remade without slavery. 6 lessons, in one title:
   `africa-after-1500/` (first six lessons).

3. **Liberation and the Two Republics** — the Grade 6
   workbook's close: the PAIGC under Amílcar Cabral —
   negotiation refused, the armed struggle in Guinea — the
   Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, and independence
   proclaimed on 5 July 1975 in unity with Guinea-Bissau;
   then the forming of the state — the one-party First
   Republic under the PAIGC/PAICV, and the Second Republic of
   multiparty democracy from 1991, with new symbols and a
   changed country. 2 lessons, in one title:
   `africa-after-1500/` (last two lessons).

**Totals: 9 lessons** (Level 1: 1 · Level 2: 6 · Level 3: 2 —
12 placements, all from two student books).
