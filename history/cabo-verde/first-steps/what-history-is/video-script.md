# Video script — What History Is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History is the science that studies people in time and space — it works from traces of the past, like maps, old texts, photographs, monuments and oral tradition, so we can understand cabo verde's present through its past.

Every country has a present — the streets, the harbour, the language, the food of today. But none of it appeared from nowhere. **History** is the science that studies **people in time and space**: who lived in a place, what they did, what changed, and what came of it.

How can we know about people who lived long before us? They left **traces**. A **map** drawn long ago shows the islands as sailors once saw them. An **old text** keeps words written at the time. A **photograph** freezes a street as it was. A **monument** — a building or stone raised on purpose — keeps a memory standing in public. And **oral tradition** carries the past in people's mouths: stories, songs and memories handed down from grandparents to grandchildren.

The historian's work is to gather these traces, test them against each other, and build the story of what really happened. That is why this course begins here: everything that follows — the discovery of the islands, the birth of a creole society, the struggle for independence — will be learned the same way, from the traces Cabo Verde's past left behind.

And the point of it all is the present. To understand why Cabo Verde is what it is today — its language, its ports, its people spread across the world — you read its past. The past explains the present.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, on your island.
