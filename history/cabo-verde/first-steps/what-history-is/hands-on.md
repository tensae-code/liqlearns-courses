# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask an older person one question: "What was this place like when you were my age?" Listen to the answer. Then look at one old thing near you — a photograph, a building, a song people still sing. You have just done the work this lesson names: finding out about the past from the traces it left.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is the science that studies people in time and space — it works from traces of the past, like maps, old texts, photographs, monuments and oral tradition, so we can understand Cabo Verde's present through its past. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, at the harbour. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
