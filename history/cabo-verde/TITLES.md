# History — Cabo Verde course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Cabo Verde's
own — built from Cabo Verde's História e Geografia de Cabo
Verde books for Grade 5 (student book) and Grade 6 (Caderno
Experimental student workbook), in Portuguese. Inside the
course the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the
titles below sit inside Levels 1–3, in path order — what the
learner proves, step by step. The grades the books come from
live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the books': the discipline first
(what History is and how it works from traces), then the past
of the islands theme by theme as the Grade 5 book files them
— discovery, peopling, the creole birth, the world routes,
decline and the steam port, the revolts of the poor, slavery's
end — and last the Grade 6 workbook's close: creole culture as
the people's wealth (stocked as the second part of the creole
lesson, in file order), the liberation struggle, independence,
and the two Republics. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher; the
"start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — first steps: what History is** (História e
Geografia de Cabo Verde, Grade 5 student book, Tema 0):

- `first-steps/` — Level 1, the opening title. What History Is
  (History as the science that studies people in time and
  space, working from maps, old texts, photographs, monuments
  and oral tradition, to understand the present through the
  past).

**Levels 2–3 — Africa after 1500: the past of the islands,
liberation and the Republics** (Grade 5 student book, "The
Past of the Islands" 1–8; Grade 6 Caderno Experimental student
workbook, Tema 2 §§9–11):

- `africa-after-1500/` — Levels 2–3, the islands' whole story
  in one title folder, in path order. Discovery and Settlement
  of Cabo Verde (Portuguese sailors find the uninhabited
  islands; Europeans and Africans, many enslaved, people
  Santiago and Fogo first, then the rest) · A Creole Society
  Is Born (the meeting of Europe and Africa makes a new
  society — creole in language, food, music and custom — and
  the workbook presents that creole culture, in Cabral's
  words, as the people's own wealth) · Cabo Verde on the World
  Routes (the strategic Atlantic stopping point; Ribeira
  Grande rises on the West African coast trade, the Church at
  its centre) · Decline and Porto Grande (the old routes fade,
  Ribeira Grande decays, the islands are neglected — then
  steam makes Mindelo's Porto Grande a great coaling port and
  São Vicente takes Santiago's place) · Popular Revolts in the
  Islands (the poor rise in 1811, 1822, 1835, 1841, 1847 and
  1910, against taxes, hunger and abuse) · The End of the
  Slave Society (slavery abolished; society, culture and
  economy remade without it in the second half of the 1800s) ·
  Liberation and Independence (the PAIGC under Amílcar Cabral:
  negotiation refused, the armed struggle in Guinea; the
  Carnation Revolution, 25 April 1974; independence proclaimed
  5 July 1975, in unity with Guinea-Bissau) · The First and
  Second Republics (the one-party First Republic under the
  PAIGC/PAICV; multiparty democracy installs the Second
  Republic in 1991, with new symbols and a changed country).
