# About the History — Cabo Verde course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own História e Geografia de Cabo Verde books for Grades 5 and
6. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Cabo Verde inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **three levels**, following the
books' own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — first steps: what History is.** The Grade 5
   book presents the discipline itself before any island
   story: History as the science that studies people in time
   and space, working from maps, old texts, photographs,
   monuments and oral tradition.
2. **Level 2 — the past of the islands.** The Grade 5 book's
   theme sequence, "The Past of the Islands" 1–8: discovery
   of the uninhabited islands, peopling (Santiago and Fogo
   first), the birth of a creole society, the archipelago on
   the international routes, abandonment and decline then
   Porto Grande, the popular revolts, and the end of the
   slave-owning society. The creole lesson's second part is
   the Grade 6 workbook's presentation of Cabo Verdean
   culture (Tema 2 §9), stocked in file order.
3. **Level 3 — liberation and the two Republics.** The Grade
   6 workbook's close: the National Liberation Struggle under
   the PAIGC and Amílcar Cabral, the proclamation of
   independence on 5 July 1975, and the formation of the
   state — First Republic, then the Second Republic of 1991.

All nine lessons stand on **student books** and count in the
acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *First the method, then the islands:* "First, learn what
  History is — a science that reads the past from its traces.
  Then walk the islands' whole story: empty islands found by
  ships, peopled with hardship, made new as a creole society;
  a great port rising and falling with the sea routes; the
  poor rising again and again; slavery ending — and at last
  Cabral's struggle, independence on 5 July 1975, and the two
  Republics of the free country."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**História e Geografia de Cabo Verde, Grade 5** — the child's
first History subject is the past of her own islands only —
an uninhabited archipelago found by Portuguese ships, peopled
by Europeans and enslaved Africans, becoming a new creole
society — taught theme by theme with documents, a
mini-dictionary of concepts and "Vou resolver" activities.
*(Student book.)*

**História e Geografia de Cabo Verde, Grade 6, Caderno
Experimental** — the 6th-year workbook continues the islands'
story where Grade 5 stopped — from creole culture to Cabral's
liberation struggle — and teaches it through source documents
(including Amílcar Cabral's own PAIGC texts) that pupils
interrogate themselves. *(Student workbook.)*
