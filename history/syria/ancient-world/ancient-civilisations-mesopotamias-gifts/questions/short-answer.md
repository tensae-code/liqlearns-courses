# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia's Gifts — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 4, Lesson 1: انتشار المعرفة (The spread of knowledge)”?
**Answer:** Cave drawings served the first hunters, but a settled, trading, law-making world needs to say far more than a picture can carry. So writing is invented — and with writing, knowledge begins to travel: a message can cross a desert, a law can outlive its king, a story can reach grandchildren a hundred generations away. The spread of knowledge, the book teaches, is the moment civilisation learns to remember everything.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     How does the story in “Unit 4, Lesson 4: تحف فنية ومنجزات علمية (Artistic masterpieces and scientific achievements)” end?
**Answer:** How far did these peoples climb? The lesson measures them by their finest work: sculpture that still commands a museum room, music organised into art, and science that mapped the heavens — preserved for us in works like the royal treasures of Mari, dug from Syria's own soil. Artistic masterpieces and scientific achievements are not decoration on the story; they are the record of how high a civilisation rose.
