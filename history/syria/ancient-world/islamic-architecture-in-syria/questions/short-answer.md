# Islamic Architecture in Syria — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 4, Lesson 2: مظاهر معمارية وفنية (Architectural and artistic forms)”?
**Answer:** Arab builders, the book says, blend two things that rarely meet: simplicity and splendour. They learn from every land their state embraced — Persian arches, Byzantine domes, local stone — and then surpass their teachers, raising mosques, palaces and cities in a style the world now calls Islamic. Syria, the state's first heartland, carries some of its earliest and finest monuments.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     How does the story in “Unit 2, Lesson مواقع تاريخية (Historic sites)” end?
**Answer:** The Grade 4 book shows the architecture standing in the landscape children can visit. Castles built for war in places enemies could hardly reach — Salah al-Din's castle, freed from the Franks by the hero Salah al-Din himself; Aleppo's citadel; the Krak and al-Marqab. The Umayyad Mosque in Damascus, built by the caliph al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik. The church of St Simeon near Aleppo. And the palaces: al-Azem in Damascus, Harun al-Rashid's in Raqqa, and al-Hayr East and West near Palmyra — a whole architecture of faith, war and rule in stone.
