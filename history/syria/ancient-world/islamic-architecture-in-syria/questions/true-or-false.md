# Islamic Architecture in Syria — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     Arab builders blend simplicity and splendour, learning from every land their state embraced and then surpassing it.
**Answer: True**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     The part “Unit 2, Lesson مواقع تاريخية (Historic sites)” is mainly about this: Napoleon Bonaparte's campaign of 1798 was forced out by 1801 by popular resistance; after the Suez Canal opened, foreign interference grew until Ahmad Urabi's revolt of 1881; Britain bombarded Alexandria in 1882, defeated Urabi at Tell el-Kebir and occupied Cairo, exiling him to Ceylon until 1901.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: Castles built for war in places enemies could hardly reach — Salah al-Din's castle, Aleppo's citadel, the Krak and al-Marqab — the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus built by the caliph al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, the church of St Simeon near Aleppo, and the palaces: al-Azem in Damascus, Harun al-Rashid's in Raqqa, al-Hayr East and West near Palmyra.
