# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 3, Lesson 2: الإمبراطوريات القديمة (The ancient empires)”?
**Answer:** City-states could not stay small forever. Sargon of Akkad marches out, swallows his rivals one by one, and stretches his rule until something new exists in the world: an empire, many lands under one crown. From that moment, the book says, empire becomes the prize of war — the thing kings fight for — and Mesopotamia, the land between the rivers next door to Syria, becomes the stage on which empires rise and devour each other.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     How does the story in “Unit 4, Lesson 3: تمدن وحضارة (Urban life and civilisation)” end?
**Answer:** What does civilisation look like when you walk its streets? Planned cities, laid out with order instead of grown by accident — and rising over them the stepped ziggurat, the staged temple-tower that organises worship in stone. Building, worship and knowledge are arranged together, deliberately, by a society that thinks in plans. The lesson's title says it in two words: تمدن وحضارة — urban life, and civilisation itself.
