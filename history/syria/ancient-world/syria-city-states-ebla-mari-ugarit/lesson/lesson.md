# Syria's City-States: Ebla, Mari and Ugarit

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Syria's City-States: Ebla, Mari and Ugarit** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 1: من المدينة إلى المملكة (From city to kingdom)

Syria's cities do not stay villages for long. They grow walls, kings and armies, until each great city rules its own land as a city-state — and city-states grow in turn toward kingdoms. Three names carry the story: Ebla, Mari and Ugarit, each a power in its own right, trading, writing and ruling while no single master yet held all Syria. From city to kingdom: that climb is the political story of ancient Syria.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2, Lesson سورية مهد الحضارة (Syria, cradle of civilisation)

The Grade 4 book walks its young readers through the three great cities one by one. Ebla, at Tell Mardikh near Aleppo, kept a huge library of thousands of clay tablets — a whole kingdom's memory in clay. Mari, on the Euphrates, had its royal palace, its temples and its statues. And Ugarit, at Ras Shamra on the coast, invented the alphabetic writing it gave to all peoples; its tablets rest today in the National Museum in Damascus, where Syrian children can stand before them.
