## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2, Lesson 3: التجارة وطرقها (Trade and its routes)** — Lapis, carnelian and rock crystal travel far routes to reach Syria by 3200 BCE, and villages begin to need each other's goods.
2. **Unit 2, Lesson 4: وسائل التجارة (The means of trade)** — Barter gives way to coined money, taxes and ships as trade grows too big for swapping goods.
3. **Unit 3, Lesson 3: تجارة دولية (International trade)** — The widened state's roads and adventures link furthest east and west in trade that carries culture with the goods.
4. **Unit 3, Lesson 4: طرق وقوافل (Roads and caravans)** — Caravans cross the land and ships the seas between the state's markets, and road-signs and staging posts grow along the routes.
5. **Unit 2, Lesson وسائل النقل في سورية (Means of transport in Syria)** — Syria was a passage for the trade routes since ancient times, among them the Silk Road, its merchants carrying goods and learning other peoples' cultures; the Phoenicians on its coast were among the first to ride the sea.
6. **Unit 4, Lesson العمل في التجارة في سورية (Work in trade in Syria)** — The Syrians have been known since ancient times for their skill in buying and selling, and since olden days the trade caravans crossed Syria coming from India to Europe.
