# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 2, Lesson 3: التجارة وطرقها (Trade and its routes)”?
**Answer:** By 3200 BCE, stones that Syria does not produce are arriving in Syria: lapis lazuli from far to the east, carnelian, rock crystal — proof that long routes already stitch the old world together. Villages that once made everything themselves begin to need each other's goods, and need creates roads. Trade, the lesson shows, is not a late addition to civilisation; it is one of its first engines.

**2.** (Sub lesson 6)     How does the story in “Unit 4, Lesson العمل في التجارة في سورية (Work in trade in Syria)” end?
**Answer:** The Syrians, the Grade 4 book tells its children, have been known since ancient times for their skill in buying and selling. Since olden days the trade caravans crossed Syria on their way from India to Europe, and a land that everyone must cross learns everyone's business. Trade is not something Syria joined late; it is one of the oldest things Syria does.
