# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Trade Routes That Linked the Old World** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2, Lesson 3: التجارة وطرقها (Trade and its routes)

By 3200 BCE, stones that Syria does not produce are arriving in Syria: lapis lazuli from far to the east, carnelian, rock crystal — proof that long routes already stitch the old world together. Villages that once made everything themselves begin to need each other's goods, and need creates roads. Trade, the lesson shows, is not a late addition to civilisation; it is one of its first engines.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 4: وسائل التجارة (The means of trade)

As trade grows too big for swapping goods, its tools change. Barter gives way to coined money, which any stranger will accept; taxes appear, as states take their share of the moving wealth; and ships take the cargoes that no camel train could carry. The means of trade — money, tax and sail — turn scattered exchanges into a system, and the old world's regions begin to live as one economy.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 3: تجارة دولية (International trade)

In the widened Arab state, trade becomes truly international. Its roads link the furthest east with the furthest west, and the adventures of its merchants carry more than goods: culture travels in the same caravans and holds. The Grade 8 book calls this trade one of the bridges between civilisations — every bale of silk or sack of spice arrives with stories, techniques and ideas packed inside it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 3, Lesson 4: طرق وقوافل (Roads and caravans)

How does a caravan cross a continent without losing its way or its water? The book follows the machinery of the routes: caravans crossing the land in organised trains, ships crossing the seas between the state's markets, and along the roads a furniture of travel grows up — road-signs to guide the way and staging posts where animals rest and merchants sleep. Roads and caravans together are the Arab state's circulatory system.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 2, Lesson وسائل النقل في سورية (Means of transport in Syria)

Syria's land was a passage for the trade routes since ancient times — among them the Silk Road itself, whose merchants crossed Syria carrying goods and learning other peoples' cultures on the way. And on Syria's coast, the Phoenicians were among the first peoples to ride the sea, turning the Mediterranean into a highway. To stand in Syria, the lesson says, is to stand where the world's goods have always passed through.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 4, Lesson العمل في التجارة في سورية (Work in trade in Syria)

The Syrians, the Grade 4 book tells its children, have been known since ancient times for their skill in buying and selling. Since olden days the trade caravans crossed Syria on their way from India to Europe, and a land that everyone must cross learns everyone's business. Trade is not something Syria joined late; it is one of the oldest things Syria does.
