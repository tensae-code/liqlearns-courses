# Video script — Syria's Historic Cities: Palmyra, Bosra and Apamea


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

The lesson is a journey between four stone cities. Palmyra rises in the middle of the Badia, its temples, tombs and great colonnaded street running down to the triumphal arch. South in Hawran stands Bosra, with its great amphitheatre; Apamea lifts its colonnade above the Ghab plain; and Rusafa, the desert city, was the retreat the Umayyad caliph Hisham chose for himself. Each city is a chapter of Syria's past that you can still walk through.

Two living cities close the journey. Damascus is the oldest inhabited capital in the world, split in two by its own history: an old city inside its wall — the Umayyad Mosque, the citadel, the old souks — and a new city spreading beyond it. Aleppo joins the same two halves, its famous citadel watching over the old streets below. In Syria, the lesson says, history is not only ruins; it is the old half of the city you live in.


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Syria.
