# Syria's Historic Cities: Palmyra, Bosra and Apamea — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 2, Lesson مواقع تاريخية (Historic sites)”?
**Answer:** The lesson is a journey between four stone cities. Palmyra rises in the middle of the Badia, its temples, tombs and great colonnaded street running down to the triumphal arch. South in Hawran stands Bosra, with its great amphitheatre; Apamea lifts its colonnade above the Ghab plain; and Rusafa, the desert city, was the retreat the Umayyad caliph Hisham chose for himself. Each city is a chapter of Syria's past that you can still walk through.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     How does the story in “Unit 3, Lesson من المدن السورية (Among Syria's cities)” end?
**Answer:** Two living cities close the journey. Damascus is the oldest inhabited capital in the world, split in two by its own history: an old city inside its wall — the Umayyad Mosque, the citadel, the old souks — and a new city spreading beyond it. Aleppo joins the same two halves, its famous citadel watching over the old streets below. In Syria, the lesson says, history is not only ruins; it is the old half of the city you live in.
