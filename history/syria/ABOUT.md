# About the History — Syria course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Syria's own Ministry of Education student books, all in Arabic. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Syria inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Legends and religious-history stories are told as the books tell them, as plot summaries in our own words.

The course as built has **four levels**, following the books' own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home.** The historian's craft learned the way Syria's youngest books teach it — what history is, how the past is dated, how evidence becomes a written history, history hunted in your own locality, and heritage: what the ancestors hand down and we hand on.
2. **Level 2 — Ancient Syria and the First Civilisations.** The first settlements and farming, crafts, religion and city-states — Ebla, Mari and Ugarit — Mesopotamia and its gifts, writing, schools and trade roads, Persia, Egypt and Carthage, and Syria's historic cities.
3. **Level 3 — The Arab State from Damascus: Medieval Syria.** The liberation wars, the Damascus-centred state and its administration, then the civilisation: society, women, feasts, farming, crafts, learning, architecture and art — with the Crusader and Mongol assaults, North Africa's liberation, and the resisters.
4. **Level 4 — The Modern Arab World: Occupation, Resistance and Independence.** Syria's own road from 1918 to the fall of the regime on 8 December 2024 as the spine, and every other Arab country's struggle told in the same frame — Lebanon, Jordan, Palestine, Iraq, the Arabian peninsula, Egypt, Sudan, the Horn and the Maghreb.

All 64 of the 64 lessons stand on **student books** and count in the acceptance reckoning below; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *A homeland where history begins with your own family's tree and ends, for now, in your own lifetime:* "Begin at home — order your day on a timeline, draw your family tree, and match the antiquities on your own banknotes to the places they stand. Walk from the Yabroud caves to the first farming villages, stand in Ebla's library and read Ugarit's alphabet, and follow the caravans down the Silk Road. Enter Damascus when it rules a world state, sit in its mosques and markets, and hold the line with Nur al-Din. Then live the modern story your grandparents lived: Maysalun, the Great Revolt, independence in 1946, unity in 1958, and the revolution that ends with the fall of the regime on 8 December 2024 — while every Arab land from Baghdad to Tangier fights the same fight in its own way. That is Syria's own story, from its own school books, and it is yours."
