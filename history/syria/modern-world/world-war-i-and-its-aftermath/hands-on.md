# Hands-on — World War I and Its Aftermath

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — World War I and Its Aftermath — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: While Sharif Hussein negotiated with Britain and joined the Allies in 1916, the Allies were secretly dividing the Arab lands in the Sykes–Picot agreement of 1916 and Britain promised the Zionists a national home in Palestine in the Balfour Declaration of 1917; the Paris peace conference of 1919 invented the mandate system, applied at San Remo in 1920. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
