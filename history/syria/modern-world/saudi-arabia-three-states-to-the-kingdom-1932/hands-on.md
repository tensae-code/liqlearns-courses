# Hands-on — Saudi Arabia: Three States to the Kingdom, 1932

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Saudi Arabia: Three States to the Kingdom, 1932 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The first Saudi state rose at Diriyah (1745–1818) and fell to Ibrahim Pasha's campaign; the second state fell to the Al Rashid in 1891; Abd al-Aziz ibn Saud retook Riyadh in 1902, then al-Ahsa, Hail and Mecca, and proclaimed the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932; King Faisal cut oil to America and Europe in the October 1973 war. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
