# Syria's Struggle for Independence — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 1, Lesson التآمر الأجنبي على الحكم العربي في بلاد الشام (The foreign conspiracy against Arab rule in Bilad al-Sham)”?
**Answer:** For two years, Damascus governed itself. Faisal's Arab government (1918–1920) spread schools, founded the Arab Academy and made Arabic the language of administration — a state being built in the open. Faisal carried the Arab demand to Paris in 1919; the American King–Crane commission toured Bilad al-Sham asking its people what they wanted, and the Syrian General Congress declared Syria's complete independence, including Palestine and Lebanon. The commission recommended independence for Syria and Iraq and no mandate at all — and its report was shelved until 1922, while the mandates were imposed anyway.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     How does the story in “Unit 1, Lesson الكفاح السياسي في سورية حتى الاستقلال (The political struggle in Syria until independence)” end?
**Answer:** After the revolts, the struggle turns political — and wins by inches. A constitution in 1928; a parliament in 1932 that elects Muhammad Ali al-Abid first president; a sixty-day strike that forces France to the 1936 treaty project. In the Second World War the nationalists win the 1943 elections under Shukri al-Quwatli; France answers by bombarding the parliament itself on 29 May 1945 — and Arab and world opinion turns decisively. On 17 April 1946 the French forces evacuate: Syria is independent.
