# Video script — Syria's Struggle for Independence


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

For two years, Damascus governed itself. Faisal's Arab government (1918–1920) spread schools, founded the Arab Academy and made Arabic the language of administration — a state being built in the open. Faisal carried the Arab demand to Paris in 1919; the American King–Crane commission toured Bilad al-Sham asking its people what they wanted, and the Syrian General Congress declared Syria's complete independence, including Palestine and Lebanon. The commission recommended independence for Syria and Iraq and no mandate at all — and its report was shelved until 1922, while the mandates were imposed anyway.

After the revolts, the struggle turns political — and wins by inches. A constitution in 1928; a parliament in 1932 that elects Muhammad Ali al-Abid first president; a sixty-day strike that forces France to the 1936 treaty project. In the Second World War the nationalists win the 1943 elections under Shukri al-Quwatli; France answers by bombarding the parliament itself on 29 May 1945 — and Arab and world opinion turns decisively. On 17 April 1946 the French forces evacuate: Syria is independent.


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Syria.
