# Hands-on — Yemen: Divided by Empire, United 1990

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Yemen: Divided by Empire, United 1990 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Britain seized Aden in 1839 and a 1904 border line split Yemen in two; the Mutawakkilite kingdom in the north fell to the revolution of 1962, while the south won independence in 1967 after the revolt of 1963; the two Yemens united in 1990 under Ali Abdullah Saleh, who stepped down after the popular revolution of 2011. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
