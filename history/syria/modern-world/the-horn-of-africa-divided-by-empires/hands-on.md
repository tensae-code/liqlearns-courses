# Hands-on — The Horn of Africa Divided by Empires

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Horn of Africa Divided by Empires — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The empires carved up the Somali lands — Djibouti to France, the north to Britain, the south and Eritrea to Italy; Sayyid Muhammad ibn Abd Allah Hassan's revolt held out against Britain from 1899 to 1920, the Somali Youth League carried the struggle from 1943, and north and south united as the Somali Republic in 1960; Eritrea won independence in 1993, Djibouti in 1977, and the Comoros declared independence in 1975. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
