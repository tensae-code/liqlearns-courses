# Video script — The Syrian Revolution and Its Martyrs


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

The Grade 4 citizenship unit closes with the martyrs. Syria declared its independence from French colonialism in 1946 and stayed free — until Hafez al-Assad's military coup of 1970 brought plunder, prisons, killing and displacement on its people. The class studies a photograph of the funeral of the martyrs of the Khalidiya massacre in Homs, and then does the lesson's real work: documenting the Syrian revolution's martyrs and the regime's violations against children, women, neighbourhoods and schools, in a report addressed to the United Nations and the Red Cross. Ten-year-olds, the book says, can be witnesses.

Hafez al-Assad's coup of 16 November 1970 begins some thirty years of despotism: the massacres of Jisr al-Shughur, Aleppo and Hama in 1982, where some 40,000 were killed, and tens of thousands disappeared into the prisons. The rule is inherited by his son Bashar in 2000. Then, in Daraa, children are arrested and tortured for writing freedom slogans on a wall — and in March 2011 the revolution rises. The regime answers with every weapon, chemical weapons among them; and the book ends the story in the pupils' own present: the liberation of all Syria's land and the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime on 8 December 2024.


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Syria.
