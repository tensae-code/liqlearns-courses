# The Syrian Revolution and Its Martyrs — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     Syria declared its independence from French colonialism in 1946 and stayed free until Hafez al-Assad's military coup of 1970 brought plunder, prisons, killing and displacement; the lesson studies a photograph of the funeral of the martyrs of the Khalidiya massacre in Homs, and the class documents the Syrian revolution's martyrs and the regime's violations in a report for the United Nations and the Red Cross.
**Answer: True**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     The part “Unit 1, Lesson سورية منذ الاستقلال وحتى الوقت الحاضر (Syria from independence to the present)” is mainly about this: Syria is among the oldest regions humans inhabited, and in every age its people developed their tools and crafts, which the trade caravans carried to the whole world; its hand industries are famous worldwide — glass, inlaid wood, copper, and the brocade called damask, of which Queen Elizabeth asked the Syrian government for a piece for her wedding dress.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: Hafez al-Assad's coup of 16 November 1970 began some thirty years of despotism — the massacres of Jisr al-Shughur, Aleppo and Hama in 1982, and tens of thousands disappeared in the prisons; the rule was inherited by his son Bashar in 2000; when the children of Daraa were arrested and tortured for writing freedom slogans, the revolution of March 2011 rose, and the story ends with the liberation of all Syria's land and the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime on 8 December 2024.
