# Hands-on — Mauritania: From Trading Posts to Independence, 1960

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Mauritania: From Trading Posts to Independence, 1960 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The gum-arabic trade drew the Portuguese in the fifteenth century, then Dutch and French trading posts; France moved in stages — the protectorate of 1905, direct control 1909–1920 — and fought the Arabic language; Sheikh Ma al-Aynayn led the resistance from the Adrar plateau; the parties united in 1958 under Mokhtar Ould Daddah, and the United Nations referendum of 1960 brought full independence with Ould Daddah as first president. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
