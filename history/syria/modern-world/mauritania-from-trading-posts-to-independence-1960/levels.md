# Mauritania: From Trading Posts to Independence, 1960 — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — The gum-arabic trade drew the Portuguese in the fifteenth century, then Dutch and French trading posts; France moved in stages — the protectorate of 1905, direct control 1909–1920 — and fought the Arabic language; Sheikh Ma al-Aynayn led the resistance from the Adrar plateau; the parties united in 1958 under Mokhtar Ould Daddah, and the United Nations referendum of 1960 brought full independence with Ould Daddah as first president. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
