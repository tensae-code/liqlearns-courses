# Video script — Mauritania: From Trading Posts to Independence, 1960


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

The gum-arabic trade draws the Portuguese in the fifteenth century, then Dutch and French trading posts along the coast. France moves inland in stages — exploration 1858–1901, the protectorate of 1905, direct control 1909–1920 — renames the land, splits its people and fights the Arabic language itself. Sheikh Ma al-Aynayn leads the resistance from the Adrar plateau, and his son al-Hiba continues it. The parties unite in 1958 under Mokhtar Ould Daddah, and the United Nations referendum of 1960 brings full independence, with Ould Daddah as first president — the last page of the Grade 9 book, and of the Arab world's long liberation.


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Syria.
