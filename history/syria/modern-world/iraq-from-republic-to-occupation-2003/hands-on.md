# Hands-on — Iraq: From Republic to Occupation 2003

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Iraq: From Republic to Occupation 2003 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Rashid Ali al-Gaylani's 1941 government was crushed by a second British occupation; then the revolution of 14 July 1958 made Iraq a republic; after al-Bakr and Saddam Hussein came the war with Iran (1980–1988), the invasion of Kuwait in 1990, the siege, and the American occupation of 2003, ending with the withdrawal of late 2011 — the same lesson tells of Arab Ahwaz (Arabistan), annexed by Reza Shah in 1925. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
