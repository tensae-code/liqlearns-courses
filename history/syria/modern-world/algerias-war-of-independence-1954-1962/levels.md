# Algeria's War of Independence, 1954–1962 — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — The Constantine massacre of 8 May 1945, in which 45,000 Algerians were martyred, taught the national movement that only arms would free the country; the Great Algerian Revolution opened on 1 November 1954 from the Aurès, a provisional government formed in Cairo in 1958, De Gaulle failed to crush it, and the Evian accords brought independence on 5 July 1962 after a struggle that cost over a million martyrs. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
