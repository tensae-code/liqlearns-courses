# Hands-on — Algeria's War of Independence, 1954–1962

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Algeria's War of Independence, 1954–1962 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Constantine massacre of 8 May 1945, in which 45,000 Algerians were martyred, taught the national movement that only arms would free the country; the Great Algerian Revolution opened on 1 November 1954 from the Aurès, a provisional government formed in Cairo in 1958, De Gaulle failed to crush it, and the Evian accords brought independence on 5 July 1962 after a struggle that cost over a million martyrs. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
