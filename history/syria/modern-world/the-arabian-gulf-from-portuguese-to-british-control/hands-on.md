# Hands-on — The Arabian Gulf: From Portuguese to British Control

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Arabian Gulf: From Portuguese to British Control — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Portuguese seized Muscat and Bahrain, and the Arabs of Oman freed Muscat in 1650 and drove them out; Britain then bound the Gulf rulers by treaties (1800, the 1820 treaty after the Qawasim resistance, Kuwait 1899), and after oil was found faced American rivalry; Kuwait won independence in 1961, the Trucial states united as the Emirates in 1971, Oman united in 1970, and the Gulf Cooperation Council was founded in 1981. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
