# Egypt: The Urabi Revolt and British Occupation, 1882 — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Napoleon Bonaparte's campaign of 1798 was forced out by 1801 by popular resistance; after the Suez Canal opened, foreign interference grew until Ahmad Urabi's revolt of 1881; Britain bombarded Alexandria in 1882, defeated Urabi at Tell el-Kebir and occupied Cairo, exiling him to Ceylon until 1901. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
