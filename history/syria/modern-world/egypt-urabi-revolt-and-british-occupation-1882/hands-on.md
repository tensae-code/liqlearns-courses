# Hands-on — Egypt: The Urabi Revolt and British Occupation, 1882

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Egypt: The Urabi Revolt and British Occupation, 1882 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Napoleon Bonaparte's campaign of 1798 was forced out by 1801 by popular resistance; after the Suez Canal opened, foreign interference grew until Ahmad Urabi's revolt of 1881; Britain bombarded Alexandria in 1882, defeated Urabi at Tell el-Kebir and occupied Cairo, exiling him to Ceylon until 1901. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
