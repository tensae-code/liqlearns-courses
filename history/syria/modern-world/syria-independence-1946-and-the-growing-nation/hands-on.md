# Hands-on — Syria's Independence 1946 and the Growing Nation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Syria's Independence 1946 and the Growing Nation — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Under French colonialism Syria's people were no more than three million, suffering poverty, disease and few hospitals; after independence from French colonialism in 1946 the state spread schools and hospitals, deaths fell, and the people grew past 23 million by 2010. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
