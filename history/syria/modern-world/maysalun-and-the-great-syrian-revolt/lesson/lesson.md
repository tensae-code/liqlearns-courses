# Maysalun and the Great Syrian Revolt

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Maysalun and the Great Syrian Revolt** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1, Lesson فرنسا تحتل سورية وتنهي الحكم العربي فيها (France occupies Syria and ends Arab rule)

The Syrian Congress rejects the Faisal–Clemenceau agreement, and San Remo hands Syria to France. General Gouraud's ultimatum of 14 July 1920 demands the Arab army's dissolution; Faisal accepts — and France marches anyway. At the pass of Maysalun, War Minister Yusuf al-Azma leads the volunteers out to meet a modern army, knowing what it means, and is martyred there on 24 July 1920. The French enter Damascus, and the Arab government — Syria's two years of self-rule — ends.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1, Lesson الشعب السوري يواجه الاحتلال الفرنسي بالثورات (The Syrian people confront the French occupation with revolts)

France splits Syria into small statelets and loots it — and the revolts answer, region by region: Saleh al-Ali on the coast, Ibrahim Hananu in the north, Hawran in 1920, an ambush of Gouraud's own convoy in the Golan in 1921. Then they join into one fire: the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925–1927 under Sultan al-Atrash — al-Kafr, the siege of Suwayda, al-Mazraa where the French lost thousands, then the Ghouta and Damascus itself, which France bombards and burns, the al-Hariqa quarter going up in flames. The revolt is crushed, but Syria has announced it will never be quietly ruled.
