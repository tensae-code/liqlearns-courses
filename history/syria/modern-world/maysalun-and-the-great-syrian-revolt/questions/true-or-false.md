# Maysalun and the Great Syrian Revolt — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     The Syrian Congress rejected the Faisal–Clemenceau agreement and San Remo put Syria under French mandate; General Gouraud's ultimatum of 14 July 1920 demanded the army's dissolution, and though Faisal accepted, France marched; War Minister Yusuf al-Azma led the volunteers to Maysalun and was martyred there on 24 July 1920, and the French entered Damascus and ended the Arab government.
**Answer: True**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     The part “Unit 1, Lesson الشعب السوري يواجه الاحتلال الفرنسي بالثورات (The Syrian people confront the French occupation with revolts)” is mainly about this: Photos of an old quarter — narrow alleys, houses joined wall to wall, the Arab courtyard house — are set against a modern quarter of wide streets and spaced towers, and the child reads the past of building in their own streets.
**Answer: False** — It is about this: France split Syria into small statelets and looted it, and the revolts answered — Saleh al-Ali on the coast, Ibrahim Hananu in the north, Hawran in 1920, the Golan ambush of Gouraud's convoy in 1921 — until the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925–1927 under Sultan al-Atrash: al-Kafr, the siege of Suwayda, al-Mazraa, then the Ghouta and Damascus itself, which France bombarded and burned (the al-Hariqa quarter).
