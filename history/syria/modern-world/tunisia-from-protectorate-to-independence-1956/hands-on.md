# Hands-on — Tunisia: From Protectorate to Independence, 1956

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Tunisia: From Protectorate to Independence, 1956 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: France invaded in 1881 and imposed the protectorate by the Treaty of Bardo, and the Kairouan revolt answered; parties carried the struggle until the armed rising of 1954, fired by Algeria's revolution, won independence in 1956; Bourguiba's republic of 1957, Ben Ali, and the 2011 revolution close the lesson. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
