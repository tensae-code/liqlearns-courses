# Video script — The Middle East Conflict Begins


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

In the June 1967 aggression, the book tells Syria's pupils, the Zionist enemy occupied wide Arab lands — Jerusalem, the West Bank, Gaza, Sinai, and Syria's own Golan, whose fall the Defence Minister Hafez al-Assad announced before the enemy had even reached Quneitra. Six years later Syria fought again, beside Egypt, in the October 1973 war. The conflict that begins in these years runs through everything that follows in the region's story.

The project begins in the nineteenth century: Herzl's book 'The Jewish State' and the Basel congress of 1897 call for a Jewish national home in Palestine — and Sultan Abd al-Hamid II refuses Herzl's 1901 offer to pay the Ottoman debts in exchange. Palestinian newspapers warn of the danger from 1905, but Britain adopts the project for its oil and the Suez route, issues the Balfour Declaration of 2 November 1917, and its mandate government opens immigration, recognises the Jewish Agency and arms the Haganah. The conflict's roots, the book insists, are planted here — in decisions taken over Palestine's head.

The United Nations' partition project of 1947, Britain's withdrawal on 14 May 1948 and the proclamation of the Zionist state; the Deir Yassin massacre and the Nakba that drives the Palestinians from most of their land. Then the long resistance: the PLO founded in 1964, the June 1967 occupation, the First Intifada of the children of stones in 1987, the Oslo agreement of 1993, the Second Intifada of 2000, the aggression on Gaza in 2008 — and Palestine's full UNESCO membership in 2011, the story carried to the pupils' own present.


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Syria.
