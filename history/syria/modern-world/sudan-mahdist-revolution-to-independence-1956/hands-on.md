# Hands-on — Sudan: From the Mahdist Revolution to Independence, 1956

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Sudan: From the Mahdist Revolution to Independence, 1956 — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Muhammad Ali's campaigns made the Sudan an Egyptian province, and its misrule raised the Mahdist revolution of 1881 under Muhammad Ahmad al-Mahdi; Britain returned with Kitchener's campaign and the Condominium agreement of 1899 made Britain the real ruler; resistance ran until the 1953 self-government agreement and independence in 1956 — and the lesson closes with the coups, the civil wars and the South's secession in 2011. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
