# Hands-on — Africa Resists the Scramble: Algeria, Ethiopia, Sudan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — Africa Resists the Scramble: Algeria, Ethiopia, Sudan — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: France used the fly-whisk incident to blockade and take Algiers in 1830; Emir Abd al-Qadir built a state and fought for fifteen years, winning the Treaty of Tafna in 1837 before Bugeaud's scorched-earth war forced his surrender in 1847 — he died in exile in Damascus in 1883 — and the resistance went on: al-Muqrani in 1871, Bouamama in the Sahara, and Lalla Fatma N'Soumer, who beat the French in 1854 and died in their prison in 1863. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
