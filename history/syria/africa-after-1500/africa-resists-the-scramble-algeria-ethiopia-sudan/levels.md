# Africa Resists the Scramble: Algeria, Ethiopia, Sudan — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — France used the fly-whisk incident to blockade and take Algiers in 1830; Emir Abd al-Qadir built a state and fought for fifteen years, winning the Treaty of Tafna in 1837 before Bugeaud's scorched-earth war forced his surrender in 1847 — he died in exile in Damascus in 1883 — and the resistance went on: al-Muqrani in 1871, Bouamama in the Sahara, and Lalla Fatma N'Soumer, who beat the French in 1854 and died in their prison in 1863. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
