# Video script — Islam in North Africa


The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.


---


This lesson tells one story.

Egypt under Byzantine rule was taxed and squeezed by Roman officials, until its people turned against their rulers. Into that discontent comes the Arab liberation — and Egypt, freed, becomes the base from which Islam reaches down the Nile into Nubia. The lesson's title carries its logic: liberate Egypt, and the doors of the whole Nile Valley swing open.

Liberation is followed by construction. Egypt becomes a province of the caliphate with Fustat as its new capital; its governors reform the administration and the courts, and order replaces extraction. Out of those reforms a new shared civilisation takes shape — the lesson calls its fruit التآلف, harmony: peoples who were strangers begin to live inside one civilisation together.

Libya's story starts from its old Phoenician ports — Barqa, Tripoli, Sabratha and Leptis — cities founded from the Levant long before. After Egypt is liberated, Libya follows: the Arab-Islamic liberation reaches its coast, and an Arab-Islamic civilisation rises there on the old foundations. Before and after, port by port, the lesson traces the same transformation Egypt knew, now on the Mediterranean's southern shore.

The last and hardest stage of the westward story is the Maghreb. The Umayyad state shoulders the liberation of Ifriqiya and the Maghreb, city by city from Kairouan to Tangier, and settles the whole region as one province of the state. With Tangier reached, the Arab state stands at the Atlantic — the expansion that began in Arabia now spans a continent's northern shore.


Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Syria.
