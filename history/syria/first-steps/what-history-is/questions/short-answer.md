# What History Is — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 2, Lesson 1: صغار كبار (Small ones, grown ones)”?
**Answer:** A class of six-year-olds looks at photographs of themselves as babies and at the young adults around them, and a simple discovery lands: the things a small child can do are not the things a grown person can do. A single life is not one fixed picture — it changes as the years pass. That change across time, in one person's own life, is where the book says history begins: the past is the story of how things became what they are.

**2.** (Sub lesson 3)     How does the story in “Unit أنا وأنت, Lesson صغار كبار (Small ones, grown ones)” end?
**Answer:** Classmates compare birthdays and find no two lives run at the same speed: each was born in a different month, and people sort naturally into child, young adult and elder. One single life, watched closely, is seen changing across its stages — small, grown, old. The lesson makes the youngest pupils historians of the one story they know best: a human life unfolding in time.
