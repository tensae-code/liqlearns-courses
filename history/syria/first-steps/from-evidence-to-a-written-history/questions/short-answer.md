# From Evidence to a Written History — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Skills unit: أدلة التاريخ (The evidence of history)”?
**Answer:** How do we know what happened long ago? The book lays out the detective's tools: buildings still standing, manuscripts written by hand, coins, tools, and miniature paintings — each one a piece of proof to be weighed, not a story to be swallowed whole. Two worked examples show the method in action: the al-Hayr palaces rising from the desert, and a Fatimid ewer, a water jug whose making tells its own tale. Evidence first; the written history comes after.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)     How does the story in “Unit وطني, Lesson بين الماضي والحاضر (Between past and present)” end?
**Answer:** The pupils are handed a puzzle from deep in Syria's past: signs carved in wedge shapes, the alphabet of ancient Ugarit. By swapping each wedge sign for an Arabic letter, they decode the old writing and find where the excavated pieces are kept today. The game carries a serious lesson — old writing is not decoration; it is evidence, and evidence can be read by anyone patient enough to learn its key.
