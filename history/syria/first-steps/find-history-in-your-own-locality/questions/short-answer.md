# Find History in Your Own Locality — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 6, Lesson 3: صور من وطني (Pictures from my homeland)”?
**Answer:** A father takes out two photographs of the same place — one old, one new — and spreads them before his child. The game is to hunt: what changed between the two pictures, and what stayed exactly the same? Streets, buildings and clothes shift; the place itself endures. The lesson sends every child home to do the same with their own streets, because the past is not only in books — it is standing outside your door.

**2.** (Sub lesson 5)     How does the story in “Unit 3, Lesson الحياة في المدينة (Life in the city)” end?
**Answer:** Most Syrian cities, the book says, are far older than they look: they rise from ancient historical periods, civilisation laid over civilisation. Their old quarters are still there to be read — narrow lanes, and covered souks where each craft keeps its own street. The lesson proves it with photographs: Damascus a hundred years ago, and al-Hamidiyah souk in 1886, caught before its famous roof was even finished.
