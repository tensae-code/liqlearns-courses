# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 6, Lesson 2: آثار من وطني (Antiquities from my homeland)** — The antiquities printed on Syria's banknotes — the Umayyad Mosque, Bosra's amphitheatre, Aleppo's citadel, Ugarit's alphabet — are matched to the governorates where they stand.
2. **Unit 1, Lesson 4: التراث الشعبي (Folk heritage)** — Feasts, tales, crafts and customs pass hand to hand down the generations and become a people's living heritage.
3. **Unit 2, Lesson 4: التراث ذاكرة الأجداد (Heritage, the memory of the ancestors)** — Heritage is what the ancestors hand down and we hand on — its root stays firm while its forms grow, from muwashshah song to daily custom.
4. **Unit أنا, Lesson ذاكرة وطن (Memory of a homeland)** — The grandfather shows tools that appeared in Syria across the twentieth century — lantern, water press, cart, washing tools — and the children compare school long ago with school today.
5. **Unit الباحث الصغير, Lesson بصمات سورية (Syrian imprints)** — The school is named after Jul Jamal, the Syrian naval officer from Latakia who gave his life defending Egypt in 1956 — a national hero whose 'imprint' the children research and record.
6. **Reading lessons: من كنوزنا الأثرية / بيت الآثار (From our archaeological treasures / The house of antiquities)** — A family plans a trip to ancient Palmyra, and Hanaa visits the National Museum in Damascus with her father — Syria's antiquities are the nation's treasures.
7. **Unit مجتمعي, Lesson تراثنا (Our heritage)** — The folk dress of Latakia, Raqqa, al-Hasaka, Aleppo, Homs and Suwayda is pictured region by region, and the games children played in old times and the folk songs are named as what the homeland hands down.
8. **Unit وطني, Lesson آثارنا الوطنية (Our national antiquities)** — The ancestors left many antiquities as a witness to their achievements — Bosra's amphitheatre, Apamea's colonnade, the Krak des Chevaliers and the kingdom of Eimar on the Euphrates.
9. **Unit 2 سورية والتاريخ, Lesson مواقع تاريخية (Historic sites)** — Wherever you travel in Syria you find archaeological sites from every age of history; these sites are a great wealth of the homeland that must be preserved and protected, proving Syria's civilising role across history.
10. **Unit 3, Lesson الحياة في البادية (Life in the Badia)** — The Badia's people still keep the authentic Arab customs — generosity, hospitality and bravery — settling disputes before the tribe's sheikh in the hair tent where coffee is drunk and poetry exchanged, and the Palmyra festival is held each year among the ruins of the oasis.
11. **Unit 4 دعائم الاقتصاد, Lesson العمل في الصناعة في سورية (Work in industry in Syria)** — Syria is among the oldest regions humans inhabited, and in every age its people developed their tools and crafts, which the trade caravans carried to the whole world; its hand industries are famous worldwide — glass, inlaid wood, copper, and the brocade called damask, of which Queen Elizabeth asked the Syrian government for a piece for her wedding dress.
12. **Unit 4, Lesson السياحة في سورية (Tourism in Syria)** — Our ancestors lived on Syria's land thousands of years ago and left a beautiful civilisational heritage that draws visitors from every corner of the world; one whole kind of tourism is the archaeological, to places like Palmyra, Ebla and the many castles.
