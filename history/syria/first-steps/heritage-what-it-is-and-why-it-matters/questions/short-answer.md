# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 6, Lesson 2: آثار من وطني (Antiquities from my homeland)”?
**Answer:** Open a Syrian wallet and history falls out: the country's banknotes carry its antiquities. The child matches each pictured treasure to the governorate where it stands — the Umayyad Mosque, the great amphitheatre of Bosra, the citadel of Aleppo, and the alphabet of Ugarit, the letters the whole world would inherit. Heritage, the lesson says, is not locked in a museum; the homeland prints it on its money because it belongs to everyone.

**2.** (Sub lesson 12)     How does the story in “Unit 4, Lesson السياحة في سورية (Tourism in Syria)” end?
**Answer:** The ancestors lived on Syria's land thousands of years ago and left behind a beautiful civilisational heritage — and that heritage now draws visitors from every corner of the world. One whole kind of tourism exists for it alone: archaeological tourism, to places like Palmyra and Ebla and the many castles on their hills. What the grandfathers built, the book says, still feeds and teaches the grandchildren.
