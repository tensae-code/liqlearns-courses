# Dating the Past — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)     What happens in “Unit 1, Lesson 4: كيف أمضي يومي؟ (How do I spend my day?)”?
**Answer:** A six-year-old takes the day's activities — waking, eating, playing, learning, sleeping — and numbers them 1 to 6 along a line. Nothing has been explained yet, but the child has already done the first move of history: putting events in order. Before anyone can tell the story of the past, the book shows, the events must stand in their right sequence, first things first.

**2.** (Sub lesson 8)     How does the story in “Unit 2 سورية والتاريخ, Lesson وسائل النقل في سورية (Means of transport in Syria)” end?
**Answer:** A printed timeline runs the whole length of the lesson: first people walking on their own feet, then pack animals, then wheeled carts, then sailing ships, then steam and oil, and at the far end trains and aeroplanes. Each stage stands in its place on the line, and the child reads a thousand changes as one ordered story. Transport, the book shows, develops stage by stage through history — and a timeline is how you prove it.
