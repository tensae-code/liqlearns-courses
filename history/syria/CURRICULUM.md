# History — Syria course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Syria inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Syria's books in hand are the Ministry of Education's student books in Arabic — combined Social Studies books for Grades 1–4 and 6, and History books for Grades 7–9 (the Grade 5 record in the filing is a second scan of the Grade 6 book and carries no placements — see ABOUT.md). The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the eras as Syria's own books present them: history begins at home, in the youngest books' own way — your day on a timeline, your family tree, old and new photographs, and the homeland's antiquities on its banknotes (Level 1); ancient Syria and the first civilisations, taught thematically by the Grade 7 book and the Grade 4 "Syria and History" unit — first settlements, farming, crafts, city-states, empires, writing and the trade roads (Level 2); the Arab state ruled from Damascus and the civilisation it built, as the Grade 8 book tells the medieval centuries (Level 3); and the modern Arab world — occupation, resistance and independence — as the Grade 9 book teaches it from Damascus outward, closing with Syria's own revolution and the fall of the regime on 8 December 2024 (Level 4). The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Syria's own Ministry of Education student books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: History Begins at Home** — the historian's craft learned the way Syria's youngest books teach it: what history is (a life changing across its stages, a family tree), how the past is dated (your day in order, grandfather's fifty years on a timeline, eras and calendars), how evidence becomes a written history (remains, coins, and Ugarit's alphabet decoded), history hunted in your own locality (old and new photographs, the old quarter against the new), and heritage — the homeland's antiquities, folk dress, songs and crafts handed down — 5 lessons.

2. **Ancient Syria and the First Civilisations** — from the Yabroud caves to the first villages, farming on the Euphrates, the first crafts, the gods of farmers and warriors — then the city-states Ebla, Mari and Ugarit, Mesopotamia's empires and gifts, schools and libraries, barter growing into money, the trade routes and Persia's rivalry — with Egypt on the Nile and Carthage founded from Syria's own coast, and Syria's historic cities still standing to be walked through — 17 lessons.

3. **The Arab State from Damascus: Medieval Syria** — the liberation wars and the world state raised with Damascus at its heart, its administration of caliphs, governors and diwans — then the civilisation inside the frame: a society of roots and mixture, women as partners in building life, feasts, farming science, crafts, learning and translation, architecture and calligraphy, and the civilisation opening to the world — shadowed by the Crusader and Mongol assaults and answered by resisters from Nur al-Din to Omar al-Mukhtar, with Islam's North African story told region by region — 15 lessons.

4. **The Modern Arab World: Occupation, Resistance and Independence** — Muhammad Ali's modernising project and the secret divisions of the First World War; Syria's own road — the Arab government of 1918, Maysalun, the Great Revolt, the political struggle to the evacuation of 17 April 1946, the 1958 unity and its secession, 1967 and 1973, and the long despotism that ends with the 2011 revolution and the fall of the regime on 8 December 2024 — then the same frame widened to every Arab land: Lebanon, Jordan and Palestine; Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Yemen and the Gulf; Egypt, Sudan and the Horn; and the Maghreb's liberations from Algeria to Mauritania — 27 lessons.

**Totals: 64 lessons** (Level 1: 5 · Level 2: 17 · Level 3: 15 · Level 4: 27 — 110 placements stocked, all from student books; 19 lessons carry sub lessons; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only — every lesson stands on a student book, as filed in ABOUT.md).
