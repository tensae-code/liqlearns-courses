# Lesson plan — Pacific Voyaging and Island Exchange

**Course:** History — Nauru · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Pacific Voyaging and Island Exchange
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Pacific Past Through the Sea and the Land (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Long ago the Pacific islands of Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue, Rotuma, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Futuna and Uvea were not separate worlds cut off by water — they were one large exchange community, joined by the sea: their peoples voyaged regularly between the islands, circulating wealth, skills and goods, so that one sea joined many homes.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Pacific Past Through the Sea and the Land — it stands at the foot of Nauru's ladder, on nothing before it in this course (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the islands were one large exchange community, joined by regular voyaging that circulated wealth, skills and goods — one sea joining many homes.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — a set of homes or villages that stay one community because people and goods keep moving between them.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the book itself works, teaching the Pacific past as the story of the sea that joins the islands.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the sea separated the islands → the book's own picture is the opposite: the sea was the road. The islands were one community *because* people voyaged across it.
- Thinks each island lived alone with only what it had → the exchange circulated wealth, skills and goods, so what one island had or knew could reach the others.
- Go back to the one thing: the islands were one large exchange community, joined by regular voyaging — one sea joining many homes. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
