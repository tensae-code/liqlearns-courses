# History — Nauru course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Nauru's
own — built from the regional USP/UNESCO Education for
Sustainable Development readers carried in Nauru's record
(Reader 1 *People and the Sea*, Reader 3 *People and the
Natural Environment*, and their Teacher Guide; English).
Inside the course the organisation is by **levels, not
grades**: the titles below sit inside Level 1, in path
order — what the learner proves, step by step. The grade
the books come from lives only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the readers': Reader 1 opens with
the voyaging past, then the sea's heritage; Reader 3 turns
landward to the heritage of gardens and the coconut tree;
the Teacher Guide closes by sending the learner to an
elder in their own locality. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — the Pacific past through the sea and the land**
(ESD Readers 1 and 3, student books, with the ESD Teacher
Guide as labelled evidence):

- `ancient-world/` — Level 1, first title. Pacific
  Voyaging and Island Exchange ("The Ocean in our blood":
  Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, Niue, Rotuma, Tokelau, Tuvalu, Futuna
  and Uvea as one large exchange community, voyaging
  regularly between islands and circulating wealth, skills
  and goods — one sea joining many homes).
- `first-steps/` — Level 1, second title. Heritage: What
  It Is and Why It Matters (sharks, turtles and whales in
  Pacific culture; traditional fishing in Tuvalu and Niue
  as a community craft of custom and sharing; environmental
  knowledge passed down through the generations; the taro
  case study of Tonga, Niue and Tuvalu; the amazing coconut
  tree — toddy in Tuvalu and Niue's own name-story; and the
  teacher guide's coconut lesson on inheritance); and Find
  History in Your Own Locality (the teacher guide's method:
  pupils talk to an elder about sacred and traditional
  practices and list traditional fishing practices and the
  fish that were caught — the oldest person in the village
  becomes the textbook).
