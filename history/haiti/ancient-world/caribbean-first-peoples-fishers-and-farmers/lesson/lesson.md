# Caribbean First Peoples: Fishers and Farmers

## Do it first

Break an old clay pot (in imagination) and look at the pieces: clay, shape, decoration. What could a stranger learn about your home from one shard? This lesson reads the Caribbean's first peoples through their pottery.

The world story lands in the Antilles. The islands' first peoples were sea-going fisher-gatherers, living from the reef and the shore, moving by canoe. Then farming villagers arrived — agriculteurs-éleveurs with fields, settled villages, and pottery. Two worlds touched on the same shores. How do we know? The ceramics carry the evidence: fired clay survives where wood and fibre rot, and in the shards — their clay, shapes and decorations mixing and changing — archaeologists read the meeting of the fisher-gatherers and the farmers, the first chapter of the Caribbean's own past.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: in the Antilles, sea-going fisher-gatherers and pottery-making farming villagers meet — their ceramics carry the evidence of two worlds touching. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: fisher-gatherers, ceramics. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
