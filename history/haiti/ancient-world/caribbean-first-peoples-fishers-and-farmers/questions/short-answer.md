# Caribbean First Peoples: Fishers and Farmers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 6, Le monde antillais des pêcheurs-cueilleurs et des agriculteurs-éleveurs": who, where, and what turned?
**Answer:** The world story lands in the Antilles. The islands' first peoples were sea-going fisher-gatherers, living from the reef and the shore, moving by canoe. Then farming villagers arrived — agriculteurs-éleveurs with fields, settled villages, and pottery. Two worlds touched on the same shores. How do we know? The ceramics carry the evidence: fired clay survives where wood and fibre rot, and in the shards — their clay, shapes and decorations mixing and changing — archaeologists read the meeting of the fisher-gatherers and the farmers, the first chapter of the Caribbean's own past.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
**Answer:** In the Antilles, sea-going fisher-gatherers and pottery-making farming villagers meet — their ceramics carry the evidence of two worlds touching.
