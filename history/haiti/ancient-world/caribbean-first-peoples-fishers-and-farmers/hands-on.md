# Hands-on — Caribbean First Peoples: Fishers and Farmers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Break an old clay pot (in imagination) and look at the pieces: clay, shape, decoration. What could a stranger learn about your home from one shard? This lesson reads the Caribbean's first peoples through their pottery.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: in the Antilles, sea-going fisher-gatherers and pottery-making farming villagers meet — their ceramics carry the evidence of two worlds touching. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
