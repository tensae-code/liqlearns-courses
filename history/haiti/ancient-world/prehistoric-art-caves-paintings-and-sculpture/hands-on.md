# Hands-on — Prehistoric Art: Cave Paintings and Sculpture

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw one animal on paper using only lines and dots, the way you would on a cave wall by firelight. Why would a hunter spend hours painting deep in the dark? This lesson asks what the first images were for.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the first images humans made of their world — painted cave walls and carved figures — appear among the hunter peoples, and when settled villagers come, they carve sculpture and raise megaliths: settled life invents new images of the unseen world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
