# Video script — Prehistoric Art: Cave Paintings and Sculpture

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The first images humans made of their world — painted cave walls and carved figures — appear among the hunter peoples, and when settled villagers come, they carve sculpture and raise megaliths: settled life invents new images of the unseen world.

Deep in caves, by torchlight, the hunter peoples painted their world: herds of animals run across the stone walls, and small carved figures fit in a hand. These are the first images humans ever made — proof that the mind that made tools also needed pictures. The book asks the pupils to look closely and to argue: is this art, in our sense? And it ends in the present: these fragile walls must be protected, and preserving the first images is a fight the living still have to win.

When people settle — farming, storing, living in villages that stay — their images change with their lives. The first villagers carve sculpture and raise megaliths, great stones standing in the landscape. The settled world has new unseen things to picture: ancestors who must be honoured where the village endures, powers of field and season. Settled life invents new images of the unseen world, and the stones it raised still stand as its witnesses.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
