# Prehistoric Art: Cave Paintings and Sculpture — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 3, Un art préhistorique (1) ?": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** Deep in caves, by torchlight, the hunter peoples painted their world: herds of animals run across the stone walls, and small carved figures fit in a hand. These are the first images humans ever made — proof that the mind that made tools also needed pictures. The book asks the pupils to look closely and to argue: is this art, in our sense? And it ends in the present: these fragile walls must be protected, and preserving the first images is a fight the living still have to win.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** The first images humans made of their world — painted cave walls and carved figures — appear among the hunter peoples, and when settled villagers come, they carve sculpture and raise megaliths: settled life invents new images of the unseen world.
