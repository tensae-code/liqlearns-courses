# The Taïno of Quisqueya — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 2, Un territoire et une société « archipéliques »": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** Long before any European sail appeared, the Taïno were already a people of the sea. On Quisqueya and across the neighbouring islands, their villages stood linked by sea paths — canoes crossing between communities of one archipelago society. In the villages there were ball courts where the communities gathered and played; around them, fields and fishing grounds fed the people. The Taïno world was not one island alone but a web: kin, trade, games and voyages tying island to island.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** The Taïno spread across an archipelago — villages, ball courts and sea paths linking Quisqueya's communities — ruled by chiefs called caciques, though pupils must test whether the 'caciquat' in the books is Taïno reality or a shape imposed by Spanish writers.
