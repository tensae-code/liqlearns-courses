# Lesson plan — The Taïno of Quisqueya

**Course:** History — Haiti · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Taïno of Quisqueya
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Historian's Craft and the First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Taïno spread across an archipelago — villages, ball courts and sea paths linking Quisqueya's communities — ruled by chiefs called caciques, though pupils must test whether the 'caciquat' in the books is Taïno reality or a shape imposed by Spanish writers.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Historian's Craft and the First Peoples — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Séance 2, Un territoire et une société « archipéliques »** — An island people spread across an archipelago — villages, ball courts and sea paths link Quisqueya's communities long before any European sail appears.
2. **Séance 3, Les Taïno et le caciquat, des constructions ethno-historiques ?** — Chiefs called caciques rule the villages — but pupils test whether the 'caciquat' they read about is Taïno reality or a shape imposed by Spanish writers.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
