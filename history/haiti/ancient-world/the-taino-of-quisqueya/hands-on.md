# Hands-on — The Taïno of Quisqueya

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at a map of the Caribbean islands. Before any European sail, people already crossed these seas and linked the islands. This lesson meets the island's first people — and asks how much of what we read about them we can trust.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the Taïno spread across an archipelago — villages, ball courts and sea paths linking Quisqueya's communities — ruled by chiefs called caciques, though pupils must test whether the 'caciquat' in the books is Taïno reality or a shape imposed by Spanish writers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
