# History — Haiti course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Haiti's own —
built from Haiti's Projet Nectar / MENFP History teacher-guide
sequences for the 7e, 8e and 9e années, in French. Inside the
course the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the
titles below sit inside Levels 1–3, in path order — what the
learner proves, step by step. The grades the books come from
live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the books': the historian's craft
first, learned on a building, an object and a date near home,
and the island's first peoples (Level 1); then the colony —
plantation, Code Noir, a century of resistance, and the
Revolution of 1791–1804 (Level 2); and last the state the
Revolution founded, its debt and its occupation (Level 3).
That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead" hints
point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — the historian's craft and the first peoples:**

- `first-steps/` — Levels 1, the opening title. What History
  Is (a local building, object and date turned into a real
  historical question) · From Evidence to a Written History
  (the corpus hunted, sorted, re-read and argued into an
  interpretation — three parts) · Find History in Your Own
  Locality (the coffee mill as witness; the past at work in
  exhibitions, commemorations, money and images) · Heritage:
  What It Is and Why It Matters (Anacaona and the Taïno past
  in debate; slavery remembered by statues and laws) · Who
  Studies Prehistory (fossils, tools and the layers of a dig)
  · Is History a Science? (docu-fiction films taken apart,
  twice).
- `africa-to-1500/` — Level 1, the deep beginning. Africa,
  Cradle of Humankind (sapiens walks out of Africa to every
  continent) · From Stone Tools to Farming (hunters and
  farmers side by side, trading and mixing).
- `ancient-world/` — Level 1, the island's first peoples and
  the first images. Prehistoric Art: Cave Paintings and
  Sculpture (the hunters' painted caves; the first villagers'
  sculpture and megaliths) · Caribbean First Peoples: Fishers
  and Farmers (the Antilles' two worlds meeting, read in
  ceramics) · The Taïno of Quisqueya (villages, ball courts
  and sea paths; the caciquat tested against its Spanish
  writers).
- `modern-world/` — Levels 1–3, Haiti's own modern story.
  In Level 1: Haiti's Forts Built to Defend Independence
  (Fort Jacques, the Citadelle, Fort Décidé — and their faces
  on the banknotes) · Haiti: The Flag and 18 May 1803
  (Arcahaie: the white torn out, the new flag sewn).

**Level 2 — plantation, resistance and revolution:**

- `modern-world/` — Level 2, in path order: Slavery and the
  Plantation Economy of Saint-Domingue (the system from the
  ground up — five parts) · The Code Noir: The Law That
  Ruled Slavery (the articles read line by line against the
  plantation's reality) · Resistance, Marronage and Revolt
  in Saint-Domingue (the Unknown Maroon; the masters'
  gazettes against the historians; Padrejean, Makandal and
  the maroon societies; Vodou under ban — four parts) · The
  French Revolution Breaks Out (1789) (the rights of man
  land in the ranked colony) · The Haitian Revolution
  (1791–1804) (the four acts and the founding texts — nine
  parts).

**Level 3 — building the state, the debt and the occupation:**

- `modern-world/` — Level 3, in path order: Building the
  Haitian State (1804–1843) (constitution by constitution —
  seven parts) · Haiti's Independence Debt: Neocolonialism
  by Debt (the 1825 ordinance, the double debt, Aristide's
  2003 demand — five parts) · The US Occupation of Haiti
  (1915–1934) (tutelage, corvée, the Cacos, collaboration,
  and the bilan — six parts).
