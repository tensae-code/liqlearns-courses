# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

List everything you ate yesterday. Which parts could a hunter-gatherer find wild, and which only exist because someone farms? This lesson tells how the two ways of life met.

The first farmers did not erase the hunters. For long ages the two worlds lived side by side in the same lands: hunter-gatherers who moved with the herds and the seasons, and sedentary farmer-herders settled by their fields. They met, and meeting changed both — they traded meat for grain, married across the line, borrowed tools, words and ways. The book's word is hybridation: mixing. The road from stone tools to farming is not a replacement but a long neighbourhood, and much of humanity's story since is the two ways still learning from each other.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: hunter-gatherers and farmer-herders live side by side, trading, mixing and borrowing each other's ways — the farming world grows beside the hunting world, not simply over its grave. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: nomads, sedentary. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
