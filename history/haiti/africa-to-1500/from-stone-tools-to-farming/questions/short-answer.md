# From Stone Tools to Farming — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 5, Nomades et sédentaires, cohabitation et hybridation": who, where, and what turned?
**Answer:** The first farmers did not erase the hunters. For long ages the two worlds lived side by side in the same lands: hunter-gatherers who moved with the herds and the seasons, and sedentary farmer-herders settled by their fields. They met, and meeting changed both — they traded meat for grain, married across the line, borrowed tools, words and ways. The book's word is hybridation: mixing. The road from stone tools to farming is not a replacement but a long neighbourhood, and much of humanity's story since is the two ways still learning from each other.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
**Answer:** Hunter-gatherers and farmer-herders live side by side, trading, mixing and borrowing each other's ways — the farming world grows beside the hunting world, not simply over its grave.
