# Africa, Cradle of Humankind

## Do it first

Put one finger on Africa on a map or globe. Now trace a road out of it — across to Asia, up to Europe, far to the Americas. This lesson walks that road with the first modern humans.

The human story begins in Africa. There Homo sapiens — our own kind — first walked, and from Africa the road led outward: across into Asia, up into Europe, island by island toward the far Pacific, and at last into the Americas. No continent was empty of wonder on the way: in the new lands, sapiens met older human kinds who had left Africa long before, and the kinds lived side by side for ages before the older ones vanished. One species, one birthplace, every continent: that is the walk this lesson takes.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Homo sapiens walks out of Africa and across every continent, meeting older human kinds on the way — Africa is where the human story begins. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Homo sapiens, cradle of humankind. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
