# Who Studies Prehistory

## Do it first

Dig a small hole in a garden (with permission) and look at the soil: darker on top, different below. Layers keep order. This lesson asks who studies the time before writing, and how the ground itself answers.

The prehistory book opens inside a dig. In the earth lie fossil bones — women, men, early human kinds — and beside them, stone tools. Found together, they force a sharp question: which hominids made these tools? The answer comes from the ground itself: a dig is dug in layers, and stratigraphy reads them — the deeper the layer, the older what lies in it. Bones and tools in the same layer belong to the same time. This is who studies prehistory: scientists who make the earth give evidence in order, layer by layer, until the time before writing can be told.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: prehistory is studied from what the ground keeps: bones and stone tools found together force the question of which hominids made them, and a dig's layers — stratigraphy — tell the order of the past. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: fossil, stratigraphy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
