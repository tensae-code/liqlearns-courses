# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Dig a small hole in a garden (with permission) and look at the soil: darker on top, different below. Layers keep order. This lesson asks who studies the time before writing, and how the ground itself answers.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: prehistory is studied from what the ground keeps: bones and stone tools found together force the question of which hominids made them, and a dig's layers — stratigraphy — tell the order of the past. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
