# What History Is

## Do it first

Stand in front of a building near your home — a school, a church, an old house. Ask out loud: who built this, when, and how could I prove it? Hold the question. This lesson is about turning that curiosity into a historian's question.

Haiti's first history book does not open with kings or dates. It opens near home: one building the pupils pass every day, one everyday object, one commemoration date on the calendar. The class looks at the three and lets curiosity work — who made this, when, why is this day remembered? Then comes the historian's move: the loose curiosity is sharpened into a problématique, one clear question that research can actually answer. A question that can be answered is the door into history; everything else in the book walks through it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: history in Haiti's first book does not begin with dates: it begins near home, with one building, one everyday object and one commemoration date, and turns the curiosity they wake into a real historical question. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: problématique, historical question. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
