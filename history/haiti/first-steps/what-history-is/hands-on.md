# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand in front of a building near your home — a school, a church, an old house. Ask out loud: who built this, when, and how could I prove it? Hold the question. This lesson is about turning that curiosity into a historian's question.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: history in Haiti's first book does not begin with dates: it begins near home, with one building, one everyday object and one commemoration date, and turns the curiosity they wake into a real historical question. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
