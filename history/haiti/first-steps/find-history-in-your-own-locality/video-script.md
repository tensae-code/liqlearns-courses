# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History lives where you live: an everyday object like a coffee mill can be read as a witness of past work and tastes, and the past is still at work today in exhibitions, commemorations, money and images.

The inquiry ends by turning to the present. The pupils trace how their local object lives on today: it appears in exhibitions, it is carried in commemorations, its face may even stare out from money and printed images. History, they learn, is not only found — it is used, every day, by the society around them. The class builds a public presentation of their object, showing both what it witnessed in the past and what work it does now.

The study object is humble: a coffee mill, the kind that stood in a kitchen and ground the beans by hand. The pupils read it the way a historian reads a document. Its worn handle tells of daily work; the coffee itself tells of trade that crossed oceans; the drink it made tells of the tastes of past households. An ordinary object, questioned properly, recovers a whole world of work, trade and taste that no chronicle bothered to write down.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
