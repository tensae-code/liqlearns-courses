# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Haiti · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Historian's Craft and the First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** history lives where you live: an everyday object like a coffee mill can be read as a witness of past work and tastes, and the past is still at work today in exhibitions, commemorations, money and images.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Historian's Craft and the First Peoples — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Séance 4, Les usages de l'histoire** — Pupils trace how their object is used today — in exhibitions, commemorations, money and images — and build a public presentation of it.
2. **Study object: the coffee mill as an everyday historical object** — A kitchen coffee mill becomes a witness — pupils read an ordinary object to recover the work, trade and tastes of past households.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- The lesson before it in path order — From Evidence to a Written History — is recommended first (never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: history lives where you live: an everyday object like a coffee mill can be read as a witness of past work and tastes, and the past is still at work today in exhibitions, commemorations, money and images.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the Haiti they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Haiti and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks only monuments and famous dates carry history → the book reads a kitchen coffee mill as a witness and traces one local object through today's exhibitions, commemorations, money and images — the ordinary carries history too.
- Go back to the one thing: history lives where you live: an everyday object like a coffee mill can be read as a witness of past work and tastes, and the past is still at work today in exhibitions, commemorations, money and images. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
