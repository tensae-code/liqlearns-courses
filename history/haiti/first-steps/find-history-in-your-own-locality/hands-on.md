# Hands-on — Find History in Your Own Locality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find the oldest everyday object in your kitchen or yard — a tool, a pot, a mill. Hold it and ask: whose hands used this before mine, and what work did it do? This lesson reads history out of just such an object.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: history lives where you live: an everyday object like a coffee mill can be read as a witness of past work and tastes, and the past is still at work today in exhibitions, commemorations, money and images. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
