# Find History in Your Own Locality — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 4, Les usages de l'histoire": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The inquiry ends by turning to the present. The pupils trace how their local object lives on today: it appears in exhibitions, it is carried in commemorations, its face may even stare out from money and printed images. History, they learn, is not only found — it is used, every day, by the society around them. The class builds a public presentation of their object, showing both what it witnessed in the past and what work it does now.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** History lives where you live: an everyday object like a coffee mill can be read as a witness of past work and tastes, and the past is still at work today in exhibitions, commemorations, money and images.
