# Hands-on — Is History a Science?

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Watch (or imagine) one scene of a film about the distant past: a cave family at a fire. List three things the film shows. Which could science actually know — and which did the film invent? This lesson teaches that sorting.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: history is tested like a science: pupils take apart docu-fiction films and sort what research knows from what the film invents — and judge again, at the sequence's end, how research becomes a story told to the public. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
