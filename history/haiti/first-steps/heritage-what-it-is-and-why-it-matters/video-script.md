# Video script — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Heritage is the past a society chooses to keep and argue over — Queen Anacaona and the Taïno past fought over in novels, comics and museums, and slavery remembered through statues, commemorations and laws: whose heritage it is, and who gets to show it, is itself a historical question.

Queen Anacaona, the Taïno leader, did not stay in the past. She lives on in novels and comics, in museum rooms and arguments — and the Taïno past itself is fought over today: who may tell it, who may display its objects, whose heritage is it? The pupils learn that heritage — patrimoine — is not just what survives. It is what the living choose to keep and show, and every choice can be debated. The images of the past are a battleground of the present.

Slavery, too, is remembered by choice. The plantation book closes by asking how societies today carry this past: statues raised or argued over, museums built, commemorations held, and laws passed — among them France's Taubira law of 2001, which named slavery a crime against humanity. Each is a decision about memory: what a society says about its worst past tells what it wants to be. Heritage, the pupils see, is the past still at work — and still contested.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
