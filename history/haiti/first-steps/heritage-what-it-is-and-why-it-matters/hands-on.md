# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing from the past that your town keeps on purpose — a statue, a song, a building, a day. Who decided to keep it? This lesson asks what heritage is, and who it belongs to.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: heritage is the past a society chooses to keep and argue over — Queen Anacaona and the Taïno past fought over in novels, comics and museums, and slavery remembered through statues, commemorations and laws: whose heritage it is, and who gets to show it, is itself a historical question. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
