# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 4, Un patrimoine et des images en débat": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** Queen Anacaona, the Taïno leader, did not stay in the past. She lives on in novels and comics, in museum rooms and arguments — and the Taïno past itself is fought over today: who may tell it, who may display its objects, whose heritage is it? The pupils learn that heritage — patrimoine — is not just what survives. It is what the living choose to keep and show, and every choice can be debated. The images of the past are a battleground of the present.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Heritage is the past a society chooses to keep and argue over — Queen Anacaona and the Taïno past fought over in novels, comics and museums, and slavery remembered through statues, commemorations and laws: whose heritage it is, and who gets to show it, is itself a historical question.
