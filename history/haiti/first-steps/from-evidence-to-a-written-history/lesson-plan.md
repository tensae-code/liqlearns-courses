# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Haiti · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Historian's Craft and the First Peoples (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a written history is built, not found: documents are hunted, gathered and sorted into a corpus, then re-read and argued over until they can carry an interpretation the historian presents in public.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Historian's Craft and the First Peoples — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Faire de l'histoire au quotidien (2): research, collection and use of a document corpus** — Pupils hunt, gather and sort documents until the pile of traces starts answering their question.
2. **Faire de l'histoire au quotidien (3): interpreting a social reality from a corpus and communicating results** — The same documents are re-read, compared and argued over until pupils can present an interpretation in public.
3. **Séance 1, Les noms de l'histoire** — Pupils map archaeological sites and the far-off museums holding Taïno objects, and ask why even the people's name is a word given by others.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a written history is built, not found: documents are hunted, gathered and sorted into a corpus, then re-read and argued over until they can carry an interpretation the historian presents in public.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the Haiti they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Haiti and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks one document alone proves the past → the books build a corpus — a gathered, sorted set of documents — and the interpretation comes from comparing them, not from any single page.
- Go back to the one thing: a written history is built, not found: documents are hunted, gathered and sorted into a corpus, then re-read and argued over until they can carry an interpretation the historian presents in public. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
