# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Gather everything in your home that tells about one past event — a photograph, a letter, a receipt, an old newspaper. Spread them out. Which ones answer your question, and which are noise? This lesson is about building that pile into a history.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a written history is built, not found: documents are hunted, gathered and sorted into a corpus, then re-read and argued over until they can carry an interpretation the historian presents in public. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
