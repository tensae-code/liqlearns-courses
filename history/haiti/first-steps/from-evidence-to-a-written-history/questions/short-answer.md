# From Evidence to a Written History — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Faire de l'histoire au quotidien (2): research, collection and use of a document corpus": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** With a question in hand, the pupils become hunters. They search out documents — writings, images, objects, recordings — wherever traces of the past survive, and they collect them. But a heap is not yet history: the traces are sorted, dated, and set beside each other until they form a corpus, a body of evidence built to answer the one question. Slowly the pile starts to speak: one document confirms another, a third contradicts, and the shape of an answer appears.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** A written history is built, not found: documents are hunted, gathered and sorted into a corpus, then re-read and argued over until they can carry an interpretation the historian presents in public.
