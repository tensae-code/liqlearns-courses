# Hands-on — Haiti's Independence Debt: Neocolonialism by Debt

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine winning your freedom — then receiving a bill for it, payable to the people who enslaved you, with warships waiting offshore. This lesson follows that bill for two hundred years.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: France sold recognition at a price: the 1825 ordinance turned independence itself into a bill — an indemnity to former slave-owners that became a 'double debt' as Haiti borrowed from French banks to pay it, draining the treasury for decades; in 2003 President Aristide demanded the money back, and the argument is alive today. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
