# Lesson plan — Haiti's Independence Debt: Neocolonialism by Debt

**Course:** History — Haiti · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Haiti's Independence Debt: Neocolonialism by Debt
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Building the State, the Debt and the Occupation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** France sold recognition at a price: the 1825 ordinance turned independence itself into a bill — an indemnity to former slave-owners that became a 'double debt' as Haiti borrowed from French banks to pay it, draining the treasury for decades; in 2003 President Aristide demanded the money back, and the argument is alive today.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Building the State, the Debt and the Occupation — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Séance 7, Reconnaissance internationale et dette (developed in the companion debt sequence)** — France offers recognition at a price — the 1825 ordinance turns independence itself into a bill Haiti must pay.
2. **Séance 1, La dette et le développement d'Haïti, un débat contemporain ?** — In 2003 President Aristide demands France give the money back — pupils open the file and ask what a 19th-century debt did to Haiti's development.
3. **Séance 2, La dette de l'indépendance, négociation et imposition** — French warships anchor off Port-au-Prince in 1825; the ordinance offers recognition in exchange for a huge indemnity to former slave-owners, softened only by the 1838 treaties.
4. **Séance 3, De la dette à « la double dette » (1)** — To pay the first instalments Haiti must borrow from French banks — now it owes the indemnity and the loans taken to pay it.
5. **Séance 4, De la dette à « la double dette » (2)** — Decades of payments and fresh loans drain the treasury — pupils follow the money and judge the book's charge: neocolonialism by debt.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the debt was an ordinary loan Haiti chose → the book shows an imposition: recognition — the right to exist among nations — was sold under threat of warships, and the indemnity forced Haiti to borrow from French banks to pay France: the double debt.
