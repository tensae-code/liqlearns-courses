# Haiti's Independence Debt: Neocolonialism by Debt — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 7, Reconnaissance internationale et dette (developed in the companion debt sequence)": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The state-building book points forward to its companion: Haiti, unrecognised and blockaded by the world's silence, is offered recognition by France — at a price. The ordinance of 1825 turns independence itself into a bill: France will acknowledge the state its former slaves founded, if Haiti pays an enormous indemnity to the former slave-owners for their 'lost property' — the land, and the people themselves. The debt sequence takes the story from here, as an investigation the pupils run themselves.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** France sold recognition at a price: the 1825 ordinance turned independence itself into a bill — an indemnity to former slave-owners that became a 'double debt' as Haiti borrowed from French banks to pay it, draining the treasury for decades; in 2003 President Aristide demanded the money back, and the argument is alive today.
