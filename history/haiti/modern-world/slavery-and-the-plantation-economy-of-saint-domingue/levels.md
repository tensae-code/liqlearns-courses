# Slavery and the Plantation Economy of Saint-Domingue — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Séance 1, De la conquête et de la colonisation européennes à l'économie de plantation
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Europeans seize the island, clear it and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by Africans — a chronology told through local remains — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Séance 2, Archéologie d'une domination
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pupils dig the plantation's traces — mill, boiling house, quarters — and read domination itself out of stones and layouts — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Séance 3, Travail servile et conditions de vie des esclaves dans les plantations, le Code noir (1)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Through engravings and texts, the enslaved day is rebuilt — buying, work, food, punishment — until the system's violence is unmistakable — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Séance 5, La parole des esclaves, leur image dans la réalité et dans les textes et les images
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The enslaved appear mostly through masters' pens and pictures — pupils hunt for their real voice and image behind the distortions — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Séance 7, L'impact de l'histoire et de la mémoire des esclaves et des esclavages dans la littérature
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Novels from Émeric Bergeaud's Stella to Madison Smartt Bell's trilogy retell Saint-Domingue — literature becomes a second archive of slavery — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
