# Slavery and the Plantation Economy of Saint-Domingue — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 1, De la conquête et de la colonisation européennes à l'économie de plantation": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The story begins with seizure. Europeans took the island, cleared its land, and rebuilt it for one purpose: sugar and coffee for Europe, produced on great estates — plantations — worked by Africans brought in chains. The book tells this chronology through local remains: the island the pupils live on still carries the traces of the rebuilding. A colony was turned into a machine for sweetness and profit, and the machine ran on enslaved labour.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Europeans seized the island and rebuilt it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans — a system read from the ground up: its archaeology, the enslaved day rebuilt from engravings and texts, the enslaved people's own voice hunted behind masters' distortions, and its story retold by literature, with the book itself warning that 'plantation' may really mean a slave-labour camp.
