# Lesson plan — Slavery and the Plantation Economy of Saint-Domingue

**Course:** History — Haiti · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Slavery and the Plantation Economy of Saint-Domingue
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Plantation, Resistance and Revolution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Europeans seized the island and rebuilt it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans — a system read from the ground up: its archaeology, the enslaved day rebuilt from engravings and texts, the enslaved people's own voice hunted behind masters' distortions, and its story retold by literature, with the book itself warning that 'plantation' may really mean a slave-labour camp.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Plantation, Resistance and Revolution — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Séance 1, De la conquête et de la colonisation européennes à l'économie de plantation** — Europeans seize the island, clear it and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by Africans — a chronology told through local remains.
2. **Séance 2, Archéologie d'une domination** — Pupils dig the plantation's traces — mill, boiling house, quarters — and read domination itself out of stones and layouts.
3. **Séance 3, Travail servile et conditions de vie des esclaves dans les plantations, le Code noir (1)** — Through engravings and texts, the enslaved day is rebuilt — buying, work, food, punishment — until the system's violence is unmistakable.
4. **Séance 5, La parole des esclaves, leur image dans la réalité et dans les textes et les images** — The enslaved appear mostly through masters' pens and pictures — pupils hunt for their real voice and image behind the distortions.
5. **Séance 7, L'impact de l'histoire et de la mémoire des esclaves et des esclavages dans la littérature** — Novels from Émeric Bergeaud's Stella to Madison Smartt Bell's trilogy retell Saint-Domingue — literature becomes a second archive of slavery.
