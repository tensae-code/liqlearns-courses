# Hands-on — Slavery and the Plantation Economy of Saint-Domingue

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Walk (in imagination) across a sugar estate: mill, boiling house, rows of quarters. Who built each part, who worked in it, who profited? This lesson reads the plantation from the ground up.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Europeans seized the island and rebuilt it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans — a system read from the ground up: its archaeology, the enslaved day rebuilt from engravings and texts, the enslaved people's own voice hunted behind masters' distortions, and its story retold by literature, with the book itself warning that 'plantation' may really mean a slave-labour camp. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
