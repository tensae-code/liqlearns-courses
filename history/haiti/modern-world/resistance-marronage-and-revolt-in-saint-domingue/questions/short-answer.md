# Resistance, Marronage and Revolt in Saint-Domingue — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 1, Résister et marronner": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The book begins at a statue: Albert Mangonès' Unknown Maroon in Port-au-Prince, the runaway blowing his conch, chain broken. From that figure the pupils trace resistance in its everyday forms — work slowed, tools broken, orders misunderstood on purpose — and then its boldest form: marronage, flight. The maroon who ran did not vanish; in the hills the runaways built a whole hidden counter-society, with its own settlements, leaders and laws — freedom rehearsed inside the slave colony.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Resistance never stopped: everyday defiance and flight built maroon counter-societies, named risings — Padrejean (1679), Quartier-Morin 1697, Maribaroux 1720, Makandal's poison network — shook the colony, and banned Vodou gatherings persisted as a meeting-place of resistance, recovered name by name because the enslaved left almost no writings.
