# Hands-on — Resistance, Marronage and Revolt in Saint-Domingue

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand before a statue in your mind: a man blowing a conch shell, a broken chain at his neck — the Unknown Maroon. Why would a country raise a statue to someone whose name it does not know? This lesson answers.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: resistance never stopped: everyday defiance and flight built maroon counter-societies, named risings — Padrejean (1679), Quartier-Morin 1697, Maribaroux 1720, Makandal's poison network — shook the colony, and banned Vodou gatherings persisted as a meeting-place of resistance, recovered name by name because the enslaved left almost no writings. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
