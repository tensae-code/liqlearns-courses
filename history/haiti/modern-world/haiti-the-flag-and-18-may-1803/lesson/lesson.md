# Haiti: The Flag and 18 May 1803

## Do it first

Take a flag you know and look at its colours one by one. Who chose them, and what story do they tell? This lesson tells how Haiti's flag was made — by tearing one flag apart and sewing a new one.

In May 1803, while the independence war still raged, the leaders of the coming Haiti met at Arcahaie. There, the story says, they took the French flag — blue, white and red — and tore the white band out of it. The blue and the red that remained were sewn together: the colours of the Black and mixed-race fighters, joined in one flag for one new people. The date of that making, 18 May, never left the calendar: it became Flag Day, and the story of the torn flag is retold every year — a commemoration date that is itself a piece of history, studied in the very first book.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: at Arcahaie in May 1803 the independence leaders tore the white from the French flag and sewed a new one; the date, 18 May, became Flag Day, retold every year. Give one real example from your own home, school or town, and name the lesson's key words: Arcahaie, Flag Day. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
