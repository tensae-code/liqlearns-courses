# The French Revolution Breaks Out (1789) — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 1, La société coloniale et la Révolution française": who, where, and what turned?
**Answer:** The Revolution book opens with the colony as it stood: Saint-Domingue, France's richest possession, a society ranked by race and wealth — great white planters at the top, small whites below them, free people of colour wealthy yet barred, and at the base the enslaved Africans who made the wealth. Into this ranked world sails the news from Paris: the Revolution has proclaimed the rights of man — men are born free and equal in rights. Every group in the colony reaches for the words for itself. The planters hear freedom from Paris's taxes; the free people of colour hear their own equality; the enslaved hear the whole sentence. The colony cracks on the meaning of one proclamation.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
**Answer:** Rich, racially ranked Saint-Domingue hears that Paris has proclaimed the rights of man — and every group in the colony reaches for the words for itself.
