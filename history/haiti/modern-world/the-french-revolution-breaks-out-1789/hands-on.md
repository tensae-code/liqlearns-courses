# Hands-on — The French Revolution Breaks Out (1789)

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A message arrives from far away: 'All men are born free and equal in rights.' Read it aloud in a colony built on slavery. Who in the room hears hope, and who hears danger? This lesson sets that scene.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: rich, racially ranked Saint-Domingue hears that Paris has proclaimed the rights of man — and every group in the colony reaches for the words for itself. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
