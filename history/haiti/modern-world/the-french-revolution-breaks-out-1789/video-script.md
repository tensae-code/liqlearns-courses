# Video script — The French Revolution Breaks Out (1789)

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Rich, racially ranked Saint-Domingue hears that Paris has proclaimed the rights of man — and every group in the colony reaches for the words for itself.

The Revolution book opens with the colony as it stood: Saint-Domingue, France's richest possession, a society ranked by race and wealth — great white planters at the top, small whites below them, free people of colour wealthy yet barred, and at the base the enslaved Africans who made the wealth. Into this ranked world sails the news from Paris: the Revolution has proclaimed the rights of man — men are born free and equal in rights. Every group in the colony reaches for the words for itself. The planters hear freedom from Paris's taxes; the free people of colour hear their own equality; the enslaved hear the whole sentence. The colony cracks on the meaning of one proclamation.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
