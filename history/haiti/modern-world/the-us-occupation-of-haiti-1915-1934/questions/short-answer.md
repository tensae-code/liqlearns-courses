# The US Occupation of Haiti (1915–1934) — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 1, Le contexte externe et interne ou les préludes à l'occupation": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The occupation book opens on a country in crisis. Presidents fall one after another; foreign debts press in; the powers watch Haiti's ports and customs. On 28 July 1915 President Vilbrun Guillaume Sam, having ordered a massacre of political prisoners, is dragged from his refuge and assassinated by a mob. The same day, United States Marines land in Port-au-Prince. The prelude matters to the book's judgment: the occupation was prepared by Haiti's internal collapse and by Washington's long-standing designs — and it begins, from its first day, as foreign rule over a sovereign state.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Nineteen years of occupation judged from Haitian newspapers, memoirs and resistance writing: the Marines land the day President Sam is assassinated in 1915, an imposed convention takes customs, finances and a US-officered gendarmerie, roads are built with forced corvée labour, the Cacos resist under Charlemagne Péralte, collaboration shifts with the years — and the pupils weigh the bilan in 1934.
