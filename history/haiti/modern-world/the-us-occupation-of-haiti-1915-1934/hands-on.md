# Hands-on — The US Occupation of Haiti (1915–1934)

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A foreign army lands in your capital and stays nineteen years. It builds roads — with forced labour. It trains an army — that outlasts it. This lesson weighs that occupation, from Haitian voices.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: nineteen years of occupation judged from Haitian newspapers, memoirs and resistance writing: the Marines land the day President Sam is assassinated in 1915, an imposed convention takes customs, finances and a US-officered gendarmerie, roads are built with forced corvée labour, the Cacos resist under Charlemagne Péralte, collaboration shifts with the years — and the pupils weigh the bilan in 1934. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
