# The US Occupation of Haiti (1915–1934) — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Séance 1, Le contexte externe et interne ou les préludes à l'occupation
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Presidents fall one after another, debts press in, and President Vilbrun Guillaume Sam is assassinated on 28 July 1915 — US Marines land the same day — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Séance 2, La mise sous tutelle de l'État haïtien ?
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: A imposed convention hands Washington Haiti's customs, finances and a US-officered gendarmerie — sovereignty survives on paper only — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Séance 3, L'occupation et le processus d'institutionnalisation
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The occupation builds roads with forced corvée labour and a centralised armed force — the machinery pupils are asked to link to Haiti's later authoritarian model — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Séance 4, Les résistances à l'occupation
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Caco guerrillas under leaders like Charlemagne Péralte fight from the hills while writers and nationalists resist in print — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Séance 5, La collaboration avec l'occupant, des relations complexes et changeantes
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Through the era's newspapers, pupils watch elites who served, hedged or turned against the occupier as the years changed the bargain — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Séance 6, Vers un bilan de l'occupation américaine ?
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Marines leave in 1934; pupils weigh roads and sanitation against humiliation, deaths and a militarised state, and deliver a verdict — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
