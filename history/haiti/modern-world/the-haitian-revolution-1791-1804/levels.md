# The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Séance 2, Les premières insurrections
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Free people of colour demand their rights and the enslaved begin to rise — the colony cracks before the great August night — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Séance 3, L'insurrection de l'été 1791: la cérémonie du Bois Caïman, entre l'histoire et le mythe
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: At Bois Caïman a Vodou ceremony seals an oath — days later the northern plain burns; pupils separate the documented rising from the founding legend — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Séance 4, Les insurrections 1791-1793 (1)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Insurgent bands under leaders like Jean-François, Biassou and the young Toussaint hold the hills while empires — French, Spanish, British — bid for them — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Séance 5, Les insurrections 1791-1793 (2)
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: War spreads across the colony; allegiances flip as Spain arms the insurgents and France's commissioners promise freedom to those who fight for the Republic — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Séance 6, L'émancipation républicaine (1793-1798)
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Sonthonax abolishes slavery in Saint-Domingue in 1793 and Paris extends it in 1794 — the formerly enslaved become citizens and soldiers of the Republic — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Séance 7, Le pouvoir louverturien (1)
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Toussaint Louverture out-generals the British and his rivals, takes the Spanish side of the island, and rules Saint-Domingue in France's name but by his own hand — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Séance 8, Le pouvoir louverturien (2): la Constitution de 1801
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Louverture's 1801 constitution makes Saint-Domingue an autonomous state with himself governor for life — Napoleon reads it as rebellion — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Séance 9, Les luttes pour l'indépendance
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Leclerc's expedition seizes Louverture, but the indigenous army under Dessalines wins at Vertières; on 1 January 1804 Haiti declares independence, and Dessalines is assassinated in 1806 — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Annex document work: biographies of Louverture and Dessalines; the 1804 Declaration of Independence and the 1805 Constitution
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pupils build illustrated biographies and mine the founding texts for who counted as a citizen in the new Black state — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
