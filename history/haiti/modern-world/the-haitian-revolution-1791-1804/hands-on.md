# Hands-on — The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804)

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line and mark 1791 at one end and 1804 at the other. In thirteen years an enslaved people defeats three empires and founds a state. This lesson walks the line, act by act.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the only slave revolution that founded a state: from the first insurrections and Bois Caïman in August 1791, through Sonthonax's abolition of 1793 and Paris's of 1794, Louverture's rise and his 1801 constitution, to Dessalines' victory at Vertières and independence on 1 January 1804 — told in the book's four acts, with the founding texts mined for who counted as a citizen. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
