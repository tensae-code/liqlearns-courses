# Lesson plan — The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804)

**Course:** History — Haiti · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804)
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Plantation, Resistance and Revolution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the only slave revolution that founded a state: from the first insurrections and Bois Caïman in August 1791, through Sonthonax's abolition of 1793 and Paris's of 1794, Louverture's rise and his 1801 constitution, to Dessalines' victory at Vertières and independence on 1 January 1804 — told in the book's four acts, with the founding texts mined for who counted as a citizen.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Séance 2, Les premières insurrections** — Free people of colour demand their rights and the enslaved begin to rise — the colony cracks before the great August night.
2. **Séance 3, L'insurrection de l'été 1791: la cérémonie du Bois Caïman, entre l'histoire et le mythe** — At Bois Caïman a Vodou ceremony seals an oath — days later the northern plain burns; pupils separate the documented rising from the founding legend.
3. **Séance 4, Les insurrections 1791-1793 (1)** — Insurgent bands under leaders like Jean-François, Biassou and the young Toussaint hold the hills while empires — French, Spanish, British — bid for them.
4. **Séance 5, Les insurrections 1791-1793 (2)** — War spreads across the colony; allegiances flip as Spain arms the insurgents and France's commissioners promise freedom to those who fight for the Republic.
5. **Séance 6, L'émancipation républicaine (1793-1798)** — Sonthonax abolishes slavery in Saint-Domingue in 1793 and Paris extends it in 1794 — the formerly enslaved become citizens and soldiers of the Republic.
6. **Séance 7, Le pouvoir louverturien (1)** — Toussaint Louverture out-generals the British and his rivals, takes the Spanish side of the island, and rules Saint-Domingue in France's name but by his own hand.
7. **Séance 8, Le pouvoir louverturien (2): la Constitution de 1801** — Louverture's 1801 constitution makes Saint-Domingue an autonomous state with himself governor for life — Napoleon reads it as rebellion.
8. **Séance 9, Les luttes pour l'indépendance** — Leclerc's expedition seizes Louverture, but the indigenous army under Dessalines wins at Vertières; on 1 January 1804 Haiti declares independence, and Dessalines is assassinated in 1806.
9. **Annex document work: biographies of Louverture and Dessalines; the 1804 Declaration of Independence and the 1805 Constitution** — Pupils build illustrated biographies and mine the founding texts for who counted as a citizen in the new Black state.
