# The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 2, Les premières insurrections": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The revolution begins before its famous night. The free people of colour, wealthy and barred, demand the rights Paris proclaimed — and are answered with violence. At the same time the enslaved begin to rise in scattered revolts across the estates. The colony's ranked order, already cracked by the words from Paris, starts to break in fact: two different revolutions, of the free coloured and of the enslaved, are moving toward each other as the summer of 1791 comes on.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** The only slave revolution that founded a state: from the first insurrections and Bois Caïman in August 1791, through Sonthonax's abolition of 1793 and Paris's of 1794, Louverture's rise and his 1801 constitution, to Dessalines' victory at Vertières and independence on 1 January 1804 — told in the book's four acts, with the founding texts mined for who counted as a citizen.
