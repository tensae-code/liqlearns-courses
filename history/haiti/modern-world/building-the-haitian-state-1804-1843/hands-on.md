# Hands-on — Building the Haitian State (1804–1843)

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two architects, one ruined house, opposite plans: one builds a palace of discipline, one gives the land away. This lesson watches a new state argue over what it should be — constitution by constitution.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: state-building read constitution by constitution: Dessalines' empire and assassination, the 1806 split into Christophe's northern kingdom and Pétion's southern republic, Boyer's reunification of 1820 and the whole island in 1822, and the Rural Code of 1826 hardening the state into authoritarian rule. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
