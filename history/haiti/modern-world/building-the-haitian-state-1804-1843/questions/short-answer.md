# Building the Haitian State (1804–1843) — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Séance 1, L'État, la société et la déclaration d'indépendance (1er janvier 1804)": who, where, and what turned? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** On the ruins of Saint-Domingue, the leaders of the new people — Black and mixed-race officers of the indigenous army — proclaim a free state that no one in 1804 thought possible: a state founded by the formerly enslaved, on land soaked by the plantation. The Declaration of 1 January 1804 announces it to the world. But proclamation is the easy part: at once the founders argue over what the state should be — who holds the land, who commands, what freedom means for the people who work it. The argument will run for forty years.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** State-building read constitution by constitution: Dessalines' empire and assassination, the 1806 split into Christophe's northern kingdom and Pétion's southern republic, Boyer's reunification of 1820 and the whole island in 1822, and the Rural Code of 1826 hardening the state into authoritarian rule.
