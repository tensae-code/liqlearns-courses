# Video script — The Code Noir: The Law That Ruled Slavery

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The Code Noir's articles, read line by line, made the law that called people property — and the gap between its paper protections and plantation reality is the lesson.

The class opens the law itself: the Code Noir, the royal code whose articles ruled slavery in France's colonies. Article by article, the pupils read what it did — it defined the enslaved person as property, movable goods to be bought, sold and inherited, and it organised the master's power in legal French. The code also carried a few paper protections — food, rest, limits written on the page. Then the lesson sets the page against the plantation the pupils have already rebuilt: between the code's paper and the estate's reality yawned a gap in which real lives were spent. Law, they learn, can be an instrument of oppression wearing the dress of order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
