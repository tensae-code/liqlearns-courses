# Hands-on — The Code Noir: The Law That Ruled Slavery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Read any rule that governs your school day. Who wrote it, whom does it protect, whom does it bind? This lesson reads a law book that did the opposite of protecting: the code that ruled slavery.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the Code Noir's articles, read line by line, made the law that called people property — and the gap between its paper protections and plantation reality is the lesson. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
