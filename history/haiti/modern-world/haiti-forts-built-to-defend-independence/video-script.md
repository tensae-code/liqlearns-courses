# Video script — Haiti's Forts Built to Defend Independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
A free Black state, fearing the slave powers would return, ringed itself with forts — Fort Jacques, the Citadelle, Fort Décidé — whose faces still stare out from Haiti's banknotes.

In 1804 Haiti did something the world of slave powers thought impossible: enslaved people had defeated their masters and founded a free Black state. But freedom had to be guarded. The new state's leaders feared the slave powers would return by sea to take it all back, so Haiti ringed itself with forts — Fort Jacques above the capital, the great Citadelle in the north, Fort Décidé and others watching the coasts and roads. The fear passed; the stones stayed. Today the forts still stand on their hills, and their faces stare out from Haiti's banknotes — independence, defended in stone, carried in every pocket.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in your town.
