# Haiti's Forts Built to Defend Independence

## Do it first

Imagine your town has just won its freedom and the old masters might come back by sea. Where would you build, and what? This lesson looks at the forts a free Haiti built to guard its independence.

In 1804 Haiti did something the world of slave powers thought impossible: enslaved people had defeated their masters and founded a free Black state. But freedom had to be guarded. The new state's leaders feared the slave powers would return by sea to take it all back, so Haiti ringed itself with forts — Fort Jacques above the capital, the great Citadelle in the north, Fort Décidé and others watching the coasts and roads. The fear passed; the stones stayed. Today the forts still stand on their hills, and their faces stare out from Haiti's banknotes — independence, defended in stone, carried in every pocket.
