# Haiti's Forts Built to Defend Independence — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of the part "Study object: Fort Jacques and Haiti's defensive forts": who, where, and what turned?
**Answer:** In 1804 Haiti did something the world of slave powers thought impossible: enslaved people had defeated their masters and founded a free Black state. But freedom had to be guarded. The new state's leaders feared the slave powers would return by sea to take it all back, so Haiti ringed itself with forts — Fort Jacques above the capital, the great Citadelle in the north, Fort Décidé and others watching the coasts and roads. The fear passed; the stones stayed. Today the forts still stand on their hills, and their faces stare out from Haiti's banknotes — independence, defended in stone, carried in every pocket.

**2.** In your own words, what is the one thing this lesson teaches?
**Answer:** A free Black state, fearing the slave powers would return, ringed itself with forts — Fort Jacques, the Citadelle, Fort Décidé — whose faces still stare out from Haiti's banknotes.
