# History — Haiti course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Haiti inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Haiti's books in hand are nine —
the Projet Nectar / MENFP History teacher-guide sequences
for the 7e, 8e and 9e années (Grades 7, 8 and 9), all
COMPLETE as filed — and their own progression is the ladder.
The Grade 7 sequences teach the historian's craft first,
through a local inquiry (one building, one everyday object,
one commemoration date), then the island's first peoples —
prehistory as live science, the Antilles' fisher-gatherers
and first farmers, and the Taïno of Quisqueya — with the
young state's own symbols studied as objects: its defensive
forts and its flag of 18 May 1803 (Level 1). The Grade 8
sequences run the colonial story: the plantation economy of
Saint-Domingue and the Code Noir, a century of resistance,
marronage and revolt, and the Haitian Revolution of
1791–1804 in four acts (Level 2). The Grade 9 sequences
build the state that revolution founded — constitution by
constitution to 1843 — then judge its two great burdens: the
independence debt of 1825, and the US occupation of
1915–1934 (Level 3). Every lesson stands on teacher-guide
evidence only — the acceptance reckoning in ABOUT.md says
so plainly. Haiti programme documents do not count as
textbooks (settled ruling): the record set's eight
curriculum programmes are filed record-only and feed no
lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Projet Nectar / MENFP History sequences — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the contents map, and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **The Historian's Craft and the First Peoples** — the
   craft first, worked four times over on one local inquiry
   (what history is; from evidence to a written history;
   finding history in your own locality; heritage as what a
   society chooses to keep and argue over), then the deep
   beginning (who studies prehistory; Africa, cradle of
   humankind; from stone tools to farming; prehistoric art;
   the Caribbean's fisher-gatherers and first farmers; the
   Taïno of Quisqueya), and the young state's symbols as
   study objects (the forts built to defend independence;
   the flag and 18 May 1803).
   13 lessons, in four titles: `first-steps/` (6 lessons),
   `africa-to-1500/` (2 lessons), `ancient-world/`
   (3 lessons), `modern-world/` (2 lessons).

2. **Plantation, Resistance and Revolution** — Saint-Domingue
   rebuilt as sugar and coffee plantations (the system read
   from its archaeology, the enslaved day, the enslaved
   voice, and its literature); the Code Noir read line by
   line; a century of resistance — marronage, named risings,
   Vodou under ban; the French Revolution's words landing in
   the ranked colony; and the Haitian Revolution in four
   acts, from Bois Caïman to Vertières and 1 January 1804.
   5 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (5 lessons).

3. **Building the State, the Debt and the Occupation** —
   the state built constitution by constitution (Dessalines'
   empire, Christophe's kingdom against Pétion's republic,
   Boyer's reunification, the Rural Code of 1826); the
   independence debt — the 1825 ordinance, the double debt,
   and the restitution argument of 2003; and the US
   occupation of 1915–1934, judged from Haitian voices to a
   weighed bilan.
   3 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (3 lessons).

**Totals: 21 lessons** (Level 1: 13 · Level 2: 5 · Level 3:
3 — 58 placements, all from nine teacher guides, evidence
only, acceptance 0).
