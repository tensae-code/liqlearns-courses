# Colonisation Direct and Indirect Rule

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Colonisation Direct and Indirect Rule". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 2, Unit 2 "Types And Nature Of Colonial Rule". As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

rule varies by power and island — America takes Pago Pago (1878) and annexes Tutuila and Manu'a (1899) for a naval base; Germany's Solf governs Sāmoa through indentured New Guinea and Chinese labour; Britain shields Fijian chiefs and land while importing 61,000 Indians, yet strips Solomon Islanders' coastal land; France alienates some 90% of New Caledonia and crushes the 1878 and 1917 uprisings.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
