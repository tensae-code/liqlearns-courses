# Why Europe Colonised Motives and the Civilizing Mission

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Why Europe Colonised Motives and the Civilizing Mission". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 2, Unit 1 "Motives Of Imperial Powers"; Part 2, Unit 3, Asia. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Part 2, Unit 1 "Motives Of Imperial Powers"

the Industrial Revolution's hunger for cheap raw materials, empire rivalry measured in colonies, the China tea trade's demand for sandalwood and bêche-de-mer, and "Christianity before the flag" carry Britain, Germany, France and America into the Pacific.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Part 2, Unit 3, Asia

in Asia the motives are the familiar ones — trade, routes, empire — but the push comes from men on the spot: British India annexes Burma in 1852 as a buffer against French Indochina, and Penang, Singapore and Malacca are taken in 1786–1824 to guard India and feed the China trade.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
