# The Atlantic Slave Trade Scale Coasts and the Gun Cycle

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "The Atlantic Slave Trade Scale Coasts and the Gun Cycle". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Unit 1, Fact Sheet One "Slaves and slavery in America". As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

from 1619 to 1803 thousands of Africans are shipped across the Atlantic and sold at markets like animals to southern plantation owners hungry for unpaid labour — between 400,000 and a million people; Virginia's law makes slave women's children slaves too, so slavery is inherited as well as captured.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: from 1619 to 1803 thousands of Africans are shipped across the Atlantic and sold at markets like animals to southern plantation owners hungry for unpaid labour — between 400,000 and a million people; Virginia's law makes slave women's children slaves too, so slavery is inherited as well as captured. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
