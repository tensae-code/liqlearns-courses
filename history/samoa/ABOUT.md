# About the History — Samoa course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — Samoa's own Social
Studies books for Years 9–11 and its History books for Years
12–13. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box
is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Samoa inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade a book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **five levels**, grouped from the
books' own progression (the placements file's book order is
the teaching order — earliest books first):

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging
   Pacific.** The Year 9 Social Studies books: heritage as
   inheritance (practices, symbols, siapo, mea sina, museums),
   finding history in your own locality, sorting evidence and
   dating the past — and the deep story: Polynesian voyaging,
   Tonga's settlement and kings, Mulinu'u, and the Mau first
   met at Vaimoso. 12 lessons, 42 placements stocked here.
2. **Level 2 — Samoa's Road to Independence, Rights and the
   Industrial World.** The Year 10 books: German rule to
   independence in 1962 and universal suffrage in 1991; the
   rights case studies (Copernicus and Bruno, America 1776,
   Ken Saro-Wiwa, Irina Ratushinskaya, the road to the
   Universal Declaration, the Mau and Black Saturday);
   identity in Samoa and Australia; the Industrial
   Revolution. 20 lessons, 29 placements stocked here.
3. **Level 3 — The Mau from Inside, Civil Rights and the
   Pacific Neighbourhood.** The Year 11 books: America's
   slave trade, Civil War, Jim Crow, NAACP, Brown v. Board,
   freedom riders, sit-ins and the 1963 March beside Samoa's
   Mau in Telesia's diary and photo album, closing with New
   Zealand's 2002 apology; Fiji's settlement, Cakobau and
   Indian indenture; copra, OPEC, the Peace Corps, the Forum
   and the USP. 18 lessons, 19 placements stocked here.
4. **Level 4 — Samoa Whole: Origins, Contact, Colony and
   Independence — and the World Beyond.** *History, Year 12*
   whole: origins weighed, inter-island voyages, contact,
   missions, indentured labour, civil wars, Palestine,
   treaties to partition, the road to Independence Day 1962,
   economies, the Depression, Japan, Russia and China.
   34 lessons, 40 placements stocked here.
5. **Level 5 — The World Whole: Germany, India, Vietnam, the
   Cold War and the Men of Power.** *History, Year 13* whole:
   Germany's migrations and the Holocaust, the Balkans,
   Yugoslavia, Hungary 1956, the Soviet bloc's collapse,
   Vietnam, British India and Gandhi, the alliances to the
   United Nations, the Cold War, Stalin. 38 lessons,
   39 placements stocked here.

A lesson sits at the level of the first book that teaches it;
later books' passes of the same lesson stock into it at that
level (so, for example, the heritage lesson carries parts
from five different books). **Totals: 122 lessons,
169 placements, all on student books.**

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Heritage first, then the nation's story, then the world:*
  "First, what the ancestors hand down — and how you find the
  past in your own village. Then Samoa's own road: colony,
  the Mau, Black Saturday, independence. Then the world
  Samoa's books set beside it — America's civil rights,
  Australia, Fiji, and at the top the senior History frame:
  Samoa whole, and the world whole."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Primary (the record-only curriculum statement)** — Primary
Social Studies is organised by strands and achievement
objectives rather than by dated history topics; the past
enters through heritage, culture and place. *(Curriculum
document, record-only — no placements.)*

**Year 9 Book One** — a combined Social Studies book built on
Pacific neighbours: Samoa's resources and village order are
taught beside the Solomon Islands and Tonga, and the past is
reached by comparing past and present practice and by
interviewing elders, not by a dated narrative. *(Student
book.)*

**Year 9 Book Two** — history is taught through PLACES — a
peninsula, a church, a protest office — read from
photographs, maps and oral sources; Samoa's Mau story and
the ancient Polynesian voyages carry the national narrative
inside a geography-shaped book. *(Student book.)*

**Year 9 Book Three** — the Culture and Heritage unit
teaches the past entirely as inheritance — what ancestors
passed on in practices, symbols and treasures (mea sina) —
using paired past/present photographs, museum interviews and
elders as primary sources. *(Student book.)*

**Year 10 Book One** — Samoa's political history is the
spine of a Civics book: German rule, the 1914 takeover,
mandate and trusteeship are taught first, and world
human-rights history is anchored back to Samoa's own Mau
and influenza story on a two-page timeline (printed
pp. 46–47). *(Student book.)*

**Year 10 Book Two** — identity is the frame: national
identity is explicitly said to be made over time by
historical events and kept alive by retold stories and oral
histories; Australia is the comparison case, studied through
its migration timeline and its reckoning with the Stolen
Generation. *(Student book.)*

**Year 10 Book Three** — world history enters as the
Industrial Revolution, taught through British work before
and after, child workers' own stories, and a comparison back
to Samoa's past and present production; Samoa's own recent
past appears as the Falealupo rainforest covenant story.
*(Student book.)*

**Year 11 Book One** — Samoa's Mau is taught twice over:
once as world-style civil-rights history beside Black
America's movement (fact sheets, timelines, a mock
newspaper), and once from inside, through a Samoan mother's
diary and photo album; the book closes the story with New
Zealand's 2002 apology, read through Samoan newspaper
reactions. *(Student book.)*

**Year 11 Book Two** — neighbouring Fiji gets a compact
settlement history (legend, contact, indenture), and Samoa's
own past is carried by the same Telesia — now as a
four-generation family "history highway" of travel to New
Zealand, told entirely from family stories. *(Student
book.)*

**History, Year 12** — a full senior History course in six
parts — Migration; Imperialism, Colonialism, Nationalism
and Decolonisation; Conflict; International Relations;
Economic Transformations; Systems of Power and Authority —
with Samoa's own story (origins, civil wars, Mau, treaties,
independence) as the home case against Palestine/Israel,
Japan, Russia and China. *(Student book.)*

**History, Year 13** — the same six-part frame as Year 12,
but the case studies go worldwide — Germany for migration,
the Balkans, Soviet satellites and Vietnam for
decolonisation, India for conflict, Stalin and Gandhi for
power — with Samoa drawn in as the comparison: the matai
system as a system of power, New Zealand's administration
of Samoa set beside Britain's India, and Samoa as the first
Pacific nation to regain independence as India was the
first British colony to do so. *(Student book.)*
