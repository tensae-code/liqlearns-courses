# History — Samoa course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Samoa inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Samoa's own books are the ladder.
The placements file's book order is the teaching order —
earliest books first. Samoa teaches Social Studies before it
teaches History: the Year 9 books begin with heritage, place
and the voyaging Pacific; the Year 10 books make Samoa's
political story the spine and hang the world's rights story
and the Industrial Revolution on it; the Year 11 books teach
the Mau twice — beside America's civil-rights movement and
from inside a Samoan mother's diary — and fill in the Pacific
neighbourhood; and the two senior books, *History* Year 12 and
Year 13, run one six-part frame (Migration; Imperialism,
Colonialism, Nationalism and Decolonisation; Conflict;
International Relations; Economic Transformations; Systems of
Power and Authority) — Year 12 with Samoa as the home case,
Year 13 with the cases gone worldwide. The five levels below
group that progression exactly as the books present it. A
lesson sits at the level of the first book that teaches it;
later books' passes of the same lesson stock into it there.
The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Samoa's own
Social Studies and History books — except the **Tip boxes**:
a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips
are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the
full book list, the combined-book contents maps, and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific** — history begins the Samoan way — heritage first: what the ancestors pass on (language, siapo, fine mats, weddings, museums), how to find history in your own locality (elders interviewed, a church's past researched, resources counted as they were fifty years ago), how evidence is sorted and the past dated — and the deep story those first steps open: the Polynesian voyages that settled the Pacific, Tonga's settlement and kings, Samoa's own gathering ground at Mulinu'u, and the Mau first met at its Vaimoso office. 12 lessons, in titles: `first-steps/` (4 lessons), `ancient-world/` (3 lessons), `modern-world/` (5 lessons). 42 placements.
2. **Level 2 — Samoa's Road to Independence, Rights and the Industrial World** — Samoa's political story told whole — German rule, the unresisted 1914 landing, mandate, trusteeship and independence in 1962, then parties and universal suffrage in 1991 — set inside the world's rights story the same books teach: Copernicus and Bruno, America's independence, the five faiths' teachings, the road to the Universal Declaration, Ken Saro-Wiwa and Irina Ratushinskaya, and the Mau and Black Saturday on Samoa's own timeline; then identity (Samoa's and Australia's — settlement, White Australia, the Stolen Generation) and the Industrial Revolution that changed the world's work. 20 lessons, in titles: `modern-world/` (15 lessons), `africa-after-1500/` (2 lessons), `ancient-world/` (3 lessons). 29 placements.
3. **Level 3 — The Mau from Inside, Civil Rights and the Pacific Neighbourhood** — the Mau taught twice over — as world-style civil-rights history beside Black America's long fight (the slave trade, the Civil War, Jim Crow, the NAACP, Brown v. Board, the freedom riders, Rosa Parks, the 1963 March) and from inside, through Telesia's diary and photo album, closing with New Zealand's 2002 apology — and the Pacific neighbourhood filled in: Fiji's settlement legend, Cakobau's conversion and Indian indenture, the copra trade, OPEC, Kennedy's Peace Corps and the Pacific's own Forum and University. 18 lessons, in titles: `africa-after-1500/` (2 lessons), `modern-world/` (15 lessons), `ancient-world/` (1 lessons). 19 placements.
4. **Level 4 — Samoa Whole: Origins, Contact, Colony and Independence — and the World Beyond** — Samoa's senior History, first year — the six-part frame whole: Migration (origins weighed — Lapita, Tagaloalagi, Hawaiki — the inter-island voyages, contact, missionaries, indentured labour, emigration), Imperialism, Colonialism, Nationalism and Decolonisation (Solf and the Mau a Pule, the Mau and Black Saturday, the UN and the Pacific's three roads out), Conflict (Samoa's civil wars and the struggle to be tafa'ifā; Palestine from Zionism to the 1973 war), International Relations (Samoa's treaties to partition, the road to Independence Day 1962, Pacific regionalism), Economic Transformations (capitalism, Samoa's subsistence economy and the market, the Great Depression, Japan's militarism) and Systems of Power (Russia 1917, Marx, China and Mao). 34 lessons, in titles: `ancient-world/` (2 lessons), `first-steps/` (1 lessons), `modern-world/` (29 lessons), `africa-after-1500/` (2 lessons). 40 placements.
5. **Level 5 — The World Whole: Germany, India, Vietnam, the Cold War and the Men of Power** — Samoa's senior History, second year — the same six-part frame turned worldwide: Migration through Germany (Weimar, the Nazis and the Holocaust, twelve million expellees, Gastarbeiter, the Berlin Wall, the Aussiedler), Decolonisation through the Balkans, Yugoslavia, Hungary 1956, the Soviet bloc's collapse, Vietnam and Ho Chi Minh, Conflict through India (British rule, the Sepoy Rebellion, Amritsar, Gandhi's satyagrahas, the Salt March, partition and Kashmir), International Relations from Bismarck's alliances to the founding of the United Nations, Economic Transformations in the Cold War (the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, containment in Korea and Vietnam, America the superpower), and Systems of Power in Stalin's Russia and Gandhi's India and South Africa. 38 lessons, in titles: `modern-world/` (38 lessons). 39 placements.

**Totals: 122 lessons, 169 placements** — every lesson on a
**student book** (all ten books that carry placements in the
set are student books; the one remaining record, the Years
1–8 curriculum statement, is RECORD-ONLY with no placements —
see ABOUT.md).
