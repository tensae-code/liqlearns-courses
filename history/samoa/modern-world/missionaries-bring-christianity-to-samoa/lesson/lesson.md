# Missionaries Bring Christianity to Samoa

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Missionaries Bring Christianity to Samoa". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 1, Unit 3 and Part 2, Unit 3. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

John Williams brings the LMS in 1830 and by 1834 Sāmoan is a written language; Peter Turner returns in 1835 at the request of Sāmoans and Tonga's King George Tupou I, planting the Wesleyan Lotu Toga; French Catholic priests follow in 1845 (Lotu Pope), and shipwrecked Mormon elders land in 1899; villages still mark the landing places.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
