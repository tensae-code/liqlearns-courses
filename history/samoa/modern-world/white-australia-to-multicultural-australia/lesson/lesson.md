# White Australia to Multicultural Australia

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "White Australia to Multicultural Australia". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Unit 2, Topic 2 "Changes Caused By Cultural Interaction: Changes in Policy"; Unit 2, Topic 2, the 1996 parliamentary motion. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2, Topic 2 "Changes Caused By Cultural Interaction: Changes in Policy"

the Australia formed in 1901 wants only European migrants under assimilation — the "White Australia" policy; after the Second World War 5.5 million immigrants, many non-English-speaking, keep their languages, the policy ends in the 1960s for "integration", and the 1970s bring multiculturalism and open migration.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2, Topic 2, the 1996 parliamentary motion

Prime Minister John Howard's parliament passes a bipartisan motion committing Australia to racial tolerance and cultural diversity, with statements testing whether the government is sorry that past governments allowed the Stolen Generation.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
