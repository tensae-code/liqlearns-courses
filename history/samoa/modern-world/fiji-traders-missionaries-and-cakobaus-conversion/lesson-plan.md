# Lesson plan — Fiji Traders Missionaries and Cakobaus Conversion

**Course:** History — Samoa · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Fiji Traders Missionaries and Cakobaus Conversion
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Mau from Inside, Civil Rights and the Pacific Neighbourhood (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Abel Tasman stumbles on Fiji in 1643 and Cook sails through in 1774; shipwrecked sailors and runaway Australian convicts are the first Europeans to live there; sandalwood traders and missionaries follow by the mid-1800s, and when Ratu Seru Cakobau accepts Christianity in 1854 the country follows and tribal warfare ends.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Mau from Inside, Civil Rights and the Pacific Neighbourhood — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to the part names in order and say each part's turn in one breath: who, where, what turned.
- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
- Go back to the one thing: Abel Tasman stumbles on Fiji in 1643 and Cook sails through in 1774; shipwrecked sailors and runaway Australian convicts are the first Europeans to live there; sandalwood traders and missionaries follow by the mid-1800s, and when Ratu Seru Cakobau accepts Christianity in 1854 the country follows and tribal warfare ends. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
