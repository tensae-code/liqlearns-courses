# Fascism and Nazism Two Dictatorships Compared

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fascism and Nazism Two Dictatorships Compared". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 4, Unit 2. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Mussolini's fascism (state supreme, one leader, communism the enemy, empire the duty) supplies the template; the book traces Hitler from rejected Vienna art student to Iron Cross messenger, the failed 1923 putsch and Mein Kampf, chancellor in 1933 and Führer in 1934 — then sets out his key ideas (Führer principle, Lebensraum, master race, anti-Semitism, Versailles voided) and the 1935 rearmament that proves them.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
