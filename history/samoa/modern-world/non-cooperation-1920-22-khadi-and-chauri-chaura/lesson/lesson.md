# Non Cooperation 1920 22 Khadi and Chauri Chaura

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Non Cooperation 1920 22 Khadi and Chauri Chaura". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 6, Unit 4. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

two British "wrongs" — Amritsar and the Khilafat — unite Hindus and Muslims behind Gandhi; he returns his medals, titles are surrendered, foreign cloth burns, the spinning wheel and khadi become the uniform of revolt (6,000 khadi-clad Congress delegates; Jinnah alone in a suit), 20,000 are jailed — and Gandhi himself halts the movement when a mob at Chauri Chaura kills twenty-two policemen.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
