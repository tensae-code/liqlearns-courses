# Irina Ratushinskaya and Soviet Repression

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Irina Ratushinskaya and Soviet Repression". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Unit 2, Case Study 4 "Freedom Of Religion". As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Ukrainian teacher Irina Ratushinskaya loses her job for refusing to discriminate against Jewish students, is sentenced in 1982 to seven years' hard labour for her religious poetry, and is freed and expelled in 1986 after an international outcry.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: ukrainian teacher Irina Ratushinskaya loses her job for refusing to discriminate against Jewish students, is sentenced in 1982 to seven years' hard labour for her religious poetry, and is freed and expelled in 1986 after an international outcry. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
