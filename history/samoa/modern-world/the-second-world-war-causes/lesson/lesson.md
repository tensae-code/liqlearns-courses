# The Second World War Causes

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "The Second World War Causes". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 4, Unit 2. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Spain's civil war rehearses the Axis and Russia; the Rome-Berlin Axis, Pact of Steel and Anti-Comintern Pact align the dictatorships; appeasement feeds Hitler Austria (Anschluss, March 1938) and the Sudetenland at Munich — the Czechs not even in the room — he takes the rest of Czechoslovakia within six months, and the Nazi-Soviet Pact of 1939 clears his road to Poland.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
