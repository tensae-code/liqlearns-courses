# German Samoa Solf and the Mau a Pule

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "German Samoa Solf and the Mau a Pule". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 2, Unit 4, first half. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Governor Solf (1900) abolishes the Tupu, installs Mataafa as Ali'i Sili, rewrites the fa'alupega to honour the Kaiser, and blocks the Sāmoan-owned Oloa copra company; orator Lauaki Mamoe petitions for a Sāmoan voice, and Solf exiles him and his supporters' families to Saipan — Lauaki dies at sea in 1915 on the ship finally bringing him home.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: governor Solf (1900) abolishes the Tupu, installs Mataafa as Ali'i Sili, rewrites the fa'alupega to honour the Kaiser, and blocks the Sāmoan-owned Oloa copra company; orator Lauaki Mamoe petitions for a Sāmoan voice, and Solf exiles him and his supporters' families to Saipan — Lauaki dies at sea in 1915 on the ship finally bringing him home. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
