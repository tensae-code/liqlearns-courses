# The United Nations Is Founded 1945

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "The United Nations Is Founded 1945". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 4, Unit 4. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

determined the new body must outmuscle the League, Roosevelt names it, Dumbarton Oaks (1944) drafts it, and 51 states sign the Charter at San Francisco in 1945; six organs follow, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948 — and the book's own balance sheet notes 30 million war dead since, and US$2.9 billion in unpaid peacekeeping bills by May 2000.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
