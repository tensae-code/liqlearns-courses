# Lesson plan — Militarism in Japan the Army Above the State

**Course:** History — Samoa · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Militarism in Japan the Army Above the State
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Samoa Whole: Origins, Contact, Colony and Independence — and the World Beyond (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Depression guts Japan's silk exports and jobs; militarists sell Manchuria as the answer — resources, land, markets; troops stage the Mukden explosion, seize Manchuria by 1932 as "Manchukuo", ignore the Lytton Commission, quit the League, and by 1937 Japan is at full war with China; depression halves Japan's silk prices and shuts half its factories by 1931; the army answers with empire — a staged explosion at Mukden excuses the seizure of Manchuria, the League's Lytton report names Japan the aggressor but cannot act, Japan quits the League in 1933 and invades all China by 1937.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Samoa Whole: Origins, Contact, Colony and Independence — and the World Beyond — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to the part names in order and say each part's turn in one breath: who, where, what turned.
- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
- Go back to the one thing: Depression guts Japan's silk exports and jobs; militarists sell Manchuria as the answer — resources, land, markets; troops stage the Mukden explosion, seize Manchuria by 1932 as "Manchukuo", ignore the Lytton Commission, quit the League, and by 1937 Japan is at full war with China; depression halves Japan's silk prices and shuts half its factories by 1931; the army answers with empire — a staged explosion at Mukden excuses the seizure of Manchuria, the League's Lytton report names Japan the aggressor but cannot act, Japan quits the League in 1933 and invades all China by 1937. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
