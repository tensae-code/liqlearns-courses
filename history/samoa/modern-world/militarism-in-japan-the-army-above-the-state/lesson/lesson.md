# Militarism in Japan the Army Above the State

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Militarism in Japan the Army Above the State". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 5, Unit 5, Japan; Part 4, Unit 2, Japan. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Part 5, Unit 5, Japan

depression guts Japan's silk exports and jobs; militarists sell Manchuria as the answer — resources, land, markets; troops stage the Mukden explosion, seize Manchuria by 1932 as "Manchukuo", ignore the Lytton Commission, quit the League, and by 1937 Japan is at full war with China.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Part 4, Unit 2, Japan

depression halves Japan's silk prices and shuts half its factories by 1931; the army answers with empire — a staged explosion at Mukden excuses the seizure of Manchuria, the League's Lytton report names Japan the aggressor but cannot act, Japan quits the League in 1933 and invades all China by 1937.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
