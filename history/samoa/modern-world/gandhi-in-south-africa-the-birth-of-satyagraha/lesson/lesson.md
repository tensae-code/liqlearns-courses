# Gandhi in South Africa the Birth of Satyagraha

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Gandhi in South Africa the Birth of Satyagraha". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 6, Unit 3. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

thrown off a first-class train to Pretoria for being Indian, the young lawyer stays twenty years for South Africa's 90,000 Indians — founds the Natal Indian Congress (1894), fights the Franchise Bill to a London veto, builds the Phoenix Settlement, launches Indian Opinion, and leads the miners' march from Natal into the Transvaal until Smuts yields: "The saint has left our shores, I sincerely hope for ever" (1914).

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
