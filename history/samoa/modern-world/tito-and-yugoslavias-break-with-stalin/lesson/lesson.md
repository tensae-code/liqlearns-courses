# Tito and Yugoslavias Break with Stalin

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Tito and Yugoslavias Break with Stalin". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 2, Unit 2. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Tito, the partisan hero who glued Serbs, Croats, Slovenes, Bosnians, Montenegrins, Albanians and Macedonians into Yugoslavia, insists in 1948 that each communist country follow its own path; Stalin expels him from the Cominform but cannot touch him — and when Tito dies in 1980 the federation's buried hatreds erupt, Croatia, Slovenia and Macedonia leaving in 1991 and Bosnia-Herzegovina in 1992.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
