# The Mau and Black Saturday

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "The Mau and Black Saturday". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Unit 2, Samoa history timeline and "The Mau Movement, 1926"; Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's diary" (diary story, plot summary only); Part 2, Unit 4, second half. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2, Samoa history timeline and "The Mau Movement, 1926"

the Talune brings Spanish influenza on 7 November 1918 and a fifth to a quarter of Western Samoa dies; grievances over interference with the fa'a Samoa, taxes and exclusion from government form the Mau in 1926; on Black Saturday, 28 December 1929, police fire on an unarmed Apia march, killing eleven including Tupua Tamasese, whose dying words plead for peace; Samoan delegations then press the UN until independence.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's diary" (diary story, plot summary only)

a Vaimoso-area mother watches her husband join the secret Mau meetings of 1926, agonises over the tax, registration and school boycott, sews purple uniforms for the Women's Mau led by the wives of Tamasese, Tuimaliifano, Nelson and Faumuina, marches with a thousand women — and buries her husband after police machine-gun the unarmed Beach Road march on Black Saturday, the day Tamasese is buried at Lepea.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

## Sub lesson 3 — Part 2, Unit 4, second half

New Zealand seizes Sāmoa unresisted on 31 August 1914; Colonel Logan's rule, the 1918 influenza disaster and paternalist racism build the Mau under Olaf Nelson, A. G. Smythe and Tupua Tamasese Lealofi III; leaders are deported, and on Black Saturday (dated 28 December 1928 in this unit, 1929 elsewhere in the book) police kill eleven marchers including Tamasese — whereupon the Women's Mau takes over the struggle.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
