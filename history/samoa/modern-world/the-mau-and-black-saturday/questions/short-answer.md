# The Mau and Black Saturday — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Tell the story of "Unit 2, Samoa history timeline and "The Mau Movement, 1926"" — who, where, and what turned. (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The Talune brings Spanish influenza on 7 November 1918 and a fifth to a quarter of Western Samoa dies; grievances over interference with the fa'a Samoa, taxes and exclusion from government form the Mau in 1926; on Black Saturday, 28 December 1929, police fire on an unarmed Apia march, killing eleven including Tupua Tamasese, whose dying words plead for peace; Samoan delegations then press the UN until independence.

**2.** What came of "Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's diary" (diary story, plot summary only)" — what does the lesson say followed from it? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** A Vaimoso-area mother watches her husband join the secret Mau meetings of 1926, agonises over the tax, registration and school boycott, sews purple uniforms for the Women's Mau led by the wives of Tamasese, Tuimaliifano, Nelson and Faumuina, marches with a thousand women — and buries her husband after police machine-gun the unarmed Beach Road march on Black Saturday, the day Tamasese is buried at Lepea.
