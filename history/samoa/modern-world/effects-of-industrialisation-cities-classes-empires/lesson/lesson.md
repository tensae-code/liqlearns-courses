# Effects of Industrialisation Cities Classes Empires

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Effects of Industrialisation Cities Classes Empires". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Unit 2, Topic 1 "Changes During The Industrial Revolution"; Unit 2, Topic 1 child workers' stories (plot summaries only). As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2, Topic 1 "Changes During The Industrial Revolution"

Britain's population leaps from 6.2 million (1750) to 14 million (1830), workers crowd into polluted cities without clean water, a new working class grows, the 1832 law gives middle-class men the vote, and the British Empire spreads over colonised lands that feed the factories.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2, Topic 1 child workers' stories (plot summaries only)

seven-year-old John Saville works as a trapper in the dark of a coal mine, opening doors for wagons that run over his leg; a young girl coal-bearer hauls baskets of coal out of an 1830s mine on her back.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
