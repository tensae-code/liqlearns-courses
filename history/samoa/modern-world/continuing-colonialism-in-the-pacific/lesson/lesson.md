# Continuing Colonialism in the Pacific

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Continuing Colonialism in the Pacific". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 2, Unit 6. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

the book asks whether colonialism really ended — Pacific states still depend on their former colonisers, and learners research the petition asking New Zealand to grant Sāmoans New Zealand citizenship, and why other Sāmoans objected.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the book asks whether colonialism really ended — Pacific states still depend on their former colonisers, and learners research the petition asking New Zealand to grant Sāmoans New Zealand citizenship, and why other Sāmoans objected. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
