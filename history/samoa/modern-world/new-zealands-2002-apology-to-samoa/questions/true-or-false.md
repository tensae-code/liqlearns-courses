# New Zealand's 2002 Apology to Samoa — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** At Samoa's 40th independence celebrations, Prime Minister Helen Clark formally apologises for New Zealand's inept administration of 1914–1962 — the 1918 influenza, the 1929 shooting of unarmed Mau protesters, the banishing of matai — presenting a waka huia; at Mangere, 78-year-old Seiuli Otto Hansell, born just after the epidemic, weeps and bows to her courage, and Tupua Tamasese Tupuola Efi, whose great-uncle was shot, calls the apology deeply moving.
**Answer: True**

**2.** In this lesson, "Unit 2, Topic 2, Helen Clark's speech and NZ Herald articles (5 June 2002)" is presented as a made-up tale with no real people, no real places and nothing that turned.
**Answer: False — the lesson tells it as Samoa's real story, with its people, its places and its turn, as the books teach it.**
