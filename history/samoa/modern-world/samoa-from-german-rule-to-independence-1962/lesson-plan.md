# Lesson plan — Samoa from German Rule to Independence 1962

**Course:** History — Samoa · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Samoa from German Rule to Independence 1962
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Samoa's Road to Independence, Rights and the Industrial World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Britain asks New Zealand to seize German Samoa at the start of the First World War; troops land unresisted at Apia in August 1914; Samoa passes through League of Nations mandate (1919) and UN trusteeship (1945), with New Zealand charged to prepare it for self-government, until in 1962 Samoa becomes the first Pacific island country to win independence; learners return to the Year 10 Book 1 history timeline (printed pp. 46–47 of that book) and choose which events on it — takeover, influenza, Mau, independence — did the work of forming Samoa's national identity; when the First World War begins, New Zealand takes Samoa from Germany and tears German men from their Samoan wives and families, imprisoning them until the war ends.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Samoa's Road to Independence, Rights and the Industrial World — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to the part names in order and say each part's turn in one breath: who, where, what turned.
- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
- Go back to the one thing: Britain asks New Zealand to seize German Samoa at the start of the First World War; troops land unresisted at Apia in August 1914; Samoa passes through League of Nations mandate (1919) and UN trusteeship (1945), with New Zealand charged to prepare it for self-government, until in 1962 Samoa becomes the first Pacific island country to win independence; learners return to the Year 10 Book 1 history timeline (printed pp. 46–47 of that book) and choose which events on it — takeover, influenza, Mau, independence — did the work of forming Samoa's national identity; when the First World War begins, New Zealand takes Samoa from Germany and tears German men from their Samoan wives and families, imprisoning them until the war ends.
