# Capitalism Private Ownership Factories New Classes

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Capitalism Private Ownership Factories New Classes". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 5, Unit 1 "Political Philosophies That Motivate Economic Action"; Part 5, Unit 1, systems compared. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Part 5, Unit 1 "Political Philosophies That Motivate Economic Action"

societies organise production by belief — pure market capitalism (private resources, profit, no government interference), market socialism (state ownership for equality), traditional subsistence, and the mixed systems most countries, Sāmoa included, actually run.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Part 5, Unit 1, systems compared

the two superpower systems are set side by side — American capitalism (private ownership, personal profit, free speech and elections) against Soviet communism (state ownership of industry and agriculture, one party, controlled media, criticism of Stalin punishable by death).

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
