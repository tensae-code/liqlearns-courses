# Stalin's Five-Year Plans and Collectivisation

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Stalin's Five-Year Plans and Collectivisation". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 6, Unit 2. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

the Five-Year Plans (1928–32, 1933–37) set steel and grain targets for a backward country, and collectivisation drives peasants — kulaks first — onto state farms; the book's verdict counts between 15 and 20 million dead from collectivisation, forced industrialisation and the purges of 1929–38, yet credits the same crash programme with the industrial strength that survived Barbarossa.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
