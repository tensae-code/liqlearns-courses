# Lesson plan — Dating the Past

**Course:** History — Samoa · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Learners build a chronological "history road" from the Mau's founding, the 1914 takeover, independence in 1962 and the cyclone that destroyed the Mau office, down to their own birth year; learners build two timelines — censuses from Babylon in 3800 BC through China, Persia, Greece, Egypt, India and Rome, and New Zealand's census story from the 1840 Treaty of Waitangi, the 1851 first census and the Depression and war postponements to 2001; learners scale a timeline (one line = 25 years, 1700–2020) and plot the peoples who migrated to Australia after the Aborigines, from 1770 to the 1980s; learners organise the Falealupo events (1990 covenant, 1993 Seacology prize, 1995 walkway gift, 1997 prize and dedication, 1999 elders' fund) into a scaled graphic timeline of words and pictures; learners draw a "history highway" of black American history from 1502 to 1920 — first Africans, the legal and then smuggled slave trades, abolitionist magazines and societies, Civil War, the Thirteenth Amendment, civil rights and citizenship acts, black men's vote (1870) and all women's vote (1920); learners read Telesia's history highway as a timeline — what it is about, the gaps between 1908, 1958, 1965, 1975, 1978 and 2000, and what changed across them; a dated table runs the Pacific's globalisation from late-1700s imperialism and Cook's 1769 voyage through the 1873–96 downturn, 1870–1900 colonialism, 1930s nationalism and the Pacific War, to post-war decolonisation; a dated spine runs from the German Empire's formation (1871) through Versailles, the Nazi invasions, the 1945 division into GDR and FRG, the 1961 border closure and the 1989 collapse of the East, to reunification in 1990 with Berlin as capital again.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 1, Activity 16 "Time Line"** — Learners build a chronological "history road" from the Mau's founding, the 1914 takeover, independence in 1962 and the cyclone that destroyed the Mau office, down to their own birth year.
2. **Unit 4, census timelines** — Learners build two timelines — censuses from Babylon in 3800 BC through China, Persia, Greece, Egypt, India and Rome, and New Zealand's census story from the 1840 Treaty of Waitangi, the 1851 first census and the Depression and war postponements to 2001.
3. **Unit 2, Activity 2 "Drawing A Timeline"** — Learners scale a timeline (one line = 25 years, 1700–2020) and plot the peoples who migrated to Australia after the Aborigines, from 1770 to the 1980s.
4. **Unit 3, Activity 5 "Drawing timelines"** — Learners organise the Falealupo events (1990 covenant, 1993 Seacology prize, 1995 walkway gift, 1997 prize and dedication, 1999 elders' fund) into a scaled graphic timeline of words and pictures.
5. **Unit 1, Activity 4 "Drawing A Graphic Timeline"** — Learners draw a "history highway" of black American history from 1502 to 1920 — first Africans, the legal and then smuggled slave trades, abolitionist magazines and societies, Civil War, the Thirteenth Amendment, civil rights and citizenship acts, black men's vote (1870) and all women's vote (1920).
6. **Unit 2, Activity 6 "Interpreting A Diagram"** — Learners read Telesia's history highway as a timeline — what it is about, the gaps between 1908, 1958, 1965, 1975, 1978 and 2000, and what changed across them.
7. **Part 5, Unit 3, "Timeline for Globalisation"** — A dated table runs the Pacific's globalisation from late-1700s imperialism and Cook's 1769 voyage through the 1873–96 downturn, 1870–1900 colonialism, 1930s nationalism and the Pacific War, to post-war decolonisation.
8. **Part 1 introduction, Germany timeline 1871–1990** — A dated spine runs from the German Empire's formation (1871) through Versailles, the Nazi invasions, the 1945 division into GDR and FRG, the 1961 border closure and the 1989 collapse of the East, to reunification in 1990 with Berlin as capital again.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Samoa's books themselves work, telling the past as stories of people, places and turns.
