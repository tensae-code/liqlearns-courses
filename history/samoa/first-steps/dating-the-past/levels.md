# Dating the Past — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1, Activity 16 "Time Line"
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: learners build a chronological "history road" from the Mau's founding, the 1914 takeover, independence in 1962 and the cyclone that destroyed the Mau office, down to their own birth year — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 4, census timelines
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: learners build two timelines — censuses from Babylon in 3800 BC through China, Persia, Greece, Egypt, India and Rome, and New Zealand's census story from the 1840 Treaty of Waitangi, the 1851 first census and the Depression and war postponements to 2001 — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2, Activity 2 "Drawing A Timeline"
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: learners scale a timeline (one line = 25 years, 1700–2020) and plot the peoples who migrated to Australia after the Aborigines, from 1770 to the 1980s — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 3, Activity 5 "Drawing timelines"
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: learners organise the Falealupo events (1990 covenant, 1993 Seacology prize, 1995 walkway gift, 1997 prize and dedication, 1999 elders' fund) into a scaled graphic timeline of words and pictures — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 1, Activity 4 "Drawing A Graphic Timeline"
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: learners draw a "history highway" of black American history from 1502 to 1920 — first Africans, the legal and then smuggled slave trades, abolitionist magazines and societies, Civil War, the Thirteenth Amendment, civil rights and citizenship acts, black men's vote (1870) and all women's vote (1920) — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 2, Activity 6 "Interpreting A Diagram"
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: learners read Telesia's history highway as a timeline — what it is about, the gaps between 1908, 1958, 1965, 1975, 1978 and 2000, and what changed across them — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Part 5, Unit 3, "Timeline for Globalisation"
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: a dated table runs the Pacific's globalisation from late-1700s imperialism and Cook's 1769 voyage through the 1873–96 downturn, 1870–1900 colonialism, 1930s nationalism and the Pacific War, to post-war decolonisation — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Part 1 introduction, Germany timeline 1871–1990
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: a dated spine runs from the German Empire's formation (1871) through Versailles, the Nazi invasions, the 1945 division into GDR and FRG, the 1961 border closure and the 1989 collapse of the East, to reunification in 1990 with Berlin as capital again — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
