# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Learners build a chronological "history road" from the Mau's founding, the 1914 takeover, independence in 1962 and the cyclone that destroyed the Mau office, down to their own birth year; learners build two timelines — censuses from Babylon in 3800 BC through China, Persia, Greece, Egypt, India and Rome, and New Zealand's census story from the 1840 Treaty of Waitangi, the 1851 first census and the Depression and war postponements to 2001; learners scale a timeline (one line = 25 years, 1700–2020) and plot the peoples who migrated to Australia after the Aborigines, from 1770 to the 1980s; learners organise the Falealupo events (1990 covenant, 1993 Seacology prize, 1995 walkway gift, 1997 prize and dedication, 1999 elders' fund) into a scaled graphic timeline of words and pictures; learners draw a "history highway" of black American history from 1502 to 1920 — first Africans, the legal and then smuggled slave trades, abolitionist magazines and societies, Civil War, the Thirteenth Amendment, civil rights and citizenship acts, black men's vote (1870) and all women's vote (1920); learners read Telesia's history highway as a timeline — what it is about, the gaps between 1908, 1958, 1965, 1975, 1978 and 2000, and what changed across them; a dated table runs the Pacific's globalisation from late-1700s imperialism and Cook's 1769 voyage through the 1873–96 downturn, 1870–1900 colonialism, 1930s nationalism and the Pacific War, to post-war decolonisation; a dated spine runs from the German Empire's formation (1871) through Versailles, the Nazi invasions, the 1945 division into GDR and FRG, the 1961 border closure and the 1989 collapse of the East, to reunification in 1990 with Berlin as capital again.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Activity 16 "Time Line"". learners build a chronological "history road" from the Mau's founding, the 1914 takeover, independence in 1962 and the cyclone that destroyed the Mau office, down to their own birth year.

Now the part called "Unit 4, census timelines". learners build two timelines — censuses from Babylon in 3800 BC through China, Persia, Greece, Egypt, India and Rome, and New Zealand's census story from the 1840 Treaty of Waitangi, the 1851 first census and the Depression and war postponements to 2001.

Now the part called "Unit 2, Activity 2 "Drawing A Timeline"". learners scale a timeline (one line = 25 years, 1700–2020) and plot the peoples who migrated to Australia after the Aborigines, from 1770 to the 1980s.

Now the part called "Unit 3, Activity 5 "Drawing timelines"". learners organise the Falealupo events (1990 covenant, 1993 Seacology prize, 1995 walkway gift, 1997 prize and dedication, 1999 elders' fund) into a scaled graphic timeline of words and pictures.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Activity 4 "Drawing A Graphic Timeline"". learners draw a "history highway" of black American history from 1502 to 1920 — first Africans, the legal and then smuggled slave trades, abolitionist magazines and societies, Civil War, the Thirteenth Amendment, civil rights and citizenship acts, black men's vote (1870) and all women's vote (1920).

Now the part called "Unit 2, Activity 6 "Interpreting A Diagram"". learners read Telesia's history highway as a timeline — what it is about, the gaps between 1908, 1958, 1965, 1975, 1978 and 2000, and what changed across them.

Now the part called "Part 5, Unit 3, "Timeline for Globalisation"". a dated table runs the Pacific's globalisation from late-1700s imperialism and Cook's 1769 voyage through the 1873–96 downturn, 1870–1900 colonialism, 1930s nationalism and the Pacific War, to post-war decolonisation.

Now the part called "Part 1 introduction, Germany timeline 1871–1990". a dated spine runs from the German Empire's formation (1871) through Versailles, the Nazi invasions, the 1945 division into GDR and FRG, the 1961 border closure and the 1989 collapse of the East, to reunification in 1990 with Berlin as capital again.

Remember it this way: follow the parts in order, and at each one ask — who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
