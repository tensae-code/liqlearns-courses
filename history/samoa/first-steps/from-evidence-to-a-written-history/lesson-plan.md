# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Samoa · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Learners sort evidence into primary (someone who was there), secondary (written up by others), oral and text sources before researching; learners read past-and-present photographs of Mulinu'u (fono, old Falefono, village) for what people built and did, to judge how it was and remains a political place; a double-hulled canoe built in Hawaii in the 1970s is sailed to Tahiti in 1976 by Satawalese navigator Mau Piailug using stars and swells, testing whether the ancestors voyaged deliberately; later voyages retrace the whole Polynesian Triangle to Rapa Nui; paired photographs dated from the 1890s to 2001 (feasts, houses, taupou, ceremonies) are sorted as practices or symbols and generalised over: most have changed a little, all were successfully passed on; learners re-read the past/present photo pairs (feast foods since 1890, taupou costume, house materials), then interview a grandparent-generation expert as a primary source on whether a chosen tradition (saofa'i, wedding, funeral, si'i) has changed; learners interrogate Tattersall's 1929–30 photographs — the Mau office at Vaimoso, prisoners carried by New Zealand marines, the thousand-women procession, the Black Saturday parade, Tamasese lying in state — with the photo-interpretation skills from Year 9; a USP historian rebuilds pre-contact and colonial Pacific history from documented oral traditions, cross-checking myths and legends against family genealogy books, Lands and Titles Court submissions and interviews until the sources agree.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 1, Topic 2 "Sacred Places", source-types box** — Learners sort evidence into primary (someone who was there), secondary (written up by others), oral and text sources before researching.
2. **Unit 1, Activity 14 "Interpreting Photographs"** — Learners read past-and-present photographs of Mulinu'u (fono, old Falefono, village) for what people built and did, to judge how it was and remains a political place.
3. **Unit 2, Topic 2, "The Voyages of the Hokule'a"** — A double-hulled canoe built in Hawaii in the 1970s is sailed to Tahiti in 1976 by Satawalese navigator Mau Piailug using stars and swells, testing whether the ancestors voyaged deliberately; later voyages retrace the whole Polynesian Triangle to Rapa Nui.
4. **Unit 1, Topic 1 photo study** — Paired photographs dated from the 1890s to 2001 (feasts, houses, taupou, ceremonies) are sorted as practices or symbols and generalised over: most have changed a little, all were successfully passed on.
5. **Unit 2, Topic 3 "Technology And Change", Activity 5 photo study and interviewing cultural experts** — Learners re-read the past/present photo pairs (feast foods since 1890, taupou costume, house materials), then interview a grandparent-generation expert as a primary source on whether a chosen tradition (saofa'i, wedding, funeral, si'i) has changed.
6. **Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's Photo Album" and Activity 3** — Learners interrogate Tattersall's 1929–30 photographs — the Mau office at Vaimoso, prisoners carried by New Zealand marines, the thousand-women procession, the Black Saturday parade, Tamasese lying in state — with the photo-interpretation skills from Year 9.
7. **Part 1, Unit 2, Dr Morgan Tuimaleali'ifano's research methods** — A USP historian rebuilds pre-contact and colonial Pacific history from documented oral traditions, cross-checking myths and legends against family genealogy books, Lands and Titles Court submissions and interviews until the sources agree.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Samoa's books themselves work, telling the past as stories of people, places and turns.

- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
