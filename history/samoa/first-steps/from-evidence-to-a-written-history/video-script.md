# Video script — From Evidence to a Written History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Learners sort evidence into primary (someone who was there), secondary (written up by others), oral and text sources before researching; learners read past-and-present photographs of Mulinu'u (fono, old Falefono, village) for what people built and did, to judge how it was and remains a political place; a double-hulled canoe built in Hawaii in the 1970s is sailed to Tahiti in 1976 by Satawalese navigator Mau Piailug using stars and swells, testing whether the ancestors voyaged deliberately; later voyages retrace the whole Polynesian Triangle to Rapa Nui; paired photographs dated from the 1890s to 2001 (feasts, houses, taupou, ceremonies) are sorted as practices or symbols and generalised over: most have changed a little, all were successfully passed on; learners re-read the past/present photo pairs (feast foods since 1890, taupou costume, house materials), then interview a grandparent-generation expert as a primary source on whether a chosen tradition (saofa'i, wedding, funeral, si'i) has changed; learners interrogate Tattersall's 1929–30 photographs — the Mau office at Vaimoso, prisoners carried by New Zealand marines, the thousand-women procession, the Black Saturday parade, Tamasese lying in state — with the photo-interpretation skills from Year 9; a USP historian rebuilds pre-contact and colonial Pacific history from documented oral traditions, cross-checking myths and legends against family genealogy books, Lands and Titles Court submissions and interviews until the sources agree.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Topic 2 "Sacred Places", source-types box". learners sort evidence into primary (someone who was there), secondary (written up by others), oral and text sources before researching.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Activity 14 "Interpreting Photographs"". learners read past-and-present photographs of Mulinu'u (fono, old Falefono, village) for what people built and did, to judge how it was and remains a political place.

Now the part called "Unit 2, Topic 2, "The Voyages of the Hokule'a"". a double-hulled canoe built in Hawaii in the 1970s is sailed to Tahiti in 1976 by Satawalese navigator Mau Piailug using stars and swells, testing whether the ancestors voyaged deliberately; later voyages retrace the whole Polynesian Triangle to Rapa Nui.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Topic 1 photo study". paired photographs dated from the 1890s to 2001 (feasts, houses, taupou, ceremonies) are sorted as practices or symbols and generalised over: most have changed a little, all were successfully passed on.

Now the part called "Unit 2, Topic 3 "Technology And Change", Activity 5 photo study and interviewing cultural experts". learners re-read the past/present photo pairs (feast foods since 1890, taupou costume, house materials), then interview a grandparent-generation expert as a primary source on whether a chosen tradition (saofa'i, wedding, funeral, si'i) has changed.

Now the part called "Unit 2, Topic 2 "Telesia's Photo Album" and Activity 3". learners interrogate Tattersall's 1929–30 photographs — the Mau office at Vaimoso, prisoners carried by New Zealand marines, the thousand-women procession, the Black Saturday parade, Tamasese lying in state — with the photo-interpretation skills from Year 9.

Now the part called "Part 1, Unit 2, Dr Morgan Tuimaleali'ifano's research methods". a USP historian rebuilds pre-contact and colonial Pacific history from documented oral traditions, cross-checking myths and legends against family genealogy books, Lands and Titles Court submissions and interviews until the sources agree.

Remember it this way: follow the parts in order, and at each one ask — who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
