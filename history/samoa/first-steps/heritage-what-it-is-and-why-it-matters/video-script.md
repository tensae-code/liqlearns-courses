# Video script — Heritage What It Is and Why It Matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

Now the part called "Unit 3, Topic 2 "Distribution Of Resources"". barter and ceremonial exchange (the si'i presentation of 'ie toga fine mats) carry honour between families, and a traditional Samoan wedding's exchanges are set against a modern wedding's.

Now the part called "Unit 3, Activity 13 reading "Dolphin Hunting in Lau Lagoon" (plot summary only)". a Malaita boy is admitted to the men's prayer house, joins the priest-led canoe hunt, and the 65 dolphins caught yield teeth saved as bride-wealth money.

Now the part called "Unit 1 introduction and Topic 1 "Practices And Symbols"". heritage is what parents and ancestors pass on — language, beliefs, clothes, dances, food — and a talking chief's staff and fly whisk show how a practice and a symbol carry the fa'aSāmoa to the next generation.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Activity 8 "Understanding And Explaining Siapo"". New Zealand-born Samoan film-maker Caroline Lolegi Vercoe confesses she cannot "read" siapo designs, and learners decode the plant-and-animal symbols of tapa cloth, then design their own.

Now the part called "Unit 2, Resource 1 "Sereana's Story" (plot summary only)". Sereana already knows Samoan weddings before her brother Lotovale's — from her parents' wedding photos on the wall, cousins' wedding videos from Apia and Hawai'i, and being taken to family weddings.

Now the part called "Unit 2, Resource 2 "Museums and museum displays"". Pacific curator Jakki Leota-Ete at the Waikato Museum cares for Samoan mea sina carried to New Zealand — a tanoa brought back in the 1930s, a fragile siapo brought by an ex-missionary in the 1960s — researching, conserving and blessing them for exhibition.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Topic 2 "Who Are We? National Identity"". a nation's identity is built over time through historical events and kept by telling certain stories over and over, by oral histories, shared culture and schools; Samoa's people are ancient in the islands but its independent state only began in 1962.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Topic 3 "What Are We? Cultural Identity"". culture is values, beliefs, customs and traditions passed from one generation to the next, kept when shared between generations, recorded in stories and books, and taught in schools.

Now the part called "Unit 1, Topic 1 resource 5A "Fijian Firewalking" (legend, plot summary only)". a Beqa warrior fishing for eels pulls up a spirit god, spares its life, and is given the power to walk unharmed on white-hot stones — the gift his tribe's firewalkers still use under strict protocol.

Now the part called "Unit 3, Fact File Two "The Traditional Uses of the Coconut and Coconut Tree"". the "Pacific Tree of Life" is used exactly as parents, grandparents and ancestors used it — flesh, milk, husk rope and fuel, leaf baskets and brooms, trunk posts, shell cups and charcoal.

Remember it this way: follow the parts in order, and at each one ask — who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
