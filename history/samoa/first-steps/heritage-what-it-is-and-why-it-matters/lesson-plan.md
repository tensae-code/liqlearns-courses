**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific (CURRICULUM.md).

**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 3, Topic 2 "Distribution Of Resources"** — Barter and ceremonial exchange (the si'i presentation of 'ie toga fine mats) carry honour between families, and a traditional Samoan wedding's exchanges are set against a modern wedding's.
2. **Unit 3, Activity 13 reading "Dolphin Hunting in Lau Lagoon" (plot summary only)** — A Malaita boy is admitted to the men's prayer house, joins the priest-led canoe hunt, and the 65 dolphins caught yield teeth saved as bride-wealth money.
3. **Unit 1 introduction and Topic 1 "Practices And Symbols"** — Heritage is what parents and ancestors pass on — language, beliefs, clothes, dances, food — and a talking chief's staff and fly whisk show how a practice and a symbol carry the fa'aSāmoa to the next generation.
4. **Unit 1, Activity 8 "Understanding And Explaining Siapo"** — New Zealand-born Samoan film-maker Caroline Lolegi Vercoe confesses she cannot "read" siapo designs, and learners decode the plant-and-animal symbols of tapa cloth, then design their own.
5. **Unit 2, Resource 1 "Sereana's Story" (plot summary only)** — Sereana already knows Samoan weddings before her brother Lotovale's — from her parents' wedding photos on the wall, cousins' wedding videos from Apia and Hawai'i, and being taken to family weddings.
6. **Unit 2, Resource 2 "Museums and museum displays"** — Pacific curator Jakki Leota-Ete at the Waikato Museum cares for Samoan mea sina carried to New Zealand — a tanoa brought back in the 1930s, a fragile siapo brought by an ex-missionary in the 1960s — researching, conserving and blessing them for exhibition.
7. **Unit 1, Topic 2 "Who Are We? National Identity"** — A nation's identity is built over time through historical events and kept by telling certain stories over and over, by oral histories, shared culture and schools; Samoa's people are ancient in the islands but its independent state only began in 1962.
8. **Unit 1, Topic 3 "What Are We? Cultural Identity"** — Culture is values, beliefs, customs and traditions passed from one generation to the next, kept when shared between generations, recorded in stories and books, and taught in schools.
9. **Unit 1, Topic 1 resource 5A "Fijian Firewalking" (legend, plot summary only)** — A Beqa warrior fishing for eels pulls up a spirit god, spares its life, and is given the power to walk unharmed on white-hot stones — the gift his tribe's firewalkers still use under strict protocol.
10. **Unit 3, Fact File Two "The Traditional Uses of the Coconut and Coconut Tree"** — The "Pacific Tree of Life" is used exactly as parents, grandparents and ancestors used it — flesh, milk, husk rope and fuel, leaf baskets and brooms, trunk posts, shell cups and charcoal.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Samoa's books themselves work, telling the past as stories of people, places and turns.
