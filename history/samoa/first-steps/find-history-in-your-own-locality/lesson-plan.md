# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Samoa · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Learners list what Samoan homes needed for housing, food, tools and transport fifty years ago and weigh the long-term damage that use did; learners interview an elder or parent about exchanges that happened in the past, write it up as a report and draw conclusions from the class's reports; each learner picks a church, asks what events in its historical past put it where it stands, and answers from elders, printed histories of Samoa's churches, the minister and observation; learners ask five people of different ages how culture and heritage are passed on (songs, myths, stories, dances, ceremonies, school), chart the answers and present them as poster, pamphlet or report; learners set Samoa in the past (weaving fine mats, walking or going by boat, asking family for help) against Samoa today (tins of coconut cream, factories) and ask what production, distribution and exchange looked like in Samoa's past; four generations of one aiga travel to New Zealand — great-grandmother Mele, a servant girl taken by steamship in 1908 who finds Auckland cold, crowded and lonely; grandfather Ioane for medical treatment in 1958; father Peleiupu, a homesick 17-year-old scholarship boy in Wellington in 1965 who finds friends through rugby; Auntie Naomi, who nurses, marries a European in Auckland and renegotiates the fa'aSamoa; and Telesia herself, flying in hours where Mele sailed for days; learners interview someone in their fifties, sixties or seventies about their memories of the early-1970s oil crisis and how it affected Samoa and its people.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 1, Activity 6 "Resources Required In The Past"** — Learners list what Samoan homes needed for housing, food, tools and transport fifty years ago and weigh the long-term damage that use did.
2. **Unit 3, Activity 11 "Interview"** — Learners interview an elder or parent about exchanges that happened in the past, write it up as a report and draw conclusions from the class's reports.
3. **Unit 1, Activity 11 "Research On A Sacred Place"** — Each learner picks a church, asks what events in its historical past put it where it stands, and answers from elders, printed histories of Samoa's churches, the minister and observation.
4. **Unit 1, Topic 2 "Treasures And Transfer", Activity 4 research** — Learners ask five people of different ages how culture and heritage are passed on (songs, myths, stories, dances, ceremonies, school), chart the answers and present them as poster, pamphlet or report.
5. **Unit 2, Activity 10 "Mix And Match"** — Learners set Samoa in the past (weaving fine mats, walking or going by boat, asking family for help) against Samoa today (tins of coconut cream, factories) and ask what production, distribution and exchange looked like in Samoa's past.
6. **Unit 2, Topic 3 "Telesia's History Highway" (family stories, plot summaries only)** — Four generations of one aiga travel to New Zealand — great-grandmother Mele, a servant girl taken by steamship in 1908 who finds Auckland cold, crowded and lonely; grandfather Ioane for medical treatment in 1958; father Peleiupu, a homesick 17-year-old scholarship boy in Wellington in 1965 who finds friends through rugby; Auntie Naomi, who nurses, marries a European in Auckland and renegotiates the fa'aSamoa; and Telesia herself, flying in hours where Mele sailed for days.
7. **Unit 3 Part B, Topic 1 elder interview** — Learners interview someone in their fifties, sixties or seventies about their memories of the early-1970s oil crisis and how it affected Samoa and its people.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Samoa's books themselves work, telling the past as stories of people, places and turns.

- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
