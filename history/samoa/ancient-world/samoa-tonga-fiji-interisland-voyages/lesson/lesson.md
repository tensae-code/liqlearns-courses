# Samoa, Tonga and Fiji: Inter-Island Voyages

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Samoa, Tonga and Fiji: Inter-Island Voyages". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Part 1, Unit 2, Tongan stories; Part 1, Unit 2, Fijian stories (plot summaries only). As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Part 1, Unit 2, Tongan stories

Tongan royalty sail to Sāmoa for highborn wives — Prince Ngata's marriage founds Tonga's Tu'i Kanokupolu line, and Vaetoifaga's daughter Salamasina grows up to hold all four pāpā titles as Sāmoa's first tafa'ifā.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Part 1, Unit 2, Fijian stories (plot summaries only)

the Tu'i Fiti's sons come to conquer the warrior Vaea, who lifts their great boat into the trees and spares them for their sister Apaula; her son is murdered in Fiji, she returns weeping — the river Loimata o Apaula, "Apaula's tears" — and Vaea's brother Va'atausili regains his strength to avenge him.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
