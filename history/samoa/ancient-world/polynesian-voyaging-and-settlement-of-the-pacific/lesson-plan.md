# Lesson plan — Polynesian Voyaging and Settlement of the Pacific

**Course:** History — Samoa · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Polynesian Voyaging and Settlement of the Pacific
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ancestors cross the vast Pacific in canoes and settle island by island — Fiji, Tonga and Samoa among the oldest on the settlement timeline from about 1500 BC, the far corners (Hawaii, Rapanui, Aotearoa) last — peopling Micronesia, Melanesia and Polynesia; the Hōkūle'a's 1985 Rarotonga-to-Bay of Islands crossing, timed to the pohutukawa bloom as the legends said, is used to argue the ancestors voyaged deliberately and two-way, not by one-way drift.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage, Places and the Voyaging Pacific — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to the part names in order and say each part's turn in one breath: who, where, what turned.
- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
- Go back to the one thing: Ancestors cross the vast Pacific in canoes and settle island by island — Fiji, Tonga and Samoa among the oldest on the settlement timeline from about 1500 BC, the far corners (Hawaii, Rapanui, Aotearoa) last — peopling Micronesia, Melanesia and Polynesia; the Hōkūle'a's 1985 Rarotonga-to-Bay of Islands crossing, timed to the pohutukawa bloom as the legends said, is used to argue the ancestors voyaged deliberately and two-way, not by one-way drift. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
