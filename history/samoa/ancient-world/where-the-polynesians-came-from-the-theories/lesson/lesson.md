# Where the Polynesians Came from the Theories

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Where the Polynesians Came from the Theories". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Unit 2, Topic 2, origins theories; Part 1, Unit 1 "Which Theories Explain Sāmoa's Origins?", Theory One. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2, Topic 2, origins theories

three rival answers compete — ancestors sailed east from Asia through Melanesia; Thor Heyerdahl's 1947 Kon-Tiki raft backs a South American origin carried on the kumara; and Polynesian oral traditions name a homeland, Hawaiki, or descent from the gods.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Part 1, Unit 1 "Which Theories Explain Sāmoa's Origins?", Theory One

Lapita pottery, carbon-dated at Mulifanua to about 2,990 years old, traces Austronesian ancestors from Taiwan through the Philippines, Indonesia and New Guinea to the Bismarcks by 1500 BC, Tonga by 1300 BC and Sāmoa by about 1000 BC — backed by DNA, the Austronesian language family and shared practices (siapo, tatau, ava).

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
