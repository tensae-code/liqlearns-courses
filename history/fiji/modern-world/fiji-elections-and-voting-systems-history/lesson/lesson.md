# Fiji: Elections and Voting Systems as History

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji: Elections and Voting Systems as History". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Fiji's elections as history: 1972, 1977 and 2006. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Party politics begins in the 1960s before independence; the Alliance wins the 1972 election (52 seats) and the split, multi-party 1977 elections, and pupils research how the 2006 Alternative Voting system worked and what changed after it.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: party politics begins in the 1960s before independence. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
