# Video script — Fiji: Indentured Indians and Blackbirding

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Governor Arthur Gordon, needing plantation labour iTaukei would not supply, has Solomon Islanders blackbirded and, from the Leonidas in 1879 to 1916, Indian girmit labourers brought from Calcutta; girmit life is marked by a broken 40-women-per-100-men quota, jealousy and fighting; in 1879 Fiji changes again as Indians arrive, most enticed by honeyed promises and deceptions to sign an indenture contract.

Now the part called "Continuity and Change: arrival of other ethnic groups". Governor Arthur Gordon, needing plantation labour iTaukei would not supply, has Solomon Islanders blackbirded and, from the Leonidas in 1879 to 1916, Indian girmit labourers brought from Calcutta — most stay after indenture ends and become farmers and businessmen.

Now the part called "The Early Sugar Industry: indentured labourers in Fiji". Girmit life is marked by a broken 40-women-per-100-men quota, jealousy and fighting, and no schooling for children, until C.F. Andrews and W.W. Pearson investigate in 1915 and their report brings the system down.

Now the part called "Indians in Fiji". In 1879 Fiji changes again as Indians arrive, most enticed by honeyed promises and deceptions to sign an indenture contract — the girmitiya, not slaves, work the plantations under a pass system, and many make Fiji home.

Remember it this way: follow the parts in order, and at each one ask — who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
