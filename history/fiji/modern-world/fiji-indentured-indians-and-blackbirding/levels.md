# Fiji: Indentured Indians and Blackbirding — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Continuity and Change: arrival of other ethnic groups
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Governor Arthur Gordon, needing plantation labour iTaukei would not supply, has Solomon Islanders blackbirded and, from the Leonidas in 1879 to 1916, Indian girmit labourers brought from Calcutta — most stay after indenture ends and become farmers and businessmen — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Early Sugar Industry: indentured labourers in Fiji
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: girmit life is marked by a broken 40-women-per-100-men quota, jealousy and fighting, and no schooling for children, until C.F. Andrews and W.W. Pearson investigate in 1915 and their report brings the system down — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Indians in Fiji
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: in 1879 Fiji changes again as Indians arrive, most enticed by honeyed promises and deceptions to sign an indenture contract — the girmitiya, not slaves, work the plantations under a pass system, and many make Fiji home — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
