## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Continuity and Change: arrival of other ethnic groups** — Governor Arthur Gordon, needing plantation labour iTaukei would not supply, has Solomon Islanders blackbirded and, from the Leonidas in 1879 to 1916, Indian girmit labourers brought from Calcutta — most stay after indenture ends and become farmers and businessmen.
2. **The Early Sugar Industry: indentured labourers in Fiji** — Girmit life is marked by a broken 40-women-per-100-men quota, jealousy and fighting, and no schooling for children, until C.F. Andrews and W.W. Pearson investigate in 1915 and their report brings the system down.
3. **Indians in Fiji** — In 1879 Fiji changes again as Indians arrive, most enticed by honeyed promises and deceptions to sign an indenture contract — the girmitiya, not slaves, work the plantations under a pass system, and many make Fiji home.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to the part names in order and say each part's turn in one breath: who, where, what turned.
- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
- Go back to the one thing: Governor Arthur Gordon, needing plantation labour iTaukei would not supply, has Solomon Islanders blackbirded and, from the Leonidas in 1879 to 1916, Indian girmit labourers brought from Calcutta; girmit life is marked by a broken 40-women-per-100-men quota, jealousy and fighting; in 1879 Fiji changes again as Indians arrive, most enticed by honeyed promises and deceptions to sign an indenture contract.
