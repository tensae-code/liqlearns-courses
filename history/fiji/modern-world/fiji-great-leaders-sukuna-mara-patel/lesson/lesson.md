# Fiji's Great Leaders: Sukuna, Mara, Patel

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji's Great Leaders: Sukuna, Mara, Patel". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: 2.6 Local Leaders. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Ratu Sukuna, first Fijian university graduate and Oxford lawyer, fights with the French Foreign Legion, founds the Native Land Trust Board and mentors independence leaders; Ratu Mara, founding father, is chief minister then first PM; A.D. Patel, farmers' barrister, founds the NFP preaching a fully integrated independent Fiji.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: ratu Sukuna, first Fijian university graduate and Oxford lawyer, fights with the French Foreign Legion, founds the Native Land Trust Board and mentors independence leaders. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
