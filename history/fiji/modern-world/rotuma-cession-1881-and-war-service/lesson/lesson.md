# Rotuma: Cession 1881 and War Service

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Rotuma: Cession 1881 and War Service". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Cession of Rotuma; Rotumans in the Second World War. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

To end the unrest Rotumans cede their island to Britain in 1881, Governor Gordon sending Lt Commander Bower to receive it (the cession instrument and Motusa memorial survive); in 1942-45 Rotuman bearer troops serve with distinction in the Solomons, Sergeant Josefa Rigamoto winning the Military Medal.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: to end the unrest Rotumans cede their island to Britain in 1881, Governor Gordon sending Lt Commander Bower to receive it (the cession instrument and Motusa memorial survive). Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
