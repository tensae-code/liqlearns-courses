Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: barter gives way to money, mission schools spread, Cakobau the Tui Viti cedes Fiji to Great Britain, and a colonial government at Levuka brings schools, hospitals, police and law — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — 2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: US Consul Williams's looted stores and a US warship's demand leave Cakobau owing debts he cannot pay (US$38,531); Thurston carries his offer to London and on 10 October 1874 Cakobau, Ma'afu and senior chiefs sign Fiji over to Britain — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 2: Significance of Cession; the Commission; the Deed of Cession
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: chaotic Fiji, a collapsing Cakobau government and thriving blackbirding force Gladstone's Britain to send Goodenough and Layard (1873); the commissioners go beyond their brief, win the chiefs round, and Sir Hercules Robinson takes an unconditional cession signed at Levuka on 10 October 1874, Cakobau pensioned as Vunivalu and Ma'afu recognised as Tui Lau — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Democratic government in Fiji: timeline from chiefdom to 1874
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the transition from traditional chiefdom to democratic government runs through the 1800s and materialises in 1874, an interim government forming on 10 March 1874 on the road to cession — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
