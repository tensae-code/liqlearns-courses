# Video script — Fiji: Cession to Britain and Colonial Rule

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Barter gives way to money, mission schools spread, Cakobau the Tui Viti cedes Fiji to Great Britain; US Consul Williams's looted stores and a US warship's demand leave Cakobau owing debts he cannot pay (US$38,531); chaotic Fiji, a collapsing Cakobau government and thriving blackbirding force Gladstone's Britain to send Goodenough and Layard (1873); the transition from traditional chiefdom to democratic government runs through the 1800s and materialises in 1874, an interim government forming on 10 March 1874 on the road to cession.

Now the part called "Early iTaukei and Europeans / Fiji Today". Barter gives way to money, mission schools spread, Cakobau the Tui Viti cedes Fiji to Great Britain, and a colonial government at Levuka brings schools, hospitals, police and law.

Now the part called "2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession". US Consul Williams's looted stores and a US warship's demand leave Cakobau owing debts he cannot pay (US$38,531); Thurston carries his offer to London and on 10 October 1874 Cakobau, Ma'afu and senior chiefs sign Fiji over to Britain.

Now the part called "Chapter 2: Significance of Cession; the Commission; the Deed of Cession". Chaotic Fiji, a collapsing Cakobau government and thriving blackbirding force Gladstone's Britain to send Goodenough and Layard (1873); the commissioners go beyond their brief, win the chiefs round, and Sir Hercules Robinson takes an unconditional cession signed at Levuka on 10 October 1874, Cakobau pensioned as Vunivalu and Ma'afu recognised as Tui Lau.

Now the part called "Democratic government in Fiji: timeline from chiefdom to 1874". The transition from traditional chiefdom to democratic government runs through the 1800s and materialises in 1874, an interim government forming on 10 March 1874 on the road to cession.

Remember it this way: follow the parts in order, and at each one ask — who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
