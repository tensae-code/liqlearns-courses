# Fiji: Cession to Britain and Colonial Rule

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji: Cession to Britain and Colonial Rule". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Early iTaukei and Europeans / Fiji Today; 2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession; Chapter 2: Significance of Cession; the Commission; the Deed of Cession; Democratic government in Fiji: timeline from chiefdom to 1874. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Early iTaukei and Europeans / Fiji Today

Barter gives way to money, mission schools spread, Cakobau the Tui Viti cedes Fiji to Great Britain, and a colonial government at Levuka brings schools, hospitals, police and law.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — 2.1.2-2.1.3 Fiji during and reasons for the Deed of Cession

US Consul Williams's looted stores and a US warship's demand leave Cakobau owing debts he cannot pay (US$38,531); Thurston carries his offer to London and on 10 October 1874 Cakobau, Ma'afu and senior chiefs sign Fiji over to Britain.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 2: Significance of Cession; the Commission; the Deed of Cession

Chaotic Fiji, a collapsing Cakobau government and thriving blackbirding force Gladstone's Britain to send Goodenough and Layard (1873); the commissioners go beyond their brief, win the chiefs round, and Sir Hercules Robinson takes an unconditional cession signed at Levuka on 10 October 1874, Cakobau pensioned as Vunivalu and Ma'afu recognised as Tui Lau.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Democratic government in Fiji: timeline from chiefdom to 1874

The transition from traditional chiefdom to democratic government runs through the 1800s and materialises in 1874, an interim government forming on 10 March 1874 on the road to cession.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
