# Fiji Diplomacy: The MSG and the Nuclear-Free Pacific

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji Diplomacy: The MSG and the Nuclear-Free Pacific". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Unit 1 Diplomacy and Fiji: Fiji in the international arena. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

A small nation works the Melanesian Spearhead Group, SPREP and SOPAC, balances Rim nations against Europe, India and the Middle East, and joins the Nuclear Free and Independent Pacific movement (8th conference report, 1999), while Japan courts Fiji's vote on whaling.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a small nation works the Melanesian Spearhead Group, SPREP and SOPAC, balances Rim nations against Europe, India and the Middle East. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
