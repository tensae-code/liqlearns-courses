# Fiji: Coups and Constitutions, 1987 to 2014

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji: Coups and Constitutions, 1987 to 2014". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Timeline 1970 to present / Leadership in Fiji; Political conditions: the coup of 2000 and the 2006 election; Challenges 1980-1987 and new leadership 1987-2006; After the 1977 election; Fiji's interim civilian government 2000; Coup d'état 1987 and the impact of the second coup; The 1990 Constitution, the review commission and the 1997 Constitution. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Timeline 1970 to present / Leadership in Fiji

Mara's Alliance wins in 1972, Bavadra's coalition wins in 1987 and Rabuka's two coups make Fiji a republic, Chaudhry becomes first Indo-Fijian PM in 1999 and is toppled by Speight in 2000, Bainimarama coups in 2006, the constitution is suspended in 2009 and rewritten in 2013 before the 2014 election.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Political conditions: the coup of 2000 and the 2006 election

Chaudhry's government, barely a year old, is seized with most MPs in the 2000 coup; the story is carried forward to the closely fought Fiji election of 2006.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

## Sub lesson 3 — Challenges 1980-1987 and new leadership 1987-2006

After the 1987 turn a new category of leadership emerges — interim ministers such as Senilagakali and Nailatikau serve governments born of coups, while non-political leaders champion integration from outside politics.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.

## Sub lesson 4 — After the 1977 election; Fiji's interim civilian government 2000

Readings carry the story from the disputed aftermath of the 1977 election to Qarase's interim civilian government of 2000, formed after a coup.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 5 — Coup d'état 1987 and the impact of the second coup

One month after the 1987 election, on 14 May, Major Sitiveni Rabuka leads a coup; his second coup destroys the chances of a negotiated settlement between the communities.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

## Sub lesson 6 — The 1990 Constitution, the review commission and the 1997 Constitution

The interim government's 1989 committee produces the 1990 constitution amid brain-drain; a three-person review commission follows, and its work issues in the 1997 Constitution Amendment Act.

Say this part back in one breath before you move on: who, where, what turned. If you can say it, you own it.
