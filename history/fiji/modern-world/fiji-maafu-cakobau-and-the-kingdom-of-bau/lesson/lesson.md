# Fiji: Ma'afu, Cakobau and the Kingdom of Bau

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji: Ma'afu, Cakobau and the Kingdom of Bau". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Ma'afu, Cakobau and the Kingdom of Bau; Traditional government in Fiji: chiefdoms, Ma'afu and Cakobau. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ma'afu, Cakobau and the Kingdom of Bau

Tongan prince Ma'afu arrives in 1847 and builds a power base in Lau; Cakobau rises at Bau, planters Wilkinson and Thurston push a joint Fijian-European government, rival annexation schemes are weighed and rejected, and the 1871 Kingdom of Bau constitution makes Cakobau king of Fiji outside the northern and eastern confederacy.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional government in Fiji: chiefdoms, Ma'afu and Cakobau

Early Fijian society governs itself through chiefdoms; Ma'afu extends his influence even to Beqa in opposition to Cakobau, yet the Bauan kingdom leads Fiji only because of Tongan intervention, and Cakobau's Tui Viti claim outruns his real power.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
