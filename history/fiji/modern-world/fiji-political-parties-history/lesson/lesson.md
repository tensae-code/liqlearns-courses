# Fiji: The History of Political Parties

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji: The History of Political Parties". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: History of political parties in Fiji; the 1966 election; Past elections table and the 2006 election. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — History of political parties in Fiji; the 1966 election

Parties form in the 1960s to contest the road to independence; Brij Lal's Broken Waves cartoon and text anchor a study of the 1966 election, and leader portraits (Jai Ram Reddy; Vanuatu's Father Walter Lini as a Pacific comparison) show who built them.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Past elections table and the 2006 election

A seats table runs across Fiji's elections to the 6-13 May 2006 result (SDL/United Fiji 36 seats), taking in the election that made Mahendra Chaudhry, of the Fiji Labour Party, Fiji's first Indian prime minister.

Notice what the story is really about underneath the names: people meeting, moving, ruling, believing or building — and the change their meeting made.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.
