# The Second World War: Causes

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "The Second World War: Causes". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: 2.2 Significant World Events: World War II and its causes. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

Japan, short of land and resources, invades Manchuria (1931) and China (1937); fascist dictators rise from WWI's turmoil seeking empires; the Great Depression destabilises governments — together they bring the 1939-1945 total war, the Holocaust and nuclear weapons.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: japan, short of land and resources, invades Manchuria (1931) and China (1937). Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
