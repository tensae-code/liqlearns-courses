# Fiji's Legal System: From Chiefs to Courts

## Do it first

Take a pencil and a page. Write the lesson's title at the top: "Fiji's Legal System: From Chiefs to Courts". Under it, leave one empty box for each part of the story, in order: Introduction: from chiefly decision-making to colonial courts. As you read each part, fill its box with three words: who, where, and what turned. By the end of the lesson your page is the whole story in your own hand — and this lesson is about exactly what fills those boxes.

## The story

The earliest iTaukei rely on their chiefs' decision-making; once the British colony begins, courts replace the chief's word, and the unit traces that handover as the root of Fiji's modern legal order.

Hold the turn in this part: who was there, where it happened, what changed, and what came of it — that turn is what makes it history, not just a list of names.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold any story in four questions — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A story you can question is a story you can retell.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the earliest iTaukei rely on their chiefs' decision-making. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: fill the boxes again from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: draw the part that struck you most, and label it with who, where and what turned.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern shows itself. Note it.
