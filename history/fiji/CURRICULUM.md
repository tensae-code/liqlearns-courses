# History — Fiji course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Fiji inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Fiji's own books are the ladder.
The placements file's book order is the teaching order —
earliest books first: the Year 3 and Year 4 Social Studies
books begin history at home, with the child's own family tree,
elders' memory and the village's continuity and change; the
Pacific ESD reader and the Year 5–7 books widen the story to
the voyaging Pacific, Lapita evidence and Fiji's own arrival
story; the Year 9–10 Social Science books tell Fiji's history
by periods and trades — pre-contact, discovery, sandalwood,
beche-de-mer, missionaries, Ma'afu and Cakobau — and then argue
the colonial century (Cession, Gordon's native policy,
indenture); the Year 11 History units ("Cultural Interaction
and Integration" and "Government and Governance") judge that
century by integration, policy, parties, elections and law,
with Rotuma as a complete Fiji story in miniature; and the
Year 12 book closes with diplomacy, country by country. The
six levels below group that progression the way Fiji's own
story divides: first steps at home, the ancient Pacific,
contact and kingdom, cession and colony, independence and its
ruptures, and Fiji facing the world. The ladder is a
recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Fiji's Ministry
of Education Social Studies, Social Science and History books
— except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the
people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home** — history begins where the child stands — what history is, found first in the family tree and the elders' answers; heritage itself, in the cultures, artefacts, festivals and sea stories a child already lives inside; evidence turned into a written history; and the dated timeline that holds Fiji's whole story on one line. 5 lessons, in title: `first-steps/` (5 lessons). 19 placements.
2. **Level 2 — The Ancient Pacific and the First Fiji** — the deep past — how societies themselves grew from gathering to farming, the Lapita voyagers and their pottery, the great Pacific exchange community of islands, and the first iTaukei settlement of Fiji in the drua canoes of Lutunasobasoba and Degei. 4 lessons, in titles: `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson), `ancient-world/` (3 lessons). 7 placements.
3. **Level 3 — Contact, Trade, Mission and Kingdom** — the meeting centuries — European discovery, Fiji before Christianity, the sandalwood and beche-de-mer trades and the beachcombers, the missionaries converting chief by chief, and Ma'afu and Cakobau building the Kingdom of Bau. 5 lessons, in title: `modern-world/` (5 lessons). 9 placements.
4. **Level 4 — Cession, Colony and the Peoples Brought In** — the colonial settlement — the Deed of Cession of 10 October 1874, Gordon's native policy of land and chiefs, the indentured Indians and the blackbirded islanders, the early Chinese settlers and the Pacific migrant communities, how the communities lived together or apart, protest and nationalism, the law's road from chiefs to courts, and Rotuma's own settlement, mission war, cession of 1881 and war service. 10 lessons, in title: `modern-world/` (10 lessons). 17 placements.
5. **Level 5 — Independence, Coups and Constitutions** — the sovereign story — independence in 1970, the coups and constitutions of 1987 to 2014, the Presidents and Prime Ministers, the great leaders Sukuna, Mara and Patel, the political parties, elections and voting systems, multi-ethnic policy, and education and sport as agents of integration. 9 lessons, in title: `modern-world/` (9 lessons). 21 placements.
6. **Level 6 — Fiji and the World** — Fiji outward — Pacific colonisation weighed country by country, the United Nations agencies and their founders, famous lives, the causes of the Second World War, the industrial revolution, the games that unite nations, and Year 12's diplomacy worked country by country: the MSG and the nuclear-free Pacific, New Zealand, the two Chinas, Australia, India and the European Union. 12 lessons, in title: `modern-world/` (12 lessons). 14 placements.

**Totals: 45 lessons, 87 placements** — every lesson on a
**student book** (all 24 books in the set are student books;
there is no teacher guide in Fiji's filing, and no lesson
stands on teacher-guide evidence only — see ABOUT.md).
