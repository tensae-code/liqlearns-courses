# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

**Course:** History — Fiji · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Children of different Fijian, Indian and other cultures name their language, worship, celebrations and special things, discovering culture differs and is shared; fans, necklaces, puja lota and water vessels are shown as made things that carry a culture's values from the past into the present; iTaukei, Hindu and Muslim neighbours celebrate weddings, Diwali, Christmas, Eid, Mundan and hair-cutting and death ceremonies differently; across the Pacific sharks are revered, turtles hold a special place; whole communities herded fish with coconut-leaf uloa/pola, Niue districts shared their coast equally, men wove kupega nets from nettle fibre; Fiji's unique past is kept in the Fiji Museum and at heritage sites children are taught to preserve, linking the arrival story to places they can visit; heritage is defined as artefacts and intangible attributes inherited from past generations.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lesson 4 Cultural Backgrounds** — Children of different Fijian, Indian and other cultures name their language, worship, celebrations and special things, discovering culture differs and is shared.
2. **Cultural Values: artefacts and totems** — Fans, necklaces, puja lota and water vessels are shown as made things that carry a culture's values from the past into the present.
3. **1.8-1.10 Cultural events and religious festivals in our communities** — ITaukei, Hindu and Muslim neighbours celebrate weddings, Diwali, Christmas, Eid, Mundan and hair-cutting and death ceremonies differently, and children learn each rite carries a community's past.
4. **Sharks, turtles and whales in Pacific stories** — Across the Pacific sharks are revered, turtles hold a special place, and whales are told of as guardians and even ancestors — animal stories that carry a people's identity.
5. **Traditional uses of the reef and ocean; traditional fishing in Tuvalu and Niue** — Whole communities herded fish with coconut-leaf uloa/pola, Niue districts shared their coast equally, men wove kupega nets from nettle fibre — knowledge now contrasted with taking more than is needed today.
6. **Preserving Our Heritage Sites** — Fiji's unique past is kept in the Fiji Museum and at heritage sites children are taught to preserve, linking the arrival story to places they can visit.
7. **3.21 National Heritage** — Heritage is defined as artefacts and intangible attributes inherited from past generations; Fiji's examples — Levuka (world heritage), Koroniyatu and Bouma National Heritage Park — are researched by pupils site by site.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
