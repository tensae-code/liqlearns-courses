# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Fiji · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Time passes, some things stay (eating, planting, family) and some change (internet, buses, electricity); a class studies an old photograph of policemen directing traffic, spots what has changed; history is defined five ways; people who study the past are historians.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History Begins at Home — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Strand 2, 2.1-2.4 Continuity, Change, Progress and Regress** — Time passes, some things stay (eating, planting, family) and some change (internet, buses, electricity); good change is called progress, harmful change regress.
2. **2.6 Studying the Past** — A class studies an old photograph of policemen directing traffic, spots what has changed, and is told plainly: answering such questions about the past is history.
3. **2.1 What is History? Why is history important?** — History is defined five ways — everything that happened, a bridge from past to future, the record historians prepare, understanding one's identity, the record of changing lives — and a cartoon is mined for evidence of history.
4. **2.1 Understanding the Past: historians** — People who study the past are historians; they tell us the important events that happened, starting here with the origins of the world's peoples and how they colonised and settled their lands.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Fiji's books themselves work, telling the past as stories of people, places and turns.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to the part names in order and say each part's turn in one breath: who, where, what turned.
- Mixes up the people or the places → draw the story as a line of pictures, one per part, and label each with its place and its people.
- Go back to the one thing: Time passes, some things stay (eating, planting, family) and some change (internet, buses, electricity); a class studies an old photograph of policemen directing traffic, spots what has changed; history is defined five ways; people who study the past are historians. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
