# Video script — From Evidence to a Written History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. A child questions parents and elders about their heritage and records the answers, turning family memory into evidence; children arrange captioned archive photographs of the 1970 independence ceremony in order and turn the evidence into a fifty-word written account; in 1947 Thor Heyerdahl sails a raft from South America for 101 days to the Tuamotu islands to test his claim that Polynesia could have been settled from Peru; a Levuka photograph shows only Fijians, yet Chinese, African Americans, Hawaiians, Germans and Malaysans also lived there; each pupil researches and reports Fiji's relationship with the USA, or prepares a 150-word speech on UN military duty, choosing and defending a topic with the teacher.

Now the part called "Activity 3 My Cultural Heritage research questionnaire". A child questions parents and elders about their heritage and records the answers, turning family memory into evidence.

Now the part called "Independence photo sequence, National Archives of Fiji". Children arrange captioned archive photographs of the 1970 independence ceremony in order and turn the evidence into a fifty-word written account.

Now the part called "The Kon-Tiki Expedition". In 1947 Thor Heyerdahl sails a raft from South America for 101 days to the Tuamotu islands to test his claim that Polynesia could have been settled from Peru — a theory tested by re-enacting the voyage.

Now the part called "Photograph evidence: Levuka in the late 19th century". A Levuka photograph shows only Fijians, yet Chinese, African Americans, Hawaiians, Germans and Malaysans also lived there — pupils learn a photograph can hide as much as it shows.

Now the part called "Unit 7 The Individual Research Topic". Each pupil researches and reports Fiji's relationship with the USA, or prepares a 150-word speech on UN military duty, choosing and defending a topic with the teacher.

Remember it this way: follow the parts in order, and at each one ask — who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
