# About the History — Fiji course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Fiji's
Ministry of Education Social Studies, Social Science and
History books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Fiji inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as
a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the books come from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **six levels**: (1) First Steps —
history begins at home, in the family tree, the elders'
memory, heritage, evidence and the timeline; (2) the
Ancient Pacific and the First Fiji — Lapita, the voyaging
exchange community and the drua settlement; (3) Contact,
Trade, Mission and Kingdom — discovery, the old religion,
sandalwood and beche-de-mer, conversion, and Ma'afu and
Cakobau; (4) Cession, Colony and the Peoples Brought In —
1874, Gordon's native policy, indenture and blackbirding,
the Chinese and Pacific arrivals, ethnic relations, protest,
the law's origins, and Rotuma; (5) Independence, Coups and
Constitutions — 1970, 1987–2014, the leaders, parties,
elections, multi-ethnic policy, education and sport; and
(6) Fiji and the World — Pacific colonisation, the UN,
famous lives, the world war and the industrial revolution,
the games, and diplomacy country by country. All 45 lessons
stand on **student books** and count in the acceptance
reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From your own family tree to Fiji facing the world:*
  "History in Fiji begins at home: a child builds a family
  tree from the oldest living memory, asks the elders, and
  learns that heritage — languages, festivals, artefacts,
  sea stories — is the past still alive in the present.
  Then the story widens: Lapita voyagers cross the Pacific,
  islands trade and marry into one exchange community, and
  the first iTaukei land at Vuda in great drua canoes.
  Europeans sight, trade and wreck; sandalwood and
  beche-de-mer pull villages into world trade; missionaries
  convert chief by chief; Ma'afu and Cakobau contend, and
  in 1874 Cakobau signs Fiji over to Britain. Gordon rules
  through the chiefs and locks the land; girmit ships bring
  Indians, traders bring Chinese, and Fiji becomes plural.
  In 1970 Fiji is independent — and then come coups,
  constitutions rewritten, parties and elections, and
  leaders judged by one test: did they unite Fiji or divide
  it? Last, Fiji faces the world: the United Nations, the
  games that unite nations, and diplomacy — New Zealand,
  China, Australia, India, Europe — a small nation asking,
  of each: friend, big brother, or neighbour?"

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Book 1 (y3-social-studies):** History begins with the child's own family — Strand 2 "Time, Continuity and Change" is taught through a family-tree project, asking elders, and naming artefacts and totems, before any national story.
- **Book 2 (y4-social-studies):** the Time strand widens from family to community — continuity vs change in the child's own village, then Fiji's national leaders researched by the child.
- **Book 3 (esd-reader-1-people-and-the-sea):** the Pacific past is taught through the sea — voyaging and exchange between islands, and traditional fishing knowledge held by elders, set against present-day overfishing and plastic.
- **Book 4 (y5-social-studies):** "Understanding the Past" is Fiji's own arrival story told in order — first iTaukei, then Europeans, then the peoples brought or drawn in under colonial rule — each group judged by its contribution to Fiji today.
- **Book 5 (y6-social-science):** Fiji's whole history is given as dated timelines (pre-contact to 1874, 1874-1970, 1970-present) that the child reads as one national story, with archive photographs as evidence.
- **Book 6 (y7-social-science):** Year 7 places Fiji inside the Pacific — three peoples (Melanesian, Micronesian, Polynesian), Lapita evidence, then a country-by-country settlement-and-colonisation survey of its neighbours.
- **Book 7 (y8-social-science-exploring-the-world):** the focus widens from Pacific to world — world organisations, world peoples, famous lives and major world events, always brought back to Fiji examples (leprosy on Makogai, Pacific Games first held in Suva).
- **Book 8 (y9-social-science-rediscovering-society):** a full "History of Fiji" chapter inside Social Science, proceeding by periods and trades — pre-contact, discovery, sandalwood, beche-de-mer, missionaries, Ma'afu and Cakobau — with named local leaders' biographies in the civics chapter.
- **Book 9 (y10-social-science-global-perspectives):** Year 10 history is argument-driven — was Cession necessary, was Gordon's native policy protection or segregation, how did communal rolls delay independence — each question worked through documents and opposing views.
- **Book 10 (y11-history-ci-unit1-origin-of-fijis-people):** Year 11 History is a themed course, "Cultural Interaction and Integration" — Fiji's plural society is built up arrival by arrival, with source extracts, mapping and matching exercises.
- **Book 11 (y11-history-ci-unit2-before-independence):** the colonial century is judged by one question — did the communities actually mix, or only live side by side under British policy?
- **Book 12 (y11-history-ci-unit3-ethnic-policy):** policy itself is the story — how governments from 1970 to 2010 talked about, counted and tried to unite Fiji's ethnic communities.
- **Book 13 (y11-history-ci-unit4-religious-groups):** religion is treated as Fiji's deepest cultural interaction — ancient faith, then Christianity chief by chief, then Hinduism and Islam arriving with indenture.
- **Book 14 (y11-history-ci-unit5-leadership):** political leadership is periodised 1970-80, 1980-87, 1987-2006 and judged by one test — did the leader integrate Fiji or divide it?
- **Book 15 (y11-history-ci-unit6-education):** school history is Fiji history in miniature — separate mission and settler schools slowly become the multi-racial schools the government in 2010 orders renamed for integration.
- **Book 16 (y11-history-ci-unit7-sports):** sport is used as dated evidence — team photographs (cricket 1895 and 1948, the 1952 rugby team to Australia) show who was allowed to play with whom, era by era.
- **Book 17 (y11-history-ci-unit8-rotuma):** one small island is given a whole unit as a complete Fiji story in miniature — legend, contact, mission war, cession, war service and migration.
- **Book 18 (y11-history-gg-unit1-types-of-government):** government types are taught through Fiji's own passage from chiefdoms to democracy, not through foreign examples alone.
- **Book 19 (y11-history-gg-unit2-administration):** administration is taught as a dual system with a birthday — Gordon's Fijian Administration of 1876 running beside the general system into the independence era.
- **Book 20 (y11-history-gg-unit3-constitutional-development):** Fiji's constitutions are taught as a sequence of settlements and ruptures — 1970, the 1987 coups, 1990, the Reeves review and 1997.
- **Book 21 (y11-history-gg-unit4-voting-system):** voting is taught backwards from real Fiji election results — 1972, 1977 and the 2006 alternative-vote election.
- **Book 22 (y11-history-gg-unit5-political-parties):** parties are introduced through their founders' lives (lawyers, priests, campaigners) and a full past-elections results table.
- **Book 23 (y11-history-gg-unit6-legal-system):** the legal system is opened historically — chiefly decision-making first, colonial courts after — before turning to present institutions.
- **Book 24 (y12-history-diplomacy-and-fiji):** Year 12 History is one theme — diplomacy — worked country by country (New Zealand, China, Australia, India, the EU) almost entirely from primary sources: cartoons, news reports, home pages and treaties, each unit closing "friend, big brother, or neighbour?"
