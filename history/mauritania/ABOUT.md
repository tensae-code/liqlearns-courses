# About the History — Mauritania course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Mauritania's own Ministry of Education and Reform of the Education System / Institut Pédagogique National (IPN) books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Mauritania inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the story as Mauritania's own books present it (see CURRICULUM.md for the derivation): Level 1 — first steps and the ancient civilisations (7 lessons); Level 2 — Arabia, Islam rises and the caliphates (13 lessons); Level 3 — Mauritania and the Sudan kingdoms: Islam, the Almoravids and the Sahara trade (9 lessons); Level 4 — the emirates, colonisation, resistance and independence (11 lessons); Level 5 — Europe transformed and the world wars (16 lessons); Level 6 — the world after 1945: liberation, the Cold War and the organisations (18 lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The arc whole:* "First, what history itself is — how the past is dated and what founds a civilisation — then the ancient worlds: Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, and ancient Mauritania's own peoples of the western Sahara. Then Arabia and Islam: the Prophet, the Rashidun, the Umayyads, the Abbasids, al-Andalus and the Ottomans. Then home again: Islam enters Mauritania, the Almoravids rise from its desert, Ghana, Mali and Songhai rule the Sudan, and caravans carry salt and gold across the Sahara. Then the Hassaniya Arabs, the emirates, France's colonisation, the resistance, and independence in 1960. Then Europe's transformations — discoveries, Renaissance, revolutions, industry — and the two world wars. And last the world after 1945: liberation across Africa and Asia, the Cold War, and the organisations Mauritania sits in, from the United Nations to the Senegal River Organisation."
