## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 3: Ancient Mauritania — settlement and peoples

Farming and herding begin in the Neolithic (7000–2000 BC); as the Sahara dries, Garamantes, Bafour and Berbers migrate in — Sanhaja Berbers in the 3rd century AD settle the ponds and wadis, the Bafour hold Adrar from Azuggi behind trained dogs until the Almoravids come, Phoenician sailors trade down the Atlantic coast from 1500 BC, and Sudanic groups hold the south. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lesson 3: Ancient Mauritania — culture, economy and chiefs

Rock engravings at al-Ghallawiya in Adrar, engraved ostrich eggs and pottery mark the culture; the first Berbers bring the wheeled chariot, horse, metal, writing and finally the camel (1st century AD), which opens the desert to caravan trade Herodotus already called silent; with no strong states, local chiefs manage water, grazing and disputes. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
