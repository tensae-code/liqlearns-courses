# Video script — Ancient Mauritania: Peoples of the Western Sahara

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Farming and herding begin in the Neolithic (7000–2000 BC); as the Sahara dries, Garamantes, Bafour and Berbers migrate in — Sanhaja Berbers in the 3rd century AD settle the ponds and wadis, the Bafour hold Adrar from Azuggi behind trained dogs until the Almoravids come, Phoenician sailors trade down the Atlantic coast from 1500 BC, and Sudanic groups hold the south. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Rock engravings at al-Ghallawiya in Adrar, engraved ostrich eggs and pottery mark the culture; the first Berbers bring the wheeled chariot, horse, metal, writing and finally the camel (1st century AD), which opens the desert to caravan trade Herodotus already called silent; with no strong states, local chiefs manage water, grazing and disputes. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Before Islam the Sanhaja tribes lead desert society for long ages under their own chiefs — the test asks the pupil to name the tribes and their leaders.
