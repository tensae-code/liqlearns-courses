# Cambodia: The Angkor Era Rises

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Cambodia: The Angkor Era Rises** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 4 Lesson 5, The Angkor era

Jayavarman II (802–850), back from captivity in Java, is proclaimed universal monarch on Phnom Kulen in 802 and unites the two Chenlas; Yasovarman I founds the city of Angkor in the 9th century, and temple-building kings raise Angkor Wat over a land watered by great reservoirs. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 1, The Angkor Empire

The empire runs 802–1432 through six centuries of temple kings: Jayavarman II (802–850), Indravarman I (877–889; Bakong and a 3,000 × 8,000 m reservoir), Yasovarman I (889–900; Bakheng and the great eastern baray). Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 4, The kings of Angkor

The reign-by-reign account runs to Indravarman II (944–968), Jayavarman V (968–1001), then Suryavarman I (1010–1050) and Udayadityavarman II (1050–1066), builder of the Baphuon and the 8 × 2.2 km western reservoir. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
