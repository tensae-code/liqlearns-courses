# Cambodia: The Longvek Era

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Cambodia: The Longvek Era** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 2 Lesson 2, The Longvek era (16th century)

Ang Chan I (1516–1566) founds a new walled capital at Longvek and holds a still-large Cambodia (map, p. 132) between rising Siam and Vietnam. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 4 Lesson 3, The Longvek era (16th–17th centuries)

Ang Chan walls a new capital at Longvek (its moats and ramparts still traceable, photos p. 110); Siam presses from the west, and after Longvek's fall the court wanders again. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 3, The Longvek era in the 16th century

Longvek is a fortress-city of moats, five gates and a royal palace with pagodas, open to Portuguese and Japanese traders — built strong after Ang Chan's death (1526) against the Siamese threat. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
