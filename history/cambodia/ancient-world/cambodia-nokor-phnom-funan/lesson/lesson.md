# Cambodia: Nokor Phnom (Funan)

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Cambodia: Nokor Phnom (Funan)** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 4 Lesson 2 §2, The Nokor Phnom (Funan) era

The Indian prince Hun Tien (Kaundinya) sails in, marries the local queen and founds a kingdom of ports and canals whose farmers and traders grow rich on gold, silver and pearls. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 2, The Funan kingdom (50–550 CE)

Chinese envoys' reports (read by Paul Pelliot) describe a delta kingdom 3,000 li across (map, p. 120) whose ports grow rich on the India–China sea road. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 2, The ancient Funan kingdom

Chinese records measure Funan at 3,000 li (1 li = 576 m); its civilisation glitters at Oc Eo — gold jewellery, Vishnu and Buddha images (photos, p. 136). Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
