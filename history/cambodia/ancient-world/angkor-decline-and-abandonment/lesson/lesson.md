# Angkor: Decline and Abandonment

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Angkor: Decline and Abandonment** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 6, The weakening of the Angkor empire

Jayavarman VIII's long reign strains the empire; Cham wars (1262) and Mongol-Yuan pressure (embassies of the 1280s) close in — the causes of decline are weighed one by one. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 7, The last kings and the leaving of Angkor

Jayavarman VIII (1243–1296), Indravarman III (1295–1307, Zhou Daguan's host), the last inscription kings to Jayavarman IX (1327–1336) — then the court's slow road away from Angkor. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
