## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Appendix story 2, History of the Om Touk festival** — A grandfather tells Nari and Bonna how the boat-race festival began, so children inherit the story behind the races, the moon salutation and the flattened-rice offering.
2. **Ch. 4 Lesson 1, Old buildings** — The old National Assembly hall (opened 1920), the National Museum (built 1917) and high wooden stilt houses survive as evidence; children learn to read a building's age and to draw the stilt house their ancestors built.
3. **Ch. 4 Lesson 1, Models of ancient Khmer ornament** — Three mother-patterns — bodhi leaf, swan's tail, snail spiral — grow by rule into the carved borders (kbach) on temples, pagodas and silk; children learn the geometry step by step and draw it themselves.
4. **Ch. 4 Lesson 2, Preserving traditional musical instruments** — The mohori and pinpeat orchestras — roneat xylophones, tro fiddles, khim — survive only if each generation keeps, repairs and plays them.
5. **Ch. 4 Lesson 3, Old buildings** — Phnom Penh's Royal Palace, first built by Ponhea Yat in 1432 and rebuilt by Norodom in 1866 — eleven buildings inside walls 465 by 402 metres — is a national treasure children must know and protect.
6. **Ch. 4 Lesson 1, Traditional musical instruments** — From temple-wall carvings to today's yike theatre (photo: drums, tro fiddle, sralai oboe), Khmer instruments — skor drums, roneat, tro, khim — carry the sound of the ancestors and must be learned to be kept.
