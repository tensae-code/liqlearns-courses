# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Appendix story 2, History of the Om Touk festival”?
**Answer:** A grandfather tells Nari and Bonna how the boat-race festival began, so children inherit the story behind the races, the moon salutation and the flattened-rice offering. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Ch. 4 Lesson 1, Old buildings” end?
**Answer:** The old National Assembly hall (opened 1920), the National Museum (built 1917) and high wooden stilt houses survive as evidence; children learn to read a building's age and to draw the stilt house their ancestors built. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
