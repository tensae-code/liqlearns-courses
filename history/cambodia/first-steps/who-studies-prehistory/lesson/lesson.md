# Who Studies Prehistory

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Who Studies Prehistory** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ch. 4 Lesson 2 §1, Cambodia's prehistory and its sites

Before any writing, people left stone axes, pottery and bronze at Samrong Sen, Mlu Prei, Anlong Phdao and the Kampot caves — the dug-up things are the only witnesses. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 1, Prehistoric Cambodia

People have lived on this land since deep prehistory; Khmer origins are traced through Southeast Asia's caves and early villages toward the first kingdoms. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 1, Prehistoric Cambodia

From the first humans on earth to the Mon-Khmer peoples of this land — how archaeologists and linguists rebuild a past with no writing. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
