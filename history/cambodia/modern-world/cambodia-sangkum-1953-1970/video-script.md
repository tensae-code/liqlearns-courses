# Video script — Cambodia: The Sangkum, 1953–1970

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Sihanouk founds the Sangkum, builds schools, roads and ports, joins the UN in 1955, stands neutral at Bandung — and wins the Preah Vihear case. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
Geneva (1954) confirms independence; Sihanouk abdicates on 2 March 1955, founds the Sangkum on 22 March, and sweeps the September 1955 elections — the prince rules as head of a national movement. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
Sihanouk (born 31 October 1922; photo, p. 112) wins independence on 9 November 1953, abdicates in March 1955, founds the Sangkum, and takes 99.80% in the September 1955 elections. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Cambodia.
