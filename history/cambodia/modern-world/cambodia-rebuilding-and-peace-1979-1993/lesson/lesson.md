# Cambodia: Rebuilding and Peace, 1979–1993

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Cambodia: Rebuilding and Peace, 1979–1993** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 5, People's Republic of Kampuchea and State of Cambodia (1979–1993)

Survivors rebuild from zero; the Paris Agreements of 23 October 1991 and UNTAC bring elections, and Angkor joins the World Heritage List in 1992. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 2 Lesson 5, The People's Republic of Kampuchea and the State of Cambodia (1979–1993)

Cadres who fled the purges (Heng Samrin, photo) found a salvation front on 2 December 1978; victory on 7 January 1979 starts the rebuilding that ends with the Paris Agreements and UNTAC. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 5, The People's Republic of Kampuchea and the State of Cambodia (1979–1991) and the Kingdom of Cambodia 1993

Survivors of the purges (Heng Samrin, photo) build the resistance that wins on 7 January 1979; the lesson runs through war and embargo to the Paris Agreements and the restored kingdom. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
