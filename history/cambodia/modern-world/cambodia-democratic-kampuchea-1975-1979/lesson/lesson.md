# Cambodia: Democratic Kampuchea, 1975–1979

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Cambodia: Democratic Kampuchea, 1975–1979** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 4, Democratic Kampuchea (1975–1979)

The Khmer Rouge take Phnom Penh, empty the cities and impose a regime whose genocide devastates Cambodia before it falls in January 1979. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ch. 2 Lesson 4, Democratic Kampuchea (1975–1979)

On 17 April 1975 the Khmer Rouge enter Phnom Penh and empty it within days; their programme — evacuate the cities, abolish money, markets and religion — turns the country into a prison camp. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — History Ch. 2 Lesson 4, The Democratic Kampuchea regime (1975–1979)

On 17 April 1975 the Khmer Rouge empty Phnom Penh in three days; Angkar's programme — evacuate the cities, abolish money and markets, defrock the monks, execute the old leaders — is listed point by point. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
