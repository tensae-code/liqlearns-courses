# Video script — Cambodia under the French Protectorate, 1863–1953

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Pressed by Siam and Vietnam, Cambodia accepts French protection in 1863; France shields the territory and rebuilds the state, yet rules it — until Sihanouk's crusade wins full independence in 1953. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
King Ang Duong (1841–1860), squeezed between Siam and Vietnam, looks for a protector; his successors sign with France in 1863, and ninety years of protection end with Sihanouk's independence crusade. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
Ang Duong (1841–1860), pressed by Siam and Vietnam, writes to Napoleon III (1854); he dies on 19 October 1860, and Norodom (portrait, r. 1861–1904) signs the protectorate on 11 August 1863. Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Cambodia.
