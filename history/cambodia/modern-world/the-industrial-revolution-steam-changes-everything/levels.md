# The Industrial Revolution: Steam Changes Everything — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — England's steam factories (chapter frontispiece) spread to France and Germany (steel by 1870), remake farming (threshing, rye and barley rotations), swell and shift populations (French regional table, 1851–1999), and put women and children into the mills. — in their own words, give one real example from the lesson, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
