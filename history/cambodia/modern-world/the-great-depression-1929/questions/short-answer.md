# The Great Depression, 1929 — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Ch. 1 Lesson 11, The United States (1919–1939)”?
**Answer:** Prosperity shadowed by the Red Scare — Palmer's raids, Sacco and Vanzetti, the Scopes trial — then the 1929 crash turns the richest country into the Depression's epicentre. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Ch. 1 Lesson 1, The United States (1860–1990)” end?
**Answer:** From independence (4 July 1776) and the 1787 constitution through continental growth to the 1929 crisis and its worldwide consequences — the long arc of the republic the lesson keeps returning to. Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
