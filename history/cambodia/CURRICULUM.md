# History — Cambodia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Cambodia inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Cambodia's books in hand run from the kindergarten teacher-facing core book to the Grade 12 baccalaureate year — the primary Social Studies books (Grades 4–6), the secondary Social Studies books (Grades 7–10), the History books for Grades 11 and 12, and the Grade 12 History Q&A exam-prep compilation whose six lessons mirror the official Grade 12 textbook. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder below follows the story as Cambodia's own books present it, in its own eras: first steps and the deep past, the ancient world and Cambodia's first kingdoms, Angkor's civilisation, its neighbours and the capitals after Angkor, the wider world opening to 1914, the world wars, and the world after 1945 closing with Cambodia's own modern regimes told one by one. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Cambodia's own Ministry of Education, Youth and Sport (MoEYS) books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: Heritage, Evidence and Prehistory** — the historian's craft and the deep past first — what history is, who studies prehistory, how the past is dated, how stone tools give way to farming, and heritage itself: the old buildings, instruments, ornaments and festival stories a child must learn to keep — 5 lessons.
2. **The Ancient World and Cambodia's First Kingdoms** — the ancient river civilisations of Egypt, Mesopotamia, the Hebrews, Phoenicia, Greece, Rome and the Maya, the first worlds of China, India, Java and the Mon — and at home Funan (Nokor Phnom) and Chenla, the rise of Angkor, Jayavarman VII, and China's Qin and Han and the Mongol storm that closes the ancient arc — 17 lessons.
3. **Angkor's Civilisation, Its Neighbours and the Capitals after Angkor** — Angkor's civilisation read through its own inscriptions, the neighbour kingdoms a Cambodian must know — Champa, Dai Viet, Ayutthaya, Lan Xang and Korea — feudal Europe beside them, then Angkor's decline and the capitals that follow: Chaktomuk under Ponhea Yat, Longvek, Oudong, and Khmer civilisation after Angkor — 12 lessons.
4. **The Wider World Opens: Voyages, Revolutions and Empires to 1914** — the sea voyages, England's, France's and America's revolutions, Islam in the Malay world, steam and the factory ideologies, Europe's colonisation of Asia, the Middle East and Mughal India mapped, and the great powers to 1914 — the United States stitched by railways, China and Japan facing the West, Victoria's empire, Bismarck's Germany, the Third Republic and Russia cracking toward 1917 — 16 lessons.
5. **The World Wars and the World between Them** — the First World War — its causes, its course, and Versailles and the League — then the world between the wars: the Depression, China and Japan moving to civil war and militarism, Fascism and Nazism compared, France and Britain draining, and the road into the Second World War, closing with the United Nations founded out of it — 10 lessons.
6. **The World after 1945 and Cambodia's Own Modern Story** — the postwar world state by state — Britain and France remade, the two Germanys, Italy, the Cold War, Czechoslovakia, Poland and Yugoslavia, the world's shared problems and globalisation — then Southeast Asia decolonised (Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, the Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand), Turkey and Iran, Israel, New Zealand and Australia, ASEAN — and Cambodia's own story whole: the French protectorate, independence in 1953, the Sangkum, the Khmer Republic and civil war, Democratic Kampuchea, rebuilding and peace, and the Second Kingdom, closing with the baccalaureate assessment that rehearses it all — 31 lessons.

**Totals: 91 lessons** (Level 1: 5 · Level 2: 17 · Level 3: 12 · Level 4: 16 · Level 5: 10 · Level 6: 31 — 135 placements stocked; 90 lessons stand on student books, so those lessons are taught in 1 country (Cambodia); 1 lesson — Cambodia: Independence, 1953 — stands on teacher-guide evidence only (the KG Social Studies core book, a teacher-facing core textbook), acceptance 0, labelled; 28 lessons carry sub lessons).
