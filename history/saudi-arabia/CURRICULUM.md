# History — Saudi Arabia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Saudi Arabia inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Saudi Arabia's book in hand is one
book — the Grade 4 Social Studies student book, unabridged —
and its own way, as filed, is the ladder: history enters as
method first — what history is, its sources, cause and
effect, timelines — practised on Saudi examples, and only
then as content: the nation's own days (Founding Day 1727,
National Day 1932, taught inside the Citizenship unit as
national symbols, and the story of the brave courier of the
unification), the Arabian Peninsula as the Arabs' homeland
and cradle of civilisations, the peninsula before Islam, the
two holy cities, the Kingdom's antiquities, and the prophets'
stories as the land's sacred past. One book, one level — the
ladder stops where the book in hand stops, not because Saudi
Arabia teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Social Studies book — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list, the combined-book contents map, and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps into Saudi Arabia's Past** — the book's own
   order: first the method (what history is; from evidence to
   a written history; cause and effect in history; dating the
   past on a timeline of the Kingdom's own dates); then the
   nation (the unification under Abdulaziz told through the
   brave courier's secret letter; Founding Day 1727 and
   National Day 1932, with Flag Day); then the deep past (the
   peninsula as cradle of civilisations; the peninsula before
   Islam — tribes, values and caravans; the rise and expansion
   of Islam through Makkah and Madinah); then heritage (the
   Kingdom's antiquities found on the map); and last the
   prophets, whose journeys cross the child's own map.
   11 lessons, in three titles: `first-steps/` (5 lessons),
   `modern-world/` (2 lessons), `ancient-world/` (4 lessons),
   in the placements file's order.

**Totals: 11 lessons** (Level 1: 11 — all from one student book).
