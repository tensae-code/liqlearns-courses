# The Arabian Peninsula, Cradle of Civilisations

## Do it first

Scratch words into a rock, and the rock may still be telling them thousands of years later. The peoples of this lesson left few books — but they left rocks. Say out loud: "A land can be old long before its famous days." This lesson walks into that old land.

The Arabian Peninsula is the Arabs' homeland since antiquity — and not an empty one. The book calls it a cradle of civilisations: peoples and kingdoms rose here, one after another, long ages before Islam.

Their names are the lesson's roll call: 'Ad and Thamud, peoples of the oldest stories; Madyan; al-Maqar; Dadan; the Nabataeans, who carved their cities in rock; and Kinda. Kingdom followed kingdom across the deserts and the watered valleys, and each vanished in its turn.

The peninsula's place made it a crossing: a bridge between Egypt on one side and Iraq on the other, so goods, ideas and travellers passed through it. And it gave the world something that never vanished: here the Arabic language was born.

These ancestors wrote their history the durable way — in rock inscriptions, carved into stone across the land. Long before paper, the peninsula's only books were its rocks, and the rocks are still there to be read.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: a "cradle of civilisations" means a place where civilisations began and grew, the way a cradle is where a baby begins — the land rocked many peoples, one after another.

Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Arabian Peninsula, cradle of civilisations, 'Ad, Thamud, Madyan, al-Maqar, Dadan, Nabataeans, Kinda, rock inscriptions, Arabic language. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
