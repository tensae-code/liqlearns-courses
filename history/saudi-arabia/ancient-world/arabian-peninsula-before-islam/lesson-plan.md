# Lesson plan — The Arabian Peninsula before Islam

**Course:** History — Saudi Arabia · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Arabian Peninsula before Islam
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps into Saudi Arabia's Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before Islam the peninsula was a tribal land with no single state — lost peoples like Tasm and Jadis, wars between tribes and towns, strong values of courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour, and a rich caravan economy centred on Makkah.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps into Saudi Arabia's Past — it stands in the book's own order in this course (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks no state means nothing was shared → the tribes shared a code (courage, generosity, protecting the neighbour) and shared trade routes, even while they warred and had no single ruler.
- Go back to the one thing: before Islam the peninsula was a tribal land with no single state — lost peoples like Tasm and Jadis, wars between tribes and towns, strong values of courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour, and a rich caravan economy centred on Makkah. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
