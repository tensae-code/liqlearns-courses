# Video script — The Arabian Peninsula before Islam

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Before islam the peninsula was a tribal land with no single state — lost peoples like tasm and jadis, wars between tribes and towns, strong values of courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour, and a rich caravan economy centred on makkah.

Before Islam, the peninsula held peoples already lost to memory — Tasm and Jadis, whose names survive as warnings and wonders — and a living land of tribes and towns with no single state over them. Tribes warred with tribes, towns with towns; unity was the one thing the land did not have.

Yet the land had a code. Its praised values were courage in danger, generosity to the guest, and protecting the neighbour who had claimed your protection. A person's honour was measured by those three.

And the land was busy and rich. Makkah stood at the meeting point of the caravan routes: the winter journey and the summer journey carried trade south and north, and goods from far China passed along the Silk Road routes toward Europe. In Makkah itself, Khadija bint Khuwaylid directed her caravans to Syria and to Yemen — a merchant of the city, sending goods across the same deserts the tribes fought over. Elsewhere there was farming in Yathrib, Taif and Yamama, and herding across the open land.

So the book's picture is whole: a land of rival tribes and merchant cities, growing rich on caravans — while waiting for a unity it did not yet have.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
