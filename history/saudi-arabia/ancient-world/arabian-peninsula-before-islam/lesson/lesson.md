# The Arabian Peninsula before Islam

## Do it first

Imagine a land of many towns and tribes, each guarding its own ground, with rich caravans crossing between them — and no single ruler over it all. Say out loud: "Rich and brave, but not yet one." That is the land in this lesson.

Before Islam, the peninsula held peoples already lost to memory — Tasm and Jadis, whose names survive as warnings and wonders — and a living land of tribes and towns with no single state over them. Tribes warred with tribes, towns with towns; unity was the one thing the land did not have.

Yet the land had a code. Its praised values were courage in danger, generosity to the guest, and protecting the neighbour who had claimed your protection. A person's honour was measured by those three.

And the land was busy and rich. Makkah stood at the meeting point of the caravan routes: the winter journey and the summer journey carried trade south and north, and goods from far China passed along the Silk Road routes toward Europe. In Makkah itself, Khadija bint Khuwaylid directed her caravans to Syria and to Yemen — a merchant of the city, sending goods across the same deserts the tribes fought over. Elsewhere there was farming in Yathrib, Taif and Yamama, and herding across the open land.

So the book's picture is whole: a land of rival tribes and merchant cities, growing rich on caravans — while waiting for a unity it did not yet have.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: hold the lesson in one contrast — no single state, yet one shared code of courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour. The values were already shared before the land was.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: before Islam the peninsula was a tribal land with no single state — lost peoples like Tasm and Jadis, wars between tribes and towns, strong values of courage, generosity and protecting the neighbour, and a rich caravan economy centred on Makkah. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Tasm, Jadis, tribes, courage, generosity, protecting the neighbour, Makkah, caravans, Khadija bint Khuwaylid, Yathrib, Taif, Yamama. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
