# The Rise and Expansion of Islam

## Do it first

A compass always points somewhere. For a whole world faith, the pointing is toward one city in this lesson's own country. Say out loud: "Two cities can become the centre of a world." This lesson tells how.

The book teaches this lesson through the two cities Saudi children know best — and it tells their story as the land's own story.

Makkah is the first city: the city of the Kaaba, the qibla of the Muslims — the direction faced in prayer. Whatever else changed around it, the Kaaba stood in Makkah, and the city's place in the faith begins there.

Madinah is the second city. To Madinah the Prophet Muhammad migrated — the Hijra — leaving the Makkah of the caravans for the town that received him. In Madinah he lived and spread Islam; there stands the Prophet's Mosque, and there is his tomb. From Madinah the new faith grew outward.

And between the two cities' story sits one mosque with the turning in its name: Masjid al-Qiblatain, the Mosque of the Two Qiblas, where the direction of prayer turned to the Kaaba in Makkah — the moment the compass of the faith settled on Makkah for good.

Two cities in one small country thus became the compass points of a world faith: one the direction of prayer, the other the city of the Prophet's migration and mosque.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: "qibla" is the direction faced in prayer — when the lesson calls Makkah the qibla of the Muslims, it means the whole praying world turns toward the Kaaba there.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Makkah and Madinah — the Kaaba's city, and the city to which the Prophet Muhammad migrated and from which Islam spread — became the compass points of a world faith, with Masjid al-Qiblatain marking where the direction of prayer turned to the Kaaba. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Makkah, Kaaba, qibla, Madinah, Prophet Muhammad, migration, Prophet's Mosque, Masjid al-Qiblatain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
