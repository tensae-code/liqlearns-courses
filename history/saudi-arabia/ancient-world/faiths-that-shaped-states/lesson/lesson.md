# Faiths That Shaped States

## Do it first

Some stories are set in faraway lands you will never see. The stories in this lesson are set, in part, on your own ground — a valley near Tabuk, two hills in Makkah. Say out loud: "The oldest stories and my own map can be the same map." Walk into them.

The book closes its history with the prophets — told as the land's sacred past, in the order the book tells them. These are the stories as the book presents them.

It begins with Adam, the first man, and Noah — called the second father of mankind, because humanity begins again through him after the flood.

Then come the Messengers of Strong Resolve. Ibrahim was born in Babylon; he smashed his people's idols, showing their powerlessness. His wife Hajar, left in the valley of Makkah with her child, ran between the hills of Safa and Marwa seeking water — a search re-enacted at every Hajj to this day, on the child's own home ground.

Musa was born in Egypt and cast into the river as a baby; grown, he fled Pharaoh and came to Madyan — which the book places near Tabuk, in the north of the child's own country — before returning to confront Pharaoh. And Isa is told with his miracles, the signs that marked his life.

So the unit's turn is the map itself: Babylon and Egypt are far, but Madyan is in Tabuk and Hajar's hills are in Makkah. Sacred story and home ground are one — and that is how the book ends its history: the oldest stories, standing on the child's own soil.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: when a story names a place, find it on the map before you move on — this whole lesson is built on the discovery that the stories happened on real, findable ground.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the prophets' stories cross the child's own map — Adam and Noah, and the Messengers of Strong Resolve: Ibrahim who smashed his people's idols, Musa who fled Pharaoh to Madyan near Tabuk, and Isa and his miracles — with Hajar's search for water between Safa and Marwa re-enacted at every Hajj. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Adam, Noah, Messengers of Strong Resolve, Ibrahim, Hajar, Safa, Marwa, Musa, Madyan, Tabuk, Isa. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose, and say the lesson's one thing aloud in your own words.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: set down its key things — Adam, Noah, Messengers of Strong Resolve, Ibrahim, Hajar, Safa, Marwa, Musa, Madyan, Tabuk, Isa — in their order.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
