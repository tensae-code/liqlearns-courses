# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Saudi Arabia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps into Saudi Arabia's Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before history can be written, it must be proved — from primary sources (antiquities and monuments, accounts by people who saw the event, books written at the time, photographs, letters and documents) rather than secondary sources, and false news must be refused.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps into Saudi Arabia's Past — it stands in the book's own order in this course (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: before history can be written, it must be proved — from primary sources (antiquities and monuments, accounts by people who saw the event, books written at the time, photographs, letters and documents) rather than secondary sources, and false news must be refused.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this book's lessons, from the land and public life of Saudi Arabia they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes primary and secondary sources up → ask one question: was it made at the time, by someone who was there? If yes, it is primary; if it was written later from other sources, it is secondary.
- Go back to the one thing: before history can be written, it must be proved — from primary sources (antiquities and monuments, accounts by people who saw the event, books written at the time, photographs, letters and documents) rather than secondary sources, and false news must be refused. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
