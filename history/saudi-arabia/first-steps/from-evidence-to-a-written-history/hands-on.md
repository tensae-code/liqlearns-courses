# Hands-on — From Evidence to a Written History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine you must prove that a journey really happened, to someone who says it never did. What would you show them — a thing left behind, a letter, a photograph, a person who was there? Say out loud: "A claim about the past needs proof." This lesson names the proofs. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the lesson's key things — primary sources, secondary sources, antiquities, documents, false news — and say each one aloud as you place it, in the order the lesson gives them.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you meet an example of it, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
