# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of something that happened in your family before you were born — a journey, a wedding, a house built. You were not there, yet you can know about it. Say out loud: "Things that happened before I was born can still be known, and they still matter." This lesson shows how. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the lesson's key things — history, past events, historian, when, what, how, who, why, what came of it — and say each one aloud as you place it, in the order the lesson gives them.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you meet an example of it, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
