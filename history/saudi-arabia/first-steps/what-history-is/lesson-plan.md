# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Saudi Arabia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps into Saudi Arabia's Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** history is the study of past events — asking when it happened, what happened, how, who took part and why, and interpreting the events to show their effect on the present and the future.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps into Saudi Arabia's Past — it stands in the book's own order in this course (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: history is the study of past events — asking when it happened, what happened, how, who took part and why, and interpreting the events to show their effect on the present and the future.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this book's lessons, from the land and public life of Saudi Arabia they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Saudi Arabia and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks history means any old story → a story becomes history when it is about real events and we ask the historian's questions of it: when, what, how, who, why, and what came of it.
- Go back to the one thing: history is the study of past events — asking when it happened, what happened, how, who took part and why, and interpreting the events to show their effect on the present and the future. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
