# Video script — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The kingdom's antiquities — the rajajil columns at sakaka, al-hijr at al-ula, al-ukhdud at najran and juwatha mosque at al-ahsa — are national treasure a child must be able to find on the map, guarded by the national museum in riyadh and its exhibitions.

Heritage is what the past leaves standing — and this lesson teaches it the practical way: by sending the child to the map.

Four sites of the Kingdom are the lesson's anchors. At Sakaka stand the Rajajil columns — standing stones raised in deep antiquity. At al-Ula is al-Hijr, the desert city carved in rock by the Nabataeans of the earlier lessons. At Najran is al-Ukhdud, the ancient site of the south. And at al-Ahsa stands Juwatha Mosque, among the oldest mosques of the land. A pupil learns each name with its place: Sakaka, al-Ula, Najran, al-Ahsa — heritage you can point to is heritage you can protect.

And the guarding has a home. The National Museum in Riyadh keeps the country's artefacts, and exhibitions carry them to the people — so the stones in the desert and the objects in the museum are one treasure, held for every child of the Kingdom.

That is why heritage matters in this book: the past of the earlier lessons is not only told. It is still standing, it can be found on the map, and it is the nation's duty — and its children's — to know where it is.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
