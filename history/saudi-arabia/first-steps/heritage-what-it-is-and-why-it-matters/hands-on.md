# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Somewhere in your country there are stones older than every living person, still standing where people of long ago left them. Could you find them on a map? Say out loud: "Old places are treasure, and treasure must be found and guarded." This lesson puts four of them on your map. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw or lay out the lesson's key things — Rajajil, Sakaka, al-Hijr, al-Ula, al-Ukhdud, Najran, Juwatha Mosque, al-Ahsa, National Museum, Riyadh — and say each one aloud as you place it, in the order the lesson gives them.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you meet an example of it, note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
