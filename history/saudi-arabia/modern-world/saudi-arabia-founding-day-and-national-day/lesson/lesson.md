# Saudi Arabia's Founding Day and National Day

## Do it first

Your family may keep two special days: the day something began, and the day it became complete. A country can keep two such days too. Say out loud: "One day for the beginning, one day for the uniting." This lesson gives you both days.

Children learn their country's two great days inside the lesson on national symbols — because the days and the symbols belong together: both tell the nation who it is.

The earlier day is Founding Day. In 1727 — 1139هـ — Imam Muhammad bin Saud founded the Saudi state. The state began in a small town, and its roots run more than 300 years deep: long before there was a kingdom, there was this beginning.

The later day is National Day. In 1932 — 1351هـ — King Abdulaziz unified the country: the lands were brought together as the one Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Two centuries stand between the founding and the unification, and the second day completes what the first began.

And there is a third day, for the nation's face: Flag Day, marking when King Abdulaziz approved the form of the flag — the flag the unified country would fly.

So the child holds the whole shape: a state founded in 1727, a country unified in 1932, a flag approved by the unifier — and both great days marked every year.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: keep the two days apart with one sentence each — 1727 founded the state; 1932 unified the kingdom. Beginning first, completion second.
