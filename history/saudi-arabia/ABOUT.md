# About the History — Saudi Arabia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Social Studies book for Grade 4. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Saudi Arabia inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **one level**:

1. **Level 1 — first steps into Saudi Arabia's past.** The
   Grade 4 book teaches history method first — what history
   is, sources, cause and effect, timelines — practised on
   Saudi examples, and only then as content: the nation's
   days and unification story, the Arabian Peninsula as the
   Arabs' homeland and cradle of civilisations, the
   peninsula before Islam, the two holy cities, the
   Kingdom's antiquities, and the prophets' stories as the
   land's sacred past. The course's 11 lessons all stand on
   a **student book** and count in the acceptance reckoning
   below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *First steps into Saudi Arabia's past:* "First, learn to
  ask like a historian: when, what, how, who, why, and what

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **One book only, carried exactly as filed:** this course
  is built from ONE book — Saudi Arabia's Grade 4 Social
  Studies student book (COMPLETE as filed). No other Saudi
  Arabia book is in hand for this course, so the course
  holds only what that one book teaches. The ladder stops
  at Level 1 because the book in hand stops there — not
  because Saudi Arabia teaches nothing more. Library debt:
  none noticed for Saudi Arabia in this set, as filed —
  the single distinct book was in the repo and readable.
- **Edition year discrepancy, as filed:** the record title
  gives the year as 1448هـ; the printed copyright page of
  the held copy reads 1446هـ. The held copy is the 1446
  edition (ISBN 978-603-8576-98-8), and this course cites
  the held edition.
- **Combined-book scope:** the book is a combined Social
  Studies book. Only its history content is placed. Its
  Citizenship units (Unit 1, apart from the Founding/
  National Day history inside Lesson 4, and Unit 6), its
  Geography units (Unit 3 and Unit 5) and its Economics
  unit (Unit 4) are named in the contents map above and
  feed no history lesson — they are not lost; they belong
  to the civics, geography and economics strands, not to
  this course.
- **Religious-history stories as told by the book:** the
  prophets unit (Unit 8) and the Qibla lesson (Unit 7,
  Lesson 27) are taught in this course as plot summaries
  of the stories as the book tells them, in our own words
  — no textbook text is transcribed.
- **UNREAD books:** none named — no Saudi Arabia book
  stands in the record as held-but-unread for this course.
  The gap is books not yet in hand at all (see the
  one-book note above), not books unread.
