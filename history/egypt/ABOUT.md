# About the History — Egypt course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Egypt's own Ministry of Education and Technical Education student books, in Arabic. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Egypt inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Legends and religious-history stories are told as the books tell them, as plot summaries in our own words.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the books' own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How Egypt Learns Its Past.** The historian's craft first, exactly as Egypt's own books open it — what history and civilisation are, how evidence becomes a written history, fact against opinion and rumour, history hunted in your own city, the landmarks you must guard, and the long story of the map itself.
2. **Level 2 — Ancient Egypt and Its African Neighbours.** The Nile kingdom whole — its rise, its three kingdom ages and the life its people lived — set inside Africa beside Kush, Axum and Carthage, as Egypt's Grade 7 book itself sets it.
3. **Level 3 — The Ancient World Around Egypt.** Every ancient civilisation Egypt's books measure against Egypt herself — Iraq, Yemen, India, China, Phoenicia, Greece and Rome — then Egypt under Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome, and the Americas' Maya, Inca and Aztec, whose pyramids and glyphs the books compare with pharaonic Egypt.
4. **Level 4 — Arabia, Islam and Medieval Egypt.** Arabia before Islam, the Prophet and Medina, the caliphs and the conquests, the caliphate's own machinery and gifts — then Egypt's long medieval story from Amr's conquest through the Tulunids, Fatimids, Ayyubids and the Mamluks of Ain Jalut, with Islam's spread across Africa.
5. **Level 5 — Egypt Between Empires: Ottomans, France and Muhammad Ali.** The Mamluks fall to the Ottomans, Napoleon lands and wakes a nation, and Muhammad Ali and his house build the modern state and carry Egypt's flag into Sudan — with the flag's own long story, from ancient battle standards to today's tricolour.
6. **Level 6 — Occupation, Liberation and the Modern Arab World.** Urabi and the occupation, the road through 1919 and 1936 to July 1952, the October War, the two revolutions of the learners' own lifetimes and Egypt's parliamentary road — widened, as the books widen it, to the voyages that opened the modern world, Europe's scramble for Africa and the Arab lands, Africa's liberation, Palestine, and Egypt among Africa's and the Arabs' own organisations.

All 57 of the 57 lessons stand on **student books** and count in the acceptance reckoning below; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *One land at the heart of the world, told in its own books' order:* "Begin in your own street: learn what a fact is, sort evidence from rumour, and walk your city's past in its landmarks. Then stand on the Nile five thousand years ago and watch villages become the first great kingdom on earth. Measure Iraq, Greece, Rome and the far Americas against Egypt. Follow the caravans out of Arabia, sit in Baghdad and Cairo, and hold the line at Ain Jalut. Watch the Mamluks fall, Napoleon land, and Muhammad Ali build a new Egypt — then fight through occupation to 1952, cross the Canal in October 1973, and stand in Tahrir Square in your own lifetime. That is Egypt's own story, from its own school books, and it is yours."
