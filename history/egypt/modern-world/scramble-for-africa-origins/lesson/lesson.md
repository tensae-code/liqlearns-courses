# The Scramble for Africa: Origins

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — European colonial expansion in Africa

After the Industrial Revolution Europe hunts raw materials, markets, investment outlets, land for its surplus people, national glory and military bases. It creeps in behind "humanitarian" slogans, trading companies, loans and capitulations, then occupies — Britain in Egypt in 1882 — and rules by crushing freedoms, exiling leaders such as Saad Zaghloul and Kwame Nkrumah, divide-and-rule and racial discrimination.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 5: Colonial expansion in the Arab countries before the First World War, until independence

England wraps the Gulf in treaties — Oman in 1798, Perim in 1799, the General Peace of 1820 against piracy and the slave trade, protectorates over Bahrain's rulers, Muscat in 1891 and Kuwait in 1899 — and seizes Aden in 1839 as Muhammad Ali's shadow falls on Arabia. France takes Algiers in 1830 over an unpaid wheat debt, breaks Amir Abd al-Qadir's fifteen-year resistance by 1847, confiscates the land for settlers and makes Muslim Algerians second-class in their own country.
