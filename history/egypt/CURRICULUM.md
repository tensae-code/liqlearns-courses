# History — Egypt course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Egypt inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Egypt's books in hand are the Ministry of Education and Technical Education's Social Studies and History student books, in Arabic, from Grade 4 Primary to Grade 12 Secondary. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the eras as Egypt's own books present them: the historian's craft first, learned the way the youngest books teach it — from the child's own home, street and city outward (Level 1); Pharaonic Egypt whole, set inside Africa as the Grade 7 book sets it (Level 2); the ancient world measured against Egypt, book by book (Level 3); Arabia, Islam and Egypt's medieval centuries (Level 4); the Ottoman fall, the French campaign and Muhammad Ali's modern state (Level 5); and occupation, liberation and the modern Arab world down to the revolutions of the learners' own lifetimes (Level 6). The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Egypt's own Ministry of Education and Technical Education student books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: How Egypt Learns Its Past** — the historian's craft first, exactly as Egypt's own books open it — what history and civilisation are, how evidence becomes a written history, fact against opinion and rumour, history hunted in your own city, the landmarks you must guard, and the long story of the map itself — 7 lessons.

2. **Ancient Egypt and Its African Neighbours** — the Nile kingdom whole — its rise, its three kingdom ages and the life its people lived — set inside Africa beside Kush, Axum and Carthage, as Egypt's Grade 7 book itself sets it — 5 lessons.

3. **The Ancient World Around Egypt** — every ancient civilisation Egypt's books measure against Egypt herself — Iraq, Yemen, India, China, Phoenicia, Greece and Rome — then Egypt under Alexander, the Ptolemies and Rome, and the Americas' Maya, Inca and Aztec, whose pyramids and glyphs the books compare with pharaonic Egypt — 12 lessons.

4. **Arabia, Islam and Medieval Egypt** — Arabia before Islam, the Prophet and Medina, the caliphs and the conquests, the caliphate's own machinery and gifts — then Egypt's long medieval story from Amr's conquest through the Tulunids, Fatimids, Ayyubids and the Mamluks of Ain Jalut, with Islam's spread across Africa — 15 lessons.

5. **Egypt Between Empires: Ottomans, France and Muhammad Ali** — the Mamluks fall to the Ottomans, Napoleon lands and wakes a nation, and Muhammad Ali and his house build the modern state and carry Egypt's flag into Sudan — with the flag's own long story, from ancient battle standards to today's tricolour — 5 lessons.

6. **Occupation, Liberation and the Modern Arab World** — Urabi and the occupation, the road through 1919 and 1936 to July 1952, the October War, the two revolutions of the learners' own lifetimes and Egypt's parliamentary road — widened, as the books widen it, to the voyages that opened the modern world, Europe's scramble for Africa and the Arab lands, Africa's liberation, Palestine, and Egypt among Africa's and the Arabs' own organisations — 13 lessons.

**Totals: 57 lessons** (Level 1: 7 · Level 2: 5 · Level 3: 12 · Level 4: 15 · Level 5: 5 · Level 6: 13 — 107 placements stocked, all from student books; 24 lessons carry sub lessons; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only — every lesson stands on a student book, as filed in ABOUT.md).
