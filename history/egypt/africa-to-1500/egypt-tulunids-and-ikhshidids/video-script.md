# Video script — Egypt: The Tulunids and Ikhshidids

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Ahmad ibn Tulun, the Turkish officer who came as a deputy in 868, gathers Egypt's reins, wins her people's love with justice and generosity, and stretches his state into the Levant as its shield.

Ahmad ibn Tulun, the Turkish officer who came as a deputy in 868, gathers Egypt's reins, wins her people's love with justice and generosity, and stretches his state into the Levant as its shield. His son Khumarawayh defeats the caliphate's armies and wins a thirty-year grant of Egypt and the Levant — the first independent Egyptian state of the Islamic age, followed by Muhammad ibn Tughj al-Ikhshid's.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Egypt.
