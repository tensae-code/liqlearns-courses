# Video script — What Egypt Gave: Life in Pharaonic Egypt

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before imagining future cities, the class looks back at Akhetaten, the planned city of the ancient Egyptians, as proof Egyptians were city planners thousands of years ago.

Before imagining future cities, the class looks back at Akhetaten, the planned city of the ancient Egyptians, as proof Egyptians were city planners thousands of years ago.

The Egyptians answer the river with work — the world's first political unity under one government, a strong army among the oldest on earth, hieroglyphic writing, irrigation engineering and trade with the neighbours. The geographer Jamal Hamdan calls Egypt the cornerstone whose only door is the Nile.

The state organises the flood — land survey, irrigation offices, canals — and Amenemhat III throws his dam across the Faiyum's mouth to store the water and win twenty-seven thousand new feddans. The craftsman inherits his father's craft and perfects it — first glass, first paper, gold work fit for Tutankhamun — and trade at home runs on trust, with an ancient testament warning the merchant never to cheat the scales.

Mud-brick rooms for the poor, gardens for the rich, palaces for kings. Linen and gold thread. And a people who worked hard and played hard — wrestling, swimming, rowing, ball games, a chess-like game of thought, flutes and drums and work-songs in the fields, and festivals for gods, nation, king and returning armies.

Many gods for one ordered world, and beneath them the belief that shaped everything — resurrection and immortality. Then Akhenaten's daring call to one god, and Egypt's religious conversation with Africa.

The written, reckoned and healed world the Egyptians built — the intellectual harvest of the Pharaonic civilisation.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Egypt.
