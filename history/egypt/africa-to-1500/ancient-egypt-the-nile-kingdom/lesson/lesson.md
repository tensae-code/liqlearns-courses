# Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: how our city looks

The ancient Egyptians settle on the banks of the Nile, farming villages appear, and the villages grow into Egypt's first cities.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 3 Lesson 5: historical landmarks in Egypt

Djoser raises the first great stone building, the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, the Giza pyramids follow, and Karnak's temples at Thebes grow column by column for a thousand years.

## Sub lesson 3 — The ancient Egyptian civilisation among Africa's civilisations

On the Nile's banks farming, villages and unity grow into the dynastic state, which trades south with Kush and Punt.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Old Kingdom, era of the pyramid builders

Djoser's minister Imhotep raises the first stone pyramid at Saqqara. Sneferu sends forty ships to Phoenicia for cedar. Khufu builds the Great Pyramid. The sun temples of Userkaf rise as Ra's priests grow strong — then Pepi II's long reign fades into revolt and the first collapse, while travellers like Harkhuf journey south into Africa.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Middle Kingdom, era of economic prosperity

Mentuhotep II reunites Egypt from Thebes. Amenemhat I founds Itjtawy and chains forts against Libyan raids. Senusret III walls Nubia at Semna and digs his canal toward the Red Sea. Amenemhat III dams Lake Moeris against famine and Queen Sobekneferu rules — then the Hyksos seize the Delta until Seqenenre, Kamose and Ahmose fight them out.

## Sub lesson 6 — The New Kingdom, era of military glory

Ahmose expels the Hyksos and founds an empire from the Euphrates to the Fourth Cataract. Thutmose I plants the "King's Son of Kush" over the south. Hatshepsut rules in peace and prosperity. Thutmose III campaigns again and again in Asia and Kush. And Akhenaten preaches the one god Aton.

## Sub lesson 7 — Factors for the rise of the ancient Egyptian civilisation: the natural gifts

A river that teaches a whole people to farm, settle and cooperate on its banks. Borders of two seas and wide deserts that keep invaders out. A meeting-place of three continents. A dry sun that preserves the dead for eternity. And stone and metal in the hills waiting to be built with.

## Sub lesson 8 — Life of the ancient Egyptian: the ages before history

Fire, farming and copper carry the Egyptian down from the plateaus to the river. Villages grow into walled cities, cities into regions, regions into two kingdoms — the red crown of the papyrus north and the white crown of the lotus south. A first unity in 4242 BC breaks apart, until Narmer of Thebes unites the two lands in 3200 BC and founds Memphis, with Naqada's painted pottery paving the road.

## Sub lesson 9 — Life of the ancient Egyptian: the Old Kingdom

The pyramid age opens — Djoser's step pyramid, Khufu's great pyramid, Khafre and the Sphinx — while the commander Weni's tomb inscription boasts that his soldiers marched through enemy country without stealing a loaf or a goat from anyone.

## Sub lesson 10 — Life of the ancient Egyptian: the Middle Kingdom

The age the book calls economic prosperity — Senusret III digs his canal, Amenemhat III tames the Faiyum's waters behind the Lahun dam, and trade and farming flourish again under strong kings.

## Sub lesson 11 — Life of the ancient Egyptian: the New Kingdom

The warrior age — Thutmose III at Megiddo, Ramesses II at Kadesh and the first written peace treaty in history, and the twin temples of Abu Simbel carved into the Nubian rock.

## Sub lesson 12 — Features of the history of ancient Egypt

The long arc of the Pharaonic state from the first unification through the ages of strength and decline — the frame on which the whole unit hangs.

## Sub lesson 13 — Political and administrative life

How the Pharaonic state was governed — king, ministers and the administrations that ran a united kingdom for three thousand years.
