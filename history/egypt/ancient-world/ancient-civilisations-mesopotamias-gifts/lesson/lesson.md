# Mesopotamia's Gifts

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Mesopotamia's Gifts** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

Ur-Nammu's code is the first written law, protecting the weak and ordering trade, and Hammurabi's laws follow, even guarding women's rights. The Sumerians invent cuneiform and the solar-lunar calendar. Ziggurats rise in mudbrick. And Iraq's trade reaches Egypt, Persia, India and the Levant.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: compare your first map and line with the story you now know, and fix what was wrong.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Ur-Nammu's code is the first written law, protecting the weak and ordering trade, and Hammurabi's laws follow, even guarding women's rights.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
