# The Rise and Expansion of Islam

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **The Rise and Expansion of Islam** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Achievements of the Rightly-Guided Caliphs

When the Prophet dies, the Companions gather at the Saqifa of Bani Sa'ida and choose Abu Bakr as the first caliph. Four caliphs in turn — Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali — spread Islam, judge between people with justice and leave decisions and deeds the book holds up as lessons in leadership.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Islamic state after the Prophet's death

Abu Bakr holds the state together through the apostasy wars and secures its borders. Umar ibn al-Khattab takes up the work — the caliphate survives its founder.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Islamic conquests in the age of the Rightly-Guided Caliphs

Khalid ibn al-Walid opens Iraq — the Chains, al-Buwayb, al-Qadisiyya — and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas besieges al-Mada'in for two months and prays in Kisra's White Palace. At Nahavand in 642, "the conquest of conquests," al-Nu'man ibn Muqarrin falls a martyr as Persia's gates break open, Yazdegerd's threatening letter to the caliph answered by the sword. Uthman pushes on to Ifriqiya, Armenia and Nubia before the movement pauses in Ali's troubled years.
