# Egypt Under Muhammad Ali

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Egypt Under Muhammad Ali** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Muhammad Ali takes power in Egypt

Crushed by Khurshid Pasha's taxes and looting soldiers, Egypt's sheikhs and people choose Muhammad Ali as wali in 1805 — "we accept only you, on our conditions". He survives the attempt to transfer him to Salonica in 1806, defeats Fraser's British campaign with the people's resistance at Rosetta in 1807, exiles the popular leader Omar Makram in 1809, and destroys the Mamluks in the Citadel massacre of 1811.

## Sub lesson 2 — Muhammad Ali builds the modern state

Seven directorates under Cairo and advisory councils. A new army — when his Albanians mutiny and the Sudanese experiment fails in Egypt's climate, he conscripts Egyptians and has French officers train them. Schools of medicine, engineering and languages, missions to Europe and the Bulaq press. The iltizam tax-farms are abolished, a state monopoly imposed, long-staple cotton planted and the Delta Barrage begun.

## Sub lesson 3 — Egypt under Muhammad Ali's successors

Abbas I closes most schools and factories. Said gives the fellah the right to bequeath his land, cuts military service to one year and opens promotion to Egyptian officers — but grants De Lesseps the Suez concessions of 1854 and 1856 and takes Egypt's first foreign loan in 1862. Ismail doubles the education budget, digs the Ibrahimiyya and Ismailia canals and rebuilds Cairo with Abdeen Palace and the Opera House — on borrowed money.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Egyptian people's revolution, Muhammad Ali's accession, and building modern Egypt

The people and their leaders raise Muhammad Ali to power in 1805 and he builds the modern state — the same story told in this older book's own words, with the Fraser campaign, Omar Makram and the Citadel at its centre.

## Sub lesson 5 — Muhammad Ali's successors and the growth of foreign influence

Said grants De Lesseps the Suez concessions of 1854 and 1856 — ninety-nine years, four-fifths Egyptian labour, fifteen per cent of profits — and takes the first foreign loan in 1862. Digging begins at Port Said in 1859. Ismail's renaissance — the Ibrahimiyya canal, sugar factories, the Saniyya girls' school of 1873, the Shura Council of Deputies of 1866 — is paid for with loans, the canal's opening celebrations of 17 November 1869 deepen the crisis, consular courts and capitulations tighten the foreign grip, and Egyptian expeditions reach the equator and the whole Red Sea coast by 1875.

## Sub lesson 6 — Chapter 2: Building the modern state in Egypt

Out of the campaign's wreckage Muhammad Ali and his house build the modern Egyptian state — the nineteenth-century transformation the book's introduction promises: its economy, army, schools and society remade.
