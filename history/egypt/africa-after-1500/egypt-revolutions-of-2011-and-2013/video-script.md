The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

The rigged elections of 2010 that gave the ruling party ninety-seven per cent, the emergency law in force since 1981, rising prices and jobless graduates ignite a revolution organised through social media. For eighteen days Tahrir Square holds, popular committees guard the streets, the army protects the installations, and on 11 February 2011 President Mubarak steps down.

The army council dissolves parliament in February 2011, the Freedom and Justice Party sweeps the elections, and Muhammad Morsi wins the presidency with fifty-one per cent. His November 2012 declaration seizes wider powers, his party fills the state's posts, debt and prices climb — until the call goes out for early elections by 30 June 2013, General al-Sisi gives one week for reconciliation, millions fill the squares on 30 June, a forty-eight-hour ultimatum runs out, and on 3 July the roadmap removes Morsi. The Rabia sit-in is dispersed in August, a new constitution passes in January 2014, and al-Sisi is elected president.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Egypt.
