# History — Honduras course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Honduras inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Honduras's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** the placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first — and the ladder follows the country's own progression through its books. The first books open at home: History starts from the child's own time, family and community — yesterday/today/tomorrow, a family tree, the founding date of your own town — and the nation enters through its symbols, its anthem (which itself narrates the national story) and one hero, Francisco Morazán. The next books give History its own block: what History is, its sources (archaeological, written, oral), then the child's municipality and department through time — first peoples and the Maya of Copán, the making of the 18 departments, fortresses, monuments and heritage. The next books are the first full national-history survey, organised in the book's own three periods — prehispánico, colonial, independiente — running Copán and the pre-Hispanic peoples, discovery and the naming of Honduras, conquest, colonial classes and mestizaje, independence, Morazán and the Federation, the Liberal Reform and foreign enclaves, and the democratic era to the 2009 crisis. The lens then widens to Central America as one historical region — the "patria grande" — method first, then pre-Hispanic cultures, discovery, a conquest made hard by many unconquered peoples, colonisation, independence and Federation, dictatorship, civil war and the Esquipulas peace, with integration (SICA, PARLACEN) taught as Morazán's unfinished dream. Next the Americas whole: History-as-science with Honduras's own historians, the peopling debate, pre-Hispanic cultural stages, the three great civilisations plus all the others, three colonial empires side by side, the independence wave country by country, the continental Liberal Reform, the Cold War, the lost decade and neoliberal Honduras. Then a world-history survey run on two rival periodisations at once — the traditional ages and the historical-materialist sequence — from prehistory and the river civilisations through Greece, Rome, feudalism and Arab culture to Renaissance, Enlightenment, French Revolution and the world wars, with Honduras returning at the end as national identity and civic dates. The secondary books deepen each lens in turn: first Honduras itself — Honduran historians' rival periodisations, the prolonged conquest (1502–1573), the two independence actas, the Federal Republic, the Liberal Reform's enclaves, and a twentieth century told from Carías to the 1982 democratic return; then the Americas in depth — how history-writing changed, cultural areas, the Federation told in depth with Morazán's testament, state-building and caudillismo, border settlements, the Mexican Revolution and Peronism; and finally the world closing survey — the two rival periodisations taught side by side, the ancient legacies, the medieval world, the three great revolutions, both world wars, decolonisation, the Cold War with Honduras's own place noted inside it, the Gulf War as "immediate history", and globalisation's balance sheet. Each level is that turn of the books, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. Every filed book is COMPLETE as filed and carries placements, so every book makes its level. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First stories at home: my own time, my family and my community — and the nation through its symbols, its anthem and Morazán** — 7 lessons: Honduras national identity and morazan, Honduras national symbols and their history, Dating the past, What history is, Find history in your own locality, Heritage what it is and why it matters, Honduras independence and the civic calendar.

2. **Level 2 — History gets its own block: what History is, its sources, and my municipality and department through time — the first peoples, the Maya of Copán, and the making of the 18 departments** — 6 lessons: Honduras peoples indigenous afro and mestizo, From evidence to a written history, Honduras departments creation and evolution, The maya, Exploration columbus vespucci and magellan, Honduras first peoples caves and origins.

3. **Level 3 — Honduras whole in three periods — pre-Hispanic, colonial, independent: Copán and the pre-Hispanic peoples, discovery and conquest, independence, Morazán and the Federation, the Liberal Reform and its enclaves, and democracy to the 2009 crisis** — 10 lessons: Honduras history in three periods, Honduras prehispanic peoples, Honduras discovery encounter and the name, Honduras conquest and colonisation, Honduras colonial society mestizaje and slavery, Honduras independence 1821 causes and acta, Honduras morazan and the federation, Honduras liberal reform and foreign enclaves, Honduras historical figures and nation builders, Honduras democracy since 1982 and the 2009 crisis.

4. **Level 4 — Central America, the patria grande: method first, then the isthmus's peoples, a hard conquest, colonisation, independence and Federation, dictatorships, civil war and the Esquipulas peace** — 8 lessons: Historiography the history of history writing, Central america prehispanic cultures, Central america conquest and colonisation, Central america indigenous resistance leaders, Central america independence federation and liberal reform, Central america dictatorship civil war and peace, Central america historical figures, Central america patria grande and integration.

5. **Level 5 — The Americas whole: the peopling debate, pre-Hispanic stages, the three great civilisations and all the others, three colonial empires side by side, the independence wave, the continental Liberal Reform, the Cold War and neoliberal Honduras** — 17 lessons: Is history a science, Honduras historians and history writing, Americas first peoples peopling theories, From stone tools to farming, Americas prehispanic cultural stages, The aztecs tenochtitlan, Inca empire cusco and the andes, Americas other prehispanic cultures, Exploration causes and results, Americas conquest and columbian exchange, Americas colonial world three empires, American independence, Haiti first black republic and brazil independence, Americas independence dates and federations, Latin america liberal reforms wave, The cold war what it was and what it did to africa, Latin america lost decade neoliberalism and globalisation.

6. **Level 6 — The world survey: two rival periodisations, prehistory and the river civilisations, Greece and Rome, feudalism and Arab culture, Renaissance, Enlightenment, the French Revolution and both world wars — closing back home with Honduras's civic dates and customs** — 18 lessons: Who studies prehistory, Ancient civilisations mesopotamia, Ancient egypt the nile kingdom, Persia cyrus roads post and zoroaster, Ancient civilisations china, Ancient civilisations athens and democracy, Ancient civilisations greece crete, Ancient civilisations rome, Ancient civilisations rome divides, Medieval world the dark age and feudalism, The rise and expansion of islam, The renaissance what it was, Scientific revolution and enlightenment, The french revolution breaks out 1789, The first world war versailles and the results, Fascism and nazism two dictatorships compared, The second world war causes, The world after 1945 losses division and rebuilding.

7. **Level 7 — Honduras in depth: Honduran historians and their rival periodisations, the prolonged conquest, the two independence actas, the Federal Republic, the Liberal Reform's enclaves, and the long twentieth century to the 1982 democratic return** — 3 lessons: Honduras periodization of history, Independence causes and conditions, Honduras cultural heritage and world heritage sites.

8. **Level 8 — The Americas in depth: how the writing of history changed, pre-Hispanic cultural areas, colonial government, the independence processes, the Federation told in depth, state-building and caudillismo, border settlements, the Mexican Revolution and Peronism** — 6 lessons: Americas cultural areas nuclear and intermediate, Bartolome de las casas defender of the indigenous, Latin america state formation and caudillismo, Latin america border settlements and 20th century wars, Mexican revolution 1910 1920, Peronismo argentina populism.

9. **Level 9 — The world closing survey: two schools of periodisation, the ancient civilisations' legacies, the crusades and the late-medieval turn, the three great revolutions, both world wars, decolonisation, the Cold War, the Gulf War and globalisation** — 12 lessons: Two schools of periodization traditional and historical materialism, Ancient egypt what egypt gave, Crusades holy wars, Late medieval revival universities bourgeoisie and printing, The industrial revolution textiles steam and rail, 1917 twice february and october in russia, The first world war course of the war, The second world war course in europe, Decolonization africa and asia, Decolonisation ghana kenya and the south, Gulf war 1990 1991 and unipolar moment, Globalization interconnected world.

**Totals: 87 lessons, 414 placements stocked** (Levels 1–9 as above; 87 lessons stand on student books and 0 stand on teacher-guide evidence only — see the acceptance reckoning in ABOUT.md).
