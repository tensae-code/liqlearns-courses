**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** The maya
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — History gets its own block: what History is, its sources, and my municipality and department through time — the first peoples, the Maya of Copán, and the making of the 18 departments (CURRICULUM.md).

**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — History gets its own block: what History is, its sources, and my municipality and department through time — the first peoples, the Maya of Copán, and the making of the 18 departments — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 25 Pueblos originarios: los primeros pobladores y los mayas** — small hunter bands cross into the continent chasing herds and settle at La Esperanza; thousands of years later the Maya flourish at Copán, extend over the west, bring maize farming and advances in astronomy, mathematics and art, and play Pok-ta-pok
2. **§2 Lección 25 Pueblos originarios (guide)** — first hunters at La Esperanza, then Copán's Maya flowering with maize, astronomy and the ball game
3. **§3 Lección 21 Raíces prehispánicas: Copán** — Copán, UNESCO World Heritage, was a Maya city of plazas, stelae, altars and tombs — Acropolis, Ball Court, Hieroglyphic Stairway, Rosalila temple — across the preclassic, classic and postclassic periods
4. **§4 Lección 21 Raíces prehispánicas: Copán (guide)** — Copán's plazas, stelae and Hieroglyphic Stairway are the evidence lesson for Maya development across three periods
5. **§5 Lección 22 Población prehispánica de Centroamérica: los mayas** — the Maya, oldest and most outstanding, build Tikal, Palenque, Chichén Itzá and Copán as ceremonial cities under king, nobles, priests and warriors; they leave hieroglyphic writing (three surviving codices), the zero, vigesimal numbers and exact calendars, then decline — land exhaustion, pests and climate are the theories offered
6. **§6 Lección 22: los mayas (guide)** — Maya social pyramid, cities, glyphs, zero and calendars are completed as exercises, ending on the rulers of Copán
7. **§7 Lección 23 Principales culturas: la maya** — city-states under a hereditary Halach Uinic and a theocratic priesthood, nobles, people and slaves, maize-bean-chile farming, cacao for the elite, 31 Maya languages still a patrimony of humanity
8. **§8 Lección 23: cultura maya (guide)** — the comparison chart fixes Maya government, economy and the zero against its peers
