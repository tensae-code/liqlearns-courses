# Lesson plan — Honduras peoples indigenous afro and mestizo

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Honduras peoples indigenous afro and mestizo
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — History gets its own block: what History is, its sources, and my municipality and department through time — the first peoples, the Maya of Copán, and the making of the 18 departments (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Honduras peoples indigenous afro and mestizo as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 4 Una misma nación** — ten peoples and eight mother tongues make one nation — Indigenous, Afro-Honduran and mestizo — and difference itself becomes the lesson in respect
2. **§2 Lección 25: los pueblos de hoy** — the Maya's living heirs, the Chortí, share today's Honduras with Lenca, Tolupán, Pech, Tawahka, Miskito, Nahua, Garífuna (arrived from St Vincent) and English-speaking Black communities, each keeping its own way of life
3. **§3 Lección 36 Diversidad cultural** — each ethnic people's food, dance, music, language, sayings, legends and games enrich the national patrimony, and Lenca women of Marcala who pushed coffee exports to Europe show peoples still making history
4. **§4 Lección 4 Una misma nación (guide)** — the teacher presents the ten peoples and eight languages as one nation's diversity and has children compare physical and cultural traits with respect
5. **§5 Lección 25: pueblos actuales (guide)** — today's ethnic map is taught department by department, ending on the Lenca Compostura ceremonies of thanks to the earth
6. **§6 Lección 36 Diversidad cultural (guide)** — acculturation is named as the threat; conserving each people's patrimony is the class's stated duty
7. **§7 Lección 33 Un país multicultural** — mestizos speak Spanish while Miskito, Garífuna, Tol, Chortí and Tawahka tongues survive; punta, tzolkin ceremonies and chilateadas, coconut foods against maize foods — on 3 August 1994 President Reina formally recognises Honduras as multicultural and multiethnic
8. **§8 Lección 33 Un país multicultural (guide)** — languages, dances and foods are mapped people by people, closing on the 1994 official recognition of a multicultural, multiethnic Honduras
9. **§9 Lección 36 Honduras multiétnica** — nine named groups — Maya Chortí, Miskito, Tawahka, Garífuna, Pech, English-speaking Black, Lenca, mestizo, Tolupán — spread over fifteen departments, each group's history, customs and beliefs a recognition of the country's past and present
10. **§10 Lección 36 Honduras multiétnica (guide)** — the nine groups are differentiated by language, place and custom on the ethnic map
