# Honduras peoples indigenous afro and mestizo

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Honduras peoples indigenous afro and mestizo" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 4 Una misma nación

ten peoples and eight mother tongues make one nation — Indigenous, Afro-Honduran and mestizo — and difference itself becomes the lesson in respect

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 25: los pueblos de hoy

the Maya's living heirs, the Chortí, share today's Honduras with Lenca, Tolupán, Pech, Tawahka, Miskito, Nahua, Garífuna (arrived from St Vincent) and English-speaking Black communities, each keeping its own way of life

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 36 Diversidad cultural

each ethnic people's food, dance, music, language, sayings, legends and games enrich the national patrimony, and Lenca women of Marcala who pushed coffee exports to Europe show peoples still making history

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 4 Una misma nación (guide)

the teacher presents the ten peoples and eight languages as one nation's diversity and has children compare physical and cultural traits with respect

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 25: pueblos actuales (guide)

today's ethnic map is taught department by department, ending on the Lenca Compostura ceremonies of thanks to the earth

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 36 Diversidad cultural (guide)

acculturation is named as the threat; conserving each people's patrimony is the class's stated duty

## Sub lesson 7 — Lección 33 Un país multicultural

mestizos speak Spanish while Miskito, Garífuna, Tol, Chortí and Tawahka tongues survive; punta, tzolkin ceremonies and chilateadas, coconut foods against maize foods — on 3 August 1994 President Reina formally recognises Honduras as multicultural and multiethnic

## Sub lesson 8 — Lección 33 Un país multicultural (guide)

languages, dances and foods are mapped people by people, closing on the 1994 official recognition of a multicultural, multiethnic Honduras

## Sub lesson 9 — Lección 36 Honduras multiétnica

nine named groups — Maya Chortí, Miskito, Tawahka, Garífuna, Pech, English-speaking Black, Lenca, mestizo, Tolupán — spread over fifteen departments, each group's history, customs and beliefs a recognition of the country's past and present

## Sub lesson 10 — Lección 36 Honduras multiétnica (guide)

the nine groups are differentiated by language, place and custom on the ethnic map
