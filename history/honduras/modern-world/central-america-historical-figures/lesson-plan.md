# Lesson plan — Central america historical figures

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Central america historical figures
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Central America, the patria grande: method first, then the isthmus's peoples, a hard conquest, colonisation, independence and Federation, dictatorships, civil war and the Esquipulas peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Central america historical figures as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Central America, the patria grande: method first, then the isthmus's peoples, a hard conquest, colonisation, independence and Federation, dictatorships, civil war and the Esquipulas peace — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 28 Personajes destacados: letras y derechos** — Asturias wins the 1967 Nobel for a literature built on Indigenous heritage and invents magical realism against dictatorship in El Señor Presidente; Monseñor Romero marks El Salvador; Honduras's Clementina Suárez, feminist poet against Carías, Roberto Sosa and Sor María Rosa stand beside Nicaragua's Chamorro, martyr of public liberties against the Somozas
2. **§2 Lección 28: Carrasco y los Nobel de la Paz** — Francisca Carrasco, a peasant who taught herself to read, marches with Costa Rica's troops against William Walker in 1856, fights at Rivas, nurses cholera victims on the walk home and is buried with a general's honours; Óscar Arias (1987) and Rigoberta Menchú (1992) take Nobel Peace Prizes
3. **§3 Lección 28 Personajes (guide)** — one figure per country and field — Asturias, Romero, Padilla, Chamorro — with Honduras's poets and Sor María Rosa
4. **§4 Lección 28: Carrasco y Nobel (guide)** — Carrasco's 1856 march against Walker and the two Nobel Peace laureates close the figures lesson
