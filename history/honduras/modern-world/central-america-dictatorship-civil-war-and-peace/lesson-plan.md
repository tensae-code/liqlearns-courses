# Lesson plan — Central america dictatorship civil war and peace

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Central america dictatorship civil war and peace
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Central America, the patria grande: method first, then the isthmus's peoples, a hard conquest, colonisation, independence and Federation, dictatorships, civil war and the Esquipulas peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Central america dictatorship civil war and peace as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Central America, the patria grande: method first, then the isthmus's peoples, a hard conquest, colonisation, independence and Federation, dictatorships, civil war and the Esquipulas peace — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 27 Democratización y autoritarismo: las dictaduras** — the 1929 crash breaks the banana-and-coffee economies and dictators rise — Ubico (1931–1944) in Guatemala, Hernández Martínez in El Salvador, whose answer to a rising is La Matanza of 10,000–30,000 including Farabundo Martí, Carías in Honduras, Somoza in Nicaragua — all keeping power by armies, rewritten laws and US companies' support
2. **§2 Lección 27: los acuerdos de paz** — Costa Rica, army-free since 1949, leads the way out — Esquipulas (1987), Chapultepec for El Salvador (1992), Guatemala's final peace (1996); guerrillas (FSLN, FMLN, URNG) disarm and become legal parties that later win power
3. **§3 Lección 27: dictaduras (guide)** — Carías, Ubico, Hernández Martínez and Somoza are compared as one regional pattern born of the Liberal Reform order and the 1929 crash
4. **§4 Lección 27: paz (guide)** — Esquipulas to Chapultepec to Guatemala 1996 — causes and consequences of the peace accords are the pupils' analysis task
