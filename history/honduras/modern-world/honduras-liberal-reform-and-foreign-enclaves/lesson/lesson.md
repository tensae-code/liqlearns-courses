# Honduras liberal reform and foreign enclaves

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Honduras liberal reform and foreign enclaves" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 27 La Reforma Liberal y la inversión extranjera

Marco Aurelio Soto and Ramón Rosa (1876–1883) set out to modernise the colonial inheritance — secular free compulsory education, church separated from State, land and tax measures for farming, foreign capital invited in

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 27: logros y enclaves

the Rosario Mining Company opens San Juancito (the mining enclave) and banana enclaves follow; new codes, the southern road, telegraph, National Post, National Library and Archive, La Gaceta and the repaired National Railway mark the reform's reach

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 27: la Reforma (guide)

Soto and Rosa's programme is taught as a young nation's wish-list — free thought, secular schooling, the vote, coffee, bananas and mines

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 27: enclaves y obras (guide)

San Juancito's Rosario mine, codes, telegraph, post and library are the reform's evidence; pupils research the National Post's own history

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 28: logros en Honduras

Honduras's reform yields harmonised codes, secular education, trained teachers, schools and libraries — the National Library Juan Ramón Molina founded 27 August 1880 under Soto

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 28: Honduras (guide)

codes, secular schooling and the 1880 National Library are Honduras's reform evidence

## Sub lesson 7 — Soto and Rosa build the modern state

from Isla del Tigre in 1876, Marco Aurelio Soto and Ramón Rosa decreed free, secular, compulsory primary education, separated church and state and founded the national archive — but concessions of free land, timber and tax exemptions handed the economy to foreign capital and the enclave

## Sub lesson 8 — Soto and Rosa build the modern state

the guide's key: secular free education, church–state separation, national archive — and the concession regime that created the foreign enclave economy
