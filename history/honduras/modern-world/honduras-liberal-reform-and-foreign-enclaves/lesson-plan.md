# Lesson plan — Honduras liberal reform and foreign enclaves

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Honduras liberal reform and foreign enclaves
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Honduras whole in three periods — pre-Hispanic, colonial, independent: Copán and the pre-Hispanic peoples, discovery and conquest, independence, Morazán and the Federation, the Liberal Reform and its enclaves, and democracy to the 2009 crisis (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Honduras liberal reform and foreign enclaves as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 27 La Reforma Liberal y la inversión extranjera** — Marco Aurelio Soto and Ramón Rosa (1876–1883) set out to modernise the colonial inheritance — secular free compulsory education, church separated from State, land and tax measures for farming, foreign capital invited in
2. **§2 Lección 27: logros y enclaves** — the Rosario Mining Company opens San Juancito (the mining enclave) and banana enclaves follow; new codes, the southern road, telegraph, National Post, National Library and Archive, La Gaceta and the repaired National Railway mark the reform's reach
3. **§3 Lección 27: la Reforma (guide)** — Soto and Rosa's programme is taught as a young nation's wish-list — free thought, secular schooling, the vote, coffee, bananas and mines
4. **§4 Lección 27: enclaves y obras (guide)** — San Juancito's Rosario mine, codes, telegraph, post and library are the reform's evidence; pupils research the National Post's own history
5. **§5 Lección 28: logros en Honduras** — Honduras's reform yields harmonised codes, secular education, trained teachers, schools and libraries — the National Library Juan Ramón Molina founded 27 August 1880 under Soto
6. **§6 Lección 28: Honduras (guide)** — codes, secular schooling and the 1880 National Library are Honduras's reform evidence
7. **§7 Soto and Rosa build the modern state** — from Isla del Tigre in 1876, Marco Aurelio Soto and Ramón Rosa decreed free, secular, compulsory primary education, separated church and state and founded the national archive — but concessions of free land, timber and tax exemptions handed the economy to foreign capital and the enclave
8. **§8 Soto and Rosa build the modern state** — the guide's key: secular free education, church–state separation, national archive — and the concession regime that created the foreign enclave economy
