# Honduras independence and the civic calendar

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Honduras independence and the civic calendar" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 26 Nos identificamos con todos: fechas cívicas

the civic calendar gathers the births of Morazán and Valle, Independence Day and Labour Day, and a picture of the signing of the Act of Independence asks what event it shows

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 27 Nos identificamos y así celebramos

1 September Flag Day, 15 September independence of Central America, 28 September arrival of the Pliegos de la Independencia, 20 July Lempira Day and 12 October arrival of the Spanish are celebrated as feasts that changed laws and authorities

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 26 Nos identificamos con todos (guide)

civic dates are taught as shared memory — próceres' births, independence, and 14 April Day of the Americas for the history the American peoples share

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 27 Nos identificamos y así celebramos (guide)

the year's civic feasts are walked through in order, from Flag Day to the Pliegos and Lempira Day, each tied to the event it commemorates

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 30 Elementos de identidad nacional: fechas cívicas

a dated civic year runs Honduran Women's Day (25 January), Suyapa (3 February), Afro-Honduran Day (12 April), Day of the Americas (14 April), Spanish Language Day (23 April) and the September independence feasts

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 30 Identidad nacional (guide)

the civic calendar is memorised as the nation's dated self-portrait
