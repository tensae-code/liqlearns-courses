# Lesson plan — Exploration columbus vespucci and magellan

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Exploration columbus vespucci and magellan
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — History gets its own block: what History is, its sources, and my municipality and department through time — the first peoples, the Maya of Copán, and the making of the 18 departments (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Exploration columbus vespucci and magellan as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — History gets its own block: what History is, its sources, and my municipality and department through time — the first peoples, the Maya of Copán, and the making of the 18 departments — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 26 El departamento a través del tiempo: Colón en Trujillo** — Columbus lands at Trujillo on 14 August 1502 and names it Punta Caxinas (today Puerto Castilla) — the department of Colón still tells the event as its own founding story
2. **§2 Lección 26 (guide)** — Columbus's 1502 landing at Trujillo is the entry event for the department-by-department historical record
3. **§3 Lección 22 El descubrimiento de nuestro territorio** — backed by Ferdinand and Isabella, Columbus sails for the Indies in 1492 with paper, compass and caravels; only on the fourth voyage (1502) does he reach Guanaja, the Island of Pines, and Punta Caxinas, where the first mass on the mainland is celebrated
4. **§4 Lección 22 El descubrimiento (guide)** — the four voyages are mapped from Iberia; the fourth reaches Guanaja and Punta Caxinas in 1502
5. **§5 Lección 23 La llegada de los españoles** — a Spain unified under Castile and Aragon, Granada taken and Constantinople fallen (1453), backs Columbus; the four voyages are told ship by ship — Rodrigo de Triana's "¡Tierra!" at Guanahaní, 17 ships and 1,500 men on the second — and the Treaty of Tordesillas splits the world between Spain and Portugal
6. **§6 Lección 23 La llegada de los españoles (guide)** — 15th-century Spain is summarised, one main idea extracted per voyage, and Tordesillas closes the lesson
7. **§7 Lección 26: la era de los descubrimientos** — compass, caravel, astrolabe and printing open the age of Columbus, Vasco da Gama and Magellan; the world is mapped, the first world economy forms, and colonialism's bases are laid
8. **§8 Lección 26: descubrimientos (guide)** — pupils identify which invention made America's discovery possible
