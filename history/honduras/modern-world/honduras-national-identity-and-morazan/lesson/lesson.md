# Honduras national identity and morazan

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Honduras national identity and morazan" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 8 Identidad nacional

a child learns that being born in Honduras makes them Honduran, that heroes and próceres did great deeds for everyone, and meets Francisco Morazán, named the country's greatest prócer

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 8 Identidad nacional (guide)

the teacher leads the class from nationality as a child's right to Morazán as prócer máximo, with children naming outstanding people of their own community

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 9: los próceres y el héroe

Morazán, Valle, Herrera, Cabañas and Reyes are named founders of the Patria, men of flesh and blood with virtues and defects, beside national hero Cacique Lempira and a painting of the Battle of La Trinidad

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 9: próceres y héroe (guide)

prócer is distinguished from héroe — civic virtue and founding deeds against brave life-risking acts — with Lempira as hero and the five próceres as founders

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 9: el legado de los próceres

Valle drafted the Act of Independence, Herrera decreed the first constitution in 1825 as first Head of State, Morazán presided over the Federation and left the unionist ideal, and Lempira gave his life defending the land

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 9: legado de los próceres (guide)

each prócer is fixed by his deed — Valle the Acta, Herrera the 1825 constitution, Morazán the unionist ideal, Cabañas and Reyes to be researched by the pupils

## Sub lesson 7 — identity as a historical construction

Honduran identity is the product of a long history — flag, shield, national flower and tree, archaeological parks and letters — built day by day on family and personal identity, in a pluricultural, multilingual society

## Sub lesson 8 — identity as a historical construction

the guide teaches identity as history-built belonging — symbols, archaeological parks and letters in a pluricultural society
