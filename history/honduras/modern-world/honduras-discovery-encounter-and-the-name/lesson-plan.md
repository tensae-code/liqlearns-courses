# Lesson plan — Honduras discovery encounter and the name

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Honduras discovery encounter and the name
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Honduras whole in three periods — pre-Hispanic, colonial, independent: Copán and the pre-Hispanic peoples, discovery and conquest, independence, Morazán and the Federation, the Liberal Reform and its enclaves, and democracy to the 2009 crisis (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Honduras discovery encounter and the name as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Honduras whole in three periods — pre-Hispanic, colonial, independent: Copán and the pre-Hispanic peoples, discovery and conquest, independence, Morazán and the Federation, the Liberal Reform and its enclaves, and democracy to the 2009 crisis — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 22: encuentro de dos culturas y el nombre Honduras** — two unlike worlds meet — armour, horses, gunpowder and monarchy against maize, nature-linked spirituality and hunting weapons; storm-tossed Spaniards thank God they escaped "estas Honduras", Guaymuras and Higueras give way to the name Honduras
2. **§2 Lección 22: encuentro y nombre (guide)** — the two cultures are compared item by item, and the storm story explains how Honduras got its name
3. **§3 Lección 23: consecuencias del descubrimiento** — Europe gains gold, silver, tobacco and empires and the Atlantic becomes the highway; America gains colonists, mestizaje, slave sales, mine extraction, an imposed culture, language and religion, unknown diseases and a falling population
4. **§4 Lección 23: consecuencias (guide)** — advantages and disadvantages of the arrival are debated for both worlds, tied to the national anthem's second stanza
5. **§5 the "encounter of cultures" debate** — 1492 was chance, not plan: Columbus miscalculated, and the book insists a populated continent cannot be "discovered" — two worlds met, with Galeano's "Las venas abiertas" assigned as the radiography of what followed
6. **§6 the "encounter of cultures" debate** — the guide teaches 1492 as a chance encounter, not a discovery of a populated continent, with Galeano's "Las venas abiertas" as the set reading
