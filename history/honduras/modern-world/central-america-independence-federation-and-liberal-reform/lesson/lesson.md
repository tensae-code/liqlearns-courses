# Central america independence federation and liberal reform

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Central america independence federation and liberal reform" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 26 Independencia de Centroamérica

Napoleon in Spain, Cádiz 1812, the French Revolution and US independence meet criollo–peninsular rivalry and pre-independence movements; independence (15 September 1821) is signed at Guatemala by Gainza, Delgado, Cañas, Aycinena and others as an elite settlement, then comes annexation to Iturbide's Mexico and absolute independence on 1 July 1823 (Chiapas stays with Mexico)

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 26: la Federación y la Reforma Liberal centroamericana

the Federation's 1824 constitution abolishes slavery; Arce presides, Valle dies travelling to take the presidency he won, and Morazán (1835–1839) separates church and State and frees education; a generation later Barrios in Guatemala and Soto and Rosa in Honduras carry the Liberal Reform — codes, the 1880 constitution, secularised cemeteries and church goods, the southern road

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 26 Independencia (guide)

Valle's and Molina's newspapers spread the idea; the Acta's signers are named one by one

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 26: Federación y Reforma (guide)

slavery's abolition under the Federation and the reform codes of 1876 onward are listed as the era's changes

## Sub lesson 5 — annexation, elections and the federation's advances

the criollo elite annexed Central America to Iturbide's Mexico (5 January 1822) over the fierce resistance of Molina, Barrundia and Córdova; freed 1 July 1823, the federation held 1824 elections (liberal Arce vs the conservatives' Valle), planned an interoceanic canal, abolished slavery, consecrated asylum and limited the death penalty — before dissolving in 1839; Morazán's 1842 testament is printed at length

## Sub lesson 6 — annexation, elections and the federation's advances

the guide teaches the 1822 annexation and its opponents, the 1824 Arce–Valle elections, the canal project, abolition of slavery and asylum — and Morazán's testament
