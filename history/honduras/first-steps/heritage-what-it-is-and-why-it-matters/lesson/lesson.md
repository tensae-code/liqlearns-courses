# Heritage what it is and why it matters

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Heritage what it is and why it matters" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 25: Formas en que el pasado se manifiesta

the past survives in the present as people read about heroes, practise traditions, visit Copán and museums, and guard places like Fuerte San Cristóbal at Gracias, where President Juan Lindo lies buried

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 29 Costumbres y tradiciones

Comayagua's sawdust carpets at Holy Week, sopa de caracol, colaciones and folk songs are what the society is; the song Conozca a Honduras asks where the Ruins of Copán and Morazán's cradle are

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 25: el pasado en el presente (guide)

traditions still kept, the Festival de las Flores at Siguatepeque and the Guancasco ceremonies at Gracias show the past alive in present practice

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 29 Costumbres y tradiciones (guide)

typical foods, dances and religious feasts, regional and national, are collected as the manifestations of what Honduran society is

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 8 Nuestras costumbres y tradiciones

traditions pass from generation to generation — patronal fairs, the Baile de los Diablitos, typical foods — and oral transmission is named living history to be guarded jealously

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 22: los escudos cuentan historia

coats of arms, old or new, are full of history; Honduras's shield, conceived in 1840, speaks of natural wealth, liberty and sovereignty, and children design a shield for their own family or town

## Sub lesson 7 — Lección 23 Raíces culturales

the fortresses of San Cristóbal (Gracias) and San Fernando (Omoa) were raised for defence against an enemy; around them live legends, fairs and traditions that are the legacy of the past to conserve

## Sub lesson 8 — Lección 27 El aporte cultural

crafts handed down for centuries, the Baile de los Diablitos danced in Comayagua for over 370 years, and the Guancasco pact-feast between Ilama, Gualala and Chinda are intangible heritage carried by oral tradition

## Sub lesson 9 — Lección 28 El valor de nuestros monumentos

before-and-after photographs of the National Post Office open the idea of tangible heritage — fortresses, historic centres, the Talgua Caves, Copán — against intangible heritage of customs, dances, foods and legends

## Sub lesson 10 — Lección 29 Participo y construyo identidad

elders transmit intangible heritage by example and story; Lempira's death is commemorated every 20 July, and oral tradition is defined as culture passed verbally from generation to generation

## Sub lesson 11 — Lección 38 Diversidad del patrimonio

Honduras's historical patrimony is the fruit of pre-Hispanic Indigenous heritage and the republican era together; old buildings are faithful witnesses — if they vanish, the memory of who we are vanishes with them

## Sub lesson 12 — Lección 8 Nuestras costumbres y tradiciones (guide)

traditions are taught as practices transmitted generation to generation, with a community celebration planned by the class itself

## Sub lesson 13 — Lección 22: escudos (guide)

municipal shields are decoded for the values and events they express, then each team designs its own

## Sub lesson 14 — Lección 23 Raíces culturales (guide)

fortresses, legends tied to real or imagined persons, and patronal fairs are gathered as the municipality's cultural roots

## Sub lesson 15 — Lección 27 El aporte cultural (guide)

a legend is read aloud by the teacher and the heritage-protection law's articles are summarised by the class

## Sub lesson 16 — Lección 28 El valor de nuestros monumentos (guide)

monuments before and now — children explain why each was conserved and what it represents to those who live around it

## Sub lesson 17 — Lección 29 Participo y construyo identidad (guide)

grandparents tell their stories and teach their songs; the class copies the song, poem or legend that represents its culture

## Sub lesson 18 — Lección 38 Diversidad del patrimonio (guide)

Comayagua's historic centre — cathedral, plaza, colonial houses — is the case study of patrimony accumulated over time

## Sub lesson 19 — Lección 8 El orgullo de ser hondureño y hondureña

Froylán Turcios's Prayer of the Honduran opens a lesson on culture's elements — customs, beliefs, values, language, dress, crafts — kept alive in patronal feasts, Comayagua's sawdust carpets and the Guancasco

## Sub lesson 20 — Lección 33: patrimonio cultural hondureño

heritage is what the peoples have been, are and will be — Maya temples, the Hieroglyphic Stairway, Comayagua's Holy Week carpets — an inheritance passed generation to generation that all must preserve and know

## Sub lesson 21 — Lección 8 El orgullo de ser hondureño (guide)

Turcios's prayer is read aloud and culture's elements are sorted by the class into customs, traditions, beliefs and feasts

## Sub lesson 22 — Lección 33: patrimonio (guide)

heritage is defined as the inheritance travelling from past generations to future ones, temples and carpets alike

## Sub lesson 23 — Lección 21: patrimonio y lugares de la memoria

material heritage (San Fernando de Omoa) and immaterial heritage (oral traditions, rituals, crafts) pass from generation to generation; museums, archives and newspaper libraries — and mining San Juancito and port Amapala — keep a society's memory

## Sub lesson 24 — Lección 35 Legado cultural de los pueblos indígenas

most Central Americans are mestizo or Indigenous in origin, and the region's foods, myths, legends, customs and dress descend from the peoples who held the land in pre-Hispanic times

## Sub lesson 25 — Lección 36 Nuestras tradiciones

humanity leaves its mark from cave engravings to books, but oral tradition — the headless horseman, the cart, la sucia, the duende — is the commonest carrier; every community has its storytellers

## Sub lesson 26 — Lección 21: patrimonio (guide)

pupils write a community legend into the workbook as intangible heritage and explain why Copán is world heritage

## Sub lesson 27 — Lección 35 Legado indígena (guide)

Indigenous dress, food and story are collected as the living legacy of pre-Hispanic peoples

## Sub lesson 28 — Lección 36 Nuestras tradiciones (guide)

the community's storytellers are named and its legends written down as oral tradition

## Sub lesson 29 — Lección 22: lo prehispánico que prevalece

maize survives the millennia onto today's table as tortilla, atol and tamales, and pupils hunt the pre-Hispanic buildings, traditions, dress, foods and words still alive in Honduras

## Sub lesson 30 — Lección 35 Características culturales de América

Anglo-Saxon against Latin American culture, and the American Capital of Culture award (Valdivia 2016) as a device for knowing each city's patrimony

## Sub lesson 31 — Lección 37 Manifestaciones culturales

every society builds a general culture from subcultures distinguished by features, language, dress, foods, beliefs and organisation — pupils draw one manifestation of their own community

## Sub lesson 32 — Lección 22: prevalencias (guide)

pre-Hispanic survivals in Honduran building, dress, food and language are the lesson's research question

## Sub lesson 33 — Lección 35 Características culturales (guide)

American capitals of culture frame the continent's varied expressions

## Sub lesson 34 — Lección 37 Manifestaciones culturales (guide)

one community manifestation is drawn and explained as the class's culture sample

## Sub lesson 35 — Lección 30: costumbres y tradiciones

patronal fairs with folk dances, palo encebado, toro fuego and ribbon races, horchata, chicha and atol de elote are customs transmitted generation to generation, orally or in writing

## Sub lesson 36 — Lección 35 Diversidad de culturas en el mundo

the world's cultures are surveyed as one diverse human patrimony of arts, languages, beliefs and ways of life

## Sub lesson 37 — Lección 30: costumbres (guide)

patronal-fair customs are collected as identity's living material

## Sub lesson 38 — Lección 35 Diversidad de culturas (guide)

world cultural diversity is presented as shared human wealth

## Sub lesson 39 — heritage you cannot touch

UNESCO's widened idea of heritage covers living traditions passed down generations — oral tales (Honduras's La Sucia, Costa Rica's Llorona, Chile's Pincoya), dances (Bolivia's Morenada), great museums and kitchens (Nicaragua's gallo pinto, Venezuela's arepa) — community-created and community-kept

## Sub lesson 40 — heritage you cannot touch

the guide teaches UNESCO's living-heritage idea — La Sucia, the Morenada, gallo pinto and the arepa as community-kept patrimony
