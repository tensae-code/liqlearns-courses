# Lesson plan — What history is

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** First steps · **Lesson:** What history is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First stories at home: my own time, my family and my community — and the nation through its symbols, its anthem and Morazán (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of What history is as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First stories at home: my own time, my family and my community — and the nation through its symbols, its anthem and Morazán — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 22 Nosotros y nuestra familia: historia personal y familiar** — grandparents tell events from before the children were born; one person's important life-events make a personal history, and all the family's stories together make the family history
2. **§2 Lección 22 Nosotros y nuestra familia (guide)** — a child (Mateo, in the workbook story) narrates his personal history aloud, and the class learns that family history is all its members' stories joined
3. **§3 Lección 20 La Historia: nuestro presente y futuro** — History studies people's past through time, with causes and consequences; every historical fact needs a person, a place and a date, and knowing origins tells a society who it is and where it wants to go
4. **§4 Lección 20 La Historia: nuestro presente y futuro (guide)** — the school's own name, first classrooms and recent changes become the class's first historical inquiry
5. **§5 Lección 20 El valor de la historia** — History is a social science studying past human deeds to know the past, understand the present and build a better future; every fact happens in a place and a time and has causes and consequences
6. **§6 Lección 20 El valor de la historia (guide)** — History's value is taught through causes and consequences — knowing them lets a people understand its present and choose a better future
7. **§7 Lección 20 Construyendo nuestra memoria histórica** — Herodotus, "father of history", makes history an inquiry (historéo, to find out); the science studies human deeds that changed a social process, so a people learns from past lessons and does not repeat its errors
8. **§8 Lección 29 Valorando los procesos históricos centroamericanos** — history is not isolated facts but processes — pre-Hispanic, colonial, independent — whose stages (Federation, Liberal Reform, dictatorships, civil wars, peace) explain the present; the class builds a timeline of Central America's important events
9. **§9 Lección 20 Construyendo nuestra memoria histórica (guide)** — the school's own history, researched from oral and written sources, is the guide's entry to Herodotus and the definition of history
10. **§10 Lección 29 Valorando los procesos (guide)** — four important Central American events are identified by each team as processes, not isolated facts
11. **§11 Lección 20 Las Ciencias Sociales al servicio de la humanidad** — History stands in the family portrait of the social sciences (with anthropology, archaeology, economics, demography, sociology) as the discipline that lets a society understand its changes through time
12. **§12 Lección 20 Las Ciencias Sociales (guide)** — History is placed among the social sciences by its object — people in society through time
