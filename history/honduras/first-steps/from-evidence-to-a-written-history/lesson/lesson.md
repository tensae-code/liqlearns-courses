# From evidence to a written history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From evidence to a written history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 21 Buscando huellas de nuestro pasado: las fuentes

without sources there is no past to know; sources are sorted into archaeological (buildings, monuments, tools), written (letters, laws, chronicles, censuses) and oral tradition (elders' testimonies)

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 21: huellas materiales

a Maya stela at Copán Ruinas and a colonial church at Gracias are vestiges that let the class reconstruct past societies, then children hunt the oldest buildings in their own plaza

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 38: los museos guardan la historia

museums conserve and exhibit the objects and collections of the peoples — art, religion, gold-work, history, anthropology — so a visit becomes a lesson in the nation's past, protected by the 1998 patrimony law

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 21 Buscando huellas (guide)

sources are classified with the class — archaeological, written, oral — and elders' testimonies are collected as tradition

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 21: visita a las huellas (guide)

teacher, children and a family adult walk to the plaza to identify and draw the oldest house or building

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 38: museos (guide)

museums are presented as society's memory store; specialised museums (art, religion, Indigenous peoples, gold-work) each hold one kind of evidence

## Sub lesson 7 — Lección 20: fuentes y ciencias auxiliares

the past is rebuilt indirectly from sources — primary against secondary, direct against indirect, a Maya stela against a book about it — with geography, archaeology, anthropology and other auxiliary sciences checking the story

## Sub lesson 8 — Lección 21 Las huellas de la historia

Tikal, Khafre's pyramid, Paestum and the Great Wall open a typology of material, documentary, oral and audiovisual sources; Ciudad Blanca in the Mosquitia is Honduras's own lost-city evidence, and "when an elder dies, a library burns"

## Sub lesson 9 — Lección 20: fuentes y auxiliares (guide)

teams define and exemplify each source type and name the auxiliary sciences that make results credible

## Sub lesson 10 — Lección 21 Las huellas de la historia (guide)

world vestiges (Tikal to the Great Wall) frame the four source natures, with elders named as living libraries

## Sub lesson 11 — Lección 20: fuentes y proceso de investigación

sources are classed primary (Copán itself) against secondary (a book on the Maya), and oral, written, material and iconographic; the historian must be objective, define the problem first, and rebuild the past from what survives

## Sub lesson 12 — Lección 20: fuentes (guide)

pupils narrate a historical event of their own community, then classify the sources it would rest on

## Sub lesson 13 — Lección 20: métodos de investigación histórica

valid historical research runs problem, questions, data collection from written and oral sources, analysis, conclusions — with biographical and chronological methods beside it

## Sub lesson 14 — Lección 20: métodos (guide)

the research steps and the biographical and chronological methods are the guide's method core

## Sub lesson 15 — material and immaterial sources

sources are testimonies that can be touched (buildings, documents, coins) or not (legends, oral tradition, Garífuna music and dance, UNESCO heritage), passed down generation to generation

## Sub lesson 16 — material and immaterial sources

the guide's answer key classifies buildings, documents and coins as material sources and legends, oral tradition and Garífuna music/dance as immaterial ones

## Sub lesson 17 — classifying sources for research

sources are sorted by character and by nature — documentary (inscriptions, letters, books), oral (songs, tales, legends) and audiovisual (music, documentaries) — the raw material the historian verifies before concluding

## Sub lesson 18 — how a historical investigation is done

choose a topic, set its scope, gather, evaluate, verify and synthesise the evidence, then present findings — working heuristically (testing vestiges) or dialectically (stages), with documentary and field techniques and helper sciences from numismatics to epigraphy; Bloch: history is "the science of men in time"

## Sub lesson 19 — classifying sources for research

the guide's key sorts sources as documentary, oral and audiovisual for classroom research

## Sub lesson 20 — how a historical investigation is done

the guide teaches the research steps, heuristic and dialectical methods, documentary and field techniques, and Bloch's "science of men in time"
