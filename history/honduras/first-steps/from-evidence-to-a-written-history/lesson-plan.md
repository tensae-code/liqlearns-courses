# Lesson plan — From evidence to a written history

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** First steps · **Lesson:** From evidence to a written history
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — History gets its own block: what History is, its sources, and my municipality and department through time — the first peoples, the Maya of Copán, and the making of the 18 departments (CURRICULUM.md).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 21 Buscando huellas de nuestro pasado: las fuentes** — without sources there is no past to know; sources are sorted into archaeological (buildings, monuments, tools), written (letters, laws, chronicles, censuses) and oral tradition (elders' testimonies)
2. **§2 Lección 21: huellas materiales** — a Maya stela at Copán Ruinas and a colonial church at Gracias are vestiges that let the class reconstruct past societies, then children hunt the oldest buildings in their own plaza
3. **§3 Lección 38: los museos guardan la historia** — museums conserve and exhibit the objects and collections of the peoples — art, religion, gold-work, history, anthropology — so a visit becomes a lesson in the nation's past, protected by the 1998 patrimony law
4. **§4 Lección 21 Buscando huellas (guide)** — sources are classified with the class — archaeological, written, oral — and elders' testimonies are collected as tradition
5. **§5 Lección 21: visita a las huellas (guide)** — teacher, children and a family adult walk to the plaza to identify and draw the oldest house or building
6. **§6 Lección 38: museos (guide)** — museums are presented as society's memory store; specialised museums (art, religion, Indigenous peoples, gold-work) each hold one kind of evidence
7. **§7 Lección 20: fuentes y ciencias auxiliares** — the past is rebuilt indirectly from sources — primary against secondary, direct against indirect, a Maya stela against a book about it — with geography, archaeology, anthropology and other auxiliary sciences checking the story
8. **§8 Lección 21 Las huellas de la historia** — Tikal, Khafre's pyramid, Paestum and the Great Wall open a typology of material, documentary, oral and audiovisual sources; Ciudad Blanca in the Mosquitia is Honduras's own lost-city evidence, and "when an elder dies, a library burns"
9. **§9 Lección 20: fuentes y auxiliares (guide)** — teams define and exemplify each source type and name the auxiliary sciences that make results credible
10. **§10 Lección 21 Las huellas de la historia (guide)** — world vestiges (Tikal to the Great Wall) frame the four source natures, with elders named as living libraries
11. **§11 Lección 20: fuentes y proceso de investigación** — sources are classed primary (Copán itself) against secondary (a book on the Maya), and oral, written, material and iconographic; the historian must be objective, define the problem first, and rebuild the past from what survives
12. **§12 Lección 20: fuentes (guide)** — pupils narrate a historical event of their own community, then classify the sources it would rest on
13. **§13 Lección 20: métodos de investigación histórica** — valid historical research runs problem, questions, data collection from written and oral sources, analysis, conclusions — with biographical and chronological methods beside it
14. **§14 Lección 20: métodos (guide)** — the research steps and the biographical and chronological methods are the guide's method core
15. **§15 material and immaterial sources** — sources are testimonies that can be touched (buildings, documents, coins) or not (legends, oral tradition, Garífuna music and dance, UNESCO heritage), passed down generation to generation
16. **§16 material and immaterial sources** — the guide's answer key classifies buildings, documents and coins as material sources and legends, oral tradition and Garífuna music/dance as immaterial ones
17. **§17 classifying sources for research** — sources are sorted by character and by nature — documentary (inscriptions, letters, books), oral (songs, tales, legends) and audiovisual (music, documentaries) — the raw material the historian verifies before concluding
18. **§18 how a historical investigation is done** — choose a topic, set its scope, gather, evaluate, verify and synthesise the evidence, then present findings — working heuristically (testing vestiges) or dialectically (stages), with documentary and field techniques and helper sciences from numismatics to epigraphy; Bloch: history is "the science of men in time"
19. **§19 classifying sources for research** — the guide's key sorts sources as documentary, oral and audiovisual for classroom research
20. **§20 how a historical investigation is done** — the guide teaches the research steps, heuristic and dialectical methods, documentary and field techniques, and Bloch's "science of men in time"
