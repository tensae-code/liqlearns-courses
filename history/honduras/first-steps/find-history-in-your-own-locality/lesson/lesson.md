# Find history in your own locality

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Find history in your own locality" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 23 Esta es nuestra comunidad: historia de La Ceiba

La Ceiba begins as a hamlet of 20 Pech families sheltering under a great sacred ceiba tree, is then settled by Garífuna and others, and grows into the city of the Carnival of Friendship

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 25 El pasado de nuestra comunidad

Morazán's statue in Tegucigalpa and José Cecilio del Valle's house in Choluteca open a hunt for each town's own monuments, historic places and important people

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 23 Esta es nuestra comunidad (guide)

La Ceiba's Pech-and-ceiba-tree origin story is the model; every town's song, name and patronal fair is treated as its history

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 25 El pasado de nuestra comunidad (guide)

children list their community's historic places and people, then build one monument from recycled material

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 22 El municipio a través del tiempo

old photographs (Comayagüela 1928, the old Presidential House) show buildings changed by weather and neglect; the class dramatises three municipal stories — independence, Morazán's birth, the capital's move from Comayagua to Tegucigalpa

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 22 El municipio a través del tiempo (guide)

the municipality's illustrious people — painters, poets, merchants, singers — are named, and its relevant events dramatised

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Find history in your own locality as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
