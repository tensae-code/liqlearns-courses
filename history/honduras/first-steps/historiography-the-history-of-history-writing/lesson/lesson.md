# Historiography the history of history writing

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Historiography the history of history writing" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 20: periodización

time itself is divided to be understood — prehistory (before writing) against history, then Ancient, Medieval, Modern and Contemporary ages, and special histories (of art, law, literature) branch off

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 20: periodización (guide)

prehistory/history and the four ages are taught with the sayings "history repeats itself" and "a people that does not know its history is condemned to repeat it"

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 21 La historia como ciencia social: dos periodizaciones

two rival maps of the past are set side by side — the traditional (prehistory/history, ages) and the historical-materialist (primitive community, slavery, feudalism, capitalism) which periodises by ways of organising production, not by dates

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 21: dos periodizaciones (guide)

pupils complete the materialist timeline and contrast it with the traditional one in writing

## Sub lesson 5 — how the writing of history changed

history begins in Greece as moralising chronicle of kings and wars, turns ecclesiastical in the Middle Ages, becomes a social science in the 18th century, and the Annales school finally widens it to everyday life, culture and feelings — with photo-history and film as new sources

## Sub lesson 6 — how the writing of history changed

the guide teaches the same arc — Greek moralising chronicle, medieval ecclesiastical history, 18th-century social science, the Annales school's everyday-life history and photo/film sources
