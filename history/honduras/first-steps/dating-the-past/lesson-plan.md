# Lesson plan — Dating the past

**Course:** History — Honduras · **Title:** First steps · **Lesson:** Dating the past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First stories at home: my own time, my family and my community — and the nation through its symbols, its anthem and Morazán (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Dating the past as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First stories at home: my own time, my family and my community — and the nation through its symbols, its anthem and Morazán — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Lección 20 Ayer, hoy y mañana** — a fallen leaf was on the branch yesterday, dreams today and is blown away tomorrow; time is measured in hours, days and years, and Comayagua, founded 8 December 1537, turns 482 in 2019
2. **§2 Lección 21 Lo que pasó y lo que está pasando** — birthdays and civic feasts return on the same date each year with a duration and a sequence; September becomes the Mes de la Patria because it holds the acts commemorating independence
3. **§3 Lección 22: El árbol genealógico** — grandparents, parents, brothers and sisters are placed on a drawn family tree so the child can see where their own story joins the family's
4. **§4 Lección 20 Ayer, hoy y mañana (guide)** — past, present and future are taught through clocks and calendars and the founding date of the children's own community, then and now
5. **§5 Lección 21 Lo que pasó y lo que está pasando (guide)** — duration and sequence are taught through recurring feasts, ending on Morazán's portrait and the question of which event his month celebrates
6. **§6 Lección 22: árbol genealógico (guide)** — the teacher has each child build the family tree and relate their own events to the events of each relative
7. **§7 Lección 20: la línea de tiempo** — a life — first steps 1990, bicycle 1998, sister born 2004, graduation 2009 — is laid on a timeline so important facts stand in the order they happened
8. **§8 Lección 20: línea de tiempo (guide)** — each child builds a personal timeline of important dates, the guide's model for ordering any past
9. **§9 Lección 21: la Edad de Piedra y los metales** — Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic in sequence, then copper, bronze (copper plus tin) and iron — each metal making stronger tools and weapons and speeding civilisation
10. **§10 Lección 21: piedra y metales (guide)** — the Stone Age stages and the copper–bronze–iron sequence are drawn as one line
