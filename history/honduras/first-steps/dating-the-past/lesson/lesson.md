# Dating the past

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Dating the past" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 20 Ayer, hoy y mañana

a fallen leaf was on the branch yesterday, dreams today and is blown away tomorrow; time is measured in hours, days and years, and Comayagua, founded 8 December 1537, turns 482 in 2019

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 21 Lo que pasó y lo que está pasando

birthdays and civic feasts return on the same date each year with a duration and a sequence; September becomes the Mes de la Patria because it holds the acts commemorating independence

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 22: El árbol genealógico

grandparents, parents, brothers and sisters are placed on a drawn family tree so the child can see where their own story joins the family's

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 20 Ayer, hoy y mañana (guide)

past, present and future are taught through clocks and calendars and the founding date of the children's own community, then and now

## Sub lesson 5 — Lección 21 Lo que pasó y lo que está pasando (guide)

duration and sequence are taught through recurring feasts, ending on Morazán's portrait and the question of which event his month celebrates

## Sub lesson 6 — Lección 22: árbol genealógico (guide)

the teacher has each child build the family tree and relate their own events to the events of each relative

## Sub lesson 7 — Lección 20: la línea de tiempo

a life — first steps 1990, bicycle 1998, sister born 2004, graduation 2009 — is laid on a timeline so important facts stand in the order they happened

## Sub lesson 8 — Lección 20: línea de tiempo (guide)

each child builds a personal timeline of important dates, the guide's model for ordering any past

## Sub lesson 9 — Lección 21: la Edad de Piedra y los metales

Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic in sequence, then copper, bronze (copper plus tin) and iron — each metal making stronger tools and weapons and speeding civilisation

## Sub lesson 10 — Lección 21: piedra y metales (guide)

the Stone Age stages and the copper–bronze–iron sequence are drawn as one line

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Dating the past as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
