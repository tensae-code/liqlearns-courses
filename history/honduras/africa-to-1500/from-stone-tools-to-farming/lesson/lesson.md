# From stone tools to farming

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From stone tools to farming" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lección 21: de nómadas a sedentarios

bands of hunters, fishers and gatherers who know fire, bury their dead and paint caves become, with the discovery of agriculture (maize, beans, potato, yuca) and domesticated dog, turkey, alpaca and llama, settled farming tribes

## Sub lesson 2 — Lección 21: sedentarios (guide)

the class hunts evidence of first settlers around its own community and describes what it finds

## Sub lesson 3 — Lección 22: de la piedra al fuego y la agricultura

a 2.5-million-year timeline runs first humans, stone tools, fire (400,000), cave painting (20,000), then agriculture and herding (10,000) — cooking, warmth and light from fire change the species' life

## Sub lesson 4 — Lección 22: aportes prehistóricos (guide)

fire, farming, herding, tools and houses are listed as prehistory's still-used inheritance

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of From stone tools to farming as Honduras's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
