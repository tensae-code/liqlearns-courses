# History — Honduras course — ABOUT

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Honduras's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How the course is organised

One course for Honduras, organised by levels, never grades (see CURRICULUM.md). Lessons sit under title folders named for the placements' own units (`first-steps/`, `africa-to-1500/`, `africa-after-1500/`, `ancient-world/`, `modern-world/`). Every placement in the placements file stocks into exactly one lesson, with exactly one provenance row in that lesson's `lesson/sources.md`. Placements at `liqlearns-courses-work/expansion-history-2026-11-15/honduras-placements.md` — 414 placements in total, the count of lines containing `story beats:` in the file; the lines govern where a book header's printed tally disagrees.

Records as filed: 38 records / 20 distinct files (see reading-log-hn.md) — TSV release URLs and books.json pushed paths name the same books; read via grab.py (release route where a TSV URL exists, tree route otherwise), cover verified with pdftoppm/pdfinfo before reading. All books are combined Ciencias Sociales (History + Geography + Civics/Ethics); History parts placed, Geography/Civics parts named in each book header only. Book 14's single guide covers BOTH Grade 7 teacher records in the targets — the release copy and the books.json "teacher-alt" record are byte-identical files (same MD5), so they are filed once. Books are numbered 1–19 in this course, in filing order.

## The way of the books

Each book's own way is carried in its entry above. The arc they make together: History starts from the child's own time, family and community, and the nation enters through its symbols, its anthem and Morazán; Grade 2 gives History its own block (what History is, its sources, the municipality and department through time); Grade 3 is the first full national survey in the book's own three periods (prehispánico, colonial, independiente), each figure taught by one deed; Grade 4 widens the lens to Central America as one historical region, the "patria grande", with integration taught as Morazán's unfinished dream; Grade 5 scales to the Americas — three civilisations and three colonial empires side by side, the independence wave, the continental Liberal Reform, the Cold War and neoliberal Honduras; Grade 6 is a world survey run on two rival periodisations at once. The secondary cycle then deepens each lens: Grade 7 is the deep national narrative (rival Honduran periodisations, the prolonged conquest, the two independence actas, the Federal Republic, the enclave reform, the road to 1982, read partly through Galeano's "Las venas abiertas" excerpts, plot-summarised only); Grade 8 widens to the whole continent in depth (historiography from chronicle to the Annales school, the Federation with Morazán's testament in full, state-building, border settlements, the Mexican Revolution, Peronism); Grade 9 closes with the world survey — the two rival periodisations taught side by side without choosing, both world wars, decolonisation, the Cold War with Honduras's own place noted inside it, and the Gulf War taught as "immediate history", the first war broadcast live. The teacher guides mirror their student books lesson for lesson and add the teaching moves — elders brought in as living sources, children interviewing parents and grandparents, local history dramatised, double timelines — and their placements are stocked into the same lessons as evidence beside the student-book tellings.
