## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Formas de vida durante el Paleolítico** — Nomad hunter-gatherers master fire and stone tools as Australopithecus gives way through Homo habilis and erectus to Neanderthals and Homo sapiens.
2. **§2 Formas de vida durante el Mesolítico** — The ice retreats, the last hunter-gatherers invent hooks, nets, boats and the first pottery, and caves give way to the first open-air villages.
3. **§3 El Neolítico y la revolución agrícola** — People notice a buried seed sprouts; farming and herding make villages sedentary, populations grow and work specialises.
4. **§4 La Edad de los Metales** — Copper, then harder bronze, then iron give farming and war stronger tools, just as the first civilisations rise.
5. **§5 Edad de Piedra: Paleolítico y Neolítico** — Hunter-gatherer nomads in bands of twenty to fifty paint cave walls and worship fertility; farming then turns predators into producers, villages appear, surpluses are bartered and population grows.
6. **§6 Edad de los Metales: cobre, bronce y hierro** — Soft copper gives way to hard bronze, and the Hittites guard the secret of iron — a metal that abounds everywhere — until it remakes tools and war; megaliths like Stonehenge rise in the same age.
7. **§7 La comunidad primitiva** — Nomad gatherers, hunters and fishers share tools and divide the catch equally with no classes and no surplus; Neolithic farming settles them, the surplus is born, barter and merchants follow.
