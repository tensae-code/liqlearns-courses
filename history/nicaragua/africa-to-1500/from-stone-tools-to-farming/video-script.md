# Video script — From stone tools to farming

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: From stone tools to farming.

Nomad hunter-gatherers master fire and stone tools as Australopithecus gives way through Homo habilis and erectus to Neanderthals and Homo sapiens.

The ice retreats, the last hunter-gatherers invent hooks, nets, boats and the first pottery, and caves give way to the first open-air villages.

People notice a buried seed sprouts; farming and herding make villages sedentary, populations grow and work specialises.

Copper, then harder bronze, then iron give farming and war stronger tools, just as the first civilisations rise.

Hunter-gatherer nomads in bands of twenty to fifty paint cave walls and worship fertility; farming then turns predators into producers, villages appear, surpluses are bartered and population grows.

Soft copper gives way to hard bronze, and the Hittites guard the secret of iron — a metal that abounds everywhere — until it remakes tools and war; megaliths like Stonehenge rise in the same age.

Nomad gatherers, hunters and fishers share tools and divide the catch equally with no classes and no surplus; Neolithic farming settles them, the surplus is born, barter and merchants follow.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
