# Video script — Heritage what it is and why it matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Heritage what it is and why it matters.

Each community's patron-saint festival, costumes and dances are presented as inherited celebrations the children must recognise as their own.

Customs handed down in the municipality are collected as living heritage the children themselves practise.

Indigenous art and tradition survive as the department's cultural legacy, not a vanished past.

Traditional celebrations carry the department's beliefs and customs forward each year.

What the first peoples left — language, food, craft, belief — is named a cultural legacy still present in Nicaragua today.

The colonial centuries also leave a mixed cultural legacy — language, religion, towns and customs that Nicaraguans still live.

Prehistory's legacy is counted in four columns — farming surpluses, social ranks, the first cities and states, and cave art, ritual dance and burial customs.

Two colonial legacies are compared — Spanish Catholic imposition resisted and kept alive in secret on the Pacific side, English-backed Miskitu self-rule on the Caribbean side.

The medieval legacy is counted in Romanesque round arches and Gothic pointed ones, Dante's journey through hell, purgatory and heaven, and the chivalric code of courtesy and protecting the weak.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
