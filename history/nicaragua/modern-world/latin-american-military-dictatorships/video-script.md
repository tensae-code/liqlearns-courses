# Video script — Latin american military dictatorships

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Latin american military dictatorships.

Born of the 1929 crisis and Cold War confrontation, dictatorships concentrate power with US support and secret police — Ubico in Guatemala, Hernández Martínez in El Salvador, Somoza García in Nicaragua from 1937.

Trujillo rules the Dominican Republic to 1961, Batista ties Cuba to the US, Pinochet's 1973 coup bombs La Moneda and opens Chile's violent dictatorship, and Bordaberry dissolves Uruguay's parliament.

After 1945 the United States swaps open military intervention for economic, political and military dominance over the region's regimes.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
