# Nicaragua independence and central american federation

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Nicaragua independence and central american federation" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — La lucha por la independencia: causas internas y externas

Discontent inside the colony and revolutions outside it combine to make independence thinkable, then unavoidable.

## Sub lesson 2 — Levantamientos populares; influencia del resto de América

Popular uprisings across Spanish America spark Central America's own revolts.

## Sub lesson 3 — Nicaragua participa en levantamientos independentistas

Nicaraguans join the independence risings in León and Granada, risking punishment before freedom is declared.

## Sub lesson 4 — Sucesos y proclamación de la Independencia

On 15 September 1821 independence is proclaimed in Guatemala and Central America breaks from Spain without a long war.

## Sub lesson 5 — Nicaragua ante la anexión a México

Freedom is immediately tested — the provinces are annexed to Iturbide's Mexico and Nicaraguan towns split over accepting it.

## Sub lesson 6 — La Federación Centroamericana y Francisco Morazán

The five provinces federate in 1823; Morazán, elected 1830, fights to hold the union together against its breakers.

## Sub lesson 7 — El proceso independentista: antecedentes

Three hundred years of absolutist monarchy concentrate all power in Spain, and the colony's own grievances prepare the break.

## Sub lesson 8 — Movimientos preindependentistas

Hidalgo's 1810 rising and Morelos carry Mexico's revolt; in Nicaragua over 8,000 people with machetes and sticks surround the governor's house in León on 13 December 1811 and form their own junta, Granada following on 22 December.

## Sub lesson 9 — La independencia llega a Nicaragua: Acta y Acta de los Nublados

León's conservative Diputación answers Guatemala's 15 September Act with its own cloudy counter-act; Granada rejects it, and the León-Granada elite rivalry ignites decades of civil war.

## Sub lesson 10 — Anexión a México

Iturbide's Plan de Iguala — Catholic exclusivity, independence as constitutional monarchy, union of castes — wins the monarchists and pulls Central America into the Mexican empire.

## Sub lesson 11 — La República Federal Centroamericana

The 1824 constitution raises liberal hopes of a modern trading nation between two oceans; Morazán defends the federation 1829-38, but cholera, revolt and state autonomy break it, Nicaragua leaving on 30 April 1838.

## Sub lesson 12 — El proceso independentista: antecedentes y Reformas Borbónicas

A monarchy absolute in name only tries to recentralise with the Bourbon Reforms from 1786; more peninsular officials and higher taxes turn criollo resentment into an independence movement.

## Sub lesson 13 — Movimientos pro-constitucionales 1811-1814

Risings in León, Masaya, Rivas and Granada answer the Cádiz crisis; the Belén conspiracy of 1813 is punished as an example and Nicaraguan priest Tomás Ruiz is among the accused.

## Sub lesson 14 — Causas y repercusiones de la independencia

Bourbon grievances meet Enlightenment ideas and foreign revolutions; independence is proclaimed for Central America on 15 September 1821, signed by Larreynaga and José Cecilio del Valle.

## Sub lesson 15 — Anexión a México y la resistencia de Cleto Ordóñez

Union with Iturbide's Mexico is decreed on 5 January 1822 and Mexican troops enforce it; Granada's Cleto Ordóñez, a popular caudillo carried on his soldiers' shoulders, leads the republican resistance with indigenous archers.

## Sub lesson 16 — República Federal y el unionismo centroamericano

Fearing Mexico and Gran Colombia and admiring the United States, the five provinces adopt the federal Constitution of 22 November 1824.

## Sub lesson 17 — Francisco Morazán y el experimento liberal

With Morazán presiding the Federation and Gálvez in Guatemala, civil marriage, labour contracts, jury trials and habeas corpus make Central America's first liberal experiment.

## Sub lesson 18 — Fin de la Federación Centroamericana

Three federal wars, empty treasuries, localism and the 1837 cholera epidemic break the union; its legacy is lost lives and a ruined economy.

## Sub lesson 19 — Movimientos populares en Nicaragua 1811

Nicaraguan producers smuggle past the Guatemalan monopoly down the San Juan river; on 13 December 1811 some 8,000 people with knives, machetes and sticks surround the governor's house in León, Granada, Rivas and Masaya and form their own juntas.
