## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Sistema de autonomía de la Costa Caribe** — Nicaragua's Caribbean Coast wins its own autonomy system, a distinct history and self-government inside one country.
2. **§2 Incorporación de la Mosquitia (1894)** — Zelaya extends Nicaragua's authority to the Atlantic, reincorporating the Mosquito Reserve created by the 1860 Treaty of Managua as the Department of Zelaya.
3. **§3 Títulos reales, personería jurídica y Ley 445** — Royal titles granted from 1591, legal personality laws of 1914-18 and the 2003 Law 445 on communal property still defend indigenous land and self-government today.
4. **§4 Ocupación inglesa en la Costa Caribe** — Pirates, loggers and planters remake the Caribbean coast; in the 18th century England allies with the Miskitu and consolidates hegemony, leaving Afro-Caribbean culture such as the Palo de Mayo.
5. **§5 Evolución de la Región Atlántica: protectorado y enclave** — Britain's protectorate over the Miskitu monarchy keeps the new state out of its own Caribbean from 1821 to 1860; the Zeledón-Wyke Treaty recognises Nicaraguan sovereignty on paper while a foreign timber and banana enclave grows at Bluefields.
6. **§6 La reincorporación de la Mosquitia (1894)** — Zelaya sends General Cabezas, who lowers the Mosquitia flag at Bluefields on 12 February 1894; a Miskitu convention legitimises reincorporation and the 1905 Harrison-Altamirano Treaty ends British claims.
7. **§7 La Costa Caribe: de disputa imperial a región** — Disputed by Spain, England and the US for its canal position since 1633, the old Mosquito Coast is at last treated as part of Nicaragua's own story.
8. **§8 La Ley de Autonomía de 1987** — Literacy reaches the coast in Spanish, English and Miskitu; the 1987 Autonomy Law and Article 181 recognise indigenous communal land, languages and self-government for the first time.
9. **§9 Ocupación inglesa en el Caribe** — Spain never conquers the Caribbean coast; pirates found Bluefields about 1590 and English traders ally with the Miskitu, raiding Granada and León in 1685 while contraband slowly replaces Spanish trade.
10. **§10 Evolución de la Región del Caribe: la monarquía misquita y el protectorado** — England legitimises its presence through a Miskitu royal family it sustains; from 1821 to 1860 a British protectorate keeps the new Nicaraguan state out of its own Caribbean, the reserve lasting until 1894 under its last hereditary chief, Robert Henry Clarence.
11. **§11 La economía y la sociedad en el Caribe de Nicaragua, siglo XIX** — Gold, timber and rubber enclaves work the Coco and Escondido rivers while Moravian missionaries, arrived at Bluefields in 1849, reshape Caribbean culture and even run the reserve's finances.
12. **§12 Incorporación de la Mosquitia (1894)** — General Cabezas occupies Bluefields and the Miskitu Convention of 20 November 1894 renames the reserve the Department of Zelaya under the Nicaraguan flag, keeping its revenues for local benefit.
