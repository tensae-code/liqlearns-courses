# Lesson plan — Nicaragua us interventions and zeledon

**Course:** History — Nicaragua · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Nicaragua us interventions and zeledon

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Intervenciones norteamericanas: primera intervención y Pactos Dawson** — From 1909 the US intervenes directly, imposing governments; the Dawson Pacts hand Adolfo Díaz's Nicaragua's finances to US control.
2. **§2 La gesta de Benjamín Zeledón** — Zeledón refuses to surrender to the marines in 1912 and dies fighting; his body is paraded to frighten others — and instead inspires Sandino.
3. **§3 Situación antes de 1924 y el Lomazo de Emiliano Chamorro** — Chamorro's 1925 Lomazo coup seizes the Loma fortress and restarts civil conflict.
4. **§4 La Guerra Constitucionalista de 1926** — Sacasa's constitutionalists rise against the coup regime, and their war brings the marines back in force.
5. **§5 Intervenciones norteamericanas 1909-1933** — Taft's Big Stick continues the Monroe Doctrine; US control is tabulated sector by sector — coffee, energy, railways and banking at 100 percent.
6. **§6 Los Pactos Dawson** — US recognition is bought with pacts that script Nicaragua's politics — a constituent assembly, Estrada president, Díaz vice-president, Zelayistas excluded.
7. **§7 Tratado Bryan-Chamorro (1914)** — Nicaragua grants the US perpetual exclusive rights to an interoceanic canal by the San Juan river route, signed in Washington on 5 August 1914.
8. **§8 Pacto del Espino Negro (1927)** — Under a black-thorn tree at Tipitapa on 4 May 1927, Stimson makes Moncada lay down his nearly victorious constitutionalist army — one general refuses, and his refusal becomes Sandino's war.
9. **§9 La gesta heroica de Benjamín Zeledón** — Ordered to surrender in 1912, Zeledón answers that he wants no cowardly peace for his children and dies defending Nicaragua's honour — the example that guides Sandino.
10. **§10 La Guerra Constitucionalista (1926)** — A violated constitution drives liberals to war in 1926; US intervention decides the outcome and prepares the Espino Negro settlement.
11. **§11 Las intervenciones norteamericanas 1909-1933** — Marines land in 1912 to guard the imposed government of Díaz and Chamorro, occupy Corinto and Bluefields and stay, in changing forms, until 1933.
12. **§12 La gesta de Benjamín Zeledón, 1912** — Zeledón's 800 artisans, professionals and peasants, badly armed and some barefoot, refuse the marines' offers of gold and honours; he signs his own death sentence rather than surrender at Coyotepe.
13. **§13 Los intereses norteamericanos en Nicaragua** — With Panama's canal built, Washington still holds Nicaragua militarily so no rival canal can compete; the 1917 Financial Plan puts every national revenue in a foreign-owned bank.
14. **§14 El Lomazo y la Guerra Constitucionalista** — Chamorro's 1925 Lomazo coup makes President Solórzano his puppet; the constitutionalist war that answers it brings workers and peasants led by Sandino into national politics for the first time.
15. **§15 El Pacto del Espino Negro, 1927** — Under a black-thorn tree at Tipitapa on 4 May 1927, Stimson makes Moncada's nearly victorious liberals surrender unconditionally to the US-imposed order.
