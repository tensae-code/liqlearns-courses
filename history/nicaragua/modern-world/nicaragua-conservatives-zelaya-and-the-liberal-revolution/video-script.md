# Video script — Nicaragua conservatives zelaya and the liberal revolution

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Nicaragua conservatives zelaya and the liberal revolution.

After the war, Tomás Martínez's pact starts three stable conservative decades that build the republic's administration and first departments.

Cattle, commerce and new roads grow, and coffee begins remaking land and class in Nicaragua.

The coffee bourgeoisie backs Zelaya, who defeats the conservatives on 11 July 1893 and pushes a liberal constitution and modernising reforms.

US pressure forces Zelaya out in 1909, closing the liberal era; the chapter closes with the culture of the century that produced Darío.

After the National War an oligarchic peace holds to 1893 — property qualifications for voting and office, income from customs and monopolies, no direct taxes.

From 1858 recovery makes Nicaragua Central America's most stable economy; coffee ties it to the world market, expropriation creates an agricultural proletariat, and the first bank opens in 1888.

Internal revolts and war costs meet external US hostility; the execution of US mercenaries Cannon and Groce on 16 November 1909 gives Washington its pretext, and Zelaya resigns on 16 December.

A small circle of intermarried families rules oligarchically for thirty years on customs and monopoly income, with no direct taxes.

Coffee spreads from the Sierras de Managua to Matagalpa and Jinotega and becomes the country's main source of foreign earnings; telegraph lines from 1876 and lake steamers stitch the republic together.

Zelaya's liberals expropriate church and communal land for coffee, build the first professional army, supreme court and police — and Zelaya, re-elected by a loyal assembly, never calls elections in sixteen years.

Zelaya cancels US concessions, borrows in London instead of New York and seeks canal partners in Germany and Japan; when US mercenaries Cannon and Groce are executed for mining the San Juan, the Knox Note demands his fall and he resigns on 16 December 1909.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
