## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Proceso independentista de América Latina** — From the 1776 US example and Haiti's 1791 slave revolution, a freedom spirit kept alive since the conquest drives the whole continent toward independence by 1898.
2. **§2 Sublevaciones preindependentistas: Túpac Amaru** — José Gabriel Condorcanqui, taking the last Inca's name, protests the mita, hangs the corregidor and shakes southern Peru and Bolivia for a year before Spain executes him.
3. **§3 Las campañas de San Martín y Bolívar; el caso de Brasil** — Hidalgo's Grito de Dolores opens Mexico's road, Central America proclaims independence 15 Sept 1821, San Martín and Bolívar free the south between 1817 and 1825, and Brazil parts from Portugal when Dom Pedro refuses to sail home.
4. **§4 Resultados de la independencia latinoamericana** — Tribute ends and republics are born, but Gran Colombia and the Central American federation fragment, caudillos and civil wars follow, and economic dependence on Europe survives.
5. **§5 Mujeres en la independencia** — Manuela Sáenz, Leona Vicario, Policarpa Salavarrieta and Juana Azurduy are pictured as heroines of the independence wars, not bystanders.
6. **§6 Antecedentes de la Independencia: el panorama internacional** — Voltaire, Rousseau and Montesquieu arm the criollos with ideas just as Napoleon invades Spain in 1808 and the captive king leaves the colonies to govern themselves.
7. **§7 La Revolución Haitiana** — On an island disputed by three empires, Haiti's enslaved people make their rebellion itself the independence; Toussaint Louverture proclaims the abolition of slavery.
8. **§8 Procesos independentistas hispanoamericanos** — Bourbon reforms raise taxes and submission, Napoleon's invasion precipitates the break, and the US and French examples show the colonies the way.
9. **§9 El proceso independentista: las Juntas de Gobierno** — Criollos first govern in the captive king's name — Chuquisaca, La Paz and Quito in 1809 are shot down, but Caracas, Buenos Aires, Bogotá and Santiago hold in 1810.
10. **§10 Independencia de América del Sur** — Morillo's counter-revolution stalls emancipation until 1820; Bolívar then frees New Granada and Ecuador and dreams a united Gran Colombia, but the 1826 Panama Congress already shows the anarchy.
11. **§11 Participación de la mujer en el proceso independentista** — Indigenous, black and mestiza women march, nurse, cook, spy and fight — Juana Ramírez's women's battalion, Policarpa Salavarrieta's espionage, Manuela Cañizares's salon where the first cry is raised — yet official history remembers mostly the ladies.
12. **§12 Consecuencias de la Independencia** — Peninsulares are replaced by criollos and mestizos, but Spanish domination only changes hands — Latin America falls under the economic neocolonialism of England and the United States.
