# Video script — Nicaragua sandino and sovereignty

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Nicaragua sandino and sovereignty.

Sandino is presented as a duty to remember, a Nicaraguan who defended the homeland, his story to be known by every generation.

Sandino is born 18 May; the book pairs him with Darío as Nicaragua's two greatest expressions and links him forward to Carlos Fonseca.

Sandino, born 18 May in Niquinohomo, becomes "General of Free Men and Women"; the book says you cannot speak of Nicaragua or homeland without him.

Blanca Arauz stands beside Sandino in the defence of national sovereignty, the telegraphist wife who carried the general's messages in wartime.

Sandino, General of Free Men and Women, is retold as the patriot who would not accept foreign occupation.

When Mena is captured Zeledón takes command of the 1912 resistance, holds out at La Barranca and El Coyotepe in Masaya with only courage and love of country, and is captured and killed after an unequal fight.

Sandino, working in Mexico, hears of the invasion, returns, and on 26 Oct 1926 rises at the San Albino mine rather than lay down arms.

Sandino's small army beats the marines by refusing long battles, vanishing on paths only locals know; after the marines leave, Sandino is murdered in 1934.

The boy who saw Zeledón's corpse carted past in 1912 refuses the 1927 pact, marries Blanca Aráuz and raises the Army in Defence of National Sovereignty from 29 men and 40 rifles at El Chipote.

The marines, unable to beat the EDSN, build a native Guardia; after peace talks with Sacasa, Sandino is seized and murdered on 21 February 1934, and over 300 Sandinista peasants are massacred at Wiwilí.

Sandino refuses the Espino Negro betrayal and withdraws to the Segovias mountains to restore the violated constitution and expel the intervention forces; solidarity committees rise from New York to Buenos Aires as marine columns are wiped out.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
