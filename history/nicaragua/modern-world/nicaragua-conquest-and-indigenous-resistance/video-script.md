# Video script — Nicaragua conquest and indigenous resistance

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Nicaragua conquest and indigenous resistance.

Spain claims the "discovered" lands and sends conquest; Gil González Dávila is welcomed by Cacique Nicaragua in 1523 and baptisms follow, but further north Diriangén asks for time to think — and prepares resistance instead.

Gil González Dávila enters from Rivas in 1523, is received by Cacique Nicaragua, and conquest follows the welcome.

Indigenous peoples do not submit quietly; Diriangén's answer to the Spanish becomes the emblem of resistance.

The conquest's bill is counted — population loss, exploitation and a changed land — with Diriangén's defiance recalled as the counter-story.

Conquest comes from the Pacific, not the Caribbean — Gil González reaches the Mar Dulce, then Pedrarias's captain Hernández de Córdoba founds Granada and León in 1524 and the two conquerors fight each other for the land.

Colonisation and resistance begin simultaneously in 1523; Wheelock's "indigenous roots of the anti-colonial struggle" frames resistance as a tradition that never ends.

Gil González reaches Cacique Nicarao in 1523 and their famous dialogue astonishes the Spaniards; days later, on 17 April, Diriangén's men attack — the first armed defence of the land — but Spanish arms and indigenous division decide the outcome.

The indigenous population collapses catastrophically; Las Casas preaches against the abuses in León and Granada and Bishop Valdivieso is murdered by the Contreras brothers for defending the indigenous, while the New Laws of 1542 arrive too late.

Diriangén's and Nicaragua's attacks are the first organised combats against a foreign invader in Nicaragua's history; in twenty-seven years of exceptional cruelty under Pedrarias and Contreras, a population estimated at a million falls to 10,000.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
