## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 12 de Octubre: Día de la resistencia indígena** — Spain claims the "discovered" lands and sends conquest; Gil González Dávila is welcomed by Cacique Nicaragua in 1523 and baptisms follow, but further north Diriangén asks for time to think — and prepares resistance instead.
2. **§2 Gil González y la conquista de Nicaragua** — Gil González Dávila enters from Rivas in 1523, is received by Cacique Nicaragua, and conquest follows the welcome.
3. **§3 Resistencia indígena** — Indigenous peoples do not submit quietly; Diriangén's answer to the Spanish becomes the emblem of resistance.
4. **§4 Consecuencias de la conquista española en Nicaragua** — The conquest's bill is counted — population loss, exploitation and a changed land — with Diriangén's defiance recalled as the counter-story.
5. **§5 Proceso de conquista de Nicaragua** — Conquest comes from the Pacific, not the Caribbean — Gil González reaches the Mar Dulce, then Pedrarias's captain Hernández de Córdoba founds Granada and León in 1524 and the two conquerors fight each other for the land.
6. **§6 Resistencia indígena como consecuencia de la conquista** — Colonisation and resistance begin simultaneously in 1523; Wheelock's "indigenous roots of the anti-colonial struggle" frames resistance as a tradition that never ends.
7. **§7 Proceso de conquista en Nicaragua y resistencia indígena** — Gil González reaches Cacique Nicarao in 1523 and their famous dialogue astonishes the Spaniards; days later, on 17 April, Diriangén's men attack — the first armed defence of the land — but Spanish arms and indigenous division decide the outcome.
8. **§8 Consecuencias de la Conquista** — The indigenous population collapses catastrophically; Las Casas preaches against the abuses in León and Granada and Bishop Valdivieso is murdered by the Contreras brothers for defending the indigenous, while the New Laws of 1542 arrive too late.
9. **§9 Resistencia indígena contra el dominio español en Nicaragua** — Diriangén's and Nicaragua's attacks are the first organised combats against a foreign invader in Nicaragua's history; in twenty-seven years of exceptional cruelty under Pedrarias and Contreras, a population estimated at a million falls to 10,000.
