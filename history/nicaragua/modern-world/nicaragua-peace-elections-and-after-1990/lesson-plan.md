## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Pacificación en Nicaragua: Esquipulas I y II** — Central America's presidents meet at Esquipulas and on 6-7 Aug 1987 sign procedures for a firm and lasting peace, choosing coexistence over war.
2. **§2 Elecciones de febrero de 1990 y Nicaragua desde 1990 hasta la actualidad** — The UNO wins the February 1990 elections and power changes hands by ballot, opening the Nicaragua of today.
3. **§3 Gobiernos liberales 1996-2000 y 2001-2006** — Alemán and Bolaños govern on free-market policies; the book charges corruption, privatised electricity and telephones, and rolled-back free health and education.
4. **§4 Gobierno de Reconciliación y Unidad Nacional y sus programas sociales** — The GRUN returns with social programmes presented as restoring what the liberal governments removed.
5. **§5 La lucha armada y los acuerdos de paz** — US-financed Contra war and Reagan's 1985 embargo besiege the Revolution; pressure aims to force negotiation, and peace talks follow.
6. **§6 Elecciones de 1990** — Under the peace accords, the US-backed UNO coalition of Violeta Barrios de Chamorro wins the 25 February 1990 elections with 54.74 percent to the FSLN's 40.82.
7. **§7 Transformaciones del gobierno de la UNO** — Chamorro abolishes patriotic military service, demobilises both armies and burns the weapons, while agrarian-reform projects are dismantled and rearmed ex-combatants reappear.
8. **§8 Los gobiernos neoliberales 1996-2006** — Alemán (1997) and Bolaños (2002) continue structural adjustment under IMF conditions while health and education are neglected, until debt relief under the HIPC initiative is negotiated.
9. **§9 El Gobierno de Reconciliación y Unidad Nacional desde 2007** — The returning GRUN runs its social programmes through women — Bono Productivo, Usura Cero, Hambre Cero, Plan Techo — under a "Christian, socialist, solidary" banner.
10. **§10 La lucha armada y los acuerdos de paz** — From 1981 the US funds the Contra army and imposes a trade embargo; the presidents' peace process answers, and Ortega's 1984 election with 67 percent is recognised abroad except in Washington.
11. **§11 El Gobierno de la UNO y los gobiernos neoliberales** — The UNO's Violeta Chamorro wins 55 percent in 1990 on war-weariness; demobilisation, privatisation and structural adjustment follow, Alemán wins in 1996, and unemployment and emigration to Costa Rica and the US deepen.
12. **§12 El Gobierno de Reconciliación y Unidad Nacional desde 2007** — The returning GRUN builds a "Christian, socialist, solidary" model of direct citizen power, running its programmes through women — Bono Productivo, Usura Cero, Plan Techo.
13. **§13 Los nuevos problemas y el modelo de poder ciudadano** — The book closes the century by turning to the GRUN's Christian, Socialist and Solidary citizen-power model, women's advance into political power after the 1920 US suffrage amendment, and the region's new problems of irregular migration and child poverty.
