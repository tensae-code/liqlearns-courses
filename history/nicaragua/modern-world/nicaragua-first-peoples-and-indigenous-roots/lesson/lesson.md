# Nicaragua first peoples and indigenous roots

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Nicaragua first peoples and indigenous roots" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Origen del nombre y de los primeros pobladores de mi municipio

Many town names are Nahuatl left by the first settlers; Managua, "surrounded by water", begins as fishing settlements on Lake Xolotlán.

## Sub lesson 2 — Influencia del medio geográfico en la forma de vida de los primeros pobladores

Lakes, rivers and land shape where the first peoples settle and how they live, a pattern each child traces in their own municipality.

## Sub lesson 3 — Manifestaciones culturales de los primeros pobladores del municipio

The first settlers leave cultural expressions that still mark the municipality's identity today.

## Sub lesson 4 — Primeros pobladores de mi departamento

Department names themselves — Matagalpa, Estelí, Masaya, Chontales, Chinandega — and river, hill, food and plant names prove an indigenous past under modern Nicaragua.

## Sub lesson 5 — Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento del departamento

Geography decides where department settlements grow, the same land-first logic scaled up from municipality to department.

## Sub lesson 6 — Actividades económicas de los primeros pobladores de mi departamento

The first peoples farm, fish and trade to live; their work is the department's first economy.

## Sub lesson 7 — Organización política y social de los primeros pobladores

Cacique-led indigenous societies organise power and community life before any Spanish town exists.

## Sub lesson 8 — Origen y forma de vida de los primeros pobladores de Nicaragua

The first peoples arrive and make lives across the territory that becomes Nicaragua, the root story every later chapter grows from.

## Sub lesson 9 — Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento; otras tribus que se establecieron

Tribe after tribe settles where water and land allow, so Nicaragua's map is layered with different peoples before Europeans come.

## Sub lesson 10 — Actividades económicas de los primeros pobladores

Farming, fishing and exchange feed the first communities; work organises their world.

## Sub lesson 11 — Organización política y social de los primeros pobladores

Caciques and social ranks order indigenous life; the book separates how they governed from how families and classes lived.

## Sub lesson 12 — Manifestaciones culturales: religión, costumbres, tradiciones, creencias y ritos

Religion and ritual are taught as the first peoples' way of explaining and holding their world together.

## Sub lesson 13 — Sociedad precolombina de Nicaragua

Before any European arrives, ranked indigenous societies with caciques, councils and markets already order life in the territory.

## Sub lesson 14 — Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento de Nicaragua

Two fertile coastal plains, two great lakes and river-crossed mountains decide where Nicaragua's first peoples settle.

## Sub lesson 15 — Organización política, tenencia de la tierra y economía precolombina

Nicarao caciques rule with the Monéxico council of elders; land is held collectively by families, cacao beans serve as money, and the council-run market anchors each village.

## Sub lesson 16 — Desarrollo y legado cultural de los grupos aborígenes

Migrant peoples from Mexico build temple-plaza towns with stepped sacrifice mounds, and their legacy still shapes Nicaraguan identity.

## Sub lesson 17 — Pobladores del Centro y Caribe: origen Macro-Chibcha y afrodescendientes

Matagalpa, Mayangna, Miskitu and Rama peoples of Chibcha origin share the centre and Caribbean with Afro-descendant Zambos, Creoles and Garífuna; war chiefs are elected for skill and dismissed for misconduct.

## Sub lesson 18 — Primeros pobladores de origen mesoamericano

War in central Mexico in the 7th-8th centuries pushes Chorotegas, Maribios and Nicaraos south; by about 800 CE they hold the Pacific strip from León to Rivas.

## Sub lesson 19 — Persistencia de los grupos aborígenes en el territorio actual

Today's towns are mapped to their ancestry — Chorotega Jinotega, Mozonte and Totogalpa, Nahoa Ostional and Salinas — the first peoples never vanished.

## Sub lesson 20 — El medio geográfico en Nicaragua

Nicaragua's lakes, plains and coasts decide where the first peoples can settle and how their cultures take shape.

## Sub lesson 21 — Sociedades precoloniales del Pacífico: la migración mesoamericana

The fall of Teotihuacán and Tula pushes Chorotegas, Maribios and Nicaraos south from Mexico between the 8th and 15th centuries; the Chorotegas reach Nicaragua about 800 CE and displace earlier peoples toward the centre.

## Sub lesson 22 — Organización política y social indígena

The Monéxico council of elders is elected by vote and in turn elects — and can remove — each town's cacique, so Pacific power is shared, not absolute.

## Sub lesson 23 — Organización económica: tierra, agricultura y subsistencia

Land is held by the community, never individually; farming feeds the village and cacao beans serve as money.

## Sub lesson 24 — La artesanía indígena

Deer-bone combs and hand-worked objects from farm, plant and hunt remains show a craft economy inside every village.

## Sub lesson 25 — El comercio y las relaciones de trabajo: los tiangues

Women traders fill the plaza markets where goods move by cacao money and barter, and only same-language traders are admitted.

## Sub lesson 26 — La cultura indígena: religión y sacrificio

Religion binds the whole culture together; offerings, including human sacrifice, are made at sacred places such as the Masaya volcano, the indigenous Popocatepetl.

## Sub lesson 27 — Grupos indígenas del Centro y Atlántico

The centre belongs to the Chontal-Matagalpa family of uncertain origin, the Caribbean rivers to Misumalpan and Chibcha-descended peoples such as the Rama, known mostly through language because documents are few.
