# Lesson plan — Nicaragua first peoples and indigenous roots

**Course:** History — Nicaragua · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Nicaragua first peoples and indigenous roots
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First stories at home: my homeland Nicaragua, its symbols and heroes, and the first peoples of my municipality and department (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Nicaragua first peoples and indigenous roots as Nicaragua's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First stories at home: my homeland Nicaragua, its symbols and heroes, and the first peoples of my municipality and department — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Origen del nombre y de los primeros pobladores de mi municipio** — Many town names are Nahuatl left by the first settlers; Managua, "surrounded by water", begins as fishing settlements on Lake Xolotlán.
2. **§2 Influencia del medio geográfico en la forma de vida de los primeros pobladores** — Lakes, rivers and land shape where the first peoples settle and how they live, a pattern each child traces in their own municipality.
3. **§3 Manifestaciones culturales de los primeros pobladores del municipio** — The first settlers leave cultural expressions that still mark the municipality's identity today.
4. **§4 Primeros pobladores de mi departamento** — Department names themselves — Matagalpa, Estelí, Masaya, Chontales, Chinandega — and river, hill, food and plant names prove an indigenous past under modern Nicaragua.
5. **§5 Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento del departamento** — Geography decides where department settlements grow, the same land-first logic scaled up from municipality to department.
6. **§6 Actividades económicas de los primeros pobladores de mi departamento** — The first peoples farm, fish and trade to live; their work is the department's first economy.
7. **§7 Organización política y social de los primeros pobladores** — Cacique-led indigenous societies organise power and community life before any Spanish town exists.
8. **§8 Origen y forma de vida de los primeros pobladores de Nicaragua** — The first peoples arrive and make lives across the territory that becomes Nicaragua, the root story every later chapter grows from.
9. **§9 Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento; otras tribus que se establecieron** — Tribe after tribe settles where water and land allow, so Nicaragua's map is layered with different peoples before Europeans come.
10. **§10 Actividades económicas de los primeros pobladores** — Farming, fishing and exchange feed the first communities; work organises their world.
11. **§11 Organización política y social de los primeros pobladores** — Caciques and social ranks order indigenous life; the book separates how they governed from how families and classes lived.
12. **§12 Manifestaciones culturales: religión, costumbres, tradiciones, creencias y ritos** — Religion and ritual are taught as the first peoples' way of explaining and holding their world together.
13. **§13 Sociedad precolombina de Nicaragua** — Before any European arrives, ranked indigenous societies with caciques, councils and markets already order life in the territory.
14. **§14 Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento de Nicaragua** — Two fertile coastal plains, two great lakes and river-crossed mountains decide where Nicaragua's first peoples settle.
15. **§15 Organización política, tenencia de la tierra y economía precolombina** — Nicarao caciques rule with the Monéxico council of elders; land is held collectively by families, cacao beans serve as money, and the council-run market anchors each village.
16. **§16 Desarrollo y legado cultural de los grupos aborígenes** — Migrant peoples from Mexico build temple-plaza towns with stepped sacrifice mounds, and their legacy still shapes Nicaraguan identity.
17. **§17 Pobladores del Centro y Caribe: origen Macro-Chibcha y afrodescendientes** — Matagalpa, Mayangna, Miskitu and Rama peoples of Chibcha origin share the centre and Caribbean with Afro-descendant Zambos, Creoles and Garífuna; war chiefs are elected for skill and dismissed for misconduct.
18. **§18 Primeros pobladores de origen mesoamericano** — War in central Mexico in the 7th-8th centuries pushes Chorotegas, Maribios and Nicaraos south; by about 800 CE they hold the Pacific strip from León to Rivas.
19. **§19 Persistencia de los grupos aborígenes en el territorio actual** — Today's towns are mapped to their ancestry — Chorotega Jinotega, Mozonte and Totogalpa, Nahoa Ostional and Salinas — the first peoples never vanished.
20. **§20 El medio geográfico en Nicaragua** — Nicaragua's lakes, plains and coasts decide where the first peoples can settle and how their cultures take shape.
21. **§21 Sociedades precoloniales del Pacífico: la migración mesoamericana** — The fall of Teotihuacán and Tula pushes Chorotegas, Maribios and Nicaraos south from Mexico between the 8th and 15th centuries; the Chorotegas reach Nicaragua about 800 CE and displace earlier peoples toward the centre.
22. **§22 Organización política y social indígena** — The Monéxico council of elders is elected by vote and in turn elects — and can remove — each town's cacique, so Pacific power is shared, not absolute.
23. **§23 Organización económica: tierra, agricultura y subsistencia** — Land is held by the community, never individually; farming feeds the village and cacao beans serve as money.
24. **§24 La artesanía indígena** — Deer-bone combs and hand-worked objects from farm, plant and hunt remains show a craft economy inside every village.
25. **§25 El comercio y las relaciones de trabajo: los tiangues** — Women traders fill the plaza markets where goods move by cacao money and barter, and only same-language traders are admitted.
26. **§26 La cultura indígena: religión y sacrificio** — Religion binds the whole culture together; offerings, including human sacrifice, are made at sacred places such as the Masaya volcano, the indigenous Popocatepetl.
27. **§27 Grupos indígenas del Centro y Atlántico** — The centre belongs to the Chontal-Matagalpa family of uncertain origin, the Caribbean rivers to Misumalpan and Chibcha-descended peoples such as the Rama, known mostly through language because documents are few.
