# Video script — Nicaragua independence and san jacinto

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Nicaragua independence and san jacinto.

Estrada, a soldier from Nandaime, grows up to command the patriots at the Battle of San Jacinto.

On 14 Sept Estrada's poorly armed patriots beat Walker's filibusters at San Jacinto, Andrés Castro felling an invader with a stone; on 15 Sept the country celebrates Central American independence in blue and white.

Pupils visit/read the inscription honouring "the illustrious General José Dolores Estrada", closing the San Jacinto story begun at his birth anniversary.

Historical events are kept alive by celebrating them; the unit frames discovery, resistance, autonomy, independence and heroes as one patriotic calendar.

The September patriotic festivals retell how Central America became independent and Nicaragua kept that freedom.

Liberal-conservative civil wars invite William Walker's filibusters; at the Acta de Matagalpa (20 Apr 1856) Nicaraguans unite across parties, and at dawn 14 Sept 1856 some 120 patriots under Estrada defeat 300 filibusters at Hacienda San Jacinto, Andrés Castro killing one with a stone.

Teacher Enmanuel Mongalo runs with a burning torch under enemy fire to set the filibusters' position alight, a schoolmaster turned hero of the Walker war.

Britain seizes Caribbean positions and the US and Britain rival over a canal route through Nicaragua, making the small country a prize.

Liberals and conservatives fight for power and their ambitions open the door to the foreign invader.

Walker arrives as a hired filibuster, makes himself president and re-legalises slavery, turning civil war into national war.

Central American armies unite against Walker and drive him out battle by battle.

At dawn on 14 Sept 1856 Estrada's outnumbered patriots hold Hacienda San Jacinto; Andrés Castro's stone becomes the war's most famous shot.

The war leaves social, economic and political costs, settled partly by the Jerez-Cañas treaty fixing the Costa Rica border.

Walker's filibusters, agents of Manifest Destiny aiming to "americanise" the continent, provoke the opposite — a defensive Central American unity and a shared Latin identity in the 1856-57 Campaña Nacional.

Troops from the sister republics march into Nicaragua's National War, making San Jacinto an isthmian victory.

The 1854 León-Granada civil war brings Walker's filibusters; teacher Enmanuel Mongalo burns their Rivas stronghold, Matagalpa archers join the allied Central American armies, and of 2,000 filibusters barely 250 survive — Walker is shot at Trujillo in 1860.

León's exiled liberals, unable to beat Granada's conservatives, hire Byron Cole to bring in Walker's filibusters — and a civil war becomes a national one.

Estrada fortifies the San Jacinto hacienda with 160 men and 66 Matagalpa indigenous archers; Andrés Castro wins his fame there, and on 1 May 1857 Walker surrenders, ending a war the book insists was fought for all Central America.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
