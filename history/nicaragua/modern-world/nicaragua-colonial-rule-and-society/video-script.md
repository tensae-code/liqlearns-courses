# Video script — Nicaragua colonial rule and society

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Nicaragua colonial rule and society.

León and Granada are founded as the first Spanish cities, planting colonial power in indigenous land.

Spain rules through a colonial administration built to command the provinces from above.

Indigenous towns are reorganised under colonial rule, their own authorities fitted into Spain's system.

The colonial economy rests on indigenous labour and on taking and working the land the indigenous farmed.

Colony sorts people into ranked groups — Spaniards, criollos, mestizos, indigenous and enslaved — and rank decides a life.

Missionaries evangelise the indigenous; when the New Laws try to protect them, governor Contreras rebels against the limits on his power.

English pirates, raiding slow Spanish galleons, ally with the Miskitu — expert turtle-fishers and sailors — so Spain never truly holds the Caribbean coast.

Granada and León are founded as conquest proceeds; survivors are congregated into 198 reducciones — supervised new towns that, paradoxically, preserve communal land and indigenous cabildos.

Cacao tribute collapses as the indigenous population falls, gold mining and pitch enrich colonisers, society hardens into peninsulares, criollos and dominated indigenous, mestizo and mulatto classes under León's governor.

Hernández de Córdoba founds León and Granada in 1524 to fix royal authority on conquered land, each city with its assigned purpose.

The New Laws dress forced labour as encomienda and repartimiento; indigo haciendas worked by indigenous labour and cattle estates in Matagalpa, Estelí and Chontales carry the colonial economy.

Colonial society is ranked strictly by origin and skin colour — peninsulares on top, then criollos, with the dominated below.

Nicaragua is governed as a province of the Kingdom of Guatemala, divided into five partidos that later become the departments, each town under its cabildo.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
