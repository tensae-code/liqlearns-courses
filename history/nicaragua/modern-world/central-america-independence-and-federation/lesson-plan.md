# Lesson plan — Central america independence and federation

**Course:** History — Nicaragua · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Central america independence and federation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Central America: the isthmus's first peoples, the Maya and the Aztecs, conquest and colony, independence and the Federation, foreign interests, liberal reform, dictatorships and peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Central america independence and federation as Nicaragua's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Central America: the isthmus's first peoples, the Maya and the Aztecs, conquest and colony, independence and the Federation, foreign interests, liberal reform, dictatorships and peace — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Independencia de Centroamérica: causas internas y externas** — Internal grievances and external revolutions converge; independence is the end of a long process, not a single day.
2. **§2 Movimientos populares de 1811 en Centroamérica** — In 1811 popular movements rise in San Salvador, León and Granada a decade before independence is won.
3. **§3 Proclamación de la Independencia de Centroamérica** — On 15 September 1821 the Act is signed in Guatemala and the isthmus leaves Spain.
4. **§4 Próceres de la Independencia de Centroamérica** — The próceres earn the title by ending colonial rule — among them priest Tomás Ruiz of Chinandega, tortured and held nearly seven years in irons for the Belén conspiracy.
5. **§5 Centroamérica ante la anexión a México** — The new provinces are annexed to Mexico and must fight again, this time to be free of an American empire.
6. **§6 La Federación Centroamericana** — The five provinces try to stay one country in a federation, the isthmus's great failed union.
7. **§7 Independencia de México (Nueva España) y Centroamérica** — Hidalgo's Grito is crushed, but when the Cádiz Constitution threatens criollo interests, Iturbide and Guerrero join in the Plan de Iguala of 24 February 1821.
