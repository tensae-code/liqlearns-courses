# History — Nicaragua course — ABOUT

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Nicaragua's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How the course is organised

One course for Nicaragua, organised by levels, never grades (see CURRICULUM.md). Lessons sit under title folders named for the placements' own units (`first-steps/`, `africa-to-1500/`, `ancient-world/`, `modern-world/`). Every placement in the placements file stocks into exactly one lesson, with exactly one provenance row in that lesson's `lesson/sources.md`. Placements at `liqlearns-courses-work/expansion-history-2026-11-15/nicaragua-placements.md` — 388 placements in total, the count of lines containing `story beats:` in the file; the lines govern where a book header's printed tally disagrees. Books are numbered 1–13 in this course, in filing order.

Records as filed: 32 records / 13 distinct books — TSV blob/release URLs and books.json pushed paths name the same books (duplicates counted once); read via grab.py, cover verified with pdfinfo/pdftoppm before reading. All main books are combined Estudios/Ciencias Sociales (History + Geography + Civics); History parts placed, Geography/Civics parts named in each book header only. Two Grade 9/10 Geography modules are pure Geography (record-only).

## The way of the books

Each book's own way is carried in its entry above. The arc they make together: History enters at age 7–8 almost entirely as Efemérides (anniversary stories) plus a whole "Mi Patria Nicaragua" unit of symbols, heroes and traditions; Grade 3 is a Geography-first year where History arrives as the child's own municipality and department; Grade 4 carries the entire national story in one sweep, framed as sovereignty defended against foreign intervention; Grade 5 widens the lens to all Central America; Grade 6 closes primary school with a full world-history sweep always read back through Latin America's experience; the two Grade 7 books retell the whole national history for secondary students (one a self-study module for rural distance students, one the regular-classroom full-year textbook); Grade 8 widens from Nicaragua to all the Americas and always ends back in Nicaragua; Grade 9 is pure world history under identical headings, civilisation by civilisation; and Grade 10 closes with Geography and Economics, History surviving only as the five socioeconomic formations that open the Economics half.
