# Hands-on — The Atlantic Slave Trade: How It Ended

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A trade that ran for centuries, that whole coasts were built around — how does such a thing end? And when it ends, is the story over? Ask both questions before you read: what abolished the trade, and what did its ending leave standing?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The slave trade is abolished — but abolition leaves African societies weakened, and opens the door to a new European advance. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
