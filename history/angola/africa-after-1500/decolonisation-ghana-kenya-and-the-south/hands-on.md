# Hands-on — Decolonisation: Ghana, Kenya and the South

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
End the course where the book ends: with the map changing colour. In one famous year, 1960, country after country raises its own flag. Ask: how did peoples who were taxed, conscripted and ruled from abroad win their countries back — and which system held out longest against the change?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Nationalism rises and new states are born: the first countries win independence, the "Year of Africa" (1960) breaks the colonial map, liberation movements free the remaining colonies — and South Africa's apartheid is the last system to fall. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
