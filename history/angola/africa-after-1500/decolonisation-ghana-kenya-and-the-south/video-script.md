# Video script — Decolonisation: Ghana, Kenya and the South

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Nationalism rises and new states are born: the first countries win independence, the "Year of Africa" (1960) breaks the colonial map, liberation movements free the remaining colonies — and South Africa's apartheid is the last system to fall.

The colonial system implanted in the last lesson did not stand forever. Across Africa **nationalism rose** — the determination of peoples to rule themselves — and the **struggle for the independences** began: organised movements demanding, and fighting for, the end of colonial rule.

The first countries **won independence**, and then came the flood: the **"Year of Africa", 1960**, when state after new state was born and the **colonial map broke** — the paper division drawn at Berlin unravelling in a single year of flags. What Berlin had joined together on a map, Africa's peoples took apart in fact.

Where independence was refused, **national liberation movements** freed the remaining colonies by struggle — the road Angola itself knows, among the last colonies held and freed by arms and organisation. And one system held out longest of all: **South Africa's apartheid**, the racial order that was the **last system to fall**, closing the long arc the book has walked.

So the course ends where its first page promised: Africa, humanity's cradle, its kingdoms taxed and traded, its people shipped and colonised — standing at last in its own states again. The story Angola's pupils are given is one rising arc, and they are its heirs.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Angola.
