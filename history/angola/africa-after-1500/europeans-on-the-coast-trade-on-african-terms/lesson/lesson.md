# Europeans on the Coast: Trade on African Terms

## Do it first

Stand on Angola's coast five hundred years ago and watch the horizon: a sail, then another — ships from a far continent, feeling their way down a coast they do not know. What are they looking for? And what will their coming open? This lesson stands at that shore.

From the 1400s, Europe's sailors push out onto the ocean. The **Portuguese** lead, and other Europeans follow: their ships **work down Africa's coasts**, cape by cape and river by river, **seeking sea routes** — above all a way around Africa to the riches of the East — **and trade** along the way.

This is the **European maritime expansion**, and Africa meets it first at the shore. The ships come to kingdoms like Kongo — organised states that trade on their own ground. At the start, the Europeans are visitors on the coast, dependent on the African powers whose lands they have reached.

But the book files this theme with a shadow over it: this expansion **opens the era that turns into the slave trade**. The sea routes the ships are feeling for will soon carry a traffic in human beings across the Atlantic — the subject of the next lessons. The sails on the horizon are the first page of that story.

Remember where the last level ended: a continent of kingdoms, trading in every direction. The ships do not enter an empty world — they enter that one, and they change it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Portuguese and other European ships work down Africa's coasts seeking sea routes and trade — opening the era that turns into the slave trade. Give one real example from a coast or port they know in Angola — the shore where ships from far away arrive, and name the lesson's key words: European maritime expansion, Portuguese ships, sea routes, coasts, the coming slave trade era. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
