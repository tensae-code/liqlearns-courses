# Hands-on — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The ships of the last lesson came seeking routes and trade. Now the trade itself turns: the ships begin to load people. Ask the hardest question of this course: who was shipped, in what numbers — and who, on the African side, helped feed such a trade, and at what cost to their own societies?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Captives are shipped across the Atlantic in vast numbers — and some African rulers and traders feed the trade while their own societies bleed people. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
