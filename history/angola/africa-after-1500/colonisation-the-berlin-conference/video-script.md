# Video script — Colonisation: The Berlin Conference

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Explorers map the interior, the European powers meet in Berlin to divide Africa on paper, then armies occupy the territories — and Africans resist the takeover.

The new European advance began with **exploration**: European explorers travelled and **mapped Africa's interior** — its rivers, lakes and peoples — knowledge that served those who would follow them. The mapped continent could now be claimed, piece by piece.

Then came the division on paper. The European powers **met in Berlin** — the **Berlin Conference** — and agreed among themselves how Africa would be **divided** between them, drawing the claims on the map without a single African ruler at the table. What was agreed in Berlin was **conquest and effective occupation** made official: a claim counted only when a power actually held the ground.

So the paper was followed by soldiers. European **armies occupied the territories**, turning drawn lines into colonial rule. And the book names the answer Africa gave: **Africans resisted the takeover** — the occupation was fought, not simply received. Colonisation was something done to resisting peoples, at the point of arms.

Angola's pupils read this as their own country's near past: the lines drawn in Berlin ran through their land too. The next lesson looks inside the colonial system those armies installed — how it administered, controlled and exploited.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Angola.
