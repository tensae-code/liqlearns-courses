# Hands-on — Colonisation: The Berlin Conference

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Picture a room in Berlin, far from Africa, in winter. Around a table, men who have never seen most of the continent draw lines on its map. Before that room, explorers walked the interior; after it, armies came. Ask: how does a continent get divided on paper — and what happens when the paper meets the people?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Explorers map the interior, the European powers meet in Berlin to divide Africa on paper, then armies occupy the territories — and Africans resist the takeover. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
