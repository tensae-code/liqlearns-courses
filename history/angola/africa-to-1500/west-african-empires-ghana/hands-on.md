# Hands-on — West African Empires: Ghana

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of two things people far apart both desperately want: in the forest lands, gold; in the desert lands, salt — without which food cannot be kept and bodies cannot live. Caravans must cross a whole desert to swap them. Whoever stands at the crossing and taxes every load grows rich. That is this lesson's empire.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ghana grows powerful by taxing the gold-and-salt trade across the Sahara — the first of the great West African empires this course meets. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
