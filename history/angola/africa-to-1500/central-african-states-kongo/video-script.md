# Video script — Central African States: Kongo

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
South of the forest, the Kingdom of Kongo rules a wide, organised state — the pre-colonial power standing on Angola's own doorstep.

South of the great forest, in the lands at Angola's northern edge, stood the **Kingdom of Kongo** — a **wide, organised state**, ruled by its kings long before Europeans reached its coasts. Of all the great kingdoms in this theme, Kongo is the one that stood on **Angola's own doorstep**.

Kongo was no small chiefdom. It was a real state: a king at its head, provinces under its rule, and an ordered government over a broad territory. When the book lines Kongo up beside Ghana and Mali, it is making a point Angola's pupils are meant to carry: statehood in Africa did not arrive with the Europeans — Africans had built wide, organised states of their own, here, at home, among them.

Hold that fact carefully, because the course is about to turn. The ships that appear in the next level will not find empty land or peoples without order. They will find kingdoms like Kongo — and what follows between the ships and the kingdoms is the hardest part of Africa's story.

One more southern state completes this theme first: Mwene Mutapa, in the gold country toward the Indian Ocean.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Angola.
