# Video script — Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
On the banks of the Nile a farming kingdom grows rich and organised — Ancient Egypt, the first great civilisation this course meets, studied by where it sat and how its people lived.

In the north-east of Africa flows the **Nile**, and on its banks the desert gives way to a narrow band of rich, watered land. There, thousands of years ago, farming people settled — and out of their farming grew one of the oldest and greatest civilisations on earth: **Ancient Egypt**.

The book follows Egypt the way a historian should: first **where it sat** — the river valley and its setting in the continent — and then **how its people lived**: the farming that fed them, the organised kingdom that ruled them, and the rich life that farming surplus made possible. A kingdom that can feed itself can build, can organise, can endure — and Egypt endured for ages, becoming the ancient world's standing wonder.

Egypt matters in this course for a simple reason: it is an **African** civilisation, born on African ground from an African river. When Angola's pupils open the story of the ancient world, the first great kingdom they meet is not from another continent — it stands on their own.

From Egypt the course turns south and west, to the great kingdoms and empires Africans built across the continent — Ghana, Mali, Kongo and Mwene Mutapa.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Angola.
