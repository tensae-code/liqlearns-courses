# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Imagine your whole dinner depends on what you can find growing wild today — some days plenty, some days nothing. Now imagine your family learns to hunt animals together, to fish the river together, and to gather in an organised way. What has changed? You have taken the first step this lesson tells.

The first people lived in **dependence on nature**: they ate what the land happened to give — fruit where it ripened, roots where they grew — and when a place gave no more, they moved on. It was a living taken entirely as it came, with nothing made and nothing planned.

Then comes the turn this lesson teaches. Early humans **stop merely gathering what nature gives** and begin to work for their living together: they **hunt** animals, they **fish** the rivers and waters, and they **gather** in company, sharing the work and the catch. Hunting and fishing are not gifts of nature lying ready — they take skill, tools, cooperation and courage.

That is why the book marks this as the **first step from dependence toward making a living**. The human being is no longer only a taker of what the season offers; the human being has begun to act on the world — to pursue, to catch, to provide. Everything later in this course — farming, herding, kingdoms and trade — grows out of this first step.

And it happens together. A hunt is a community's work; a catch is shared. The first human communities of the last lesson are now communities that provide for themselves — still in Africa, still at the very beginning of the human story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Early humans take the first step beyond dependence on nature: instead of merely taking what nature gives, they begin to hunt, fish and gather together — the first step toward making a living. Give one real example from their own home or town in Angola — a fishing place, a market, or food gathered from the land near them, and name the lesson's key words: dependence on nature, hunting, fishing, gathering, making a living, first communities. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
