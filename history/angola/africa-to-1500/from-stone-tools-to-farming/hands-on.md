# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine your whole dinner depends on what you can find growing wild today — some days plenty, some days nothing. Now imagine your family learns to hunt animals together, to fish the river together, and to gather in an organised way. What has changed? You have taken the first step this lesson tells.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Early humans take the first step beyond dependence on nature: instead of merely taking what nature gives, they begin to hunt, fish and gather together — the first step toward making a living. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
