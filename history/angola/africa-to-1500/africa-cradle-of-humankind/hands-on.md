# Hands-on — Africa, Cradle of Humankind

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Hold a baby in your mind for a moment — a cradle is where a baby begins. Now think of the whole human family as that baby. Where was its cradle? Dig in the ground of different continents, the way archaeologists do, and ask: where do the oldest human bones of all come from?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The first human bones are found in Africa, so Africa is humanity's cradle: there the hominids slowly evolved toward Homo sapiens, living first on the wild fruit and roots nature gave them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
