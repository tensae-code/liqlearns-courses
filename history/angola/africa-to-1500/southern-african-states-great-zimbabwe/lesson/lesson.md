# Southern African States: Great Zimbabwe

## Do it first

Follow the gold one more time — not north across the desert now, but east, toward a different sea. In the southern gold country a state holds the mines and the routes to the coast. Ask: what does control of gold country give a state that faces the Indian Ocean?

The last of this theme's great states stands in the **south**. There the **Mwene Mutapa** state held the **gold country** — the land whose gold fed the trade routes running east to the **Indian Ocean coast**. (The book files this state under Tema 3.1's great kingdoms and empires; this lesson carries the title the course files it under — the southern African states, Great Zimbabwe's country.)

Mwene Mutapa's power follows the same deep pattern as Ghana's and Mali's, turned toward a different sea: **control the gold, and control the trade in it**. Where Ghana taxed loads crossing the desert, Mwene Mutapa held the gold lands themselves and the road from them to the coast, where ocean traders waited.

So the theme closes with the whole map filled in: Egypt on the Nile, Ghana and Mali on the desert routes, Kongo south of the forest, and Mwene Mutapa in the southern gold country. Africa before 1500 was a continent of kingdoms and empires, trading in every direction.

Then, on the coasts, sails appear. The next level tells what the European ships brought — and what they began.
