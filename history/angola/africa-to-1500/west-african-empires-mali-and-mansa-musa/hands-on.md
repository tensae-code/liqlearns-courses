# Hands-on — West African Empires: Mali and Mansa Musa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The first empire of the gold-and-salt lands has faded. The caravans still cross, the gold still moves — but a new, bigger power now holds the routes. Ask: what does a successor empire do with the same trade, at a larger scale, under a famous king?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Mali succeeds Ghana as the trading empire of the western Sudan — larger and richer, ruled in its golden age by Mansa Musa. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
