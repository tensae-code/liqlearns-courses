# West African Empires: Mali and Mansa Musa

## Do it first

The first empire of the gold-and-salt lands has faded. The caravans still cross, the gold still moves — but a new, bigger power now holds the routes. Ask: what does a successor empire do with the same trade, at a larger scale, under a famous king?

Empires rise on the shoulders of empires. As Ghana's power faded, **Mali** took its place as the **trading empire of the western Sudan** — the same gold-and-salt world Ghana had taxed, now held by a successor state that was **larger and richer** than the one before it.

Mali's golden age has a name the whole world of its time learned: **Mansa Musa**, its ruler. Under him Mali stood at its height — an empire whose wealth, carried on the same Sahara routes, made its king's name famous far beyond Africa. When this course names a golden age, it means exactly this: the moment when an empire's riches, order and fame all peak together.

The pattern of the last lesson holds, at a greater scale: position on the trade routes, taxed and guarded, turned into power. Mali shows the pattern grown — a bigger territory, a richer court, a wider fame.

The course now leaves the western Sudan and turns to the forest's southern edge and the far south — to Kongo, standing on Angola's own doorstep, and to Mwene Mutapa in the gold country of the south.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Mali succeeds Ghana as the trading empire of the western Sudan — larger and richer, ruled in its golden age by Mansa Musa. Give one real example from their own town in Angola — a business or a market that took over from an older one and grew bigger, and name the lesson's key words: Mali, Mansa Musa, golden age, western Sudan, successor empire, gold-and-salt trade. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
