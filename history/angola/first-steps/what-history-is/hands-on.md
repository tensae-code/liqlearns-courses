# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask an older person one question: "What was this place like when you were my age?" Listen carefully. Then ask yourself: how could you check whether what they remember is complete? You have just felt the problem this lesson names: the past is gone, and we can only reach it by investigating what it left behind.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History — its name comes from the Greek word "histor", meaning investigation — is the science that studies human life through time, and it does not work alone: geography, anthropology, linguistics, chronology and archaeology help it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
