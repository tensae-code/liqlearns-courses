# Video script — What History Is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History — its name comes from the Greek word "histor", meaning investigation — is the science that studies human life through time, and it does not work alone: geography, anthropology, linguistics, chronology and archaeology help it.

Every people has a past, but the past does not sit still waiting to be read. The men and women who lived it are gone; their houses fall, their words fade. So how can anyone know what really happened long ago? Angola's book opens its whole course with that question, and it gives the answer a name: **History**.

The word comes from the Greek **"histor"**, which means **investigation** — a searching-out. History is the **science that studies human life through time**: who people were, where they lived, what they did, and what changed, age by age. It is an investigation, not a guess: the historian gathers evidence, tests it, and only then tells the story.

And History does not work alone. It is helped by a team of sister sciences. **Geography** tells where things happened, on what land and rivers. **Anthropology** studies human beings and their ways of life. **Linguistics** follows languages, whose old words carry old movements and meetings. **Chronology** puts events in their right order in time. And **archaeology** digs up the things people left in the ground — tools, pots, bones and walls — the evidence of people who never wrote anything down.

That team matters most exactly where this course is going: to Africa's earliest people, who left no books at all. Their story can only be investigated — from bones, stones and the ground itself. So the course begins with the method, and then turns it on the continent where the human story itself began.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Angola.
