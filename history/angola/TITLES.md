# History — Angola course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Angola's
own — built from Angola's Grade 6 *Manual de História*
student book, in Portuguese. Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below
sit inside Levels 1–3, in path order — what the learner
proves, step by step. The grade the book comes from lives
only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: African history as one
rising arc — the method first (what History is), then
Africa as humanity's cradle and its first communities, its
ancient civilisations and great kingdoms, the slave-trade
era, colonisation, and the liberation struggles — with
Angola's own past carried inside the continental story.
That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced —
a learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead"
hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — first steps: what History is and the first
people** (Manual de História, 6.ª Classe, Tema 1 and the
opening of Tema 1.2):

- `first-steps/` — Level 1, the opening title. What History
  Is (History, from the Greek "histor" — investigation —
  the science that studies human life through time, helped
  by geography, anthropology, linguistics, chronology and
  archaeology).
- `africa-to-1500/` — Levels 1–2, Africa's beginnings and
  its ancient kingdoms in one title folder, in path order.
  Its Level 1 lessons: Africa, Cradle of Humankind (the
  first human bones found in Africa; the hominids evolving
  toward Homo sapiens on wild fruit and roots) · From Stone
  Tools to Farming (the first step beyond dependence on
  nature — hunting, fishing and gathering together).

**Level 2 — ancient Africa and its great kingdoms** (Temas
2–3.1), in `africa-to-1500/`, in path order:

- Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom (the rich, organised
  farming kingdom on the Nile's banks, studied by where it
  sat and how its people lived) · West African Empires:
  Ghana (powerful by taxing the gold-and-salt trade across
  the Sahara, first of the great West African empires) ·
  West African Empires: Mali and Mansa Musa (Ghana's
  successor, larger and richer, in its golden age under
  Mansa Musa) · Central African States: Kongo (the wide,
  organised kingdom south of the forest, on Angola's own
  doorstep) · Southern African States: Great Zimbabwe (the
  Mwene Mutapa state, filed under this title — the southern
  gold country and its trade toward the Indian Ocean coast).

**Level 3 — Africa after 1500: slave trade, colonisation
and freedom** (Temas 4–6):

- `africa-after-1500/` — Level 3, the after-1500 story in
  one title folder, in path order. Europeans on the Coast:
  Trade on African Terms (Portuguese and other European
  ships working down the coasts seeking sea routes and
  trade, opening the slave-trade era) · The Atlantic Slave
  Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle (captives shipped
  in vast numbers; some African rulers and traders feeding
  the trade while their societies bled people) · The
  Atlantic Slave Trade: How It Ended (abolition — its
  causes and consequences: societies weakened, the door
  opened to a new European advance) · Colonisation: The
  Berlin Conference (exploration, the division of Africa on
  paper at Berlin, conquest and effective occupation,
  resisted) · Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule (the
  colonial system implanted — administration, control and
  the exploitation of African labour) · Decolonisation:
  Ghana, Kenya and the South (nationalism, the first
  independences, the "Year of Africa" 1960, the liberation
  movements, and apartheid the last system to fall).
