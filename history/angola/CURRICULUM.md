# History — Angola course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Angola inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Angola's book in hand is one —
the Grade 6 *Manual de História* student book (the Grade 5
book in the record is UNREAD, named in ABOUT.md) — and its
own themes are the ladder. The book teaches African history
as one rising arc: what History itself is, Africa as
humanity's cradle and its first communities, the ancient
civilisations and great kingdoms, the slave-trade era,
colonisation, and the liberation struggles — with Angola's
own past carried inside the continental story rather than
in a separate national book. So the ladder follows the
book's own themes in order: the method and the first people
(Level 1, Tema 1 and Tema 1.2's opening), ancient Africa and
its great kingdoms (Level 2, Temas 2–3.1), and Africa after
1500 — slave trade, colonisation and freedom (Level 3,
Temas 4–6). The ladder stops where the book in hand stops,
not because Angola teaches nothing more.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Grade 6 História student book — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: What History Is and the First People** —
   the discipline itself first, as the book's Tema 1 presents
   it: History, from the Greek "histor" (investigation), the
   science that studies human life through time, helped by
   geography, anthropology, linguistics, chronology and
   archaeology; then the first people — Africa as humanity's
   cradle, the hominids evolving toward Homo sapiens on wild
   fruit and roots, and the first step beyond dependence on
   nature: hunting, fishing and gathering together.
   3 lessons, in two titles: `first-steps/` (1 lesson) and
   `africa-to-1500/` (first 2 lessons).

2. **Ancient Africa and Its Great Kingdoms** — the book's
   Temas 2–3.1: Ancient Egypt, the rich, organised farming
   kingdom on the Nile; then the great kingdoms and empires
   one by one — Ghana taxing the gold-and-salt trade across
   the Sahara, Mali succeeding it, larger and richer, in
   Mansa Musa's golden age, Kongo ruling a wide, organised
   state on Angola's own doorstep, and Mwene Mutapa holding
   the southern gold country and its trade to the Indian
   Ocean coast. 5 lessons, in one title: `africa-to-1500/`
   (last 5 lessons).

3. **Africa After 1500: Slave Trade, Colonisation and
   Freedom** — the book's Temas 4–6: the European maritime
   expansion down the coasts, opening the slave-trade era;
   the Atlantic slave trade itself — captives shipped in
   vast numbers, some of Africa's ruling classes feeding the
   trade while their societies bled people; its abolition,
   which left societies weakened and opened the door to a
   new advance; colonisation — exploration, the Berlin
   Conference's division on paper, effective occupation
   resisted, and the colonial system implanted on African
   labour; and the struggle for the independences —
   nationalism, new states, the "Year of Africa" (1960),
   the liberation movements, and apartheid the last system
   to fall. 6 lessons, in one title: `africa-after-1500/`
   (6 lessons).

**Totals: 14 lessons** (Level 1: 3 · Level 2: 5 · Level 3: 6
— 14 placements, all from one student book).
