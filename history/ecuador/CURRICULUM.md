# History — Ecuador course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Ecuador inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Ecuador's books in hand teach the
past in the country's own progression: history begins at age
seven as the child's own past — family, parish, heritage
(Level 1, from the Grade 2 and Grade 3 books and the reading
booklets); then the deep past and the ancient world whole —
Africa's first humans, the river civilisations, Greece, Rome,
the faiths, and America's Maya, Aztecs and Incas, closing on
Ecuador's own first peoples and the Inca turn (Level 2, from
the Grade 11 Bachillerato book with the Grade 5 book's opening
temas); then conquest, colony and independence — slavery and
Maroon resistance, the Renaissance and the voyages, Cajamarca,
the colonial centuries, the French Revolution's waves, and
Ecuador's own road through Pichincha to the republic of 1830
(Level 3, from the Grade 5 and Grade 8 books); and last
Ecuador and the modern world — the republic's own story from
the 1800s through Alfaro, the banana boom, oil and restored
democracy to dollarised 21st-century Ecuador, inside Latin
America's unfinished liberation and the modern world's
movements, industries, Cold War and economic schools (Level 4).
The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Estudios Sociales and Historia books of the Ministerio de
Educación del Ecuador — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list, the combined-book contents map, and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps → History at Home and Heritage** — what
   history is (traces, time, culture and work, with women
   carrying half the hidden story), history found in your own
   locality (the family interview, the parish and canton and
   the people they honour), historiography (Velasco's first
   history of Quito weighed against its critics), and heritage
   in twelve parts (the minga, the World Heritage treasures,
   the peoples and their festivals, Susana Reyes's dance, and
   the legends — La Dama Tapada, Cochasquí, La Princesa Súa).
   4 lessons, in one title: `first-steps/` (4 lessons).

2. **The Deep Past and the Ancient World** — Africa the
   cradle of humankind, stone tools to farming, bands growing
   into states, Ecuador's first peoples and chiefdoms, the
   river civilisations (Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, India),
   Greece and Crete with the Amazons, Rome and its law,
   feudalism, Byzantium, the rise of Islam, Christianity, the
   faiths that shaped states, the Maya, the Aztecs, the Incas
   and the Qhapaq Ñan, and the Inca expansion into Ecuador
   ending with Atahualpa. 19 lessons, in three titles:
   `africa-to-1500/` (3 lessons), `ancient-world/` (16 lessons).

3. **Conquest, Colony and Independence** — slavery before the
   Atlantic trade and Queen Nanny's Maroon resistance, the
   Renaissance, the scientific revolution and Enlightenment,
   the voyages of Columbus, Vespucci, Magellan and Portugal,
   conquest and colony in Latin America and in Ecuador
   (Cajamarca, 1533), colonial art, the French Revolution of
   1789, nationalism crossing the Atlantic, American
   independence, and Ecuador's own independence and Gran
   Colombia, closing with the founding of 1830. 14 lessons,
   in two titles: `africa-after-1500/` (2 lessons),
   `modern-world/` (12 lessons).

4. **Ecuador and the Modern World** — Ecuador in the 1800s,
   the Liberal Revolution to the banana boom, the oil boom and
   the return to democracy, Ecuador in the 21st century,
   Latin America after independence, its social movements and
   revolutions and its crisis, migration and development,
   women through history, social movements, media and women,
   capitalism's rise, the Industrial Revolution, the Cold War,
   and the history of economic thought. 13 lessons, in one
   title: `modern-world/` (13 lessons).

**Totals: 50 lessons** (Level 1: 4, Level 2: 19, Level 3: 14,
Level 4: 13) — 68 placements stocked, all on student books.
