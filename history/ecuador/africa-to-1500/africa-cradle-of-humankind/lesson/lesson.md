# Africa: Cradle of Humankind

## Do it first

Stand up and walk across the room on two feet, then try to imagine doing every daily task on all fours. List three things upright walking and free hands made possible.

In Africa ape-like ancestors walk upright, make tools and become human across the Palaeolithic. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In Africa, ape-like ancestors walked upright, made tools and became human across the long Palaeolithic — humanity's story begins on one continent. Give one real example from your own life, and name the lesson's key words: Africa: Cradle of Humankind. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: In Africa, ape-like ancestors walked upright, made tools and became human across the long Palaeolithic — humanity's story begins on one continent.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
