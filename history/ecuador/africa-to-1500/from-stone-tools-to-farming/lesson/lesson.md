# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Sort six drawings — a hunter, a seed basket, a goat, a village house, a potter, a market stall — into 'before farming' and 'after farming'. Defend each choice.

Farming, animal domestication, sedentary villages, work specialisation and trade replace hunting and gathering. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Neolithic revolution replaced hunting and gathering with farming, animal domestication, settled villages, specialised work and trade. Give one real example from your own life, and name the lesson's key words: From Stone Tools to Farming. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The Neolithic revolution replaced hunting and gathering with farming, animal domestication, settled villages, specialised work and trade.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
