# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Hold a class vote on a real small question. Then ask: who was allowed to speak, who counted the votes, who was left out? That gap is where the Greek story starts.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Greek city-states invented democracy, philosophy and theatre and spread them around the Mediterranean — and Greek myth tells of the Amazons, a women-only warrior people ruled by a queen, whose wars with Heracles, Theseus and Achilles the Greeks never stopped retelling. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
