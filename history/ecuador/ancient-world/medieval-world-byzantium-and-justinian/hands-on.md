# Hands-on — Medieval World: Byzantium and Justinian

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Find Rome and Constantinople on a map. One empire's western half fell; the eastern half stood for a thousand more years. List two things a walled city on a sea strait has that an inland city does not.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Constantinople kept Rome alive in the East for a thousand years, in Greek and Orthodox splendour, after the West had fallen. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
