# Hands-on — Ecuador Founded, 1830

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw Ecuador split down the Andes: coast on one side, sierra on the other. List what each side sells, eats and wants from a government. That split is the new republic's first problem.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Gran Colombia broke apart in 1830, and Juan José Flores became first president of a poor, disunited republic split between coast and sierra oligarchies. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
