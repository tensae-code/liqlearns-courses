# Hands-on — Ecuador: Oil Boom and Return to Democracy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Oil is found under the jungle. Money pours in; then the debt comes due and people emigrate. Draw the boom and the bust as two halves of one circle.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The 1964 agrarian reform banned the huasipungo, oil money boomed in the 1970s, democracy returned in 1979 — then debt crisis, mass migration and Indigenous movements challenged the neoliberal republic. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
