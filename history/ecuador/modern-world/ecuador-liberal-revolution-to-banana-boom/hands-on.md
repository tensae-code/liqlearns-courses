# Hands-on — Ecuador: Liberal Revolution to Banana Boom

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A revolutionary wins, transforms the country — and is dragged to his death by a crowd. Then bananas, not battles, remake the coast. Say what this teaches about how change really comes.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Eloy Alfaro's Liberal Revolution won and Alfaro was dragged to his death in 1912; plutocracy and the 1925 Juliana revolution followed, Velasco Ibarra returned again and again, war with Peru struck in 1941, and the banana boom remade the coast. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
