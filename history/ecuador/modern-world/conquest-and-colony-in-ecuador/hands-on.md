# Hands-on — Conquest and Colony in Ecuador

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A room filled with treasure is offered for one man's life — and he is killed anyway. Say what that moment taught the Andes about the newcomers, and why resistance never stopped after it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Pizarro's 160 men seized Atahualpa at Cajamarca in 1533 and killed him despite a ransom room of treasure; colony, mestizaje and Afro-Ecuadorian roots followed — and Indigenous and mestizo uprisings kept resisting cultural domination. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
