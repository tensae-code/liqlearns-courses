# Hands-on — Women Through History

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one woman in your own family's story who changed something — a schooling, a piece of land, a right. The book's women did the same at the scale of nations.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Women of Greece and Rome, Indigenous Ecuadorian leaders Dolores Cacuango and Tránsito Amaguaña — born to huasipungo labourers in Pesillo, humiliated out of school, who rebelled, founded Ecuador's first Indigenous organisations and bilingual schools with Cacuango and fought for agrarian reform into old age — and Rigoberta Menchú fought across eras for education, land and equality. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
