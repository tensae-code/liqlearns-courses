# Hands-on — Ecuador: Independence and Gran Colombia

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Put three dates on a line: 1809, 1820, 1822. For each, say in one sentence what changed. A country's freedom can have more than one birthday.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Quito's 1809 cry, Guayaquil's 1820 independence and Sucre's victory at Pichincha in 1822 freed the land; Bolívar and San Martín met at Guayaquil, and Ecuador joined Bolívar's Gran Colombia union. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
