# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing your town or family hands down — a feast, a dish, a way of working together, an old story. Say who taught it to you and who you will teach it to.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Heritage is what a people hands down and must protect: the minga's communal work, randi-randi reciprocity, the festivals, dances and dishes of each town, Quito and Cuenca, Galápagos and Sangay, the toquilla hat and marimba music — treasures of the whole world — and the old legends, like La Dama Tapada, La Princesa Súa and the pyramids of Cochasquí, still retold today. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
