# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

**Course:** History — Ecuador · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and Heritage (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Heritage is what a people hands down and must protect: the minga's communal work, randi-randi reciprocity, the festivals, dances and dishes of each town, Quito and Cuenca, Galápagos and Sangay, the toquilla hat and marimba music — treasures of the whole world — and the old legends, like La Dama Tapada, La Princesa Súa and the pyramids of Cochasquí, still retold today.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **La minga as inherited tradition** — Indigenous ancestors hand down the minga, communal work that weaves the village together, and their voices still guide the community from the past.
2. **Patrimonios culturales del Ecuador** — Quito, declared World Heritage in 1978 for its historic value, Cuenca, Galápagos, Sangay, the toquilla hat and marimba music are treasures belonging to the whole world that children must protect.
3. **Leyenda La Dama Tapada (old stories)** — A veiled lady wanders Ecuador's fields seeking true love, a brave youth follows her and she vanishes into the mist, and the tale is retold as a lesson from old stories.
4. **Diversidad humana, cultural y étnica del Ecuador** — More than twenty ethnic groups make Ecuador pluriethnic and multicultural, and their traditions, beliefs, legends, festivals and dress are handed down generation to generation, changing as modernity and foreign customs mix in.
5. **Diversidad cultural y étnica del Ecuador** — Otavalos, Saraguros, Cañaris, Salasacas, Tsáchilas, Huaoranis, Kichwas and others each keep their own culture, and their festivals — Inti Raymi, Pase del Niño, Mama Negra, the montubio rodeo — blend Indigenous roots with what arrived from the Spaniards.
6. **Acciones de cooperación y el trabajo solidario** — The minga, collective work for the community kept alive above all by Indigenous communities, and randi-randi, Kichwa "giving-giving" reciprocity, teach that a people's inherited way of working together is itself a heritage to practise.
7. **Actividades culturales de la parroquia y del cantón** — Each town's festivals, dances and typical dishes are traditions that children must know, live and pass to the next generation, or the people's identity is lost.
8. **Patrimonio natural y cultural** — Ecuador holds six UNESCO recognitions — Quito (1978), Galápagos, Sangay, Cuenca, the Zápara oral tradition and the toquilla-straw hat — and heritage is the people's letter of introduction to the world, to be located, valued and cared for.
9. **Susana Reyes, la danza como signo de identidad** — Dancer Susana Reyes, a washerwoman's daughter, dances for Quito's washerwomen and founds La Casa de la Danza in 1992, turning the Pachamama vision into some 100 choreographies that carry Ecuadorian national identity.
10. **Álbum de las festividades cívicas culturales del Ecuador** — Pupil teams research a month of Ecuador's civic and cultural celebrations from varied sources and build a class album so the whole school community learns the country's festival heritage.
11. **Leyenda de las pirámides de Cochasquí (María Eugenia Paz y Miño, plot summary only)** — Bruna asks whether Ecuador has pyramids, her father takes her to Cochasquí where fifteen ancient grass-covered flat-topped pyramids hide in a pasture, a mysterious guide shows them the site, the Andean peaks and the stars, and by morning guide and house have vanished — a legend still retold on full-moon nights.
12. **La Princesa Súa (tradición oral, from Arte oral del Ecuador, Ministerio de Cultura 2009; plot summary only)** — At Atacames the cacique's daughter falls in love with the Spanish Captain de León, hears he has died fighting Ton-Zupa's teaones, and throws herself from their meeting cliff; the captain returns, learns of her death and follows her — the place is still called El Peñón del Suicida.
