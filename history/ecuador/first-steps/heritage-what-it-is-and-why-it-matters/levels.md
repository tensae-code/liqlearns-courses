# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — La minga as inherited tradition
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Indigenous ancestors hand down the minga, communal work that weaves the village together, and their voices still guide the community from the past — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Patrimonios culturales del Ecuador
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Quito, declared World Heritage in 1978 for its historic value, Cuenca, Galápagos, Sangay, the toquilla hat and marimba music are treasures belonging to the whole world that children must protect — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Leyenda La Dama Tapada (old stories)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: A veiled lady wanders Ecuador's fields seeking true love, a brave youth follows her and she vanishes into the mist, and the tale is retold as a lesson from old stories — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Diversidad humana, cultural y étnica del Ecuador
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: More than twenty ethnic groups make Ecuador pluriethnic and multicultural, and their traditions, beliefs, legends, festivals and dress are handed down generation to generation, changing as modernity and foreign customs mix in — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Diversidad cultural y étnica del Ecuador
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Otavalos, Saraguros, Cañaris, Salasacas, Tsáchilas, Huaoranis, Kichwas and others each keep their own culture, and their festivals — Inti Raymi, Pase del Niño, Mama Negra, the montubio rodeo — blend Indigenous roots with what arrived from the Spaniards — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Acciones de cooperación y el trabajo solidario
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The minga, collective work for the community kept alive above all by Indigenous communities, and randi-randi, Kichwa "giving-giving" reciprocity, teach that a people's inherited way of working together is itself a heritage to practise — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Actividades culturales de la parroquia y del cantón
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Each town's festivals, dances and typical dishes are traditions that children must know, live and pass to the next generation, or the people's identity is lost — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Patrimonio natural y cultural
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Ecuador holds six UNESCO recognitions — Quito (1978), Galápagos, Sangay, Cuenca, the Zápara oral tradition and the toquilla-straw hat — and heritage is the people's letter of introduction to the world, to be located, valued and cared for — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Susana Reyes, la danza como signo de identidad
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Dancer Susana Reyes, a washerwoman's daughter, dances for Quito's washerwomen and founds La Casa de la Danza in 1992, turning the Pachamama vision into some 100 choreographies that carry Ecuadorian national identity — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Álbum de las festividades cívicas culturales del Ecuador
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pupil teams research a month of Ecuador's civic and cultural celebrations from varied sources and build a class album so the whole school community learns the country's festival heritage — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 11 — Leyenda de las pirámides de Cochasquí (María Eugenia Paz y Miño, plot summary only)
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Bruna asks whether Ecuador has pyramids, her father takes her to Cochasquí where fifteen ancient grass-covered flat-topped pyramids hide in a pasture, a mysterious guide shows them the site, the Andean peaks and the stars, and by morning guide and house have vanished — a legend still retold on full-moon nights — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 12 — La Princesa Súa (tradición oral, from Arte oral del Ecuador, Ministerio de Cultura 2009; plot summary only)
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: At Atacames the cacique's daughter falls in love with the Spanish Captain de León, hears he has died fighting Ton-Zupa's teaones, and throws herself from their meeting cliff; the captain returns, learns of her death and follows her — the place is still called El Peñón del Suicida — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
