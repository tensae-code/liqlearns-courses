# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Name one thing your town or family hands down — a feast, a dish, a way of working together, an old story. Say who taught it to you and who you will teach it to.

## Sub lesson 1 — La minga as inherited tradition

Indigenous ancestors hand down the minga, communal work that weaves the village together, and their voices still guide the community from the past. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Patrimonios culturales del Ecuador

Quito, declared World Heritage in 1978 for its historic value, Cuenca, Galápagos, Sangay, the toquilla hat and marimba music are treasures belonging to the whole world that children must protect. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Leyenda La Dama Tapada (old stories)

A veiled lady wanders Ecuador's fields seeking true love, a brave youth follows her and she vanishes into the mist, and the tale is retold as a lesson from old stories. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 4 — Diversidad humana, cultural y étnica del Ecuador

More than twenty ethnic groups make Ecuador pluriethnic and multicultural, and their traditions, beliefs, legends, festivals and dress are handed down generation to generation, changing as modernity and foreign customs mix in. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 5 — Diversidad cultural y étnica del Ecuador

Otavalos, Saraguros, Cañaris, Salasacas, Tsáchilas, Huaoranis, Kichwas and others each keep their own culture, and their festivals — Inti Raymi, Pase del Niño, Mama Negra, the montubio rodeo — blend Indigenous roots with what arrived from the Spaniards. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 6 — Acciones de cooperación y el trabajo solidario

The minga, collective work for the community kept alive above all by Indigenous communities, and randi-randi, Kichwa "giving-giving" reciprocity, teach that a people's inherited way of working together is itself a heritage to practise. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 7 — Actividades culturales de la parroquia y del cantón

Each town's festivals, dances and typical dishes are traditions that children must know, live and pass to the next generation, or the people's identity is lost. ## Sub lesson 8 — Patrimonio natural y cultural

Ecuador holds six UNESCO recognitions — Quito (1978), Galápagos, Sangay, Cuenca, the Zápara oral tradition and the toquilla-straw hat — and heritage is the people's letter of introduction to the world, to be located, valued and cared for. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 9 — Susana Reyes, la danza como signo de identidad

Dancer Susana Reyes, a washerwoman's daughter, dances for Quito's washerwomen and founds La Casa de la Danza in 1992, turning the Pachamama vision into some 100 choreographies that carry Ecuadorian national identity. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 11 — Leyenda de las pirámides de Cochasquí (María Eugenia Paz y Miño, plot summary only)

Bruna asks whether Ecuador has pyramids, her father takes her to Cochasquí where fifteen ancient grass-covered flat-topped pyramids hide in a pasture, a mysterious guide shows them the site, the Andean peaks and the stars, and by morning guide and house have vanished — a legend still retold on full-moon nights. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 12 — La Princesa Súa (tradición oral, from Arte oral del Ecuador, Ministerio de Cultura 2009; plot summary only)

At Atacames the cacique's daughter falls in love with the Spanish Captain de León, hears he has died fighting Ton-Zupa's teaones, and throws herself from their meeting cliff; the captain returns, learns of her death and follows her — the place is still called El Peñón del Suicida. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.
