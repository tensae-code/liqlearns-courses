# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name three traces of the past in your own home — an old tool, a photograph, a song someone taught you. For each one, say what it can tell about the people who came before you.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is built from traces of the past — time, culture and work define it, and in America work itself remade whole peoples, with women carrying half of that hidden story. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
