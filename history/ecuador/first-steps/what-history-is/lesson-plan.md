# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — Ecuador · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and Heritage (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History is built from traces of the past — time, culture and work define it, and in America work itself remade whole peoples, with women carrying half of that hidden story.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and Heritage (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **El papel de la "historia y trabajo" en la evolución de los pueblos americanos** — American peoples are remade by work, from the first farmers to today's workers, and women carry half that hidden story.
2. **La Historia** — Pupils learn what history is, how time, culture and work define it, and how historians build the past from traces.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- **Historiography: The History of History Writing** (`first-steps/historiography-the-history-of-history-writing/`) is recommended first — it stands just before this lesson on the path (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History is built from traces of the past — time, culture and work define it, and in America work itself remade whole peoples, with women carrying half of that hidden story.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school, and say how it shows the lesson's teaching at work today.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
