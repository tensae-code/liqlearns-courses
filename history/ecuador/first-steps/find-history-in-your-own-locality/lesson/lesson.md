# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Ask an older person in your family or street: what is the oldest story you know about our place? Write down one event from long ago, one from now, and one hope for the future.

## Sub lesson 1 — La historia de mi familia

A child interviews older relatives about ancestors, sorts family events into past, present and future, and learns every family has a history that makes it unique. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mi parroquia y mi cantón (skill: founding origins)

Pupils use varied sources to investigate the founding origins and most significant features of their own parish and canton, and learn whose name each one honours. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Acontecimientos y personajes de la parroquia y del cantón

Every parish and canton has its own history and its own representative figures — Jefferson Pérez, Matilde Hidalgo de Prócel, Eduardo Kingman among them — who leave footprints in their birthplace and bind its people together. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Sub lesson 4 — La gente ecuatoriana

Ecuadorians share one territory, one history and many traditions across mestizo, Indigenous and Afro-Ecuadorian peoples, and each child researches their own family's origin and tells its story to the class. Hold that turn in mind: who is in this part of the story, where it happens, what changes because of it, and what comes of it — that is how this course tells every story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Every family, parish and canton has its own history: interview the elders, sort events into past, present and future, and investigate your own place's founding origins and the people it honours. Give one real example from your own life, and name the lesson's key words: Find History in Your Own Locality. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
