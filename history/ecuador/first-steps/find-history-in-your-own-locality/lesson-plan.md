# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Ecuador · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and Heritage (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Every family, parish and canton has its own history: interview the elders, sort events into past, present and future, and investigate your own place's founding origins and the people it honours.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and Heritage (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **La historia de mi familia** — A child interviews older relatives about ancestors, sorts family events into past, present and future, and learns every family has a history that makes it unique.
2. **Mi parroquia y mi cantón (skill: founding origins)** — Pupils use varied sources to investigate the founding origins and most significant features of their own parish and canton, and learn whose name each one honours.
3. **Acontecimientos y personajes de la parroquia y del cantón** — Every parish and canton has its own history and its own representative figures — Jefferson Pérez, Matilde Hidalgo de Prócel, Eduardo Kingman among them — who leave footprints in their birthplace and bind its people together.
4. **La gente ecuatoriana** — Ecuadorians share one territory, one history and many traditions across mestizo, Indigenous and Afro-Ecuadorian peoples, and each child researches their own family's origin and tells its story to the class.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Every family, parish and canton has its own history: interview the elders, sort events into past, present and future, and investigate your own place's founding origins and the people it honours.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school, and say how it shows the lesson's teaching at work today.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
