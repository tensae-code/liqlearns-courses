# Hands-on — Historiography: The History of History Writing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take any story you have heard about the past. Write two questions you would ask before believing it: who told it, and how could they know?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ecuador's first history was written in exile by the Jesuit Juan de Velasco — and later critics argue parts of it read as legend, so an 'official history' must always be weighed against its critics. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
