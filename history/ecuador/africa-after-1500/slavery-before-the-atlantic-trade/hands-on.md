# Hands-on — Slavery Before the Atlantic Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three jobs in the ancient world. For each, ask: could the empire have run without forced labour? Then ask the book's question about your own century.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ancient empires built their wealth on enslaved people — and the book asks pupils to judge whether abolition in the 1800s really ended slavery or only changed its forms. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
