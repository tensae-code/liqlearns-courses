# Hands-on — Maroon Resistance and Queen Nanny

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand very still against a wall or a tree until a partner walks past without noticing you. That stillness, in the mountains, was a weapon. Say what else Nanny needed besides camouflage.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Nanny, an escaped slave of African royal descent, freed others, founded mountain-top Nanny Town in British Jamaica, camouflaged her Maroons as trees to ambush soldiers, and held out until hunger — not British guns — tested her people. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
