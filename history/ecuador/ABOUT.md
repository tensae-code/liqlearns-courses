# About the History — Ecuador course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Estudios Sociales and Historia books of the Ministerio de
Educación del Ecuador. The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Ecuador inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as
a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four levels**, derived from the
progression of Ecuador's own books as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and Heritage.**
   History begins as the child's own past: what history is,
   the family and the locality investigated, Velasco's first
   history weighed, and heritage — the minga, the treasures,
   the festivals and the legends — named and kept.
2. **Level 2 — The Deep Past and the Ancient World.**
   Africa the cradle, farming, the growth of states,
   Ecuador's first peoples, the river civilisations, Greece,
   Rome, the medieval faiths, and America's Maya, Aztecs and
   Incas, closing on the Inca turn into Ecuador.
3. **Level 3 — Conquest, Colony and Independence.**
   Slavery and Maroon resistance, the Renaissance and the
   voyages, conquest and colony in Latin America and in
   Ecuador, colonial art, revolution and nationalism, and
   Ecuador's independence, Gran Colombia and founding.
4. **Level 4 — Ecuador and the Modern World.** The
   republic's own story — 1800s, Alfaro to the banana boom,
   oil and democracy, the 21st century — inside Latin
   America's unfinished liberation and the modern world's
   movements, capitalism, industry, Cold War and economic
   schools.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From your own family's story to the whole human story:*
  "Start at home: interview your grandparents, learn your
  parish, keep your heritage. Then walk the whole road —
  Africa's first humans, the river kingdoms, Greece and
  Rome, the Maya, the Aztecs and the Incas on their great
  road — until the road reaches Ecuador, Cajamarca breaks
  it, Pichincha frees it, and the republic argues its way
  through Alfaro, bananas, oil and dollars to today."

## The way of the books

Ecuador's own way, as filed book by book: History enters at
age seven only as the child's own past — family history on a
past/present/future line, ancestral customs (the minga) and
World Heritage places — before any national narrative
(Grade 2). The older SM Grade 3 edition builds identity from
the parish outward — founding origins of the child's own
parroquia and cantón first, then the country's ethnic
diversity, traditions handed down generation to generation,
and its UNESCO heritage — with a Buen Vivir character story
in every unit. Grade 5 is one continuous national story told
in period blocks — aboriginal roots, Conquest, Gran
Colombia, then República I, II and III — each block closing
with a reading (Lectura), a challenge (Reto) and an
evaluation; world history enters only as Ecuador's frame.
The Grade 8 Superior book abandons the national chronology
for a thematic, work-centred reading of world and Latin
American history — history-and-labour, class societies,
revolutions, liberation — taught through stories like Queen
Nanny and through Ecuador's own contemporary challenges.
The Bachillerato Historia course runs the whole human story
— evolution, river civilisations, Greece/Rome/Byzantium,
faiths, pre-Columbian America, colony, revolutions, economic
systems — but keeps bending it home: Ecuadorian Indigenous
women, the Inca legacy and dollarised 21st-century Ecuador
close the arc. The reading-plan booklets pair the Social
Studies course with anthologies of Ecuadorian and world
stories and poems; only their History-bearing pieces are
placed here.
