# Africa: Cradle of Humankind

## Do it first

Stand up and walk across the room on two feet, then pick up a stone or a stick and use it as a tool. Write down the two things your body just did that no ape does in the same way.

The story of everyone begins in one place: Africa. Between seven and five million years ago, one line of apes took a road no other animal had taken — they came down from the trees and walked upright, on two feet, freeing their hands. Much later came Homo habilis, the handy one, who struck stones into the first tools. Then fire was tamed: warmth, cooked food, light against the dark, and a circle to gather around. Step by slow step the road runs to Homo sapiens — to us. That is why the book calls Africa the cradle of humankind: every people on earth, Costa Rica's included, traces its first steps there.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Every human story starts in Africa: there our ancestors stood upright, made the first tools and tamed fire, on the long road to Homo sapiens — which is why the book calls Africa the cradle of humankind. Give one real example from your own life — your own two feet — walking upright across your own yard is the first step of this story, repeated — and name the lesson's key words: Africa, cradle of humankind, Homo habilis, fire. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Every human story starts in Africa: there our ancestors stood upright, made the first tools and tamed fire, on the long road to Homo sapiens — which is why the book calls Africa the cradle of humankind.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
