# Hands-on — Africa: Cradle of Humankind

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand up and walk across the room on two feet, then pick up a stone or a stick and use it as a tool. Write down the two things your body just did that no ape does in the same way.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Every human story starts in Africa: there our ancestors stood upright, made the first tools and tamed fire, on the long road to Homo sapiens — which is why the book calls Africa the cradle of humankind. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
