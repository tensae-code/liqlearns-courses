# Lesson plan — Africa: Cradle of Humankind

**Course:** History — Costa Rica · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Africa: Cradle of Humankind
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and First Civilisations (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Every human story starts in Africa: there our ancestors stood upright, made the first tools and tamed fire, on the long road to Homo sapiens — which is why the book calls Africa the cradle of humankind.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and First Civilisations (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Every human story starts in Africa: there our ancestors stood upright, made the first tools and tamed fire, on the long road to Homo sapiens — which is why the book calls Africa the cradle of humankind.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — your own two feet — walking upright across your own yard is the first step of this story, repeated.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks humans appeared suddenly, fully formed → the book walks step by step: upright walking first, tools next, fire after, across millions of years.
- Go back to the one thing: Every human story starts in Africa: there our ancestors stood upright, made the first tools and tamed fire, on the long road to Homo sapiens — which is why the book calls Africa the cradle of humankind. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
