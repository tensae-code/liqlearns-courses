# Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

## Do it first

Draw a river. On its banks, draw what a farming people would need the river to do for them each year, and who would have to organise it.

Egypt grows along one river. The Nile floods on its yearly rhythm, lays down black soil, and a desert land becomes a garden — if the work is organised. Around that organisation a whole world rises: the pharaoh at the top, officials and priests and scribes beneath, farmers in the fields, and a culture of temples, tombs and writing that still astonishes. The book studies Egypt beside Mesopotamia as one of the first civilisations: a society whose order — river, state and belief together — left a legacy the world still reads.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Egypt is a civilisation built by a river: the Nile's floods order the farming, the pharaoh's state orders the people, and its legacy stands beside Mesopotamia's as one of the first civilisations. Give one real example from your own life — any river in your own country — its floods and dry seasons still order the farming year around it — and name the lesson's key words: Egypt, the Nile, pharaoh, first civilisations. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
