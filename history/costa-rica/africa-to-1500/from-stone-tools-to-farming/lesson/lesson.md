# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

List everything you ate yesterday. Beside each item, write whether a wandering hunter could have carried it along, or whether it had to be grown or raised in one place.

For most of the human story, people moved: bands of hunters and gatherers followed the animals and the seasons, carrying everything they owned. Then, in the Neolithic revolution, the pattern breaks. People settle. They plant seeds and wait for them; they tame animals and keep them close; they store grain against the hungry months. A band that plants cannot wander — so villages rise, and human beings begin to occupy space permanently. Everything later — towns, cities, states — stands on this turn: food grown in one place feeds people who stay in one place.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Neolithic revolution is the turn from wandering and hunting to settling and farming: people plant, keep animals, and stay in one place for good. Give one real example from your own life — the maize or beans in your own kitchen — grown in one place and stored, exactly the settled life this lesson describes — and name the lesson's key words: Neolithic revolution, settle, farming, domesticate. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The Neolithic revolution is the turn from wandering and hunting to settling and farming: people plant, keep animals, and stay in one place for good.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
