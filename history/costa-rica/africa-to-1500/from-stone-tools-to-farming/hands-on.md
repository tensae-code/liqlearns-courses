# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List everything you ate yesterday. Beside each item, write whether a wandering hunter could have carried it along, or whether it had to be grown or raised in one place.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Neolithic revolution is the turn from wandering and hunting to settling and farming: people plant, keep animals, and stay in one place for good. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
