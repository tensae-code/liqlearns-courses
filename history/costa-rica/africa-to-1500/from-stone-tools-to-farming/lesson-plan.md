# Lesson plan — From Stone Tools to Farming

**Course:** History — Costa Rica · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From Stone Tools to Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and First Civilisations (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Neolithic revolution is the turn from wandering and hunting to settling and farming: people plant, keep animals, and stay in one place for good.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and First Civilisations (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Neolithic revolution is the turn from wandering and hunting to settling and farming: people plant, keep animals, and stay in one place for good.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — the maize or beans in your own kitchen — grown in one place and stored, exactly the settled life this lesson describes.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks farming began everywhere at once as a single invention → the book presents a slow revolution: settling, planting and domesticating change how people occupy space, permanently.
- Go back to the one thing: The Neolithic revolution is the turn from wandering and hunting to settling and farming: people plant, keep animals, and stay in one place for good. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
