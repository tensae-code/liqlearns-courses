# About the History — Costa Rica course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Estudios Sociales books of the Colegio Nacional de
Educación a Distancia (CONED) / Universidad Estatal a
Distancia (UNED), with one private-publisher Grade 11 book
(Publicaciones Porras y Gamboa) filed beside them. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Costa Rica inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as
a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **five levels**, derived from the
eras of the story as Costa Rica's own books present them:

1. **Level 1 — The Ancient World: First Peoples and First
   Civilisations.** Humanity's African beginning and the
   Neolithic turn; Mesopotamia and Egypt; Greece and Rome;
   the Maya, Aztec and Inca studied whole; and Costa Rica's
   indigenous peoples — the national past beginning before
   the Spanish (Grade 7 CONED anthology).
2. **Level 2 — The Medieval World and the Turn to Modern
   Times.** Rome's western fall, feudalism, Christianity's
   consolidation and Islam's birth and expansion, the
   cities-trade-universities-and-plague turn, then
   Renaissance, Reformation and Spain's exploration (Grade
   9 CONED anthology, first unit).
3. **Level 3 — Conquest, Colony and the Making of Costa
   Rica.** The conquest, the colonial system and its
   exploitation and heritage, absolutism, Enlightenment,
   the French Revolution, Latin American independence, the
   Industrial Revolution, and Costa Rica's own building:
   1821–1848, the coffee state, the agro-export model and
   daily life (Grade 9 CONED anthology, rest).
4. **Level 4 — The World Wars, the Cold War and the World
   Today.** Imperialism, the two world wars, the Russian
   Revolution, the Depression, the bipolar Cold War whole,
   and the multipolar present read from Latin America
   (Grade 10 CONED anthology).
5. **Level 5 — Costa Rica's Own Twentieth Century.** The
   national century told twice — CONED anthology and
   Porras y Gamboa book in parallel: agro-export crisis,
   democracy and social struggles, women's suffrage, 1948,
   import substitution, the welfare state, the electoral
   system, the 1980s crisis and the movements since 1980
   (the two Grade 11 books).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *One arc, from the cradle to your own street:* "Start
  where everyone starts — in Africa, walking upright,
  taming fire, learning to farm. Walk through the first
  cities and the classical world, meet the Maya, the Aztec
  and the Inca, and arrive at Costa Rica's own first
  peoples. Then follow the world as Costa Rica's books
  follow it — Rome falling, faiths rising, cities and
  universities, explorers crossing an ocean — until every
  world process lands at home: conquest and colony, coffee
  and banana, wars and cold wars read from San José, and
  finally your own country's century — its crises, its
  democracy built by struggle, its 1948, its welfare state
  and its movements — told twice, so you can weigh it
  twice."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Grade 7 CONED anthology** — the CONED anthologies
compile source readings around a guiding question per
theme, fusing History with Geography in one Estudios
Sociales course: the ancient world is read as "the human
footprint on geographic space", and Costa Rica's own past
enters through its indigenous peoples before any national
narrative. *(Student book.)*

**Grade 8 CONED anthology** — in the CONED sequence the
Octavo year of Estudios Sociales is given entirely to
physical geography and climate — the History strand
pauses for a full year. *(Student book; record-only for
History.)*

**Grade 9 CONED anthology** — the Noveno anthology runs
one long arc — "the journey of humanity from the Middle
Ages to 19th-century Costa Rica" — in which every world
process (feudalism, Renaissance, conquest, Enlightenment,
independence, industrialisation) is landed in Costa Rica:
colonial institutions, the coffee state and banana
enclave, and everyday Costa Rican life close the book.
*(Student book.)*

**Grade 10 CONED anthology** — the Décimo anthology is
world history with a geopolitical lens — imperialism, the
two world wars, the Russian revolution, the Depression,
the bipolar Cold War world and today's multipolar
tensions — read from Costa Rica outward to Latin America
and the globe. *(Student book.)*

**Grade 11 CONED anthology** — the Undécimo anthology
turns fully homeward: after a geography-of-globalisation
unit, Costa Rica's own 20th century is the history —
agro-export crisis, democratic and social struggles, the
1948 civil war and social reform, the welfare state, and
the post-1980 neoliberal turn. *(Student book.)*

**Grade 11 Porras y Gamboa book** — a private-publisher
twin of the CONED anthology — same MEP Grade 11
programme, but told as one illustrated narrative with
"Aplico mi conocimiento" workbook pages: learners
role-play being president in 1929, map the Estado
empresario (CODESA), and weigh the CAFTA referendum — the
national story ends by judging the neoliberal turn's cost
to the middle class. *(Student book.)*
