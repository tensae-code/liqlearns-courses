# The Maya

## Do it first

Picture a map of Central America and Mexico in your mind: write where you think the Maya lived, and one thing you have heard they built or invented.

The Maya are this continent's own great civilisation, and the book studies them whole. Where and when: the forests and highlands of what is now Mexico and Central America, across many centuries. How they lived: farmers of maize, traders, builders of cities with pyramids and calendars, organised in an economy, a society and a politics of their own. What they left: writing, astronomy, art and architecture — a cultural legacy the world still studies. And the story does not end in ruins: Maya descendant peoples live in the region today, heirs of that civilisation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Maya are studied whole: a great civilisation of this continent — its lands and times, how its people worked, lived and were ruled, the culture it left, and its descendants living today. Give one real example from your own life — the Maya descendant peoples of the region today — the lesson ends with them, not only with ruins — and name the lesson's key words: the Maya, organisation, cultural legacy, descendants. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
