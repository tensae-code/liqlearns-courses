# Ancient Civilisations: Rome

## Do it first

Look at your own town: name one thing organised 'the Roman way' — a road, a law, a calendar month, a word in Spanish that comes from Latin.

Rome begins as one city and becomes a world. The book studies how Roman society was organised — its orders and ranks, its citizens and its law — and the cultural contributions it spread across an empire: roads and aqueducts, a language, Latin, that became Spanish and its sister tongues, and ideas of law and citizenship that outlived the empire itself. With Greece, Rome completes the classical pair: two ancient societies from which Western societies, Costa Rica's among them, trace their descent.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rome completes the classical pair: its way of organising society and its cultural contributions are the second root from which Western societies are traced. Give one real example from your own life — the Spanish language you speak every day — its Latin roots are Rome's contribution living in your mouth — and name the lesson's key words: Rome, social organisation, cultural contributions, Latin. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
