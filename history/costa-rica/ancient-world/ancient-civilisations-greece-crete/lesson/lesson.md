# Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

## Do it first

Write down three words, customs or ideas in your own life that you have heard called 'Greek', or that come from the ancient world.

On a rocky land cut by the sea, the Greeks build small cities and a civilisation out of proportion to their numbers. The book presents classical Greece — from the 5th century BC onward — as an origin of Western societies: it studies the Greek space of sea and city-states, the structure of their society, and the contributions they handed down — ways of governing, of arguing, of building, of asking what is true. When later societies in the West look for their roots, one root runs back to Greece.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Greece of classical antiquity is studied as an origin of Western societies: its land and cities, its social structure, and the culture it handed down. Give one real example from your own life — the word 'democracy' itself — a Greek word still used in your own country's politics — and name the lesson's key words: Greece, classical antiquity, Western societies. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
