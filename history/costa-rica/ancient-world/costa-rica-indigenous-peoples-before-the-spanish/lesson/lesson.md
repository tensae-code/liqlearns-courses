# Costa Rica's Indigenous Peoples Before the Spanish

## Do it first

Name one place near your home with an indigenous name, or one food your family eats that the first peoples grew. Write what it tells you about who lived there first.

Before any ship, this land was already a home. The book introduces the pueblos originarios — the original peoples of the territory that is today Costa Rica — in their own territories: peoples who farmed, fished, traded, made gold and pottery and stone spheres, and ordered their societies in their own ways. This is where the national past begins in Costa Rica's own telling: not with colonisation, but with the indigenous societies who were here first, and whose descendants are part of the country still.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Costa Rica's story does not begin with the Spanish: it begins with its own indigenous peoples, living in their territories, with societies of their own. Give one real example from your own life — maize, beans and cacao on your own table — foods of the first peoples, still feeding Costa Rica — and name the lesson's key words: indigenous peoples, territories, before the Spanish, Costa Rica. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
