# The Rise and Expansion of Islam

## Do it first

Picture a world map in your mind and put a point on Arabia. Write how far a new idea could travel in a hundred years on camel and ship routes.

In Arabia, in the 7th century, a new religion is born: Islam. It does not stay home. Within generations it expands across Arabia and far beyond — to Persia, to North Africa, to Spain — carried by armies, traders and teachers. The medieval world is now two great worlds of faith facing each other, and the book follows their long meeting across the centuries (Tema 2-C, printed p. 38): trading, borrowing, fighting, and shaping each other. The map of the Middle Ages cannot be read without both.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Islam is born in Arabia and expands swiftly, so the medieval world becomes a meeting — and a contest — between the Christian and Muslim worlds. Give one real example from your own life — words in Spanish from Arabic — the al- words in your own language are footprints of that medieval meeting — and name the lesson's key words: Islam, Arabia, expansion, medieval world. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
