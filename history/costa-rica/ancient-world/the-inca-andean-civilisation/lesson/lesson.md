# The Inca: Andean Civilisation

## Do it first

Imagine ruling a land of high mountains where the valleys are a week's walk apart. Write how you would move food and orders up and down.

In the Andes, where the land stands on edge, the Inca build a state that turns mountains into a system: terraces climbing the slopes, storehouses full against lean years, roads of stone joining valley to valley. The book studies the Inca whole — their space and time along the cordillera, their economic, social and political organisation, the cultural legacy of their building and weaving, and their living descendants in the Andes today. The hardest landscape in the Americas carried one of its greatest states.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Inca state organised the Andes — mountains, roads and storehouses — into one great society, studied whole: its organisation, its legacy and its living descendants. Give one real example from your own life — mountain roads in your own country — joining steep places into one land is still the same problem the Inca solved — and name the lesson's key words: the Inca, the Andes, state, legacy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
