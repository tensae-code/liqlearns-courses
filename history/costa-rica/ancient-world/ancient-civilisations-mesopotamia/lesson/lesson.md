# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

## Do it first

Imagine a town of ten thousand people with no writing and no written laws. Write down three problems that would appear in the first week.

Between two rivers — the Tigris and the Euphrates — people first did something new under the sun: they built cities. A city is not just many houses. Grain must be counted, canals shared, quarrels judged, goods weighed and traded. So Mesopotamia organises an economy, a society and a culture: kings and priests, merchants and farmers, temples rising over the plain. And it leaves two tools the world never put down again — laws written for all to see, and writing itself, pressed into clay. The first cities stand between the rivers, and we still live in their invention.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Mesopotamia, the land between the rivers, raises the first cities — and a city needs organisers, laws and writing, which are its lasting legacy. Give one real example from your own life — your own town's market — someone must fix weights, times and rules, the same city problems Mesopotamia first answered — and name the lesson's key words: Mesopotamia, first cities, laws, writing. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
