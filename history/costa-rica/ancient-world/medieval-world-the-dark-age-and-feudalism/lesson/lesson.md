# The Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism

## Do it first

If no police or army could protect your street, who would you turn to? Write what you would owe them in return.

After Rome falls, danger rides the roads: invasions come wave after wave, and no distant emperor can protect a village. So Europe reorganises itself around protection. A lord with a castle and armed men offers safety; a vassal swears service in return for land; peasants work that land and give its produce, tied to the soil and to the lord above them. Feudal society is a ladder of promises — protection downward, service and food upward — built because insecurity demanded it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: When invasion makes life unsafe, Europe reorganises into feudalism: lords protect, vassals serve, peasants work the land — each tied to the others and to the soil. Give one real example from your own life — a bargain of protection for work — the feudal tie in small, still recognisable wherever safety is exchanged for service — and name the lesson's key words: feudalism, lords, vassals, peasants. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
