# Christianity: From Persecuted Sect to State Religion

## Do it first

Look at a calendar of your town's year: mark the feasts and holidays that come from the Church. Count how much of the year it shapes.

Across the medieval centuries one faith spreads over a whole continent and settles deep. Christianity expands into every kingdom and consolidates — churches in every town, monasteries copying books, feast days ordering the year. The book studies what that means beyond belief: the Church comes to structure medieval society itself — who teaches, who records, who judges, who cares for the poor — and to shape the way Europe thinks about the world, time and the soul. A religion becomes the frame of a civilisation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the medieval centuries Christianity expands and takes firm hold across Europe, coming to structure its society and its way of thinking. Give one real example from your own life — Semana Santa in your own town — the medieval Church's shaping of the year's rhythm, still lived — and name the lesson's key words: Christianity, medieval Europe, society, thought. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
