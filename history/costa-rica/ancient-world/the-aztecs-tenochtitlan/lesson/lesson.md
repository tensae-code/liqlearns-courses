# The Aztecs: Tenochtitlan

## Do it first

Draw a great city standing on a lake. Mark where the food comes in, who rules, and who does the work.

On a lake in the high valley of Mexico the Aztecs raise Tenochtitlan, a capital of canals and causeways, and around it an empire. The book sets their imperial society beside the Maya and the Inca and studies it the same way: how it was organised — its rulers, its tribute, its markets and its wars; the legacy it left in Mexico's memory and land; and the present situation of its descendant peoples. An empire is more than its armies: it is a whole society, and it is studied whole.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Aztecs built an imperial society centred on Tenochtitlan, studied beside the Maya and Inca: how it was organised, what it left, and where its descendant peoples stand today. Give one real example from your own life — any capital city today — it still must be fed from far away, the Aztec capital's problem too — and name the lesson's key words: the Aztecs, Tenochtitlan, empire, descendants. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
