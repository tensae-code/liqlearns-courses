# Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides

## Do it first

Draw a great wall with cracks in it. Write beside each crack one reason a great empire might weaken.

The empire that organised the West does not end with a single blow. The book tells a decline: the western Roman Empire weakens — its frontiers pressed, its unity strained, its power thinning — until it falls. But an ending is also a beginning: out of the empire's ruins, new peoples, new kingdoms and new ways of living assemble themselves, and the medieval world begins. Every age in this course is born from the one before it, and this is the first great birth.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The western Roman Empire declines and falls — and the medieval world begins in its ruins. Give one real example from your own life — any great organisation in your own town that grew too big to hold together — empires face the same strain — and name the lesson's key words: Rome divides, decline, fall, medieval world. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
