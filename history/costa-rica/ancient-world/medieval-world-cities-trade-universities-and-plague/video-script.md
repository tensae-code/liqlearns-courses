# Video script — The Medieval World: Cities, Trade, Universities and Plague

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From the 12th to the 15th century the medieval world turns: cities revive, trade reaches far, universities are founded — and great epidemics shake the old order toward modern times.

Between the 12th and 15th centuries the medieval world begins to move. Cities revive and grow; merchants and craftsmen — new social actors — rise with long-distance trade; the first universities are founded, and learning gets a home of its own. Then the shaking: great epidemics sweep the crowded cities, empty villages, and crack people's faith in the old order. The book calls these centuries the transit to the modern age: the feudal world is not yet gone, but the new one is already visible in its streets.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
