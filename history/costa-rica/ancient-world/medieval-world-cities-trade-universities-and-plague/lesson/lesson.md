# The Medieval World: Cities, Trade, Universities and Plague

## Do it first

Name three things a city has that a village does not. Then write what a sickness spreading through a crowded city could change.

Between the 12th and 15th centuries the medieval world begins to move. Cities revive and grow; merchants and craftsmen — new social actors — rise with long-distance trade; the first universities are founded, and learning gets a home of its own. Then the shaking: great epidemics sweep the crowded cities, empty villages, and crack people's faith in the old order. The book calls these centuries the transit to the modern age: the feudal world is not yet gone, but the new one is already visible in its streets.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: From the 12th to the 15th century the medieval world turns: cities revive, trade reaches far, universities are founded — and great epidemics shake the old order toward modern times. Give one real example from your own life — your nearest city's market and its schools — the medieval city's children: trade and learning in one place — and name the lesson's key words: cities, trade, universities, epidemics. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
