# Hands-on — The Medieval World: Cities, Trade, Universities and Plague

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name three things a city has that a village does not. Then write what a sickness spreading through a crowded city could change.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From the 12th to the 15th century the medieval world turns: cities revive, trade reaches far, universities are founded — and great epidemics shake the old order toward modern times. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
