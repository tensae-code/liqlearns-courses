# Costa Rica Building the Nation-State, 1821–1848

## Do it first

Imagine your town had to make its own laws, money and army, separate from the next town. Write two things that would go well and two that would go badly.

Independence in 1821 leaves Costa Rica as a scatter of towns — Cartago, San José, Heredia, Alajuela — each pulling its own way, localisms stronger than any nation. The book walks the road out of that scatter: the slow centralisation of power, the fights and settlements between the towns, the building of common institutions. The road's end is a date: 1848, the founding of the Republic. A nation-state is not declared in a day; Costa Rica's took a generation to build.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: After 1821 Costa Rica is a scatter of towns pulling their own way; the road from localisms to centralisation ends in 1848 with the founding of the Republic. Give one real example from your own life — rivalries between towns in your own province — the localisms of 1821 in a milder, living form — and name the lesson's key words: localisms, centralisation, Republic founded 1848. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
