# Hands-on — Exploration: Causes and Results

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand at the sea's edge in your mind: write what a sailor of 1490 would need — ship, food, courage, a reason — to sail into the unknown.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Spain's overseas exploration of the 15th and 16th centuries opens the Atlantic: two worlds that did not know each other are joined by sea — and conquest follows. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
