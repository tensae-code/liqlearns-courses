# Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment

## Do it first

Write one thing 'everyone has always done' in your town. Then write the question a reasoner would ask about it: why, and who says so?

A new way of thinking spreads through Europe: the Enlightenment. Its weapon is reason, sharpened by the scientific revolution — observe, question, test, conclude. Turned on society, that weapon attacks inherited authority: why should kings rule by birth, why should custom command without reasons, why should old books settle new questions? The book draws the line forward plainly: thought like this prepares political revolution. Before thrones fall in the street, they fall in the mind.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Enlightenment turns reason and science against inherited authority: if kings and customs can be questioned, political revolution is being prepared. Give one real example from your own life — questioning a rule at school with reasons instead of 'because I said so' — the Enlightenment move in miniature — and name the lesson's key words: Enlightenment, reason, authority, revolution prepared. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
