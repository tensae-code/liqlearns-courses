# The Reformation: Luther and Protestantism

## Do it first

Think of the churches in your town. Write down how many different ones there are, and ask: was it always so?

For a thousand years the West had one Church. In the 16th century that unity breaks. The Protestant Reformation — Luther its famous name — challenges the old Church's authority and teachings, and princes, cities and peoples take sides. Western Christianity splits, and Europe's map is redrawn in two faiths, with wars and settlements following for generations. The book marks the break plainly: after the Reformation, 'the Christian world' is never one again.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the 16th century the Protestant Reformation breaks the unity of Western Christianity, and Europe's religious map is redrawn for good. Give one real example from your own life — the different churches on your own street — their variety begins in this break — and name the lesson's key words: Reformation, Luther, Protestantism, unity broken. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
