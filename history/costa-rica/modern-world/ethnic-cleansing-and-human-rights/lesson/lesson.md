# Ethnic Cleansing and Human Rights

## Do it first

Write the difference between winning an argument with a group and trying to remove that group from a place entirely.

Contemporary geopolitics has a dark face, and the book looks at it directly: ethnic cleansings — whole peoples driven from their homes by violence — and the violation of human rights that travels with them. These are studied not as distant horrors but as the extreme end of the tensions of the previous lessons: what happens when rivalry and hatred are let loose on civilians. Naming them plainly is the lesson's work — they can only be refused by people who can recognise them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ethnic cleansing and human-rights violations are the dark face of contemporary geopolitics — the lesson names them plainly so they can be recognised and refused. Give one real example from your own life — the rights charters your own country signs — they exist because of the dark face this lesson studies — and name the lesson's key words: ethnic cleansing, human rights, contemporary geopolitics. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
