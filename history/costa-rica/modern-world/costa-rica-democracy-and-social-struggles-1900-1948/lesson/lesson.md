# Costa Rica: Democracy and Social Struggles, 1900–1948

## Do it first

Write one right you have today that somebody once had to march, strike or vote to win. Who do you think won it?

## Sub lesson 1 — The construction of democracy and the social struggles (the CONED telling)

The CONED telling names the builders: workers, parties and reformers. In the first half of the 20th century Costa Rican democracy is not a gift from above but a construction from below — social battles (luchas sociales) fought over wages, votes, land and dignity. Each battle widens the circle of who counts in the country's decisions. Democracy here is a verb before it is a noun: something people do, together, against resistance.

## Sub lesson 2 — The construction of democracy and the social struggles (the Porras y Gamboa telling)

The Porras y Gamboa telling gives the construction its beams: electoral reform — cleaner, broader voting — and the struggles of the coffee world itself, workers and producers organising in the industry that paid for the country. Between the reform of the ballot and the organisation of the harvest, democracy widens in the decades before 1948. The next lessons show two of its hardest tests: women's exclusion, and the war that remade the republic.
