# Hands-on — Costa Rica: Democracy and Social Struggles, 1900–1948

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write one right you have today that somebody once had to march, strike or vote to win. Who do you think won it?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Costa Rican democracy was built by struggle: workers, parties and reformers fight the social battles of 1900–1948 — electoral reform won, coffee workers and producers organised — that widen it before 1948. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
