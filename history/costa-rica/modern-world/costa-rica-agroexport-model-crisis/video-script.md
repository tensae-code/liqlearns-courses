# Video script — Costa Rica: The Agro-Export Model in Crisis

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The agro-export model that built Costa Rica — coffee and bananas sold to the world — enters repeated crisis in the first half of the 20th century, and the country must answer: taxes reformed, workers organising, a president facing 1929.

The model that built the nation turns against it. The CONED anthology opens Costa Rica's 20th century with the crises of the agro-export model: a country that lives by selling coffee and bananas abroad is hostage to prices and wars it does not control, and in the first half of the century the crises come again and again. Each crisis asks the same question louder: can a nation stand on two crops? The rest of this level is the century of answers.

The Porras y Gamboa book tells the same crisis at street level. The First World War strikes Costa Rica's trade; President González Flores answers with tax reforms that make the wealthy pay more — and make him enemies; workers begin organising for their own defence; and then 1929 breaks over the coffee economy. The book even puts the learner in the president's chair, deciding under the crash. Dependence is the disease; reform, organisation and hard choices are the attempted cures.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
