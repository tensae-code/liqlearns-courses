# The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

## Do it first

Two strong students in a class never fight each other directly, but every group project becomes their contest. Write how that feels for everyone else.

The second war ends, and the world does not get one peace — it gets two camps. After 1945 the planet splits into rival blocs, East and West, armed against each other yet never fighting directly. Instead, the book shows, their geopolitical rivalry is expressed in proxy conflicts across the globe: Korea, Vietnam, Africa, Latin America — other people's countries become the chessboard of a war called cold only in the capitals. Tema I-A walks toward the bipolar world; Tema I-B reads the rivalry's expressions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: After 1945 the world splits into two rival blocs, and their rivalry is fought out in proxy conflicts across the globe — other people's countries become the chessboard. Give one real example from your own life — a small country caught between two big powers' arguments — the proxy pattern, still seen in today's news — and name the lesson's key words: Cold War, two blocs, rivalry, proxy conflicts. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
