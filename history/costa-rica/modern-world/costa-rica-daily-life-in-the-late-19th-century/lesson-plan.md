## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History ends at home: this lesson lives one ordinary day with Costa Ricans of the late 19th century — their work, food, homes and feasts.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — your grandmother's stories of a house without electricity — the late 19th century is only a few lifetimes from your kitchen.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
