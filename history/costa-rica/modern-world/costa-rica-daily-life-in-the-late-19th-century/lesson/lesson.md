# Costa Rica: Daily Life in the Late 19th Century

## Do it first

Write your own day's timetable, hour by hour. Then cross out everything that needs electricity, motors or telephones — what is left is close to a day in 1880.

The Grade 9 book ends where its readers live: at home. After empires and revolutions, the last theme is la vida cotidiana — how ordinary Costa Ricans lived in the second half of the 19th century. Their houses and clothes, their work in field and kitchen, their food, their feasts and faith, their days measured by sun and church bell rather than clock and screen. It is the right ending: the nation-state and the coffee motor of the earlier lessons were built, in the end, out of these ordinary days.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History ends at home: this lesson lives one ordinary day with Costa Ricans of the late 19th century — their work, food, homes and feasts. Give one real example from your own life — your grandmother's stories of a house without electricity — the late 19th century is only a few lifetimes from your kitchen — and name the lesson's key words: daily life, 19th century, ordinary people, Costa Rica. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
