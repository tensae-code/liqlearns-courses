# The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First

## Do it first

List five things in your home made by hand and five made in factories. Which list was longer a hundred and fifty years ago?

In 19th-century Europe a new kind of society is born: industrial society. Steam and machines gather workers into factories; factories gather people into fast-growing cities; railways and ships tie it all into world markets hungry for food and raw materials. The book plants this lesson in Costa Rica's story on purpose: that hungry industrial world is exactly what Costa Rica's agro-export economy — coffee, then bananas — will plug into. The next lessons are unintelligible without this one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Industrial Revolution births a new society in 19th-century Europe — machines, factories and cities — the change Costa Rica's agro-export economy will soon plug into. Give one real example from your own life — the railway and the coffee it carried — Costa Rica plugging into the industrial world this lesson describes — and name the lesson's key words: Industrial Revolution, factories, industrial society, agro-export. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
