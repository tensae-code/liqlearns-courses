# Hands-on — The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List five things in your home made by hand and five made in factories. Which list was longer a hundred and fifty years ago?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Industrial Revolution births a new society in 19th-century Europe — machines, factories and cities — the change Costa Rica's agro-export economy will soon plug into. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
