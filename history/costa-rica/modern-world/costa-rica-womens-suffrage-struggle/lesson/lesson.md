# Costa Rica: Women's Suffrage Struggle

## Do it first

Ask who in your family could vote in 1940 and who could not. Write what it does to a country when half its people cannot choose its rulers.

The Porras y Gamboa book stops inside its democracy unit to ask who was left out. Costa Rican women worked — in homes, fields, factories and schools — carrying half the society's labour, while the ballot box stayed closed to them. The lesson studies their social and labour situation, and then their answer to it: las luchas de las mujeres por el sufragio, the organised, argued, marched-for fight for the vote. It is a long fight, and its victory comes only with the new order after 1948 — which is why this lesson stands here, inside the building of democracy, as one of its truest measures.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Costa Rican women worked the society's work and were refused its vote — this lesson tells their long fight for suffrage. Give one real example from your own life — your grandmother's or great-grandmother's first vote — this struggle's victory inside your own family — and name the lesson's key words: women's suffrage, women's work, the vote. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
