# Cold War: Social and Political Struggles

## Do it first

Write about a demand in your own town — water, a road, a wage. Then write how an outsider might try to use that demand for their own fight.

The Cold War frame is not the whole picture. Inside it, peoples fight their own battles: workers for wages, students for universities, peasants for land, citizens for rights — social and political demands (luchas y reivindicaciones) that belong to the people pressing them. The blocs try to capture each struggle for their side, and the book's long section (printed pp. 84–125) teaches the reader to tell the difference: a people's own claim is not a superpower's pawn, even when the superpowers reach for it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Inside the Cold War frame, peoples fight their own battles: movements press social and political demands that belong to them, while the blocs try to capture them. Give one real example from your own life — a community protest in your own province — its demands are its own, whoever tries to claim them — and name the lesson's key words: social struggles, political demands, movements, Cold War frame. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
