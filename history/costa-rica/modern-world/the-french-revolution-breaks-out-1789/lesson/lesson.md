# The French Revolution Breaks Out, 1789

## Do it first

Write the three words of the Revolution's famous promise — liberty, equality, fraternity — and beside each, one place in your life where it is kept and one where it is not.

In 1789 the absolute order cracks in France itself. The French Revolution breaks the old world of king, privilege and estates, and proclaims a new political world: citizens, rights, liberty, equality. The book does not leave the story in Paris: those ideas board ships. Across the Atlantic they travel to America, where colonists read them and begin to ask why they should be ruled from across an ocean. The next unit's independences are lit by this fire.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In 1789 the French Revolution breaks the absolute order and proclaims a new political world — and its ideas cross the Atlantic to America. Give one real example from your own life — voting and rights in your own country — the Atlantic crossing of those ideas ends at your own ballot — and name the lesson's key words: French Revolution, 1789, liberty, ideas cross the Atlantic. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
