# Cold War Economic Models

## Do it first

Plan a town's bakery two ways: one where a family owns it and sets the prices, one where the state owns it and sets the prices. Write one good thing and one hard thing in each.

Under the flags, the Cold War was a contest of kitchens. Two economic models organised the rival societies: the capitalist model, where markets and private property arrange production, and the socialist model, where the state plans and owns. The book studies them as lived systems — what work, shops, housing and everyday life looked like under each — because the blocs were not only armies and parties; they were two answers to the question of how a whole society should eat, work and live.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Cold War was also two ways of organising a whole life: the capitalist and socialist economic models arranged rival societies and their everyday days differently. Give one real example from your own life — who owns the shops and buses in your own town — the model question answered around you every day — and name the lesson's key words: economic models, capitalism, socialism, everyday life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
