# Costa Rica: The 1980s Crisis and Export Promotion

## Do it first

Your family owes more than it earns. Write the painful choices: earn more, spend less, sell something. A country in debt crisis faces the same list, larger.

## Sub lesson 1 — The crisis of the 1980s (the CONED telling)

The CONED telling opens the final unit with the break: la crisis de la década de 1980. The welfare model, already gathering its crisis, breaks under debt and world recession. What follows is a turn: export promotion and trade opening replace import substitution; the benefactor state is cut back; Costa Rica goes out to sell to the world again, in new products and under new rules. Every Costa Rican alive today lives downstream of this turn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Costa Rica since the crisis of the 1980s (the Porras y Gamboa telling)

The Porras y Gamboa telling names the turn's machinery: the debt crisis; structural adjustment programmes (PAES) that shrink the state and open the economy; free-trade treaties binding the new model in law. And then the country's own verdict mechanism: the CAFTA referendum, in which the free-trade road is decided by popular vote — the neoliberal turn judged, narrowly approved, by the people who live it. Crisis, adjustment, treaty, referendum: the modern Costa Rican settlement.
