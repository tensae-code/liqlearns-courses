# Absolute Monarchies and State Formation

## Do it first

Write what happens in a classroom, a team or a home when one person decides everything and no one may question. Then write what could challenge them.

Out of the feudal scatter of lords, early-modern kings build something tighter: the absolute monarchy. Power is concentrated — armies, taxes, courts and law gathered into the king's own hands, and around that single centre the modern state is formed. The book sets this state form carefully in place, because the rest of the unit leans on it: the Enlightenment will challenge exactly this — one unquestionable will ruling all — and revolution will break it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Early-modern kings gather all power into their own hands and form absolute monarchies — the state form the Enlightenment will soon challenge. Give one real example from your own life — any group where one voice decides all — the absolute pattern in small, and the wish to challenge it — and name the lesson's key words: absolute monarchy, power concentrated, state formation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
