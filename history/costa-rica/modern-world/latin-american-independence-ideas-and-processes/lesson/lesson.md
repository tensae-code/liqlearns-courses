# Latin American Independence: Ideas and Processes

## Do it first

Write one idea that could make a colony want to rule itself, and one hard problem it would face the morning after independence.

Nations are born twice: first in ideas, then in events. The book's Tema 1-A (printed p. 132) gathers the independence ideas — Enlightenment reason, the French and North American examples, creole grievances — and Tema 1-B (printed p. 140) follows them becoming processes: declarations, wars, congresses, the long unmaking of the Spanish empire in America. Colony by colony, new nations emerge — and Central America, Costa Rica inside it, takes its own road in the next lessons.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Across Spanish America, independence ideas become independence processes — colony by colony, new nations emerge, Costa Rica among them. Give one real example from your own life — 15 September in your own town — the celebration of exactly this emergence of new nations — and name the lesson's key words: independence, ideas, processes, new nations. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
