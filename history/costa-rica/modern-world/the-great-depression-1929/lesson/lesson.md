# The Great Depression, 1929

## Do it first

Write what happens in your street, week by week, if the main workplace closes and no one can sell what they grow.

In 1929 the capitalist world's machinery seizes. The crash on the stock market becomes the Great Depression of the 1930s: factories close, banks fail, harvests rot unsold, and millions lose work. The book studies the crisis where it lands — in everyday life, in kitchens and queues — and at the level of national economies, Costa Rica's coffee economy among the struck. The lesson's judgement is structural: 1929 exposes the crises the capitalist model carries inside itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The crash of 1929 and the depression of the 1930s expose the capitalist model's crises — felt in everyday life and in whole national economies. Give one real example from your own life — a closed shop or a lost job in your own family — the depression's everyday face in small — and name the lesson's key words: 1929 crash, Depression, everyday life, capitalist model. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
