# The First World War: Causes

## Do it first

Write how a quarrel between two schoolmates can pull in their friends until the whole yard is fighting — then ask what nations have that yards do not.

In 1914 the imperial world turns its weapons on itself. The book traces the war's causes — the rivalries, alliances and arms races that made Europe a loaded trap — and then follows what the war forced: societies mobilised whole, economies bent to the front, politics shaken to their roots. The First World War is the hinge of the century: everything after it, from revolution in Russia to crisis in Costa Rica's coffee trade, hangs on this door.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In 1914 the world goes to war: the lesson traces why it came, and the social, economic and political changes the war forced on everyone. Give one real example from your own life — an argument that spreads through a whole school — alliances pulling everyone in work the same way — and name the lesson's key words: First World War, 1914, causes, changes. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
