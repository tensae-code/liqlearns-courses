# Costa Rica: The Electoral System After 1949

## Do it first

Write the rules a fair election needs: who votes, who counts, who accepts the result. Then write what happens to a party the rules shut out.

## Sub lesson 1 — The electoral political system (the CONED telling)

The CONED telling turns from the economy to the rules of power: the sistema político electoral built after 1949 — elections regular and guarded, institutions to run them, parties to contest them. This system is the frame inside which the democratic decades of the welfare state happen: governments change by counting votes, not by arms — the 1948 war's great answer. A frame is invisible when it works; this lesson makes it visible.

## Sub lesson 2 — The electoral political system (the Porras y Gamboa telling)

The Porras y Gamboa telling presents the same post-1949 electoral order — and keeps one figure standing outside the frame: the Communist Party, outlawed under the new constitution, its voters and organisers excluded from the legal game. The lesson holds both facts together: a durable electoral democracy, and a founding exclusion inside it. Costa Rica's democratic decades are told whole, frame and shadow together.
