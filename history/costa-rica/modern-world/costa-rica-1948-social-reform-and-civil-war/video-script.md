# Video script — Costa Rica 1948: Social Reform and Civil War

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The 1940s bring Costa Rica its great social reforms — social guarantees, the labour code, the University and the Caja — and then the civil war of 1948, the founding rupture from which modern Costa Rica and the 1949 Constitution come.

The CONED telling binds the decade into one arc: reforma social y guerra civil. The 1940s open with social reform — guarantees written for workers, a labour code — the state stepping into daily life as protector. The same decade closes in the civil war of 1948, fought over a disputed election and the country's direction. The book calls 1948 the founding rupture of modern Costa Rica: the old republic breaks, and the one the reader lives in is founded on the far side of the war.

The Porras y Gamboa telling names the decade's furniture: Calderón Guardia's reforms — the University of Costa Rica founded in 1940, the Caja Costarricense de Seguro Social in 1941, the institution Costa Ricans still trust with their health — then the wartime Bloque de la Victoria, Picado's government, the disputed 1948 elections, the civil war itself, and the settlement that ends the story: the Constitution of 1949, charter of the republic that follows.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
