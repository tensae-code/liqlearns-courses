# Costa Rica: The Coffee and Banana Agro-Export Model

## Do it first

Follow one bag of coffee or one bunch of bananas from a Costa Rican field to a foreign breakfast table. Write every hand and ship it passes through.

Costa Rica plugs into the world. Coffee — grown in the central valley, carried by rail to the ports — becomes the motor of the whole country: not only its economy but its society and its culture turn around the harvest. Then a second motor starts on the other coast: banana production rises on the Caribbean, with its railways, ports and foreign companies. The book names the result: the agro-export model — a country inserted into the world market by what its fields sell abroad. The 20th century will test that model to breaking point.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Coffee becomes the motor of Costa Rica's economy, society and culture, and bananas rise on the Caribbean coast: the country joins the world market as an agro-exporter. Give one real example from your own life — the train to Limón and the banana zones of the Caribbean — this model's landscape is still your country's — and name the lesson's key words: coffee, banana, agro-export, world market. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
