# Costa Rica: The Welfare State, 1950–1980

## Do it first

List everything the state provides in your town — clinic, school, water, bank. Then write what life would be if each charged its full cost.

## Sub lesson 1 — The welfare state (the CONED telling)

The CONED telling gives the era its name: el Estado benefactor, the welfare or benefactor state. For three decades the Costa Rican state is provider and builder — health, education, credit, services — shaping a society around public institutions. The telling is honest to the end: the section closes looking at the crisis gathering over the benefactor state itself (it even sets the learner a 'Crisis del Estado Benefactor' crossword). The provider state's own weight becomes its question.

## Sub lesson 2 — The welfare state and Costa Rican society, 1950–1980 (the Porras y Gamboa telling)

The Porras y Gamboa telling inventories the machine: state banking putting credit in public hands, the Estado empresario — the state as entrepreneur, CODESA its emblem — running factories and services, health and education reaching every town. Its fruit: a broad middle class, Costa Rica's famous social shape. Its edges: urban problems growing in the cities, and land seizures in Guanacaste where the model's promises ran out. A whole society, built by a state — and priced, in the end, beyond it.
