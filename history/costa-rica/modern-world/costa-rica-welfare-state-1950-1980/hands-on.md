# Hands-on — Costa Rica: The Welfare State, 1950–1980

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List everything the state provides in your town — clinic, school, water, bank. Then write what life would be if each charged its full cost.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: For three decades the welfare state — the Estado benefactor — shapes Costa Rican society: state banking, the Estado empresario, health and education build a broad middle class, until the model's own crisis gathers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
