# The Spanish Conquest of America and Costa Rica

## Do it first

Write three ways a small arriving group could defeat larger peoples without winning every battle fairly.

The ships were few; the peoples of America were many. Yet within generations the Spanish conquest overwhelms the continent's indigenous societies, Costa Rica's included. The book refuses the simple story of braver soldiers: the conquest works by three forces together — war, with steel, horses and guns; disease, the invisible army that emptied towns before any battle; and alliance, as indigenous peoples divided and some joined the newcomers against their own rivals. Great societies fall, and a colonial world is built on their land.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Spanish conquest overwhelms America's indigenous societies — Costa Rica's among them — not by soldiers alone, but by war, disease and alliances together. Give one real example from your own life — sickness arriving in a community that has never met it — one of the conquest's three weapons, still understandable today — and name the lesson's key words: conquest, war, disease, alliance. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
