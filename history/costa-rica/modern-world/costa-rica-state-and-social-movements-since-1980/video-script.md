# Video script — Costa Rica: State and Social Movements Since 1980

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From 1980 to today the national story closes — for now — with the state, elections and social movements answering the neoliberal turn, and two-party rule and its discontents under judgement.

The CONED anthology closes the national story in the present tense: Estado, sistema electoral y movimientos sociales desde los 80 — the state reshaped by the crisis, the electoral system carrying the democracy forward, and social movements pressing their claims in the streets and communities. Three actors, one stage, from the 1980s to today. The story that began with the first peoples ends — for now — with the reader's own Costa Rica.

The Porras y Gamboa telling closes the book's 'yesterday and today in Costa Rica' with the answer-back: social movements responding to the neoliberal turn — defending public institutions, land, water and wages — while two-party rule, the old order of the democratic decades, frays under its discontents. The national story ends open: a state, a people in movement, and a settlement still being argued. That argument is the reader's inheritance.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
