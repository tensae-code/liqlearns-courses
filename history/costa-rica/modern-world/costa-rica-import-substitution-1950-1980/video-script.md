# Video script — Costa Rica: Import Substitution, 1950–1980

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From 1950 to 1980 Costa Rica tries to make at home what it used to import — diversifying production and building industry — until the import-substitution model exhausts itself.

After 1948 the country changes its recipe. The CONED telling: from 1950 to 1980 Costa Rica diversifies its production — more crops, more activities — and industrialises by substituting imports: making at home, behind protective walls, what it used to buy abroad. The agro-export hostage years are the lesson behind the policy: never again depend on two crops and foreign factories. For three decades this model, with the welfare state of the next lesson, organises Costa Rican life.

The Porras y Gamboa telling keeps the promise and adds the ending: import substitution and agricultural diversification promise industry, protected factories, new jobs — and for a time deliver. But the model exhausts itself: small markets, costly inputs, protected inefficiency. By the close of the period the recipe that answered the agro-export crisis has become a problem of its own — the ground on which the 1980s crisis will break.
