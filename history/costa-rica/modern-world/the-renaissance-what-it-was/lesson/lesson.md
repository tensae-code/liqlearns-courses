# The Renaissance: What It Was

## Do it first

Write the name of one old thing — a building, a story, a song — that people in your town have brought back or restored because it was worth keeping.

At the close of the medieval world, European eyes turn backward — to go forward. The Renaissance is a revaluing: scholars and artists lift up classical antiquity again, its Greek and Roman books, statues and buildings, and with them a new valuation of the human being — humanism — the person as capable, curious and central. Old manuscripts are hunted, old statues dug up, and in their light new art and new thought are made. The modern age begins by remembering.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Renaissance revalues two things at the close of the medieval world: the classical ancient world, and the human being at the centre of it. Give one real example from your own life — an old building restored in your own town — the Renaissance gesture in small: the past valued again — and name the lesson's key words: Renaissance, humanism, classical antiquity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
