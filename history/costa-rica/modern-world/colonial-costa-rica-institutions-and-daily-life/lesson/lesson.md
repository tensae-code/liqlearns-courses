# Colonial Costa Rica: Institutions and Daily Life

## Do it first

Draw your town as the colony would organise it: the main square, the church, the government house. Who sits where, and who is left outside?

Conquest is an event; colony is a system. The book organises it: America divided into administrative divisions, ruled through institutions — councils, courts, church and crown officials — and Costa Rica studied inside that frame with emphasis on its own case. Then the lens drops from the offices to the streets: colonial cities with their grid and square, their urban structure, and the everyday life of the people who lived in them — markets, houses, feasts and work. A colony is both the institution and the day.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The colony is a system: divisions and institutions rule from above, and below them a daily life of cities and streets goes on — studied with Costa Rica at the centre. Give one real example from your own life — the central park and church of your own town — the colonial city's plan, still under your feet — and name the lesson's key words: colony, institutions, cities, daily life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
