# Why Europe Colonised: Motives and the Civilizing Mission

## Do it first

Write two reasons a rich industrial country might want land far away, and one reason it would give in public to justify taking it.

Industrial Europe, squeezed by its late-century 'great depression', looks outward for markets, materials and land — and expands overseas, seizing societies across Africa, Asia and the Pacific. The Grade 10 book does not stop at the map of empires: it asks the harder question — how did imperialism change the societies it seized? Socially, economically, environmentally, politically: whole worlds are rearranged around Europe's needs. That question, asked from Costa Rica, opens the 20th century's story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Industrial Europe expands overseas and seizes other peoples' lands; the book asks what imperialism did to the societies it seized — socially, economically, environmentally, politically. Give one real example from your own life — a foreign company controlling land or a port in your own region — the imperial pattern's modern shadow — and name the lesson's key words: imperialism, expansion, seized societies. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
