# History — Costa Rica course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Costa Rica inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Costa Rica's books in hand are the
CONED distance-education Estudios Sociales anthologies, whose
own sequence is the teaching order — earliest books first —
with the private Porras y Gamboa Grade 11 book as a parallel
telling of the last level. The ladder follows the eras as the
books themselves divide them: the ancient world first — Africa
the cradle, the first farmers, the first civilisations, and
America's own Maya, Aztec and Inca, closing with Costa Rica's
indigenous peoples, so the national past begins before the
Spanish (Level 1); then the medieval world and the turn to
modern times — Rome's fall, feudalism, the two great faiths,
cities and universities, Renaissance, Reformation and Spain's
exploration (Level 2); then conquest, colony and the making of
Costa Rica — the conquest's three forces, the colonial system
and its exploitation and heritage, absolutism challenged by
Enlightenment and revolution, independence, industry, and
Costa Rica's own road from localisms to the Republic, the
coffee state and the agro-export model, closing at home with
daily life (Level 3); then the world of the Grade 10 book —
imperialism, the two world wars with revolution and depression
between them, the Cold War whole, and today's multipolar
tensions read from Latin America (Level 4); and last Costa
Rica's own 20th century as the two Grade 11 books tell it —
the agro-export crisis, democracy built by struggle (women's
suffrage inside it), 1948, the welfare state and its electoral
order, the 1980s break and the movements since (Level 5). The
Grade 8 anthology makes no level: as filed it is Geography
only, record-only for History, and the ladder steps over that
year as the CONED sequence itself pauses the History strand.
The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Estudios Sociales books of the Colegio Nacional de
Educación a Distancia (CONED) / Universidad Estatal a
Distancia (UNED), with one private-publisher Grade 11 book
(Publicaciones Porras y Gamboa) filed beside them — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book
contents map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Africa to 1500 + Ancient World → First Peoples and
   First Civilisations** — humanity evolves in Africa, the
   cradle of humankind, and settles into farming; the first
   civilisations rise between the rivers and on the Nile;
   Greece and Rome form the classical pair; and America's
   own civilisations — Maya, Aztec, Inca — close with Costa
   Rica's indigenous peoples in their territories before
   the Spanish. 10 lessons, in two titles: `africa-to-1500/`
   (3 lessons) and `ancient-world/` (7 lessons).

2. **Ancient World + Modern World → The Medieval World and
   the Turn to Modern Times** — Rome's west declines and
   falls; feudal societies reorganise Europe; Christianity
   consolidates and Islam is born and expands; cities,
   trade, universities and plague turn the old order; and
   the Renaissance, the Reformation and Spain's overseas
   exploration open the modern age. 8 lessons, in two
   titles: `ancient-world/` (5 lessons) and `modern-world/`
   (3 lessons).

3. **Modern World → Conquest, Colony and the Making of
   Costa Rica** — the Spanish conquest by war, disease and
   alliance; the colonial system — institutions and daily
   life, extraction, slavery and the heritage weighed
   today; absolute monarchies challenged by Enlightenment
   and the French Revolution; Latin American independence;
   the Industrial Revolution; and Costa Rica building
   itself: localisms to the Republic of 1848, the
   coffee-military state and consolidation, coffee and
   banana joining the world market, and daily life in the
   late 19th century. 12 lessons, in one title:
   `modern-world/` (12 lessons).

4. **Africa After 1500 + Modern World → The World Wars, the
   Cold War and the World Today** — European imperialism
   and what it did to the societies it seized; the First
   World War, the Russian Revolution, the 1929 Depression
   and the Second World War with its racial exterminations;
   the bipolar Cold War — its proxy conflicts, its own
   peoples' struggles, its two economic models — and its
   end; then the multipolar present: China's rise, tensions
   with a Cold War background, ethnic cleansing and human
   rights, and Latin America's place. 13 lessons, in two
   titles: `africa-after-1500/` (1 lesson) and
   `modern-world/` (12 lessons).

5. **Modern World → Costa Rica's Own Twentieth Century** —
   the two Grade 11 books tell the national century twice,
   each topic in two sub lessons: the agro-export model in
   crisis; democracy built by social struggles, with
   women's suffrage struggle standing as its own lesson
   from the Porras book; social reform and the civil war
   of 1948; import substitution and the welfare state,
   1950–1980; the electoral system after 1949; the 1980s
   crisis and the export-promotion turn; and state and
   social movements since 1980. 9 lessons, in one title:
   `modern-world/` (9 lessons).

**Totals: 52 lessons** (Level 1: 10, Level 2: 8, Level 3: 12,
Level 4: 13, Level 5: 9) — 60 placements stocked, every one
on a student book: taught in 1 country (Costa Rica). No
lesson in this course stands on teacher-guide evidence; the
filing holds no Costa Rica teacher guide.
