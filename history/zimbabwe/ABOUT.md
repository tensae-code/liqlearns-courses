# About the History — Zimbabwe course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own PlusOne Social Science teacher guides. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Zimbabwe inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **four levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My
   Country.** The HBC spiral's opening arc: the child's own
   story (name, totem, family, locality), the nation (anthem,
   flag, peoples, pledge, calendar), and heritage in its two
   kinds, carried by Ubuntu/Unhu/Vumunhu. 8 lessons, 44
   placements.
2. **Level 2 — Zimbabwe Long Ago: Early People and the
   Pre-colonial States.** The Khoisan, the Bantu migration of
   about 2,000 years ago, Great Zimbabwe and the pre-colonial
   states compared, and pre-colonial family life. 6 lessons,
   12 placements.
3. **Level 3 — Strangers Arrive, Colonisation and the Two
   Chimurengas.** First contact, the motives and the paper
   road to colonisation, the Rudd Concession and the Pioneer
   Column, the First Chimurenga of 1896–97, the Second
   Chimurenga of 1966–79, and independence on 18 April 1980.
   8 lessons, 10 placements.
4. **Level 4 — Independent Zimbabwe: Land, Towns and Living
   Heritage.** Land reform, the growth of settlements,
   Indigenous Knowledge Systems, and work, leisure, transport
   and communication across time. 5 lessons, 6 placements.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *My family, my heritage, my country:* "Your story starts at
  home — your name, your totem, your family's tree — and it
  widens ring by ring: family, friends, school, the heritage
  site you can walk to. Then the country itself: ten
  provinces, many peoples, one flag whose colours are
  sentences — green the land, gold the minerals, red the
  heroes' blood, white peace — and one value holding it all:
  a person is a person through other people."
- *Zimbabwe long ago:* "First the hunters, painting their
  lives on rock. Then, about 2,000 years ago, newcomers with
  iron and cattle, settling for good. Then stone cities
  without cement, and kingdoms made three ways — on gold, on
  the spear, in ranks."
- *Strangers, colonisation and the Chimurengas:* "Three kinds
  of stranger arrive. Then the pen does what the spear had
  not, a king is tricked out of his minerals, and a column
  marches in. Twice the country rises — 1896, then 1966 —
  and the second rising ends at a table in 1979 and a flag in
  1980."
- *Independent Zimbabwe:* "The war's grievance outlives the
  war: the land. Villages grow into cities, old knowledge is
  named a system and judged fairly, and the runners on the
  footpaths become rail, road and air."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Book 1 — PlusOne Social Science ECD A Teacher's Guide:** Heritage-Based Curriculum (HBC) infant Social Science starts History as the child's own story — name, totem/family name, family and locality — then the nation (anthem, flag colours explained as land, minerals, heroes' blood, the people), then heritage split into things you can touch and things you do/say. *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 2 — PlusOne Social Science Grade 1 Teacher's Guide (author Miriam Muzunze):** the HBC spiral repeats ECD's identity → nation → heritage arc a year on with named content: Zimbabwe's 10 provinces and ethnic groups (Ndau of Chipinge, Tonga of Binga, Chewa of Hwange), the flag's five colours, Great Zimbabwe and Victoria Falls as tangible heritage, and Ubuntu/Unhu/Vumunhu named as the value system heritage carries. *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 3 — PlusOne Social Science ECD B Teacher's Guide (author Chenai D. Masama):** the ECD B year widens the family circle to the extended family and adds "national colours" as its own lesson, keeping the same story-first, sing-and-act pedagogy. *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 4 — PlusOne Social Science Grade 2 Teacher's Guide (author Miriam Muzunze; cover shows a school group at Great Zimbabwe):** Grade 2 adds the nation's calendar to the symbols — Independence Day and Heroes Day with named heroes and heroines (Josiah Tongogara, Herbert Chitepo, John Nkomo, Sally Mugabe, Ruth Chinamano) buried at the National Heroes Acre, visited on an educational tour. *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 5 — PlusOne Social Science Grade 3 Teacher's Guide (author Moreblessing Masuka):** Grade 3 turns identity into method — children draw a family tree to "find their origins" and capture stories for the next generation — and turns heroes local, from district heroes up to Mbuya Nehanda, Joshua Nkomo and Josiah Tongogara; heritage sites are learned by drawing, model-building and story-writing. *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 6 — PlusOne Social Science Grade 4 Teacher's Guide (author Moreblessing Masuka):** Grade 4 is where Zimbabwe's deep past enters — the Khoisan first, then the Bantu migration of about 2,000 years ago with iron and farming — taught by group research and presentation, and immediately cashed out as heritage: the languages, stone cities and plant medicines modern Zimbabwe still lives on. Note: the guide's own answer key says the Bantu "migrated from central Africa in Kenya" — a garbled origin line kept as printed. *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 7 — PlusOne Social Science Grade 5 Teacher's Guide (author Moreblessing Masuka; cover shows children playing nhodo):** Grade 5 Topic 1 is titled outright "Identity: Family history and local heritage", and Topic 2 becomes narrative history: four pre-colonial states compared in tables (time, place, leadership, economy, religion, decline), then first European contact debated as a two-team poster argument — the guide explicitly teaches "balanced perspectives when studying history". *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 8 — PlusOne Social Science Grade 6 Teacher's Guide (author Moreblessing Masuka):** Grade 6 Topic 2 is a full national narrative in four acts — colonisation by treaty trickery and the Pioneer Column, the First Chimurenga of 1896–97, the Second Chimurenga of 1966–79, and the symbols-and-holidays settlement that follows independence — with spirit mediums given a named role in both wars. *(Teacher guide.)*
- **Book 9 — PlusOne Social Science Grade 7 Teacher's Guide (author Moreblessing Masuka):** the primary cycle closes by turning independence into civics and then into its unfinished business — the land question — taught straight: willing-buyer-willing-seller first, Britain's "backtracked" funding promises, then fast-track reform from 2000, with benefits (empowerment, food production, cultural restoration) as the guide's settled verdict. *(Teacher guide.)*
