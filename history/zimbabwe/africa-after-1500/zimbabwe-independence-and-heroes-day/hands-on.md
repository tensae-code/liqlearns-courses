# Hands-on — Zimbabwe: Independence and Heroes Day

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Do the opening act of lesson/lesson.md again, slowly, and name each step aloud as you do it. Hold the lesson's one thing in mind as you read: Zimbabwe's year is founded on its story: Independence Day on 18 April marks the 1980 break from colonial rule, Heroes Day names those who died for that freedom and lays them at the National Heroes Acre, and the year's other holidays — Defence Forces Day, Unity Day on 22 December and Workers Day — each retell a chapter of the national story.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw or write this lesson's story as a row of scenes — one scene for each part of the lesson, in order (one for each sub lesson). Under the row, write the lesson's key words: Independence Day, 18 April 1980, Heroes Day, National Heroes Acre, Unity Day, Defence Forces Day, Workers Day. Say the story aloud, scene by scene, as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place where the lesson's story is still alive — a name, a custom, a site, a day kept on purpose — note it: what you saw, and which part of the lesson it belongs to. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
