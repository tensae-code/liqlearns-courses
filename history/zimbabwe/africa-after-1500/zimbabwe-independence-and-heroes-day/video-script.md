# Video script — Zimbabwe: Independence and Heroes Day

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Zimbabwe's year is founded on its story: Independence Day on 18 April marks the 1980 break from colonial rule, Heroes Day names those who died for that freedom and lays them at the National Heroes Acre, and the year's other holidays — Defence Forces Day, Unity Day on 22 December and Workers Day — each retell a chapter of the national story.

Part 1 — Topic 2, Unit 6 National events and celebrations. On 18 April the country marks the 1980 break from colonial rule, and on Heroes Day it names those who died for that freedom — Tongogara, Chitepo, Nkomo, Sally Mugabe, Ruth Chinamano — and lays them at the Heroes Acre.

Part 2 — Topic 2, Unit 6 National events / celebrations. The national calendar returns at Grade 3 with the district added — the country's milestones are now also the town's own days of remembrance.

Part 3 — The five holidays. The year is re-founded in holidays — Independence Day, Heroes Day, Defence Forces Day, Unity Day (22 December 1987) and Workers Day — each one a chapter of the national story retold annually.

Remember it this way: Zimbabwe's year is founded on its story: Independence Day on 18 April marks the 1980 break from colonial rule, Heroes Day names those who died for that freedom and lays them at the National Heroes Acre, and the year's other holidays — Defence Forces Day, Unity Day on 22 December and Workers Day — each retell a chapter of the national story.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
