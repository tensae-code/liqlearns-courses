# Lesson plan — Zimbabwe: Independence and Heroes Day

**Course:** History — Zimbabwe · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zimbabwe: Independence and Heroes Day
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Zimbabwe's year is founded on its story: Independence Day on 18 April marks the 1980 break from colonial rule, Heroes Day names those who died for that freedom and lays them at the National Heroes Acre, and the year's other holidays — Defence Forces Day, Unity Day on 22 December and Workers Day — each retell a chapter of the national story.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 2, Unit 6 National events and celebrations** — On 18 April the country marks the 1980 break from colonial rule, and on Heroes Day it names those who died for that freedom — Tongogara, Chitepo, Nkomo, Sally Mugabe, Ruth Chinamano — and lays them at the Heroes Acre.
2. **Topic 2, Unit 6 National events / celebrations** — The national calendar returns at Grade 3 with the district added — the country's milestones are now also the town's own days of remembrance.
3. **The five holidays** — The year is re-founded in holidays — Independence Day, Heroes Day, Defence Forces Day, Unity Day (22 December 1987) and Workers Day — each one a chapter of the national story retold annually.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Zimbabwe's books themselves work, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.
