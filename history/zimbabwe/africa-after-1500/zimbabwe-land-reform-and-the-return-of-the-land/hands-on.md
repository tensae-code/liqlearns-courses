# Hands-on — Zimbabwe: Land Reform and the Return of the Land

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Do the opening act of lesson/lesson.md again, slowly, and name each step aloud as you do it. Hold the lesson's one thing in mind as you read: the land question outlived the war: land stayed in settler hands under willing-buyer-willing-seller, Britain's funding promises failed, and in 2000 fast-track reform under Robert Mugabe redistributed the land to black Zimbabweans — taught in Zimbabwe's books as empowerment, food production and cultural restoration.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw or write this lesson's story as a row of scenes — one scene for each part of the lesson, in order. Under the row, write the lesson's key words: land reform, willing-buyer-willing-seller, fast-track reform 2000, Robert Mugabe, empowerment, food production. Say the story aloud, scene by scene, as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place where the lesson's story is still alive — a name, a custom, a site, a day kept on purpose — note it: what you saw, and which part of the lesson it belongs to. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
