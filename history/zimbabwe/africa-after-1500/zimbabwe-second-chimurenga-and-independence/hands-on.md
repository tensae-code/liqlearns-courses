# Hands-on — Zimbabwe: The Second Chimurenga and Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Do the opening act of lesson/lesson.md again, slowly, and name each step aloud as you do it. Hold the lesson's one thing in mind as you read: the Second Chimurenga opened at Chinhoyi in 1966, when ZANU and ZAPU's armies, ZANLA and ZIPRA, took up the gun; thirteen years of war over land, taxes and unequal schooling ended at Lancaster House in 1979, and independence came on 18 April 1980 — a people who had no say under colonial rule now choosing their leaders, controlling their resources and keeping their own identity.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw or write this lesson's story as a row of scenes — one scene for each part of the lesson, in order (one for each sub lesson). Under the row, write the lesson's key words: Second Chimurenga, Chinhoyi 1966, ZANU, ZAPU, ZANLA, ZIPRA, Lancaster House 1979, independence 18 April 1980, self-governance. Say the story aloud, scene by scene, as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place where the lesson's story is still alive — a name, a custom, a site, a day kept on purpose — note it: what you saw, and which part of the lesson it belongs to. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
