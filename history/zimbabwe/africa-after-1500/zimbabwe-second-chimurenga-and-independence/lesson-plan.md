# Lesson plan — Zimbabwe: The Second Chimurenga and Independence

**Course:** History — Zimbabwe · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zimbabwe: The Second Chimurenga and Independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Strangers Arrive, Colonisation and the Two Chimurengas (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Second Chimurenga opened at Chinhoyi in 1966, when ZANU and ZAPU's armies, ZANLA and ZIPRA, took up the gun; thirteen years of war over land, taxes and unequal schooling ended at Lancaster House in 1979, and independence came on 18 April 1980 — a people who had no say under colonial rule now choosing their leaders, controlling their resources and keeping their own identity.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Strangers Arrive, Colonisation and the Two Chimurengas — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Second Chimurenga/Umvukela** — ZANU and ZAPU's armies, ZANLA and ZIPRA, open fire at Chinhoyi in 1966; thirteen years of war over land, taxes and unequal schooling end at Lancaster House in 1979 — and independence comes on 18 April 1980.
2. **Topic 2, Unit 4 Independence and self-governance** — 1980 is re-read as a definition — a people who had no say under colonial rule now choose their leaders, control their resources and keep their own identity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the Second Chimurenga opened at Chinhoyi in 1966, when ZANU and ZAPU's armies, ZANLA and ZIPRA, took up the gun; thirteen years of war over land, taxes and unequal schooling ended at Lancaster House in 1979, and independence came on 18 April 1980 — a people who had no say under colonial rule now choosing their leaders, controlling their resources and keeping their own identity.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Zimbabwe story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Zimbabwe's books themselves work, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of the new Zimbabwe flag rising at independence in 1980, with the long road from Chinhoyi to Lancaster House drawn behind it — the student points to the flag and says what a self-governing people can now do.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: the Second Chimurenga opened at Chinhoyi in 1966, when ZANU and ZAPU's armies, ZANLA and ZIPRA, took up the gun; thirteen years of war over land, taxes and unequal schooling ended at Lancaster House in 1979, and independence came on 18 April 1980 — a people who had no say under colonial rule now choosing their leaders, controlling their resources and keeping their own identity. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
