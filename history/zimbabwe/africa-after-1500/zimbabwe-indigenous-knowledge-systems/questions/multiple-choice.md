# Zimbabwe: Indigenous Knowledge Systems — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** Which of these tells this lesson's story? (Sub lesson 1)
a) The knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books — is named as a system in its own right, worthy of the classroom.  b) the past can be known without any story, evidence or memory at all  c) nothing in Zimbabwe's story ever changed, and no one in it ever acted  d) only foreign rulers have a past, and Zimbabwe's own people have none
**Answer: a) The knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books — is named as a system in its own right, worthy of the classroom.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2.** What else does this lesson teach? (Sub lesson 2)
a) every age in Zimbabwe lived in exactly the same way, with nothing handed down  b) The old food-keeping knowledge — drying, smoking, storing — is set beside today's methods and judged on its own merits, IKS as living technology.  c) nothing in Zimbabwe's story ever changed, and no one in it ever acted  d) the past can be known without any story, evidence or memory at all
**Answer: b) The old food-keeping knowledge — drying, smoking, storing — is set beside today's methods and judged on its own merits, IKS as living technology.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
