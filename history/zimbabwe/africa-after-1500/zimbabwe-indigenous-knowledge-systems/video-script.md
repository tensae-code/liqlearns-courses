# Video script — Zimbabwe: Indigenous Knowledge Systems

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Indigenous Knowledge Systems are a system in their own right: the knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books, and food kept by drying, smoking and storing — judged on its own merits beside today's methods.

Part 1 — Topic 3, Unit 12 Indigenous Knowledge Systems (IKS). The knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books — is named as a system in its own right, worthy of the classroom.

Part 2 — Topic 3, Unit 10 Indigenous and contemporary ways of preserving food. The old food-keeping knowledge — drying, smoking, storing — is set beside today's methods and judged on its own merits, IKS as living technology.

Remember it this way: Indigenous Knowledge Systems are a system in their own right: the knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books, and food kept by drying, smoking and storing — judged on its own merits beside today's methods.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
