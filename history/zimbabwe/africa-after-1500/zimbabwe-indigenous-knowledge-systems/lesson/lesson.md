# Zimbabwe: Indigenous Knowledge Systems

## Do it first

How do you keep food without a fridge, heal without a pharmacy, read weather without a forecast? Zimbabwe's oldest knowledge answered all three. This lesson names that knowledge a system — and puts it beside today's methods to be judged fairly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 3, Unit 12 Indigenous Knowledge Systems (IKS)

The knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books — is named as a system in its own right, worthy of the classroom.

## Sub lesson 2 — Topic 3, Unit 10 Indigenous and contemporary ways of preserving food

The old food-keeping knowledge — drying, smoking, storing — is set beside today's methods and judged on its own merits, IKS as living technology.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Indigenous Knowledge Systems are a system in their own right: the knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books, and food kept by drying, smoking and storing — judged on its own merits beside today's methods. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: Indigenous Knowledge Systems, IKS, healing, farming, weather, craft, preserving food, drying, smoking, storing. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
