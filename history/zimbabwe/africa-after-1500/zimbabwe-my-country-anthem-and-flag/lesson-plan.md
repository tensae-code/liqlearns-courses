# Lesson plan — Zimbabwe: My Country, Anthem and Flag

**Course:** History — Zimbabwe · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zimbabwe: My Country, Anthem and Flag
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Zimbabwe's anthem, flag, national colours, school pledge and symbols are a kit of belonging: the flag's colours carry the country's story — green for the land, gold for its minerals, red for the blood heroes lost to make the country free, black for its people, white for peace — and the pledge turns that story into a daily promise.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **My country, National anthem, National flag** — Children learn their country is Zimbabwe, stand at attention for its special song, and decode the flag — green for the land, gold for minerals, red for the blood heroes lost to make the country free, black for its people.
2. **Topic 2, Units 7–9 National flag, National school pledge, National anthem** — The flag's five colours are sung as a rhyme — red for the blood of those who fought for freedom, white for peace — and the morning pledge turns that story into a daily promise of hard work, honesty and unity.
3. **National anthem, National flag, National colours** — The anthem and flag return, and the colours step out of the flag to be learned on their own, so the freedom story in red and the peace in white become the child's first national vocabulary.
4. **Topic 2, Units 3–5 National anthem, National symbols, National school pledge** — Anthem, symbols and pledge are gathered as one kit of belonging, recited and sung until the nation's story is carried in the child's own voice.
5. **Topic 2, Unit 7 National symbols** — The symbols are revisited as a set to be explained, not just recognised — each one carries a piece of the country's story.
6. **Topic 2, Unit 10 Importance of the national school pledge** — The pledge is now studied for why it matters — a daily spoken promise that binds the schoolchild to the country's story.
7. **Topic 2, Unit 10 National symbols and celebrations** — The settlement after the war is symbolic — flag, Zimbabwe bird and coat of arms on money and documents, so the state children fought for is seen every day.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Zimbabwe's books themselves work, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.
