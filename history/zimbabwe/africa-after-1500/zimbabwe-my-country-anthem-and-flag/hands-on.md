# Hands-on — Zimbabwe: My Country, Anthem and Flag

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Do the opening act of lesson/lesson.md again, slowly, and name each step aloud as you do it. Hold the lesson's one thing in mind as you read: Zimbabwe's anthem, flag, national colours, school pledge and symbols are a kit of belonging: the flag's colours carry the country's story — green for the land, gold for its minerals, red for the blood heroes lost to make the country free, black for its people, white for peace — and the pledge turns that story into a daily promise.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw or write this lesson's story as a row of scenes — one scene for each part of the lesson, in order (one for each sub lesson). Under the row, write the lesson's key words: national anthem, national flag, national colours, school pledge, national symbols, Zimbabwe bird, coat of arms. Say the story aloud, scene by scene, as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place where the lesson's story is still alive — a name, a custom, a site, a day kept on purpose — note it: what you saw, and which part of the lesson it belongs to. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
