# Lesson plan — Zimbabwe: Heroes from Nehanda to the Liberation War

**Course:** History — Zimbabwe · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zimbabwe: Heroes from Nehanda to the Liberation War
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Strangers Arrive, Colonisation and the Two Chimurengas (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Zimbabwe's heroes run in one line from the district's own heroes up to the nation: Mbuya Nehanda, the spirit medium who mobilised the First Chimurenga and was executed for it with Sekuru Kaguvi, and Joshua Nkomo and Josiah Tongogara of the liberation war — heroes children draw proud and brave for the classroom wall.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Strangers Arrive, Colonisation and the Two Chimurengas — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 2, Unit 8 Local heroes and heroines** — Heroes shrink from the national acre to the district, then grow back through three faces — Mbuya Nehanda, the brave woman who fought for freedom, Joshua Nkomo, and Josiah Tongogara — and children draw them proud and brave for the classroom wall.
2. **Spirit mediums and heroes of the First Chimurenga** — The war is mobilised by the spirits — Mbuya Nehanda, Sekuru Kaguvi and Mukwati predict battles, protect fighters and urge the people on — and Nehanda and Kaguvi are executed for it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Zimbabwe's books themselves work, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.
