# Video script — Zimbabwe: My Country and Its Peoples

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Zimbabwe is one country made of many peoples: Harare is its capital, it has 10 provinces, and each of its peoples — Ndau, Tonga, Chewa, Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Ndebele, Venda and others — is known by where it lives, the tongue it speaks and the customs it keeps.

Part 1 — Topic 2, Unit 6 My country and ethnic groups. Zimbabwe is named as home with Harare its capital and 10 provinces, and its peoples are introduced one by one — Ndau dancing muchongoyo at Chipinge, Tonga fishing at Binga, Chewa dancing Gule Wamkulu at Hwange.

Part 2 — Topic 2, Unit 7 Ethnic groups and languages. The peoples lesson returns with languages attached, so each group is known by where it lives and the tongue it speaks.

Part 3 — Topic 3, Unit 11 Customs of different cultural groups. Each cultural group in Zimbabwe keeps its own customs, and the lesson lines them up side by side so difference itself becomes the national picture.

Part 4 — Topic 1, Unit 6 Different languages in Zimbabwe. The country's many tongues are counted and mapped, so language itself becomes the census of Zimbabwe's peoples.

Part 5 — Topic 1, Unit 3 Different ethnic groups of Zimbabwe and their cultures. The peoples of Zimbabwe are paraded group by group, each with its culture, so the nation is learned as a gathering of distinct peoples.

Remember it this way: Zimbabwe is one country made of many peoples: Harare is its capital, it has 10 provinces, and each of its peoples — Ndau, Tonga, Chewa, Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Ndebele, Venda and others — is known by where it lives, the tongue it speaks and the customs it keeps.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
