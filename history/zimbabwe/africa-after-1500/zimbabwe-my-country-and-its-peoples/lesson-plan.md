# Lesson plan — Zimbabwe: My Country and Its Peoples

**Course:** History — Zimbabwe · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zimbabwe: My Country and Its Peoples
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Zimbabwe is one country made of many peoples: Harare is its capital, it has 10 provinces, and each of its peoples — Ndau, Tonga, Chewa, Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Ndebele, Venda and others — is known by where it lives, the tongue it speaks and the customs it keeps.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 2, Unit 6 My country and ethnic groups** — Zimbabwe is named as home with Harare its capital and 10 provinces, and its peoples are introduced one by one — Ndau dancing muchongoyo at Chipinge, Tonga fishing at Binga, Chewa dancing Gule Wamkulu at Hwange.
2. **Topic 2, Unit 7 Ethnic groups and languages** — The peoples lesson returns with languages attached, so each group is known by where it lives and the tongue it speaks.
3. **Topic 3, Unit 11 Customs of different cultural groups** — Each cultural group in Zimbabwe keeps its own customs, and the lesson lines them up side by side so difference itself becomes the national picture.
4. **Topic 1, Unit 6 Different languages in Zimbabwe** — The country's many tongues are counted and mapped, so language itself becomes the census of Zimbabwe's peoples.
5. **Topic 1, Unit 3 Different ethnic groups of Zimbabwe and their cultures** — The peoples of Zimbabwe are paraded group by group, each with its culture, so the nation is learned as a gathering of distinct peoples.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Zimbabwe is one country made of many peoples: Harare is its capital, it has 10 provinces, and each of its peoples — Ndau, Tonga, Chewa, Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Ndebele, Venda and others — is known by where it lives, the tongue it speaks and the customs it keeps.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Zimbabwe story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Zimbabwe's books themselves work, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.
