# Zimbabwe: My Country and Its Peoples

## Do it first

Name the place you live. Now name one other people of Zimbabwe and one thing they are known for — a dance, a fishing shore, a language. This lesson gathers all of them into one picture: one country, many peoples.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 2, Unit 6 My country and ethnic groups

Zimbabwe is named as home with Harare its capital and 10 provinces, and its peoples are introduced one by one — Ndau dancing muchongoyo at Chipinge, Tonga fishing at Binga, Chewa dancing Gule Wamkulu at Hwange.

## Sub lesson 2 — Topic 2, Unit 7 Ethnic groups and languages

The peoples lesson returns with languages attached, so each group is known by where it lives and the tongue it speaks.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 3, Unit 11 Customs of different cultural groups

Each cultural group in Zimbabwe keeps its own customs, and the lesson lines them up side by side so difference itself becomes the national picture.

## Sub lesson 4 — Topic 1, Unit 6 Different languages in Zimbabwe

The country's many tongues are counted and mapped, so language itself becomes the census of Zimbabwe's peoples.

## Sub lesson 5 — Topic 1, Unit 3 Different ethnic groups of Zimbabwe and their cultures

The peoples of Zimbabwe are paraded group by group, each with its culture, so the nation is learned as a gathering of distinct peoples.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Zimbabwe is one country made of many peoples: Harare is its capital, it has 10 provinces, and each of its peoples — Ndau, Tonga, Chewa, Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Ndebele, Venda and others — is known by where it lives, the tongue it speaks and the customs it keeps. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: Zimbabwe, provinces, ethnic groups, Ndau, Tonga, Chewa, languages, customs, Harare. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
