# Zimbabwe: Culture, Language and Respect for Elders

## Do it first

Say one blunt sentence — 'Give me that.' Now say it again the polite way, the way you would speak to an elder. The words changed, and something else changed with them. This lesson names that something: culture, carried in language.

## The story

A child learns to swap a blunt demand for polite words to an elder, and language itself becomes the way respect — and culture — is handed down.

That is the whole of this short lesson, and it is enough: language is how culture is handed down: swapping a blunt demand for polite words to an elder is culture being passed on, one sentence at a time.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: language is how culture is handed down: swapping a blunt demand for polite words to an elder is culture being passed on, one sentence at a time. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: culture, language, respect for elders, polite words. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
