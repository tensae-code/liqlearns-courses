# Lesson plan — Zimbabwe: Ubuntu, Unhu, Vumunhu

**Course:** History — Zimbabwe · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Zimbabwe: Ubuntu, Unhu, Vumunhu
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ubuntu — Unhu, Vumunhu — is the value that carries Zimbabwe's heritage: a person is a person through other people, so respect, sharing and care for elders are the rules the community lives by.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Topic 3, Unit 12 Attributes of Ubuntu/Unhu/Vumunhu** — The heritage topics close by naming the value that carries them — a person is a person through other people, so respect, sharing and care for elders become the rules the community lives by.
2. **Topic 3, Unit 9 Attributes of unhu/ubuntu/vumunhu** — Unhu is unpacked into its attributes, so the value stops being a word and becomes the checklist of how a good person treats others.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Ubuntu — Unhu, Vumunhu — is the value that carries Zimbabwe's heritage: a person is a person through other people, so respect, sharing and care for elders are the rules the community lives by.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Zimbabwe story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Zimbabwe's books themselves work, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.
