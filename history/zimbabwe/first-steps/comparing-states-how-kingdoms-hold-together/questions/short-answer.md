# Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** What is this lesson's one thing? (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** Kingdoms hold together — and fall — in ways that can be compared: kings advised by chiefs and spirit mediums, classes of royals and commoners, the indigenous court (the dare) hearing disputes, and declines that come by drought of resources or by outside interference.

**2.** What happens in the part called "Comparing the states"? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** The class lays the kingdoms side by side — kings advised by chiefs and spirit mediums like Chaminuka and Nehanda, classes of royals and commoners, and declines by drought of resources or by Portuguese interference.
