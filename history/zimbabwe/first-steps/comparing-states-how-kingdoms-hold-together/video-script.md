# Video script — Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Kingdoms hold together — and fall — in ways that can be compared: kings advised by chiefs and spirit mediums, classes of royals and commoners, the indigenous court (the dare) hearing disputes, and declines that come by drought of resources or by outside interference.

Part 1 — Topic 2, Unit 9 The indigenous court system. Before the modern courthouse, the dare — the indigenous court — held the community together, hearing disputes and settling them under its own rules.

Part 2 — Comparing the states. The class lays the kingdoms side by side — kings advised by chiefs and spirit mediums like Chaminuka and Nehanda, classes of royals and commoners, and declines by drought of resources or by Portuguese interference.

Part 3 — Topic 2, Unit 5 Systems of governance. Ways of ruling are compared — the indigenous systems Zimbabwe had and the systems that came later — so learners can ask what holds a state together.

Remember it this way: kingdoms hold together — and fall — in ways that can be compared: kings advised by chiefs and spirit mediums, classes of royals and commoners, the indigenous court (the dare) hearing disputes, and declines that come by drought of resources or by outside interference.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
