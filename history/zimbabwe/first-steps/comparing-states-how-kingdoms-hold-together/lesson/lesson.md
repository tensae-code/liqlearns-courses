# Comparing States: How Kingdoms Hold Together

## Do it first

Put two kingdoms side by side in your mind and ask of each: who advises the ruler? Who judges disputes? What could make it fall? This lesson teaches that comparing — the way Zimbabwe's own books lay their states side by side.

## Sub lesson 1 — Topic 2, Unit 9 The indigenous court system

Before the modern courthouse, the dare — the indigenous court — held the community together, hearing disputes and settling them under its own rules.

## Sub lesson 2 — Comparing the states

The class lays the kingdoms side by side — kings advised by chiefs and spirit mediums like Chaminuka and Nehanda, classes of royals and commoners, and declines by drought of resources or by Portuguese interference.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 2, Unit 5 Systems of governance

Ways of ruling are compared — the indigenous systems Zimbabwe had and the systems that came later — so learners can ask what holds a state together.
