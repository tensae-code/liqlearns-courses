# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Say your own full name out loud. Now say the name of the person who keeps you safe at home, and one person one ring wider — a grandparent, an aunt, a teacher. You have just drawn the first map this lesson teaches: your story starts at home, and it widens ring by ring.

## Sub lesson 1 — Myself, my family, siblings and guardians

A small child learns to say her own name, age and totem, names mother, father, siblings and the guardian who keeps her safe, and finds her story starts at home.

## Sub lesson 2 — Myself and my family, my friends, my school, roles of family members, family norms and values

The child's world widens in rings — family first, then friends, then school — and each ring has people with roles and shared rules that hold it together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Myself, my family, my extended family, my friends, my school, my teacher

The family story grows a ring — grandparents, aunts and uncles join parents and siblings — and the child learns the wider kin who carry the family's memory.

## Sub lesson 4 — Relatives, family norms and values; Relationship between the family and the community

The family is set inside the community that surrounds it, and the child learns the two hold each other up through shared norms and values.

## Sub lesson 5 — Topic 1, Units 1–2 Types of families; Family tree

A child draws her family as a tree and discovers the drawing is a tool — it shows relationships, finds her origins, and captures stories and traditions before they are lost.

## Sub lesson 6 — Research your local heritage site

The class project sends each child to ask parents and teachers for the heritage site nearest home, find why it matters, and present it — history begins with the place you can walk to.

## Sub lesson 7 — Topic 1, Units 1, 3 Types of families; Family gatherings and aspirations

The family gathers and its gatherings become the place where the child hears who the family has been and what it hopes to become.

## Sub lesson 8 — Units 1–4 Roles of family and institutions, community gatherings, totem system

Family history is now the topic's printed name, and the totem system returns as the key that unlocks who belongs where in the local story.

## Sub lesson 9 — Topic 14, Unit 53 Prominent people and organisations in voluntary work, past and present

Local history is also biography — the named helpers of the district, past and present, become the child's first gallery of lives worth remembering.

## Sub lesson 10 — Topic 14, Unit 45 Prominent people involved in voluntary work in the past and present

The biography gallery returns at the top of primary school — past and present local helpers as the community's remembered history.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: history begins at home: a child's own name, family and locality are the first history there is, and the family tree, the local heritage site and the named helpers of the district are the tools that turn that home story into history. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: family history, locality, family tree, origins, extended family, local heritage site, prominent people. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
