# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Zimbabwe · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** history begins at home: a child's own name, family and locality are the first history there is, and the family tree, the local heritage site and the named helpers of the district are the tools that turn that home story into history.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: My Family, My Heritage and My Country — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Myself, my family, siblings and guardians** — A small child learns to say her own name, age and totem, names mother, father, siblings and the guardian who keeps her safe, and finds her story starts at home.
2. **Myself and my family, my friends, my school, roles of family members, family norms and values** — The child's world widens in rings — family first, then friends, then school — and each ring has people with roles and shared rules that hold it together.
3. **Myself, my family, my extended family, my friends, my school, my teacher** — The family story grows a ring — grandparents, aunts and uncles join parents and siblings — and the child learns the wider kin who carry the family's memory.
4. **Relatives, family norms and values; Relationship between the family and the community** — The family is set inside the community that surrounds it, and the child learns the two hold each other up through shared norms and values.
5. **Topic 1, Units 1–2 Types of families; Family tree** — A child draws her family as a tree and discovers the drawing is a tool — it shows relationships, finds her origins, and captures stories and traditions before they are lost.
6. **Research your local heritage site** — The class project sends each child to ask parents and teachers for the heritage site nearest home, find why it matters, and present it — history begins with the place you can walk to.
7. **Topic 1, Units 1, 3 Types of families; Family gatherings and aspirations** — The family gathers and its gatherings become the place where the child hears who the family has been and what it hopes to become.
8. **Units 1–4 Roles of family and institutions, community gatherings, totem system** — Family history is now the topic's printed name, and the totem system returns as the key that unlocks who belongs where in the local story.
9. **Topic 14, Unit 53 Prominent people and organisations in voluntary work, past and present** — Local history is also biography — the named helpers of the district, past and present, become the child's first gallery of lives worth remembering.
10. **Topic 14, Unit 45 Prominent people involved in voluntary work in the past and present** — The biography gallery returns at the top of primary school — past and present local helpers as the community's remembered history.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course's path — nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: history begins at home: a child's own name, family and locality are the first history there is, and the family tree, the local heritage site and the named helpers of the district are the tools that turn that home story into history.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Zimbabwe story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Zimbabwe's books themselves work, teaching the past as stories of named people and named places.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: history begins at home: a child's own name, family and locality are the first history there is, and the family tree, the local heritage site and the named helpers of the district are the tools that turn that home story into history. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
