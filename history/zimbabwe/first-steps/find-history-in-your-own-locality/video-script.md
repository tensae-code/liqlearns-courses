# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. History begins at home: a child's own name, family and locality are the first history there is, and the family tree, the local heritage site and the named helpers of the district are the tools that turn that home story into history.

Part 1 — Myself, my family, siblings and guardians. A small child learns to say her own name, age and totem, names mother, father, siblings and the guardian who keeps her safe, and finds her story starts at home.

Part 2 — Myself and my family, my friends, my school, roles of family members, family norms and values. The child's world widens in rings — family first, then friends, then school — and each ring has people with roles and shared rules that hold it together.

Part 3 — Myself, my family, my extended family, my friends, my school, my teacher. The family story grows a ring — grandparents, aunts and uncles join parents and siblings — and the child learns the wider kin who carry the family's memory.

Part 4 — Relatives, family norms and values; Relationship between the family and the community. The family is set inside the community that surrounds it, and the child learns the two hold each other up through shared norms and values.

Part 5 — Topic 1, Units 1–2 Types of families; Family tree. A child draws her family as a tree and discovers the drawing is a tool — it shows relationships, finds her origins, and captures stories and traditions before they are lost.

Part 6 — Research your local heritage site. The class project sends each child to ask parents and teachers for the heritage site nearest home, find why it matters, and present it — history begins with the place you can walk to.

Part 7 — Topic 1, Units 1, 3 Types of families; Family gatherings and aspirations. The family gathers and its gatherings become the place where the child hears who the family has been and what it hopes to become.

Part 8 — Units 1–4 Roles of family and institutions, community gatherings, totem system. Family history is now the topic's printed name, and the totem system returns as the key that unlocks who belongs where in the local story.

Part 9 — Topic 14, Unit 53 Prominent people and organisations in voluntary work, past and present. Local history is also biography — the named helpers of the district, past and present, become the child's first gallery of lives worth remembering.

Part 10 — Topic 14, Unit 45 Prominent people involved in voluntary work in the past and present. The biography gallery returns at the top of primary school — past and present local helpers as the community's remembered history.

Remember it this way: history begins at home: a child's own name, family and locality are the first history there is, and the family tree, the local heritage site and the named helpers of the district are the tools that turn that home story into history.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
