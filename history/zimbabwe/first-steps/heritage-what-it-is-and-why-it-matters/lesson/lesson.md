# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Touch one old thing near you — a pot, a basket, a photograph. Now greet an elder the respectful way, with the greeting done properly. You have just held both kinds of heritage this lesson teaches: the kind you can touch, and the kind you can only do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tangible heritage

Children handle baskets, clay pots and wooden plates and learn that special things we can touch and keep carry the family's past into the present.

## Sub lesson 2 — Intangible heritage

Children practise kneeling and clapping hands to greet an elder and learn that stories, songs and respectful greetings are heritage you cannot touch but can do and say.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 3, Unit 10 Tangible heritage

Heritage is defined as what we get from the past that matters today, and children meet it as things they can see and touch — monuments, animals, buildings and old clay pots — and learn to protect it, not litter or damage it.

## Sub lesson 4 — Topic 3, Unit 11 Intangible heritage

What cannot be touched is handed down by doing — Muchongoyo dance, ngano storytelling, the languages Shona and Ndebele, and the games pada and nhodo pass from one generation to the next.

## Sub lesson 5 — Tangible heritage

The things-you-can-touch return a year on, and children sort the kept objects of home and community into the inheritance they must look after.

## Sub lesson 6 — Intangible heritage

The things-you-do return with the things-you-can-touch, and songs, greetings and games are practised again so the doing itself does not die out.

## Sub lesson 7 — Topic 3, Unit 8 Tangible and intangible heritage

The two kinds of heritage are now taught side by side in one unit, and the child must sort the kept thing from the practised thing for herself.

## Sub lesson 8 — Topic 3, Unit 11 Family ceremonies and rituals

The family's year is marked by ceremonies from birth onward, and each ritual hands the child a piece of who the family is.

## Sub lesson 9 — Medicine and wild fruits

The inheritance is also tasted and healed with — tsangamidzi ginger for a stomach ache and wild mazhanje fruit — knowledge passed hand to hand down the generations.

## Sub lesson 10 — Topic 6, Unit 23 Indigenous types of shelter

The old ways of building a home are read as heritage — what our people built with, and how, tells who they were.

## Sub lesson 11 — Topic 3, Units 11–12 Rites of passage for boys and girls; Indigenous ways of marriage and ceremonies

Growing up and marrying are done the old way, in ceremonies that mark the turn and bind two families into one.

## Sub lesson 12 — Topic 1, Unit 4 Preservation of cultural identity

Identity can be lost — the unit turns preservation into a duty, naming what a people must deliberately keep.

## Sub lesson 13 — Topic 3, Units 11–12, 14 Courtship practices; Indigenous marriage practices; Heirship in the family

Courtship, marriage and inheritance are taught as the old law of the family — who may court, how a marriage is made, and who inherits when a parent dies.

## Sub lesson 14 — Topic 3, Unit 9 Preservation of heritage sites

The heritage arc ends in stewardship — the sites the child first coloured at ECD must now be deliberately preserved, as a named national task.

## Sub lesson 15 — Topic 3, Units 7–8 Indigenous courtship and marriage counsellors; Globalisation, courtship and marriage

The tete and sekuru — the traditional marriage counsellors — now face a globalised world, and the lesson asks what of the old way survives the meeting.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: heritage is what we get from the past that matters today, in two kinds — tangible heritage, the things we can touch and keep, and intangible heritage, the things we do and say — and both kinds survive only when each generation practises, guards and deliberately preserves them. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: heritage, tangible heritage, intangible heritage, preservation, ceremonies, rituals, rites of passage. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
