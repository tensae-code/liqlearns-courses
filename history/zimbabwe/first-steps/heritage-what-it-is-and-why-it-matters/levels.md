# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tangible heritage
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children handle baskets, clay pots and wooden plates and learn that special things we can touch and keep carry the family's past into the present. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Intangible heritage
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children practise kneeling and clapping hands to greet an elder and learn that stories, songs and respectful greetings are heritage you cannot touch but can do and say. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 3, Unit 10 Tangible heritage
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Heritage is defined as what we get from the past that matters today, and children meet it as things they can see and touch — monuments, animals, buildings and old clay pots — and learn to protect it, not litter or damage it. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Topic 3, Unit 11 Intangible heritage
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: What cannot be touched is handed down by doing — Muchongoyo dance, ngano storytelling, the languages Shona and Ndebele, and the games pada and nhodo pass from one generation to the next. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Tangible heritage
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The things-you-can-touch return a year on, and children sort the kept objects of home and community into the inheritance they must look after. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Intangible heritage
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The things-you-do return with the things-you-can-touch, and songs, greetings and games are practised again so the doing itself does not die out. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Topic 3, Unit 8 Tangible and intangible heritage
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The two kinds of heritage are now taught side by side in one unit, and the child must sort the kept thing from the practised thing for herself. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Topic 3, Unit 11 Family ceremonies and rituals
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The family's year is marked by ceremonies from birth onward, and each ritual hands the child a piece of who the family is. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Medicine and wild fruits
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The inheritance is also tasted and healed with — tsangamidzi ginger for a stomach ache and wild mazhanje fruit — knowledge passed hand to hand down the generations. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Topic 6, Unit 23 Indigenous types of shelter
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The old ways of building a home are read as heritage — what our people built with, and how, tells who they were. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 11 — Topic 3, Units 11–12 Rites of passage for boys and girls; Indigenous ways of marriage and ceremonies
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Growing up and marrying are done the old way, in ceremonies that mark the turn and bind two families into one. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 12 — Topic 1, Unit 4 Preservation of cultural identity
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Identity can be lost — the unit turns preservation into a duty, naming what a people must deliberately keep. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 13 — Topic 3, Units 11–12, 14 Courtship practices; Indigenous marriage practices; Heirship in the family
Sub lesson 13 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Courtship, marriage and inheritance are taught as the old law of the family — who may court, how a marriage is made, and who inherits when a parent dies. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 14 — Topic 3, Unit 9 Preservation of heritage sites
Sub lesson 14 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The heritage arc ends in stewardship — the sites the child first coloured at ECD must now be deliberately preserved, as a named national task. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 15 — Topic 3, Units 7–8 Indigenous courtship and marriage counsellors; Globalisation, courtship and marriage
Sub lesson 15 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The tete and sekuru — the traditional marriage counsellors — now face a globalised world, and the lesson asks what of the old way survives the meeting. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
