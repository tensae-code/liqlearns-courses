# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Picture a hunter with a bow and a stone-tipped arrow, following game across dry land, and a painter leaving the hunt on a rock wall. This lesson meets Zimbabwe's first people — and the stone tools in its title are theirs.

## The story

The first Zimbabweans hunt with bows, poisoned arrows and stone tools, move with food and water, wear animal skins, speak in clicks, and paint their lives on rock.

That is the whole of this short lesson, and it is enough: Zimbabwe's first people were the Khoisan: hunter-gatherers who hunted with bows, poisoned arrows and stone tools, moved with food and water, wore animal skins, spoke in clicks, and painted their lives on rock.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Zimbabwe's first people were the Khoisan: hunter-gatherers who hunted with bows, poisoned arrows and stone tools, moved with food and water, wore animal skins, spoke in clicks, and painted their lives on rock. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: Khoisan, stone tools, hunting, poisoned arrows, rock painting, clicks. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
