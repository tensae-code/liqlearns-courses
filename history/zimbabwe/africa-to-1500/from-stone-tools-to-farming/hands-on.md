# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Do the opening act of lesson/lesson.md again, slowly, and name each step aloud as you do it. Hold the lesson's one thing in mind as you read: Zimbabwe's first people were the Khoisan: hunter-gatherers who hunted with bows, poisoned arrows and stone tools, moved with food and water, wore animal skins, spoke in clicks, and painted their lives on rock.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw or write this lesson's story as a row of scenes — one scene for each part of the lesson, in order. Under the row, write the lesson's key words: Khoisan, stone tools, hunting, poisoned arrows, rock painting, clicks. Say the story aloud, scene by scene, as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place where the lesson's story is still alive — a name, a custom, a site, a day kept on purpose — note it: what you saw, and which part of the lesson it belongs to. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
