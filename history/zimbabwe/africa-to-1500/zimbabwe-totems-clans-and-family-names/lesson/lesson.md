# Zimbabwe: Totems, Clans and Family Names

## Do it first

Say your family name. If you know your totem, say that too. This lesson teaches the system those names belong to — a system old enough to organise marriages, respect and belonging across a whole people.

## The story

The totem a child was teased for not knowing at ECD becomes a system — clan names that tell who may marry whom, who must be respected, and where a family comes from.

That is the whole of this short lesson, and it is enough: the totem system is a clan system carried in names: a family's totem and clan name tell who may marry whom, who must be respected, and where a family comes from.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the totem system is a clan system carried in names: a family's totem and clan name tell who may marry whom, who must be respected, and where a family comes from. Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: totem, clan, family names, mutupo, respect, origins. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the totem system is a clan system carried in names: a family's totem and clan name tell who may marry whom, who must be respected, and where a family comes from.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
