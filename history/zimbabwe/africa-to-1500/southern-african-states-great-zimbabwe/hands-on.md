# Hands-on — Southern African States: Great Zimbabwe

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Do the opening act of lesson/lesson.md again, slowly, and name each step aloud as you do it. Hold the lesson's one thing in mind as you read: Great Zimbabwe is the stone city at the heart of Zimbabwe's story: capital of an 11th–15th-century kingdom that traded gold and ivory as far as Asia, built the Great Enclosure without cement, and gave the modern country its name and its bird — standing with Khami Ruins and Matobo Hills as the heritage children must learn to guard.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw or write this lesson's story as a row of scenes — one scene for each part of the lesson, in order (one for each sub lesson). Under the row, write the lesson's key words: Great Zimbabwe, Great Enclosure, stone city, gold and ivory trade, Khami Ruins, Matobo Hills, Zimbabwe bird. Say the story aloud, scene by scene, as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place where the lesson's story is still alive — a name, a custom, a site, a day kept on purpose — note it: what you saw, and which part of the lesson it belongs to. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
