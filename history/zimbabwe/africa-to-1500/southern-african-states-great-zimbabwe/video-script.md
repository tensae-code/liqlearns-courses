# Video script — Southern African States: Great Zimbabwe

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing. Great Zimbabwe is the stone city at the heart of Zimbabwe's story: capital of an 11th–15th-century kingdom that traded gold and ivory as far as Asia, built the Great Enclosure without cement, and gave the modern country its name and its bird — standing with Khami Ruins and Matobo Hills as the heritage children must learn to guard.

Part 1 — Great Zimbabwe. The great stone ruins stand in the lesson as Zimbabwe's own man-made monument, named alongside Victoria Falls and Hwange as the inheritance children must learn to guard.

Part 2 — Topic 6, Unit 22 Shelter and heritage sites. Shelter grows up into stone — children draw and build models of Great Zimbabwe's walls and towers, Khami Ruins and Matobo Hills, and write the story of a visit where a guide tells how the ancient Shona lived there.

Part 3 — Topic 2, Unit 6 Contribution of the early people to the Zimbabwean heritage. The early people's gifts are still standing — Great Zimbabwe, Khami and Chesvingo in stone — and still spoken, in the Bantu word munhu, person, that Shona keeps today.

Part 4 — Great Zimbabwe. The stone city is capital of an 11th–15th-century kingdom that traded gold and ivory as far as Asia, built the Great Enclosure without cement, and gave the modern country its name and its bird.

Part 5 — Topic 6, Unit 23 Shelter and heritage sites. The heritage-sites unit returns at Grade 5 under Shelter, tying the stone walls children model to the houses people still build.

Remember it this way: Great Zimbabwe is the stone city at the heart of Zimbabwe's story: capital of an 11th–15th-century kingdom that traded gold and ivory as far as Asia, built the Great Enclosure without cement, and gave the modern country its name and its bird — standing with Khami Ruins and Matobo Hills as the heritage children must learn to guard.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
