# Southern African States: Great Zimbabwe

## Do it first

Picture walls of fitted stone rising with no cement between them, a tower inside them, and gold travelling from this city to the sea and on to Asia. This lesson walks into Great Zimbabwe — the place the modern country is named after.

## Sub lesson 1 — Great Zimbabwe

The great stone ruins stand in the lesson as Zimbabwe's own man-made monument, named alongside Victoria Falls and Hwange as the inheritance children must learn to guard.

## Sub lesson 2 — Topic 6, Unit 22 Shelter and heritage sites

Shelter grows up into stone — children draw and build models of Great Zimbabwe's walls and towers, Khami Ruins and Matobo Hills, and write the story of a visit where a guide tells how the ancient Shona lived there.

## Sub lesson 3 — Topic 2, Unit 6 Contribution of the early people to the Zimbabwean heritage

The early people's gifts are still standing — Great Zimbabwe, Khami and Chesvingo in stone — and still spoken, in the Bantu word munhu, person, that Shona keeps today.

## Sub lesson 4 — Great Zimbabwe

The stone city is capital of an 11th–15th-century kingdom that traded gold and ivory as far as Asia, built the Great Enclosure without cement, and gave the modern country its name and its bird.

## Sub lesson 5 — Topic 6, Unit 23 Shelter and heritage sites

The heritage-sites unit returns at Grade 5 under Shelter, tying the stone walls children model to the houses people still build.
