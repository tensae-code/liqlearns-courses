# Hands-on — Zimbabwe: Early People — Khoisan and Bantu

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Do the opening act of lesson/lesson.md again, slowly, and name each step aloud as you do it. Hold the lesson's one thing in mind as you read: about 2,000 years ago Bantu newcomers walked into the Khoisan's land carrying iron-working and farming, settled in permanent villages with cattle, and became the peoples Zimbabwe is made of — Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Ndau, Ndebele, Tonga, Venda and others.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil and draw or write this lesson's story as a row of scenes — one scene for each part of the lesson, in order. Under the row, write the lesson's key words: Bantu migration, iron-working, farming, permanent villages, cattle, Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Ndau, Ndebele, Tonga, Venda. Say the story aloud, scene by scene, as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see a place where the lesson's story is still alive — a name, a custom, a site, a day kept on purpose — note it: what you saw, and which part of the lesson it belongs to. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
