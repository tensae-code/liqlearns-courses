# Papua New Guinea: Traditional Trade Routes and Networks

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Papua New Guinea: Traditional Trade Routes and Networks** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

Arrows on a map show goods flowing between peoples long before Europeans — students name the trade items and explain what the trade meant to everyone it touched. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Long before Europeans, goods flowed between Papua New Guinea's peoples along traditional trade routes — and the trade bound together everyone it touched. Give one real example: arrows on a map show goods flowing between peoples long before Europeans — students name the trade items and explain what the trade meant to everyone it touched. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
