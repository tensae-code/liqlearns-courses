# History — Papua New Guinea course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Papua New Guinea inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Papua New Guinea's books in hand are eleven English teacher guides from the Department of Education, Papua New Guinea — the Standards Based Social Science teacher guides for Grades 3–8 (2017–2019), the Junior High Social Science teacher guides for Grades 9 and 10 (2020), the Senior High History teacher guides for Grades 11 and 12 (2020), and the older Outcomes Based Education Upper Secondary History teacher guide for Grades 11–12 (2008) — all eleven COMPLETE as filed, and **every one of them a teacher guide: the filing holds no student book for Papua New Guinea, so every lesson in this course stands on teacher-guide evidence only (acceptance 0), labelled as such in every lesson's sources and in ABOUT.md's acceptance reckoning.** The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder below groups the books the way Papua New Guinea's own story divides in them: first steps at home and in the local community, the nation and deep time, arrivals, colonisation and the road to Independence, the national history in full, the Pacific Region and Asia, and the world wars era read critically. Each lesson sits at the level of the first book that teaches it. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Papua New Guinea's own Department of Education books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: History at Home and in the Local Community** — history begins at home, exactly as Papua New Guinea's Grade 3 and Grade 4 guides open it — what history is, how the past is known from spoken and written evidence, the legends and heritage a family hands down, where your own family came from, and the family tree and timeline as the child's first tools for dating the past; Grade 4 widens the same craft to the whole local community — its early settlers, its clans and tribes, an elder's lived story, and the district's significant dates plotted in order — 6 lessons.

2. **The Nation and Deep Time: First People, and a Changing Life** — the Grade 5 guide turns from the local community to the nation — evidence and its reliability, how history is recorded for the future, the nation's significant events and the named leaders who carried the country to self-government and Independence — and the Grade 6 guide goes under it all to deep time: the island of New Guinea itself forming, the first people's origins and arrival, the early settlers' fitted life of food, shelter and clothing, and then the long then-and-now study of how food, shelter, clothing, communication and technology changed — 6 lessons.

3. **Arrivals, Colonisation and the Road to Independence** — the Grade 7 guide tells history through arrivals — explorers, missionaries, miners and traders reaching the island one after another, their impacts on the people already there, one missionary's whole life read as a biography source, and problem-solving in the past through Moka and compensation — with museums and the Hiri Moale Festival keeping the heritage; the Grade 8 guide then stretches the national story full-length: colonisation and its boundaries, both World Wars as they hit PNG communities, colonial governance, self-government, Independence and Bougainville autonomy, closing with the leaders who made the nation — 8 lessons.

4. **The National History in Full: Colonisation, War, Trusteeship and Change** — the Grade 9 guide makes History a full strand of its own, built on John Waiko's histories: colonisation power by power, Australia's colonisation of Papua, the colonial administrations compared, the policies on land and labour, the Second World War in PNG, the UN trusteeship road to independence, Big Man and chieftaincy systems, culture transmission and the great exchange roads, foreign governments and change, the Christian missions, the migrants who came for work and business, the geography that fought the explorers, the disasters that changed communities, the Bougainville conflict, and a closing inquiry unit where students themselves collect, interpret and document PNG events — 11 lessons.

5. **The Pacific Region and Asia** — the Grade 10 guide repeats the Grade 9 architecture but widens the stage from PNG to the whole Pacific Region — Melanesia, Polynesia, Micronesia — and then to Asia: the events that shaped the region, colonisation region by region, governments changing over time, the leaders and groups who shaped them, dominant and minority groups, foreign governments and military intervention (RAMSI the worked case), the Sandline affair, Asia's economic rise and its conflicts, geography shaping Asia's history, and a final inquiry unit that tests historical accounts — the Rossel Island Massacre the case — for validity and reliability — 10 lessons.

6. **The World Wars Era and Critical History** — the senior guides turn fully outward and then critical: the Grade 11 History guide runs the world wars and their great leaders, Asia's decolonisation and transitions to democracy, China's rise, migration, the economic causes of conflict, and an inquiry unit ending with PNG eyewitness accounts of the war and France's nuclear tests in the Pacific; the older Grades 11–12 OBE guide runs PNG's early history, imperialism, both world wars, decolonisation and PNG as a nation in a leaner, story-first syllabus; and the Grade 12 guide turns the same architecture into critical history — sources and how religions spread, why empires fell, Christianity's reshaping of cultures, the UN and the Sustainable Development Goals judged, PNG's own constitutional National Goals, child labour, the exploitation of Papua New Guineans, the disasters that remade PNG towns, and a final inquiry on media, ideology and the powerful individuals who bend societies — 35 lessons.

**Totals: 76 lessons** (Level 1: 6 · Level 2: 6 · Level 3: 8 · Level 4: 11 · Level 5: 10 · Level 6: 35 — 163 placements stocked; all 76 lessons stand on teacher-guide evidence only, so every lesson's acceptance count is 0, as filed; 34 lessons carry sub lessons).
