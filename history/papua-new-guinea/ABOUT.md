# About the History — Papua New Guinea course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Papua New Guinea's own Department of Education books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Papua New Guinea inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the story as Papua New Guinea's own books present it (see CURRICULUM.md for the derivation): Level 1 — first steps: history at home and in the local community (6 lessons); Level 2 — the nation and deep time: first people, and a changing life (6 lessons); Level 3 — arrivals, colonisation and the road to independence (8 lessons); Level 4 — the national history in full: colonisation, war, trusteeship and change (11 lessons); Level 5 — the Pacific Region and Asia (10 lessons); Level 6 — the world wars era and critical history (35 lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The arc whole:* "First, history at home — what history is, how we know what happened in the past, the legends and heritage your family hands down, where your own family came from, and the family tree and the timeline. Then the nation and deep time — the island itself forming, the first people's origins, the early settlers' life, and how food, shelter, clothing, messages and tools changed. Then the arrivals — explorers, missionaries, miners and traders — what they did to the people already here, James Chalmers' life, and Moka, the Highlands way of settling wrongs. Then colonisation whole: the powers and their boundaries, both World Wars on PNG soil, colonial government, the UN trusteeship, self-government, Independence on 16 September 1975, Bougainville's separate road, and the leaders who built the nation — Somare, Tei Abal, Josephine Abaijah, Susan Karike. Then the stage widens — the whole Pacific Region and Asia — and last the senior books turn critical: the world wars and their great leaders, empires falling, the UN judged, PNG's own National Goals, and the disasters that remade PNG towns, Rabaul 1994 among them."
