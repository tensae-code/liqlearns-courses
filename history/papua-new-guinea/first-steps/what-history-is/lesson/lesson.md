# What History Is

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **What History Is** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — What is history?

Children look at pictures of Independence and life before it, name what already happened, and land on history as the study of past events. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why do we learn about the past?

The past is not gone — knowledge, skills, stories and legends handed down generation to generation still shape how the class lives today. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History is the study of past events — what already happened, learned from the pictures, stories and traces the past leaves behind. Give one real example: children look at pictures of Independence and life before it, name what already happened, and land on history as the study of past events. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
