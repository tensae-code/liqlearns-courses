# Who Studies Prehistory

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Who Studies Prehistory** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

Before the story, the tools — history, oral history, primary and secondary source, palaeontology, archaeology, anthropology and linguistics defined as the trades that recover a past nobody wrote down. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The past nobody wrote down is recovered by named trades — history, oral history, palaeontology, archaeology, anthropology and linguistics — each working from its own kind of evidence. Give one real example: before the story, the tools — history, oral history, primary and secondary source, palaeontology, archaeology, anthropology and linguistics defined as the trades that recover a past nobody wrote down. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
