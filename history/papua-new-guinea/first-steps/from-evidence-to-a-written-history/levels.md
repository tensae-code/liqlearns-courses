# From Evidence to a Written History — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — How do we know what happened in the past?
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The past survives two ways — spoken from person to person, and written in books, stones, accounts and objects — and children learn to tell the two apart — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional stories
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Elders' stories, chants, paintings and tattoos carry a person or a place's history before any book does, and the class learns and retells one — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — What is history?
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: A photo or artefact from long ago opens the lesson; children guess its age and use, then learn history is recorded in photographs, song and dance recordings, documents, real objects and stories — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — My story – a guest speaker
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: An elder comes to class and tells the place's history from a lived life — what changed, what was witnessed (children are also sent home to ask who saw the flag raised on Independence Day) — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — How do we know what happened in the past?
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Old photographs, films, artefacts, fossils, songs, government documents and living witnesses are sorted into "western" and "PNG evidence" — then the class asks the hard question: is this evidence… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Historical issues and events in PNG
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Students work as historians on one event — 1939 war begins in Europe, 1941 Pearl Harbour, 1942 Rabaul falls and Port Moresby is bombed, 1943–44 occupation of Lae and Salamaua, 1945 surrender —… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Interpretation and documentation of significant historical events in PNG
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The course ends by making history, not just reading it — students research early exploration, WWII or Independence from different sources and document the event as video, essay, booklet, pictorial or… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Comparison of major events that shaped the history of the Pacific Region
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Oceania holds a third of the world's languages in PNG alone and "the Pacific Way" is identity itself — students document the region's political, economic, social, religious and cultural history… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Impact of national issues or events on individuals / Outcomes of contested events and issues / Effects of national events and issues on people and the environment
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The strand closes with open inquiry — students research how one national event changed individual minds, feelings and lives, how a contested event's outcomes could be improved, and what national… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 10 — Validity and reliability of a past event or issue
Sub lesson 10 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Primary sources — diaries, letters, drawings, memoirs of witnesses — touch past lives directly, but every written history is an author's interpretation; nine ways to verify a primary source are… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 11 — People's perception of past events
Sub lesson 11 questions from the type files. Pass mark: PNG's own witnesses close the book — villagers who did not know why Australians fought Japanese, some joining each side; Arthur Duna's account of the Buna landing, when there was no time to gather… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 12 — Evidence study
Sub lesson 12 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Three primary sources, one event — identify each source's type and author, set its context, compare the viewpoints, and judge what each is worth as evidence — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 13 — Sources of historical events
Sub lesson 13 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Anything can be a source — a chronicle, a pot, even glacial ice holding a thousand-year-old sky; students use artefacts, archaeology and primary and secondary sources to document one people's culture… — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 14 — Collection and interpretation of historical data
Sub lesson 14 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Historical research defined as a critical investigation — weighing the validity of evidence about an event's phases and characteristics at one point in time, from written and oral sources alike — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
