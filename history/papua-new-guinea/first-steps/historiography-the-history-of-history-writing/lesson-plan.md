# Lesson plan — Historiography: The History of History Writing

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Historiography: The History of History Writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Nation and Deep Time: First People, and a Changing Life (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** How history is recorded changes with its tools — from firelight stories, tattoos and itang specialists to film, computers and the internet — and each age keeps, stores and spreads the past in its own way.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How do we record history for the future?** — The record itself changes — film, internet, email and storage devices now carry pictures and sound to the whole world at once, where oral and written records once travelled slowly.
2. **Construction, storage, management and dissemination of Pacific History** — Pacific history is kept in bodies and buildings — tattooing at puberty, Chuuk's itang specialists trained in history, oratory and war strategy, East Sepik artefacts stored in the Haus Tambaran, and storytelling, dance and initiation passing it parent to child.
3. **Impacts of technology on people's behaviour** — The information century rewires the keeping of history itself — computers and the internet now deconstruct, reconstruct, transmit and disseminate the past, and lectures, diaries and coursework move onto screens.
4. **Review of historical events** — The historiographic essay — a systematic review that describes, summarises and critically evaluates everything written on a topic, exposing the gaps where new research fits.
