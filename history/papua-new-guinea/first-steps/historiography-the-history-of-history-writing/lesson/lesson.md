## Sub lesson 1 — How do we record history for the future?

The record itself changes — film, internet, email and storage devices now carry pictures and sound to the whole world at once, where oral and written records once travelled slowly. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. ## Sub lesson 2 — Construction, storage, management and dissemination of Pacific History

Pacific history is kept in bodies and buildings — tattooing at puberty, Chuuk's itang specialists trained in history, oratory and war strategy, East Sepik artefacts stored in the Haus Tambaran, and storytelling, dance and initiation passing it parent to child. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Impacts of technology on people's behaviour

The information century rewires the keeping of history itself — computers and the internet now deconstruct, reconstruct, transmit and disseminate the past, and lectures, diaries and coursework move onto screens. ## Sub lesson 4 — Review of historical events
