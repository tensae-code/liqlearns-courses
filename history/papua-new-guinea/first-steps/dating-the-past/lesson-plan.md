# Lesson plan — Dating the Past

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and in the Local Community (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The past is put in order with timelines — family trees, dated events and scaled lines that let us read decades, centuries and even the 200-million-year story of the land itself.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and in the Local Community (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **People in my family / My family tree** — Parents, brothers and sisters go onto a drawn family tree and family events onto a simple timeline — the child's first act of dating the past.
2. **What was life like in the past? / Let's use a timeline** — Past life is rebuilt event by event, then the district's significant dates are plotted in order so the class can read its own age.
3. **The Island of New Guinea** — All land once joined, then split; the Australian landmass drifts north and crushes up New Guinea's mountains — shellfish fossils on Mt Wilhelm prove the peaks rose from the sea — and children draw the 200-million-year story on a scaled timeline.
4. **Constructing Timelines** — Timelines record the past as a chain — title, scale, events, arrows into past and future; children construct one step by step (example: PNG's Independence) and learn to read decades and centuries off it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
