# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Parents, brothers and sisters go onto a drawn family tree and family events onto a simple timeline — the child's first act of dating the past. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Past life is rebuilt event by event, then the district's significant dates are plotted in order so the class can read its own age. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

All land once joined, then split; the Australian landmass drifts north and crushes up New Guinea's mountains — shellfish fossils on Mt Wilhelm prove the peaks rose from the sea — and children draw the 200-million-year story on a scaled timeline. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Timelines record the past as a chain — title, scale, events, arrows into past and future; children construct one step by step (example: PNG's Independence) and learn to read decades and centuries off it. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Papua New Guinea.
