# Lesson plan — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and in the Local Community (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Heritage is what a people hands down — its legends, festivals, treasured things and ways of passing culture on — and keeping it alive is how a people's past stays part of its present.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and in the Local Community (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Traditional Warriors / Time of magicians and sorcerers / Worship of spirits / Comparing myths and legends** — Each clan remembers its great warriors, magicians and spirits in legends; children tell one, then compare the legends side by side.
2. **Museums** — A museum is where a people's important things — artefacts of artistic, historical and scientific value — are kept, studied and shown; children name PNG's museums and build a classroom museum of their own.
3. **Hiri Moale Festival** — The festival re-lives the old hiri voyages — Motu sailors took clay pots by lakatoi canoe to the Gulf and brought sago home; the trade goods, the canoes and the canoe races are still named and celebrated.
4. **Culture transmission** — With 800-plus languages, PNG's past travels by firelight — myths of how mountains, plants and animals began, migration and warfare stories, initiation rituals, songs, carvings, kundu and garamut carrying knowledge from the dead to the unborn.
5. **Cultural practices in PNG** — The old exchange roads still run — Moka, Te, Hiri, Kula and Vitiaz trades are practised today, changed by western goods (a Highlands moka finished with cartons of soft drink), beside the Goroka Show, Yam Festival and Tolai Warwargira.
6. **Transmission of culture in the Pacific Region** — Across the Pacific, culture survives the same way PNG's does — language, marriage practice, ritual and belief taught by parents and close kin, generation to generation.
7. **Sustaining culture in the Pacific** — One ocean, one inheritance — voyaging, kinship and trade bind Melanesia, Polynesia and Micronesia; cultural landscapes, monuments, song and dance carry the seafaring ancestors' skill into the present.
8. **Culture of Asia and other groups** — The past is rebuilt to be remembered — monuments, museums, war cemeteries, trenches and walls of fame, books and films like "The Vietnam War", and Asia's annual festivals keep reconstructed events and cultures alive for the young.
9. **Cultural heritage tasks** — Design a poster to preserve your own cultural heritage, then research how your parents' schooling, health and leisure differ from yours — one generation's change measured at the family table.
