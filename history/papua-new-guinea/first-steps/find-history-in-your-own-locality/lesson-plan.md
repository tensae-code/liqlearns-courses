# Lesson plan — Find History in Your Own Locality

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Find History in Your Own Locality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and in the Local Community (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History lives in your own place — your family's ancestor, your clan and tribe, your district's turning points and its remembered leaders — and you can trace, date and write it yourself.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: History at Home and in the Local Community (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Where do you come from?** — Every child traces back to a common family or clan ancestor, reads the clan's timeline, and finds their own place in the line.
2. **Clans in the local community / Tribes in the community** — Families sharing one ancestor form clans, clans sharing one culture form tribes — children name and map the original groups of their own place.
3. **Significant events in the history of the district** — The district's turning-point events are chosen, dated and shown on one timeline — a first local history written by the class itself.
4. **Province's history** — The same turning-point method comes home — each province's significant political, social, spiritual and disaster events are listed and drawn on its own timeline.
5. **Important person – province** — One named provincial figure, one contribution — children describe what their own province's leader did and why the province remembers them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
