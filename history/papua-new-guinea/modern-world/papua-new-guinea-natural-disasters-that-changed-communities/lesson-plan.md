# Lesson plan — Papua New Guinea: Natural Disasters That Changed Communities

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Papua New Guinea: Natural Disasters That Changed Communities
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The National History in Full: Colonisation, War, Trusteeship and Change (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The same sea that carried the first migrants can erase a coastline in minutes — tsunamis, earthquakes, eruptions like Rabaul in 1994, cyclones and El Niño have remade Papua New Guinean communities again and again.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Tidal waves and tsunamis** — The sea that carried the first migrants can also erase a coastline in minutes — causes and effects of tidal waves and tsunamis, with PNG's disaster history (the 1998 tsunami in the guide's sources) as the warning.
2. **El Niño in the Pacific** — Every few years the warm pool slides east — drought where rain belongs in Indonesia, Australia and PNG, floods on South America's dry coast, a weakened Indian monsoon — a climate event with a human history.
3. **Global effects of tsunami** — Waves that travel at jet speed in deep water and rear up in the shallows — destruction, death, disease, environmental ruin, cost and lifelong psychological wounds counted effect by effect.
4. **Earthquake devastation** — Three earthquakes side by side — Christchurch 2011 (185 dead), Tohoku 2011 (magnitude 9, 15,897 dead, US$360 billion), Haiti 2010 (230,000 dead) — devastation compared country by country.
5. **Volcanic eruptions and its aftermath** — Blanche Bay itself was blown out 1,400 years ago; on 19 September 1994, after 27 hours of shaking, Tavurvur and Vulcan erupt together at dawn and destroy the picturesque, historical town of Rabaul — at least seven die and a town's history is buried in ash.
6. **Cyclones and tornadoes** — One storm, three names — hurricane in the Atlantic, typhoon in the Northwest Pacific, cyclone in the South Pacific and Indian Ocean — and what each has done to the coasts it struck.
