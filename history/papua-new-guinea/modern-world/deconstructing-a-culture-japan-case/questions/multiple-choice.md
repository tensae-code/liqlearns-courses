# Deconstructing a Culture: The Japan Case — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** What is the heart of the story in “Deconstructing cultures”?
a) Japan takes itself apart as the example — kimono statues in parks, the language taught in every school, self-discipline, family bonds and group duty preserved deliberately as national culture.  b) Armies as history-makers — students investigate when soldiers, not voters, changed Asian states, and present the evidence as portfolios.  c) 2 July 1966, Mururoa atoll — France, having lost Algeria, moves its bomb tests to its remote Pacific territory; the lagoon boils, coconut palms bend, and a radioactive cloud rises over Fangataufa and Mururoa.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Japan takes itself apart as the example — kimono statues in parks, the language taught in every school, self-discipline, family bonds and group duty preserved deliberately as national culture.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2.** What is the one thing this lesson teaches?
a) A culture can be deconstructed to see how it holds together — Japan preserves its language, its kimono, self-discipline, family bonds and group duty deliberately as national culture.  b) You lose your job on Black Thursday and keep a journal; the Depression spreads worldwide, and from the wreckage the dictators rise — their ideas profiled and their propaganda in cartoons and posters dissected.  c) Armies as history-makers — students investigate when soldiers, not voters, changed Asian states, and present the evidence as portfolios.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) A culture can be deconstructed to see how it holds together — Japan preserves its language, its kimono, self-discipline, family bonds and group duty deliberately as national culture.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
