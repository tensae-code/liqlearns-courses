# Deconstructing a Culture: The Japan Case

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Deconstructing a Culture: The Japan Case** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

Japan takes itself apart as the example — kimono statues in parks, the language taught in every school, self-discipline, family bonds and group duty preserved deliberately as national culture. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A culture can be deconstructed to see how it holds together — Japan preserves its language, its kimono, self-discipline, family bonds and group duty deliberately as national culture. Give one real example: Japan takes itself apart as the example — kimono statues in parks, the language taught in every school, self-discipline, family bonds and group duty preserved deliberately as national culture. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
