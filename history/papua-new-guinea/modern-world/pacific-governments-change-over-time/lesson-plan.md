# Lesson plan — Pacific Governments Change over Time

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Pacific Governments Change over Time
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Pacific Region and Asia (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pacific governments mutate over time — traditional systems, colonial rule, then reorganised participation and independence — and elections every five years now make and unmake Pacific governments, with coups in Fiji and crises in Vanuatu and Solomon Islands as the exceptions that test the rule.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — The Pacific Region and Asia (recommended, never forced).

Give one real example of it from the lesson: governments mutate — traditional systems, colonial rule, then reorganised participation: Samoa's Legislative Council majority in 1947, French territories' autonomy in 1956, Western Samoa first to independence in 1962; elections every five years now make and unmake Pacific governments, with coups in Fiji and crises in Vanuatu and Solomon Islands as the exceptions that test the rule.
