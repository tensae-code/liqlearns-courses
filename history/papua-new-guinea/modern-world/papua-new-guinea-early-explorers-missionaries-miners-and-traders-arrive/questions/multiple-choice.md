# Papua New Guinea: Early Explorers, Missionaries, Miners and Traders Arrive — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the heart of the story in “Early Explorers / Missionaries / Miners and Traders”?
a) Four kinds of outsiders reach the island of New Guinea one after another — navigators charting coasts, missionaries carrying the word of God, then miners and traders after wealth — children name them, date them and trace their routes on the map.  b) Outside powers take the island and draw governing boundaries between them; children name and map the colonial powers, plot colonisation's events on a timeline, and judge what colonisation did to PNG and its people.  c) Fearing other powers and wanting gold and sugar labour, Australia takes Papua under the Papuan Act; Hubert Murray rules three decades — 99-year land leases, the 1907 labour indenture system, hospitals for TB and leprosy.  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Four kinds of outsiders reach the island of New Guinea one after another — navigators charting coasts, missionaries carrying the word of God, then miners and traders after wealth — children name them, date them and trace their routes on the map.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**Answer: a) Five European groups build colonial power — administrators (the Company, then Governor Hahl), gold miners who open Sudest, Misima and Wau-Bulolo, settlers like Queen Emma of Rabaul (welcomed by some, resisted by the Binandere), and mission churches whose local catechists and pastors carry schools and health centres.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
