# Video script — Papua New Guinea: Early Explorers, Missionaries, Miners and Traders Arrive

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Four kinds of outsiders reach the island of New Guinea one after another — navigators charting coasts, missionaries carrying the word of God, then miners and traders after wealth — children name them, date them and trace their routes on the map. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Five European groups build colonial power — administrators (the Company, then Governor Hahl), gold miners who open Sudest, Misima and Wau-Bulolo, settlers like Queen Emma of Rabaul (welcomed by some, resisted by the Binandere), and mission churches whose local catechists and pastors carry schools and health centres. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

The Spice Islands and a rumoured golden southern continent pull Europeans in; mapping creeps up the rivers from the coast until the 1930 patrols walk into a Highlands world Europe did not know existed. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Empire gets a face — one foreigner's life, impact and legacy reconstructed from the evidence he left behind. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Papua New Guinea.
