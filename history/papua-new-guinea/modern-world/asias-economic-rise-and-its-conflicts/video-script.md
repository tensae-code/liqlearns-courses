# Video script — Asia's Economic Rise and Its Conflicts

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Since the 1960s Asia grows richer faster than any region — East Asian extreme poverty falls from 56% to 12% — yet subnational conflicts multiply against the theory that prosperity brings peace; China's turn from export industrialisation remakes its economy and its environment, and millions migrate for work. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

The four Asian Tigers industrialise between the 1960s and 1990s; China and India's rise shifts world power from West to East and raises the fear of a clash with US dominance. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Asian governments bet on western capitalism and win — agricultural exporters become industrial powers in twenty years; Malaysia and Singapore steer with industrial policy, Deng Xiaoping blends Marx with markets as "capitalism with Chinese characteristics", and a 56%-middle-class Asia plans its next stage. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Students collect media evidence and write on the influence of China or India in the 21st century — the region's new giants weighed from PNG's side of the fence. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Papua New Guinea.
