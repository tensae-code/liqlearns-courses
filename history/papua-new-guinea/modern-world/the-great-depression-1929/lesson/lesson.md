# The Great Depression, 1929

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **The Great Depression, 1929** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

You lose your job on Black Thursday and keep a journal; the Depression spreads worldwide, and from the wreckage the dictators rise — their ideas profiled and their propaganda in cartoons and posters dissected. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: You lose your job on Black Thursday and keep a journal — the Depression spread worldwide, and from the wreckage the dictators rose, their ideas profiled and their propaganda dissected. Give one real example: you lose your job on Black Thursday and keep a journal; the Depression spreads worldwide, and from the wreckage the dictators rise — their ideas profiled and their propaganda in cartoons and posters dissected. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: compare your first map and line with the story you now know, and fix what was wrong.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: You lose your job on Black Thursday and keep a journal — the Depression spread worldwide, and from the wreckage the dictators rose, their ideas profiled and their propaganda dissected.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
