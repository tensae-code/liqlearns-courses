# Lesson plan — Papua New Guinea: Foreign Governments and Change

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Papua New Guinea: Foreign Governments and Change
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The National History in Full: Colonisation, War, Trusteeship and Change (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Foreign governments and organisations never stopped shaping Papua New Guinea and the Pacific — from colonial transformation projects to police partnerships, the Manus programme and today's aid — and their actions are judged by historical example.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The National History in Full: Colonisation, War, Trusteeship and Change (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Governments and change in PNG / Impact of foreign government actions in PNG / Governments' reactions and contributions to change and sustainability** — Colonial rulers set out to transform peoples they called backward — western schooling erodes traditional knowledge even as laws like corpse-burial protect health; English becomes the language of government, and today's governments still react to change with laws such as the 2016 Cybercrime Code.
2. **Plans and policies of change and sustainability in PNG / Foreign government intervention and change in PNG** — Intervention never ended — it changed costume: Australian police partnerships, the Manus asylum programme, WHO immunisation and aid from Australia, New Zealand, the EU and UN agencies, judged by historical example.
3. **Contributions by external organisations to the Pacific Region** — Foreign governments, interest groups, multinationals and powerful individuals create, maintain and change Pacific power systems — the class investigates who holds the region's strings.
4. **Government and change in the Pacific / Impact of foreign government actions in the Pacific Region / Impact of the changes in the Pacific Region** — Foreign money and muscle keep steering the region — UN, EU, IMF and World Bank support, and when Solomon Islands collapses into ethnic conflict, Australia and New Zealand step in to restore peace.
5. **Global development policies and agendas** — "development" itself has a history — the field built to lift previously colonised countries out of poverty becomes a world industry of classifications, policies and agendas that Asia and the Pacific must answer to.
6. **Foreign policy and aid** — PNG looks outward — the "Look North" policy, NGOs, multinationals and aid donors in the country, and two rival verdicts on aid itself: modernisation against neo-colonial dependency.
