# Video script — Papua New Guinea: Colonisation — Powers and Impacts

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Outside powers take the island and draw governing boundaries between them; children name and map the colonial powers, plot colonisation's events on a timeline, and judge what colonisation did to PNG and its people. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Portuguese de Menezes names Papua ("frizzy hair") in 1526 and de Retes names New Guinea in 1545, but the real taking comes in 1884 — Germany seizes the north-east as Kaiser-Wilhelmsland through the German New Guinea Company while Britain proclaims its protectorate over the south, MacGregor governing "by patrol". Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Gold-rush wealth in Australia seeks outlets and the colonists become imperialists themselves — economic, political and strategic motives, in PNG as in Asia and Africa, with little regard for the colonised. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Papua New Guinea.
