# Lesson plan — Papua New Guinea: Colonisation — Powers and Impacts

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Papua New Guinea: Colonisation — Powers and Impacts
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Arrivals, Colonisation and the Road to Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Outside powers took the island of New Guinea and drew governing boundaries between themselves — Germany seizing the north-east and Britain proclaiming its protectorate over the south in 1884 — driven by economic, political and strategic motives, with deep impacts on Papua New Guinea and its people.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What is Colonization? / Impacts of Colonization / Timeline of Colonization** — Outside powers take the island and draw governing boundaries between them; children name and map the colonial powers, plot colonisation's events on a timeline, and judge what colonisation did to PNG and its people.
2. **German and British colonisation of Papua and New Guinea** — Portuguese de Menezes names Papua ("frizzy hair") in 1526 and de Retes names New Guinea in 1545, but the real taking comes in 1884 — Germany seizes the north-east as Kaiser-Wilhelmsland through the German New Guinea Company while Britain proclaims its protectorate over the south, MacGregor governing "by patrol".
3. **Motives for colonisation of PNG and the Pacific** — Gold-rush wealth in Australia seeks outlets and the colonists become imperialists themselves — economic, political and strategic motives, in PNG as in Asia and Africa, with little regard for the colonised.
