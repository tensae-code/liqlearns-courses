# Papua New Guinea in the Two World Wars

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Papua New Guinea in the Two World Wars** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — World War I / World War II / Impacts of World War II

Two world wars started far away arrive in PNG — the colonisers fight each other in WWI, and in WWII whole provinces feel the fighting; children research and document the impacts on communities, individuals and groups. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Impact of World War II

Japan takes Rabaul in 1942 and makes it its South-West Pacific headquarters; ANGAU runs the unoccupied territory with PNG carriers, stretcher-bearers and interpreters; American abundance — and black soldiers with white soldiers' privileges — astonishes villagers, while bombs destroy gardens, homes and lives. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Significance of War Tracks during World War II

Three jungle tracks become lifelines — Kokoda, Black Cat and Bulldog — up near-vertical slopes through rivers, forest and mosquitoes, with PNG carriers hauling supplies and ammunition to exhausted soldiers; today trekkers walk Kokoda while the other two fall silent. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 4 — Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels

On the Owen Stanley Track, PNG carriers — "impromptu angels with the fuzzy-wuzzy hair" — carry badly wounded Australians down the mountain "as gentle as a nurse", and mothers in Australia pray for them by name; students map every battle site with dates and participants. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
