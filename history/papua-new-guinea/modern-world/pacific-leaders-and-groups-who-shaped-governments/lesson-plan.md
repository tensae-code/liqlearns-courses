# Lesson plan — Pacific Leaders and Groups Who Shaped Governments

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Pacific Leaders and Groups Who Shaped Governments
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Pacific Region and Asia (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Power in the Pacific also sits outside parliaments — Tonga's monarchy and chiefs steering a modern state, Bainimarama returning Fiji to the Pacific Islands Forum, Queen Emma's plantations and Rabuka's coups.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — The Pacific Region and Asia (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Influence of individuals and groups in the Pacific Region** — Power also sits outside parliaments — Bainimarama returning Fiji to the Pacific Islands Forum after ten years, and Tonga's monarchy, the Pacific's last, with chiefs still steering national development inside modern states.
2. **Promoters and sustainers of change in the Pacific Region** — Queen Emma Forsyth's copra plantations reshape the New Guinea Islands under German rule and the plantation economy outlives her by a century; in Fiji, Sitiveni Rabuka becomes the Pacific's first coup leader.
