## Sub lesson 1 — Influence of great leaders (just before WWI – WWII and aftermath)

One generation of giants — Hitler hunting lebensraum through Austria, Czechoslovakia and Poland, Stalin, Mussolini, Churchill, Wilson, Roosevelt, Truman and Martin Luther King — each studied in depth for how a single person bends politics, society and belief. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Folio

Ten politically significant lives from WWII to the present, at least one Papua New Guinean, profiled and judged — early life, beliefs, rise, achievements good and bad, and the legacy each left. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Influence of individuals on systems of power, authority and governance

Weber's charisma — authority legitimised by a leader's exceptional qualities, a relationship between leader and followers that challenges every settled order and is "often seen as revolutionary". Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 4 — Impact of powerful individuals and groups on society

1 September 1969 — young officers led by Gaddafi overthrow King Idris in a bloodless coup; the Revolutionary Command Council abolishes the monarchy, makes education and medical care free, and per-capita income climbs — students explain why such leaders and governments are removed from power. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns.
