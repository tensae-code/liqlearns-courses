# Lesson plan — Papua New Guinea: Colonial Governance to Self-Government

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Papua New Guinea: Colonial Governance to Self-Government
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Arrivals, Colonisation and the Road to Independence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colonial government actually ran Papua New Guinea through administrators, patrols and local leaders — Germany, Britain and then Australia each in turn — until the long transition from colonial rule toward self-government began.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Arrivals, Colonisation and the Road to Independence (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Colonial Governance / Leaders During Colonial Governance / Impacts of Colonial Governance** — How the colonial government actually ran PNG, who led inside it, what it did to the country — and how the long transition from colonial rule toward self-government began.
2. **Australia's administration in Papua and New Guinea** — Billy Hughes claims New Guinea at Paris in 1919 as Australia's fortress wall; the mandate years bring the 1929 Rabaul strike, Edie Creek and Bulolo gold, the Leahy patrols into the Highlands, then war, the 1949 union of the two territories, self-government in 1972 and Independence on 16 September 1975.
3. **Australia's postwar administration essay** — How and why New Guinea was handed to Australia, the League of Nations looking over its shoulder, and German rule compared with Australian rule from the sources.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
