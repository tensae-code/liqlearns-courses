# Video script — Papua New Guinea: Colonial Governance to Self-Government

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

How the colonial government actually ran PNG, who led inside it, what it did to the country — and how the long transition from colonial rule toward self-government began. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Billy Hughes claims New Guinea at Paris in 1919 as Australia's fortress wall; the mandate years bring the 1929 Rabaul strike, Edie Creek and Bulolo gold, the Leahy patrols into the Highlands, then war, the 1949 union of the two territories, self-government in 1972 and Independence on 16 September 1975. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

How and why New Guinea was handed to Australia, the League of Nations looking over its shoulder, and German rule compared with Australian rule from the sources. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Papua New Guinea.
