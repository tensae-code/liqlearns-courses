# Lesson plan — Pacific Colonisation: Reasons and Impacts

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Pacific Colonisation: Reasons and Impacts
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Pacific Region and Asia (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colonisation spread across the Pacific from Britain's settlement of Australia in 1788 — whalers, then competition for spice, sandalwood, pearl shell and bêche-de-mer — and the colonisers brought a whole way of life whose social, economic, political and cultural impacts are still counted.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — The Pacific Region and Asia (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Colonisation in the Pacific** — Britain settles Australia in 1788 and the invasion spreads — whalers, then competition for spice, sandalwood, pearl shell and bêche-de-mer; students analyse the social, economic, political and cultural impacts and visit Queen Emma's plantation at Rabaul.
2. **Colonizers' cultures in the Pacific** — Colonisers bring a whole way of life — administration and mission stations, schools, new clothes for "naked" natives, ancestral worship branded pagan, and a new language, Tok Pisin, born from the contact itself.
3. **Economic factors and colonization** — The coloniser's shopping list — raw materials, gold, silver, land, spices, captive markets and cheap labour — and the bill: a widening economic gap between coloniser and colony that outlives the flags.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
