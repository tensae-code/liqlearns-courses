# Pacific Colonisation: Reasons and Impacts

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Pacific Colonisation: Reasons and Impacts** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Colonisation in the Pacific

Britain settles Australia in 1788 and the invasion spreads — whalers, then competition for spice, sandalwood, pearl shell and bêche-de-mer; students analyse the social, economic, political and cultural impacts and visit Queen Emma's plantation at Rabaul. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colonizers' cultures in the Pacific

Colonisers bring a whole way of life — administration and mission stations, schools, new clothes for "naked" natives, ancestral worship branded pagan, and a new language, Tok Pisin, born from the contact itself. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Economic factors and colonization

The coloniser's shopping list — raw materials, gold, silver, land, spices, captive markets and cheap labour — and the bill: a widening economic gap between coloniser and colony that outlives the flags. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
