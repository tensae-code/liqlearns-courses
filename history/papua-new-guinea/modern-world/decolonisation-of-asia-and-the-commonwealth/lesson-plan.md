# Lesson plan — Decolonisation of Asia and the Commonwealth

**Course:** History — Papua New Guinea · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Decolonisation of Asia and the Commonwealth
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — The World Wars Era and Critical History (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** After 1945 the empires let go — the Depression starved the colonies, Japan conquered Europe's Asia and broke its prestige, the exhausted Europeans could no longer afford empire, and nationalism rose like a tide — each colony winning freedom its own way, peaceful in some, revolution or long civil war in others.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — The World Wars Era and Critical History (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Foreign influence on governance in Asia and Commonwealth countries** — 1945–1960 the empires let go, each colony its own way — peaceful and orderly in some, revolution in Indonesia and India, long civil war in French Indo-China — while the new UN and the Cold War pull the newborn states in opposite directions.
2. **Decolonisation and Independence** — WWII breaks the empires; students map Africa, Asia and the Pacific with independence dates, interrogate USA and UN involvement, and build five case studies — colonisation, the strategy that won freedom, the key people, and progress since.
3. **Factors involved in systems of power and authority globally** — Why the empires fell after 1945 — the Depression starved the colonies, Japan conquered Europe's Asia and broke its prestige, the exhausted Europeans could no longer afford empire, and nationalism rose like a tide.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
