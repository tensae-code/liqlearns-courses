# Video script — Papua New Guinea: Nation Builders — Independence Leaders

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

Grand Chief Michael Somare, Sir Maori Kiki, Vincent Eri and Sir John Guise — what each man did in the events of self-government and Independence, and what it built for the nation. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Sir Michael Somare, Dame Josephine Abaijah, and Sir Tei Abal — the Enga medical assistant with no formal schooling who reached Parliament in 1964, led the Opposition at Independence in 1975, and said moka talks were "much the same as meetings of Parliament" — each leader's contribution to independence is profiled and their home province mapped. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

The named changemakers — Somare, Julius Chan, Maori Kiki, Tei Abal, John Momis, WWII hero Yauwiga of Sepik, Kondom Agaundo, the Bully Beef Club and the Pangu Party — many already half-forgotten, their legacies still shaping the country. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

A 15-year-old Gulf Province schoolgirl, Susan Karike, beats the Australian government's proposed flag in the 1971 national competition — her bird-of-paradise flag rises over Independence Hill on 16 September 1975 beside Somare and first Governor-General Sir John Guise. Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Papua New Guinea.
