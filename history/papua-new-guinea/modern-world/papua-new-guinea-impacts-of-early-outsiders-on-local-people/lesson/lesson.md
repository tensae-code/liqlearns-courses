# Papua New Guinea: Impacts of Early Outsiders on Local People

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Papua New Guinea: Impacts of Early Outsiders on Local People** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Impacts of Missionaries on Local People / Impacts of Miners and Traders

The arrivals put PNG on the world record but changed local lives for good and ill — the class appraises what explorers, missionaries, miners and traders each did to the people already there. Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cultural influence

Cultures change by contact and diffusion; western schooling, phones and Christianity spread through PNG until people rarely call on ancestors for help — old skills are lost as new ones are learnt, faster in some places than others. Stay with the people in it and the place they stand in, and watch for the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn shapes everyone who comes after — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Responses to imperialism

First contact is role-played, then judged — how colonial powers imposed and held control, what imperialism did to indigenous societies, and how reactions differed from Africa to the Asia-Pacific. Follow who is in it, where they stand, and the moment the story turns. After that turn, life is different for the people who follow — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
