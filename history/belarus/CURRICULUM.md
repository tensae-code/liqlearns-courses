# History — Belarus course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Belarus inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in ABOUT.md and in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Belarus's own books give the ladder
their shape. The books teach the tools of history first —
dating the past, finding history in your own locality, and the
heritage a learner chooses to carry forward (Level 1). Then
the national story runs in the country's own order as its
atlases and senior book present it: the ancient lands, from
the first people at the glacier's edge to Polotsk, Turov and
Christianity (Level 2); the Commonwealth centuries, from the
Union of Lublin to the partitions (Level 3); the long
nineteenth century inside the Russian Empire, drilled as map
work the pupil draws personally (Level 4); and the Soviet and
sovereign republic, told by the senior book in three acts —
war-ruined Soviet republic, industrial Soviet republic,
sovereign state (Level 5). The ladder is a recommended path,
never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Belarus's own
History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list
and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps in History** — Belarus's books give the learner the tools first: Belarusian dates lined up against world dates, the "Our Region" lessons where pupils research their own town's part in each period, and the heritage they choose to carry forward. 3 lessons, in title folders: `first-steps/` (3 lessons).

2. **Level 2 — Ancient Belarusian Lands** — The Grade 6 atlas's paired map spreads: Belarus found on the map of Europe, the Stone Age and the ice, farming's arrival, bronze, the Indo-Europeans, the Balts whose river names stay Baltic to this day, the first Slavs and their tribal unions, Polotsk and Turov, Kievan Rus, fragmentation, and Christianity on the seam of East and West. 14 lessons, in title folders: `ancient-world/` (13 lessons), `africa-to-1500/` (1 lesson).

3. **Level 3 — The Commonwealth Centuries** — The Grade 8 atlas's story of the Grand Duchy as a European state: the Union of Lublin, the Livonian War, the Reformation and the Brest Union, Khmelnytsky's war, magnate estates and towns, two centuries of foreign policy and culture, the Northern War, the Enlightenment's answer in the Education Commission, and the partitions that end the state in 1795. 21 lessons, in title folders: `modern-world/` (21 lessons).

4. **Level 4 — In the Russian Empire: The Long Nineteenth Century** — The Grade 9 contour maps: every theme an empty outline map the pupil must fill, shade and sign — the five gubernias, Napoleon's road across Belarus in 1812, the 1830–31 uprising, canals and first factories, the abolition of serfdom, Kalinowski's uprising, the Stolypin reforms, industry, strikes and parties, the 1905–1907 revolution, the First World War front, and the Belarusian language's own map. 14 lessons, in title folders: `modern-world/` (14 lessons).

5. **Level 5 — Soviet and Sovereign Belarus** — The Grade 11 senior book's three acts: postwar recovery and repression, the Thaw, stagnation, perestroika and the road to 1991, then the sovereign republic — its constitution and first presidential election, its political life, its socially oriented market model, its multi-vector foreign policy, and its own reckoning of twenty years of sovereignty. 13 lessons, in title folders: `modern-world/` (13 lessons).

**Totals: 65 lessons** (Level 1: 3 — Level 2: 14 — Level 3: 21 — Level 4: 14 — Level 5: 13), stocking all 86 placements filed for Belarus.
