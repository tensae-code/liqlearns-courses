# About the History — Belarus course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Belarus's own
History books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Belarus inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only here and in each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is
taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came
of it. The course has **65 lessons in 5 levels**, stocking
**86 placements**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps in History** (3 lessons): dating
   the past, finding history in your own locality, and the
   heritage a learner chooses to carry forward.
2. **Level 2 — Ancient Belarusian Lands** (14 lessons): the
   land on the map of Europe, the first people, the Balts,
   the first Slavs, Polotsk and Turov, and Christianity.
3. **Level 3 — The Commonwealth Centuries** (21 lessons):
   the Grand Duchy and the Commonwealth from the Union of
   Lublin to the partitions.
4. **Level 4 — In the Russian Empire: The Long Nineteenth
   Century** (14 lessons): gubernias, 1812, uprisings,
   reforms, industry and revolution, taught as map work.
5. **Level 5 — Soviet and Sovereign Belarus** (13 lessons):
   the BSSR from postwar recovery to perestroika, and the
   sovereign Republic of Belarus.

## The way of the books

Belarus's own way, as filed book by book: the Grade 6 and
Grade 8 atlases teach history as paired map spreads — the
left page puts the same centuries on the East-European stage
with a timeline strip, the right page zooms to the Belarusian
lands; every theme opens with a symbol-picture and a short
narrative text, so the national story is always read inside
the regional one, and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania is always
read as a European state, not a province. The Grade 9 contour
maps drill the nineteenth century as map work — every theme
an empty outline map the pupil must fill, shade and sign, so
partitions, invasions, uprisings, reforms and revolutions all
become lines the pupil draws personally. The Grade 11 senior
book tells the whole senior course as the national story in
three acts — war-ruined Soviet republic, industrial Soviet
republic, sovereign state — with every section opening on a
timeline strip and a learning task, closing with document
work, a "Historical portrait" / "Cultural-historical
environment" box, and local-history ("Our Region") lessons
where pupils research their own town's part in each period.
