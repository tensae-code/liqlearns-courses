# Lesson plan — The Balts on Belarusian Lands

**Course:** History — Belarus · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** The Balts on Belarusian Lands
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient Belarusian Lands (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Baltic tribes work iron, farm the clearings and fill the land so completely that its river names stay Baltic to this day.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Ancient Belarusian Lands (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The pre-Slavic population of the territory of Belarus: the Balts** — Baltic tribes work iron, farm the clearings and fill the land so completely that its river names stay Baltic to this day.
2. **The settlement of the ancient Balts in Europe (with "How iron was obtained")** — Baltic peoples hold the south-eastern Baltic shore while bog iron is smelted in forest furnaces.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Baltic tribes work iron, farm the clearings and fill the land so completely that its river names stay Baltic to this day.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Belarus's books themselves work, telling the past as stories, maps and tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the thread of the story → go back to its turn: who was there, where it happened, and what changed. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: Baltic tribes work iron, farm the clearings and fill the land so completely that its river names stay Baltic to this day. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
