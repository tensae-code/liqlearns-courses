# Lesson plan — Christianity Comes to the Belarusian Lands

**Course:** History — Belarus · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Christianity Comes to the Belarusian Lands
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Ancient Belarusian Lands (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Bishoprics at Polotsk and Turov, St Sophia's cathedral and Euphrosyne of Polotsk's cross carry the new faith and book culture into the towns.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Ancient Belarusian Lands (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Christian church and culture of the Belarusian lands, 11th — first half of the 13th century: Christianity on the territory of Belarus** — Bishoprics at Polotsk and Turov, St Sophia's cathedral and Euphrosyne of Polotsk's cross carry the new faith and book culture into the towns.
2. **Christianity in Eastern Europe** — The faith spreads slowly against pagan resistance and splits in 1054 into Orthodox East and Catholic West, with Belarus on the seam.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Bishoprics at Polotsk and Turov, St Sophia's cathedral and Euphrosyne of Polotsk's cross carry the new faith and book culture into the towns.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Belarus's books themselves work, telling the past as stories, maps and tasks.
