# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Trade Routes That Linked the Old World** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Temas 12–15: America, Europe, Asia, Africa

America is native land and mixing of cultures, Europe a land of seasons and exchanges of knowledge, Asia the land of the Silk Road, and Africa the land of the Black Athena Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tema 21: Commerce and cultural contact

Trade carries goods and cultures together, so contact between peoples remakes both Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tema 20: Mercantilism and the Silk Road

Governments push exports and block imports while cities grow rich; on the Silk Road the emissary Zhang Qian fails his mission but opens the route whose mixing remakes Central Asia Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
