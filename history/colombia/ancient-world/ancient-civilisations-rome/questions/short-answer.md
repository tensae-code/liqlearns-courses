# Ancient Civilisations: Rome — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Tema 22: Sacred and urban architecture”?
**Answer:** Kramer describes a typical Mesopotamian city, Rome founds cities across its world, and Babylon raises the Hanging Gardens on the Euphrates Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Tema 13: Origins and consolidation of the Roman Empire” end?
**Answer:** Rome is not built in a day — seven kings, then consuls; the Punic Wars break Carthage, Hannibal finds asylum in Syria, Caesar is murdered, the Second Triumvirate follows, and Augustus reforms the state and gives Roman law Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
