# Hands-on — The Maya

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a blank page, draw a simple map of where this story happens — The Maya — and mark the place where the story turns. Write one line under the map saying what changed there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Maya and Aztecs built enormous empires that were disappearing with the Spanish arrival, leaving only direct Maya descendants; the game's questions then run the whole conquest — why Africans were captured, who backed Columbus, what the Taíno called San Salvador, and which treaty split the world Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
