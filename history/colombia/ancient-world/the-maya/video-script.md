# Video script — The Maya

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

The Maya and Aztecs built enormous empires that were disappearing with the Spanish arrival, leaving only direct Maya descendants; the game's questions then run the whole conquest — why Africans were captured, who backed Columbus, what the Taíno called San Salvador, and which treaty split the world Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
Maya communities still speak their languages and live by family customs inherited from the ancestors who built a writing system and a calendar Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Colombia.
