# About the History — Colombia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Colombia's own Ministerio de Educación Nacional books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Colombia inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the story as Colombia's own books present it (see CURRICULUM.md for the derivation): Level 1 — first steps: your own past, time and place (7 lessons); Level 2 — the deep past and Colombia's ancient peoples (11 lessons); Level 3 — colony, independence and Colombia's peoples (6 lessons); Level 4 — the ancient and medieval worlds widen (30 lessons); Level 5 — revolutions, constitutions and the nineteenth century (16 lessons); Level 6 — the twentieth century: world war, conflict and reform (16 lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The arc whole:* "First, your own story — who you are, your family tree, the road your vereda built, and time itself as a river and a train. Then the deep past: Africa, the first tools, maize, and Colombia's own ancient peoples, the Tayrona and the Muisca, beside the Maya. Then the ships: Columbus seen by a sailor and by a Taíno girl, the conquest's three routes, the slave trade and the African legacy that still sings. Then the Colony, the broken promises to the Comuneros, independence, Morillo, Angostura and Gran Colombia. Then the wider ancient and medieval worlds, the revolutions and constitutions — Colombia's rebuilt on paper from 1843 to 1991 — and last the twentieth century whole, with Colombia inside it: coffee and María Cano, the banana massacre, Gaitán and the Bogotazo, La Violencia, the Frente Nacional, the conflict and the peace talks, down to the agrarian laws and the Constitution of 1991."

## The way of the books

Colombia's own way, as filed book by book above, runs through the whole course: history begins as the child's own story — body, family, vereda and region — learned by interviewing elders and drawing timelines and family trees; Grade 4 gives the first full national arc, told from both sides (a sailor's letters and a Taíno girl's viewpoint, the chroniclers read for their interests); the secondary series teach the past sideways through the pueblos originarios of every continent, pair every medieval system with its American twin, and work source-first — the PAF source-analysis sheet, Santander against Bolívar in their own phrases, Gaitán's speeches and the conservative version of the Bogotazo letting rival sources argue. Review itself is play: snakes-and-ladders, a horse-race quiz, a time-and-space monopoly, a secret-message puzzle, a conquest board game and a who-said-what matching game are all filed as books and placed where they teach history.
