# The US and Latin America: Monroe to the Maine — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the heart of the story in “Tema 18: Relations of the United States with Latin America”?
a) Monroe's discourse warns Europe off; on 13 May 1846 Congress declares war on Mexico and takes Arizona, California, Nevada and New Mexico; and the Maine's explosion gives the excuse for war on Spain  b) The law of 15 June 1853 separates Church and State, the Liberal party splits, a constitution consolidates nine sovereign states, Núñez presides from 8 April 1880 for four periods, the Concordat of 1887 follows, and the Constitution of 1886 is reformed down to 1958 and 2005  c) Beneath the beautiful age's glitter, the great powers' rivalries tighten toward war  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) Monroe's discourse warns Europe off; on 13 May 1846 Congress declares war on Mexico and takes Arizona, California, Nevada and New Mexico; and the Maine's explosion gives the excuse for war on Spain** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) What is the heart of the story in “Tema 9: The United States and its interventionist role in Latin America and the Caribbean”?
a) The Platt Amendment's articles let Washington bind Cuba's foreign agreements, the template of early-century intervention  b) Vietnam begins as a kind of national revolution, a ruler is overthrown mid-conflict, and the United States sinks into the war  c) Players are asked how families, barrios and jobs have changed, whether Black communities endured over 300 years of slavery, and what the free enslaved people who fled to palenques in colonial times were called  d) A story that is not taught in this lesson
**Answer: a) The Platt Amendment's articles let Washington bind Cuba's foreign agreements, the template of early-century intervention** — That is what this lesson teaches.
