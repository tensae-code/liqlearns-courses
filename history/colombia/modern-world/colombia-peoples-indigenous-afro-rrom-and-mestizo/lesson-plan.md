## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Guía 11: Colombia's ethnic and cultural wealth** — Indigenous peoples, Black/Afro-Colombian, Raizal and Palenquero communities, and the Rrom (Gitano) people are presented one by one, and Colombia is then named a mestizo people born of their meeting
2. **Guía 7: Black, Afro-Colombian, Raizal and Palenquero communities** — Colombia is mestizo with African roots that persist and change; Afro-Colombian children narrate their own lives — Antonio Mow, raizal of San Andrés, and Juana Pabla Matamba, palenquera — the 1991 Constitution recognises Afro-Colombian rights, and Manuel Zapata Olivella heads the roll of the distinguished
3. **Guía 8: Colombia's Indigenous peoples** — 94 Indigenous peoples live in Colombia, many bilingual or trilingual, educated first by elders' counsel, protected in resguardos; Páez leader and senator Jesús Enrique Piñacué and the Wayúu of La Guajira carry the telling
4. **Tema 22: Cultural diversity in America** — Diversity predates the Europeans; Colombia's surviving Indigenous groups are listed by territory — Emberá, the peoples of Vaupés and more — and the Guambianos are reconstructing their own history, memory and legacies, long forgotten and ignored
5. **Módulo 3 / Guía 7: Afro-descendants and their environment** — Cirilo cannot believe a man will tell him everything he always wanted to know about his ancestors; in the Baudó the newborn's ombligo ritual binds the child to its land

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
