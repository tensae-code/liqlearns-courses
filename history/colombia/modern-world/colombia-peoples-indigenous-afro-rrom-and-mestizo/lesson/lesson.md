# Colombia's Peoples: Indigenous, Afro-Colombian, Rrom and Mestizo

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 11: Colombia's ethnic and cultural wealth

Indigenous peoples, Black/Afro-Colombian, Raizal and Palenquero communities, and the Rrom (Gitano) people are presented one by one, and Colombia is then named a mestizo people born of their meeting Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 7: Black, Afro-Colombian, Raizal and Palenquero communities

Colombia is mestizo with African roots that persist and change; Afro-Colombian children narrate their own lives — Antonio Mow, raizal of San Andrés, and Juana Pabla Matamba, palenquera — the 1991 Constitution recognises Afro-Colombian rights, and Manuel Zapata Olivella heads the roll of the distinguished Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Guía 8: Colombia's Indigenous peoples

94 Indigenous peoples live in Colombia, many bilingual or trilingual, educated first by elders' counsel, protected in resguardos; Páez leader and senator Jesús Enrique Piñacué and the Wayúu of La Guajira carry the telling Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

## Sub lesson 4 — Tema 22: Cultural diversity in America

Diversity predates the Europeans; Colombia's surviving Indigenous groups are listed by territory — Emberá, the peoples of Vaupés and more — and the Guambianos are reconstructing their own history, memory and legacies, long forgotten and ignored Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
