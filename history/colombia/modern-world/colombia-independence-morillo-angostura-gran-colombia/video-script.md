# Video script — Colombia's Independence: Morillo, Angostura and Gran Colombia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

The early acts still name Fernando VII while demanding criollo power; Pablo Morillo besieges Cartagena from 18 August 1815 in the Spanish reconquest; on 17 December 1819 the Congress of Angostura proclaims the Republic of Colombia, with Santander vice-president of Cundinamarca, Bolívar's union dream fails at the Congress of Panama, and bolivarianos and santanderistas split Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
The causes of New Granada's independence are built from sources — Captain Antonio Baraya's company guards the council houses as the movement opens, and a merchant lover of liberty asks whether Europe will let an old serpent devour the fairest part of the globe Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
Nariño's authority is disowned and war declared on Santafé; Bolívar's arrival forces Viceroy Sámano to flee; Gran Colombia forms, Quito separates in May 1830, haciendas harden social relations, and the Guerra de los Supremos follows Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Colombia.
