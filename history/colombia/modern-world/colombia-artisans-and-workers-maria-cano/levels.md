# Colombia's Artisans and Workers: María Cano — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 4: Artisans and the labour movement in Colombia
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Late-1800s artisans form Democratic Societies against free trade; river, tramway, oil and banana workers follow, their early strikes are violently repressed, and María Cano of Medellín — a woman and no worker herself — commits her reading-formed ideas to the workers' struggle — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 14: Workers unite to defend their rights
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: After a disaster no union groupings arise in Colombia until after the First World War, and politicians' incursion into the unions then blocks their free development — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tema 16: Popular movements and social reform in Colombia
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: María Cano leads from the century's start; on 12 November 1928 the great banana strike explodes and on 5 December the strikers are massacred; rural movements follow, Lleras Restrepo tries to associate the peasants, and Ley 200 de 1936 anchors the reform — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
