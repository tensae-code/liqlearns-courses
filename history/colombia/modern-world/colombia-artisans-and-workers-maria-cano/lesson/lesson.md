# Colombia's Artisans and Workers: María Cano

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Colombia's Artisans and Workers: María Cano** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 4: Artisans and the labour movement in Colombia

Late-1800s artisans form Democratic Societies against free trade; river, tramway, oil and banana workers follow, their early strikes are violently repressed, and María Cano of Medellín — a woman and no worker herself — commits her reading-formed ideas to the workers' struggle Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 14: Workers unite to defend their rights

After a disaster no union groupings arise in Colombia until after the First World War, and politicians' incursion into the unions then blocks their free development Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tema 16: Popular movements and social reform in Colombia

María Cano leads from the century's start; on 12 November 1928 the great banana strike explodes and on 5 December the strikers are massacred; rural movements follow, Lleras Restrepo tries to associate the peasants, and Ley 200 de 1936 anchors the reform Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
