## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Guía 4: Artisans and the labour movement in Colombia** — Late-1800s artisans form Democratic Societies against free trade; river, tramway, oil and banana workers follow, their early strikes are violently repressed, and María Cano of Medellín — a woman and no worker herself — commits her reading-formed ideas to the workers' struggle
2. **Guía 14: Workers unite to defend their rights** — After a disaster no union groupings arise in Colombia until after the First World War, and politicians' incursion into the unions then blocks their free development
3. **Tema 16: Popular movements and social reform in Colombia** — María Cano leads from the century's start; on 12 November 1928 the great banana strike explodes and on 5 December the strikers are massacred; rural movements follow, Lleras Restrepo tries to associate the peasants, and Ley 200 de 1936 anchors the reform
