# Video script — Colombia's Colony: Viceroyalties, Church and Comuneros

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.

After the conquest Spain rules through viceroyalties; pirates such as Morgan and Drake raid the coasts, the Church judges other faiths, and at Zipaquirá Archbishop Caballero y Góngora promises the Comuneros their demands — then the promises are broken and the movement dissolves Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
The colony organises labour and land through the mita turns, the encomienda grants and the resguardo towns, while the Church collects tithes and names parish priests Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
Carlos V defines an administration for the Indies in 1524, officials face the Juicio de Residencia, the viceroyalty traces to the Capitulaciones de Santa Fe, and Reales Audiencias and the Cabildo — Spain's medieval town council adapted — govern America Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
France holds northern North America and Antillean islands and Holland is present too; the mita obliges a quota of Indigenous workers, colonial society ranks its castes with the enslaved at the bottom — a ranking North America's states already argue over — and colonised communities rapidly learn the imposed language while keeping their own Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in Colombia.
