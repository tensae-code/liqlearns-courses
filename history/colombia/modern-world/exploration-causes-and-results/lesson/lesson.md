# Exploration: Causes and Results

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Exploration: Causes and Results** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 3C–D: New worlds and hidden worlds / Imagine you are a Taíno Indigenous girl

Learners write letters home as one of Columbus's sailors, then retell the arrival as a Taíno girl watching the foreigner's impact Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 5: The division of the world between Spanish and Portuguese / Treaty of Tordesillas

Portugal sails too, and the two crowns split the world with an imaginary north–south line at Tordesillas Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tema 23: Factors that allowed the Europeans' arrival in America

The 15th century is Europe's century of scientific change; the Catholic Monarchs agree terms with Columbus, he makes four voyages, the world is divided, and the travel chronicles show crew abuses against Indigenous women and two irreconcilable visions of the arrival Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
