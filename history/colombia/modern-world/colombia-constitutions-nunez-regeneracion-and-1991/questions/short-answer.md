# Colombia's Constitutions: Núñez, Regeneración and 1991 — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Guía 5: National identity / Núñez and the constitutions”?
**Answer:** Rafael Núñez's project of change is debated from his own discourse, the liberal Constitution of 1863 written at Rionegro is read as one of Colombians' many attempts, and learners tabulate Colombia's constitutions Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Guías 15–16: The Constitution of 1991 / The Constitutions of 1886 and 1991” end?
**Answer:** A timeline runs from 1843, when Pedro Alcántara Herrán is elected mid-civil-war, to 1990, when university students' initiative makes the National Constituent Assembly possible; the 1991 charter ends the Church–State settlement of 1886, replaces the state of siege with graduated emergencies, and is reformed 26 times in 17 years Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
