# Colombia's Peasant Movement and Coffee

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 3: The Colombian peasant movement

Peasant families work the coffee harvests together, organise, and are driven off the land; rural statistics and the question of whether their organisations still exist close the guide Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Módulo 4 / Guía 10: Migrations and conflicts of our land

Colonos travel to baldío lands, peasants answer the agrarian conflict with land invasions and the first peasant leagues, and López Pumarejo's thought is set against Laureano Gómez's discourse Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Módulo 4 / Guías 10–12: Migrations and displacements in our territory / Peasant communities in defence of their territory

By the late 1800s and early 1900s western Colombia becomes the economic centre as colonists suffer the "illness of the cold lands" and tertian fevers; today peasant women and leaders demand territoriality and an end to forced displacement Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.

## Sub lesson 4 — Tema 15: Sharpening of the coffee crisis in Colombia and the Second World War

Coffee production jumps 62% by 1933, the Federación de Cafeteros organises a fund and becomes decisive for industrial development itself Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.
