# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 10: Monuments tell stories

Monuments are introduced as objects that narrate the past, and children research a chosen monument's history and design one for their own barrio Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 6: Expressions of culture / Teach your culture

Every group makes its own culture — knowledge, values, foods and traditions that give it identity — and each child must teach theirs to others Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Guía 2: Cultural legacies that endure

Myths interpreted the world in dualities of good and evil, and the African heritage is traced forward into the Colombian context Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
