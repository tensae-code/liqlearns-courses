**Course:** History — Colombia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Guía 1: You change with time / the time train** — The child orders past, present and future on a "tren del tiempo" and sees their own body and character change as time passes
2. **Guía 10: Changes in my community through time / Housing through time** — Homes move from adobe and mud to brick and then to "intelligent" houses, so learners walk through time by walking through houses
3. **Guía 10: Jobs and dress through time** — The first groups fished, hunted and gathered, grandparents sewed and carried by hand, and new inventions create new professions; Roman dress (the toga) is the one world-history glimpse
4. **Carrera Equina review questions 4–16** — Players are asked how families, barrios and jobs have changed, whether Black communities endured over 300 years of slavery, and what the free enslaved people who fled to palenques in colonial times were called
5. **Unidad 1 / Guía 1: How is time measured?** — Humans invent the notion of time to measure duration, then hours, weeks and months from the Sun's cycles, so measuring comes before dating
6. **Guía 2: The history of the clock / Juanito's timelines** — Clocks evolve until Galileo Galilei times a pendulum's swing, and children then order important events on a línea de tiempo and draw the line of their own life
7. **Guía 6D: How do things change?** — Children ask neighbours and relatives who have lived elsewhere how things change, treating change over time and place as observable fact
8. **El monopolio del Tiempo y el Espacio, squares 4 and 31** — Players pick the oldest of three clocks and then order María's life on her timeline — first pregnant, house bought, or cows bought
9. **Tema 11: Historical periods and techniques of adaptation** — Prehistory is humanity's remotest age; with new techniques humans pass from a predatory to a productive economy and settled societies appear

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
