# Find History in Your Own Locality — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 3: How are the people of my region?
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Children compare dress, food, music and festivals across Colombia's regions and then record their own region's customs — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 5: Does your barrio have a history? / Noíl in Nabusímake
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Each barrio is said to have its own history and profile, and Noíl's Arhuaco community at Nabusímake shows an Indigenous town's own way of life — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Guía 5: The story of the road at La Portada
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Delegates Matilde and Helena negotiate with the municipality, which supplies machinery while the community works, and the vereda gets its road — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — Guía 10D: After the history of my municipality / Reporter of my history
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The child becomes a reporter, interviews an adult about the community's history and lays it out as a frieze of its games and past — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Guía 11D: The history and culture of your community
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Every community has a history, and groups pick one cultural expression, research it in books and by asking adults, and present it — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Guía 2C: Questions about the place where you live
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: With parents, grandparents or neighbours, children answer questions about their home place's past, using family memory as the source — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
