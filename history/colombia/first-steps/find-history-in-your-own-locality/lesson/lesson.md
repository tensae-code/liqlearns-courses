# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Find History in Your Own Locality** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guía 3: How are the people of my region?

Children compare dress, food, music and festivals across Colombia's regions and then record their own region's customs Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guía 5: Does your barrio have a history? / Noíl in Nabusímake

Each barrio is said to have its own history and profile, and Noíl's Arhuaco community at Nabusímake shows an Indigenous town's own way of life Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Guía 5: The story of the road at La Portada

Delegates Matilde and Helena negotiate with the municipality, which supplies machinery while the community works, and the vereda gets its road Hold the place in your mind as the story moves. The turn in the middle is the heart of it: before the turn things stand one way, after it they stand another, and the people in the story must live in the new way.
