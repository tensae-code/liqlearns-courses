# History — Colombia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Colombia inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Colombia's books in hand run from the Grade 1 Escuela Nueva fichas to the Grade 9 Secundaria Activa module — the Escuela Nueva cartillas and their board-game insertos for Grades 1–5, then the two rural and urban secondary series (Postprimaria Rural and Secundaria Activa) for Grades 6–9. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder below groups the books the way Colombia's own story divides in them: first steps in the child's own past, the deep past and Colombia's ancient peoples, colony, independence and Colombia's peoples, the ancient and medieval worlds widened, revolutions, constitutions and the nineteenth century, and the twentieth century of world war, conflict and reform. Each lesson sits at the level of the first book that teaches it. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Colombia's own Ministerio de Educación Nacional books (Escuela Nueva, Postprimaria Rural and Secundaria Activa) — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: Your Own Past, Time and Place** — the historian's craft at child height first — who am I, what a family is, how time is measured and ordered on the time train and the line of one's own life, how elders are interviewed and evidence decoded, how monuments and heritage speak, and how each barrio, vereda and region keeps its own history — closing with Colombia named as Indigenous, Afro-Colombian, Rrom and mestizo at once — 7 lessons.
2. **The Deep Past and Colombia's Ancient Peoples** — human origins in Africa, the first tools and the first Americans, maize and the first peoples of Colombia, the Tayrona and the Muisca, the Maya and Aztec worlds the game board passes, the arrival of Columbus told from a sailor's letters and a Taíno girl's viewpoint, the naming of America, the three conquest routes of Quesada, the chroniclers read for their interests, the Atlantic slave trade and the African legacy, and Colombia's ethnic diversity as the fruit of the whole arc — 11 lessons.
3. **Colony, Independence and Colombia's Peoples** — the Colony whole — viceroyalties, pirates, the Church and the broken promises to the Comuneros — then the three revolutions that frame independence, Morillo's reconquest, Angostura and Gran Colombia, Santander against Bolívar in their own words, and the people's movements that follow: peasants and coffee, artisans and workers with María Cano, the grandmothers' oral history, and Colombia's peoples told again at Grade 5 depth — Indigenous peoples in their resguardos, Afro-Colombian, Raizal and Palenquero voices, and history itself reviewed by matching speeches to speakers — 6 lessons.
4. **The Ancient and Medieval Worlds Widen** — the ancient and medieval worlds widened in the two Grade 6–7 series — original peoples of every continent yesterday and today, their technologies (the quipu), goldwork and territory struggles, Mesopotamia, China, Greece, Rome and its division, Egypt's gifts, Athens and democracy, Byzantium and Justinian, Christianity and Islam expanding, feudal Europe, medieval cities, art and Indo-Christian art, the Renaissance, and the encounter judged from America: Cortés, the divided Inca, and the chronicles' two visions — 30 lessons.
5. **Revolutions, Constitutions and the Nineteenth Century** — the revolutions and the long nineteenth century — Enlightenment read in primary fragments and traced into Colombia's constitution, the French Revolution and Napoleon, American independence, the industrial revolutions, the labour movement to the Paris Commune, imperialism and the Berlin Conference, the United States consolidated through civil war and turned on Latin America from Monroe to the Maine — and Colombia rebuilt on paper again and again: independence's second telling, Núñez, the Regeneración, the constitutions tabulated from 1843 to 1991, and 19th-century daily life, science and women — 16 lessons.
6. **The Twentieth Century: World War, Conflict and Reform** — the twentieth century whole — capitalism against socialism, 1917 twice, the First World War, the Mexican Revolution, Panama's separation, the Depression landing in Colombia, populism and dictatorships, the Second World War, the Cold War, 1989 and the USSR's dissolution — and Colombia inside every frame: modernisation and rival state ideals, the coffee crisis and the Federación de Cafeteros, the banana massacre of 1928, liberal dominance, La Violencia, the Bogotazo and Gaitán, the Frente Nacional and the FARC's birth in 1964, the internal conflict, guerrillas, paramilitaries and the peace talks, student movements, and the agrarian reform laws from Ley 200 to the 1991 Constitution — 16 lessons.

**Totals: 86 lessons** (Level 1: 7 · Level 2: 11 · Level 3: 6 · Level 4: 30 · Level 5: 16 · Level 6: 16 — 154 placements stocked; all 86 lessons stand on student books, so those lessons are taught in 1 country (Colombia); 34 lessons carry sub lessons).
