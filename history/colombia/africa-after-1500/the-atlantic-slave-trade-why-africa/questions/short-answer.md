# The Atlantic Slave Trade: Why Africa — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What happens in “Guía 7: The African legacy in America and in Colombia”?
**Answer:** Enslaved Africans bring rites, songs, dances, gods and deep knowledge; the Laws of the Indies answer denunciations of abuse, the free flee and fortify palenques, and oral tradition, trees and drums carry the legacy — filed here as the Colombia telling of the Atlantic trade (see also Book 10 Guía 6 note below) Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) How does the story in “Módulo 6 / Guía 16: Conflicts in the societies of the 5th–15th centuries” end?
**Answer:** Europe's geographic expansion over America revives the slave system to exploit its resources; at home the feudal lord's derecho de pernada abuses his serfs, Spain lives the Reconquista — 1492 is not only Columbus's year — and Caribe peoples resist in the Sierra Nevada and along the Magdalena Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
