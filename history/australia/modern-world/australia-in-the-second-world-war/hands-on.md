# Hands-on — Australia in the Second World War

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write one question a government might not want answered in wartime, and one reason a citizen might need it answered anyway. The Darwin research project lives between those two lines.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Australia's home front carries the Second World War — women's roles change under the pressure of total war — and the research project investigates why the government withheld details about the bombing of Darwin. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
