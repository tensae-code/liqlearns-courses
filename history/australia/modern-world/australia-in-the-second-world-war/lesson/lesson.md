# Australia in the Second World War

## Do it first

Write one question a government might not want answered in wartime, and one reason a citizen might need it answered anyway. The Darwin research project lives between those two lines.

Total war conscripts the kitchen as well as the trench. Australia's home front mobilised everyone: rationing and manpower controls reaching into every household, and women's roles transformed — into the services, the factories and the land army, doing work the pre-war world had reserved for men. And the home front was managed as well as mobilised: the book's research project turns detective on the government's own conduct, asking why officials withheld details about the bombing of Darwin — the scale of the raid and its losses played down while the war's danger was played up. Censorship, morale and truth: the project teaches that a democracy at war still has to answer for what it hides.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Australia's home front carries the Second World War — women's roles change under the pressure of total war — and the research project investigates why the government withheld details about the bombing of Darwin. Give one real example from your own life — the women in your own family's wartime photographs, in uniforms and overalls their mothers never wore — this lesson's home front is theirs — and name the lesson's key words: home front, women's roles, bombing of Darwin, censorship. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
