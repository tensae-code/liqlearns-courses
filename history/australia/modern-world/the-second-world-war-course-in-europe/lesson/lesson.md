# The Second World War: Course in Europe

## Do it first

On a map of Europe, shade three theatres: the Mediterranean, the East, and the skies over the cities. Then write one line on the crime the book places at the war's centre. Keep that order: theatres first, centre always in view.

The European war is the largest land war in history. The book runs its course: Germany's early conquests; the war spilling into North Africa and the Mediterranean, where Australian troops held Tobruk and fought at El Alamein; and the Eastern Front, where the German invasion of the Soviet Union opened a war of annihilation that consumed millions of soldiers and civilians. And the book is explicit about the centre: the Holocaust — the Nazi murder of Europe's Jews and other targeted peoples — is not an appendix to this war but its core crime, the purpose toward which the Nazi war machine, in the end, bent its conquests. The theatres explain how the war moved; the centre explains what it was.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The war in Europe runs through North Africa and the Mediterranean to the Eastern Front — with the Holocaust at the centre of the European war, not at its edge. Give one real example from your own life — the war memorials and unit histories in your own town, many naming Tobruk and El Alamein — Australia's war ran through this lesson's theatres — and name the lesson's key words: European war, Eastern Front, North Africa, the Holocaust. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
