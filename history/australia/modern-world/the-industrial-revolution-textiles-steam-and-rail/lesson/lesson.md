# The Industrial Revolution: Textiles, Steam and Rail

## Do it first

Write a chain of four boxes: 'better farming' → 'more hands free' → 'mills need workers' → 'towns grow'. The real revolution followed a chain like that — this lesson forges each link.

The depth study builds the revolution link by link. First, agriculture improves — new rotations, enclosures, better stock — feeding more people with fewer hands, and releasing workers the new industries will need. Then cotton leads: the textile mills, hungry for thread and cloth, pull invention after invention into being. Under it all, coal and iron: coal to fire the engines, iron to build them. And steam transport — the locomotive and the steamship — shrinks distance, dragging raw materials in and finished goods out at speeds the horse age never dreamed of. The factory age is not one invention; it is this chain, each link pulling the next into the world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Industrial Revolution runs on a chain: agriculture improves, cotton leads, and coal, iron and steam transport power the factory age. Give one real example from your own life — a train line or highway that remade your own town — steam and rail did that to a whole civilisation first — and name the lesson's key words: textiles, steam, rail, agriculture. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
