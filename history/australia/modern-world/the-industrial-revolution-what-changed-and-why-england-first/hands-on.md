# Hands-on — The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three things in your home that were made in a factory and one thing made by hand. The factory things are this lesson's children. Write one line: what had to exist before factories could?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Industrial Revolution is the making of the modern world: production, work, cities and power all change — and the book opens by asking what changed, and why it happened in England first. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
