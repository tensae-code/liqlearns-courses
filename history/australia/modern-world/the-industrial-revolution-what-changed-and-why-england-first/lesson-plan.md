## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Industrial Revolution is the making of the modern world: production, work, cities and power all change — and the book opens by asking what changed, and why it happened in England first.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — the phone in your pocket — designed in one country, made in another, shipped worldwide: the industrial world's grandchild.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
