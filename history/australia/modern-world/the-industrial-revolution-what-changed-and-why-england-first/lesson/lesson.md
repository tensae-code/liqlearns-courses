# The Industrial Revolution: What Changed and Why England First

## Do it first

List three things in your home that were made in a factory and one thing made by hand. The factory things are this lesson's children. Write one line: what had to exist before factories could?

Stage 5 opens with the event that made the modern world: the Industrial Revolution. The overview asks its two governing questions. What changed? Not one machine but everything — how goods are made, where people live, how time is ruled, who holds power, how fast the world moves. And why England first? The book sets learners hunting the combination — coal, capital, colonies, labour, invention — that lit the fire in one country before all others. Every Stage 5 story that follows, from Australia's convicts to the world wars, happens inside the world this revolution made, so the course begins by naming it whole.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Industrial Revolution is the making of the modern world: production, work, cities and power all change — and the book opens by asking what changed, and why it happened in England first. Give one real example from your own life — the phone in your pocket — designed in one country, made in another, shipped worldwide: the industrial world's grandchild — and name the lesson's key words: Industrial Revolution, England first, modern world, factories. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
