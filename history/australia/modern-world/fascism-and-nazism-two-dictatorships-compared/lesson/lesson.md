# Fascism and Nazism: Two Dictatorships Compared

## Do it first

Write two columns: 'rights taken by law' and 'rights taken by violence'. Nazism used both, in that order. Keep the columns: this lesson fills them year by year.

The Holocaust study begins with the threat before the murder. From 1933, Nazism rules by law: decree by decree, statute by statute, Germany's Jews are defined, excluded, impoverished and stripped of citizenship — persecution wearing the costume of legality. Then, on the night of 9–10 November 1938, the costume comes off: Kristallnacht, the Night of Broken Glass — synagogues burned, shops smashed, thousands arrested, in a pogrom the state organised and the public watched. The book's sequence is the warning it wants learned: genocide is a road, built in stages — law first, violence second, and each stage testing whether anyone will stop the next.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Nazism rules Germany first by law and then by pogrom — the legal destruction of rights from 1933 to 1938, and Kristallnacht on 9–10 November 1938 — on the road to genocide. Give one real example from your own life — the rights your own citizenship guarantees — this lesson shows a state dismantling the same list, legally, in five years — and name the lesson's key words: Nazism, Kristallnacht, pogrom, road to genocide. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
