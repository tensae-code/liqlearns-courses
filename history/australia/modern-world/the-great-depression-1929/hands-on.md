# Hands-on — The Great Depression, 1929

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask at home: what would your family cut first if no money came in for a month? Write the first three cuts. Millions of families in 1929 had to make that list for real, for years.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Between the world wars the Great Depression bites — work vanishes, savings die, governments flounder — and the book's research project asks what life was like inside it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
