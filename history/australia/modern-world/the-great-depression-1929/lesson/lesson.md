# The Great Depression, 1929

## Do it first

Ask at home: what would your family cut first if no money came in for a month? Write the first three cuts. Millions of families in 1929 had to make that list for real, for years.

Between the two world wars stands the Great Depression, and the overview refuses to let it stay a graph. The crash of 1929 becomes, year by year, closed factories, dead savings, farms lost, and queues of unemployed men in every industrial country — Australia, tied to the world's markets, hit as hard as any. The book's research project asks the human question directly: what was life like inside it? Learners dig into the daily evidence — the dole, the shanties, the families split to find work — and the overview's point lands: economic systems are not abstractions; when they fail, they fail at kitchen tables. That lesson hangs over the dictatorships and the second war that follow.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between the world wars the Great Depression bites — work vanishes, savings die, governments flounder — and the book's research project asks what life was like inside it. Give one real example from your own life — the budget cuts your own family would make in a hard month — the Depression made whole nations live that list — and name the lesson's key words: Great Depression, 1929, between the wars, unemployment. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
