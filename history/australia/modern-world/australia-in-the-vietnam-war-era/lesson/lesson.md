# Australia in the Vietnam War Era

## Do it first

Write one line on what a nation owes the soldiers it sends to a war it later doubts. Vietnam asked Australia that question in public, in the streets. Keep the line for the lesson's end.

The Vietnam era tests every post-war certainty. At home, the era opens in anti-communist crusade — the attempt to crush communists in Australia's own politics and unions. Then the commitment: Australian troops fighting in Vietnam beside America, conscription sending ballot-chosen twenty-year-olds to the jungle, and the nation dividing as it had not since 1917 — moratorium marches of hundreds of thousands against the war their country was fighting. The lasting impact runs from veterans' hard homecomings to a generation's distrust of government war-making. And the book's research project turns the era's lens on Maralinga, where Britain tested nuclear weapons on Aboriginal land in the 1950s with Australian consent — the era's deepest question asked of its own soil: whose safety counted, and whose did not?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Australia in the Vietnam era fights communism at home and in Vietnam, divides over the war, and lives with its lasting impact — with the research project turning to the nuclear tests at Maralinga. Give one real example from your own life — the Vietnam memorials and the moratorium marches' photographs in your town's museum — this era is within living memory at your own kitchen table — and name the lesson's key words: Vietnam War, conscription, moratorium, Maralinga. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
