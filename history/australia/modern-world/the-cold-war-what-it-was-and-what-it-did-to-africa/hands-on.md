# Hands-on — The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw two magnets facing each other with smaller countries as iron filings between them. That is the bipolar world. Write one line on where Australia filed itself, and why.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Australia lives the Cold War and what followed it — the bipolar world of two superpowers, seen from an Australian seat: alliances, fears and wars at the edges of a frozen centre. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
