# The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

## Do it first

Draw two magnets facing each other with smaller countries as iron filings between them. That is the bipolar world. Write one line on where Australia filed itself, and why.

After 1945 the world freezes into two camps — the bipolar world of the American and Soviet superpowers, armed with nuclear weapons and competing for every uncommitted nation. The overview takes Australia's seat in that world: a Western ally, anxious about communism's advance through Asia, sending troops to the Cold War's hot edges and policing the idea at home. And it looks beyond: the Cold War ends, the Soviet camp collapses, and the overview asks what followed — a world Australia still navigates. The lesson's stance is the book's own: world history is always also national history, read from the seat you sit in.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Australia lives the Cold War and what followed it — the bipolar world of two superpowers, seen from an Australian seat: alliances, fears and wars at the edges of a frozen centre. Give one real example from your own life — the alliance politics in today's news — Australia still sits where this lesson's Cold War placed it — and name the lesson's key words: Cold War, bipolar world, Australia, alliances. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
