# Video script — The Cold War: What It Was and What It Did to Africa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Australia lives the Cold War and what followed it — the bipolar world of two superpowers, seen from an Australian seat: alliances, fears and wars at the edges of a frozen centre.

After 1945 the world freezes into two camps — the bipolar world of the American and Soviet superpowers, armed with nuclear weapons and competing for every uncommitted nation. The overview takes Australia's seat in that world: a Western ally, anxious about communism's advance through Asia, sending troops to the Cold War's hot edges and policing the idea at home. And it looks beyond: the Cold War ends, the Soviet camp collapses, and the overview asks what followed — a world Australia still navigates. The lesson's stance is the book's own: world history is always also national history, read from the seat you sit in.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
