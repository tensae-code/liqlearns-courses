# Australia After 1945: War, Peace and Rights

## Do it first

Write three headings — 'War and peace', 'Rights and freedoms', 'A changing world' — and under each, one thing you already know Australia did after 1945. This overview organises the rest of the course's last level around your three headings.

One overview frames the whole post-war course. Australia after 1945 lives three stories at once: war and peace — a nation that keeps going to war in its region while seeking security in alliance; the fight for rights and freedoms — Aboriginal Australians, women, migrants and others pressing the nation's promises until they are kept; and a developing world — decolonisation remaking Asia and the Pacific on Australia's doorstep, ending the old white, British-facing certainties. Every depth study that follows in this level hangs on one of those three frames, and the overview's gift is the frame itself: post-war Australia, whole, before its parts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Australia after 1945 is framed in one overview: war and peace abroad, the fight for rights and freedoms at home, and a developing world reshaping the nation's region and its people. Give one real example from your own life — the Australia you live in — multicultural, rights-arguing, Asia-facing — was built inside this overview's frame — and name the lesson's key words: Australia after 1945, rights and freedoms, war and peace, developing world. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
