# The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results

## Do it first

Draw Australia's north coast and mark Darwin. Write one line on what changes for a country when a world war stops being 'overseas' and arrives on its own map. 1942 is that line, drawn in bombs.

In December 1941 the war jumps oceans. Japan strikes across the Pacific and Southeast Asia — Singapore falls, and with it the strategy Australia had trusted for its defence; Darwin is bombed; the war stands on Australia's doorstep for the first time since colonisation. The book follows the Pacific war's course — the desperate fighting in New Guinea, Australians among the prisoners of war suffering under Japanese captivity — to its ending in 1945, in ruined cities and atomic fire. The results reshape everything: the old empires broken in Asia, America supreme in the Pacific, and Australia awake to the truth that its safety would now be decided in its own region.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Pacific war comes to Australia's doorstep — and its prisoners of war and its ending reshape the whole region Australia lives in. Give one real example from your own life — the wartime relics and memorials of Australia's north — Darwin's harbour still keeps the wrecks this lesson explains — and name the lesson's key words: Pacific war, Australia's doorstep, prisoners of war, the ending. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
