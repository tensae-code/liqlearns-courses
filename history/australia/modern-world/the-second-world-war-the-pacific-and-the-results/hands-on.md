# Hands-on — The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw Australia's north coast and mark Darwin. Write one line on what changes for a country when a world war stops being 'overseas' and arrives on its own map. 1942 is that line, drawn in bombs.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Pacific war comes to Australia's doorstep — and its prisoners of war and its ending reshape the whole region Australia lives in. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
