# Lesson plan — Australia: Postwar Migration Experiences

**Course:** History — Australia · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Australia: Postwar Migration Experiences
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7 — Australia After 1945: Rights, Culture and a Changing Nation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Post-war migration remakes Australia: government policies and patterns, hardship and hostels, world events driving the arrivals — and the migrant contribution that changes the nation itself.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 7 — Australia After 1945: Rights, Culture and a Changing Nation (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Post-war migration remakes Australia: government policies and patterns, hardship and hostels, world events driving the arrivals — and the migrant contribution that changes the nation itself.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — the foods, names and languages of your own street — the post-war migration this lesson studies is still arriving and still cooking.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

- Thinks migration just 'happened' after the war → the book shows it governed: policies chose who could come, world events drove the leaving, and hostels and hardship waited at arrival.
- Go back to the one thing: Post-war migration remakes Australia: government policies and patterns, hardship and hostels, world events driving the arrivals — and the migrant contribution that changes the nation itself. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
