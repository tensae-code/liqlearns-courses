# Hands-on — Australia: Postwar Migration Experiences

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down the furthest place any branch of your family came from, and one thing they brought — a food, a word, a skill. Post-war Australia is millions of such lines arriving at once.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Post-war migration remakes Australia: government policies and patterns, hardship and hostels, world events driving the arrivals — and the migrant contribution that changes the nation itself. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
