# Australia: Postwar Migration Experiences

## Do it first

Write down the furthest place any branch of your family came from, and one thing they brought — a food, a word, a skill. Post-war Australia is millions of such lines arriving at once.

After 1945 Australia decides to populate or perish, and opens its gates as never before. The study follows the migration experiences whole: the government policies and patterns — assisted passages, chosen countries, the slow dismantling of the old White Australia restrictions; the world events driving the arrivals — war's displaced, then Europe's poor, then Asia's upheavals; the hardship and hostels of arrival, families in Nissen huts learning a new language and a new queue; and the migrant contribution — the work, food, faiths, languages and sheer energy that remade a British outpost into a multicultural nation. Australia's population story and its culture story are, after 1945, this story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Post-war migration remakes Australia: government policies and patterns, hardship and hostels, world events driving the arrivals — and the migrant contribution that changes the nation itself. Give one real example from your own life — the foods, names and languages of your own street — the post-war migration this lesson studies is still arriving and still cooking — and name the lesson's key words: post-war migration, migration policies, hostels, migrant contribution. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
