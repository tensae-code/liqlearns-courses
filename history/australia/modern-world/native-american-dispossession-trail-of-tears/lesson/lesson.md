# Native American Dispossession: The Trail of Tears

## Do it first

Write one line on what a government gains by moving a whole people off their land, and one line on what that people loses that can never be repaid. Hold both lines against this lesson.

Australia's book sets a mirror beside its own contact story: America's. The Lewis and Clark expedition — blogged by learners as the research project — opens the American west to a young republic hungry for land. What follows is dispossession as policy: Indigenous nations of the east, the Cherokee among them, ordered from their homelands and marched west on the routes remembered as the Trail of Tears, thousands dying on the road. The book's purpose is the comparison that frames Australia's own history: two settler nations, two first peoples, one pattern of land hunger, law and loss — and the learner who sees the pattern in America is being taught to see it at home.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: America's Indigenous peoples are dispossessed along the Trail of Tears — the comparison Australia's book sets beside its own contact history, with the Lewis and Clark expedition blogged as the research project. Give one real example from your own life — any forced removal in the news of your own lifetime — the Trail of Tears is its American ancestor — and name the lesson's key words: Trail of Tears, dispossession, Indigenous peoples, Lewis and Clark. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
