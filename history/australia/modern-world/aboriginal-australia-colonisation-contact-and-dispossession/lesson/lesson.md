# Aboriginal Australia: Colonisation, Contact and Dispossession

## Do it first

Write down one law or custom of the place you live that a newcomer might not even see. Aboriginal law was like that for the arriving Europeans — present everywhere, invisible to them. Keep that thought for the lesson.

This is the Stage 4 book's closing study, and it is Australia's own. It begins before the Europeans: Aboriginal life on country, governed by law and storied by the Dreaming — the deep account of creation, law and belonging that ties people to land. Then first contact, and the dispute that never honestly ended: Europeans claiming land as if empty, Aboriginal people resisting dispossession from the first years. The study follows the long aftermath — frontier violence, missions and reserves, the era of 'protection' under which Aboriginal people battled to survive controls over their work, movement and children — and names the whole inheritance plainly: the colonial legacy, the unfinished business every later Australian rights lesson in this course takes up.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Aboriginal Australia — its land, Dreaming and law — meets European colonisation: first contact, dispute and dispossession, and the long battle to survive under 'protection'; this is Australia's own contact story, told from its own book. Give one real example from your own life — the oldest continuous living culture on earth is this lesson's subject — and it is the country this course belongs to: Australia itself — and name the lesson's key words: Aboriginal Australia, Dreaming, dispossession, colonisation and contact. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
