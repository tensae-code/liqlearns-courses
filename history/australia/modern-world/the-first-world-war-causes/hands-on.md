# Hands-on — The First World War: Causes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write a chain of four boxes: 'rivals arm' → 'alliances bind' → 'a shot is fired' → 'everyone must fight'. Europe in 1914 was that chain, loaded. This lesson inspects each link.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The origins of the First World War are traced before Australia answers the call — alliances, empires, arms races and one assassination lighting a Europe wired to explode. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
