# The First World War: Causes

## Do it first

Write a chain of four boxes: 'rivals arm' → 'alliances bind' → 'a shot is fired' → 'everyone must fight'. Europe in 1914 was that chain, loaded. This lesson inspects each link.

Before a single Australian enlists, the book traces where the war came from. Europe in 1914 is a system built to ignite: great empires competing for colonies and trade, an arms race filling arsenals and timetables, and two alliance blocs binding every power to fight if any one of them fights. Into that system falls one assassination at Sarajevo — a spark that, in a safer Europe, might have stayed local. Instead the alliances drag nation after nation into mobilisation, and within weeks the continent is at war and its empires are calling their dominions — Australia among them — to answer. Causes, in this book, are never one thing: they are the wiring plus the spark.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The origins of the First World War are traced before Australia answers the call — alliances, empires, arms races and one assassination lighting a Europe wired to explode. Give one real example from your own life — any quarrel you have seen where friends of each side got dragged in — alliance logic at playground scale is the war's logic at world scale — and name the lesson's key words: First World War, origins, alliances, 1914. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
