# Australia: Rights and Freedoms

## Do it first

Write a timeline with five empty boxes. This lesson fills them: a declaration, a ride, a court case, a report, an apology. Keep the boxes — the order is the argument.

This is the longest road in the Australian course, and the book walks it whole. It opens at the world's table: the United Nations and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, with Australia's own Doc Evatt helping to chair and draft — rights declared universal in 1948. At home, the declaration meets its test: early Aboriginal activism organising against the protection regimes; the Stolen Generations, children removed under those regimes, whose story will wait decades for its hearing; Charles Perkins and the 1965 Freedom Ride, a bus of students exposing country-town discrimination to the nation's cameras; land rights fought for and partly won; Mabo, the court case that destroyed the fiction of empty land; Bringing Them Home, the report that forced the Stolen Generations into the open; and the Apology of 2008, the nation saying sorry in its parliament. Declaration, resistance, exposure, law, truth-telling, apology — the study teaches rights as a road built by walking it.
