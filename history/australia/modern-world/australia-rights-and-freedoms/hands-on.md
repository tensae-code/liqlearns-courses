# Hands-on — Australia: Rights and Freedoms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write a timeline with five empty boxes. This lesson fills them: a declaration, a ride, a court case, a report, an apology. Keep the boxes — the order is the argument.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From the UN and the Universal Declaration — with Australia's Doc Evatt at the table — through early Aboriginal activism and the Stolen Generations, the 1965 Freedom Ride, land rights and Mabo, to Bringing Them Home and the Apology: Australia's rights story told whole. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
