# Hands-on — Effects of Industrialisation: Cities, Classes, Empires

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name the biggest change a new factory, mine or railway would bring to a small town near you. Write it down — then multiply it by a whole country. That is this lesson's scale.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Industrialisation changes ways of life everywhere it touches — new cities, new classes, a global reach — and the book follows its impact all the way to Australia and across the globe. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
