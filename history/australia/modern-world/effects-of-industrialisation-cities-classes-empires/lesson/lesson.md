# Effects of Industrialisation: Cities, Classes, Empires

## Do it first

Name the biggest change a new factory, mine or railway would bring to a small town near you. Write it down — then multiply it by a whole country. That is this lesson's scale.

A revolution is measured by what it does to ordinary days. This study follows industrialisation's effects: ways of life transformed — work ruled by the clock instead of the season, families crowded into new industrial cities, new classes facing each other across the factory gate. Then the impact travels. It reaches Australia — a colony remade as a market, a supplier and a copy of the industrial world's ways — and spreads across the globe, binding distant peoples into one system of mines, plantations, ports and mills. The study's lesson is the one Stage 5 keeps teaching: no modern event stays home; everything lands somewhere else too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Industrialisation changes ways of life everywhere it touches — new cities, new classes, a global reach — and the book follows its impact all the way to Australia and across the globe. Give one real example from your own life — your own suburb or town, planned around work and transport — industrialisation's city-shape is the one you live in — and name the lesson's key words: industrialisation, changing ways of life, global impact, Australia. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
