# China and the World: Qing to Modern

## Do it first

Write one line on what a country keeps when its old order falls, and one line on what it must invent. China's modern story is the working-out of those two lines.

The China study closes on its turn. Out of the fallen Qing order, China emerges into the modern world — not as the Middle Kingdom receiving tribute, but as one nation among nations, forced to learn the modern world's ways while defending its own civilisation's core. The book gives this emergence its own short lesson because it is the hinge of the region Australia lives in: every later story in this course — the Pacific war, the Cold War, Vietnam, migration — has modern China standing in it. The dynastic wheel has stopped; a new story, still running, has begun.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: China emerges from empire into the modern world — the turn the whole dynastic story ends in, and the beginning of China's modern place in the world. Give one real example from your own life — goods and students and news moving between your country and China today — the modern relationship this lesson's turn began — and name the lesson's key words: China, Qing, modern world, emergence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
