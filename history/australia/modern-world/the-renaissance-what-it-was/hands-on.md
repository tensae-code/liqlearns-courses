# Hands-on — The Renaissance: What It Was

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one thing people in your town once believed that almost nobody believes now, and one new idea that replaced it. Write both down. Whole ages turn on such swaps — this lesson catches one turning.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: At the close of the medieval age, new ideas about the world emerge — the turn toward the Renaissance: old learning reborn, human beings and their world looked at with fresh eyes. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
