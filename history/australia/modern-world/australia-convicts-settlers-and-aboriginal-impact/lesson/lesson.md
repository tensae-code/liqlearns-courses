# Australia: Convicts, Settlers and the Aboriginal Impact

## Do it first

Write two sentences: one beginning 'The convicts gained…' and one beginning 'The Aboriginal people lost…'. The whole tragedy and making of colonial Australia stands between those sentences.

Australia's modern founding was a movement of peoples with two faces. On one face: the convicts — Britain's transported prisoners, men and women shipped to the far side of the world — and the free settlers who followed, together building the colonies' roads, farms and towns; the book's research project follows the female convicts, the most vulnerable and least remembered of the founders. On the other face: devastation. European settlement — its diseases, its fences, its hunger for land — crashed into Aboriginal societies that had held the continent for tens of thousands of years. The study refuses to separate the faces: the building and the devastation are one event, and Australia's history begins honestly only when both are told together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Convicts and free settlers build the Australian colonies — the research project follows the female convicts — while European settlement devastates the Aboriginal people who were already there. Give one real example from your own life — the towns, farms and roads of Australia today — built in the convict and settler generations this lesson studies — and name the lesson's key words: convicts, free settlers, female convicts, Aboriginal impact. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
