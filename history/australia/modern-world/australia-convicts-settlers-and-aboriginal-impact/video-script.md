# Video script — Australia: Convicts, Settlers and the Aboriginal Impact

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Convicts and free settlers build the Australian colonies — the research project follows the female convicts — while European settlement devastates the Aboriginal people who were already there.

Australia's modern founding was a movement of peoples with two faces. On one face: the convicts — Britain's transported prisoners, men and women shipped to the far side of the world — and the free settlers who followed, together building the colonies' roads, farms and towns; the book's research project follows the female convicts, the most vulnerable and least remembered of the founders. On the other face: devastation. European settlement — its diseases, its fences, its hunger for land — crashed into Aboriginal societies that had held the continent for tens of thousands of years. The study refuses to separate the faces: the building and the devastation are one event, and Australia's history begins honestly only when both are told together.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
