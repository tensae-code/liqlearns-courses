# Nationalism: The Feeling That Made Nation-States

## Do it first

Write down one song, flag or story that makes people in your country feel 'we are one people'. That feeling has a name and a history — this lesson gives both.

Among the progressive ideas and movements of the modern century, the book singles out nationalism: the feeling that a people — bound by language, history and culture — is a nation, and that a nation deserves a state of its own. It sounds obvious now; it was revolutionary then, in a world of empires and kings ruling mixed peoples by inheritance. Where the feeling caught, maps were redrawn: peoples united into new nations, empires cracked along national seams, and eventually colonised peoples — Australians forming their own nation among them — turned the idea against the empires that taught it. A feeling, this lesson insists, can be a historical force as real as steam.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Nationalism — the feeling that a people is a nation and deserves its own state — is one of the century's progressive ideas, and it remade the map of the world. Give one real example from your own life — the anthem sung at your school or stadium — nationalism's feeling, performed by you — and name the lesson's key words: nationalism, nation-states, progressive ideas, the nation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
