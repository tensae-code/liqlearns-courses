# Australia: Popular Culture After 1945

## Do it first

List the last three songs, shows or games you loved and write where each came from. If the map surprises you, you have found this lesson's subject: culture travels, and it changes the places it lands.

After 1945 a new kind of invasion arrives by antenna and record player. The study follows post-war popular culture: the flood of American and British influence — films, music, styles, slang — and television's arrival in 1956, which put the world in the lounge room and reorganised family evenings around it. Sport carries national feeling to mass audiences; music and film give the young a culture of their own, to their parents' alarm. The book's point is historical, not nostalgic: beliefs and values — about America, about Britain, about authority, about being Australian — were remade by what people watched, heard and cheered. Culture is not the decoration on history; here it is one of its engines.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Post-war popular culture — American and British influence, television's arrival, sport, music and film — changes what Australians believe and value: a nation's culture remade in its lounge rooms. Give one real example from your own life — your own playlist and screen time — the post-war culture war over American influence is now fought in every pocket — and name the lesson's key words: popular culture, television, sport, music and film. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
