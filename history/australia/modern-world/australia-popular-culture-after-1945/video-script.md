# Video script — Australia: Popular Culture After 1945

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Post-war popular culture — American and British influence, television's arrival, sport, music and film — changes what Australians believe and value: a nation's culture remade in its lounge rooms.

After 1945 a new kind of invasion arrives by antenna and record player. The study follows post-war popular culture: the flood of American and British influence — films, music, styles, slang — and television's arrival in 1956, which put the world in the lounge room and reorganised family evenings around it. Sport carries national feeling to mass audiences; music and film give the young a culture of their own, to their parents' alarm. The book's point is historical, not nostalgic: beliefs and values — about America, about Britain, about authority, about being Australian — were remade by what people watched, heard and cheered. Culture is not the decoration on history; here it is one of its engines.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
