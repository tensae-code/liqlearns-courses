# Hands-on — Australia: Making a Nation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write your own one-line definition of 'a nation'. Then write whose experiences a nation's story must include to be honest. This lesson tests both lines.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Australia makes itself a nation: settlement expands, living and working conditions are fought over, self-government and democracy are won — and Indigenous and non-European experiences, closing with Indigenous and women's rights, stand inside the making, not outside it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
