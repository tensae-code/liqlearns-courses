# Video script — Australia: Making a Nation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Australia makes itself a nation: settlement expands, living and working conditions are fought over, self-government and democracy are won — and Indigenous and non-European experiences, closing with Indigenous and women's rights, stand inside the making, not outside it.

The colonies become a nation by work, not by proclamation alone. Settlement expands across the continent; living and working conditions are fought over — in shearers' strikes, union halls and parliaments — until self-government and democracy are won from Britain piece by piece, and social legislation begins building the new Australian nation's famous 'fair go': protections and pensions that made Australia a social laboratory watched by the world. The study's arc runs from dirt floors to a democratic federation, and its engine is ordinary people organising for a fairer share of the country they were building.

The nation-making study turns its lens to the people the first telling left at the edges. Indigenous contact experiences — what colonisation looked like from Aboriginal country — and non-European migrants' experiences — the Chinese on the goldfields and every community that built Australia without being British — stand inside the making of the nation, as evidence, not decoration. The study closes where honesty points: with rights — Indigenous rights still withheld while democracy grew, and women's rights fought for and won, Australia among the first nations to give women both the vote and the right to sit in parliament. A nation, the book insists, is judged by who its story includes.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
