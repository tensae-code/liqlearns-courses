# Lesson plan — Australia: Making a Nation

**Course:** History — Australia · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Australia: Making a Nation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Making of the Modern World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Australia makes itself a nation: settlement expands, living and working conditions are fought over, self-government and democracy are won — and Indigenous and non-European experiences, closing with Indigenous and women's rights, stand inside the making, not outside it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — The Making of the Modern World (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Making a nation** — Settlement expands, living and working conditions are fought over, self-government and democracy are won, and social legislation builds the new Australian nation.
2. **Indigenous contact and non-European experiences** — Indigenous contact experiences and non-European migrants' experiences stand inside nation-making, closing with Indigenous and women's rights.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Australia makes itself a nation: settlement expands, living and working conditions are fought over, self-government and democracy are won — and Indigenous and non-European experiences, closing with Indigenous and women's rights, stand inside the making, not outside it.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — the vote, the wage rules and the parliament of Australia today — won in the fights this lesson studies.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.
