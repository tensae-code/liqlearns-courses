# Hands-on — Australia in the First World War

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write two lines: one on what a young nation proves by fighting bravely far from home, and one on what it argues about at home while the fighting lasts. Australia lived both lines at once.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Australia's First World War runs from Gallipoli and the Western Front to the home front — prisoners of war, the conscription referendums that divide the nation, commemoration, and the making of the Anzac legend. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
