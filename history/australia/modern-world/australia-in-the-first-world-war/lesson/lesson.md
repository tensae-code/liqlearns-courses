# Australia in the First World War

## Do it first

Write two lines: one on what a young nation proves by fighting bravely far from home, and one on what it argues about at home while the fighting lasts. Australia lived both lines at once.

Australia's war begins at Gallipoli in 1915 — a failed landing on a Turkish shore that became, in defeat, the young nation's founding story — and grinds on to the Western Front's slaughter, where Australian divisions fought and died in the mud of France and Belgium. The book holds the whole war: the prisoners of war behind the wire; the home front of work, grief and fundraising; and the conscription referendums of 1916 and 1917, when the nation twice voted No to forcing men to fight overseas and split — bitterly, along lines of class, faith and birthplace — in the arguing. Out of all of it comes commemoration and the Anzac legend: the story Australia tells itself about courage, mateship and sacrifice, made as much at home as at the front.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Australia's First World War runs from Gallipoli and the Western Front to the home front — prisoners of war, the conscription referendums that divide the nation, commemoration, and the making of the Anzac legend. Give one real example from your own life — the dawn service and the two-up and the memorial in your own town each Anzac Day — this lesson is where that commemoration was made — and name the lesson's key words: Gallipoli, Western Front, conscription referendums, Anzac legend. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
