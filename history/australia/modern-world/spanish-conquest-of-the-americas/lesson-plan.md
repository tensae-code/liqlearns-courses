## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: The Spanish journey across the Atlantic ends in the conquest of the Aztec empire — gold and glory the lure, La Malinche at the story's centre, and colonial control the lasting result.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — any story you know where a small arriving force defeats a great local power — alliances and disease usually stand behind it, as here.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

- Thinks a few hundred Spaniards conquered an empire by bravery alone → the book's own cast answers: gold and glory drove them, La Malinche's tongues guided them, and America's peoples were divided among themselves.
- Go back to the one thing: The Spanish journey across the Atlantic ends in the conquest of the Aztec empire — gold and glory the lure, La Malinche at the story's centre, and colonial control the lasting result. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
