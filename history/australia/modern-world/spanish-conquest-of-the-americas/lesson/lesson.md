# The Spanish Conquest of the Americas

## Do it first

Write two columns: 'What the Spanish wanted' and 'What the Aztecs had'. Fill two lines each. The conquest happens where the columns collide — keep them for the lesson.

The Spanish journey begins in hunger for gold and glory — and in a world remade by the Renaissance turn. The book follows the quest across the Atlantic to the conquest of the Aztec empire: a small Spanish force, thousands of Indigenous allies who had their own scores with Aztec rule, European weapons and horses, and disease doing silent work no army could. At the story's centre stands La Malinche — the enslaved woman whose languages made negotiation and betrayal both possible, whom the book asks us to weigh as 'mother of Mexico?' And after the fall of Tenochtitlan comes the lasting part: colonial control, Spain ruling America's peoples and wealth for three centuries. Conquest is an event; colonisation is the system it built.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Spanish journey across the Atlantic ends in the conquest of the Aztec empire — gold and glory the lure, La Malinche at the story's centre, and colonial control the lasting result. Give one real example from your own life — any story you know where a small arriving force defeats a great local power — alliances and disease usually stand behind it, as here — and name the lesson's key words: Spanish Conquest, Aztec empire, La Malinche, gold and glory. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
