# Capitalism: Private Ownership, Factories, New Classes

## Do it first

Write down who owns the place where someone in your family works, who works there, and who decides. Three different answers — that triangle is capitalism's shape. This lesson names it.

Ideas move the modern world as surely as machines. The overview sets out the age's great economic idea — capitalism: property privately owned, production organised in factories, society re-sorted into new classes of owners and wage-workers — and, in the same breath, the rival ideas it provoked. Every grievance the factory system created called an answer into being, and the answers — reform, union, socialism, revolution — fight through the rest of the Stage 5 book, in Australia as much as in Europe. The learner is being given the course's toolkit: name the system, and you can name its critics.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Capitalism remakes the modern world — private ownership, factories and new classes — and rival ideas rise to answer it: the economic, social and political ideas of the age are one story. Give one real example from your own life — the triangle in your own family's work — owner, worker, decider — capitalism's shape at kitchen-table scale — and name the lesson's key words: capitalism, private ownership, factories, new classes. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
