# The Second World War: Causes

## Do it first

Write three boxes: 'a peace that humiliates' → 'an economy that collapses' → 'a dictator who promises revenge'. Europe between the wars filled every box. This lesson checks each one.

The second war's origins lie in the first war's ending. The book traces them in the Europe of the dictators: a peace settlement that humiliated Germany without disarming its resentments; the Great Depression, which emptied the democracies' confidence and filled the dictators' rallies; and the dictators themselves — Hitler above all — who turned grievance into programme: rearm, expand, avenge. Step by tolerated step — each aggression met with protest and no resistance — the road to 1939 is built. As with 1914, the book's method is the lesson: wars have origins, origins have ingredients, and ingredients can be named before the explosion, not only after it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The origins of the Second World War are traced in the Europe of the dictators — the peace that failed, the Depression's desperation, and the dictators who turned both into a road to war. Give one real example from your own life — the dictators' rallies you have seen in film — the book asks what made millions cheer, and the answer is in the peace and the Depression before them — and name the lesson's key words: Second World War, origins, dictators, Europe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
