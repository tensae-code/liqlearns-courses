# Progressive Ideas: Chartism to Socialism

## Do it first

Write a three-line petition: one change you want, one reason, one signature line. The Chartists gathered millions of signatures on petitions like yours — this lesson follows what such paper can and cannot do.

The progressive-ideas study widens into the century's whole argument. The Chartists petition — between 1836 and 1860 millions sign for the people's charter: votes for working men, fair parliaments — and are refused, and refuse to disappear. Darwin's ideas shake belief itself, recasting humanity's place in nature and arming every later argument about progress. And against capitalism and imperialism rise the systematic answers: egalitarianism, demanding real equality, and socialism, arguing the factories and land should serve all, not owners alone. The book teaches these as one shelf of weapons: every movement in the Australian lessons ahead — unions, suffrage, labour parties — reaches for something forged here.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The century answers capitalism with ideas: Chartists petition for the vote, Darwin's ideas shake belief, and egalitarians and socialists build the case against capitalism and imperialism. Give one real example from your own life — the vote every adult in your country holds — the Chartists petitioned for exactly that, and were refused for years — and name the lesson's key words: Chartism, Darwinism, egalitarianism, socialism. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
