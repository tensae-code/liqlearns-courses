# The US Civil Rights Movement

## Do it first

Write one line on what a protest on the other side of the world can give a protest at home. Australia's freedom riders answered that question with a bus: this lesson shows where they got it.

Inside the rights-and-freedoms study, the book turns to America. The US civil rights movement — the long fight against segregation, from buses and lunch counters to the great marches and the laws they forced — is studied in its own right, and then in its Australian reflection. Australia's activists watched it: on the new television, in the papers, in the shared language of freedom rides and sit-ins deliberately borrowed. Charles Perkins said openly that the American movement taught the Freedom Ride its tactics and its name. Mirror and spark, the book calls it: a mirror in which Australia saw its own discrimination plainly, and a spark that helped light its fire.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: America's civil rights movement is studied as the mirror and spark for Australia's own freedom struggles — a movement watched on Australian televisions and answered on Australian roads. Give one real example from your own life — the protest footage in today's newsfeeds, watched worldwide within hours — the Freedom Ride generation had the same idea with television and newspapers — and name the lesson's key words: US civil rights movement, mirror and spark, freedom struggles, segregation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
