# Video script — Who Studies Prehistory

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Historians are not alone with the deep past: anthropologists and archaeologists read human remains and old things, and dating methods like dendrochronology and radiocarbon tell how old they are.

Some pasts are too old for any writing, so other experts take the case. Anthropologists study human beings and their ways; archaeologists dig up and read the things people left behind. To give those finds their ages, science helps: dendrochronology counts the rings of old wood, and radiocarbon dating measures the slow decay inside once-living things. The book tests the team on two famous bodies. Tutankhamun, the boy pharaoh, carries injuries that experts still argue over. And Ötzi the Iceman, found frozen in the Ötztal Alps in 1991 after more than five thousand years, arrived with his clothes, his tools and his last meals still readable — a whole life, recovered by the team this lesson names.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
