# Hands-on — Who Studies Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Look at your own hand, then at the oldest object in your home. Write down: who could study that object to learn about the people who used it, and how could anyone tell how old it is?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Historians are not alone with the deep past: anthropologists and archaeologists read human remains and old things, and dating methods like dendrochronology and radiocarbon tell how old they are. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
