# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — Australia · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** No history can be written until sources are analysed and used — and for ancient Australia, the sources have their own special section and their own special care.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: No history can be written until sources are analysed and used — and for ancient Australia, the sources have their own special section and their own special care.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — two family photographs of the same day that disagree — the lesson's source work, done at your own kitchen table.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as the books themselves work, telling the past as a story.

- Go back to the one thing: No history can be written until sources are analysed and used — and for ancient Australia, the sources have their own special section and their own special care. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
