# From Evidence to a Written History

## Do it first

Find two things at home that tell about the same past event — a photo and a story someone tells, or two messages about one day. Write what each one shows, and what only the two together can prove.

Between the clue and the written history stands the source work. A source is anything the past left behind — an object, a picture, a text, a place — and the historian's job is to analyse it: who made it, when, why, and what it can honestly be made to say. Only sources that survive that questioning can be used to build a history. The book gives ancient Australia its own special section in this work, because Australia's deepest past left no writing: its sources are stone tools, camp sites, rock art and the knowledge Aboriginal people themselves carry — sources that must be read with particular care and respect before any history of ancient Australia can be written at all.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: No history can be written until sources are analysed and used — and for ancient Australia, the sources have their own special section and their own special care. Give one real example from your own life — two family photographs of the same day that disagree — the lesson's source work, done at your own kitchen table — and name the lesson's key words: sources, evidence, ancient Australia, analysis. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
