# Hands-on — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one old thing in your town — a building, a tree, a monument — that people would fight to save. Write down what would be lost if it vanished, and who should protect it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The past is worth conserving: when rising water threatened Ramesses II's temples at Abu Simbel, the world cut them apart and moved them to safety — heritage is the past we choose to keep. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
