# Hands-on — What History Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one question about the past you would love to answer — who built something near you, or why your town has its name. Write the question down, then write two clues you could look for to answer it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: History is detective work on the past: historians investigate significant questions from clues, forming hypotheses and testing them against evidence. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
