# What History Is

## Do it first

Think of one question about the past you would love to answer — who built something near you, or why your town has its name. Write the question down, then write two clues you could look for to answer it.

Every history in this course begins the way this book begins: with a detective. A historian does not start with answers. A historian starts with a significant question — a question that matters — and hunts for clues. Each clue is weighed: where did it come from, can it be trusted, what does it really show? From the clues the historian forms a hypothesis, a best-guess answer, and then tests that guess against more evidence, keeping it only if the evidence holds it up. That is the craft underneath every story in this course: question, clues, hypothesis, test.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History is detective work on the past: historians investigate significant questions from clues, forming hypotheses and testing them against evidence. Give one real example from your own life — a question about your own street or town — its name, its oldest building — investigated from clues, exactly as this lesson teaches — and name the lesson's key words: history, historian, evidence, hypothesis. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
