# Hands-on — Japan Under the Shoguns

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write two columns: 'What a country gains by opening to the world' and 'What it risks'. Fill two lines in each. Japan's shoguns weighed exactly these columns for over two hundred years.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Japan passes from early medieval times to the Tokugawa shogunate — Shinto and Buddhism side by side, castles and forests, isolation under threat, and then modernisation: a country that chose its own road into the modern world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
