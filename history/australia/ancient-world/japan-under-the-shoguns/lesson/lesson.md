# Japan Under the Shoguns

## Do it first

Write two columns: 'What a country gains by opening to the world' and 'What it risks'. Fill two lines in each. Japan's shoguns weighed exactly these columns for over two hundred years.

Japan's medieval story runs on its own road. From early medieval times the islands develop their own order — Shinto and Buddhism standing side by side in the people's faith, warriors rising as real rulers while emperors reign above them. Under the Tokugawa shogunate the order hardens: castles crown the towns, the forests are managed as treasure, and Japan chooses isolation, closing its doors against a threatening outside world for more than two centuries. Then the doors are forced, and the story turns again — to modernisation, Japan remaking itself at speed. The research eye of the book stays on the choice: a country that decided for itself when to shut the world out and when to seize it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Japan passes from early medieval times to the Tokugawa shogunate — Shinto and Buddhism side by side, castles and forests, isolation under threat, and then modernisation: a country that chose its own road into the modern world. Give one real example from your own life — any rule in your school that keeps outsiders out to keep things safe — the shoguns ran that rule for a whole country — and name the lesson's key words: Japan, shoguns, Tokugawa, isolation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
