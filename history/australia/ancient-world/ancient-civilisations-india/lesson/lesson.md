# Ancient Civilisations: India

## Do it first

Find a river on a map of India — the Indus or the Ganges. Write one line on what a river valley gives a first civilisation, remembering Egypt. Then write one idea — about life, duty or the soul — that you know came out of India.

India's ancient story begins in a river valley, like Egypt's — but the Indus Valley cities astonish in their own way: planned streets, drains, great baths, a civilisation of order whose writing we still cannot read. Over that world came Aryan arrivals, bringing new languages, gods and social orders, and out of the meeting grew the beliefs and philosophies that mark India ever after — deep teachings about duty, the soul and release from suffering. The book follows the story through its great rulers: Ashoka, the conqueror who turned from war to preach peace and carved his edicts on pillars across his empire, and the Gupta Empire, a golden age of art and learning — with daily life, as always in this book, carrying the story at ground level.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ancient India rises in the Indus Valley, is reshaped by Aryan arrivals, deepened by its beliefs and philosophies, ruled by Ashoka and the Guptas — a civilisation carried by its ideas as much as its kings. Give one real example from your own life — a belief or practice from India alive in your own town today — yoga, a festival, a word — India's ideas still travelling — and name the lesson's key words: India, Indus Valley, Ashoka, Gupta Empire. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
