# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a coastline full of bays and islands. Mark where you would put farms, where ports, and where a city could guard the road between them. That map is Greece's beginning — write one line saying why the sea shapes everything.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Ancient Greece is studied whole — its setting among sea and islands, its politics and society, its everyday life, its wars and its religion form one Greek world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
