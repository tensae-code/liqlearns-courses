# The Vikings

## Do it first

Draw a longship: a long narrow boat with a curled prow. Write under it two lists — what such a ship could carry to a raid, and what it could carry to a new home. The Vikings did both: keep both lists in mind.

Out of Scandinavia's fjords and farms came the Vikings, and the book studies them as a whole depth study, not a cartoon. Their land first: poor soil and long winters pushing a seafaring people outward. Their raids: the sudden longship attacks that terrified Europe's coasts and rivers. Their voyages: further than the raids — to Iceland, Greenland, and the edge of America itself. And beneath the fame, their everyday life — farms, families, law assemblies, gods and crafts — and their legacy: the towns they founded, the lands they settled, the words and genes they left across the north. Raider and farmer in one people: the whole study is needed to hold both truths.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Vikings are a whole world in themselves — their land, their raids, their voyages, their everyday life and their legacy reach from Scandinavia to America. Give one real example from your own life — any seafaring people you know of who both raided and settled — the Vikings' double story is a sailor's story everywhere — and name the lesson's key words: Vikings, longship, raids and voyages, Scandinavia. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
