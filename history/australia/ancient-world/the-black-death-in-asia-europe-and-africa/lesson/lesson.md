# The Black Death in Asia, Europe and Africa

## Do it first

Write down what people in your town do when a sickness spreads — who they trust, what they close, what they fear. Medieval people faced the worst sickness in memory with far less knowledge: keep your list beside theirs.

The book begins before the plague, with medieval medicine — its humours, herbs and prayers, a system sincere and learned that had no weapon against what was coming. Then the Black Death travels: out of Asia along the trade roads, into Europe's ports and towns, and into Africa — killing, in place after place, a third or more of everyone alive. The study follows the impact (villages emptied, fields untended, wages and faith shaken), the responses (flight, prayer, blame, and new laws), and the effects that outlasted the dying: a world short of hands is a world where the old feudal order cracks. No event in the medieval book changes more with less warning.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Black Death spreads through Asia, Europe and Africa — and the book begins before it, with medieval medicine, then follows the plague's impact, the responses it provoked, and the world it changed. Give one real example from your own life — the sickness rules your own school lived by in recent years — closures, distance, fear — the Black Death's responses in modern dress — and name the lesson's key words: Black Death, plague, medieval medicine, impact. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
