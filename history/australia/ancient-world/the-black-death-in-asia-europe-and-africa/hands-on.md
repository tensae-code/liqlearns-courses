# Hands-on — The Black Death in Asia, Europe and Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down what people in your town do when a sickness spreads — who they trust, what they close, what they fear. Medieval people faced the worst sickness in memory with far less knowledge: keep your list beside theirs.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Black Death spreads through Asia, Europe and Africa — and the book begins before it, with medieval medicine, then follows the plague's impact, the responses it provoked, and the world it changed. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
