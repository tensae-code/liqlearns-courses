# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In Athens the citizens themselves ruled: the ecclesia debated and decided — and the book makes learners re-enact that debate, with the Olympic Games and two famous Greeks as the human faces of the Greek world.

Athens tried something the ancient world found astonishing: the citizens ruled themselves. In the ecclesia, the assembly, any citizen could stand, argue and vote on the city's decisions — war, laws, money. The book does not just describe that assembly; its research project re-enacts it, putting learners in the ecclesia to debate as Athenians debated. Around the politics stand the human faces of the Greek world: the Olympic Games, where the rival cities met in contest instead of war, and two famous Greeks, whose lives show what that world praised and what it produced. Democracy here is not a definition — it is a practice the learner performs.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
