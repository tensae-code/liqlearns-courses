# Ancient Civilisations: The Great Wall and China's Gifts

## Do it first

Draw a wall across the top of a page and a road through a gate in it. Write one thing the wall keeps out and two things that travel the road anyway. That is China's frontier in one drawing.

An empire with frontiers lives three lives at once. On China's borders the book finds contact — peoples meeting, marrying, learning; trade — caravans and markets carrying silk out and new goods in; and warfare — raids, campaigns and the great walls raised to hold the line. The study's closing question is the legacy: what did early China leave the world? Not the walls, which crumbled and were rebuilt and crumbled again, but the gifts that travelled the frontier roads — its inventions, its crafts, its ways of governing and recording. As with Egypt and India, the book ends a civilisation's study by weighing what it gave, and China's gifts prove heavier than its fortifications.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: On China's frontiers, contact, trade and warfare run side by side — and early China's legacy, its gifts to the world, outlasts every wall built to guard it. Give one real example from your own life — any border town you know — walls and gates, soldiers and markets, all in one place, as on China's frontier — and name the lesson's key words: Great Wall, frontier, trade, China's legacy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
