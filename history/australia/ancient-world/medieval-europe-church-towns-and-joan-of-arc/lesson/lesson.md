# Medieval Europe: Church, Towns and Joan of Arc

## Do it first

Write the first two lines of a letter from a young person who believes she has been given a mission too big for her. Sign it 'Joan'. This lesson is about the world she wrote from — churches, towns and faith at war.

Three forces remake medieval Europe in this study. First, the Catholic Church: it builds the cathedrals that still crown Europe's cities, carries learning, and frames every life from baptism to burial. Second, the meeting of Islam and the West — in Spain, in Sicily, and in the Crusades' clash — a meeting of war and of learning at once. Third, the towns: commerce grows, markets and guilds enrich burghers who fit nowhere on the feudal ladder. And the study lands on one significant individual: Joan of Arc, the peasant girl who led France's armies and was burned for it — the research project writes a letter in her voice, from inside that world of faith, war and rising towns.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Medieval Europe is built by the Catholic Church's cathedrals, shaken by Islam's meeting with the West, and changed by growing towns and commerce — and its research project writes a letter from Joan of Arc. Give one real example from your own life — a great church or mosque in your own town, still standing at its centre — the medieval Church's building habit, alive today — and name the lesson's key words: Catholic Church, cathedrals, towns and commerce, Joan of Arc. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
