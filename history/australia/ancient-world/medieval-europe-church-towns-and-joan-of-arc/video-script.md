# Video script — Medieval Europe: Church, Towns and Joan of Arc

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Medieval Europe is built by the Catholic Church's cathedrals, shaken by Islam's meeting with the West, and changed by growing towns and commerce — and its research project writes a letter from Joan of Arc.

Three forces remake medieval Europe in this study. First, the Catholic Church: it builds the cathedrals that still crown Europe's cities, carries learning, and frames every life from baptism to burial. Second, the meeting of Islam and the West — in Spain, in Sicily, and in the Crusades' clash — a meeting of war and of learning at once. Third, the towns: commerce grows, markets and guilds enrich burghers who fit nowhere on the feudal ladder. And the study lands on one significant individual: Joan of Arc, the peasant girl who led France's armies and was burned for it — the research project writes a letter in her voice, from inside that world of faith, war and rising towns.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
