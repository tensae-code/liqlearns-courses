# Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides

## Do it first

Draw one big circle labelled 'one empire'. Now draw lines splitting it into pieces, and label each piece with what it keeps — language, laws, faith, armies. Write one line: what survives when an empire divides?

Empires do not only fall; they transform. The overview follows the Roman world as it changes and divides — an empire too large for one ruler, split between east and west; the west breaking into kingdoms, the east holding on in Greek-speaking strength. Roads, laws, cities and faith all pass through the division and come out changed on the other side. This is the hinge of the whole Stage 4 course: antiquity turning into the medieval world, and the learner is asked to watch the turn itself, not just the worlds before and after it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Roman world transforms and divides as antiquity turns into the medieval world — one empire becomes the seed of several worlds. Give one real example from your own life — a big organisation you know that split into smaller ones, each keeping part of the old ways — Rome's division at small scale — and name the lesson's key words: Rome divides, transformation, medieval world, empire. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
