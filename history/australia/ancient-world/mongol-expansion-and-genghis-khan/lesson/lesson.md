# Mongol Expansion and Genghis Khan

## Do it first

Write the name 'Temujin' and under it three things an orphan boy on the steppe would need to become ruler of half the world. Keep the list: this lesson tests it.

Temujin was an outcast boy on the Mongol steppe; he died as Genghis Khan, founder of the largest land empire in history. The book walks the whole arc: the rise — a genius for loyalty, discipline and organisation uniting the steppe's warring clans; the Mongol army of conquest, horse-archers moving faster than any enemy's news; the law of the Mongol lands, one order imposed across conquered peoples; Yuan China, where his grandson's dynasty ruled the world's richest state and Marco Polo served and marvelled at the khan's court; and at the end, defeat and legacy — an empire that broke kingdoms and, in breaking them, joined East and West in one trading world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Temujin rises as Genghis Khan and builds the largest land empire the world had seen — conquest, the law of the Mongol lands, Yuan China, Marco Polo at the khan's court, defeat and legacy in one arc. Give one real example from your own life — any leader you know who united quarrelling groups into one force — Temujin did it for the whole steppe — and name the lesson's key words: Mongol Empire, Genghis Khan, Yuan China, Marco Polo. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
