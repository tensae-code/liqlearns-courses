# Ancient Civilisations: India's Order and Gifts

## Do it first

Name one number, one medicine idea, or one story that reached your country from far away. Write one line: how do gifts travel — by armies, by traders, or by teachers?

The India study closes by looking outward. Indian influence spread across Asia — along trade roads and sea routes, with merchants, monks and storytellers — until Indian scripts, stories, gods and ways of building stood from Central Asia to the islands of the south-east. The book asks learners to weigh that spread in the Indus Valley research project: India's art, its science and its technology assessed as gifts to the world. The weighing matters. Empires spread by taking; India also spread by giving — and the study leaves learners to judge which kind of spread lasts longer.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: India's influence spreads across Asia, and its art, science and technology are weighed as gifts to the world — India's order travels as far as its armies never went. Give one real example from your own life — the number system you used in maths today — India's gifts reached your exercise book without any army — and name the lesson's key words: India's gifts, spread of influence, art and science, Indus Valley. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
