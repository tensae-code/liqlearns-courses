# The Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism

## Do it first

Draw a ladder with three rungs. Put a lord on top, a knight in the middle, a peasant at the bottom. Beside each rung write what that person gives the rung above and what they get back. That ladder is feudal Europe.

## Sub lesson 1 — The medieval world

Before the depth studies begin, the book surveys the medieval world whole. Out of Rome's division grows a new order: feudal bonds tying lord to knight to peasant, land given for loyalty and service; faith standing at the centre of life, its churches in every village and its calendar in every year; and daily life — the fields, the manor, the seasons — carrying on beneath the castles. The survey's job is the map before the journeys: one whole world, feudal, faithful and farming, so that the Vikings, the Church and the towns of the coming studies have a world to stand in.

## Sub lesson 2 — Medieval Europe — feudal society

The depth study steps inside the survey's map. Feudal society is a ladder of promises: the lord holds the land and owes protection; the knight holds land from the lord and owes service in war; the peasant works the land and owes its produce, receiving a place and protection in return. Around that ladder the full topic spreads — everyday life in hall and cottage, crime and punishment in a world of ordeal and oath, and the towns, growing at the ladder's edge, where money and markets begin to loosen the old bonds. The ladder held Europe together for centuries; the towns at its edge are where its story turns next.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: After Rome, Europe orders itself by feudal bonds — lords, knights and peasants bound in a ladder of land and loyalty — and the survey first, then the depth study, shows that order whole: everyday life, crime and punishment, and towns. Give one real example from your own life — any team or school where each level owes duties up and protection down — the feudal ladder's small modern echo — and name the lesson's key words: feudalism, lords, knights, peasants, medieval Europe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
