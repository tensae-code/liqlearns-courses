# Hands-on — The Aztecs: Tenochtitlan

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a city on a lake: causeways to the shore, canals for streets, a great temple at the centre. Write one line on what its builders must solve first — water, food, or defence. Tenochtitlan solved all three.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Before the Spanish arrive, the pre-Columbian world stands on its own: the Aztecs build Tenochtitlan on a lake and raise a whole belief around it — a civilisation complete before contact. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
