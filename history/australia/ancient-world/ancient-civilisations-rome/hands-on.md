# Hands-on — Ancient Civilisations: Rome

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw one straight road from a small town to a great city. Along it, write three things that would travel the road — soldiers, goods, ideas. Rome's story runs on such roads: write one line on what a road does to a small town's future.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Rome grows from Republic to Empire: its everyday life, its contacts and conflicts, its religion and the figure of Julius Caesar carry the story of the ancient world's greatest power. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
