# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Rome

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Rome grows from Republic to Empire: its everyday life, its contacts and conflicts, its religion and the figure of Julius Caesar carry the story of the ancient world's greatest power.

Rome begins as one city among many in Italy and ends ruling the whole Mediterranean world. The book walks that rise whole: the Republic, where citizens and senate shared power; the everyday life of Roman streets, houses and baths; Rome's contacts and conflicts as its armies and roads reached outward; its religion, borrowed and built; and at the turn, Julius Caesar — the general whose victories abroad and ambition at home broke the Republic and opened the road to Empire, where one man ruled. A time-travel research project sends learners into that world to report back. Rome's lesson is the double face of power: the same roads and laws that organised a world also carried conquest into it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
