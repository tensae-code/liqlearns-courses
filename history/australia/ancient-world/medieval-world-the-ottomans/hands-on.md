# Hands-on — The Medieval World: The Ottomans

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a map, find where Asia meets Europe — the straits at Constantinople. Write one line: why would an empire that held both shores be hard to stop? That is the Ottomans' geography.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Ottoman Empire rises from small beginnings, expands into Europe, and reaches its height under Suleiman the Magnificent — an empire whose everyday life the book studies alongside its sultans. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
