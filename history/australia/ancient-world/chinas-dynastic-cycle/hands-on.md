# Hands-on — China's Dynastic Cycle

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a wheel and label it: 'a dynasty rises — rules well — decays — falls — a new dynasty rises'. China's history turned on that wheel for two thousand years. Write one line: what finally breaks a wheel like that?

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Under the Dragon Throne, China's Middle Kingdom turns on its dynastic cycle — traditional society, culture and beliefs holding for millennia — until contact, conflict and change under the Empress Dowager Cixi bring the dynasty down into the modern world. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
