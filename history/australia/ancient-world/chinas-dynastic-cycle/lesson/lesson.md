# China's Dynastic Cycle

## Do it first

Draw a wheel and label it: 'a dynasty rises — rules well — decays — falls — a new dynasty rises'. China's history turned on that wheel for two thousand years. Write one line: what finally breaks a wheel like that?

China enters the Stage 5 book as the Middle Kingdom: an empire that saw itself as the centre of the world, its traditional society, culture and beliefs ordered under the Dragon Throne and renewed, dynasty after dynasty, on the great cycle of rise, decay and replacement. The study follows the cycle's last turn. Under the Empress Dowager Cixi, the old order meets what it cannot absorb: contact with industrial empires, conflict it keeps losing, and change pressing from inside and out at once. The dynasty falls, and China is hurled into the modern world — the wheel that turned for two thousand years breaking, in one lifetime, against the age this course is about.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Under the Dragon Throne, China's Middle Kingdom turns on its dynastic cycle — traditional society, culture and beliefs holding for millennia — until contact, conflict and change under the Empress Dowager Cixi bring the dynasty down into the modern world. Give one real example from your own life — any old institution in your country that renews itself by replacing its head — China's dynasties did that at the scale of a civilisation — and name the lesson's key words: dynastic cycle, Middle Kingdom, Dragon Throne, Empress Dowager Cixi. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
