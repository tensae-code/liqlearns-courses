# Video script — Polynesian Expansion Across the Pacific

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Polynesian voyagers settle the whole Pacific — the greatest sea journey in the ancient world — and the study follows life and death on Easter Island, the land of the long white cloud, Maoritanga and Polynesian power.

The last great settlement of the ancient world happened on water. Polynesian voyagers — navigating by stars, swells and birds, in canoes built without a nail of metal — spread across the Pacific's vast triangle until nearly every habitable island held their descendants. The book follows the settlement's far corners: Easter Island, where a society raised giant statues and met life and death at the ocean's loneliest address; Aotearoa, the land of the long white cloud, where Maoritanga — Māori culture and identity — grew from the voyagers' heritage; and the question of Polynesian power, how chiefs and communities ordered these scattered worlds. It is a depth study of skill: the Pacific was not crossed by luck, but by the most sophisticated sea knowledge of its age.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
