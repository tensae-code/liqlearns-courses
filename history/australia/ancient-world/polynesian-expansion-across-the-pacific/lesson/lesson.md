# Polynesian Expansion Across the Pacific

## Do it first

Draw a triangle on a map of the Pacific with corners at Hawaii, Easter Island and New Zealand. Inside it write: 'settled by canoe'. Write one line on what those sailors must have known about stars, winds and birds.

The last great settlement of the ancient world happened on water. Polynesian voyagers — navigating by stars, swells and birds, in canoes built without a nail of metal — spread across the Pacific's vast triangle until nearly every habitable island held their descendants. The book follows the settlement's far corners: Easter Island, where a society raised giant statues and met life and death at the ocean's loneliest address; Aotearoa, the land of the long white cloud, where Maoritanga — Māori culture and identity — grew from the voyagers' heritage; and the question of Polynesian power, how chiefs and communities ordered these scattered worlds. It is a depth study of skill: the Pacific was not crossed by luck, but by the most sophisticated sea knowledge of its age.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Polynesian voyagers settle the whole Pacific — the greatest sea journey in the ancient world — and the study follows life and death on Easter Island, the land of the long white cloud, Maoritanga and Polynesian power. Give one real example from your own life — any story your own people tell about ancestors arriving by water — the Pacific triangle is that story written across an ocean — and name the lesson's key words: Polynesian voyagers, Pacific, Easter Island, Maoritanga. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
