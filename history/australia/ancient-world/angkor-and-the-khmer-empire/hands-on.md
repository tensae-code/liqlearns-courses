# Hands-on — Angkor and the Khmer Empire

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a temple tower like a stone mountain. Under it write two lists: what a king gains by building it, and what thousands of ordinary workers give to raise it. Angkor stands between those two lists.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Khmer Empire rises in Southeast Asia, builds Angkor's temples, orders its people's roles and everyday life — and then declines, leaving its stones to tell the story. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
