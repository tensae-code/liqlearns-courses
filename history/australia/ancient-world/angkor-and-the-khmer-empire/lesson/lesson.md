# Angkor and the Khmer Empire

## Do it first

Draw a temple tower like a stone mountain. Under it write two lists: what a king gains by building it, and what thousands of ordinary workers give to raise it. Angkor stands between those two lists.

In the forests of Southeast Asia the Khmer Empire raised the largest religious building on earth. The book follows its whole arc: the rise of Khmer kings ruling from Angkor, a city of temples and reservoirs; the building — Angkor's stone mountains raised as heavens on earth for god-kings; the order of roles and everyday life beneath them, farmers feeding the court from engineered waters; and then the decline, as the empire faded and the forest took the stones back. The research project blogs the empire — learners posting Angkor's story as if reporting it — rise, glory, order and fall in one people's telling.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Khmer Empire rises in Southeast Asia, builds Angkor's temples, orders its people's roles and everyday life — and then declines, leaving its stones to tell the story. Give one real example from your own life — any great building in your country raised by many hands for one ruler's glory — Angkor is that story at its largest — and name the lesson's key words: Angkor, Khmer Empire, temples, decline. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
