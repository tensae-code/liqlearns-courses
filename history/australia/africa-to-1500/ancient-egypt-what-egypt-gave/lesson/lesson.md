# Ancient Egypt: What Egypt Gave

## Do it first

Choose three things in your home whose roots you could trace to an old civilisation — writing, a calendar, a tool. Write one line for each, as if it were a label in a museum exhibit.

The Egypt study does not end at the fall of the pharaohs. It ends with a question: what did Egypt give the world? The book hands that question to the learners as a research project — an exhibit, curated by the students themselves. To fill it they must weigh Egypt's gifts: its writing, its calendar, its building, its ideas about the soul and the ordered state, and decide what truly travelled from the Nile into the wider world. Curating is the historian's craft from Level 1 turned outward — choosing the evidence, writing the labels, and defending the claim that this civilisation still gives to people who never saw the Nile.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Egypt's legacy is what it gave the world — and the book makes learners curate that legacy themselves, assembling an exhibit of Egypt's gifts. Give one real example from your own life — the calendar on your wall — one of the gifts this lesson's exhibit would claim for Egypt — and name the lesson's key words: legacy, Egypt's gifts, exhibit, research project. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
