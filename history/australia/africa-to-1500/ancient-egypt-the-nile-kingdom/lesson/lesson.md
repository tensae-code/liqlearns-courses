# Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

## Do it first

Draw a river. On its banks, draw what a farming people would need the river to do for them each year, and who would have to organise it.

Egypt grows along one river. The Nile floods on its yearly rhythm, lays down black soil, and a desert land becomes a garden — if the work is organised. Around that organisation a whole world rises, and the book studies it whole: the chronology of the pharaohs, the river setting that made it possible, the society from pharaoh to farmer, the everyday life of houses, food and work, and the religion that bound it all — gods, temples and the journey after death. Egypt is the course's first full civilisation, and the study's lesson is that a civilisation is not its monuments: it is a whole people's ordered life, and the Nile is the thread that ties the order together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Pharaonic Egypt is a whole world built on the Nile: its chronology, its river setting, its society, its everyday life and its religion stand together as one civilisation. Give one real example from your own life — any river in your own country — its floods and dry seasons still order the farming year around it — and name the lesson's key words: Egypt, the Nile, pharaoh, civilisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
