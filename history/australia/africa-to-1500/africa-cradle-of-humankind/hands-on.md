# Hands-on — Africa: Cradle of Humankind

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a map of the world, put your finger on Africa. Then trace, slowly, every road a walking people could take out of it — up the Nile, across to Asia, down to the far islands. Write down the last place your finger reaches.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Humanity begins in Africa and spreads across the world — the first chapter of the ancient world is an African chapter, and every people's story starts there. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
