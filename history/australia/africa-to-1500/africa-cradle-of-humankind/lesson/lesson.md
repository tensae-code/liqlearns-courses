# Africa: Cradle of Humankind

## Do it first

On a map of the world, put your finger on Africa. Then trace, slowly, every road a walking people could take out of it — up the Nile, across to Asia, down to the far islands. Write down the last place your finger reaches.

The ancient world does not begin with kings or cities. It begins in Africa, where humanity itself begins. From that African cradle, generation by generation, people spread — walking into new valleys, new coasts, new continents — until the whole world held human life. The book places this spread first, as the opening chapter of everything that follows: before Egypt, before Greece, before any empire, there is this one great journey out of Africa. Every civilisation studied later in the course is a descendant of that journey.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Humanity begins in Africa and spreads across the world — the first chapter of the ancient world is an African chapter, and every people's story starts there. Give one real example from your own life — your own family's deepest roots — this lesson says every family's road, followed far enough back, starts in Africa — and name the lesson's key words: Africa, cradle of humankind, human origins, migration. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
