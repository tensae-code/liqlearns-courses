# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

List everything you ate yesterday. Beside each item, write whether a hunter could find it wild or a farmer must grow it. Count how much of your life depends on farming.

How do we know societies emerged? The book answers: from the evidence, in order. First the tools — shaped stone, worked by hands that planned. Then the turn that changed everything: farming. People who plant and harvest can stay in one place, store food, feed more children, and free some hands for new work — pots, walls, weaving, worship. Settlements grow into villages, villages into towns, and at the end of the evidence trail stand whole civilisations. The overview teaches the learner to read that trail like the historians of the first level: tools, farming, civilisations — each stage proved by what was left behind.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The evidence shows societies emerging step by step — tools first, then farming, then whole civilisations: farming is the turn that makes settled, growing societies possible. Give one real example from your own life — the bread on your own table — grown, not gathered: the farming turn feeding you today — and name the lesson's key words: stone tools, farming, emerging societies, civilisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
