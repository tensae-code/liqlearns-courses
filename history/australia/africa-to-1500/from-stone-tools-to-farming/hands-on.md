# Hands-on — From Stone Tools to Farming

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List everything you ate yesterday. Beside each item, write whether a hunter could find it wild or a farmer must grow it. Count how much of your life depends on farming.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The evidence shows societies emerging step by step — tools first, then farming, then whole civilisations: farming is the turn that makes settled, growing societies possible. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
