# History — Australia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Australia inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Australia's books in hand are
Jacaranda's *Retroactive 1* (NSW Australian Curriculum History,
Stage 4) and *Retroactive 2* (Stage 5), and the placements
file's book order is the teaching order — the Stage 4 book
first, the Stage 5 book second. Inside that order, the ladder
follows the eras as the books themselves divide them: the
historian's craft first, as Depth Study 1 of Stage 4 teaches it
(Level 1); the ancient world — Africa the cradle, Egypt,
Greece, Rome, India, China (Level 2); the medieval and
expanding world the Stage 4 overviews and depth studies survey
— Rome dividing, feudalism, the Vikings, Church and towns, the
Ottomans, Angkor, the shoguns, the Polynesian voyagers, the
Mongols, the Black Death, the Aztecs, and the Renaissance turn
(Level 3); the Stage 4 book's closing contact study — the
Spanish conquest, Aboriginal Australia's colonisation and
dispossession, and America's Trail of Tears set beside it as
the book's own comparison (Level 4); then Stage 5's making of
the modern world — industry, empire, capitalism and its rival
ideas, the forced and free movements of peoples, and Australia
making itself a nation (Level 5); China's dynastic turn and
the world wars, with the Depression between them and the
Holocaust at the European war's centre (Level 6); and last
Australia after 1945 — the Cold War from an Australian seat,
the rights-and-freedoms road from the Universal Declaration to
the Apology, popular culture, the environment movement,
post-war migration, and the Vietnam era with Maralinga
(Level 7). The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced
gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government-curriculum textbooks — this
country's own *Retroactive 1* and *Retroactive 2*, the NSW
Australian Curriculum History books for Stages 4 and 5
(Jacaranda) — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from
us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons
are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps → First Steps: The Historian's Craft** —
   history as detective work: the historian's questions and
   hypotheses, the other experts who read prehistory and date
   it, sources analysed before any history is written, and
   heritage — why the past is conserved, at Abu Simbel and
   Stonehenge. 4 lessons, in one title: `first-steps/`
   (4 lessons).

2. **Africa to 1500 + Ancient World → The Ancient World:
   First Peoples and First Civilisations** — humanity begins
   in Africa and spreads; tools, farming, then civilisations;
   Egypt whole and Egypt's legacy; Greece whole, Athens'
   democracy, Rome, India and its gifts, China under the First
   Emperor and China's legacy. 11 lessons, in two titles:
   `africa-to-1500/` (4 lessons) and `ancient-world/`
   (7 lessons).

3. **Ancient World + Modern World → The Medieval and
   Expanding World** — Rome transforms and divides; feudal
   Europe surveyed and then studied in depth; the Vikings;
   Church, towns and Joan of Arc; the Ottomans; Angkor;
   Japan under the shoguns; the Polynesian expansion; the
   Mongols; the Black Death across three continents; the
   Aztecs before contact; and the Renaissance turn. 12
   lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/` (11 lessons) and
   `modern-world/` (1 lesson).

4. **Modern World → Contact, Conquest and Colonisation** —
   the Spanish conquest of the Americas; Aboriginal
   Australia — land, Dreaming and law — under colonisation,
   contact and dispossession; and America's Trail of Tears,
   the comparison the book itself sets beside Australia's
   story. 3 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/`
   (3 lessons).

5. **Modern World + Africa After 1500 → The Making of the
   Modern World** — the Industrial Revolution (what changed,
   why England first; textiles, steam and rail; its effects
   on cities, classes and empires); Europe's imperial
   expansion; capitalism and the progressive ideas that
   answered it, Chartism to socialism; nationalism; the
   Atlantic slave trade and Australia's convicts and settlers
   as the age's movements of peoples; and Australia making a
   nation, with Indigenous and non-European experiences
   inside the making. 10 lessons, in two titles:
   `modern-world/` (8 lessons) and `africa-after-1500/`
   (2 lessons).

6. **Ancient World + Modern World → China's Turn and the
   World Wars** — China's dynastic cycle breaks into the
   modern world; the First World War's origins and Australia
   in it — Gallipoli, the home front, the conscription
   referendums, the Anzac legend; the Great Depression
   between the wars; the Second World War's origins, its
   European course with the Holocaust at its centre, its
   Pacific course to Australia's doorstep, and Australia's
   home front with the censored bombing of Darwin; and the
   Holocaust studied whole — law, pogrom, ghettos, the Final
   Solution. 11 lessons, in two titles: `ancient-world/`
   (1 lesson) and `modern-world/` (10 lessons).

7. **Modern World → Australia After 1945: Rights, Culture
   and a Changing Nation** — the Cold War from an Australian
   seat; the post-war frame of war and peace, rights and a
   developing world; the rights-and-freedoms road — the
   Universal Declaration and Doc Evatt, the Stolen
   Generations, the Freedom Ride, Mabo, the Apology — with
   America's civil rights movement as its mirror and spark;
   popular culture; the environment movement; post-war
   migration; and the Vietnam era, closing on Maralinga. 8
   lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (8 lessons).

**Totals: 59 lessons** (Level 1: 4, Level 2: 11, Level 3: 12,
Level 4: 3, Level 5: 10, Level 6: 11, Level 7: 8) — 61
placements stocked, every one on a student book: taught in
1 country (Australia). No lesson in this course stands on
teacher-guide evidence; the filing holds no Australia teacher
guide.
