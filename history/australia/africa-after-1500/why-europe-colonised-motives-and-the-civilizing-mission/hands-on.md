# Hands-on — Why Europe Colonised: Motives and the Civilizing Mission

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write two lists: 'What an industrial country needs from far away' and 'What it tells itself about taking it'. Empires live in the gap between those lists.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In the 19th century Europe's empires expand across the globe, carrying the modern world by force — imperial expansion is the Industrial Revolution's shadow, reaching every shore including Australia's. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
