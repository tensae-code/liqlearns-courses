# Why Europe Colonised: Motives and the Civilizing Mission

## Do it first

Write two lists: 'What an industrial country needs from far away' and 'What it tells itself about taking it'. Empires live in the gap between those lists.

The modern world did not spread by invitation. In the 19th century Europe's empires expand across the globe — Africa partitioned, Asia pried open, the Pacific claimed island by island — carrying the industrial world by force: its goods and guns, its flags and its faith, its claim to be civilizing the peoples it ruled. Australia itself sits inside this expansion, a colony of the empire that led it. The overview's cold lesson is that the modern world's arrival and its violence are the same event seen from different shores, and the course will keep landing world processes on Australian soil exactly as this overview lands empire on the world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the 19th century Europe's empires expand across the globe, carrying the modern world by force — imperial expansion is the Industrial Revolution's shadow, reaching every shore including Australia's. Give one real example from your own life — goods in your own shops that travel from three continents — the imperial trade web's descendant, now ruled by contracts instead of gunboats — and name the lesson's key words: imperial expansion, empires, 19th century, colonisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
