# The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

## Do it first

Write one line on what a person is, that no price can be put on. Hold it against a system that priced millions. This lesson studies that system from the side of the enslaved.

The movement of peoples in the industrial age was not all migration. Its darkest current was forced: the Atlantic slave trade, which tore millions of Africans from their homes and shipped them in chains to labour in the Americas. The book studies that trade from the inside — enslaved Africans' experiences: the capture, the coffle, the middle passage, the plantation — and slavery's impacts: on the enslaved, on the societies that profited, and on Africa itself, drained of its young. Placed inside the Industrial Revolution's own depth study, the lesson is deliberate: the age of steam and progress was built partly on stolen people, and their story is not a footnote to it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Industrial Revolution moves peoples by force: enslaved Africans' experiences and slavery's impacts are studied in their own right — the human cargo at the foundation of the Atlantic world. Give one real example from your own life — the freedoms you used this morning without thinking — movement, work, family — are exactly what this trade stole, by the million — and name the lesson's key words: Atlantic slave trade, slavery, movement of peoples, experiences and impacts. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
