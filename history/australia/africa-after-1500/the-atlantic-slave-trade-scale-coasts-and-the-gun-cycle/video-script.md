# Video script — The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told animated-map style — who, where, what turned — in plain words.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Industrial Revolution moves peoples by force: enslaved Africans' experiences and slavery's impacts are studied in their own right — the human cargo at the foundation of the Atlantic world.

The movement of peoples in the industrial age was not all migration. Its darkest current was forced: the Atlantic slave trade, which tore millions of Africans from their homes and shipped them in chains to labour in the Americas. The book studies that trade from the inside — enslaved Africans' experiences: the capture, the coffle, the middle passage, the plantation — and slavery's impacts: on the enslaved, on the societies that profited, and on Africa itself, drained of its young. Placed inside the Industrial Revolution's own depth study, the lesson is deliberate: the age of steam and progress was built partly on stolen people, and their story is not a footnote to it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
