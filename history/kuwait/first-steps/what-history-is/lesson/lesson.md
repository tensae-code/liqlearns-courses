# What History Is

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — what history is. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: history is a science with pillars — not a pile of dates — and it begins by asking what a civilisation is and what raises one, as the Gulf's own land and water raised its first towns.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: What civilisation is and the factors that raised it in the Gulf

Start with a question a child can ask: what makes a civilisation? It is not just old stones. A civilisation rises where the land and water let people settle, grow food, trade and organise themselves into ordered towns. Around the Gulf, geography did that work: the sea gave food and roads, the shores gave harbours, and scattered villages grew into towns with rulers, crafts and learning. That is the first step of this whole course — history begins by asking what raised a civilisation, and the Gulf is the first example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1 Lesson 1: The concept of the science of history, its characteristics, importance and pillars

Secondary History opens with method, and the first question is the subject itself: what is history? Not a pile of dates to memorise — a science, with its own characteristics, its importance, and its pillars, the foundations it stands on. And why does a nation keep such a science? Because a people that studies its past knows itself. Grade 10 will spend a term on Kuwait; first, it sharpens the tool the term will use.
