# Fact or Opinion

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — fact or opinion. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: a past can be forged as well as told truly, and telling the difference matters: a lie about yesterday bends a whole society's awareness today.

History has an enemy: forgery. A forged past — a document faked, an event invented, a hero manufactured — does not stay on the page. This lesson sets forged pasts beside true ones so learners can watch the damage: a lie about yesterday bends a whole society's awareness today, steering what a people believes about itself. Telling fact from forgery is therefore not a scholar's game; it is a citizen's duty.

Give one real example from the story, and name the lesson's key words: forgery, societal awareness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
