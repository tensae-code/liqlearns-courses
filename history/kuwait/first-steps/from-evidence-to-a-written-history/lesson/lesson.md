# From Evidence to a Written History

This lesson tells the story properly: a written history is built, not found: the researcher gathers sources, follows set research steps, and cross-examines each account of an event the way a judge hears witnesses before judging what happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1 Lesson 2: Sources for studying history and the steps of research in it

Where does written history come from? Follow one fact. It begins in a source — something dusty, partial, perhaps damaged: a document, an object, an account. The researcher works through set steps: gathering the sources, testing them, comparing them, and only then writing. At the end of the steps, the fact stands as written history. This lesson teaches the road every fact in every history book has travelled.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1 Lesson 3: The historical researcher and analysing the historical event

The researcher is not a copyist; the researcher is a judge. Faced with an event, accounts disagree — witnesses saw different things, or wanted different things believed. So the historical researcher cross-examines: each account is tested, weighed against the others, and only then does the researcher judge what happened. Analysing a historical event is courtroom work done in a library.
