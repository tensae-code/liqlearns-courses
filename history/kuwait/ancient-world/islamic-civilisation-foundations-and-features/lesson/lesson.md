# Islamic Civilisation: Foundations and Features

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — islamic civilisation: foundations and features. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: Islamic civilisation joins faith, knowledge and building into one whole: it is defined by its values, ethics, inventions and works, with a material side and a spiritual side, and its marks can be spotted in any city, book or building it left behind.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1 Lesson 1: Islamic civilisation as the most important civilisation

Grade 8 opens with a weighing. The book sets the great civilisations of history side by side and asks learners to argue the case: why does the Islamic civilisation stand first for them? Its claim, as the book teaches it, is the joining — faith, knowledge and building held in one civilisation, a way of life that prayed, studied and constructed at once. Whether a learner argues it well or badly, the exercise is the point: civilisations are compared, not just listed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1 Lesson 3: The concept of Islamic civilisation and the bases it rose on

What exactly is Islamic civilisation? The book makes learners build the definition themselves, piece by piece: its values and its ethics; its inventions and its works. Then they sort what they have built into two sides — the material side, the things made and built, and the spiritual side, the faith and thought that moved the makers. A definition you have assembled yourself is one you actually hold.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 1 Lesson 4: Characteristics of Islamic civilisation

A civilisation, like a person, has recognisable marks. This lesson draws out the characteristics of Islamic civilisation one by one — the features that repeat across its lands and centuries. The test the book sets is spotting: given any city it raised, any book it wrote, any building it left behind, can the learner name the marks and say — this is Islamic civilisation's work? Characteristics are a field guide for reading the past.
