# Lesson plan — Islamic Civilisation: Foundations and Features

**Course:** History — Kuwait · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Islamic Civilisation: Foundations and Features
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Islamic Civilisation: How It Worked, and What Europe Learned (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Islamic civilisation joins faith, knowledge and building into one whole: it is defined by its values, ethics, inventions and works, with a material side and a spiritual side, and its marks can be spotted in any city, book or building it left behind.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Islamic Civilisation: How It Worked, and What Europe Learned (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 1 Lesson 1: Islamic civilisation as the most important civilisation** — Learners weigh the great civilisations against each other and argue why the Islamic one, joining faith, knowledge and building, stands first for them.
2. **Unit 1 Lesson 3: The concept of Islamic civilisation and the bases it rose on** — Learners rebuild the definition from its pieces — values, ethics, inventions and works — and sort its material and spiritual sides.
3. **Unit 1 Lesson 4: Characteristics of Islamic civilisation** — The civilisation's marks are drawn out one by one so learners can spot them in any city, book or building it left behind.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Kuwait's books themselves work, starting from Kuwait and widening to the Gulf and the world.
