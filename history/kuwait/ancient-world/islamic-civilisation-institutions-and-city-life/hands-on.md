# Hands-on — Islamic Civilisation: Institutions and City Life

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Islamic civilisation worked as a living order: a state of caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police; an economy of markets, crafts, farming and trade; and cities rising in planned stone around their mosques and streets. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of a planned Islamic city street — mosque, market stalls and stone houses along one road), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
