# Lesson plan — Islamic Civilisation: Institutions and City Life

**Course:** History — Kuwait · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Islamic Civilisation: Institutions and City Life
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Islamic Civilisation: How It Worked, and What Europe Learned (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Islamic civilisation worked as a living order: a state of caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police; an economy of markets, crafts, farming and trade; and cities rising in planned stone around their mosques and streets.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Islamic Civilisation: How It Worked, and What Europe Learned (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2 Lesson 1: Political and administrative manifestations of Islamic civilisation** — Caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police take shape as a working state, with Ali ibn Abi Talib's police chief, the sahib al-shurta, keeping the streets.
2. **Unit 2 Lesson 2: Economic manifestations** — Markets, crafts, farming and trade fill the Islamic world's purse, the wealth that pays for its cities and schools.
3. **Unit 2 Lesson 3: Economic manifestations continued, and urban manifestations** — The money story runs on into streets, mosques and houses as Islamic cities rise in planned stone.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Islamic Civilisation: Foundations and Features** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Islamic civilisation worked as a living order: a state of caliphate, ministry, diwans, provinces and police; an economy of markets, crafts, farming and trade; and cities rising in planned stone around their mosques and streets.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Kuwait story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Kuwait's books themselves work, starting from Kuwait and widening to the Gulf and the world.
