# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — trade routes that linked the old world. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: the old Gulf traded in both directions at once: caravans linked it overland to Iraq, Egypt, Syria and Yemen, while its ships carried goods by sea to India, Yemen and East Africa.

The old Gulf never lived alone. Overland, camel caravans linked its ports and oases to Iraq, Egypt, Syria and Yemen, carrying goods road by road. By sea, Gulf ships sailed the monsoon routes to India, Yemen and East Africa and came home laden. Land and sea together made the Gulf a crossroads of the old world — a place whose wealth came from connecting other places.
