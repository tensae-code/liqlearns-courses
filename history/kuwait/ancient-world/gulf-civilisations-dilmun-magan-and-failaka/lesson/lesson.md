# Gulf Civilisations: Dilmun, Magan and Failaka

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — gulf civilisations: dilmun, magan and failaka. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: the Gulf's position between the old world's lands made it a working sea from the earliest times: Dilmun shipped copper, wood, dates and pearls, Tarut farmed palms, Hili raised round stone tombs, and Magan smelted copper into swords.

Look at the map first. The Gulf lies between the old world's great lands, on the water road between Mesopotamia and India — and position is power. Dilmun, centred on Bahrain, became the trading middle-place, shipping copper, wood, dates and pearls. Tarut farmed its palms. At Hili, people buried their dead in great round stone tombs that still stand. And Magan smelted its copper and worked it into swords. Four names, one lesson: the Gulf was a working, trading world thousands of years ago.
