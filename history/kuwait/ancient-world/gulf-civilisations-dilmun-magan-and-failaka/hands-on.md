# Hands-on — Gulf Civilisations: Dilmun, Magan and Failaka

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: the Gulf's position between the old world's lands made it a working sea from the earliest times: Dilmun shipped copper, wood, dates and pearls, Tarut farmed palms, Hili raised round stone tombs, and Magan smelted copper into swords. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of a Dilmun sailing ship loaded with copper and date baskets, with palm farms and round stone tombs on the shore behind it), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
