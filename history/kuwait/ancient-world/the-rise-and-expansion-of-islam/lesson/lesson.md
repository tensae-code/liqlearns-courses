# The Rise and Expansion of Islam

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — the rise and expansion of islam. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: Islam rose in Arabia in one lifetime — the call in Makkah, the Hijra to Madinah where a state was founded, the campaigns that defended it and united Arabia — and within the first Islamic century it had reached the Gulf and far beyond, carried on by the caliphates.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Islamic civilisation in the Gulf states region

In the first Islamic century, the new faith reached the Gulf — and it built. Mosques rose along both shores, and their remains still mark the map: Bahrain's Khamis Mosque among the oldest, and sites as far as Suhar in Oman. The book sets learners a sorting task — which site stands in which country? — because the point is the spread: within a hundred years, the Gulf had become part of the Islamic world, and its stones still say so.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 1 Lesson 2: A sketch of the birth of the Islamic state and its civilisation

Here is the whole arc in one sketch. The call begins in Makkah with Muhammad. Within a generation it has spread across Arabia — by the Farewell Sermon in year 10 of the Hijra, Arabia stands united under it. The young state's armies reach the frontiers of the two great empires of the age, the Sasanian and the Byzantine. Then the caliphates carry the civilisation on, capital by capital: Madinah and Kufa, Damascus, Baghdad, and in time the Ottoman capital. A sketch is a map in words — this one covers a thousand years in a page.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 1: The Peninsula, cradle of the Message — the Prophet's birth, life and call

The narrative now follows one life. Muhammad is born into the tribe of Quraysh, keepers of Makkah. At about twelve he travels to Syria with his uncle Abu Talib; at fifteen he is present at the Fijar war; at twenty-five, in 595, he marries Khadija. When the Kaaba is rebuilt in 605, it is his judgement that settles the tribes' quarrel over the Black Stone. Then comes the revelation. The call begins in secret, widens to his kin, then goes openly public — and Quraysh, whose order it threatens, turns against him.

## Sub lesson 4 — Chapter 1: The Hijra and founding the Islamic state in Madinah

Persecution drives the turn: the Prophet migrates from Makkah to Yathrib — the Hijra. In Madinah, as the city is now known, he builds in order: the mosque raised; the feuding tribes of Aws and Khazraj reconciled; the Muhajirun, who migrated with him, and the Ansar, who received them, bound as brothers; and the Sahifa charter set down, ordering the city's life together. Count what has happened: a community with a centre, a peace and a charter. A state exists.

## Sub lesson 5 — Chapter 1: The campaigns and the unification of Arabia

The young state must survive to grow. The book marches the campaigns in order: Nakhla; Badr in year 2 of the Hijra; Uhud in year 3; the Trench in year 5, when Madinah itself is defended; and in year 6 the truce of Hudaybiyya, a peace that proves a victory. In year 8 Makkah is opened — the city of the call receives it in peace. And in year 10 the Farewell Pilgrimage seals the work: Arabia stands united. Defence first, then unification — that is the campaign story's shape.
