## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 3: Civilisations that stood in Kuwait — Ubaid at Subiya and Hellenistic Failaka

Kuwait's own ground is older than Kuwait. At Subiya, on the mainland shore, people of the Ubaid age settled seven thousand years ago — potters shaping vessels, grinders working grain, a settled life on this coast in 5500–4500 BCE. Much later, Greeks came to the island offshore. Failaka had wells and water, so they settled it, called it Ikaros, and traded there — leaving coins and Dilmun seals in its soil for finders to dig up. Two ages, one shore: people have been choosing this coast for a very long time.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: The land of Kuwait — civilisational and historical roots

Grade 10's Kuwait story begins before Kuwait. Long before any state stood here, the land gathered civilisations on its coast and islands — the Ubaid settlers, the Dilmun traders, the Greeks of Failaka. The book calls these the country's civilisational and historical roots: deep roots, reaching down thousands of years, that the modern country claims as its own. A young state, an old land — that is the claim this lesson plants.
