# Lesson plan — Kuwait: Ubaid and Hellenistic Failaka

**Course:** History — Kuwait · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Kuwait: Ubaid and Hellenistic Failaka
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Ancient Gulf and the Birth of Islam (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** civilisations stood on Kuwait's own ground long before the state: Ubaid potters and grain-grinders settled at Subiya thousands of years ago, and Greeks later settled well-watered Failaka island, called it Ikaros, and left coins and seals behind.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The Ancient Gulf and the Birth of Islam (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 2 Lesson 3: Civilisations that stood in Kuwait — Ubaid at Subiya and Hellenistic Failaka** — Potters and grain-grinders settle at Subiya in Ubaid times (5500–4500 BCE), then Greeks settle well-watered Failaka, call it Ikaros, and leave coins and Dilmun seals behind.
2. **Unit 2 Lesson 1: The land of Kuwait — civilisational and historical roots** — Before any state, Kuwait's land gathers civilisations on its coast and islands, the deep roots the modern country claims as its own.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Kuwait's books themselves work, starting from Kuwait and widening to the Gulf and the world.
