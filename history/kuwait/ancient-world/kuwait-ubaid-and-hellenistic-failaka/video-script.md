# Video script — Kuwait: Ubaid and Hellenistic Failaka

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Civilisations stood on Kuwait's own ground long before the state: Ubaid potters and grain-grinders settled at Subiya thousands of years ago, and Greeks later settled well-watered Failaka island, called it Ikaros, and left coins and seals behind.

Look at the map. Here is Kuwait, at the head of the Gulf, where its own story meets the Gulf's and the world's.

Potters and grain-grinders settle at Subiya in Ubaid times (5500–4500 BCE), then Greeks settle well-watered Failaka, call it Ikaros, and leave coins and Dilmun seals behind.
Before any state, Kuwait's land gathers civilisations on its coast and islands, the deep roots the modern country claims as its own.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
