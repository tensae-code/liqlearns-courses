# Hands-on — Kuwait: Ubaid and Hellenistic Failaka

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: civilisations stood on Kuwait's own ground long before the state: Ubaid potters and grain-grinders settled at Subiya thousands of years ago, and Greeks later settled well-watered Failaka island, called it Ikaros, and left coins and seals behind. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of Ubaid potters shaping pots at Subiya, with Failaka island and a Greek ship across the water), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
