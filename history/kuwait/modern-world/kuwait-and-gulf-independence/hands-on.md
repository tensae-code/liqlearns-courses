# Hands-on — Kuwait and Gulf Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: as Britain withdrew, the Gulf peoples pressed for freedom, and Sheikh Abdullah Al Salem — the Father of Independence — led Kuwait to independence on 19 June 1961, with the other Gulf states following. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of Kuwait's flag rising on 19 June 1961 while crowds fill the street below), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
