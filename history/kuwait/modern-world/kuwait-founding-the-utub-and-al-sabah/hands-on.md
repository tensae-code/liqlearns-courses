# Hands-on — Kuwait's Founding: The Utub and Al Sabah

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Kuwait was born from a migration: drought in Najd drove the Utub families — Al Sabah, Al Khalifa and Al Jalahma — from al-Hadar to Zubara and on to al-Qurain, which became Kuwait, the little fort; in 1716 the families allied and shared the work of rule, trade and sea, and in 1718 the people pledged Sheikh Sabah bin Jaber as first ruler, founding a way of ruling by chosen leadership and shared counsel. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of the Utub families arriving at al-Qurain's little fort by the sea, told by a grandfather to his grandson Hamad), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
