# Kuwait's Founding: The Utub and Al Sabah

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — kuwait's founding: the utub and al sabah. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: Kuwait was born from a migration: drought in Najd drove the Utub families — Al Sabah, Al Khalifa and Al Jalahma — from al-Hadar to Zubara and on to al-Qurain, which became Kuwait, the little fort; in 1716 the families allied and shared the work of rule, trade and sea, and in 1718 the people pledged Sheikh Sabah bin Jaber as first ruler, founding a way of ruling by chosen leadership and shared counsel.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 3 Lesson 3: The Utub migration and the birth of Kuwait

Now the story narrows to one journey — Kuwait's own founding, told in the book by a grandfather to Hamad. In the mid-1600s, drought gripped Najd, in Arabia's heart. Three families — Al Sabah, Al Khalifa and Al Jalahma, allied together as the Utub — left al-Hadar and moved to Zubara, on the coast. Zubara did not hold them. They moved on, searching for a lasting home, and came at last to a small place on the northern shore: al-Qurain.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 3 Lesson 4: Naming Kuwait and choosing Al Sabah to rule

Al-Qurain took its name from its little hill, the first thing sailors saw from the sea. The settlement grew a small fort — a kuwayt, a little fort — and the name Kuwait stuck. In 1716 the three Utub families made their alliance firm and divided the work between them: rule, trade and the sea. Then came the choosing. In 1718 the people pledged Sheikh Sabah bin Jaber as their first ruler — not a conqueror imposed on them, but a leader they themselves raised. That choosing is the seed of Kuwait.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2 Lesson 2: The Utub migration and the birth of Kuwait's system of rule

When the Utub arrived, they brought more than families — they brought a way of ruling. Leadership among them was chosen, and rule was exercised with shared counsel: the families allied, divided the work, and raised their sheikh by pledge. The book reads Kuwait's whole political order back to this seed. The founding migration is therefore two stories at once: how Kuwait was settled, and how Kuwait came to be governed the way it is.
