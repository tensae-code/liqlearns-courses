# Kuwait: From Emirate to Modern State

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — kuwait: from emirate to modern state. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: Kuwait grew from emirate to modern state under its later rulers — Jaber Al Ahmad, the Amir of Hearts, who proposed the Gulf Council, opened Kuwait Towers in 1979 and won women their political rights, then Saad Al Abdullah, the Hero of Liberation — and today's Kuwait under Sheikh Meshal Al Ahmad practises balanced diplomacy and mediation, answering aggression with sovereignty held firm.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Kuwait's leaders and making the transformation from emirate to modern state

The transformation has faces. Jaber Al Ahmad, the Amir of Hearts, ruled from 1977 to 2006: he proposed the Gulf Council, opened Kuwait Towers over the bay in 1979, and in his reign Kuwaiti women won their political rights. After him, Saad Al Abdullah carries the book's proudest title — Hero of Liberation, for the years of occupation and return. Ruler by ruler, the book shows the emirate becoming a modern state: towers, councils, rights, and a liberation survived.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2 Lesson 5: Contemporary Kuwait — sovereignty, diplomacy and humanitarian work

The term ends in the present day. Today's Kuwait, under Sheikh Meshal Al Ahmad, practises balanced diplomacy and mediation between others, and gathers world honours for its humanitarian work. And the book writes the newest page without flinching: it names Iran's 2026 missile and drone attacks, and Kuwait's answer — sovereignty held firm. The history book closes where the learner lives: the story is still being written.
