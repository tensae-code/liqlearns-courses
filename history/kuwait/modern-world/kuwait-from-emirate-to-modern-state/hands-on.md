# Hands-on — Kuwait: From Emirate to Modern State

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Kuwait grew from emirate to modern state under its later rulers — Jaber Al Ahmad, the Amir of Hearts, who proposed the Gulf Council, opened Kuwait Towers in 1979 and won women their political rights, then Saad Al Abdullah, the Hero of Liberation — and today's Kuwait under Sheikh Meshal Al Ahmad practises balanced diplomacy and mediation, answering aggression with sovereignty held firm. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of Kuwait Towers rising over the bay in 1979, with the modern city's lights coming on around them), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
