# The United Nations: Founding, and Kuwait Joins

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — the united nations: founding, and kuwait joins. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: out of the Second World War's ruin, the world judged the League of Nations (1920) a failure and built the United Nations in 1945 instead; Kuwait joined in 1963, and its foreign minister Sheikh Sabah Al Ahmad Al Jaber Al Sabah raised Kuwait's flag at the organisation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 3 Lesson 2: The United Nations — founding, main organs, aims

The book tells the United Nations' founding through Kuwait's own flag. The Second World War ended in 1945, and the world judged its first peace-keeping body, the League of Nations of 1920, a failure — so it built a stronger one: the United Nations, with its founding, its main organs and its aims. Kuwait's chapter comes in 1963, when the young state joined — and its foreign minister, Sheikh Sabah Al Ahmad Al Jaber Al Sabah, later Kuwait's Amir, raised Kuwait's flag at the organisation himself. A world story, held at one end by a Kuwaiti hand.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 6: The United Nations organisation

The Grade 12 telling ends where the Grade 9 telling began: with the United Nations. Out of the second war's ruin, the victors built a new organisation to keep the peace their League of Nations had failed to keep. Kuwait's own flag would rise there in 1963 — but that is the earlier lesson's story. Here the UN stands as the world wars' last result: ruin answered by organisation.
