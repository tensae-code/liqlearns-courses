# Hands-on — The United Nations: Founding, and Kuwait Joins

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: out of the Second World War's ruin, the world judged the League of Nations (1920) a failure and built the United Nations in 1945 instead; Kuwait joined in 1963, and its foreign minister Sheikh Sabah Al Ahmad Al Jaber Al Sabah raised Kuwait's flag at the organisation. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of Kuwait's flag being raised at the United Nations in 1963 by its young foreign minister), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
