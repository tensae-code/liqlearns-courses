# Hands-on — Kuwait's Old Economy: Herding, Crafts and Trade

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: before oil, Kuwait and the Gulf lived by herds, hands and ships: herders watered flocks while women milked and churned in the leather samil, farmers worked palms and fields, craftspeople turned wool, clay and fibre into every household's goods, and builders raised the named sailing ships that fishermen worked from. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of women churning milk in a leather samil beside the tents while herders water the flock), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
