# Video script — Kuwait's Old Economy: Herding, Crafts and Trade

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Before oil, Kuwait and the Gulf lived by herds, hands and ships: herders watered flocks while women milked and churned in the leather samil, farmers worked palms and fields, craftspeople turned wool, clay and fibre into every household's goods, and builders raised the named sailing ships that fishermen worked from.

Look at the map. Here is Kuwait, at the head of the Gulf, where its own story meets the Gulf's and the world's.

Herders water the flocks while women milk them and churn the milk in the leather samil, and palm and field farming feed the settlements.
Wool is clipped, women gather to work it, and the old crafts turn local wool, clay and fibre into the goods every household needs.
Builders raise the Gulf's named sailing ships and fishermen work some of the oldest grounds in the region, the twin crafts that fed and carried the coast.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
