# English and French Voyages of Discovery

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — english and french voyages of discovery. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: England and France followed late into the discovery race, planting flags and claims across North America and the seas Portugal and Spain had opened — until the race for new lands ended at the earth's frozen ends, the North and South Poles, the last blank spaces on the map.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 2: The English and French discovery voyages

Latecomers can still take much. England and France followed Portugal and Spain into the discovery race — late, but hungry. They planted their flags and claims across North America and pushed into the seas the Iberians had opened. The map of the new world would not be two colours but four, and the rivalry among them is the seed of the colonial story that follows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 2: Discovery voyages to the North and South Poles

Every race has a finish line. When the coasts and continents were claimed, one blank remained on the map: the frozen ends of the earth. The discovery voyages reached the North and South Poles last — the hardest journeys, to the places no one could settle or farm. With the Poles, the age of blank spaces ends: the whole earth now stands on the map.
