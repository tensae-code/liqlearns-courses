# Hands-on — English and French Voyages of Discovery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: England and France followed late into the discovery race, planting flags and claims across North America and the seas Portugal and Spain had opened — until the race for new lands ended at the earth's frozen ends, the North and South Poles, the last blank spaces on the map. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of English and French flags planted on a North American shore, with a polar expedition's ship locked in ice beyond), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
