## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 3 Lesson 1: Birth of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation

Some organisations are born from wounds. Britain's Balfour promise of 1917 opened the Palestine question; the Nakba expelled Palestinians from their homes; in 1967 Jerusalem was lost. The pressure built until King Faisal called, in 1965, for the Muslim states to gather as one. Then, in 1969, fire struck al-Aqsa Mosque — and the gathering happened. The Islamic Conference, later renamed the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, was born: the Muslim states' one voice in the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 3 Lessons 4–5: A sketch of OIC summits — the first, fifth, thirteenth and fourteenth

An organisation lives in its meetings. The first summit gathered in Morocco in 1969, called over the al-Aqsa fire. The line of summits runs on across the decades — the fifth chaired by Kuwait's own ruler, a point of pride the book underlines — to the thirteenth at Istanbul in 2016 and the fourteenth after it. Count the summits and you count the organisation's life: born in crisis, kept alive by gathering.
