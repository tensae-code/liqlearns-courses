# Hands-on — The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation: Founding

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: the OIC was born from a wound: Britain's 1917 Balfour promise, the Nakba's expulsions and the 1967 loss of Jerusalem built the pressure; King Faisal called in 1965 for an Islamic gathering, and after the 1969 al-Aqsa fire the Islamic Conference — later the OIC — was born as the Muslim states' one voice, its summits running from Morocco in 1969, through the fifth that Kuwait's ruler chaired, to Istanbul's thirteenth in 2016 and the fourteenth after it. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of the first summit gathering in Morocco in 1969, delegates seated in one great circle), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
