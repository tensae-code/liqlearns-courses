# Lesson plan — The World Between the Wars, 1919–1939

**Course:** History — Kuwait · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The World Between the Wars, 1919–1939
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — The Modern World: Europe Outward, from Renaissance to World Wars (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the twenty years between the wars were a shaky peace: new states and rising dictators filled them, and they led straight back to war.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — The Modern World: Europe Outward, from Renaissance to World Wars (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **The First World War: Causes** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the twenty years between the wars were a shaky peace: new states and rising dictators filled them, and they led straight back to war.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this course's lessons, from the Kuwait story itself as the books tell it.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life, as Kuwait's books themselves work, starting from Kuwait and widening to the Gulf and the world.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: the twenty years between the wars were a shaky peace: new states and rising dictators filled them, and they led straight back to war. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
