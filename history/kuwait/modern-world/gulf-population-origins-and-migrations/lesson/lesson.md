# Gulf Population: Origins and Migrations

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — gulf population: origins and migrations. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: the Gulf's peoples were made by movement: Arab tribes migrated into the region — pushed by drought, thin rain, quarrels and hard trade — until the east coast filled with new communities that knit into new political bodies.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 3 Lesson 1: Origins of the Gulf states' peoples

Where did the Gulf's peoples come from? The book starts by untangling the words: a people's stock is where it traces itself from; migration is the moving; internal migration moves inside a land, external migration comes in from outside. Then the tracing begins — Arab tribes moving into the Gulf over the generations, settling its shores, until the coast filled with new communities. The Gulf's population is a story of arrivals.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 3 Lesson 2: Causes of internal migration in the Gulf

Why did they move? The book gives the pushes plainly: drought and thin rain that starved the herds, quarrels that split tribes, and hard trade that left families hungry. Push after push drove tribe after tribe toward Arabia's east coast, where water and the sea offered a living. And the moves did not end in camps: on the coast, the arriving tribes knit together into new political bodies — the beginnings of states.
