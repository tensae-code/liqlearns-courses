# Exploration: Causes and Results

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — exploration: causes and results. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: Europe sailed out because it wanted the spices and gold of the East without the middlemen and had gained the ships and compasses to try — and the results overturned the world: trade routes shifted from the Mediterranean to the oceans, prices and peoples were overturned, and the old world's map and power never looked the same.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 2: Causes of the geographic discovery movement

Why did Europeans suddenly sail into unknown seas? The book gives the causes in order. Europe wanted the East's spices and gold — but without the middlemen who taxed every bale on the land roads. At the same moment, it gained the means: better ships, and compasses to steer by. Want plus means equals motion: Europe pushed out onto the oceans, and the discovery movement began.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 2: Results of the voyages and geographic discoveries

Now count the results. The voyages moved the world's trade off the Mediterranean and onto the oceans — the old middle sea declined, the Atlantic ports rose. Prices were overturned as new goods and new silver flooded in. Peoples were overturned too, on every shore the ships touched. And the map itself changed: the old world's picture of the earth — and its balance of power — never looked the same again.
