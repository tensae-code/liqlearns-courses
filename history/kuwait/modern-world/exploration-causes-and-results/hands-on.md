# Hands-on — Exploration: Causes and Results

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Europe sailed out because it wanted the spices and gold of the East without the middlemen and had gained the ships and compasses to try — and the results overturned the world: trade routes shifted from the Mediterranean to the oceans, prices and peoples were overturned, and the old world's map and power never looked the same. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson (a picture of a laden spice ship leaving a Mediterranean port while a new ocean route is drawn across the map behind it), and say each part aloud as you draw it: who is there, where it happens, and what turned.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
