# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Before you read, do this: say out loud, in your own words, what you already think this story is about — from stone tools to farming. Keep your guess in mind. This lesson tells the story properly: human beings moved step by step from hunting and gathering in small cave-dwelling bands to farming, settled villages and tamed animals — and each step changed what people did to the earth around them.

Grade 9 opens the human story at the very beginning, as a story of people and the earth. First, small bands hunt and gather, dwelling in caves, taking only what the wild gives. Then the great turn: farming. People settle by water, tame animals, and build houses of mud and stone; around 5000 BCE, in Mesopotamia, they invent the wooden plough. Each step gives humans more — and takes more from the earth. Industry, and then the information revolution, turn the old partnership into a burden the earth carries. The whole environment book that follows is this story's latest chapter.
