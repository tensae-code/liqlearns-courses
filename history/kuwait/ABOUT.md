# About the History — Kuwait course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Kuwait's own
Ministry of Education History and Social Studies books. The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from
us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons
are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Kuwait inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **six levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know the Past, and the
   First Humans.** What history and civilisation are, sources
   and the researcher's steps, truth against forgery, and
   humanity's road from cave bands to farming.
2. **Level 2 — The Ancient Gulf and the Birth of Islam.**
   Dilmun, Magan and Failaka; Ubaid and Greek Kuwait; the old
   trade routes; Arabia before Islam; the Prophet's life, the
   Hijra and the unification of Arabia; the Rashidun caliphs.
3. **Level 3 — Islamic Civilisation: How It Worked, and What
   Europe Learned.** Its foundations and characteristics, its
   state, markets and cities, and Europe's borrowing.
4. **Level 4 — Kuwait Is Born: Peoples, the Old Economy and
   the Sea.** The migrations, the Utub and the choosing of Al
   Sabah, the other Gulf states, herding, crafts, ships and
   pearl diving.
5. **Level 5 — Kuwait and the Gulf in the Modern World.**
   History as the guard of national identity, the rulers'
   march to a modern state, the 1899 agreement, independence
   1961, the 1962 constitution, and the GCC, the OIC, the UN
   and OPEC.
6. **Level 6 — The Modern World: Europe Outward, from
   Renaissance to World Wars.** Renaissance, discovery
   voyages, colonialism, industry, the three revolutions,
   and the two world wars to the post-1945 world.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *First steps:* "First, learn the craft: what history is,
  how a fact travels from a dusty source into a written

  revolution and two world wars."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

**Grades 6–9 Social Studies** — History is taught inside a
widening frame: every unit starts from Kuwait, then widens —
to the Gulf states (Grade 6), the whole Arab homeland
(Grade 7), the Islamic world (Grade 8) and the world
(Grade 9). A grandfather-dialogue storyteller (Hamad and his
grandfather) carries the Grade 6 founding story; guide
characters lead the later grades (the Grade 7 Ambassador of
Knowledge, Urban Engineer and Crisis Watcher among them);
and a values unit, "Pauses with Good Kuwait", closes each
term. Grade 9 Term 2 tells the United Nations story through
Kuwait's own flag, raised at the UN in 1963 by the young
foreign minister who later became Amir. *(Student books.)*

**Grade 10, Kuwait alone** — Secondary History opens with
method — what history is, its sources, forgery versus
truth — then spends the term on Kuwait alone: roots, rulers
one by one (Jaber Al Ahmad "Amir of Hearts", Saad Al
Abdullah "Hero of Liberation"), and the march from emirate
to modern state, diplomacy and humanitarian work.
*(Student book.)*

**Grade 11, Islamic History** — a single narrative arc:
Arabia before the Message, the Prophet's life told event by
event, then caliph by caliph — with maps, timelines and a
short assessment (تقويم) closing each section. The Term-1
volume in hand holds Chapters 1–2 of the six-chapter course
its introduction describes. *(Student book.)*

**Grade 12, the modern world** — a Europe-outward arc in six
chapters across the year — Renaissance, discoveries,
colonialism, industry, revolutions, world wars — each
chapter split into concept, causes, course and results, with
the same introduction carried in both term books, ending at
the United Nations and "the features of our contemporary
world". *(Student books.)*
