# Muhammad Ali Pasha and the Building of Modern Egypt — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — An Albanian soldier from Macedonia rises in the Ottoman army, ends the Mamluks at the Cairo Citadel feast of 1811, and builds modern Egypt — self-sufficient economy, factories, schools, a European-style army and fleet, campaigns in Arabia, Greece and the Levant — until the London Treaty of 1840 and the firman of 1841 fix his house in Egypt and Sudan — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
