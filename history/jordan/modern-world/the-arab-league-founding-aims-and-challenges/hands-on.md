# Hands-on — The Arab League Founding Aims and Challenges

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The League of Arab States. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Founded in 1945 with its seat in Cairo under the Alexandria Protocol, first secretary-general the Egyptian Abdul Rahman Azzam; its council, secretariat and technical committees guard members' sovereignty, settle Arab disputes peacefully and help Arab states to independence — yet, the book admits, no binding consensus, rival national interests and unhealed crises limit it; Jordan hosts its summits again and again.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Founded in 1945 with its seat in Cairo under the Alexandria Protocol, first secretary-general the Egyptian Abdul Rahman Azzam; its council, secretariat and technical committees guard members' sovereignty, settle Arab disputes peacefully and help Arab states to independence — yet, the book admits, no binding consensus, rival national interests and unhealed crises limit it; Jordan hosts its summits again and again. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
