# The First World War Versailles and the Results — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Allies (France, Britain, Tsarist Russia, then the USA in 1917 despite its isolationism) face the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Bulgaria and the Ottoman state); Germany collapses in the autumn of 1918; at Paris the Big Four impose Versailles on Weimar Germany and Sèvres on the Ottomans (later replaced by Lausanne) — a peace so vindictive, the book warns, that it prepares the second war and the totalitarian regimes, communist, Nazi and Fascist; the class itself re-runs Versailles as a simulation, each group pleading a nation's case — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
