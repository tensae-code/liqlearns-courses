# Hands-on — The French Revolution Breaks Out 1789

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The French Revolution; Results of the French Revolution. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Bourbon absolutism, three estates and Rousseau's Social Contract arm the Third Estate; the Bastille falls and the revolution begins.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bourbon absolutism, three estates and Rousseau's Social Contract arm the Third Estate; the Bastille falls and the revolution begins. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
