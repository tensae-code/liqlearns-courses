## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Opening story** — In the municipal library a child finds the Hashemite medal of the Great Battle of Ma'an, struck for the forces of the Great Arab Revolt that helped make modern Jordan
2. **Journey in Ma'an Governorate** — A grandfather recalls Ma'an's part in the Great Arab Revolt and its great battle against the Turks; Emir Abdullah took the Ma'an palace as his residence in 1920, and it is now a national museum
3. **The Great Arab Revolt** — A school trip to the Aqaba museum opens the story: Sharif Hussein bin Ali declares the revolt in Mecca on 10 June 1916 for freedom and independence; Ottoman rule ends and Arab kingdoms rise in the Hijaz, Syria, Iraq and East Jordan — but Britain breaks its promises (Sykes–Picot, Balfour, the mandates)
4. **The Great Arab Revolt (the Arab Nahda)** — The Arabs do not dream of separation until the Ottoman state joins the Central Powers and Jamal Pasha "the Butcher" hangs Arab patriots in Beirut (1915) and Damascus (1916); the secret societies al-Fatat and al-Ahd choose Sharif Hussein in the Damascus Protocol (1915), thirty-five Arab deputies authorise him to lead, and his five letters trade promises with McMahon's five (July 1915–March 1916); on 10 June 1916 the revolt is proclaimed in Mecca, his sons Ali, Abdullah, Faysal and Zayd free the Hijaz except Medina's garrison, and at Ma'an in 1918 the Turkish garrisons fall — 343 heroes receive the Medal of Ma'an; meanwhile Britain signs Sykes-Picot with France and, as the Zionist movement (born at Basel, 1897) wins the Balfour Declaration, the promises collapse
5. **Jordan under the Syrian Arab Kingdom and the local governments** — Faysal enters Damascus in 1918 and builds the kingdom later called the Syrian Arab Kingdom; the King-Crane Commission tours Jordan in June–July 1919 asking the people their wishes; the Syrian General Congress (2 July 1919) speaks for all Bilad al-Sham — with Jordanian members from Karak, Ajloun and Ramtha — but San Remo (25 April 1920) imposes the mandates, splits Syria into Palestine, Lebanon and the rest, and binds Transjordan to the Balfour promise; Jordanian volunteers fight the French at Maysalun and in the south of Lebanon; then, in the vacuum, East Jordan governs itself (August 1920–April 1921): the Ajloun government at Irbid under Ali Khulqi al-Sharayri, the Salt government under Mazhar Arslan between Wadi al-Mujib and the Zarqa, and the Moab government at Karak — sealed by the Umm Qais conference of September 1920
6. **The Great Arab Revolt (the Arab Nahda)** — Jamal Pasha's gallows take thirty-seven Arab figures in Beirut and Damascus; Sharif Hussein — Abdul Hamid II's own appointee as Amir of Mecca in 1908, loyal to the Caliphate until the war — is chosen by the movement; the Damascus Protocol's map is spelled out (from the Mersin–Adana line at latitude 37 to the Indian Ocean, Aden excepted, and from Iran's borders to the Red Sea, with all foreign capitulations abolished and a defensive treaty with Britain); al-Fatat is founded in Paris in 1911 with Faysal among its members
7. **Jordan under the Syrian Arab Kingdom and the local governments** — As the vocational telling — Faysal's Damascus government, the King-Crane tour of Jordan (10 June–21 July 1919), the Syrian General Congress with its Karak, Ajloun and Ramtha members, San Remo's mandates, Jordanian volunteers at Maysalun, and the three local governments (Ajloun at Irbid, Salt, Moab at Karak) under Herbert Samuel's summons, August 1920–April 1921
