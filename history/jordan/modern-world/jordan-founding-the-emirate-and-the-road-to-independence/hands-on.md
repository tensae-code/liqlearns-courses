# Hands-on — Jordan Founding the Emirate and the Road to Independence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The founding of the Jordanian Emirate. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: France seizes Damascus and Syria's leaders beg Sharif Hussein to send a son; Abdullah reaches Amman and on 11 April 1921 forms the Council of Advisors under Rashid Tali', staffed by Syrian Istiqlal men; the 1928 Jerusalem agreement promises a Basic Law and hands legislation and executive power to the Amir, yet keeps British troops and an annual grant; the People's Party and the national conferences answer with a charter — reject Balfour, refuse to pay for Britain's Frontier Force, demand real elections and a government answerable to parliament; the 1935 Defence Law muzzles the opposition, until the March 1946 treaty ends the mandate; the state emblem, designed in 1934 at the founder's wish, is set over Raghadan Palace.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: France seizes Damascus and Syria's leaders beg Sharif Hussein to send a son; Abdullah reaches Amman and on 11 April 1921 forms the Council of Advisors under Rashid Tali', staffed by Syrian Istiqlal men; the 1928 Jerusalem agreement promises a Basic Law and hands legislation and executive power to the Amir, yet keeps British troops and an annual grant; the People's Party and the national conferences answer with a charter — reject Balfour, refuse to pay for Britain's Frontier Force, demand real elections and a government answerable to parliament; the 1935 Defence Law muzzles the opposition, until the March 1946 treaty ends the mandate; the state emblem, designed in 1934 at the founder's wish, is set over Raghadan Palace. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
