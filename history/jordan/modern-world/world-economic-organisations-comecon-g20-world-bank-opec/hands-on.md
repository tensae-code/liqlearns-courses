# Hands-on — World Economic Organisations Comecon G20 World Bank Opec

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Regional and international economic organisations. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: The Soviet answer to the West, COMECON (1949), organises the socialist economies; after the financial crises of the 1990s the G7 fathers the G20 (1999), nineteen states plus the EU; the World Bank in Washington lends against poverty; and when the oil companies cut posted prices in 1959 without asking, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, Iran and Venezuela found OPEC in 1960 — while the Arab exporters' own OAPEC struggles to match its weight.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Soviet answer to the West, COMECON (1949), organises the socialist economies; after the financial crises of the 1990s the G7 fathers the G20 (1999), nineteen states plus the EU; the World Bank in Washington lends against poverty; and when the oil companies cut posted prices in 1959 without asking, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, Iran and Venezuela found OPEC in 1960 — while the Arab exporters' own OAPEC struggles to match its weight. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
