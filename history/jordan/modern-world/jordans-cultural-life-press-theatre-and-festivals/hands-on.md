# Hands-on — Jordans Cultural Life Press Theatre and Festivals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Cultural life since 1921. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: The founder-king is a poet who fills his court with writers; Jordan's first newspaper, al-Haqq Ya'lu, is printed at Ma'an in 1920–21 carrying the revolt's principles, the press reaches Amman in 1923 and the official al-Sharq al-Arabi follows (renamed 1926); a Culture and Arts Department (1966) becomes a Ministry of Culture and Youth (1977); Jordan Television opens in black and white at Umm al-Hiran in 1968; Prince Hassan founds the Arab Thought Forum in 1981; Amman is Arab Capital of Culture in 2002; and the Jerash, Azraq and Fuheis festivals and the Reading-for-All Family Library carry culture to every town.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The founder-king is a poet who fills his court with writers; Jordan's first newspaper, al-Haqq Ya'lu, is printed at Ma'an in 1920–21 carrying the revolt's principles, the press reaches Amman in 1923 and the official al-Sharq al-Arabi follows (renamed 1926); a Culture and Arts Department (1966) becomes a Ministry of Culture and Youth (1977); Jordan Television opens in black and white at Umm al-Hiran in 1968; Prince Hassan founds the Arab Thought Forum in 1981; Amman is Arab Capital of Culture in 2002; and the Jerash, Azraq and Fuheis festivals and the Reading-for-All Family Library carry culture to every town. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
