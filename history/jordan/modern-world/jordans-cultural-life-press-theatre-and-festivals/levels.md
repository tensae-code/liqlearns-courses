# Jordans Cultural Life Press Theatre and Festivals — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — The founder-king is a poet who fills his court with writers; Jordan's first newspaper, al-Haqq Ya'lu, is printed at Ma'an in 1920–21 carrying the revolt's principles, the press reaches Amman in 1923 and the official al-Sharq al-Arabi follows (renamed 1926); a Culture and Arts Department (1966) becomes a Ministry of Culture and Youth (1977); Jordan Television opens in black and white at Umm al-Hiran in 1968; Prince Hassan founds the Arab Thought Forum in 1981; Amman is Arab Capital of Culture in 2002; and the Jerash, Azraq and Fuheis festivals and the Reading-for-All Family Library carry culture to every town — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
