# European Colonialism in the Arab World

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Colonialism in the Arab homeland before the First World War; Colonialism in the Arab homeland after the First World War. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Colonialism in the Arab homeland before the First World War

Pope Alexander VI splits the world between Spain and Portugal in 1493; the Arab navigator Ibn Majid, born at Ras al-Khaimah in 1432, pilots Vasco da Gama to India in 1498 and the Portuguese seize the Arabian coasts; Napoleon storms Egypt and exposes Ottoman weakness; foreign capitulations and Orientalist scholarship pry the empire open; Britain garrisons Kuwait in 1793 while the Qawasim fight her in the Gulf; Dey Hussein's fly-whisk insult hands France her excuse, and Algiers falls in 1830.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colonialism in the Arab homeland after the First World War

France works to erase Arabic in the Maghreb, seizes the awqaf and fans Arab against Berber; Italy whispers "peaceful penetration" over Libya; in the East, Britain issues the Balfour Declaration on 2 November 1917 promising a Jewish national home in a Palestine it does not own; Damascus answers with the Syrian General Congress of March 1920 crowning Faysal king of a united Syria — and San Remo tears the country into mandates, France splitting Syria into statelets and handing Alexandretta to Turkey in 1939.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
