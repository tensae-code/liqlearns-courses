# Hands-on — European Colonialism in the Arab World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Colonialism in the Arab homeland before the First World War; Colonialism in the Arab homeland after the First World War. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Pope Alexander VI splits the world between Spain and Portugal in 1493; the Arab navigator Ibn Majid, born at Ras al-Khaimah in 1432, pilots Vasco da Gama to India in 1498 and the Portuguese seize the Arabian coasts; Napoleon storms Egypt and exposes Ottoman weakness; foreign capitulations and Orientalist scholarship pry the empire open; Britain garrisons Kuwait in 1793 while the Qawasim fight her in the Gulf; Dey Hussein's fly-whisk insult hands France her excuse, and Algiers falls in 1830.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Pope Alexander VI splits the world between Spain and Portugal in 1493; the Arab navigator Ibn Majid, born at Ras al-Khaimah in 1432, pilots Vasco da Gama to India in 1498 and the Portuguese seize the Arabian coasts; Napoleon storms Egypt and exposes Ottoman weakness; foreign capitulations and Orientalist scholarship pry the empire open; Britain garrisons Kuwait in 1793 while the Qawasim fight her in the Gulf; Dey Hussein's fly-whisk insult hands France her excuse, and Algiers falls in 1830. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
