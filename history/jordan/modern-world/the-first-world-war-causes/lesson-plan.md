# Lesson plan — The First World War Causes

**Course:** History — Jordan · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The First World War Causes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Empires, Revolutions, Colonialism and Liberation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Two alliance blocs face off, a Serbian student assassinates Archduke Franz Ferdinand, and in August 1914 Europe is at war; Prince Faysal, the Sharif's son, leads the Arab Revolt's northern army into Damascus.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Empires, Revolutions, Colonialism and Liberation — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The world in the twentieth century — the First World War** — Two alliance blocs face off, a Serbian student assassinates Archduke Franz Ferdinand, and in August 1914 Europe is at war; Prince Faysal, the Sharif's son, leads the Arab Revolt's northern army into Damascus
2. **The First World War (1914–1918), the causes** — Colonial rivalry, the alliance blocs, and nationalism (Italian and German unity achieved, Balkan nationalisms pulling Russia, protector of the Slavs, against Austria-Hungary) load the gun through the Morocco crises and the Balkan crises of 1908–1913; a Serbian student fires it, assassinating Archduke Franz Ferdinand at Sarajevo in June 1914

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Jordan, the region and the world you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
