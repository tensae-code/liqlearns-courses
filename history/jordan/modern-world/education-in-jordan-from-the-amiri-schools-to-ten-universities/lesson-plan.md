# Lesson plan — Education in Jordan from the Amiri Schools to Ten Universities

**Course:** History — Jordan · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Education in Jordan from the Amiri Schools to Ten Universities
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Jordan Made: From the Great Arab Revolt to the Modern Kingdom (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Emirate legislates early (Primary Instruction Law 1926, the Ma'arif system 1939), staffed by teachers from Syria and Palestine, beside the missionary schools (Mutran, Terra Sancta, the Rosary Sisters) and the lone Sanai' vocational school in Amman; unity with the West Bank (1950) multiplies schools — Khaduri in Tulkarm, Shobak's agricultural school, the Amman Teachers' Institute (1952) — and free textbooks follow; the 1967 displacement of 350,000 forces the double-shift school day; the 1994 Education Law, schools for deaf and blind pupils, military-culture schools and the Queen Rania Award (2006) mark the modern age; higher education begins with scholarships to Arab universities and ends, for now, with ten public universities from the Hashemite in Zarqa to the German-Jordanian in Na'ur.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — Jordan Made: From the Great Arab Revolt to the Modern Kingdom — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).
