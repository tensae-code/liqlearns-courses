# Education in Jordan from the Amiri Schools to Ten Universities

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Education 1921 to today. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Education 1921 to today

The Emirate legislates early (Primary Instruction Law 1926, the Ma'arif system 1939), staffed by teachers from Syria and Palestine, beside the missionary schools (Mutran, Terra Sancta, the Rosary Sisters) and the lone Sanai' vocational school in Amman; unity with the West Bank (1950) multiplies schools — Khaduri in Tulkarm, Shobak's agricultural school, the Amman Teachers' Institute (1952) — and free textbooks follow; the 1967 displacement of 350,000 forces the double-shift school day; the 1994 Education Law, schools for deaf and blind pupils, military-culture schools and the Queen Rania Award (2006) mark the modern age; higher education begins with scholarships to Arab universities and ends, for now, with ten public universities from the Hashemite in Zarqa to the German-Jordanian in Na'ur.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
