## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Liberation in South Yemen and the Arab Gulf. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Liberation in South Yemen and the Arab Gulf

Sinan Pasha takes Aden for the Ottomans in 1538 but the interior stays its own; the National Front forms in 1963, a bomb at Aden airport wounds the British High Commissioner, and Britain cannot hold the South; in the Gulf, Britain brands the Trucial Coast the "Pirate Coast", binds the sheikhdoms with perpetual protection treaties and forbids oil concessions without her consent — then, days before the Emirates unite in 1971, Iran seizes the Greater and Lesser Tunbs and lands on Abu Musa, as Prime Minister Hoveyda announces it to his parliament; Qatar and Bahrain nurse their own national movements around the British Political Resident's base, and Oman — Ya'ariba imams, Sultan bin Ahmad's treaty of 1798, the Dhofar revolution — fights its long road out of the Portuguese and British shadow.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
