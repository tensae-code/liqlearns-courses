# Japans Two Renaissances Meiji and After 1945 — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — An archipelago of thousands of islands, 378,000 km² and some 127 million people; samurai warlords sideline the emperors into a dual rule until the Meiji movement under Mutsuhito — slogan "rich country, strong army" — abolishes feudalism and centralises the state; destroyed in 1945, three million dead and its cities in ruins, Japan rises a second time on American aid, the world's most productive and disciplined workers, care for inventors, and an education system — six compulsory primary years, 9.4% of government spending in 2014 — that guards Japanese values while it modernises — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
