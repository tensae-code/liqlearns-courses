# Hands-on — Japans Two Renaissances Meiji and After 1945

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Japan. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: An archipelago of thousands of islands, 378,000 km² and some 127 million people; samurai warlords sideline the emperors into a dual rule until the Meiji movement under Mutsuhito — slogan "rich country, strong army" — abolishes feudalism and centralises the state; destroyed in 1945, three million dead and its cities in ruins, Japan rises a second time on American aid, the world's most productive and disciplined workers, care for inventors, and an education system — six compulsory primary years, 9.4% of government spending in 2014 — that guards Japanese values while it modernises.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: An archipelago of thousands of islands, 378,000 km² and some 127 million people; samurai warlords sideline the emperors into a dual rule until the Meiji movement under Mutsuhito — slogan "rich country, strong army" — abolishes feudalism and centralises the state; destroyed in 1945, three million dead and its cities in ruins, Japan rises a second time on American aid, the world's most productive and disciplined workers, care for inventors, and an education system — six compulsory primary years, 9.4% of government spending in 2014 — that guards Japanese values while it modernises. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
