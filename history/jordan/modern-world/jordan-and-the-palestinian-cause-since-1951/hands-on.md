# Hands-on — Jordan and the Palestinian Cause Since 1951

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Jordan's stance since 1951. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Israel raids the West Bank's frontline villages through the 1950s–60s and diverts the Jordan River, driving the Arabs to the Cairo summit; Resolution 242 (22 November 1967) sets the land-for-peace terms Jordan still invokes; after Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon scatters the PLO to Tunis, Algiers and South Yemen, Amman and the PLO talk (1983–86) over Resolutions 242 and 338; Oslo follows in 1993; the second intifada brings Gaza's withdrawal, blockade and wars — and Jordan answers with field hospitals in the West Bank and Gaza under the Jordan Hashemite Charity Organisation and Abdullah II's diplomacy in every forum.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Israel raids the West Bank's frontline villages through the 1950s–60s and diverts the Jordan River, driving the Arabs to the Cairo summit; Resolution 242 (22 November 1967) sets the land-for-peace terms Jordan still invokes; after Israel's 1982 invasion of Lebanon scatters the PLO to Tunis, Algiers and South Yemen, Amman and the PLO talk (1983–86) over Resolutions 242 and 338; Oslo follows in 1993; the second intifada brings Gaza's withdrawal, blockade and wars — and Jordan answers with field hospitals in the West Bank and Gaza under the Jordan Hashemite Charity Organisation and Abdullah II's diplomacy in every forum. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
