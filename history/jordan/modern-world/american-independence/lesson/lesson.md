# American Independence

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: The American Revolution; Stages of the American Revolution. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — The American Revolution

Europeans flood the New World and displace its native peoples; after the Seven Years' War Britain taxes the colonies, and Boston answers by dumping the tea ships' cargo in the harbour.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stages of the American Revolution

Bunker Hill, 17 June 1775; the Second Continental Congress at Philadelphia raises an army under Washington; Saratoga turns the war; the Peace of Paris in 1783 recognises the first state to throw off European colonialism.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Europeans flood the New World and displace its native peoples; after the Seven Years' War Britain taxes the colonies, and Boston answers by dumping the tea ships' cargo in the harbour. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: American Independence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
