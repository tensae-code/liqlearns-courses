# Hands-on — The European Union from Coal and Steel to the Euro

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The European Union. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: A ruined Europe is rescued by America's Marshall Plan (1947); the Coal and Steel Community grows into a union of — after Britain's exit in 2020 — 27 states, with a parliament, a commission, a development fund and a common market, and one money: the euro, launched in bank dealings in 1999 and in pockets in 2002; Jordan's ties run from diplomatic relations in 1977 to the 2002 Association Agreement aiming at a free-trade area.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A ruined Europe is rescued by America's Marshall Plan (1947); the Coal and Steel Community grows into a union of — after Britain's exit in 2020 — 27 states, with a parliament, a commission, a development fund and a common market, and one money: the euro, launched in bank dealings in 1999 and in pockets in 2002; Jordan's ties run from diplomatic relations in 1977 to the 2002 Association Agreement aiming at a free-trade area. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
