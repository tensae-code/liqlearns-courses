# Arab Liberation Movements the Levant and Iraq — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Syria answers partition with revolts at Deir ez-Zor (1921), Jabal Druze (1922) and the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925; Jordanians reject the 1928 treaty with Britain as domination and hold five national congresses (1928–1933) under the Jordanian Arab Party of Muhammad Subhi Abu Ghanima; Iraq explodes in the 1920 Revolution against British exploitation, and King Ghazi (1933–1939) defies Britain with his own radio station defending Palestine before dying in a car crash; after 1941 Lebanon wins independence in 1943 and the last French soldier leaves Syria in 1946 — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
