# Hands-on — The United Nations Founding Organs and Jordans Role

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The United Nations; Jordan in the United Nations. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: The League of Nations (1919, Geneva) fails — America never joins and no war is prevented — so in 1945 the UN rises in New York with 193 members and six official languages; the Security Council's fifteen members include five with the veto; agencies fan out — ILO in Geneva, FAO in Rome, IAEA in Vienna, UNRWA headquartered in Amman for Palestine's refugees; Jordan, a member since 1955, sends peacekeepers worldwide, sits on the Security Council in 1965–66, 1982–83 and 2014–15, and King Abdullah II addresses the General Assembly's 79th session in 2024.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The League of Nations (1919, Geneva) fails — America never joins and no war is prevented — so in 1945 the UN rises in New York with 193 members and six official languages; the Security Council's fifteen members include five with the veto; agencies fan out — ILO in Geneva, FAO in Rome, IAEA in Vienna, UNRWA headquartered in Amman for Palestine's refugees; Jordan, a member since 1955, sends peacekeepers worldwide, sits on the Security Council in 1965–66, 1982–83 and 2014–15, and King Abdullah II addresses the General Assembly's 79th session in 2024. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
