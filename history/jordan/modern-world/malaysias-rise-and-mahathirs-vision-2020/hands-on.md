# Hands-on — Malaysias Rise and Mahathirs Vision 2020

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Malaysia. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: A trading crossroads that welcomed Arab and Indian merchants for centuries outgrows colonial rule, Singapore's separation and the 1997 Asian crisis by diversifying — cars, electronics, steel, textiles; Mahathir Mohamad, doctor and prime minister, sets a thirty-year plan (1990–2020) to stand level with the West on world-class education and social justice, planned by a committee of national and foreign experts; Jordan and Malaysia exchange embassies from 1965, Malaysia's first in Amman in 1994.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A trading crossroads that welcomed Arab and Indian merchants for centuries outgrows colonial rule, Singapore's separation and the 1997 Asian crisis by diversifying — cars, electronics, steel, textiles; Mahathir Mohamad, doctor and prime minister, sets a thirty-year plan (1990–2020) to stand level with the West on world-class education and social justice, planned by a committee of national and foreign experts; Jordan and Malaysia exchange embassies from 1965, Malaysia's first in Amman in 1994. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
