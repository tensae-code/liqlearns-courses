# Hands-on — The Second World War the Pacific and the Results

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The course, the Arabs' war, and the results. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: In 1941 Germany invades the Soviet Union, Japan seizes Hong Kong, the Philippines and Singapore and bombs Pearl Harbor, dragging America in; El Alamein turns the desert war in 1942–43; the Arab lands fight under mandates — Rashid Ali al-Gaylani's Iraq revolts against Britain in May 1941 with German support, Allied forces take Syria and Lebanon from Vichy France, and Jordanian soldiers march in London's Victory Parade on 8 June 1946 on the road to Jordan's independence the same year; the toll: the totalitarian regimes fall, some 60 million die, crimes are committed against millions of Jews, Roma, Russians and Japanese, and women's public role is transformed.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In 1941 Germany invades the Soviet Union, Japan seizes Hong Kong, the Philippines and Singapore and bombs Pearl Harbor, dragging America in; El Alamein turns the desert war in 1942–43; the Arab lands fight under mandates — Rashid Ali al-Gaylani's Iraq revolts against Britain in May 1941 with German support, Allied forces take Syria and Lebanon from Vichy France, and Jordanian soldiers march in London's Victory Parade on 8 June 1946 on the road to Jordan's independence the same year; the toll: the totalitarian regimes fall, some 60 million die, crimes are committed against millions of Jews, Roma, Russians and Japanese, and women's public role is transformed. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
