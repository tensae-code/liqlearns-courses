# Hands-on — Jordan Since 1999 Abdullah Ii and the Reform Papers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Political life since 1999. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Abdullah II takes the oath on 7 February 1999; the 14th parliament (2003–2007) seats the first women's quota of six; the National Centre for Human Rights opens in 2002 and the National Agenda launches in 2005; the king's Discussion Papers invite the nation to argue its democratic road; after the royal committee of 26 April 2011 the constitution gains a Constitutional Court and an Independent Election Commission, and the 2015 Decentralisation Law elects governorate councils.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Abdullah II takes the oath on 7 February 1999; the 14th parliament (2003–2007) seats the first women's quota of six; the National Centre for Human Rights opens in 2002 and the National Agenda launches in 2005; the king's Discussion Papers invite the nation to argue its democratic road; after the royal committee of 26 April 2011 the constitution gains a Constitutional Court and an Independent Election Commission, and the 2015 Decentralisation Law elects governorate councils. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
