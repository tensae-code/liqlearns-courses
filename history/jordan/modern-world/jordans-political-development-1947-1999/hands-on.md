# Hands-on — Jordans Political Development 1947 1999

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Political development 1947–1999. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Independence leads into the 1948 war, in which the Arabs lose some 78% of historic Palestine, then the Unity of the Two Banks (1950); Abdullah I is assassinated at al-Aqsa during Friday prayer on 20 July 1951; Talal follows, then Hussein — born in Amman on 14 November 1935, schooled at Victoria College, Alexandria — is proclaimed in 1952 as Talal's health fails; the army's command is arabised, and the first party government (Suleiman al-Nabulsi, 1956–57) rises and falls; June 1967 costs Jordan 696 martyrs, 421 wounded, 520 prisoners and 350,000 displaced — but at Karameh on 21 March 1968 the Arab Army, losing 86 martyrs, breaks the myth of Israel's invincibility; Jordanian armour fights on the Golan in October 1973; parliament sleeps until 1984, then the 1989 elections, the 1991 National Charter, the end of martial law and the 1992 Parties Law reopen political life; Hussein dies on 7 February 1999 and the world fills Amman for "the funeral of the century".

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Independence leads into the 1948 war, in which the Arabs lose some 78% of historic Palestine, then the Unity of the Two Banks (1950); Abdullah I is assassinated at al-Aqsa during Friday prayer on 20 July 1951; Talal follows, then Hussein — born in Amman on 14 November 1935, schooled at Victoria College, Alexandria — is proclaimed in 1952 as Talal's health fails; the army's command is arabised, and the first party government (Suleiman al-Nabulsi, 1956–57) rises and falls; June 1967 costs Jordan 696 martyrs, 421 wounded, 520 prisoners and 350,000 displaced — but at Karameh on 21 March 1968 the Arab Army, losing 86 martyrs, breaks the myth of Israel's invincibility; Jordanian armour fights on the Golan in October 1973; parliament sleeps until 1984, then the 1989 elections, the 1991 National Charter, the end of martial law and the 1992 Parties Law reopen political life; Hussein dies on 7 February 1999 and the world fills Amman for "the funeral of the century". Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
