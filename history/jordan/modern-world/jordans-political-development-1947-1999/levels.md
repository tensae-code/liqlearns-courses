# Jordans Political Development 1947 1999 — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Independence leads into the 1948 war, in which the Arabs lose some 78% of historic Palestine, then the Unity of the Two Banks (1950); Abdullah I is assassinated at al-Aqsa during Friday prayer on 20 July 1951; Talal follows, then Hussein — born in Amman on 14 November 1935, schooled at Victoria College, Alexandria — is proclaimed in 1952 as Talal's health fails; the army's command is arabised, and the first party government (Suleiman al-Nabulsi, 1956–57) rises and falls; June 1967 costs Jordan 696 martyrs, 421 wounded, 520 prisoners and 350,000 displaced — but at Karameh on 21 March 1968 the Arab Army, losing 86 martyrs, breaks the myth of Israel's invincibility; Jordanian armour fights on the Golan in October 1973; parliament sleeps until 1984, then the 1989 elections, the 1991 National Charter, the end of martial law and the 1992 Parties Law reopen political life; Hussein dies on 7 February 1999 and the world fills Amman for "the funeral of the century" — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
