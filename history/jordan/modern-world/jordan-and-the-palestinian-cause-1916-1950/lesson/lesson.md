# Jordan and the Palestinian Cause 1916 1950

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Jordan's stance 1916–1950. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Jordan's stance 1916–1950

Zionism organises at Basel in 1897; Britain plants settlements and arms the newcomers, and Palestine answers with the revolts of 1920, al-Buraq 1929 and the Great Revolt of 1936–39; Sharif Hussein refuses to sign Palestine away ("I do not accept…"); Amir Abdullah protests Balfour to Churchill in Jerusalem in 1921 and demands Palestine's and Transjordan's unity and Britain's wartime promises kept; the Peel map of 1937 proposes partition; 1948 brings war, the armistices of 1949, the West Bank under Jordan and Gaza under Egypt.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
