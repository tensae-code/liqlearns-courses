# Jordan and the Palestinian Cause 1916 1950 — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — Zionism organises at Basel in 1897; Britain plants settlements and arms the newcomers, and Palestine answers with the revolts of 1920, al-Buraq 1929 and the Great Revolt of 1936–39; Sharif Hussein refuses to sign Palestine away ("I do not accept…"); Amir Abdullah protests Balfour to Churchill in Jerusalem in 1921 and demands Palestine's and Transjordan's unity and Britain's wartime promises kept; the Peel map of 1937 proposes partition; 1948 brings war, the armistices of 1949, the West Bank under Jordan and Gaza under Egypt — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
