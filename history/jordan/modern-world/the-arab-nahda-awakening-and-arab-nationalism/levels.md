# The Arab Nahda Awakening and Arab Nationalism — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — After centuries of Ottoman isolation, the French campaign of 1798–1801, Muhammad Ali's reforms, student missions and missionary schools carry European ideas of freedom, constitution and nation into Arabic; Tahtawi preaches liberty and equality, al-Shidyaq social justice; the Sanusiyya in Libya and the Mahdiyya in Sudan renew Islam as resistance; and Sharif Hussein of Mecca, Amir from 1908, becomes the pioneer of Arab nationalism — "the Arabs are Arabs before they are Muslims or Christians or Jews" — whose Great Arab Revolt founds the kingdoms of the Hijaz, Syria and Iraq and the Emirate of Transjordan — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
