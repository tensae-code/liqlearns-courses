# Hands-on — The Industrial Revolution What Changed and Why England First

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The Industrial Revolution. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Feudal Europe of nobles, clergy and commons gives way to machines; the revolution starts in England first, fattened on capital gathered from colonies in Asia, Africa and the New World.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Feudal Europe of nobles, clergy and commons gives way to machines; the revolution starts in England first, fattened on capital gathered from colonies in Asia, Africa and the New World. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
