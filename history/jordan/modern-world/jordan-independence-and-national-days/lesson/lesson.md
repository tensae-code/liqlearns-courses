# Jordan Independence and National Days

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Our national occasions; King Abdullah II ibn al-Hussein; Development in my homeland; Kings of Jordan; The Jordanian state; The state's civilisational achievements. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our national occasions

The Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan became independent on 25 May 1946, and pupils celebrate Independence Day, the King's Accession Day (9 June) and Flag Day.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — King Abdullah II ibn al-Hussein

Abdullah, eldest son of King Hussein, is born in Amman in 1962, marries Rania in 1993, and takes his constitutional powers as King on 7 February 1999.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Development in my homeland

The same place photographed in past and present shows development as change for the better over time — housing, transport and education change, and Jordan Valley (Ghor) farming is renewed with modern irrigation.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — Kings of Jordan

Emir Abdullah founds the state, independence is proclaimed as the Hashemite Kingdom, the two banks unite in 1950, Abdullah I is martyred at al-Aqsa in 1951, and under Hussein the army command is Arabised in 1956.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Jordanian state

From the founding of the Emirate of East Jordan through popular marches demanding full independence, the 1952 constitution, King Abdullah I martyred at al-Aqsa's door on 20 July 1951, the unity of the two banks, and Jordan helping found the Arab League in 1945.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 6 — The state's civilisational achievements

Al-Salt Secondary School, the Emirate's oldest school; the first health department in 1921; the University of Jordan in 1962; King Hussein Medical City — a young state builds schools, hospitals and universities.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
