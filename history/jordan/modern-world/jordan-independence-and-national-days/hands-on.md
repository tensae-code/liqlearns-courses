# Hands-on — Jordan Independence and National Days

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Our national occasions; King Abdullah II ibn al-Hussein; Development in my homeland; Kings of Jordan; The Jordanian state; The state's civilisational achievements. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: The Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan became independent on 25 May 1946, and pupils celebrate Independence Day, the King's Accession Day (9 June) and Flag Day.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan became independent on 25 May 1946, and pupils celebrate Independence Day, the King's Accession Day (9 June) and Flag Day. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
