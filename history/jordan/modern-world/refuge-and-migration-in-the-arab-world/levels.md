# Refuge and Migration in the Arab World — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — A refugee flees a well-founded fear of persecution and cannot go home; the UNHCR guards the right of asylum; the Arab world's waves are traced to colonial legacies and wars — Israel keeps displacing Palestinians after 1967, barring return and building on the West Bank; Iraqis flee their decades of crises; and Jordan and Turkey become the states most affected by Syria's war, hosting millions of registered Syrian refugees — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
