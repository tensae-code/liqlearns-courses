# Refuge and Migration in the Arab World

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Asylum (refuge) and migration. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Asylum (refuge) and migration

A refugee flees a well-founded fear of persecution and cannot go home; the UNHCR guards the right of asylum; the Arab world's waves are traced to colonial legacies and wars — Israel keeps displacing Palestinians after 1967, barring return and building on the West Bank; Iraqis flee their decades of crises; and Jordan and Turkey become the states most affected by Syria's war, hosting millions of registered Syrian refugees.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
