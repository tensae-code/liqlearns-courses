# Hands-on — Refuge and Migration in the Arab World

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Asylum (refuge) and migration. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: A refugee flees a well-founded fear of persecution and cannot go home; the UNHCR guards the right of asylum; the Arab world's waves are traced to colonial legacies and wars — Israel keeps displacing Palestinians after 1967, barring return and building on the West Bank; Iraqis flee their decades of crises; and Jordan and Turkey become the states most affected by Syria's war, hosting millions of registered Syrian refugees.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A refugee flees a well-founded fear of persecution and cannot go home; the UNHCR guards the right of asylum; the Arab world's waves are traced to colonial legacies and wars — Israel keeps displacing Palestinians after 1967, barring return and building on the West Bank; Iraqis flee their decades of crises; and Jordan and Turkey become the states most affected by Syria's war, hosting millions of registered Syrian refugees. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
