# The Cold War What It Was and What It Did to Africa

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: The Cold War; The Cold War (1945–1991). As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Cold War

NATO (1949) faces the Warsaw Pact; the Bandung Conference of 1955 answers with non-alignment — Nasser, Tito and Nehru refuse both camps.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Cold War (1945–1991)

Two victors glare across a divided world — capitalist democratic America and her NATO allies against communist Soviet Russia and the Warsaw Pact — fighting by proxy: Korea (1950–1953), Vietnam (1955–1975), the Cuban missile crisis, and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (1979–1989); Gorbachev answers with glasnost and perestroika, the Berlin Wall falls in November 1989, and the cold war ends without the hot one.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
