# From Stone Tools to Farming

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Prehistoric ages, Old/Middle/New Stone Age and Copper Age; Once Upon a Time (كان يا ما كان); Development of housing in my homeland. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Prehistoric ages, Old/Middle/New Stone Age and Copper Age

Cave-dwelling hunters discover fire, settle, farm and grind grain, and copper tools found at Abu Ghosh north of the Dead Sea mark the metal turn.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Once Upon a Time (كان يا ما كان)

Rashid finds grandfather Mustafa's old oil lamp; long ago people lived in caves, struck stones to discover fire, made tools of stone and bone, and hunted and gathered.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Development of housing in my homeland

Shelter changes from cave to hair tent to mud house to stone house as people settle.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Cave-dwelling hunters discover fire, settle, farm and grind grain, and copper tools found at Abu Ghosh north of the Dead Sea mark the metal turn. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: From Stone Tools to Farming. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
