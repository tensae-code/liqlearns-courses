# Lesson plan — From Stone Tools to Farming

**Course:** History — Jordan · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** From Stone Tools to Farming
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Ancient World and Jordan's Own Ancient Ground (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Cave-dwelling hunters discover fire, settle, farm and grind grain, and copper tools found at Abu Ghosh north of the Dead Sea mark the metal turn.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The Ancient World and Jordan's Own Ancient Ground — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Prehistoric ages, Old/Middle/New Stone Age and Copper Age** — Cave-dwelling hunters discover fire, settle, farm and grind grain, and copper tools found at Abu Ghosh north of the Dead Sea mark the metal turn
2. **Once Upon a Time (كان يا ما كان)** — Rashid finds grandfather Mustafa's old oil lamp; long ago people lived in caves, struck stones to discover fire, made tools of stone and bone, and hunted and gathered
3. **Development of housing in my homeland** — Shelter changes from cave to hair tent to mud house to stone house as people settle

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **How We Know About the Past** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Cave-dwelling hunters discover fire, settle, farm and grind grain, and copper tools found at Abu Ghosh north of the Dead Sea mark the metal turn.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Jordan, the region and the world you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.
