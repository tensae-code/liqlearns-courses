## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Cuneiform writing** — Around 3500 BCE Mesopotamian scribes press wedge marks into wet clay tablets and writing is born
2. **Bronze Age and Iron Age** — Bronze (copper + tin) makes stronger weapons, then iron farming tools feed more people and world trade stirs
3. **Writing and means of communication** — People draw things on walls, clay and stone, write on bark then paper with pens, and today on computers; messages once travelled by smoke and carrier pigeon (zagil)
4. **The Sumerian civilisation** — On the Euphrates' bank the Sumerians invent irrigation canals, the plough and cuneiform writing; King Gilgamesh of Uruk becomes an epic, half man and half god, and the ziggurat of Ur climbs over the flood plain
5. **The Akkadian civilisation** — Before Naram-Sin's Victory Stele the learner meets the first empire: Akkad's kings bind the city-states, spread trade roads and security — then tyranny, priestly quarrels and Sumerian revolts wear the empire down
6. **The Babylonian civilisation** — In the Louvre's halls, Hammurabi's Babylon — the king who united the two river lands under law and justice; his people carve the Lion of Babylon, draw land maps on clay, chart the stars and compound medicines
7. **The Assyrian civilisation** — In a library of clay tablets, Assyria the military civilisation: Sargon II builds Khorsabad, Ashurbanipal gathers the Nineveh library, and carved palaces record every conquest
8. **The Chaldean civilisation** — An excavator reads Babylon's second rise: Nebuchadnezzar II makes Chaldean Babylon flourish in arms, building and art — the Hanging Gardens among its wonders — until weak kings and inner wars open the gate to Cyrus of Persia
