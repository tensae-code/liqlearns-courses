# Ancient Civilisations Mesopotamia

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Cuneiform writing; Bronze Age and Iron Age; Writing and means of communication; The Sumerian civilisation; The Akkadian civilisation; The Babylonian civilisation; The Assyrian civilisation; The Chaldean civilisation. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cuneiform writing

Around 3500 BCE Mesopotamian scribes press wedge marks into wet clay tablets and writing is born.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bronze Age and Iron Age

Bronze (copper + tin) makes stronger weapons, then iron farming tools feed more people and world trade stirs.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Writing and means of communication

People draw things on walls, clay and stone, write on bark then paper with pens, and today on computers; messages once travelled by smoke and carrier pigeon (zagil).

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Sumerian civilisation

On the Euphrates' bank the Sumerians invent irrigation canals, the plough and cuneiform writing; King Gilgamesh of Uruk becomes an epic, half man and half god, and the ziggurat of Ur climbs over the flood plain.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Akkadian civilisation

Before Naram-Sin's Victory Stele the learner meets the first empire: Akkad's kings bind the city-states, spread trade roads and security — then tyranny, priestly quarrels and Sumerian revolts wear the empire down.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 6 — The Babylonian civilisation

In the Louvre's halls, Hammurabi's Babylon — the king who united the two river lands under law and justice; his people carve the Lion of Babylon, draw land maps on clay, chart the stars and compound medicines.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 7 — The Assyrian civilisation

In a library of clay tablets, Assyria the military civilisation: Sargon II builds Khorsabad, Ashurbanipal gathers the Nineveh library, and carved palaces record every conquest.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 8 — The Chaldean civilisation

An excavator reads Babylon's second rise: Nebuchadnezzar II makes Chaldean Babylon flourish in arms, building and art — the Hanging Gardens among its wonders — until weak kings and inner wars open the gate to Cyrus of Persia.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
