## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Umayyad state — rise and expansion** — Al-Hasan yields the caliphate to Mu'awiya in 41 AH and Damascus rules 661–750; the armies besiege Constantinople, cross Transoxiana under al-Walid, found Kairouan, take Tangier, and Tariq ibn Ziyad carries Islam into Iberia
2. **Umayyad civilisational achievements** — A school visit to Qasr Amra's painted walls opens the age of the Umayyad Mosque of Damascus (built 705–715 in ten years), bimaristan hospitals, courts of grievances, and trade exporting grain, sugar and cotton to India and China
3. **The end of the Umayyad state** — Tribal splits (Qays and Yaman), mawali grievances and revolts weaken the state, and the Abbasid call kindles at Humayma in southern Jordan, on the trade and pilgrimage road, far from Umayyad eyes
4. **The Umayyad state in al-Andalus** — When the eastern Umayyads fall, Abd al-Rahman ibn Mu'awiya — "al-Dakhil", the Falcon of Quraysh — crosses to al-Andalus, takes Cordoba and rules 756–1031; scholars like Abbas ibn Firnas, Ibn Zuhr and the musician Ziryab make it Europe's beacon
5. **Jordan in the Umayyad age** — Loyal, near Damascus and astride the Shami Hajj road, Jordan becomes the caliphs' own countryside — al-Walid's seal-die names Jund al-Urdunn, caliphs lodge at al-Muwaqqar, al-Qastal, Zizia and Amman's Citadel (with its palace, mint and great pool), and nine desert palaces rise in the badia: Qusayr Amra, whose frescoes of hunting scenes carry Greek and Arabic inscriptions together (UNESCO 1985), al-Mushatta left unfinished, Azraq, al-Hallabat (Nabataean station, Roman fort, Umayyad palace in one), al-Tuba on the Balqa trade road, and al-Muwaqqar, where a Kufic-inscribed column capital in the pool once measured the water for the pilgrims
6. **Jordan in the Umayyad age** — Five reasons bind Jordan to the caliphs — tribal loyalty, Damascus's nearness, the Hajj road, the trade routes between Arabia, the Levant, Egypt and Iraq, and the fertile land; the palace list grows to nine with al-Harrana and al-Burqu' beside Qusayr Amra, al-Mushatta, Azraq, al-Hallabat, al-Tuba, al-Muwaqqar and al-Qastal
