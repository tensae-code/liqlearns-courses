# The Umayyad Caliphate Damascus and Expansion — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Umayyad state — rise and expansion
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Al-Hasan yields the caliphate to Mu'awiya in 41 AH and Damascus rules 661–750; the armies besiege Constantinople, cross Transoxiana under al-Walid, found Kairouan, take Tangier, and Tariq ibn Ziyad carries Islam into Iberia — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Umayyad civilisational achievements
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: A school visit to Qasr Amra's painted walls opens the age of the Umayyad Mosque of Damascus (built 705–715 in ten years), bimaristan hospitals, courts of grievances, and trade exporting grain, sugar and cotton to India and China — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — The end of the Umayyad state
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tribal splits (Qays and Yaman), mawali grievances and revolts weaken the state, and the Abbasid call kindles at Humayma in southern Jordan, on the trade and pilgrimage road, far from Umayyad eyes — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Umayyad state in al-Andalus
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: When the eastern Umayyads fall, Abd al-Rahman ibn Mu'awiya — "al-Dakhil", the Falcon of Quraysh — crosses to al-Andalus, takes Cordoba and rules 756–1031; scholars like Abbas ibn Firnas, Ibn Zuhr and the musician Ziryab make it Europe's beacon — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — Jordan in the Umayyad age
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Loyal, near Damascus and astride the Shami Hajj road, Jordan becomes the caliphs' own countryside — al-Walid's seal-die names Jund al-Urdunn, caliphs lodge at al-Muwaqqar, al-Qastal, Zizia and Amman's Citadel (with its palace, mint and great pool), and nine desert palaces rise in the badia: Qusayr Amra, whose frescoes of hunting scenes carry Greek and Arabic inscriptions together (UNESCO 1985), al-Mushatta left unfinished, Azraq, al-Hallabat (Nabataean station, Roman fort, Umayyad palace in one), al-Tuba on the Balqa trade road, and al-Muwaqqar, where a Kufic-inscribed column capital in the pool once measured the water for the pilgrims — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Jordan in the Umayyad age
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Five reasons bind Jordan to the caliphs — tribal loyalty, Damascus's nearness, the Hajj road, the trade routes between Arabia, the Levant, Egypt and Iraq, and the fertile land; the palace list grows to nine with al-Harrana and al-Burqu' beside Qusayr Amra, al-Mushatta, Azraq, al-Hallabat, al-Tuba, al-Muwaqqar and al-Qastal — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
