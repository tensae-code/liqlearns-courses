# Hands-on — Coins and Mints of Jordan from Decapolis to the Umayyad Dinar

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Coinage (al-maskukat) in Jordan. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: From barter to numismatics — Rome lets the Decapolis cities strike their own copper and bronze, stamped with the city's name and Latin values; the first Muslims keep Byzantium's gold dinar and Persia's silver dirham, then arabise them step by step (Umar adds "alhamdulillah", Uthman "Allahu akbar", Mu'awiya writes his name even in Pahlavi); Abd al-Malik puts his own image where the emperor's stood, then strikes the pure Islamic dinar in 77 AH/696; the little copper fals (from Latin follis, "money-bag") serves daily trade, and Amman and Jerash work as mints.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: From barter to numismatics — Rome lets the Decapolis cities strike their own copper and bronze, stamped with the city's name and Latin values; the first Muslims keep Byzantium's gold dinar and Persia's silver dirham, then arabise them step by step (Umar adds "alhamdulillah", Uthman "Allahu akbar", Mu'awiya writes his name even in Pahlavi); Abd al-Malik puts his own image where the emperor's stood, then strikes the pure Islamic dinar in 77 AH/696; the little copper fals (from Latin follis, "money-bag") serves daily trade, and Amman and Jerash work as mints. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
