# Coins and Mints of Jordan from Decapolis to the Umayyad Dinar — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — From barter to numismatics — Rome lets the Decapolis cities strike their own copper and bronze, stamped with the city's name and Latin values; the first Muslims keep Byzantium's gold dinar and Persia's silver dirham, then arabise them step by step (Umar adds "alhamdulillah", Uthman "Allahu akbar", Mu'awiya writes his name even in Pahlavi); Abd al-Malik puts his own image where the emperor's stood, then strikes the pure Islamic dinar in 77 AH/696; the little copper fals (from Latin follis, "money-bag") serves daily trade, and Amman and Jerash work as mints — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
