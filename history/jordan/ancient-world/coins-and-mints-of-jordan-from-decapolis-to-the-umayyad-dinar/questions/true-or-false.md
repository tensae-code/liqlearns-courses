# Coins and Mints of Jordan from Decapolis to the Umayyad Dinar — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** From barter to numismatics — Rome lets the Decapolis cities strike their own copper and bronze, stamped with the city's name and Latin values; the first Muslims keep Byzantium's gold dinar and Persia's silver dirham, then arabise them step by step (Umar adds "alhamdulillah", Uthman "Allahu akbar", Mu'awiya writes his name even in Pahlavi); Abd al-Malik puts his own image where the emperor's stood, then strikes the pure Islamic dinar in 77 AH/696; the little copper fals (from Latin follis, "money-bag") serves daily trade, and Amman and Jerash work as mints.
**Answer: True**

**2.** The part “Coins and Mints of Jordan from Decapolis to the Umayyad Dinar” teaches that its events changed nothing for anyone.
**Answer: False** — The lesson teaches the opposite: From barter to numismatics — Rome lets the Decapolis cities strike their own copper and bronze, stamped with the city's name and Latin values; the first Muslims keep Byzantium's gold dinar and Persia's silver dirham, then arabise them step by step (Umar adds "alhamdulillah", Uthman "Allahu akbar", Mu'awiya writes his name even in Pahlavi); Abd al-Malik puts his own image where the emperor's stood, then strikes the pure Islamic dinar in 77 AH/696; the little copper fals (from Latin follis, "money-bag") serves daily trade, and Amman and Jerash work as mints.
