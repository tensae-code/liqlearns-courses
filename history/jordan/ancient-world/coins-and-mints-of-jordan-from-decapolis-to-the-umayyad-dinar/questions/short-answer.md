# Coins and Mints of Jordan from Decapolis to the Umayyad Dinar — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Retell, in your own words, what happens in “Coinage (al-maskukat) in Jordan”.
**Answer:** from barter to numismatics — Rome lets the Decapolis cities strike their own copper and bronze, stamped with the city's name and Latin values; the first Muslims keep Byzantium's gold dinar and Persia's silver dirham, then arabise them step by step (Umar adds "alhamdulillah", Uthman "Allahu akbar", Mu'awiya writes his name even in Pahlavi); Abd al-Malik puts his own image where the emperor's stood, then strikes the pure Islamic dinar in 77 AH/696; the little copper fals (from Latin follis, "money-bag") serves daily trade, and Amman and Jerash work as mints.
