# Hands-on — Arab Islamic Civilisation State Society Science and Art

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Government and administration; The judiciary; The army, the fleet and the police; Economic life; Social life; Scientific and cultural life; Arab Islamic arts. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Abu Bakr's first sermon sets the contract — "I have been put over you though I am not the best of you; if I do well help me, if I do wrong set me straight" — then choosing the caliph slides from shura to hereditary succession under Umayyads, Abbasids and Ottomans; wazirs of delegation and of execution, governors and the diwans (army, land-tax, post) run the machine.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Abu Bakr's first sermon sets the contract — "I have been put over you though I am not the best of you; if I do well help me, if I do wrong set me straight" — then choosing the caliph slides from shura to hereditary succession under Umayyads, Abbasids and Ottomans; wazirs of delegation and of execution, governors and the diwans (army, land-tax, post) run the machine. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
