The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

This lesson is about one thing.

Government and administration.

Abu Bakr's first sermon sets the contract — "I have been put over you though I am not the best of you; if I do well help me, if I do wrong set me straight" — then choosing the caliph slides from shura to hereditary succession under Umayyads, Abbasids and Ottomans; wazirs of delegation and of execution, governors and the diwans (army, land-tax, post) run the machine.

The judiciary.

The Prophet judges disputes himself; Umar separates the judges from the governors; the qadi settles quarrels, watches the markets and guards the endowments; under the Abbasids a chief judge, qadi al-qudat, oversees them all, the schools of law multiply, and a court of grievances, the mazalim, hears complaints against the powerful.

The army, the fleet and the police.

Volunteers with swords and spears win Badr in 624; Mu'awiya begs Umar for a fleet and is refused, yet within a generation Muslim ships fight Byzantium at the Battle of the Masts; Abbasid fleets reach the Indian Ocean, Andalusi fleets guard the Mediterranean, and in 1533 Khayr al-Din Barbarossa makes the Ottoman fleet master of the sea — while in every city the sahib al-shurta walks the night streets with his lantern-men.

Economic life.

Farmers share-crop by muzara'a and mugharasa, terrace the hillsides, drain the marshes and lift river water with the noria wheels of Hama and the shaduf; Damascus weaves and bladesmiths, Fez tans leather, guilds train boy to journeyman to master; caravans and ships stitch the Islamic world into one market, Abd al-Malik arabises the coinage, and the waqf endowment pays for schools, roads and hospitals.

Social life.

Arabs, mawali, dhimmis and freed slaves live in one society; Islam urges freeing slaves and lets a slave buy his own freedom by mukataba; houses cluster round mosque and market, wardrobes run from the jubba to the turban, a quoted etiquette text teaches the witty not to overfill their mouths, and the feasts — Fitr, Adha, the Mawlid, even Nayruz and Mahrajan — gather everyone.

Scientific and cultural life.

A boy finishes the kuttab and joins a circle in the mosque; Ibn Sina of Afshana writes the medicine of centuries, Abu Hanifa founds a school of law; Baghdad's Bayt al-Hikma and a thousand libraries store the world's books; Hunayn ibn Ishaq and the translators carry Plato, Hippocrates, Euclid and Archimedes into Arabic, and bimaristan hospitals heal the sick.

Arab Islamic arts.

Abd al-Malik crowns Jerusalem with the Dome of the Rock; Saladin's commander Izz al-Din Usama builds Ajloun Castle to watch the Crusader roads; in Granada the Alhambra's Court of the Lions pours water from stone lions' mouths; the Mamluk Sultan Hassan raises his mosque-school in Cairo; and everywhere arabesque geometry, plant scrolls, mashrabiyya screens and Kufic letters turn craft into art.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
