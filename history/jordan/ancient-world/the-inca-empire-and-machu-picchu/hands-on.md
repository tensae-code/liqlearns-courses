# Hands-on — The Inca Empire and Machu Picchu

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The Inca part. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: High in the Andes the Inca organise whole villages in the collective ayllu system, speak Quechua, count in decimals, watch the sun and stars, divide the year into twelve months of thirty days, and build Machu Picchu, the stone city in the clouds.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: High in the Andes the Inca organise whole villages in the collective ayllu system, speak Quechua, count in decimals, watch the sun and stars, divide the year into twelve months of thirty days, and build Machu Picchu, the stone city in the clouds. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
