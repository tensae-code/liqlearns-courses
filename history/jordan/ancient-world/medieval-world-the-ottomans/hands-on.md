# Hands-on — Medieval World the Ottomans

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The rise of the Ottoman state; The conquest of Constantinople (Istanbul); Public life in the Ottoman state; The Arab homeland under Ottoman rule; The end of the Ottoman state; Political and administrative conditions in Ottoman Jordan; Social and economic life in Ottoman Jordan; Political and administrative conditions in Ottoman Jordan; Social and economic conditions in Ottoman Jordan. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: A Turkish frontier tribe in Anatolia becomes a state: Orhan (1326–1360) takes Bursa as capital and founds the new army — the Janissaries raised through the devshirme.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A Turkish frontier tribe in Anatolia becomes a state: Orhan (1326–1360) takes Bursa as capital and founds the new army — the Janissaries raised through the devshirme. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
