# Medieval World the Ottomans — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — The rise of the Ottoman state
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: A Turkish frontier tribe in Anatolia becomes a state: Orhan (1326–1360) takes Bursa as capital and founds the new army — the Janissaries raised through the devshirme — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — The conquest of Constantinople (Istanbul)
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Mehmed II takes Constantinople in 857 AH / 1453 CE; the Byzantine Empire falls, the city becomes the Ottoman capital and a great trading centre, and the state turns to building fleets — historians mark it the end of the middle ages — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Public life in the Ottoman state
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Grand Vizier at the Sublime Porte, the sipahi cavalry on land grants, dhimmi communities keeping their rites under their own heads, and Sinan raising mosques and schools from Bursa to Istanbul — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Arab homeland under Ottoman rule
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Selim I breaks the Mamluks at Marj Dabiq and Ridaniyya (1517), enters Damascus and Cairo, and takes the title Servant of the Two Holy Sanctuaries; Suleiman the Lawgiver reforms law and administration while Portugal threatens the sea roads — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 5 — The end of the Ottoman state
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Ibn Khaldun's cycle turns: separatist amirs (Fakhr al-Din, Ali Bey the Great, Zahir al-Umar), Tanzimat reforms (1826–1876), the Union and Progress society forcing the constitution in 1908, defeat in the Great War — and Mustafa Kemal Atatürk founds the Turkish Republic — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 6 — Political and administrative conditions in Ottoman Jordan
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Osman ibn Ertugrul's state (1299–1923) takes Jordan after Marj Dabiq in 1516 for its Hajj road and its farmland; the Shami Hajj caravan crosses yearly, its surra gifts buying Bedouin safe-passage; provinces are drawn and redrawn (Sham, Tripoli, Aleppo), order decays, villages empty, and drought drives tribes north from Najd and the Hijaz; Muhammad Ali's Egypt rules the Levant 1831–1840 — and Ajloun's village sheikhs petition his governor Muhammad Sharif Pasha in 1839, a document the class reads as a source; Jordanian notables sit in the provincial councils and Tawfiq al-Majali in the Ottoman parliament, until census, conscription and tax-gathering ignite the protests and the Karak revolt — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 7 — Social and economic life in Ottoman Jordan
Sub lesson 7 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Three peoples share the land — the fallahin majority in tight-walled villages of iwan houses, raising the awna (faz'a) to build and harvest together, baking in the tabun, drawing water from springs and cisterns, learning in the kuttab by the mosque, grinding at the Zarqa River water-mills; the townsfolk of the qasabas; and the Bedouin in the bayt al-sha'r, its poles named wasit and muqaddam, a nine-pole kharbush for a sheikh; teaching runs from zawaya and waqf kuttabs to the Rushdiyya and the missionary schools that bring English and French; the iltizam tax-farmers squeeze the villages, in the words of a quoted complaint, "with the utmost severity"; trade shrinks under highway robbery while Ottoman liras, majidis and paras circulate beside English and French gold — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 8 — Political and administrative conditions in Ottoman Jordan
Sub lesson 8 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The academic telling adds a people to Jordan's Ottoman centuries — the Circassian and Chechen muhajirs, driven from the Caucasus by their bloody war with Tsarist Russia and settled along the railway lands at Zarqa, Sukhna, Jerash, Amman, Sweileh, Wadi al-Seir and Na'ur, where their farming carts and villages reshape the Balqa — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 9 — Social and economic conditions in Ottoman Jordan
Sub lesson 9 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The 1864 Vilayet Law orders the land into provinces, districts and villages, each village with its council of elders under a mukhtar who keeps order, settles disputes and gathers the taxes; two Bedouin patterns are distinguished — the fully nomadic camel-herders moving all year for water and pasture, and the semi-settled tribes farming a fixed dira — beside the rain-fed (ba'l) fields of the fallahin — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
