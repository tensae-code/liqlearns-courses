# The Great Wall and Chinas Gifts — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons. Pass mark: the learner can state the lesson's one thing — An emperor-son-of-heaven rules a hereditary empire; scribes write in picture-signs with no alphabet; Islam walks the Silk Roads into China and the Great Mosque of Xi'an rises in 742; farmers stand honoured above traders; silk, porcelain and bronze sail the maritime Silk Road from Quanzhou — the ancestor of today's Belt and Road — in their own words, give one real example from the story, and answer the lesson's questions. Practice deepens by more items and harder corners: first the plain cases from the lesson, then cases where the lesson's opposite (its false friend) tempts the wrong answer, then the lesson applied in a new place — a new situation the learner has not seen in the lesson.
