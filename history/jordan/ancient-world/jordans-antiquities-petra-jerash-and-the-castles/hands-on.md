# Hands-on — Jordans Antiquities Petra Jerash and the Castles

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Our beautiful antiquities; Umm al-Rasas; Shawbak castle; Archaeological sites; Journey in Karak Governorate; Journey in Zarqa Governorate; Journey in Madaba Governorate; Journey in Tafila Governorate; Journey in Ajloun Governorate; Aspects of Greek civilisation in Jordan; Aspects of the Greek civilisation in Jordan. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Jordan's sites tell its long history — rock-carved Petra, black-stone Umm al-Jimal, Qasr al-Abd with its carved animals, Amman's Citadel hill, and the defence castles of Tafila, Karak and Ajloun guarding the trade roads.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Jordan's sites tell its long history — rock-carved Petra, black-stone Umm al-Jimal, Qasr al-Abd with its carved animals, Amman's Citadel hill, and the defence castles of Tafila, Karak and Ajloun guarding the trade roads. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
