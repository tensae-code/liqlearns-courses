## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Our beautiful antiquities** — Jordan's sites tell its long history — rock-carved Petra, black-stone Umm al-Jimal, Qasr al-Abd with its carved animals, Amman's Citadel hill, and the defence castles of Tafila, Karak and Ajloun guarding the trade roads
2. **Umm al-Rasas** — South of the capital, Umm al-Rasas keeps its castle and mosaic-floored churches, a world-heritage site
3. **Shawbak castle** — Shawbak, once called Montreal ("the royal mountain"), was built in the age of the Frankish (Crusader) invasions of Bilad al-Sham
4. **Archaeological sites** — Petra, Jerash, Karak castle, Ajloun and Umm al-Jimal stand as the tangible heritage of the civilisations that passed over Jordan's land
5. **Journey in Karak Governorate** — Samir climbs Karak castle, among Jordan's largest, which the Franks (Crusaders) rebuilt on its hilltop ringed with towers
6. **Journey in Zarqa Governorate** — Qasr Shabib, a Roman fort the Muslims used as a guarded station; black-basalt Azraq castle raised over Nabataean and Roman work; and Qasr Amra — the desert castles guard Jordan's east
7. **Journey in Madaba Governorate** — The Map Church's mosaic floor, Mount Nebo's Siyagha church looking over the Dead Sea, Machaerus, and the sites adopted for Christian pilgrimage after Pope John Paul II's visit in 2000
8. **Journey in Tafila Governorate** — Busayra, the old Edomite capital, and the shrines of the Companions Ka'b ibn Umayr, Jabir al-Ansari and Farwa ibn Amr al-Judhami layer Tafila's Islamic memory over its ancient ground
9. **Journey in Ajloun Governorate** — Ajloun castle, built by Izz al-Din Usama, a commander of Saladin, on Jabal Bani Awf to command the roads between Syria, Jordan and the south; below it, Tell Mar Elias keeps its Byzantine churches
10. **Aspects of Greek civilisation in Jordan** — Alexander of Macedon (born 356 BCE, Aristotle's pupil, Philip II's son) takes Egypt and the Levant in 332 BCE; his generals divide the empire and Jordan passes through their wars; at Iraq al-Amir in Wadi al-Seir west of Amman a palace of huge stones carved with lions rises; arches and Corinthian capitals spread, Greek becomes the language of rule, Greek coin-types shape even Nabataean money (Harith III; Harith IV with his queen) — and Greek and local culture fuse into the Hellenistic civilisation as Rome rises in the west
11. **Aspects of the Greek civilisation in Jordan** — Alexander's successors fight over Jordan; the Decapolis roll here adds Capitolias (Bayt Ras) to Philadelphia, Gerasa, Abila, Gadara and Pella, and Rabbath Ammon is renamed Philadelphia in an emperor's honour; Iraq al-Amir's lion-carved palace stands for the age
