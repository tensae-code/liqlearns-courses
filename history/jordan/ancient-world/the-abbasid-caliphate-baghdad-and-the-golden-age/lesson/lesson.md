## Sub lesson 1 — The Abbasid state — rise and development

The secret call runs from al-Humayma, the Umayyads fall at the Zab, Baghdad rises, and under Harun al-Rashid the state peaks — the House of Wisdom makes the capital a centre of learning, run through diwans and the new office of vizier.

## Sub lesson 2 — Civilisational aspects of the Abbasid state

Sinbad's tales open an age of translation — Hunayn ibn Ishaq is paid his book's weight in gold — paper spreads, observatories rise, and Maryam al-Astrulabi perfects the astrolabe at Aleppo.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — The weakness of the Abbasid state

Governors break away — Idrisids first in the far Maghreb — Baghdad falls to the Mongols, and the Mamluk sultan Qutuz stops the Mongol advance at Ain Jalut in 1260.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — Sigrid Hunke (1913–1999)

The German orientalist of Kiel answers Europe's distorted schoolbooks with "Allah's Sun over the Occident": the Arabs named the stars, built observatories, and al-Razi (d. 923) stands among medicine's greats — Arab science as world heritage.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — Jordan in the Abbasid age

The Abbasid revolution is plotted on Jordanian soil — Abd al-Malik grants al-Humayma in the Balqa to Ali ibn Abdullah ibn al-Abbas to keep him under his eye, and there his son Muhammad turns the village into the secret da'wa's headquarters, the birthplace of caliphs; Abdullah ibn Ali destroys Marwan II's army at the Zab, the capital moves east to Baghdad, and Jordan fades to a province at the edge of the story it started.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 6 — Jordan in the Abbasid age

The Zab (132 AH/750) ends the Umayyads; the Humayma da'wa story is retold, the capital moves to Baghdad, caliphs still prize Jordan for the Hajj and trade roads — and must repeatedly face rebellions and revolts on its soil as their attention thins.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
