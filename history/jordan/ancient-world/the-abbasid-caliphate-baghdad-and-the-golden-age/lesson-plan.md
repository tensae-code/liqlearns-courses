## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Abbasid state — rise and development** — The secret call runs from al-Humayma, the Umayyads fall at the Zab, Baghdad rises, and under Harun al-Rashid the state peaks — the House of Wisdom makes the capital a centre of learning, run through diwans and the new office of vizier
2. **Civilisational aspects of the Abbasid state** — Sinbad's tales open an age of translation — Hunayn ibn Ishaq is paid his book's weight in gold — paper spreads, observatories rise, and Maryam al-Astrulabi perfects the astrolabe at Aleppo
3. **The weakness of the Abbasid state** — Governors break away — Idrisids first in the far Maghreb — Baghdad falls to the Mongols, and the Mamluk sultan Qutuz stops the Mongol advance at Ain Jalut in 1260
4. **Sigrid Hunke (1913–1999)** — The German orientalist of Kiel answers Europe's distorted schoolbooks with "Allah's Sun over the Occident": the Arabs named the stars, built observatories, and al-Razi (d. 923) stands among medicine's greats — Arab science as world heritage
5. **Jordan in the Abbasid age** — The Abbasid revolution is plotted on Jordanian soil — Abd al-Malik grants al-Humayma in the Balqa to Ali ibn Abdullah ibn al-Abbas to keep him under his eye, and there his son Muhammad turns the village into the secret da'wa's headquarters, the birthplace of caliphs; Abdullah ibn Ali destroys Marwan II's army at the Zab, the capital moves east to Baghdad, and Jordan fades to a province at the edge of the story it started
6. **Jordan in the Abbasid age** — The Zab (132 AH/750) ends the Umayyads; the Humayma da'wa story is retold, the capital moves to Baghdad, caliphs still prize Jordan for the Hajj and trade roads — and must repeatedly face rebellions and revolts on its soil as their attention thins
