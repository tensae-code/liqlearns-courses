# Hands-on — The Abbasid Caliphate Baghdad and the Golden Age

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: The Abbasid state — rise and development; Civilisational aspects of the Abbasid state; The weakness of the Abbasid state; Sigrid Hunke (1913–1999); Jordan in the Abbasid age; Jordan in the Abbasid age. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: The secret call runs from al-Humayma, the Umayyads fall at the Zab, Baghdad rises, and under Harun al-Rashid the state peaks — the House of Wisdom makes the capital a centre of learning, run through diwans and the new office of vizier.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The secret call runs from al-Humayma, the Umayyads fall at the Zab, Baghdad rises, and under Harun al-Rashid the state peaks — the House of Wisdom makes the capital a centre of learning, run through diwans and the new office of vizier. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
