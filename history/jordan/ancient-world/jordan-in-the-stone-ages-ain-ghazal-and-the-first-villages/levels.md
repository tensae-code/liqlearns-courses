## Sub lesson 1 — Jordan in the Stone Ages

Pass mark: On a timeline that runs from the first tools to the invention of writing (3500 BCE) and Alexander (332 BCE), Jordan's hunters leave their tools at Dawqara in Zarqa; at Eyon al-Hammam in Wadi Zaqallab (Irbid) lies what the book calls the oldest organised cemetery in the world, some 16,000 years old; Neolithic villagers at Ain Ghazal in Amman model the world's oldest plaster statues; and at Wadi Faynan by Wadi Araba, Chalcolithic smiths smelt copper and a class of craftsmen is born — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
