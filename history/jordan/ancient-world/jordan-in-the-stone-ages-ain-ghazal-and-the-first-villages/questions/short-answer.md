# Jordan in the Stone Ages Ain Ghazal and the First Villages — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** Retell, in your own words, what happens in “Jordan in the Stone Ages”. (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** on a timeline that runs from the first tools to the invention of writing (3500 BCE) and Alexander (332 BCE), Jordan's hunters leave their tools at Dawqara in Zarqa; at Eyon al-Hammam in Wadi Zaqallab (Irbid) lies what the book calls the oldest organised cemetery in the world, some 16,000 years old; Neolithic villagers at Ain Ghazal in Amman model the world's oldest plaster statues; and at Wadi Faynan by Wadi Araba, Chalcolithic smiths smelt copper and a class of craftsmen is born.

**2.** Who is in the story of “Jordan in the Stone Ages”, where does it happen, and what turns? (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** dawqara in the Zarqa valley is dated by this book to 2.5 million years, among the oldest sites of the age in Asia, with al-Lajjun in Tafila and Karak; the same villages as the vocational telling (Eyon al-Hammam's cemetery, Ain Ghazal's plaster statues, Faynan's copper) — and a fifth age the vocational book folds away: the Bronze Age of walled, gated, templed towns like Bab al-Dhra in the southern Ghawr of Karak.
