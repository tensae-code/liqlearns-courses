# Video script — Jordan in the Stone Ages Ain Ghazal and the First Villages

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
On a timeline that runs from the first tools to the invention of writing (3500 BCE) and Alexander (332 BCE), Jordan's hunters leave their tools at Dawqara in Zarqa; at Eyon al-Hammam in Wadi Zaqallab (Irbid) lies what the book calls the oldest organised cemetery in the world, some 16,000 years old; Neolithic villagers at Ain Ghazal in Amman model the world's oldest plaster statues; and at Wadi Faynan by Wadi Araba, Chalcolithic smiths smelt copper and a class of craftsmen is born.

Dawqara in the Zarqa valley is dated by this book to 2.5 million years, among the oldest sites of the age in Asia, with al-Lajjun in Tafila and Karak; the same villages as the vocational telling (Eyon al-Hammam's cemetery, Ain Ghazal's plaster statues, Faynan's copper) — and a fifth age the vocational book folds away: the Bronze Age of walled, gated, templed towns like Bab al-Dhra in the southern Ghawr of Karak.
