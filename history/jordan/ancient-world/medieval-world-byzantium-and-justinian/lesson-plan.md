# Lesson plan — Medieval World Byzantium and Justinian

**Course:** History — Jordan · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Medieval World Byzantium and Justinian
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Islam Rises: The Caliphates and Jordan in the Islamic Ages (CURRICULUM.md).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Byzantine Jordan (395–636)** — Theodosius splits the empire between Honorius in the West (Rome falls to the Goths in 476) and Arcadius in the East at Constantinople; Jordan's churches multiply, and Madaba lays the oldest mosaic map of the Holy Land (560 CE), still on its church floor today; south-east of Amman at al-Raqim, the Cave of the Sleepers recalls the Emperor Decius, who persecuted the Christians who hid there
2. **Byzantine Jordan** — To the Madaba map and the Cave of the Sleepers this telling adds al-Maghtas at Wadi al-Kharrar north of the Dead Sea, where Christ was baptised — a UNESCO-listed pilgrimage site drawing thousands — and Madaba's own Mosaic Institute, teaching the Byzantine craft as a living Jordanian livelihood
