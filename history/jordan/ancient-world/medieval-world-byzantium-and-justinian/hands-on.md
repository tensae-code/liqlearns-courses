# Hands-on — Medieval World Byzantium and Justinian

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a line across a page — a road or a timeline. Mark the parts of this story on it in order: Byzantine Jordan (395–636); Byzantine Jordan. Under each mark, write who is there and what turns. This lesson's story is: Theodosius splits the empire between Honorius in the West (Rome falls to the Goths in 476) and Arcadius in the East at Constantinople; Jordan's churches multiply, and Madaba lays the oldest mosaic map of the Holy Land (560 CE), still on its church floor today; south-east of Amman at al-Raqim, the Cave of the Sleepers recalls the Emperor Decius, who persecuted the Christians who hid there.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Theodosius splits the empire between Honorius in the West (Rome falls to the Goths in 476) and Arcadius in the East at Constantinople; Jordan's churches multiply, and Madaba lays the oldest mosaic map of the Holy Land (560 CE), still on its church floor today; south-east of Amman at al-Raqim, the Cave of the Sleepers recalls the Emperor Decius, who persecuted the Christians who hid there. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson's story echoing in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of your town. Each time you see an echo (or its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
