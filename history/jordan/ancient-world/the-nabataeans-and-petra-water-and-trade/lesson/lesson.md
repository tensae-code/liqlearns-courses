# The Nabataeans and Petra Water and Trade

## Sub lesson 1 — The Nabataean kingdom

The Arab Nabataeans adapt to the desert, harvesting rainwater with dams, channels and rock-cut cisterns, protecting Petra from floods and trading widely.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Nabataean Kingdom

An Arab tribe settles Edom's land in the 6th century BCE and carves Petra, the rose-red city — the 1,200-metre Siq, the Khazneh tomb, the theatre, Qasr al-Bint (built, not carved, its walls laced with wood against earthquakes) and the Deir; they write Aramaic in 22 letters, speak Greek to foreign merchants, sell Dead Sea salt and bitumen to Egypt's embalmers, and grow rich protecting caravans with towers, stations and taxes — until weak kings and Rome end them; Burckhardt reveals Petra to the West in 1812, UNESCO lists it in 1985, and a Petra Region Authority (2009) guards it now.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Nabataean Kingdom

Petra is chosen among the New Seven Wonders of the World in this telling; the Nabataeans survive the Greek age independent, beat off two Greek military campaigns, and under Harith III exploit the Ptolemaic–Seleucid struggle to stretch the kingdom to Damascus — holding out until Rome annexes them in 106 CE.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
