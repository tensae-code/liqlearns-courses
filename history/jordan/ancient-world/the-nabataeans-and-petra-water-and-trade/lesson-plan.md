## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Nabataean kingdom** — The Arab Nabataeans adapt to the desert, harvesting rainwater with dams, channels and rock-cut cisterns, protecting Petra from floods and trading widely
2. **Petra** — Samir and Salma enter the rose-red city through the Siq rock corridor and see the Treasury, the Court and the Monastery the Nabataeans carved
3. **The Nabataean kingdom** — Arab Nabataeans unite Jordan's kingdoms, carve Petra in rose-red rock, channel water along the Siq — and their queens share rule and appear on the coins
4. **The Nabataean Kingdom** — An Arab tribe settles Edom's land in the 6th century BCE and carves Petra, the rose-red city — the 1,200-metre Siq, the Khazneh tomb, the theatre, Qasr al-Bint (built, not carved, its walls laced with wood against earthquakes) and the Deir; they write Aramaic in 22 letters, speak Greek to foreign merchants, sell Dead Sea salt and bitumen to Egypt's embalmers, and grow rich protecting caravans with towers, stations and taxes — until weak kings and Rome end them; Burckhardt reveals Petra to the West in 1812, UNESCO lists it in 1985, and a Petra Region Authority (2009) guards it now
5. **The Nabataean Kingdom** — Petra is chosen among the New Seven Wonders of the World in this telling; the Nabataeans survive the Greek age independent, beat off two Greek military campaigns, and under Harith III exploit the Ptolemaic–Seleucid struggle to stretch the kingdom to Damascus — holding out until Rome annexes them in 106 CE
