# Jordan in the Crusader Ayyubid and Mamluk Ages — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** Europe's kings exploit a divided Islamic world and take Jerusalem in 1099, planting Edessa, Antioch, Tripoli and the Kingdom of Jerusalem; Baldwin raises Montreal castle at Shobak and seizes Ayla (Aqaba) to command the Syria–Egypt road, and the Franks rebuild Karak — until Nur al-Din Zangi reads Jordan's importance and moves, setting the stage for Hattin (1187). (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer: True**

**2.** The part “Jordan in the Ayyubid age” teaches that its events changed nothing for anyone. (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer: False** — The lesson teaches the opposite: Saladin, founder of the Ayyubid state (1173–1229 in the book's dating), treats Jordan as the link between Egypt and Syria, the muster-ground and the Hajj road; having freed Karak he grants it to his brother al-Adil, and al-Mu'azzam Isa rebuilds its walls, imports craftsmen and makes the city feed itself; al-Nasir Dawud follows; Ayyubid mosques rise at Rimoun, Irbid, Tabaqat Fahl, Harthas and Bayt Ras, and Jordanian scholars — from Ayla, Shobak, Husban, Ajloun, Ba'un and Karak — teach as far as the Ba'uniyya dome in Damascus, before the Mongols enter Damascus in 1260.
