## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Jordan under the Frankish invasion of Bilad al-Sham** — Europe's kings exploit a divided Islamic world and take Jerusalem in 1099, planting Edessa, Antioch, Tripoli and the Kingdom of Jerusalem; Baldwin raises Montreal castle at Shobak and seizes Ayla (Aqaba) to command the Syria–Egypt road, and the Franks rebuild Karak — until Nur al-Din Zangi reads Jordan's importance and moves, setting the stage for Hattin (1187)
2. **Jordan in the Ayyubid age** — Saladin, founder of the Ayyubid state (1173–1229 in the book's dating), treats Jordan as the link between Egypt and Syria, the muster-ground and the Hajj road; having freed Karak he grants it to his brother al-Adil, and al-Mu'azzam Isa rebuilds its walls, imports craftsmen and makes the city feed itself; al-Nasir Dawud follows; Ayyubid mosques rise at Rimoun, Irbid, Tabaqat Fahl, Harthas and Bayt Ras, and Jordanian scholars — from Ayla, Shobak, Husban, Ajloun, Ba'un and Karak — teach as far as the Ba'uniyya dome in Damascus, before the Mongols enter Damascus in 1260
3. **Jordan in the Mamluk age** — At Ain Jalut in 1260, between Bisan and Nablus, the Mamluks break Kitbugha's Mongols, and Jordan becomes Egypt's shield and granary: two deputy-provinces (the Karak niyaba runs from Wadi al-Mujib to Aqaba), postal stations with fresh horses, sugar cane booming in the Jordan Valley, towns growing — Aqaba, Shobak, Karak, Husban, Amman, Salt, Irbid, Ajloun; Sultan Qansuh al-Ghuri rebuilds Aqaba's castle on Crusader ruins and an Ayyubid tower, and the new Amir al-Urban, Shatti ibn Uqba of the Banu Uqba, guards the Hajj road for the sultan
4. **Jordan during the Frankish campaigns** — King Fulk of Anjou builds Karak castle as the Frankish centre east of the Kingdom of Jerusalem; its lord Reynald de Chatillon breaks Saladin's truce by seizing a trade caravan passing Karak from Egypt — the provocation that leads to Hattin; Nur al-Din had already tested Karak's walls and withdrawn
5. **Jordan in the Ayyubid age** — This book dates the Ayyubid state 569–648 AH/1174–1250; al-Nasir Dawud founds the Ayyubid Emirate of Karak embracing all of today's Jordan plus Nablus, Jerusalem and Hebron, rebuilds the castle, raises the Nasiri Hall, and gathers scholars, jurists and poets; al-Mu'azzam Isa orders the rational sciences taught — philosophy, astronomy, medicine and mathematics — and endows the schools
6. **Jordan in the Mamluk age** — Ain Jalut is fought near Jenin, and the quiz names Qutuz the Muslim commander; a quoted Mamluk text explains the manawir — beacon fires by night and smoke by day whose number and shape signal Tatar movements; the Mamluks farm the mukus customs, guard the Shami and Egyptian Hajj roads with khans like Qatrana and stations at al-Hasa and Aqaba; and Jordan's scholars shine — Aisha al-Ba'uniyya, author of al-Malamih al-Sharifa, Muhammad ibn Yunus al-Ajluni, and Qasim al-Irbidi teaching hadith and fiqh at Damascus's Atabakiyya school
