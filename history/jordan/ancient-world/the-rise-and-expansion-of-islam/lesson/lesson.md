# The Rise and Expansion of Islam

## Sub lesson 1 — Beginning of the Islamic call

Muhammad, respected in Mecca before the call, receives revelation; Khadijah, Ali, Abu Bakr and Zayd believe first; Quraysh persecute and boycott Banu Hashim, some Muslims flee to Abyssinia, and the Hijra to Yathrib follows.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Society in Madinah al-Munawwarah

After the Hijra (622) the Prophet writes the Madinah Charter, the first constitutional document, binding Muhajirun, Ansar of Aws and Khazraj, and the Jews into one just community, and Madinah becomes a trading centre.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — The call outside the Arabian Peninsula

After Hudaybiyyah the Prophet sends letters to kings and rulers; envoy al-Harith ibn Umayr is killed in southern Jordan, leading to Mu'tah against the Byzantines; the Farewell Pilgrimage and the Prophet's death close the story, and Abu Bakr is chosen.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — Yarmouk name

Yarmouk University takes its name from the Battle of Yarmouk, the decisive early-Islamic battle fought in Jordan's north.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Companions' shrines at al-Mazar

At Mu'tah's southern shrine stand the tombs of Zayd ibn Haritha, Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and Abdullah ibn Rawaha, the commanders who fell at Mu'tah.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 6 — The era of the Rashidun Caliphs

A visit to the Yarmouk torch site opens the era: the battle of 15 AH / 636 CE, Abu Bakr's sermon, the Qur'an gathered under Uthman, Jerusalem taken in 638, garrison cities founded (Basra, Kufa, Fustat) and Umar starting the Hijri calendar.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 7 — The end of the Sasanian state

Defeat piles on defeat until Nihawand, "the Conquest of Conquests", opens Persia to the Muslim armies and the Sasanian state falls.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 8 — Jordan in the dawn of Islam

The Ghassanids, an Azd tribe from Yemen settled in the Balqa and allied to Rome, attack the Prophet's envoys, so at Mu'tah by Karak the three appointed commanders fall one after another until Khalid ibn al-Walid raises dust all night to fake reinforcements and saves the army; in the Year of Delegations (9 AH/630) Adhruh and al-Jarba near Ma'an, John ibn Ru'ba of Ayla, and Farwa the Judhami governor make their peace; Abu Bakr sends four armies through Jordan — Amr to Palestine, Yazid to Damascus, Shurahbil to Jordan itself — and at the Yarmouk in 636 Khalid's gathered host breaks Mahan's Byzantines; Umar's Covenant to Jerusalem is quoted in full, and the Companions' shrines (Abu Ubayda in the Valley, Mu'adh ibn Jabal at Shuna, Farwa at Afra) still dot the map.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 9 — Jordan in the dawn of Islam

The academic telling widens the road in — after Mu'tah, Amr ibn al-As strikes the Quda'a and Bali alliance at Dhat al-Salasil by Ma'an (8 AH/629); Usama ibn Zayd's raid reaches Moab in 11 AH/632 as a reconnaissance in force; Amr's army is first into Jordan and wins at Wadi Araba, then Shurahbil wins Tabaqat Fahl (13 AH/634) after Ajnadayn; Umar divides Bilad al-Sham into four ajnad in 18 AH/639; and Ayla (Aqaba) becomes the first Islamic-planned city outside Arabia, the gate of Jordan and Palestine.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
