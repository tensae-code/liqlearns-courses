# Trade Routes That Linked the Old World

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Trade then and now; Industries and trade. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Trade then and now

People once bartered wheat for oil, peddlers carried goods on donkeys and camels from village to village, then fixed shops, malls and e-commerce took over.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Industries and trade

Purple dye from sea shells, glass and cedar ships make the Phoenicians famous across the ancient world, while Amorite and Aramean caravans cross the desert guided by men who know its wells.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
