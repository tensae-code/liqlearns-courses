## Sub lesson 1 — Jerash

Jerash in the north keeps its colonnaded street and south and north theatres, with antiquities from the Greek, Roman, Byzantine and Islamic ages.

## Sub lesson 2 — Journey in Irbid Governorate

At Umm Qais (Gadara) and Abila the children walk Roman theatres of basalt stone seating thousands and a Byzantine church with mosaic floors, wondering how their builders raised the columns.

## Sub lesson 3 — Journey in Jerash Governorate

Hadrian's Arch, raised for the emperor's visit in 129 CE, the Oval Plaza, the South Theatre and the Temple of Artemis make Jerash a Roman city still standing.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — Roman Jordan and the Decapolis

Pompey takes Syria and Palestine for Rome in 64 BCE, but the Nabataeans keep their autonomy for a yearly tribute; the Ten Cities league runs from Damascus to Amman to hold the trade road — Philadelphia (Amman), Gerasa (Jerash, from the Canaanite Gersho, "place of many trees": South Theatre, Temple of Artemis, hippodrome, colonnaded street), Abila (Quwayliba by Irbid, its rock tombs and churches still largely unexcavated), Gadara (Umm Qais above the Yarmouk, the Golan and Lake Tiberias) and Pella (Tabaqat Fahl, named for Alexander's birthplace) — self-governing, minting their own coins under Rome's protection, and stitched together by Trajan's King's Highway from Egypt to the Euphrates.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — Aspects of the Roman civilisation in Jordan

The Ten Cities govern themselves and mint under Rome; at Umm Qais the museum keeps the poet Aribus's tombstone — "Traveller, as you are now, so once was I; as I am now, so shall you be: enjoy life, for you are mortal" — and Trajan's King's Highway binds the cities to Egypt and the Euphrates.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
