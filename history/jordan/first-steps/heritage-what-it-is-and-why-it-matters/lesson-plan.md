## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Our customs and traditions** — Jordanian society keeps its authentic customs — welcoming guests, helping the needy, wearing traditional dress — and children visit a folk-life museum to see them
2. **Customs and traditions; folk costumes** — A girl finds a photo of her grandmother's old dress; Jordan's folk dress (thobe, shemagh and egal) differs between desert and countryside and is part of Jordanian identity kept across centuries
3. **Culture** — Culture's elements include history itself — Jordan's identity is layered from the civilisations that lived on its land: Ammonite, Moabite, Nabataean and more
4. **Jordanian cultural heritage** — The dabka dance, inscribed by UNESCO, folk costumes that change from region to region, wedding songs and the manqala game carry Jordan's intangible heritage
5. **Models from Jordanian heritage** — The jaha delegation asks for a bride with its great coffee cup, mansaf crowns the feasts, makmoura and musakhan come from village ovens, and saj and taboon breads carry the taste of the badia and the countryside
