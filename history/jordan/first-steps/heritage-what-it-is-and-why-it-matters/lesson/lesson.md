# Heritage What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Our customs and traditions; Customs and traditions; folk costumes; Culture; Jordanian cultural heritage; Models from Jordanian heritage. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our customs and traditions

Jordanian society keeps its authentic customs — welcoming guests, helping the needy, wearing traditional dress — and children visit a folk-life museum to see them.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Customs and traditions; folk costumes

A girl finds a photo of her grandmother's old dress; Jordan's folk dress (thobe, shemagh and egal) differs between desert and countryside and is part of Jordanian identity kept across centuries.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Culture

Culture's elements include history itself — Jordan's identity is layered from the civilisations that lived on its land: Ammonite, Moabite, Nabataean and more.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — Jordanian cultural heritage

The dabka dance, inscribed by UNESCO, folk costumes that change from region to region, wedding songs and the manqala game carry Jordan's intangible heritage.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — Models from Jordanian heritage

The jaha delegation asks for a bride with its great coffee cup, mansaf crowns the feasts, makmoura and musakhan come from village ovens, and saj and taboon breads carry the taste of the badia and the countryside.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
