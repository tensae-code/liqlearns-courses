# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Interviewing grandparents; Home activity. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Interviewing grandparents

Pupils interview a grandfather or grandmother about the nature of life long ago and send it to the teacher.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — Home activity

Pupils visit an archaeological site in their own governorate with their family and write three facts they learned.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Pupils interview a grandfather or grandmother about the nature of life long ago and send it to the teacher. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Find History in Your Own Locality. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Pupils interview a grandfather or grandmother about the nature of life long ago and send it to the teacher.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson's story echoes in real life. Note it.
