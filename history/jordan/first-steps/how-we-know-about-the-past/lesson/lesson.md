# How We Know About the Past

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Old coins; The Umariyya Covenant as a document; Primary and secondary historical sources. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Old coins

A Nabataean coin of King Obodas II, an Umayyad dinar minted in Amman in 79 AH / 699 CE, and the first Jordanian dinar of 1949 — coins are evidence that names kings, cities and dates.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Umariyya Covenant as a document

The class reads Umar ibn al-Khattab's covenant to the people of Iliya (Jerusalem) word by word — safety for their lives, property, churches and crosses — a primary text analysed in class.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — Primary and secondary historical sources

In a museum the learner separates first-hand evidence — inscriptions, Islamic coins, an Umayyad papyrus dated 24 January 722, Qur'an manuscripts — from later accounts, and tests both against the Umm Qais Treaty document of 20 September 1920.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
