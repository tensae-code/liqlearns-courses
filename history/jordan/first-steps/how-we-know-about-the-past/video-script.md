# Video script — How We Know About the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A Nabataean coin of King Obodas II, an Umayyad dinar minted in Amman in 79 AH / 699 CE, and the first Jordanian dinar of 1949 — coins are evidence that names kings, cities and dates.

Old coins.

A Nabataean coin of King Obodas II, an Umayyad dinar minted in Amman in 79 AH / 699 CE, and the first Jordanian dinar of 1949 — coins are evidence that names kings, cities and dates.

The Umariyya Covenant as a document.

The class reads Umar ibn al-Khattab's covenant to the people of Iliya (Jerusalem) word by word — safety for their lives, property, churches and crosses — a primary text analysed in class.

Primary and secondary historical sources.

In a museum the learner separates first-hand evidence — inscriptions, Islamic coins, an Umayyad papyrus dated 24 January 722, Qur'an manuscripts — from later accounts, and tests both against the Umm Qais Treaty document of 20 September 1920.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
