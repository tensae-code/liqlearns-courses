# Lesson plan — Dating the Past

**Course:** History — Jordan · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Dating the Past
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know the Past and Heritage at Home (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pupils order the Prophet's birth, the revelation, the two Hijras and a battle on a timeline.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know the Past and Heritage at Home — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 6 unit-end timeline exercise** — Pupils order the Prophet's birth, the revelation, the two Hijras and a battle on a timeline
2. **Unit 2 closing skills lesson: the calendar** — Pupils compare the Gregorian (solar, from Christ's birth) and Hijri calendars and place Jordan's events — 1921, 1956, Independence Day, Karameh 1968, 1999 — on a timeline

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **What History Is** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Pupils order the Prophet's birth, the revelation, the two Hijras and a battle on a timeline.
2. Give one real example of it from the story as taught — a person, a place and the turn in their fate.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of Jordan, the region and the world you can draw yourself. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as the books themselves work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the world of Dating the Past as Jordan's books show it — the people, the place and the turn at the centre of this story — the student points to who is there, where it happens and what turns, and says each aloud.
