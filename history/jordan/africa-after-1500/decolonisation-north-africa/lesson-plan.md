## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Liberation in the Maghreb, the Libya part** — Italy holds Libya from 1912; Omar Mukhtar leads the popular resistance for nine years until, wounded and thrown from his horse, he is captured and hanged after a show trial in 1931, aged eighty — and the poet Ahmad Shawqi elegises him
2. **The Tunisia and Morocco parts** — France imposes her protectorate on the Bey of Tunis in 1881, fights Arabic and crushes every national movement, yet cannot stop the struggle even when Axis troops land in 1942; in Morocco the Treaty of Fes (1912) makes a protectorate, Spain takes the Rif, Tangier goes international — until France and Spain must combine against al-Khattabi's Rif revolution, the Berber Dahir backfires, and Muhammad V returns as king of a united Morocco
3. **The Algeria part** — Messali Hadj's Algerian People's Party (1937) demands separation from France and Arabic as the official language, while Ferhat Abbas offers wartime loyalty for rights; the answer comes in an eight-year revolution (1954–1962) that unites every Algerian force, wins Africa and the UN to its side, and costs more than a million martyrs before France negotiates
4. **Liberation in the Nile Valley, the Egypt part** — Ahmad Urabi's revolution of 1881 rallies all Egypt against foreign interference and fails; two days after the 1918 armistice Saad Zaghlul asks to plead Egypt's case in Paris, is refused, arrested and exiled to Malta — and Egypt erupts in the revolution of 1919; King Farouk succeeds Fuad in 1936, Britain forces the Wafd's Nahhas on him in wartime, and the demand to cancel the 1936 treaty rolls toward the Free Officers
5. **The Sudan part** — Muhammad Ali's Egypt rules Sudan until Britain takes Egypt in 1882 and the two impose the joint Condominium of 1899; the Mahdist revolution of 1880 had already risen against Egyptian rule; after the Second World War Ismail al-Azhari's National Unionist Party wins the elections, parliament votes self-determination, and the British and Egyptian armies withdraw
