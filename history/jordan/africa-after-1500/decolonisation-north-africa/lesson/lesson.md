# Decolonisation North Africa

## Do it first

Before you read, draw a line across a page. At the left end write "before", at the right end "after". Under it, write the names of the parts of this story in the order you expect to meet them: Liberation in the Maghreb, the Libya part; The Tunisia and Morocco parts; The Algeria part; Liberation in the Nile Valley, the Egypt part; The Sudan part. As you read, check your order against the story's own order — history is learned by following what turned, and when.

## Sub lesson 1 — Liberation in the Maghreb, the Libya part

Italy holds Libya from 1912; Omar Mukhtar leads the popular resistance for nine years until, wounded and thrown from his horse, he is captured and hanged after a show trial in 1931, aged eighty — and the poet Ahmad Shawqi elegises him.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Tunisia and Morocco parts

France imposes her protectorate on the Bey of Tunis in 1881, fights Arabic and crushes every national movement, yet cannot stop the struggle even when Axis troops land in 1942; in Morocco the Treaty of Fes (1912) makes a protectorate, Spain takes the Rif, Tangier goes international — until France and Spain must combine against al-Khattabi's Rif revolution, the Berber Dahir backfires, and Muhammad V returns as king of a united Morocco.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Algeria part

Messali Hadj's Algerian People's Party (1937) demands separation from France and Arabic as the official language, while Ferhat Abbas offers wartime loyalty for rights; the answer comes in an eight-year revolution (1954–1962) that unites every Algerian force, wins Africa and the UN to its side, and costs more than a million martyrs before France negotiates.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 4 — Liberation in the Nile Valley, the Egypt part

Ahmad Urabi's revolution of 1881 rallies all Egypt against foreign interference and fails; two days after the 1918 armistice Saad Zaghlul asks to plead Egypt's case in Paris, is refused, arrested and exiled to Malta — and Egypt erupts in the revolution of 1919; King Farouk succeeds Fuad in 1936, Britain forces the Wafd's Nahhas on him in wartime, and the demand to cancel the 1936 treaty rolls toward the Free Officers.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — the next part builds on exactly this.

## Sub lesson 5 — The Sudan part

Muhammad Ali's Egypt rules Sudan until Britain takes Egypt in 1882 and the two impose the joint Condominium of 1899; the Mahdist revolution of 1880 had already risen against Egyptian rule; after the Second World War Ismail al-Azhari's National Unionist Party wins the elections, parliament votes self-determination, and the British and Egyptian armies withdraw.

Hold the turn in mind: who is in this part, where it happens, and what changes because of it — this is where the lesson's story lands.
