# Lesson plan — Crime and Punishment in Medieval England

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Crime and Punishment in Medieval England
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — England's Story: Conquest, Crime and Punishment (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Medieval justice was shared between local communities, the king and the Church, and royal power slowly pulled more of it to the centre.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — England's Story: Conquest, Crime and Punishment (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1.1 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Anglo-Saxon England** — local communities, the king and the Church share justice; 1066 rewrites what counts as crime.
2. **1.2 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in Norman England** — William tightens royal grip with the Forest Laws, the murdrum fine and trial by combat.
3. **1.3 Crime, punishment and law enforcement in the later Middle Ages** — Henry II centralises justice, yet manor courts and local punishments carry on below.
4. **1.4 Case study: the influence of the Church on crime and punishment** — the Church offers sanctuary and Benefit of Clergy; Henry II fails to cut that power back.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
