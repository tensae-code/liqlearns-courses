**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Ancient World · **Lesson:** Anglo-Saxon and Norman England

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In one generation, conquest remade England: a disputed succession brought invasion in 1066, and William rebuilt land, law, church and government around Norman power.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1.1 Anglo-Saxon society** — two million people under king, earls, thegns, ceorls and slaves, with unusually fluid status.
2. **1.2 The last years of Edward the Confessor and the succession crisis** — childless Edward leans on the Godwins; Harold's embassy and Tostig's exile in 1065 leave the succession open.
3. **1.3 The rival claimants for the throne** — Harold claims the deathbed promise and the Witan; William and Hardrada challenge him, and Hardrada falls at Stamford Bridge.
4. **1.4 The Norman invasion** — William lands at Pevensey; at Hastings a rumour of his death nearly breaks his army before Harold's shield wall is drawn downhill.
5. **2.1 Establishing control** — the earls submit at Berkhamstead; William rewards followers, builds castles and creates the Marcher earldoms.
6. **2.2 The causes and outcomes of Anglo-Saxon resistance, 1068–71** — Edwin and Morcar, Edgar the Aethling and Hereward raise risings that are bought off or crushed in turn.
7. **2.3 The legacy of resistance to 1087** — the Harrying of the North devastates the region, then the transfer of land to Normans is completed.
8. **2.4 Revolt of the Earls, 1075** — Norman earls rebel with Waltheof in 1075 and are defeated by loyal Normans and English together.
9. **3.1 The feudal system and the Church** — William rebuilds society as a feudal ladder and Normanises the Church under Lanfranc.
10. **3.2 Norman government** — William keeps Anglo-Saxon government, strengthens sheriffs, and orders the Domesday survey of 1085-86.
11. **3.3 The Norman aristocracy** — the new lords display superiority in stone, dress and French speech; Bishop Odo rises and falls.
12. **3.4 William I and his sons** — the Conqueror quarrels with Robert; at his death in 1087 Normandy and England are split between his sons.
