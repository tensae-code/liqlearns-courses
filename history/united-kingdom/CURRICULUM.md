# History — United Kingdom course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for the United Kingdom inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** the United Kingdom's books in hand are
four Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History student books (three from Pearson
Education Limited, 2016, and the Weimar and Nazi Germany book from
Hodder Education, 2016), all COMPLETE as filed. The placements
file's book order is the teaching order, earliest books first.
Inside that order, the ladder follows the studies as the books
themselves present them: the historian's craft first, stocked
from all four books' own method spreads (Level 1); England's own
story next — the Norman depth study and the thousand-year crime
and punishment thematic study with its Whitechapel environment
study (Level 2); the Russia and the Soviet Union depth study,
1917–1941 (Level 3); and the Weimar and Nazi Germany depth study,
1918–39 (Level 4). The ladder is a recommended path, never a
forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Edexcel GCSE History student books — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: The Historian's Craft** — what history itself is — change, continuity and turning points — how the past is dated on one line, how evidence becomes a written history, and why historians' interpretations differ: What History Is · Dating the Past · From Evidence to a Written History · Historiography: The History of History Writing — 4 lessons.

2. **England's Story: Conquest, Crime and Punishment** — Anglo-Saxon England conquered and remade after 1066, then one thread followed for a thousand years — crime, law enforcement and punishment from medieval villages through the Bloody Code, Peel's police and Pentonville to abolition — closing in Whitechapel in 1888, where policing met its limit in the Ripper murders: Anglo-Saxon and Norman England · Crime and Punishment in Medieval England · Crime and Punishment in Early Modern England · Crime and Punishment, 1700 to 1900 · Crime and Punishment Since 1900 · Whitechapel: Policing and the Ripper Murders — 6 lessons.

3. **Russia and the Soviet Union: Revolution to Terror** — two revolutions in 1917, the Bolsheviks holding power through civil war and terror, Stalin's seizure of power and rule by terror, propaganda and cult, collectivisation and the Five-Year Plans, and everyday life in the 1930s: 1917 Twice: February and October in Russia · The Bolsheviks in Power, 1917 to 1924 · Stalin Seizes Power and Rules by Terror · Collectivisation and the Five-Year Plans · Life in Stalin's Soviet Union — 5 lessons.

4. **Germany: Weimar, Hitler and the Nazi State** — a republic born from defeat and blamed for Versailles, attacked from left and right, steadied in borrowed golden years, broken by the Depression, and replaced step by step by a dictatorship that controlled courts, churches, arts and sport, remade daily life, and persecuted its minorities: Weimar Germany: Origins and Early Challenges · The First World War: Versailles and the Results · Weimar Recovery and the Golden Years · Hitler's Rise to Power, 1919 to 1933 · The Great Depression, 1929 · Nazi Dictatorship, 1933 to 1934 · Nazi Control, Propaganda and Opposition · Life in Nazi Germany, 1933 to 1939 · Nazi Persecution of Minorities — 9 lessons.

**Totals: 24 lessons** (Level 1: 4 · Level 2: 6 · Level 3: 5 · Level 4: 9 — 102 placements stocked, all on student books; 22 lessons carry sub lessons; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only — every book in this course is a student book, as filed in ABOUT.md).
