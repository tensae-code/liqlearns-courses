# Lesson plan — From Evidence to a Written History

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From Evidence to a Written History
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A written history is built from evidence: you weigh sources and interpretations, then shape them into a focused answer.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Writing Historically spreads and Preparing for your exams (Thematic study and Historic Environment)** — after each chapter, evidence becomes focused writing, and sample answers show how an answer improves.
2. **Sources and Interpretations work throughout (Bayeux Tapestry, Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, William of Malmesbury)** — learners test what the Tapestry, the Chronicle and later chroniclers can each prove, and where each is partisan.
3. **Sources and Interpretations and Writing Historically spreads** — posters, photographs and historians' extracts are weighed on every spread and built into answers.
4. **Exam-skill sections throughout (inference, utility and causation questions, Revise and practice)** — each Germany chapter closes with source and interpretation skills and model paragraphs.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: A written history is built from evidence: you weigh sources and interpretations, then shape them into a focused answer.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: An annotated sample answer at two levels shows a learner exactly how the same evidence becomes a stronger piece of writing.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in the United Kingdom, as the books themselves work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Copies a source as if it were the whole story -> Ask who made it, when and why, then check it against a second source before writing.
- Go back to the one thing: A written history is built from evidence: you weigh sources and interpretations, then shape them into a focused answer. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
