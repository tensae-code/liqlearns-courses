# Lesson plan — Historiography: The History of History Writing

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Historiography: The History of History Writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Historians can study the same events and reach different interpretations, because they select different evidence and write from different positions and times.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Explaining why historians' interpretations differ** — rival readings of Lenin and Stalin show how evidence, timing and the historian's position produce different histories.
2. **2.4 What are interpretations of history?, 3.3 How interpretations differ and 5.5 Why the interpretations differ** — paired readings of 1923, the recovery and the lean years show how selection and timing change the same years.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- **From Evidence to a Written History** (`first-steps/from-evidence-to-a-written-history/`) is recommended first — it stands just before this lesson on the path (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Historians can study the same events and reach different interpretations, because they select different evidence and write from different positions and times.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: Rival readings of Lenin and Stalin, such as Volkogonov's, show the same revolution told two defensible but different ways.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in the United Kingdom, as the books themselves work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks one differing account must simply be lying -> Compare what evidence each historian selected and when they wrote before judging either one.
- Go back to the one thing: Historians can study the same events and reach different interpretations, because they select different evidence and write from different positions and times. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
