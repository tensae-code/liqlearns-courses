# Video script — Historiography: The History of History Writing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Historians can study the same events and reach different interpretations, because they select different evidence and write from different positions and times.

Why do historians disagree about the same events? The Russia book compares rival readings of Lenin and Stalin, Volkogonov's among them, to show the machinery: different evidence survives and is selected, the historian writes at a different time with different archives open, and their own position shapes the questions they ask. Different histories of 1917 are made, not simply found.
The Germany book teaches the same skill three times over: paired historians' readings of the crisis year 1923, of the so-called golden years, and of the lean years are set side by side. Selection and timing do the work: the same years can be made to look safe, or built on quicksand, depending on what the historian chooses and when they stop the story.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the United Kingdom.
