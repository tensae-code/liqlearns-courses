# Lesson plan — What History Is

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What History Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History studies change over time: what changed, what stayed the same, and what turned a society in a new direction.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **About change: change, continuity, turning points and the factors that drive change** — change is not always progress, and technology and attitudes together turn a society.
2. **Thinking Historically: Cause & Consequence, with Writing Historically and Preparing for your exam** — cause and consequence as the depth study's organising idea, practised as structured writing.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson on the path — nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: History studies change over time: what changed, what stayed the same, and what turned a society in a new direction.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: Britain's first mobile phone call in 1985 shows the pattern: new technology and new attitudes together can turn everyday life.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in the United Kingdom, as the books themselves work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks change always means progress -> The books open by showing change can help, harm, or do both at once; a turning point is judged by what followed it, not by whether it was new.
- Go back to the one thing: History studies change over time: what changed, what stayed the same, and what turned a society in a new direction. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
