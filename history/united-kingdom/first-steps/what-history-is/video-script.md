# Video script — What History Is

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
History studies change over time: what changed, what stayed the same, and what turned a society in a new direction.

The book opens with a warning: change is not the same as progress. When Britain made its first mobile phone call in 1985, a new device met new attitudes about being reachable, and together they began turning everyday life. A historian's first job is to spot that kind of turn, name what drove it, and judge what it really changed.
The Anglo-Saxon and Norman depth study is organised around two questions: why did things happen, and what followed from them? Learners meet that thinking on every spread, then practise turning causes and consequences into structured historical writing, the same skill the exam will ask for.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the United Kingdom.
