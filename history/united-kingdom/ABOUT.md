# About the History — United Kingdom course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History student books (Pearson
Education Limited and Hodder Education, English, 2016). The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from
us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons
are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for the United Kingdom inside the History
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation
is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by
what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it
as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
Legends and religious-history stories are told as the books
tell them, as plot summaries in our own words.

The course as built has **four levels**, following the books'
own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft.** What
   history is, dating the past, evidence into written
   history, and why interpretations differ — stocked from
   all four books' own method spreads.
2. **Level 2 — England's Story: Conquest, Crime and
   Punishment.** The Anglo-Saxon and Norman depth study,
   then the thousand-year thematic study of crime, law
   enforcement and punishment, closing with its Whitechapel
   historic-environment study.
3. **Level 3 — Russia and the Soviet Union: Revolution to
   Terror.** The modern depth study of Russia 1917–1941,
   whole.
4. **Level 4 — Germany: Weimar, Hitler and the Nazi State.**
   The modern depth study of Germany 1918–39, whole.

All 24 of the 24 lessons stand on **student books** and count
in the acceptance reckoning below; no lesson stands on
teacher-guide evidence only, and the filing carries no
teacher guide at all.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Four school books, three countries' stories, one craft:*
  "Begin with the craft itself: what change is, how a
  timeline holds a thousand years, how evidence becomes
  writing, and why honest historians disagree. Then walk
  England's own story: a kingdom conquered in 1066 and
  counted in Domesday, and a thousand years of crime and
  punishment, from sanctuary and the murdrum fine through
  the Bloody Code to Peel's police, Pentonville's silence
  and a Whitechapel beat in 1888. Cross to Russia: two
  revolutions in one year, a civil war, and Stalin's terror,
  plans and posters. End in Germany: a republic born from
  defeat, golden years built on loans, a depression, and a
  dictatorship built step by legal step. That is the United
  Kingdom's own school history, from its own books, and it
  is yours."

## Contents map (combined books)

No combined book stands in this filing: each of the four
books is a History book, and every part of each book that
the filing places is a History part placed in this course.
No part is named-not-placed in the filing. Book 1's linked
historic-environment depth study (Whitechapel, its sections
5.1–5.5) is placed in full as the lesson **Whitechapel:
Policing and the Ripper Murders**, as filed; the four books'
method apparatus (timelines, Writing Historically spreads,
Sources and Interpretations work and exam-skill sections) is
placed in the Level 1 craft lessons, as filed, not treated
as apparatus and skipped.

- **Book 4 (Weimar and Nazi Germany, 1918–39)** — a modern
  depth study of Germany's road from defeat to dictatorship,
  chapter by chapter around sources and rival
  interpretations, with a named exam-skill section closing
