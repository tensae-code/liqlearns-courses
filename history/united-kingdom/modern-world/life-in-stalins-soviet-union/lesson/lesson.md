## Sub lesson 1 — 4.3 Life in the Soviet Union in the 1930s

Behind the posters lay ordinary life: queues for food and goods, families sharing communal flats and one kitchen, Stakhanovite heroes on the walls. Schooling and healthcare genuinely expanded, and many felt life improving. And over everything lay the everyday fear that a knock at the door at night meant arrest, for oneself or for a neighbour who then vanished.

## Sub lesson 2 — 4.4 The position of women and ethnic minorities

Industrialisation drew women into paid work in huge numbers, a real emancipation and a double shift, since the domestic burden at home stayed theirs as well. The Union's republics saw their national cultures first fostered, languages written and theatres opened, then subordinated to Moscow's line, their leaders purged alongside everyone else's.
