# Lesson plan — Life in Stalin's Soviet Union

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Life in Stalin's Soviet Union
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Russia and the Soviet Union: Revolution to Terror (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Everyday Soviet life in the 1930s mixed real gains in schooling, health and work with queues, crowded flats and the fear of a knock at night.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Russia and the Soviet Union: Revolution to Terror (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **4.3 Life in the Soviet Union in the 1930s** — queues, communal flats and everyday fear stand behind the posters, beside expanded schooling and healthcare.
2. **4.4 The position of women and ethnic minorities** — women are drawn into work in huge numbers but keep the domestic burden; republics' cultures are fostered, then subordinated.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Everyday Soviet life in the 1930s mixed real gains in schooling, health and work with queues, crowded flats and the fear of a knock at night.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: Women entered work in huge numbers, yet the domestic burden at home stayed on their shoulders too.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in the United Kingdom, as the books themselves work.
