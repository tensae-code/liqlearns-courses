# Collectivisation and the Five-Year Plans

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — 4.1 Agriculture and collectivisation

Stalin forced the peasants into collective farms, liquidated the kulaks, the better-off peasants, as a class, and seized the grain the villages needed to live. Ukraine and other regions starved in millions while the state continued to export food abroad. Collectivisation fed the cities and the Plans, and broke the peasantry that had fed Russia for centuries.

## Sub lesson 2 — 4.2 Changes in industry

From 1928-29 the Five-Year Plans set heroic targets for coal, steel, electricity and tractors, and managers and workers were driven, by exhortation and by fear, to meet them. Magnitogorsk rose from the steppe; Stakhanovites were celebrated for superhuman shifts. A backward agricultural country industrialised at breakneck speed, and the speed itself was part of the brutality.
