# Video script — Life in Nazi Germany, 1933 to 1939

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The regime remade daily life: women pressed toward home and children, schools and youth Nazified, and full employment bought with rearmament, frozen wages and longer hours.

The regime's ideal woman lived by Kinder, Küche, Kirche: children, kitchen, church. She married early, bore many 'Aryan' children for the nation, and left paid work and politics to men. The ideal was taught in schools, celebrated in propaganda and rewarded in policy, a deliberate reversal of everything Weimar's new women had claimed.
Policy pushed the ideal hard: marriage loans forgiven child by child, and the Mother's Cross awarded in bronze, silver and gold for large families. The birth rate rose. Yet by the late 1930s a labour shortage quietly drew women back into the factories and offices the ideology said were not theirs. Necessity edited doctrine, without ever admitting it.
Education was rebuilt as indoctrination. Rust's ministry Nazified the curriculum: race science taught as biology, maths problems calculated in bombs and 'aliens', history retold as the Führer's story. Elite Napolas and Adolf Hitler Schools trained a future leadership caste, and teachers were sworn to the regime and organised in its league. The classroom became the regime's first recruiting office.
Outside school, the Hitler Youth swallowed childhood itself. All rival youth groups were banned and membership became compulsory. Seven million boys drilled, marched and camped in training for soldiering, while girls of the League of German Maidens trained for motherhood and service. Many quietly stopped turning up when they could, a small evasion inside a total claim.
The six million unemployed were erased by a combination of work and accounting. The Reich Labour Service put young men in uniform with spades, autobahn building employed thousands directly, and rearmament poured orders into the factories. At the same time Jews and many women were struck off the count, and conscription absorbed the young. Full employment was real work, real rearmament, and a doctored register at once.
Workers' lives were sweetened and squeezed together. Strength through Joy offered cheap holidays, concerts and cruises no worker could have afforded before, and savers paid in for a Volkswagen almost none received. Meanwhile wages were frozen at depression levels, the unions that might have protested were gone, and hours lengthened. Whether workers were better off is a judgement the book teaches learners to argue both ways.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the United Kingdom.
