# Lesson plan — Hitler's Rise to Power, 1919 to 1933

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Hitler's Rise to Power, 1919 to 1933
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Germany: Weimar, Hitler and the Nazi State (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Hitler built a fringe party into a national movement, failed in a putsch, waited through lean years, and was handed power in January 1933 after depression and backstairs intrigue.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **5.1 Hitler's early career** — a failed art student is radicalised in Vienna and Munich, finds his voice as an army informant, and is sent to spy on the party he ends up leading.
2. **5.2 The early growth of the Nazi Party** — Drexler's DAP becomes the NSDAP with a 25-point programme; speeches, the SA and a newspaper build a movement.
3. **5.3 The Munich Putsch** — the Munich Putsch fails under police fire, but the trial gives Hitler a platform and prison gives him Mein Kampf.
4. **5.4 The lean years, 1924–28** — banned then refounded, the party reorganises nationally with Goebbels' propaganda, yet wins only 12 seats in 1928.
5. **6.2 Growth in support for the Nazis** — fear of communism, posters, rallies, SA spectacle and Hitler's flights turn the Nazis into the party of the desperate and the frightened.
6. **6.3 Political developments in 1932** — Brüning falls, Papen and Schleicher intrigue, the Nazis become the largest party, and Hindenburg appoints Hitler Chancellor on 30 January 1933.
