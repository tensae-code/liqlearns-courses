# Video script — Hitler's Rise to Power, 1919 to 1933

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Hitler built a fringe party into a national movement, failed in a putsch, waited through lean years, and was handed power in January 1933 after depression and backstairs intrigue.

Hitler's early career is a story of failure turning political. A failed Austrian art student, radicalised in Vienna and Munich, found his voice as an army informant after the war, lecturing soldiers against the revolution. Sent to spy on a tiny workers' party in Munich, he discovered he could hold a room, and stayed to lead it.
Drexler's small DAP became the NSDAP, with a 25-point programme mixing nationalism, antisemitism and promises to workers. Hitler's speeches were the engine; the SA, the party's brownshirted strong-arm arm, and its newspaper turned a Munich fringe group into a movement with a style of its own: flags, uniforms, spectacle and violence.
In November 1923 Hitler burst into a Munich beer hall to seize Bavaria and march on Berlin. Police fire stopped the march in the street. By any plain measure the putsch failed, yet the trial gave Hitler a national platform to defend himself, and a comfortable prison term gave him the time to dictate Mein Kampf. Failure became the making of him.
Banned after the putsch, the party was refounded and reorganised into a national machine, with Goebbels building its propaganda and local branches everywhere ready for a breakthrough. The breakthrough refused to come: in the recovery-era election of 1928 the Nazis won just 12 seats. Voters with jobs and hopes ignored them. The machine waited for misery to feed it.
After the Crash, the machine found its fuel. Fear of communism drove businessmen and landowners to fund and favour the Nazis; Goebbels' posters and rallies filled the streets; SA spectacle promised order through strength; and Hitler flew across Germany, landing in city after city like a saviour from the sky. The Nazis became the party of the desperate and the frightened alike.
In 1932 the republic's last defences were political intrigues. Brüning fell; von Papen and Schleicher schemed against each other for power; the Nazis became the largest party in July. President Hindenburg detested Hitler, but Papen talked him into appointing Hitler Chancellor on 30 January 1933, believing the conservatives could tame him in a cabinet where Nazis were outnumbered. It was the republic's fatal miscalculation.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the United Kingdom.
