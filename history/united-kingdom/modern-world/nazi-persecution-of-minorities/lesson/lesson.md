# Nazi Persecution of Minorities

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — 13.1 Nazi racial beliefs

At the doctrine's centre stood a ladder of races: 'Aryans' at the top, Jews at the bottom, and every people ranked between. This was prejudice turned into state doctrine, taught in schools, printed in charts and used to mark whole peoples for exclusion from the national community. Once the ladder was official, every later persecution could present itself as administration.

## Sub lesson 2 — 13.2 The persecution of the Jews

The persecution of the Jews advanced step by legal step. The boycott of Jewish shops in 1933 marked them out; the Nuremberg Laws of 1935 stripped them of citizenship and forbade marriages; and Kristallnacht in November 1938 smashed shops and synagogues across Germany in a state-organised pogrom. Each step drove Jews further from German life, and each step tested how far the next could go.

## Sub lesson 3 — 13.3 The treatment of minorities

Jews were not the only targets. Roma, gay men, disabled people and those labelled 'asocial' were registered, forcibly sterilised, sent to camps or killed, described in official language as burdens on the community, lives unfit for the Volksgemeinschaft, the people's community. The ladder of races had a bottom with many names on it, and exclusion slid, group by group, toward murder.
