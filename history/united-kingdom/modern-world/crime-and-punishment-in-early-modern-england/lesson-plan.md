# Lesson plan — Crime and Punishment in Early Modern England

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Crime and Punishment in Early Modern England
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — England's Story: Conquest, Crime and Punishment (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Between 1500 and 1700, religious division and growing towns created new crimes and new ways of policing and punishing them.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — England's Story: Conquest, Crime and Punishment (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **2.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1500–c1700** — after 1517, heresy, treason, vagabondage and witchcraft are newly criminalised.
2. **2.2 Law enforcement and punishment, c1500–c1700** — towns outgrow the hue and cry; constables and the night watch appear, and transportation begins.
3. **2.3 Case study: the crimes and punishment of the Gunpowder Plotters, 1605** — the Gunpowder Plotters of 1605 are caught and executed with exemplary harshness.
4. **2.4 Witchcraft and the law, c1500–c1700** — a society believes in witchcraft; Hopkins hunts in the 1640s, then belief fades by about 1700.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Between 1500 and 1700, religious division and growing towns created new crimes and new ways of policing and punishing them.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: Matthew Hopkins hunted witches in the 1640s, yet within two generations witchcraft had stopped being prosecuted as a serious crime.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
