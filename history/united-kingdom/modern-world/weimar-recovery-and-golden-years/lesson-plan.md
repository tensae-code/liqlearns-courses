# Lesson plan — Weimar Recovery and the Golden Years

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Weimar Recovery and the Golden Years
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Germany: Weimar, Hitler and the Nazi State (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From 1924 Stresemann steadied Germany at home and abroad, and cities enjoyed golden years, but the recovery rested on borrowed American money and left many Germans out.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Germany: Weimar, Hitler and the Nazi State (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **3.1 Reasons for economic recovery** — the Rentenmark, Dawes Plan loans and restarted reparations steady the economy, on borrowed money.
2. **3.2 Stresemann's policies abroad** — Locarno, League entry in 1926 and the Young Plan restore Germany's standing; Stresemann dies weeks before the Crash.
3. **4.1 The standard of living** — wages and welfare rise, yet farmers, the Mittelstand and those ruined in 1923 never share the recovery.
4. **4.2 The position of women** — women vote, work and adopt new freedoms in the cities, while much of Germany expects them back in the home.
5. **4.3 Cultural changes** — Bauhaus, cabaret, cinema and art flourish without censorship, and opponents call the culture decadent.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
