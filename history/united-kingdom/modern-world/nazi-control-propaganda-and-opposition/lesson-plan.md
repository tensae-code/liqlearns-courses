# Lesson plan — Nazi Control, Propaganda and Opposition

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Nazi Control, Propaganda and Opposition
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Germany: Weimar, Hitler and the Nazi State (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The regime watched Germany through a police state, licensed its courts, churches, arts and sport, and filled every home with one message, yet small oppositions still refused.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Germany: Weimar, Hitler and the Nazi State (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **8.1 The development of the Nazi police state** — SS, Gestapo and SD watch the whole population, and camps hold the regime's enemies.
2. **8.2 The Nazi control of the legal system** — judges join the Nazi lawyers' league and the People's Court tries treason without appeal.
3. **8.3 Nazi policies towards the Church** — a Concordat buys quiet while a Reich Church is built; resisting pastors like Niemöller end in camps.
4. **8.4 Goebbels and the Ministry of Propaganda** — Goebbels' ministry licences every journalist, actor and musician; cheap radios, rallies and film carry one message home.
5. **8.5 The Nazi control of the Arts** — jazz and modern art are banned as degenerate while folk and monumental art glorify the regime.
6. **8.6 The Nazi control of sport** — sport is Nazified through the clubs, and the 1936 Berlin Olympics showcase Aryan Germany to the world's cameras.
7. **9.1 The extent of support for the Nazi regime** — many Germans conform for jobs, order and pride restored; the book weighs real popularity against fear.
8. **9.2 Opposition from young people** — Edelweiss Pirates and swing gangs refuse in small ways, and the regime answers with prison and the gallows.
9. **9.3 Opposition from the Churches** — Niemöller's Confessional Church and Bishop Galen's sermons defend faith; Niemöller spends 1937-45 in prison and camps.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
