# Lesson plan — Crime and Punishment, 1700 to 1900

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Crime and Punishment, 1700 to 1900

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** As trade and cities grew, so did organised crime, and Britain answered first with a harsher death penalty, then with police, prisons and reform.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900** — highway robbery, poaching and smuggling gangs grow with trade and taxes; union men are punished.
2. **3.2 Changing attitudes to punishment, c1700–c1900** — the Bloody Code peaks at 222 capital crimes, then opinion turns toward prisons and reform.
3. **3.3 Law enforcement, c1700–c1900** — the Bow Street Runners begin the shift from catching thieves to preventing crime.
4. **3.4 Case study: the separate system at Pentonville Prison** — Pentonville, opened 1842, tries silence and isolation to reform prisoners, then softens.
5. **3.5 Case study: the reforms of Robert Peel** — Peel cuts the capital statutes, reforms prisons and founds the Metropolitan Police in 1829.
