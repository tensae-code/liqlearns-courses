# Video script — 1917 Twice: February and October in Russia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Russia had two revolutions in 1917: hunger and war toppled the Tsar in February, and a weak provisional government let the Bolsheviks seize power in October.

Russia in early 1917 was a vast peasant empire ruled by the Tsar, and the war was breaking it. Wages had doubled, but food prices had quadrupled. Peasants hoarded grain rather than sell it for worthless money, and town workers queued hungry outside empty shops. The empire's machinery, its railways, its food supply, its army, was failing all at once.
In February 1917 Petrograd's crowds took to the streets over bread, the soldiers sent to clear them refused to fire and joined them, and the Duma stepped in to form a government. Nicholas II abdicated. The turn of this story is its strangeness: nobody planned the revolution that toppled a dynasty of 300 years. It happened because nobody would defend the old order.
The Provisional Government under Kerensky shared power uneasily with the soviets, the workers' and soldiers' councils, in a double authority that satisfied neither side. It kept Russia in the hated war and delayed the land reform peasants were seizing for themselves. Support bled away, until the Kornilov affair, a feared military coup, forced it to arm the very radicals who would overthrow it.
In October the Bolsheviks struck. Lenin supplied the leadership and the slogan, 'Bread, Peace, Land', Trotsky the organisation of the seizure itself. Against them stood a government nobody would defend. Power was taken in Petrograd in a few days, and the second revolution of 1917 opened the road to a one-party state.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the United Kingdom.
