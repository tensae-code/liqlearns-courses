# Nazi Dictatorship, 1933 to 1934

## Do it first

Think of one place, person or event from your own life in the United Kingdom that connects to this lesson's story: Within eighteen months Hitler turned a chancellorship into a complete dictatorship: emergency decree, Enabling Act, opposition removed, the SA purged, and the army's oath to him personally. Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

## Sub lesson 1 — 7.1 The importance of the Reichstag fire

On 27 February 1933 the Reichstag building burned. The Nazis blamed the communist van der Lubbe, and within a day an emergency decree suspended civil rights across Germany: free speech, free press and freedom from arrest without charge were gone. Thousands of communists and opponents were arrested before the March election, which was held under a legal terror.

## Sub lesson 2 — 7.2 The importance of the Enabling Act

The Enabling Act of March 1933 was the dictatorship's legal foundation. With communist deputies arrested or in hiding and SA men lining the chamber, the Reichstag voted to let Hitler legislate without parliament or president for four years. Parliament had voted itself out of power, and every later act of the regime could dress itself in this law.

## Sub lesson 3 — 7.3 The removal of opposition

Through 1933 Gleichschaltung, coordination, brought Germany's institutions into line one by one: state governments taken over, the trade unions smashed on 2 May and replaced, and party after party dissolved or banned until, by July, the Nazi Party was the only political organisation left standing. A whole civil society was switched off in six months.

## Sub lesson 4 — 7.4 The importance of the Night of the Long Knives

Röhm and the SA leadership wanted a second revolution, absorbing the army itself into their brownshirt millions. The army's generals demanded the threat be removed. On 30 June 1934, the Night of the Long Knives, Hitler had Röhm and the SA leaders murdered, along with other old enemies. He had bought the army's loyalty with his oldest comrades' blood.

## Sub lesson 5 — 7.5 The support of the army

When Hindenburg died in August 1934, Hitler merged the offices of Chancellor and President in himself as Führer. The army, bought the month before, swore its oath of loyalty not to the constitution but to Hitler personally. Every check was now gone: lawmaker, head of state and commander in one person, with the army sworn to the man. The dictatorship was complete.
