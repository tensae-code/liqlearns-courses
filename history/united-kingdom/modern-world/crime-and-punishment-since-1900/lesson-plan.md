# Lesson plan — Crime and Punishment Since 1900

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Crime and Punishment Since 1900
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — England's Story: Conquest, Crime and Punishment (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Since 1900, changing attitudes and new technology have kept redefining crime itself, while punishment moved from execution toward rehabilitation.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — England's Story: Conquest, Crime and Punishment (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **4.1 Crime and definitions of crime, c1900–present** — 1960s attitudes decriminalise acts such as homosexuality in 1967, while technology creates new crimes.
2. **4.2 Law enforcement, c1900–present** — 200 local forces give way to fingerprints, the 999 number, forensic science and community policing.
3. **4.3 Changes in punishment, c1900–present** — execution shrinks, young offenders are treated separately from 1908, and rehabilitation rises.
4. **4.4 Case study: conscientious objectors in the First and Second World Wars** — conscription in 1916 makes refusing to fight a crime; objectors are sorted, imprisoned or sent to the front unarmed.
5. **4.5 Case study: the Derek Bentley case and the abolition of capital punishment** — Bentley is hanged amid doubts; the scandal drives the 1957 defence and the abolition campaign.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
