# Stalin Seizes Power and Rules by Terror

## Do it first

Think of one place, person or event from your own life in the United Kingdom that connects to this lesson's story: After Lenin's death Stalin outmanoeuvred every rival, then ruled through terror, censorship and a cult that rewrote the past around himself. Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

## Sub lesson 1 — 3.1 The struggle for power, 1924–29

Lenin died in 1924 leaving a Testament that warned the party against Stalin. Stalin posed instead as Lenin's loyal disciple, the keeper of the flame, and outmanoeuvred his rivals one wing at a time: first Trotsky with one alliance, then that alliance's leaders with another. By 1929 he stood alone at the top, and the NEP was ended.

## Sub lesson 2 — 3.2 The use of terror in the 1930s

In the 1930s terror became a system with targets. The NKVD worked to arrest quotas, so people were seized for a scribble across Stalin's photograph in a newspaper, for a joke, or for nothing at all. Purges emptied the party, the army and the professions, show trials staged confessions to impossible crimes, and the gulag camps swallowed millions.

## Sub lesson 3 — 3.3 Propaganda and censorship

Propaganda and censorship reached into every art. Socialist realism conscripted novels, paintings and music to show life not as it was but as it was supposed to become. Writers were named 'engineers of souls', and posters called out 'Woman, get on the locomotive!' The aim was openly stated: to manufacture a new kind of person, the Soviet man and woman.

## Sub lesson 4 — 3.4 The cult of Stalin

The cult of Stalin rewrote the past itself. Posters, paintings, poems and songs showed Stalin as Lenin's intimate friend and the true planner of October 1917, roles that had not been his, while Trotsky and others were painted out of photographs and history alike. A living leader became the story of the revolution, and the story was compulsory.
