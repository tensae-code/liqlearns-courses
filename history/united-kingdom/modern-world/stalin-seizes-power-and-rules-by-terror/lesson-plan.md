# Lesson plan — Stalin Seizes Power and Rules by Terror

**Course:** History — United Kingdom · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Stalin Seizes Power and Rules by Terror
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Russia and the Soviet Union: Revolution to Terror (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** After Lenin's death Stalin outmanoeuvred every rival, then ruled through terror, censorship and a cult that rewrote the past around himself.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Russia and the Soviet Union: Revolution to Terror (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **3.1 The struggle for power, 1924–29** — Lenin's Testament warns against Stalin; Stalin poses as the loyal disciple and outmanoeuvres each rival in turn.
2. **3.2 The use of terror in the 1930s** — the NKVD works to arrest quotas; purges, show trials and the gulag follow.
3. **3.3 Propaganda and censorship** — socialist realism conscripts art; 'engineers of souls' set out to manufacture a new kind of person.
4. **3.4 The cult of Stalin** — posters, poems and songs rewrite the past, showing Stalin as Lenin's intimate friend and October's planner.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: After Lenin's death Stalin outmanoeuvred every rival, then ruled through terror, censorship and a cult that rewrote the past around himself.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: NKVD officers worked to arrest quotas, so a scribble on Stalin's photograph, or nothing at all, could be enough to be seized.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
