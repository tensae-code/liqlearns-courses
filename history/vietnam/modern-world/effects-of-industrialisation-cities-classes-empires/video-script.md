# Video script — Effects of industrialisation cities classes empires

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Effects of industrialisation cities classes empires.

Economically, the machines change how production is organised, create many new materials and raise labour productivity, and transform agriculture, transport and communications as well; the steel table tells the power shift — from 1880 to 1900 England's steel output rises from 1.3 to 4.9 million tonnes (+377%), the USA's from 1.2 to 10.2 (+850%) and Germany's from 0.7 to 6.4 (+910%), and whereas in 1860 England and France stood first and second in world industrial production, by 1913 the USA and Germany hold those places; socially, the revolutions create many new industrial centres that are also populous cities — London, Manchester, Paris, Berlin — and form two opposing classes, the industrial bourgeoisie, who own the means of production, and the proletariat, who work for wages, their contradiction sharpening day by day into the proletariat's struggles against the bourgeoisie; culturally, the industrial lifestyle spreads, spiritual life is enriched by the appearance of the telephone, radio and cinema, and cultural exchange between countries and continents is strengthened — while the book's warning box names the negative face: environmental pollution, the exploitation of women's and children's labour, invasion and rivalry over colonies.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
