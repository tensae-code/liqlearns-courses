# Video script — Northeast asia since 1991

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Northeast asia since 1991.

The World Bank figures in the opener give China, Japan and Korea alone a fifth of world GDP and a quarter of its people; Japan stagnates through alternating recession and weak growth yet stays a great economic-financial centre (GDP $3,580 billion in 1991, $5,010 billion in 2021; life expectancy near 85) until China passes it in 2010 after four decades as the world's second economy; South Korea, one of the four Asian "dragons", shifts decisively to high-tech industry — Samsung, LG, Hyundai — multiplying GDP some 5.5 times (1991–2021) to a top-ten ranking with per-capita income near $35,000; China sets the goal of a "socialist market economy", grows for thirty years at the world's highest rate to $17,820 billion (about 18% of world GDP in 2021), builds growth-pole regions from Pudong to Chengdu, and from 2013 pursues the Belt and Road initiative — though the book notes low growth quality, pollution and a wide rich–poor gap.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
