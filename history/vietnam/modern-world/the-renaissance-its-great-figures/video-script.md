# Video script — The renaissance its great figures

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: The renaissance its great figures.

Renaissance literature reaches its peak across poetry, the novel and drama — Dante Alighieri with the Divine Comedy and the Vita Nuova, Petrarch with his lyric poems, Giovanni Boccaccio with the Decameron (Mười ngày), François Rabelais of France with Gargantua and Pantagruel, Miguel de Cervantes of Spain with Don Quixote, and above all William Shakespeare of England with Hamlet, Othello and Romeo and Juliet; art begins in Italy and spreads across Europe, peaking in the 15th–16th centuries with Leonardo da Vinci (the Last Supper, the Mona Lisa), Michelangelo (the David, the Pietà) and Raphael (the Sistine Madonna, the School of Athens — pictured with the Greek scholars gathered in it); architecture stresses geometry, symmetry and proportion, its typical work the dome of St Peter's Basilica in Italy (the Vatican); and the age's outstanding science is astronomy — Nicolaus Copernicus of Poland with the heliocentric theory, Giordano Bruno of Italy proving the Sun is only the centre of the Solar System, and Galileo Galilei of Italy, who makes the telescope to observe the sky — while technique advances in weaving, mining, metallurgy, shipbuilding and weapons-making, machines are built, and water power is used in many branches of production.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
