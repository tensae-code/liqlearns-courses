# China carved up and the xinhai revolution — matching

Match each part with its story.

**1.** Bài 14, China becomes the empires' prey — a) (Sub lesson 1)
**2.** Bài 14, the Xinhai Revolution of 1911 and Sun Yat-sen — b) (Sub lesson 2)

Meanings:
a) in 1840 Britain makes war on the pretext that the Qing have seized and burned British merchants' opium; the Treaty of Nanjing (1842) opens five ports, exacts an indemnity and takes Hong Kong; then the spheres are drawn — Germany in Shandong, Britain in the Yangtze basin, Russia and Japan in the Northeast, France in Yunnan, Guangxi and Guangdong — until the Boxer Protocol of 1901 leaves China, in the book's formula, a feudal semi-colony
b) the Qing edict of May 1911 "nationalising" the railways in fact hands their rights to the empires, and outrage ignites the revolution; on 10 October 1911 the rising wins at Wuchang and spreads through the south and centre; the Republic of China is founded with Sun Yat-sen — a Guangdong peasant's son educated in Japan, America and England — as provisional president, but by February 1912 the Qing emperor's abdication forces Sun to yield the presidency to Yuan Shikai; the quoted assessment credits Xinhai with ending thousands of years of feudal monarchy and influencing all Southeast Asia including Vietnam, while faulting it for leaving the landlords, the peasants' land hunger and the imperialists untouched

**Answer key:** 1. a; 2. b
