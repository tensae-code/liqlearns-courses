# Scientific revolution and enlightenment — short answer

Answer in your own words, from the lesson.

**1.** Tell the story of "Bài 2, the Enlightenment thinkers prepare the French Revolution" — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** Montesquieu argues for limiting the king's power and securing citizens' freedoms, Voltaire for an enlightened monarchy, freedom of belief and the right of property, and Rousseau that monarchy must be abolished altogether for a republic — the ideas that, in the book's words, clear the road for the revolution to explode.

**2.** Tell the story of "Bài 13, the great scientific achievements of the 18th–19th centuries" — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Newton's law of universal gravitation (with the apple anecdote — watching an apple fall straight down in the family garden, he asks why it never falls sideways or upward), Lomonosov's law of the conservation and transformation of energy, Darwin's theory of evolution; beside them the social sciences advance — German materialism and dialectics in Feuerbach and Hegel, English political economy in Adam Smith and Ricardo, utopian socialism in Saint-Simon, Fourier and Owen — until Marx and Engels' scientific socialism makes, in the book's phrase, a revolution in human thought.
