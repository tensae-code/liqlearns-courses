# Southeast asia under western colonialism

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Southeast asia under western colonialism" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 4, how the Western powers entered Southeast Asia

The book's table runs country by country — Portugal takes Malacca in 1511 (its ship, the Flor do Mar, is now a museum there) and the eastern Indonesian islands; Spain seizes most of the Philippines by the mid-16th century and rules 350 years; the Dutch complete the conquest of Indonesia by the mid-19th century; Britain, France and the Netherlands contest Malaya, Burma and Indochina; Siam is entered by traders in the 16th century and pressed only after Britain has taken India and parts of Malaya and Burma.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 4, Southeast Asia under colonial rule

Native rulers surrender, collaborate or reign as puppets while colonial officials govern; the economy is plundered — land seized for plantations, no heavy industry allowed, railways and ports built for extraction and repression; Western culture erodes traditions and "enslavement" policies aim to assimilate; and a quoted Vietnamese history of the region shows the method — the British first merged Malaya, Singapore and Brunei into one unit, then split them into four separate regimes, so that each piece could be ruled more easily.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 4, the peoples resist, 16th–mid-19th centuries

On Mactan island in 1521 Lapu-Lapu's people resist the Spanish landing; Indonesia rises against the Dutch under Trunojoyo (1675), Surapati (1683–1719) and Diponegoro (1825–1830); Burma's army under General Bandula fights the first British invasion (1824–1826) until Bandula falls in 1825 — all these risings fail, the book notes, but none of the peoples submits quietly.
