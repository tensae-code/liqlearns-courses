# Lesson plan — Southeast asia under western colonialism

**Course:** History — Vietnam · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Southeast asia under western colonialism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Revolutions, industry and a divided country: the bourgeois revolutions and industrialisation beside Vietnam's partition, the Tây Sơn, the Nguyễn and the first French colonial exploitation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Southeast asia under western colonialism as Vietnam's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — Revolutions, industry and a divided country: the bourgeois revolutions and industrialisation beside Vietnam's partition, the Tây Sơn, the Nguyễn and the first French colonial exploitation — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Bài 4, how the Western powers entered Southeast Asia** — the book's table runs country by country — Portugal takes Malacca in 1511 (its ship, the Flor do Mar, is now a museum there) and the eastern Indonesian islands; Spain seizes most of the Philippines by the mid-16th century and rules 350 years; the Dutch complete the conquest of Indonesia by the mid-19th century; Britain, France and the Netherlands contest Malaya, Burma and Indochina; Siam is entered by traders in the 16th century and pressed only after Britain has taken India and parts of Malaya and Burma
2. **§2 Bài 4, Southeast Asia under colonial rule** — native rulers surrender, collaborate or reign as puppets while colonial officials govern; the economy is plundered — land seized for plantations, no heavy industry allowed, railways and ports built for extraction and repression; Western culture erodes traditions and "enslavement" policies aim to assimilate; and a quoted Vietnamese history of the region shows the method — the British first merged Malaya, Singapore and Brunei into one unit, then split them into four separate regimes, so that each piece could be ruled more easily
3. **§3 Bài 4, the peoples resist, 16th–mid-19th centuries** — on Mactan island in 1521 Lapu-Lapu's people resist the Spanish landing; Indonesia rises against the Dutch under Trunojoyo (1675), Surapati (1683–1719) and Diponegoro (1825–1830); Burma's army under General Bandula fights the first British invasion (1824–1826) until Bandula falls in 1825 — all these risings fail, the book notes, but none of the peoples submits quietly
