# Lesson plan — Vietnam dang ngoai peasant uprisings

**Course:** History — Vietnam · **Title:** Modern world · **Lesson:** Vietnam dang ngoai peasant uprisings
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Revolutions, industry and a divided country: the bourgeois revolutions and industrialisation beside Vietnam's partition, the Tây Sơn, the Nguyễn and the first French colonial exploitation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Vietnam dang ngoai peasant uprisings as Vietnam's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — Revolutions, industry and a divided country: the bourgeois revolutions and industrialisation beside Vietnam's partition, the Tây Sơn, the Nguyễn and the first French colonial exploitation — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Bài 7, why Đàng Ngoài exploded: the crisis of the Lê–Trịnh regime** — the chronicles quoted here do the accusing — in 1730 tens of thousands of Hải Dương people are conscripted to dig rivers and haul bricks for Lord Trịnh Giang's pagodas; the lord's palace keeps four or five hundred arrogant eunuchs "hated and feared by the whole country"; a tax is laid on every trade, so that for the silk tax people cut down their mulberry trees and for the fish tax hide their nets; famine follows, and "a hundred đồng cannot buy one full meal"
2. **§2 Bài 7, the great risings of the 18th-century North** — hoàng Công Chất (1739–1769) gathers the poor, builds his base at Mường Thanh in the far north-west, protects the border and settles people's lives so the Tây Bắc peoples support him to the end, and his son fights on after him; Nguyễn Danh Phương (1740–1751) holds the Tam Đảo mountains until he is captured; Nguyễn Hữu Cầu (1741–1751), the "Quận He" who swims like the great he fish that never harms a soul at sea, strikes from Đồ Sơn, threatens Thăng Long itself and ranges into Thanh Hoá and Nghệ An — all fail, but they shake the Lê–Trịnh regime to its foundations and clear the ground for Tây Sơn
