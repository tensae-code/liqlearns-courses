# Video script — Cuban revolution 1959

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Cuban revolution 1959.

Latin America is the United States' "backyard" of pro-American dictatorships, yet national-democratic revolutions stir from Bolivia to Nicaragua (the book's box follows the Sandino Front to its 1990 defeat by US intervention); in Cuba, Batista's US-backed dictatorship (March 1952) is attacked at the Moncada barracks by Fidel Castro and 135 youths (July 1953); exiled in Mexico, Castro founds the July 26 Movement, lands from the Granma with 81 fighters (November 1956), and on 1 January 1959 enters Havana — the Republic of Cuba then builds socialism from 1961, turning a sugar monoculture toward machine-building and energy, with the region's best education and life expectancy up from 64 (1960) to 74 (1991).

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
