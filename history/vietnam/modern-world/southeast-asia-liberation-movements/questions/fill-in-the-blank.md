# Southeast asia liberation movements — fill in the blank

Fill each blank from the lesson.

**1.** Fill in the blank: The story of "Bài 15, the liberation movements of Southeast Asia, later 19th–early 20th centuries" turns on this: __________. (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** By the century's end only Siam keeps a relative independence — Britain completes Burma's conquest in 1885 and makes it a province of India, America takes the Philippines from Spain in 1898, France holds all Indochina; the struggles answer — Aceh fights 3,000 Dutch troops ashore in 1873 and Sumatra and Kalimantan rise again and again until the railway unions (1905, 1908) and the Indonesian Communist Party (1920) appear; Cavite rises in 1872, Rizal's reform and Bonifacio's violence prepare the revolution of 1896–98 that founds the Philippine Republic before America annexes it — Rizal himself, executed in 1896, has his square in Manila today; in Indochina the book names Vietnam's Cần Vương (1885–1896) and Yên Thế (1884–1913), Cambodia's risings from Acha Xoa to Prince Sivotha (1861–1892), Laos's Phakadouk (1901–1903) and Ong Kẹo (1901–1907), and notes the first fighting alliance of the three peoples against the common enemy.

**2.** Fill in the blank: The story of "Bài 3, Southeast Asia's liberation movements between the wars" turns on this: __________. (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Communist parties are founded across the region (Indonesia May 1920, Vietnam early 1930, Malaya and Siam April 1930, the Philippines November 1930); the Java and Sumatra risings of 1926–27 and Vietnam's Nghệ-Tĩnh soviets of 1930–31 are drowned in blood, while bourgeois parties — the Indonesian National Party, Burma's Thakin movement, the Malay youth union — and older struggles like Laos's Ong Kẹo and Com-ma-dam (1901–1937) and Cambodia's monk A-cha Hem-chieu (1930–35) keep the cause alive.
