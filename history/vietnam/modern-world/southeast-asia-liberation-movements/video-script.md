# Video script — Southeast asia liberation movements

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Southeast asia liberation movements.

By the century's end only Siam keeps a relative independence — Britain completes Burma's conquest in 1885 and makes it a province of India, America takes the Philippines from Spain in 1898, France holds all Indochina; the struggles answer — Aceh fights 3,000 Dutch troops ashore in 1873 and Sumatra and Kalimantan rise again and again until the railway unions (1905, 1908) and the Indonesian Communist Party (1920) appear; Cavite rises in 1872, Rizal's reform and Bonifacio's violence prepare the revolution of 1896–98 that founds the Philippine Republic before America annexes it — Rizal himself, executed in 1896, has his square in Manila today; in Indochina the book names Vietnam's Cần Vương (1885–1896) and Yên Thế (1884–1913), Cambodia's risings from Acha Xoa to Prince Sivotha (1861–1892), Laos's Phakadouk (1901–1903) and Ong Kẹo (1901–1907), and notes the first fighting alliance of the three peoples against the common enemy.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
