# Vietnam phan boi chau and dong du

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Vietnam phan boi chau and dong du" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 19, Phan Bội Châu and the road abroad

Phan Bội Châu (1867–1940) of Nghệ An, a Hương examination laureate, concludes after Cần Vương's defeat that Vietnam must seek foreign help — "by whatever means restore the country"; in 1904 he founds the Hội Duy tân, in 1905 goes to Japan for arms and money, and launches the Đông du movement sending patriotic youths to study there; expelled in 1909, he founds the Việt Nam Quang phục hội in Guangdong in 1912 to "drive out the French, restore Vietnam, found the Democratic Republic of Vietnam", sends assassins home in 1913, and is arrested and jailed in Guangdong — the end of his most active phase.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 12 Phan Bội Châu and Phan Châu Trinh go abroad: Phan Bội Châu works Japan and China — 200 youths sent to study under Đông Du (1905), the Điền–Quế–Việt and East Asia alliances (1908), then the Vietnam Restoration Society at Guangzhou (early 1912) and feelers to German and Russian representatives; Phan Châu Trinh works France from 1911 — petitions to the government, exposure of colonial rule to French opinion, and a hand in founding patriotic Vietnamese organisations there

The two rival roads of the 1900s — borrowed armies versus borrowed consciences — are both, at root, foreign-policy operations.
