# The industrial revolution textiles steam and rail — short answer

Answer in your own words, from the lesson.

**1.** Tell the story of "Bài 6.1, the First Industrial Revolution" — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**Answer:** The first industrial revolution runs from the later 18th century, beginning in England and spreading to many countries of Europe and North America — a quoted definition fixes its essence: its starting point is the appearance of machines, and in substance it is a technical revolution, the leap from manual labour to machine labour; the advances come first in textiles: James Hargreaves' spinning jenny (1764) works 16–18 spindles under one worker and multiplies productivity many times over the hand wheel, Richard Arkwright's water-powered spinning machine (1769) makes thread finer and stronger, Edmund Cartwright's steam-powered loom (1785) raises the weaver's productivity nearly 40 times, and James Watt's improved steam engine (1784) creates a new source of power that starts England's industrialisation; the internal combustion engine follows (John Barber 1791, Samuel Brown 1823), metallurgy and transport advance together — Henry Cort's puddling method of smelting ore (1784), explained in the box as blowing hot air to melt the ore into iron, simpler and yielding much iron for machines and goods — and the first steam locomotive runs on rails in 1804 (Richard Trevithick); abroad, Robert Fulton applies the steam engine to build the first passenger steamship in the USA in 1807, Belgium industrialises from the early 19th century around steel, coal mining and textiles, and France, slowed by revolution and political instability, industrialises from about 1830 — in twenty years (1830–1850) its pig-iron output triples and its railway length grows a hundredfold, and by mid-century France is basically an industrial country.
