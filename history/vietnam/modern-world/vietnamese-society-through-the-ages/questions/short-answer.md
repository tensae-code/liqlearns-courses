# Vietnamese society through the ages — short answer

Answer in your own words, from the lesson.

**1.** Tell the story of "Chuyên đề 1, II.3, Vietnamese society from Văn Lang to Đổi mới" — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**Answer:** In Văn Lang–Âu Lạc society already divides into king, lạc hầu and lạc tướng aristocracy, commoners and a few slaves, organised in village communities (làng, chiềng, chạ) that remain the base unit through the Chinese-rule centuries, when a Han landlord-official class confronts the Vietnamese while the villages keep wide autonomy; the independent monarchies raise cities — Thăng Long the capital, the ports Vân Đồn, Phố Hiến, Thanh Hà, Hội An — where a "market" quarter grows beside the citadel, officials are chosen by examination beside the hereditary aristocracy, and residents fall into the four groups: scholars, farmers, artisans, merchants; colonial rule breaks the frame — great landlords and landless tenant farmers in Nam Kì, French officials, workers, bourgeoisie and petty bourgeoisie appear, and most Vietnamese are pushed down to "slaves who have lost their country"; the modern ages follow — the working people's new society of 1945–54, the divided decades, the unified subsidy years sliding into crisis, and Đổi mới's diversified society of entrepreneurs and foreign residents.

**2.** State the one thing this lesson teaches, in your own words.
**Answer:** The story of Vietnamese society through the ages as Vietnam's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
