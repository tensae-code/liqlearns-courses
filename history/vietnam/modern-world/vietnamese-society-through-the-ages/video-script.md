# Video script — Vietnamese society through the ages

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story — an animated map: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson tells one story: Vietnamese society through the ages.

In Văn Lang–Âu Lạc society already divides into king, lạc hầu and lạc tướng aristocracy, commoners and a few slaves, organised in village communities (làng, chiềng, chạ) that remain the base unit through the Chinese-rule centuries, when a Han landlord-official class confronts the Vietnamese while the villages keep wide autonomy; the independent monarchies raise cities — Thăng Long the capital, the ports Vân Đồn, Phố Hiến, Thanh Hà, Hội An — where a "market" quarter grows beside the citadel, officials are chosen by examination beside the hereditary aristocracy, and residents fall into the four groups: scholars, farmers, artisans, merchants; colonial rule breaks the frame — great landlords and landless tenant farmers in Nam Kì, French officials, workers, bourgeoisie and petty bourgeoisie appear, and most Vietnamese are pushed down to "slaves who have lost their country"; the modern ages follow — the working people's new society of 1945–54, the divided decades, the unified subsidy years sliding into crisis, and Đổi mới's diversified society of entrepreneurs and foreign residents.

That is the turn of this story — remember who was in it, where it happened, and what came of it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
