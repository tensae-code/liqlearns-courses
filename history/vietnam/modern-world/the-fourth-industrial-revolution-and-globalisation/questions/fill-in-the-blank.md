# The fourth industrial revolution and globalisation — fill in the blank

Fill each blank from the lesson.

**1.** Fill in the blank: The story of "Bài 21, Industry 4.0, globalisation and Vietnam" turns on this: __________. (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** Industry 4.0 — the term first used in Germany in the early 2010s for the union of IoT, Big Data, AI and cloud — shortens the road from invention to production and binds science, technique and technology into one; for Vietnam the book quotes the Party's Resolution 52-NQ/TW (27 September 2019): the Fourth Industrial Revolution "brings both opportunities and challenges" — seize them to raise productivity and competitiveness, guard against becoming a mere assembly workshop, against information insecurity, and against AI's pressure on jobs; the same revolution drives globalisation from the 1980s onward — in trade and global finance, in the sharing and interpenetration of cultures, and in the growing weight of regional and international organisations from the EU and ASEAN to the United Nations.

**2.** Fill in the blank: The story of "Bài 7.2–3, the Fourth Industrial Revolution and the meaning of the modern revolutions" turns on this: __________. (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** The fourth industrial revolution (Industry 4.0) begins in the first years of the 21st century and is still unfolding, bound up with breakthroughs in artificial intelligence (AI), the Internet of Things (IoT), cloud computing, Big Data, 3D printing, automation, new materials, gene technology and nanotechnology — 3D printing builds a house with under 50% of the labour, 60% less waste and about 60% lower cost than traditional building (part of a Dubai building printed in 3D is pictured), and nanotechnology works at the scale where 1 nanometre is a billionth of a metre, applied in medicine, biology, energy, electronics, mechanics, garments, food and environmental protection; the opener is a person — the robot Sophia, granted citizenship by the Saudi Arabian government on 25 October 2017, the first robot citizen in the world, able to communicate, show emotion and lecture, interviewed in Vietnam in 2018; the meaning sections follow: economically, smart factories applying automation and AI multiply productivity many times over, e-commerce lets consumers approach global trade, and the modern revolutions push regionalisation and globalisation of the world economy; socially, automation frees human labour especially in dangerous and toxic work, the workforce polarises toward high skills, remote work spreads — but the warning box counts the costs: AI makes many workers jobless and widens the rich–poor gap, personal-information security is threatened, false information spreads, and over-reliance on AI in education can weaken independent thinking; culturally, searching and sharing information becomes easy and cultural exchange between countries and regions quick and convenient.
