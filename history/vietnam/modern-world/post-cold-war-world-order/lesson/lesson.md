# Post cold war world order

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Post cold war world order" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 18, a multipolar order forms

The USSR's collapse in 1991 ends the Yalta two-pole order (1945–1991) — the book's box defines it: two superpowers, America's capitalist system against the USSR's socialist system; confrontation gives way to dialogue and détente, peace becomes the main trend though local civil wars and ethnic and religious conflicts persist, and a multipolar order forms around the US, EU, Japan, China and the Russian Federation; America aspires to a US-dominated unipolar order but cannot achieve it — the EU deepens its integration, Japan seeks a matching political position, Russia recovers, and China, the world's second economy since 2010, is America's fiercest superpower rival (the lesson closes with a US–China trade-war cartoon).

## Sub lesson 2 — Chuyên đề 2.III.1 Wars that did not stop: Bosnia 1992–1995 kills about 100,000 over ethnic and religious divides; the Israel–Palestine conflict grinds on; Libya, Somalia and Sudan slide into civil war, hunger and epidemic

The Cold War ends but the shooting does not — the Balkans, Gaza and Africa keep the century's habit.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chuyên đề 2.IV.2 The peace struggle after the Cold War: the movement takes up poverty, environment, pandemics and globalisation's harms; Global Zero's 300 leaders campaign to abolish nuclear weapons; Vietnam sends its first Level-2 field hospital to UN peacekeeping in South Sudan in 2018

Yesterday's victim of war becomes today's blue-helmet doctor.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bài 3 The world after the Cold War: the Berlin Wall's fall as symbol; the new trends are the economy at the centre of every state's strategy, accelerating globalisation, dialogue replacing confrontation, and multipolarity — no single state can impose its will — with the US–China gap shrinking from 12-to-1 in GDP in 2000 to about 1.3-to-1 by 2021, and the G20 (1999) gathering two-thirds of humanity

The two-pole world gives way to a many-centred one, and Vietnam's own Đổi Mới is Exhibit A of the economy-first turn.
