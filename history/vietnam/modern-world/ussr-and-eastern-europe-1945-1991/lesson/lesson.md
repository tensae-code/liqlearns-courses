## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 10, the USSR: recovery, zenith, dissolution

The USSR rebuilds by 1950 — industry up 73% in the 1946–50 plan, over 6,000 factories restored or built — and by the 1960s is the second industrial power with some 20% of world industrial output; it launches the first satellite (1957) and Gagarin (1961) and gives free education to a population three-quarters secondary- or university-educated (1971); stagnation from the mid-1970s brings Gorbachev (March 1985) and perestroika with glasnost and multi-party politics; after the failed coup of 19 August 1991 the republics secede and on 25 December 1991 Gorbachev resigns and the USSR dissolves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 10, Eastern Europe's people's democracies

With the Red Army's help, people's democracies arise in 1944–46 (the GDR in 1949) and build through land reform, nationalisation and CMEA cooperation — Albania fully electrified by 1970, the GDR's national income quadrupled (1949–70), Bulgarian industry multiplied 55-fold (1939–75); decline follows from the mid-1970s — income growth falls from 3.3% (1981–85) to 0.5% (1989) under $128.6 billion of foreign debt (1987) — and the book photographs Poland's Solidarity strike of August 1980 before the 1989 elections bring anti-socialist forces to power and the CMEA dissolves in 1991.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 4 Socialism spreads across Eastern Europe: the Red Army's victory lets people's democracies rise — Poland and Romania 1944, Hungary, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and Albania 1945, Bulgaria 1946, the GDR 1949 — land reform and nationalisation first, then industrialisation with Soviet help

Behind the advancing Red Army, nine states change their system in five years.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bài 4 Why the model collapsed: growth stalls from the later 1970s, the Berlin Wall falls in 1989, Germany unifies by absorbing the GDR in 1990, and in December 1991 the USSR dissolves — the book names four causes, from the rigid subsidy model to reform that abolished the Party's leading role

The system that won the war against fascism loses the peace to stagnation and its own reforms.
