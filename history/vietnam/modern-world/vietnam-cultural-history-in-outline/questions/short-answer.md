# Vietnam cultural history in outline — short answer

Answer in your own words, from the lesson.

**1.** Tell the story of "Chuyên đề 1, II.1, the course of Vietnamese cultural history" — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. (Sub lesson 1)
**Answer:** The field's object is the whole cultural life of the nation — its achievements, values, traditions and customs; the outline runs from Stone Age traces some 180,000 years old, through the three civilisation centres of the state-founding age — Văn Lang–Âu Lạc (7th century BCE), Lâm Ấp, later Chăm-pa (from 192), and Phù Nam (from about the 1st century CE) — built on wet-rice farming, river and sea life, village communities and ancestor worship, selectively absorbing from India and China; under Chinese rule the culture survives by resisting assimilation, under the independent monarchies it absorbs selectively (and meets the West from the mid-16th century), chữ Quốc ngữ transforms thought, press and literature, the Party's 1943 Outline on Vietnamese Culture sets the principles "nationalise, scientise, popularise", and after August 1945 and into Đổi mới the book sums the culture up in three great traditions — patriotism, solidarity, tolerance and humanity — a unity in diversity.

**2.** Tell the story of "Bài 10.3e–h, Đại Việt's writing and literature, art, and science and technique" — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. (Sub lesson 2)
**Answer:** Chữ Hán is the official script, used in state administrative documents, education and examinations; on its basis the Vietnamese create chữ Nôm, used widely from the 13th century — the box records that by the old histories, in 1282 Hàn Thuyên writes a ritual text in chữ Nôm to drive the crocodiles from the Red River, that Hồ Quý Ly has chữ Nôm used to expound the Confucian classics, and that the great poets Nguyễn Trãi and Lê Thánh Tông both compose in Nôm — and with Catholicism's arrival, from the early 17th century chữ Quốc ngữ appears and is gradually perfected; literature has two parts — folk literature (legends, epics, fairy tales, fables, jokes, proverbs, folk songs) reflecting social life and distilling experience, and written literature mainly in chữ Hán and chữ Nôm: in Hán, Nam quốc sơn hà (anonymous), Hịch tướng sĩ by Trần Quốc Tuấn, Bạch Đằng giang phú by Trương Hán Siêu, Bình Ngô đại cáo by Nguyễn Trãi, Truyền kì mạn lục by Nguyễn Dữ and Thượng kinh kí sự by Lê Hữu Trác; in Nôm, Thiên Nam ngữ lục (anonymous), the Truyện Kiều by Nguyễn Du, Qua Đèo Ngang by Bà Huyện Thanh Quan, and the works of Hồ Xuân Hương; art peaks in the capitals — Hoa Lư (Đinh–Tiền Lê), Thăng Long (Lý–Trần–Lê), Tây Đô (Hồ) and Huế (Nguyễn) — and a quoted source (Phan Huy Lê ed., History of Vietnam, vol. I) describes the palace architecture concentrated in the Thăng Long citadel around the Cần Nguyên palace from the Lý era, the 2003–2004 excavations at the Imperial Citadel site uncovering palace foundations, pillar bases, wells, drainage systems and ornate building materials (phoenix-head and dragon-patterned roof ornaments are pictured), while pagodas, towers, temples, communal houses, shrines and churches rise all over the country (Trấn Quốc pagoda in Hanoi, successor of the Tiền Lý-era Khai Quốc pagoda, and the Lê-restoration Quan Âm statue at Bút Tháp pagoda are pictured); sculpture reaches a high level in architectural carving and statues; folk painting has two main kinds, worship paintings and Tết paintings, mainly woodblock-printed on dó paper and finished by hand, the Lê restoration era giving the famous lines Đông Hồ (Bắc Ninh), Hàng Trống (Hanoi) and Làng Sình (Huế); performing arts divide into court and folk — in 1437 King Lê Thái Tông charges Nguyễn Trãi and Lương Đăng with the court music, and among the people tuồng, chèo and water puppetry (a performance form from the Lý era, pictured) develop widely, singing guilds are founded, ca trù appears around the 15th century at court and spreads into folk life, and hát văn (chầu văn) accompanies the Mother Goddess worship rites; science and technique close the survey: every dynasty values the recording of history and founds specialised agencies — the Lý has its Sử ký under Đỗ Thiện, the Trần founds the Quốc sử viện (National History Institute) and produces the famous Đại Việt sử ký of Lê Văn Hưu, the early Lê court's historians include Phan Phu Tiên, Ngô Sĩ Liên and Vũ Quỳnh and its typical national history is the Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư of Ngô Sĩ Liên, and the Nguyễn founds the Quốc sử quán and compiles the Đại Nam thực lục and the Khâm định Việt sử thông giám cương mục; geography produces the địa chí works — Nguyễn Trãi's Dư địa chí, Bùi Dương Lịch's Nghệ An ký, Lê Quang Định's Hoàng Việt nhất thống dư địa chí, Trịnh Hoài Đức's Gia Định thành thông chí and the Nguyễn court's Đại Nam nhất thống chí — and maps fixing the territory and sea borders, the Hồng Đức bản đồ of the early Lê and the Đại Nam nhất thống toàn đồ of the Nguyễn; mathematics gives Lương Thế Vinh's Đại thành toán pháp and Vũ Hữu's Lập thành toán pháp; military science gives Trần Quốc Tuấn's Binh thư yếu lược and Vạn Kiếp tông bí truyền thư and Đào Duy Từ's Hổ trướng khu cơ, and from the late 14th century the Vietnamese invent large divine cannons and in the 16th–17th centuries cast many kinds of cannon and build cannon-armed warships, absorbing Western technique; and medicine gives Tuệ Tĩnh's Nam dược thần hiệu and Lê Hữu Trác's Hải Thượng y tông tâm lĩnh.
