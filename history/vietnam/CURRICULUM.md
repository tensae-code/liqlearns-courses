# History — Vietnam course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Vietnam inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Vietnam's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** the placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first — and the ladder follows Vietnam's own progression through its books. Vietnam teaches History region by region and story by story first (Grade 4: the child's own locality, then each region of the country in turn, every lesson built around named places, festivals and "Câu chuyện Lịch sử" story boxes), then runs the whole national story in one chronological sweep (Grade 5: first states, a thousand years of northern domination, the dynasties, colonisation, 1945, 1954, 1975, Đổi mới), then rebuilds the subject from first principles in lower secondary (Grade 6: what history is, how evidence works, how time is counted, prehistory, the ancient civilisations, Southeast Asia, Vietnam to the tenth century; Grade 7: the medieval world beside Đại Việt to the Lê sơ; Grade 8: the bourgeois revolutions and industrialisation beside Vietnam's partition, Tây Sơn and the first colonial exploitation; Grade 9: the modern world and Vietnam interleaved, world chapter answered by Vietnam chapter). The upper-secondary books then teach History as a discipline and a civic practice (Grade 10: historiography, heritage, civilisations in widening circles, the four industrial revolutions, the 54 ethnic groups — plus the Fine Arts theory-and-history books, whose genuine History parts are placed with their scope noted), go deep on war and peace, the two world systems and sea sovereignty (Grade 11, with its study topics and art book), and close with beliefs, Japan as a development mirror, the United Nations, ASEAN, the whole 1945-to-today story and Hồ Chí Minh (Grade 12, with its study topics and art book). Each level is one stage of that progression, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
