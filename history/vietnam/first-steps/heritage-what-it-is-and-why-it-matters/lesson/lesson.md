# Heritage what it is and why it matters

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Heritage what it is and why it matters" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 3, the traditional culture of your own locality (festivals, dishes, customs)

Tết bánh chưng wrapping opens a lesson where children catalogue their own province's festivals, foods and customs as things handed down.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 6, festivals and folk arts of the Northern Midlands and Mountains (Gầu Tào, Lồng Tồng, Then singing, Xoè Thái dance, highland markets)

Highland peoples pray for a good year through festival, song and dance that UNESCO listed in 2019 and 2021.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 7, the Đền Hùng temple complex and the Giỗ Tổ Hùng Vương festival

Every year on the 10th day of the 3rd lunar month a palanquin procession climbs to the Upper Temple in Phú Thọ, using even an emperor Khải Định's stele text as a source.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bài 10, village culture of the Red River Delta (village gate, banyan tree, well, communal house, festivals, Quan họ singing)

The old delta village gathers round its đình, where the village tutelary god is worshipped and festivals like Lim and Cổ Loa are held.

## Sub lesson 5 — Bài 13, keeping the Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám site (stelae on UNESCO's Memory of the World register, 2010)

A stele's own words, "talent is the vital energy of the nation", are used to teach children why the site must be preserved.

## Sub lesson 6 — Bài 17, world heritages of Central Vietnam (Mỹ Sơn, the Hồ citadel, Hội An, Huế court music, the Nguyễn dynasty woodblocks, gong culture)

Carved wooden blocks of the Nguyễn dynasty even carry the documents asserting Vietnam's claim to the Hoàng Sa and Trường Sa islands.

## Sub lesson 7 — Bài 20, preserving and promoting Hội An old town

Lantern nights and careful restoration keep an almost intact trading port alive for visitors.

## Sub lesson 8 — Bài 21, the Central Highlands gong festival

Ê Đê, Gia Rai, Ba Na and other peoples speak to their gods and mark births, coming of age and new rice with gongs, in a cultural space UNESCO listed in 2005.

## Sub lesson 9 — Bài 24, Southern Vietnam's river culture (floating houses, floating markets, áo bà ba and khăn rằn)

In the Mekong delta people live, dress and trade on the water, at floating markets like Cái Răng and Ngã Năm.

## Sub lesson 10 — Bài 18, Beijing's Forbidden City

Palace of the Ming and Qing emperors with over 9,000 rooms — and a Vietnamese thread: the captured architect Nguyễn An helped build and repair it under the Ming.

## Sub lesson 11 — Bài 19, Laos's signature heritage (Plain of Jars; Luang Prabang; Thạt Luổng)

Some 2,000 stone jars from 500 BC whose makers are unknown; Luang Prabang, capital of Lan Xang from the 14th century under its first king Pha Ngừm; the 45-metre golden Thạt Luổng stupa on Laos's emblem.

## Sub lesson 12 — Bài 20, Cambodia's Angkor Wat and Angkor Thom

Early-12th-century Angkor Wat's stones are fitted without any mortar and its image flies on Cambodia's flag; Angkor Thom, the Khmer empire's last capital, centres on the Bayon's towers of Buddha faces.

## Sub lesson 13 — Chuyên đề 2, I, what cultural heritage is and how Vietnam ranks it

The topic opens with Festival Huế 2000, organised by Thừa Thiên Huế province with the French Embassy — Vietnam's first international-scale festival, held every two years since; heritage is defined as the system of material and spiritual values a community creates and accumulates over a long history and hands down the generations, and its four meanings are diagrammed — a priceless asset and core value of the community, a livelihood and resource for development, a promoter of peace and solidarity among nations, and a practical contribution to protecting the environment; heritage is classified two ways — material versus spiritual by the human need it serves (food, houses and tools against literature, art and knowledge), tangible versus intangible by its form (relics, antiquities and national treasures against beliefs, folk songs, dances, games and crafts) — and Vietnam's historical-cultural relics are ranked provincial, national and special national by the chair of the provincial People's Committee, the Minister of Culture, Sports and Tourism and the Prime Minister respectively, with the finest (Hạ Long Bay, the Thăng Long Imperial Citadel centre, Hội An) entering UNESCO's World Heritage list.

## Sub lesson 14 — Chuyên đề 2, II, preserving heritage and bringing it to life

Preservation means protecting and keeping a heritage's existence and values in their original form, and it is the foundation — a heritage can only be promoted (brought into social life as a resource) if it first survives as itself, while good promotion creates the material and spiritual means to preserve better; the work must stand on scientific bases: identify the heritage's historical, scientific, educational and economic values, follow the legal framework (Vietnam's Cultural Heritage Law of 2024 and the international conventions), survey the heritage's real state, and balance the interests of state, enterprise, community and individual; the warning case is Dresden's Elbe Valley — listed by UNESCO in 2004, it let a bridge be built across the river to ease city traffic and in 2009 was struck off the World Heritage list for destroying the landscape; the topic closes by assigning roles and duties — the political system makes the legal frame, enterprises supply capital and people, the resident community owns the heritage and plays the key role, citizens take part directly, and schools teach its value.

## Sub lesson 15 — Bài 3.1, historiography and the work of preserving cultural and natural heritage

A heritage's value shows in many aspects — history, culture, architecture, art, natural landscape — and history, as an interdisciplinary science, plays the most important role in affirming that value; UNESCO's guidance is quoted: cultural and natural heritages are priceless and irreplaceable assets, not of one nation only but of humankind — any of them lost, degraded or destroyed impoverishes the heritage treasury of all the world's peoples; historians' research results are the firm scientific basis for preservation, guaranteeing the core requirements — keeping the original elements that compose the relic, its authenticity, its integrity, its outstanding value — and the lesson's photographs set Versailles palace in France, the Huế Imperial City relic complex and Hạ Long Bay side by side as the same kind of trust; tangible heritage built of earth, stone, brick, wood and leaves decays with time, intangible heritage faces oblivion (Xoan-singing artists of Phú Thọ are shown teaching schoolchildren), and natural heritage like Sơn Đoòng cave in Phong Nha–Kẻ Bàng needs protection to keep its biodiversity and scientific value.

## Sub lesson 16 — Bài 3.2, historiography, history and culture in the development of tourism

Each nation's and humankind's historical and cultural values are a precious resource for tourism, above all cultural tourism — Vietnam's Tourism Law of 2010 is quoted defining cultural-tourism resources (historical-cultural relics, revolutionary relics, archaeology, architecture, traditional cultural values, festivals, folk arts and other cultural values, and creative works of human labour usable for tourism); the European figures show the scale — tourism generates over 10% of EU GDP and employs over 10 million people, and cultural heritage makes up about 40% of Europe's tourism value, its unique heritage of museums, theatres, archaeological sites and historic cities making the continent a leading destination — and in 2019 the Thăng Long Imperial Citadel centre alone received 461,715 visitors, 230,459 of them international; the relationship runs both ways: visitors' demand pushes governments and people to protect, restore and promote relics (the restored Ngọ Môn gate in Huế is pictured), and part of tourism revenue is reinvested in preserving, honouring, restoring and managing the heritage.

## Sub lesson 17 — Bài 10.4–5, the meaning of Đại Việt civilisation and preserving the civilisations' values

Đại Việt civilisation marks the nation's strong revival after more than a thousand years of northern domination, showing the enduring vitality of the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc civilisation and the people's indomitable spirit; its brilliant achievements reflect the nation's leap forward in politics, economy and culture, creating the premise for the country to overcome nature's harsh challenges and foreign invasion, and its great values are an internal cultural resource and strength of the Vietnamese nation in today's era of integration and development; preserving and promoting those values keeps the national cultural identity, educates the young generations in tradition and contributes to the country's development — the solutions run from digitising heritage for long-term storage and for research and study, through communication that raises the people's awareness and promotes the values to the world, to education that binds knowledge to real experience; the pupils' own charge is specific: join extracurricular activities and study at museums and relics to feel the creative process and the heritage's value directly, use technology to create digital content (videos, images, articles) introducing the heritage so it reaches people and international friends vividly and fast, and become active "propagandists" for heritage in family, school and society — the civilisation heritage, material and spiritual, is a great cultural resource for developing the cultural industry and tourism.

## Sub lesson 18 — Bài 3 The nation's art memory: the Vietnam Fine Arts Museum, founded in 1966 at 66 Nguyễn Thái Học, Hanoi, keeps and displays some 2,000 typical works across the ancient, early-modern and modern periods so each era's art can be read in sequence

One building in the capital holds the whole visual autobiography of the country.

## Sub lesson 19 — Chuyên đề 1 Worshipping the national heroes: from the maxim "when drinking water, remember the source", temples, shrines and memorials to resistance heroes, village founders, healers and teachers cover the country — the Trưng sisters' temple at Mê Linh among them — and their death-day rites teach each generation whom to imitate

Gratitude is organised into a calendar, so the heroes return every year on schedule.

## Sub lesson 20 — Bài 17 The world's verdict: UNESCO's 24th General Conference (Paris, 1987) resolves that 1990 mark the centenary of Hồ Chí Minh's birth, honouring an outstanding symbol of national self-affirmation whose cultural, educational and artistic contributions crystallise his nation's millennia-old tradition; Osip Mandelstam's 1923 profile already hears in his calm voice "a future culture"; statues and memorials stand from Laos and India to France, Russia, Cuba and Panama, and his name marks squares, schools and streets on four continents

The colonised schoolboy of 1908 is commemorated by the world body's own resolution and in stone on four continents.

## Sub lesson 21 — Bài 1 Exhibiting history itself: among the book's examples of thematic display is Quảng Ninh Museum's 2022 exhibition "Propaganda posters following the flow of Vietnamese revolutionary history", where the revolutionary poster — a primary source of the resistance decades — is curated as public heritage

Yesterday's wall poster becomes today's museum piece, and the exhibition is how the state hands the source to the next generation.
