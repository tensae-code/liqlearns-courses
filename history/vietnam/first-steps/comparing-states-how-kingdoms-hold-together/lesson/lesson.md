## Chuyên đề 3, I.1, three models of the Vietnamese monarchy

The Lý–Trần state is a "centralised monarchy close to the people": the king holds supreme power and the state is unified from centre to locality, but it rules with "reassure the people" policies — in spring 1038 King Lý Thái Tông ploughs the ceremonial field at Bố Hải himself and answers his protesting courtiers (quoted from the Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư): "If I do not plough myself, what will I use for the offerings, and what example will the world follow?" — and in 1300 Trần Quốc Tuấn counsels King Trần Anh Tông: "Relax the people's strength to make the plan deep-rooted and firm — that is the best strategy for keeping the country"; the early Lê state is a bureaucratic centralised monarchy — for the first time the central apparatus is divided into three branches (civil administration, military, supervision: the Six Ministries, Six Courts and Six Offices watched by the Censorate), mirrored locally by the Tam ty; the Nguyễn state is an autocratic centralised monarchy — the king empowers his own helper organs (Grand Secretariat, Imperial Academy, Privy Council), strengthens the judicial and supervisory organs, and after Minh Mạng's reform runs the provinces directly from the court, tightly controlling local power.
