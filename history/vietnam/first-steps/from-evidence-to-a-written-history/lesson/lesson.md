# From evidence to a written history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From evidence to a written history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 1, artefacts and pictures as learning tools

The Cổ Loa bronze arrowheads are held up as surviving things from the past that let us describe people who left no writing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 2, the kinds of source (material, written, oral)

Historians work like detectives: a Ngọc Lũ drum's patterns, Thăng Long citadel finds, the Văn Miếu stelae and Hồ Chí Minh's 1969 Testament are each interrogated for what they can tell.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 1, source exercise and the science of history

At Cebu in the Philippines two memorial plaques tell one event two ways: the Lapu-Lapu plaque records that on 27 April 1521 Lapu-Lapu and the local people repelled the Spanish invaders and killed their commander Ferdinand Magellan, making Lapu-Lapu the first Filipino to drive out a European invader, while the Magellan plaque records that Magellan died that day fighting Lapu-Lapu's warriors of Mactan island, and that the ship Victoria under Juan Sebastián Elcano sailed from Cebu on 1 May 1521 and reached Sanlúcar de Barrameda in Spain on 6 September 1522, completing the first sea voyage around the world — pupils must find what the two plaques share and where they differ, and why; the lesson then defines historiography (Sử học) as the science that studies humankind's past — its object the whole past of individuals, groups, communities, nations, regions and humanity (the pictured book covers are a world history, a national history and a history of Hanoi) — with scientific and social functions and the tasks of cognition, education and prediction: drawing lessons from the past to foresee the future of a country and of humankind.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bài 1 Art history hand in hand with archaeology: survey, exploration, excavation, then analysis — the archaeologist supplies the sources, the art historian verifies the artefact and dates its style; the five research steps from choosing the object to judging its value

Satellite photo first, trowel second, verdict last — the dig writes the first draft of the art's history.

## Sub lesson 5 — Bài 2 How to research an artist: gather from many channels, cross-check the origin of every source (museum and institute research and historical documents rank highest), filter out the unreliable, then systematise the data and set the author inside their historical and social context

Biography done properly is source criticism first and storytelling second.
