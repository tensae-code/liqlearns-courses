# What history is

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "What history is" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 1, History and life: what history is and why study it

From ENIAC (1946) to the laptop, everything changes with time; Hồ Chí Minh's couplet "Our people must know our history" and his 1954 words at Đền Hùng open the subject.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chuyên đề 1, I.3–4, history by field; national history and world history

The same past can be studied field by field — political, military, economic, cultural, social and intellectual history, and also the histories of science and technology, education, religion and belief, literature and diplomacy — each field giving a deep, concrete knowledge that helps explain the whole; a nation's history is the shared history of all communities on its territory from the first humans to today, while world history is not the sum of national histories but the interaction of many peoples, trends and forces across them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 1.1, historical reality and history as people perceive it

History is everything that happened in the past; the past itself (historical reality) exists objectively and cannot be changed, but the history people hold is a reconstruction — told as stories, written down, researched, performed, memorialised, re-enacted in rites and customs — and between the two there is always a gap, because a reconstruction depends on the seeker's needs and abilities, on conditions and methods, and above all on how much authentic evidence survives; the lesson opens with Hanoi's Long Biên bridge (built 1898–1902), a historical object tied to many events of the capital and the country, works the Âu Lạc evidence — bronze arrowheads found at Cổ Loa in 1959 and bronze arrowhead moulds in 2004, beside Tô Hoài's story of the magic crossbow — and tells the folk tale of the blind men feeling an elephant, each certain of his part, to show why no account is the whole elephant.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bài 2.1, why history must be learned and explored for a lifetime

Every person, family, nation and humankind has a past, a present and a future, and to understand the present and trust the future one cannot avoid seeking the past — the old saying runs "review the old, know the new" (ôn cố, tri tân); the Hùng Vương era was first known through folk legends and the giỗ Tổ rites, and over more than half a century historians' research has raised knowledge of it step by step; two voices frame the duty: Ngô Sĩ Liên writes in the Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư that history records events so that good and bad deeds alike serve as mirrors and warnings for later generations, and Hồ Chí Minh's 1942 verse insists "Our people must know our history, to understand clearly the roots of the Vietnamese nation"; some pasts stay mysterious — how Egypt's pyramids were built still has no exact answer — and those gaps and mysteries are themselves the spur to keep exploring; in the age of globalisation, exploring history also teaches the achievements of all civilisations and the respect for difference that integration requires.
