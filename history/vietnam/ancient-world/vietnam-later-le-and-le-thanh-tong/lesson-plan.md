# Lesson plan — Vietnam later le and le thanh tong

**Course:** History — Vietnam · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Vietnam later le and le thanh tong
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The whole national story in one sweep: the first states, a thousand years of northern domination, the dynasties, colonisation, 1945, 1954, 1975 and Đổi mới — with the world's first civilisations beside it (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Vietnam later le and le thanh tong as Vietnam's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — The whole national story in one sweep: the first states, a thousand years of northern domination, the dynasties, colonisation, 1945, 1954, 1975 and Đổi mới — with the world's first civilisations beside it — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Bài 12, the Later Lê dynasty and King Lê Thánh Tông** — lê Lợi founds the dynasty in 1428 at Đông Kinh; under Lê Thánh Tông (1460–1497) come reforms, the Hồng Đức map, the national criminal code, regular examinations and stelae at the Văn Miếu — and a warning that not one inch of the founder's land may be given away
2. **§2 Bài 12, story of Trạng Lường Lương Thế Vinh** — the maths prodigy weighs a Ming envoy's elephant by marking a boat's waterline and swapping the elephant for stones, then writes the Đại thành toán pháp, the age's great maths book
