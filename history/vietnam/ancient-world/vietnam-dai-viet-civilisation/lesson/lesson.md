## Bài 10.1–2, the Đại Việt civilisation: concept, basis and development

Đại Việt civilisation is the material and spiritual cultural creation reaching a high level in the era of the country's independence, from after the Bắc thuộc period to before French colonial rule — the box explains that "Đại Việt" is the country's name from 1054 to 1804, which historians use to name the civilisation of the independence era; it has roots in the ancient civilisations on Vietnam's land, in the water-based civilisation and the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc state and civilisation formed from the 7th century BCE, resting on a wet-rice agricultural economy and village culture; through more than a thousand years under the northern feudal dynasties the Vietnamese residents keep their independence will, rise up continuously and win independence for brief periods, and through more than ten centuries of struggle the Văn Lang–Âu Lạc traditional culture is preserved and nourished like an underground stream, to revive strongly in the independence era; through the Ngô, Đinh, Tiền Lê, Lý, Trần, Hồ and Hậu Lê dynasties the centralised monarchy is built and perfected and Đại Việt becomes a strong state with influence in East and Southeast Asia, while selectively absorbing the civilisations outside — India's, China's, the West's — in thought, politics, education and culture, law and technique, with even Chăm-pa civilisation values such as tower-building technique and rice varieties gradually becoming part of Đại Việt civilisation; the development table runs period by period: Ngô–Đinh–Tiền Lê (after the Bạch Đằng victory of 938 Ngô Quyền proclaims himself king with his capital at Cổ Loa; the Đinh and Tiền Lê rule from Hoa Lư; national independence is fully restored and the folk economy and culture begin to achieve); Lý–Trần–Hồ (in 1010 Lý Thái Tổ moves the capital to Thăng Long, opening the brilliant development of the Đại Việt state, whose Lý–Trần hallmark is tam giáo đồng nguyên, the harmonious blending of Confucianism, Buddhism and Taoism — though by the late Trần and under the Hồ, Confucianism gradually prevails and Buddhism loses its place; and from 1407 to 1427 Ming domination and its policy of cultural destruction damage Đại Việt civilisation heavily); Lê sơ (in 1428 the early Lê is founded and Đại Việt becomes a power in Southeast Asia, its civilisation achieving brilliantly on the basis of Confucianism made the sole orthodoxy, with Confucian education and officials recruited by examination); Mạc–Lê Trung Hưng (in 1527 the Mạc is founded, encouraging industry, commerce and culture — a hallmark of Mạc-era civilisation is its foreign-trade economy — and in the Lê restoration era the civilisation develops toward the folk and begins contact with Western civilisation); and Tây Sơn–Nguyễn before 1858 (at the end of the 18th century the Tây Sơn movement erupts, overthrows the feudal governments of North and South, abolishes the Đàng Trong–Đàng Ngoài division, crushes the Siamese and Qing invasions and founds a dynasty, laying the foundation for national unification; in 1802 the Nguyễn is founded and builds the unified state, and the hallmark of Nguyễn-era civilisation is unity, the differences between regions reduced).
