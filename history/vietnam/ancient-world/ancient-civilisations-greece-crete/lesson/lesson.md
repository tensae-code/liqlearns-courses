## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 25, the history of the Olympic Games

Heracles founds the games, first held in 776 BC; every four years the city-states stop their wars to compete, and winners receive only an olive wreath.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 10, Greece: a sea-divided land and its city-states

Mountains split Greece into small plains fit for vines and olives, but its harbours face the sea; from the 8th century BC dozens of city-states trade through ports like Piraeus.

## Sub lesson 4 — Bài 5.1, the achievements of Greek civilisation

The ancient Greeks build their phonetic alphabet from about the 9th–8th centuries BCE, completed with 24 letters by the late 4th century BCE; Greek literature, fed by mythology, is founded on Homer's two epics, the Iliad and the Odyssey, and its drama gives Aeschylus (the Oresteia, Prometheus Bound), Sophocles (Oedipus Rex) and Euripides (The Women of Troy); its art — the Discus Thrower, the Athena and the Venus de Milo statues, paintings of the Battle of Marathon and the Abduction of Persephone — and its architecture, the Parthenon raised in the 5th century BCE on the Acropolis at Athens, the Temple of Zeus and the Mausoleum, shape Western art ever after; Greek science holds the Earth to be a sphere and the Sun the centre about which the heavenly bodies move, and its names are Thales, Pythagoras, Euclid and Archimedes; medicine begins with Hippocrates, regarded as the father of Western medicine, with diagnosis, herbal treatment, anatomy and anaesthesia; Greek historiography is born in the 5th century BCE with its first historian Herodotus and his History of the Greco-Persian Wars, followed by Thucydides' History of the Peloponnesian War and Xenophon's Hellenica; its philosophy divides into materialists (Thales, Heraclitus, Democritus) and idealists (Socrates, Plato, Pythagoras); and from 776 BCE the Greeks hold the Olympic Games every four years at the temple of Zeus in Olympia — five events (running, long jump, javelin, discus, wrestling), open to healthy free men only, the winner crowned with a wild-olive wreath and welcomed home as a hero — with the Panathenaic Games at Athens from 566 BCE beside them.
