## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 1, how feudal society formed in Western Europe

Rome in crisis from the 3rd century is overrun by Germanic peoples and falls in 476; Germanic kingdoms rise, Charlemagne's Frankish empire is split into what become France, Germany and Italy, and lords and serfs crystallise as the two great classes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 1, the feudal manor and lord–serf relations

By the 9th century each lord is a petty king on his manor — own army, law, court, taxes, even coinage, immune from the king — while serfs owe rents and dues down to wedding and funeral taxes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 1, the rise of the medieval towns

From the late 11th century craftsmen flee the manors or buy their freedom and gather at crossroads; towns like Florence become, in Marx's words quoted here, "the brightest flowers" of medieval Europe, and Bologna's university (1088) is the world's oldest.

## Sub lesson 4 — Shared Theme 2, medieval European towns and the merchants

From the 11th century runaway and ransomed craftsmen found towns at the crossroads — Paris grows to some 50,000 people by the 12th century, London becomes England's greatest seaport; merchants form guilds, run the great Champagne fairs, and their money-changing benches (banca) become banks — the class whose open, curious culture will seed the Renaissance.
