# Lesson plan — Southeast asia kingdoms 10th to 16th

**Course:** History — Vietnam · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Southeast asia kingdoms 10th to 16th
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The medieval world and Đại Việt: feudal Europe, China, India and Southeast Asia beside Vietnam from Ngô Quyền to the Lê sơ, and Chăm-pa (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Southeast asia kingdoms 10th to 16th as Vietnam's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The medieval world and Đại Việt: feudal Europe, China, India and Southeast Asia beside Vietnam from Ngô Quyền to the Lê sơ, and Chăm-pa — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Bài 6, the kingdoms consolidate, late 10th–early 16th centuries** — pagan unifies Myanmar, the Khmer empire flourishes, Srivijaya commands Sumatra; the Mongol invasions of the 13th century push the Thai to unite Sukhothai and Ayutthaya, the Lao to found Lan Xang, and the islands to gather under Majapahit, while Malacca is founded
2. **§2 Bài 6, Malacca, the world's market, and stronger states** — a quoted account calls 15th-century Malacca perhaps the one place on Earth where Chinese porcelain and silk, Javanese rice, West Asian woollens and Southeast Asian timber, incense and pepper all change hands; meanwhile law codes (Indonesia's Sivasasana, Đại Việt's Hình thư) tighten royal power
3. **§3 Bài 6, the culture of the kingdoms** — theravada Buddhism spreads from the 13th century and Islam comes with Arab and Indian traders; Thai script forms about 1200, Lao in the 14th century, the Viet create chữ Nôm from Han characters; the Nagarakretagama and the Malay Annals are written, and Angkor Wat and the Shwedagon rise
