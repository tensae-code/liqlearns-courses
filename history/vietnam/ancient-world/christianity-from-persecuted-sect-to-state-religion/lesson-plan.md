# Lesson plan — Christianity from persecuted sect to state religion

**Course:** History — Vietnam · **Title:** Ancient world · **Lesson:** Christianity from persecuted sect to state religion
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — The medieval world and Đại Việt: feudal Europe, China, India and Southeast Asia beside Vietnam from Ngô Quyền to the Lê sơ, and Chăm-pa (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of Christianity from persecuted sect to state religion as Vietnam's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — The medieval world and Đại Việt: feudal Europe, China, India and Southeast Asia beside Vietnam from Ngô Quyền to the Lê sơ, and Chăm-pa — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Bài 1, the birth of Christianity** — the faith of the poor and oppressed arises in Jerusalem at the start of the Common Era and by the 4th century has become the Roman Empire's state religion, the medieval Church its heir
2. **§2 Bài 5.1, the birth of Christianity inside the Roman Empire** — christianity (Cơ Đốc giáo/Ki-tô giáo) is formed in the 1st century CE in the eastern territory of the Roman Empire, in the setting of the struggles of slaves and the poor against Roman oppression; by tradition its founder is Jesus, born at Nazareth (in today's Israel); at first the Roman ruling class seeks to destroy the new religion, but it wins more and more followers and spreads widely, and by the early 4th century the Roman authorities stop suppressing it and recognise Christianity as the official religion of the empire; the Greeks and Romans before it worship many gods, holding festivals, sacrifices and prayers to honour them, their gods depicted in human form with human characters
3. **§3 Chuyên đề 1 Christianity's four centuries in Vietnam: Western missionaries preach from the 1500s, the Nguyễn court's bans make the 1800s dangerous, Catholicism roots in the North until 1954 and weighs heavily in the South to 1975, while Protestantism, arriving in the early 1900s, spreads widely only from the mid-1980s, above all among the highland minorities** — the persecuted import of one century becomes the established church of the next
