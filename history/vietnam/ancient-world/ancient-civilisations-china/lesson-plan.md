## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§1 Bài 18, Qin Shi Huang's tomb and the terracotta army** — thousands of life-size terracotta soldiers stand guard over the First Emperor's mausoleum, shown among China's signature ancient works
2. **§2 Bài 9, ancient China: rivers and dynasties** — on the Yellow River and Yangtze, the Xia (21st–16th centuries BC) yield to Shang, then Zhou; the Warring States end when Qin unifies China in 221 BC
3. **§3 Bài 4.2c, the achievements of ancient-to-medieval Chinese civilisation** — china's schools of thought form very early to explain the world and propose ways to govern — Confucianism, Taoism, Mohism, Legalism and the theories of yin-yang, the eight trigrams and the five elements become the ideological foundation of ancient China and influence many countries, Japan, Korea and Vietnam among them, while Buddhism, introduced in the first centuries CE, is adapted, flourishes, and spreads onward through the region; Chinese writing begins with the Shang era's oracle-bone script carved on tortoise shells and animal bones and bronze inscriptions, develops into today's Chinese characters, rises to the art of calligraphy, and is borrowed and adapted by Japan, Korea and Vietnam; literature peaks in Tang poetry — Li Bai, Du Fu and Bai Juyi — and the Ming–Qing chapter novels (Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Water Margin, Journey to the West, Dream of the Red Chamber); architecture and sculpture, bound closely together, give the Great Wall, the Forbidden City, the Summer Palace and the Ming Tombs, and the Terracotta Army in Qin Shi Huang's tomb in Shaanxi is pictured; in science China counts in decimals, computes π correctly to seven decimal places for the first time and invents the abacus, is first to record solar and lunar eclipses and makes calendars early, heals by medicine, acupuncture and surgery (the famous physicians Hua Tuo and Zhang Zhongjing), writes history from the Spring and Autumn Annals, China's first annals, to Sima Qian's Shiji, and gives the world the four great inventions — papermaking, printing, gunpowder and the compass — achievements that travel to the neighbours, to West Asia and then to Europe; a quoted judgement closes the lesson: unlike Harappa, Sumer and Egypt, ancient China overcame the challenge of the nomad peoples at its borders, its institutions and cultural values surviving intact into the early 20th century, so Chinese civilisation is sometimes described as the longest continuously existing civilisation in the world
4. **§4 Bài 2 Ancient China's art: a civilisation of 5,000–6,000 years whose Great Wall, Forbidden City and thousand-armed Buddha became world heritage** — the longest-lived civilisation leaves the longest wall and a city of palaces as its signature
