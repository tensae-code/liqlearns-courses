## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 10, the Roman imperial state

From a small city-state in central Italy, Rome grows into an empire by the 1st century BC; in 27 BC Octavian is raised as Augustus, first emperor, above Senate and a now-formal People's Assembly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 10, Greek and Roman culture

The Latin alphabet and Roman numerals we still use, Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, Sophocles, Pythagoras and Archimedes, Herodotus the historian, and the Colosseum.

## Sub lesson 3 — Bài 5.1, the achievements of Roman civilisation

On the basis of Greek writing the Romans build the script now called Latin — first used for the Latin language, then for many others, and today the most widely used script in the world — and the Roman numeral system pictured in the lesson's table is still in use; Roman literature gives Ovid's poem collections (Heroines, The Art of Love), and Roman architecture the Colosseum and the Arch of Constantine; Rome computes the year at 365 and a quarter days, its calendar coming very close to the solar calendar system in common use today; its scholars include Pliny, author of the Natural History, and Claudius Ptolemy, who proposes the geocentric theory; the Greeks and Romans also apply their science to life — making concrete, using lever systems, building stone-throwers, arrow-shooters and water pumps (a model of the Greek catapult and the statue of Pythagoras with his measuring rod on Samos are pictured); and the Romans love sport, giving much time to watching and joining it — in the arenas the slave-owners stage fights between gladiators, or between gladiators and wild beasts.
