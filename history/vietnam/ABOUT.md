# History — Vietnam course — ABOUT

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Vietnam's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How the course is organised

One course for Vietnam inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades** (CURRICULUM.md carries the ladder and how it is derived from the books' own progression). Lessons sit under four title folders — First steps, Ancient world, Modern world and Africa to 1500 — exactly as filed in the placements. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it. A lesson stands at the level of the earliest book that teaches it; every later telling of the same story in a deeper book is stocked into the same lesson as its own part, in file order. The school grade each book comes from appears only here and in each lesson's sources, never as the course's organisation.

## The books (full list, exactly as filed)

The placements file records: 30 records / 15 distinct books — each book appears twice (TSV release/blob URL + books.json pushed path naming the same file); read via grab.py, cover verified with pdftoppm/pdfinfo before reading. Grades 4-9 are combined History and Geography (Lịch sử và Địa lí); History parts placed, Geography parts named in book headers only. Grades 10-12 History and History study-topics are History-only; the three Fine Arts theory/history books are filed with their scope noted.

## The way of the books

- **Book 1:** Vietnam's own way: History is taught region by region (home province, then each region of Vietnam in turn), always as named places, festivals and "Câu chuyện Lịch sử" story boxes with a named hero or legend, plus source excerpts (stele texts, the Edict on the Transfer of the Capital) even at Grade 4.
- **Book 2:** Vietnam's own way: Grade 5 runs the whole national story in one chronological sweep — first states, a thousand years of northern domination, the dynasties, colonisation, 1945, 1954, 1975, Đổi mới — and every lesson is built around named "Câu chuyện Lịch sử" story boxes, many quoted from the national chronicles (Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư, Đại Nam thực lục).
- **Book 3:** Vietnam's own way: the lower-secondary course rebuilds the story from first principles — what history is, how evidence works, how time is counted — then prehistory, the ancient civilisations, Southeast Asia, and Vietnam to the 10th century, quoting the chronicles (Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư) and even a Yuan-era account (An Nam chí lược) as pupil-facing sources.

- **Book 7:** Vietnam's own way: the upper-secondary elective book teaches History as a discipline and a civic practice — one topic on how history is written and divided into fields (each field then surveyed through Vietnam's own past), one on heritage as something pupils must classify, rank and defend, and one on the state and law, where medieval law codes and five modern constitutions are read as primary sources.
- **Book 8:** Vietnam's own way: Grade 10 is organised by themes rather than chronology — the discipline itself first (what history is, its role, heritage), then civilisations in widening circles (the world, Southeast Asia, Vietnam's own land), then the four industrial revolutions, and finally the community of Vietnam's 54 ethnic groups, closing with the national great-unity bloc as the frame that holds the whole national story together.

- **Book 10:** Vietnam's own way: the Grade 11 elective goes deep on three things — traditional art read dynasty by dynasty as national evidence, the twentieth century taught as one long argument between war and peace (with Vietnam's own wars counted inside the world's), and history carried by named people: kings, generals, poets, a physician and an encyclopaedist.
- **Book 11:** Vietnam's own way: Grade 11 runs three arcs in parallel — the world's two rival systems (capitalism from its revolutions, socialism from 1917), Southeast Asia's road out of colonial rule, and Vietnam read through its wars of defence, its three great reformers, and its sea sovereignty — so the pupil's own country is always the measuring rod.
- **Book 12:** Vietnam's own way: where Grade 10's art book surveyed the past, Grade 11's teaches the pupil to work like a historian of art — verify the sources on an artist, date a work from documents, and read paintings as evidence, with Vietnam's resistance generation as the case study.

- **Book 14:** Vietnam's own way: the final year closes the whole school arc with the world first — the UN, the Yalta order, the Cold War's fall, ASEAN — then runs Vietnam's own 1945–today story as one unbroken Fatherland narrative (revolution, two resistances, border wars, Đổi Mới, diplomacy) and ends with an entire theme on Hồ Chí Minh as national hero and world cultural figure; every lesson is built on named primary sources (charters, declarations, party documents, memoirs) with "Em có biết?" side boxes.
- **Book 15:** Vietnam's own way: in the final year the art elective turns from studying the past to staging it — the pupil learns to select, classify, label and publicise works, the same curatorial craft by which Vietnam's museums present the nation's history to the public.
