# Iran: Ancient Statecraft — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   What happens in “Chapter 3, Lesson 11 The art of statecraft (آیین کشورداری)”?
**Answer:** How did kings hold so vast a land? Satraps, inspectors, roads and the king's justice — the machinery of ruling is the lesson, not the battles Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   How does the story in “Chapter 10, Lesson 20 How did the empires of ancient Iran administer the country?” end?
**Answer:** The child's first statecraft question — satrapies, roads, taxes and the king's law as the answer to governing a continent Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
