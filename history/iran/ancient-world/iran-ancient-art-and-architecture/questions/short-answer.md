**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   What happens in “Chapter 3, Lesson 16 Art and architecture”?
**Answer:** Persepolis's columns and Sasanian arches are read as arguments in stone — what a civilisation builds is what it believes about itself Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   How does the story in “Chapter 12, Lesson 24 Knowledge and art in ancient Iran” end?
**Answer:** The book closes its ancient arc with what the mind made — knowledge and art as the civilisation's surviving voice Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
