# Dating the Past

## Do it first

Before you read, write one line: what do you think the story of **Dating the Past** will be about? Then draw a rough map from memory of where you think it happens. Keep both — you will check them at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 1, Lesson 2 History; time and place

An event without a when and a where is a rumour — the lesson builds the historian's two coordinates, chronology and geography, before the story starts Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 6, Lesson 21 The calendar

A calendar is a people's way of counting its own time — solar Hijri years, months named for Zoroastrian spirits, and the dates a nation keeps Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 1, Lessons 1–2, 6 I was born; I have grown up; Change in the family

An eight-year-old puts her own life in order — baby photos before school photos — and learns that families, like countries, have a past that changes Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
