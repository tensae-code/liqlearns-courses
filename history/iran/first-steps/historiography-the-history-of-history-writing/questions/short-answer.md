# Historiography: The History of History Writing — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   What happens in “Chapter 1, Lesson 1 History and historiography”?
**Answer:** History-writing itself has a history — each age rewrites the past with its own questions, so the book teaches suspicion of any single telling Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   How does the story in “Lesson 1 Historiography and the sources of the contemporary period” end?
**Answer:** The contemporary past has different witnesses — newspapers, memoirs, photographs, state archives — and the final-year student learns each source's bias before the story begins Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
