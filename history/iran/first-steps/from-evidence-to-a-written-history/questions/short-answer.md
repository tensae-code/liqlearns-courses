# From Evidence to a Written History — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   What happens in “Chapter 3, Lesson 7 Study and exploration of the distant pasts”?
**Answer:** Iran's own deep past must be pieced from shards, inscriptions and foreign reports — the lesson shows the detective work behind the narrative that follows Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   How does the story in “Chapter 1, Lesson 1 Sources of research in the history of Islam and Islamic Iran” end?
**Answer:** A new period needs new sources — chronicles, coins, buildings and documents of the Islamic centuries are introduced as the evidence base Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
