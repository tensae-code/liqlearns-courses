# History — Iran course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Iran inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Iran's books in hand are the Ministry of Education's History books for the Humanities branch (History 1, 2 and 3) and its combined Social Studies books for primary and middle school, all in Persian, all COMPLETE as filed. The placements file's book order is the teaching order — earliest books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the story as Iran's own books present it, in the spiral they themselves use (the same eras return at rising depth, from a Grade 3 child's own timeline to the Grade 12 analytical telling): the historian's craft first, then the ancient world and ancient Iran, then Islam and the Iranian-Islamic civilisation, then the Mongol catastrophe, the Timurid flowering and the Safavid state, then the Qajar century, the Constitutional Revolution and the Pahlavis, and last oil, revolution, republic and war. Levels, NOT grades: the grade numbers live only in ABOUT.md and sources.md. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Iran's own Ministry of Education History and Social Studies books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: How History Is Known** — heritage first, then what history is and who writes it, dating the past by time and place (and by the child's own life and calendar), the archaeologist's spade where no writing survives, building a written history from evidence, and testing whether a source can be believed — 7 lessons.

2. **The Ancient World and Ancient Iran** — the first villages, then the first civilisations whole (Mesopotamia, Egypt, India, China, Greece and Crete, Rome), then Iran's own ancient story: Elam's dawn, Cyrus and the Achaemenids, the Medes, the Parthians and Sasanians, and ancient Iran studied by theme — statecraft, society and family, economy, religion, language and science, art and architecture — 17 lessons.

3. **Islam and the Iranian-Islamic Civilisation** — Islam's rise in Mecca and Medina, the Farewell Hajj, the first caliphs, Karbala, the Umayyads and Abbasids, then Iran's own Islamic centuries: Islam's arrival, the local dynasties and the Iranian-Islamic renaissance, capable viziers and prosperous cities, Ghaznavids, Seljuks and Khwarazmshahs, the Seljuk heritage, the golden age of science and learning, with Europe's medieval world as the outside mirror — 13 lessons.

4. **Mongols, Timurids and the Safavid State** — Genghis and Timur devastate Iran twice, the rebuilding under Mongol and Timurid rule, the culture and art that flower out of catastrophe, then the Safavids: Shah Ismail's and Shah Abbas's state and economy, and Isfahan's culture and civilisation — closing with Europe's Renaissance as the outside mirror — 6 lessons.

5. **Qajars, Constitution and the Pahlavis** — the survey bridge and the two worlds at 1800, Nader Shah to the Qajars defending unity, the Qajar founding, the Naser al-Din Shah era, Qajar society, the search for progress, the Constitutional Revolution and its two periods under fire, Iran in the First World War, the 1921 coup, Reza Shah's rise, rule and fall in 1941, and the Second World War and the world after 1945 — 19 lessons.

6. **Oil, Revolution, Republic and War** — colonialism named and resisted, oil nationalisation and Mosaddegh, the 1953 coup of 28 Mordad, a quarter century of American dominance, the White Revolution, the clergy movement, 15 Khordad 1963, exile, the road to revolution and its victory in 1979, the provisional government, the first presidency, the republic established and consolidated, the imposed war of 1980–88, the revolution's ideals audited, the republic after the war, and the national symbols, calendar and important days that bind the story — 18 lessons.

**Totals: 80 lessons** (Level 1: 7 · Level 2: 17 · Level 3: 13 · Level 4: 6 · Level 5: 19 · Level 6: 18 — 127 placements stocked; all 80 lessons stand on student books; 31 lessons carry sub lessons).
