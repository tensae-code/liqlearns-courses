# Lesson plan — Iran: Safavid Culture and Civilisation

**Course:** History — Iran · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Iran: Safavid Culture and Civilisation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Mongols, Timurids and the Safavid State (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Isfahan becomes half the world — mosques, squares, carpets and a court culture that brands Iran's identity to this day
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Mongols, Timurids and the Safavid State (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 3, Lesson 14 Culture and civilisation in the Safavid era** — Isfahan becomes half the world — mosques, squares, carpets and a court culture that brands Iran's identity to this day
2. **Chapter 5, Lesson 10 Social, economic, scientific and cultural conditions of Iran in the Safavid era** — The united state at work — bazaars, silk, scholars and Isfahan's building boom
3. **Chapter 6, Lesson 11 Isfahan, half the world** — A class trip in words — the Safavid capital's square, mosques and bridges earn their proverb: Isfahan is half the world
4. **Chapter 6, Lesson 12 Why did culture and art flourish in the Safavid period?** — Peace, wealth and royal patronage are the child's first causal chain — a united rich state buys beauty, and beauty survives it

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
