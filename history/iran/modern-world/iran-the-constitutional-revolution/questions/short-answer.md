# Iran: The Constitutional Revolution — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1)   What happens in “Lesson 3 The grounds of the Constitutional movement”?
**Answer:** Despotism at home, colonial hands abroad, awakening intellectuals and clergy — the ingredients of constitutionalism assemble one by one Follow the people in it, the place they stand in, and the moment the story turns. What changes at that turn is what the people who come after must live with — that is why this part is told, and told in this order.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2)   How does the story in “Lesson 4 The beginning of the people's movement against internal despotism and foreign colonialism” end?
**Answer:** The people move — protests and sanctuary-taking force Muzaffar al-Din Shah to grant a constitution and a national assembly in 1906 Picture the place first, then the people in it, then the turn. Nothing in this part is decoration: each step leads to the next, and the end of the part is different from its beginning because of what happens in between.
