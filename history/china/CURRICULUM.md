# History — China course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for China inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — China's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** China's books tell the story in widening and deepening arcs, in the placements file's own order, earliest books first: first the younger books tell China's story from early humans through the dynasties to Qing, with the first world civilisations beside it; then Ming-Qing at its height and in crisis meets the opening modern world and the crisis after 1840; then the modern world whole and the People's Republic's own story; then the whole world story again as one senior outline (with its teacher guide, PARTIAL as filed); then China's whole story again as one senior outline, five thousand years in one spine; and finally history by themes — state systems, economy and social life, and cultural exchange. The ladder follows that progression: each level is one arc of the country's telling, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest arc that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
