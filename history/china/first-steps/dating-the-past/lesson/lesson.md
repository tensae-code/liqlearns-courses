# Dating the past

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Dating the past" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Unit 8 Inquiry: How the past is recorded

Pupils work a year-ruler — Confucius (551–479 BCE), Western Han (202 BCE–9 CE), Han Wudi's era names, the ganzhi cycle that names the 1894 war, centuries and decades

## Appendix: Chronology of ancient Chinese history (lower volume)

A dated spine from Sui to Qing that pupils use to place every lesson's events in order

## Appendix: World history chronology (lower volume)

A dated spine of world events from colonial resistance to the present that pupils use to order the whole book

## Appendix: Chronology of modern Chinese history (lower volume)

A dated spine from 1949 to the present for placing every lesson of the PRC story

## Appendix: Chronology of Chinese and world history

One dated spine, China and the world together, for the whole senior course

## Appendix: reference chronology

The dated spine of the whole Chinese-history volume, Xia to the present

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Dating the past as China's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
