# China ming rule and imperial power

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "China ming rule and imperial power" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Lesson 14: Ming rule

Zhu Yuanzhang, blaming Yuan's fall on weak centre, abolishes the chancellorship forever, splits provinces into three offices that cannot command each other, and makes the six ministries answer to the emperor alone

## Unit 5 Lesson 1: The great unified state rises

Zhu Yuanzhang abolishes the chancellorship, creates the palace guard and secret-police factories and moves the capital to Beijing under Zhu Di, while Zheng He's voyages open the sea road to Asia and Africa

## Lesson 13: From Ming's founding to the Qing entry

Zhu Yuanzhang's terror and centralisation, Zhu Di's capital and fleets, then a Ming too rigid to reform, falling to Li Zicheng and the Manchu banners in 1644
