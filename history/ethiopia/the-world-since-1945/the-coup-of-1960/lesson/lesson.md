# December 1960 — the Coup That Lasted Two Days

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: December 1960 — the Coup That Lasted Two Days. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The coup of 1960

On 14 December 1960 the coup makers — Brigadier-General Mengistu Neway and Germame Neway, whose photograph from Bahru Zewde's History of Modern Ethiopia the book prints — proclaimed a new government: the Emperor's son Asfawosen as salaried constitutional monarch, Ras Imru Haile Selassie as prime minister, General Mulugeta Buli as chief of staff. Loyalist and rebel forces fought in Addis Ababa for barely two days; the Emperor returned, police chief General Tsige Dibu was killed, Germame and Colonel Workneh Gebeyehu committed suicide, and Mengistu was captured and sentenced to death. The book's stated main cause of failure: lack of support.

## Consolidation of autocracy and oppositions to the monarchy

Power centralises in the emperor; opposition grows — the 1960 coup attempt, the student movement, Eritrean insurgency, peasant revolts (Bale, Gojjam) — through the famine that discredits the throne.
