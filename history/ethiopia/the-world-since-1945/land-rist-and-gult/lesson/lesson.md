# Land, Rist and Gult — Who Ate from Whose Field

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Land, Rist and Gult — Who Ate from Whose Field. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Agriculture, land tenure and tenancy

Agriculture is the economy's backbone — subsistence farming for most rural society, coffee and oilseeds the bulk of exports, the largest livestock population in Africa — and land was the basis of power. The indigenous tenure systems were communal rist and gult (the right, granted by the emperor to ranks of the warrior class and local rulers, to collect tribute — gibir — from peasants): the tenancy relations built on them are the seedbed of the opposition that follows.
