# The Cold War — Blocs, Containment, Arms Race

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Cold War — Blocs, Containment, Arms Race. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The superpowers and the beginning of the Cold War

WWII produced two superpowers, the USA and the USSR, whose wartime partnership turned to confrontation by 1945–47 as Stalin consolidated control of Central and Eastern Europe (Tehran, Yalta, Potsdam mark the souring): a "cold" war of no direct combat, fought by military build-up, influence expansion and ideological delegitimation — capitalist free markets and multiparty politics against a centralised one-party communist system. From 1945–53 the USSR built the Eastern Bloc of puppet communist governments (Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary named) and repressed resistance; the US answered with containment and the Marshall Plan (whose motives pupils must explain), and the rivalry became an arms race before spreading from Europe to the Middle East, Asia and Latin America.

## Cold War realities

Ideological bipolarity in practice: NATO and the Warsaw Pact, the arms and space races, Korea as hot war, and the non-aligned response.
