# The Student Movement — the Empire's Children Turn Critics

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Student Movement — the Empire's Children Turn Critics. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Ethiopian student movement

The universities' students became the regime's most articulate opposition — the lesson is built around a video of Tilahun Gizaw's speech, which pupils watch and use to describe the movement's nature: the students who named the land question and carried the revolution's vocabulary into the streets.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
