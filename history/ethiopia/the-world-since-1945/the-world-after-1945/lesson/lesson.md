# The World After 1945

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The World After 1945. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Aftermath and consequences of World War II

Human and material losses, divided Germany and Europe, two superpowers, and the demand for a new world organisation.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches, in your own words: Human and material losses, divided Germany and Europe, two superpowers, and the demand for a new world organisation. Give one real example from the story. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Retell this lesson's story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Draw the story as a row of scenes, one scene per part of the lesson, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and note one place where its teaching connects to your own locality.
