# Creation and Evolution — Two Accounts of Human Origin

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Creation and Evolution — Two Accounts of Human Origin. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Two theories of human origin

The creationist view holds that humans and all living things were created by God or a supernatural being; the evolutionist (scientific) view, formulated by Charles Darwin (1809–1882), holds that living things including humans arose by gradual succession and variation from earlier forms. The book notes the scientific view dominates scientific thinking while large numbers of people worldwide hold the creationist view, and sets the two side by side for debate.

## Theories of human origin

Creation accounts and the scientific theory of evolution are set side by side as the two families of explanation for human origins, with Darwin and natural selection introduced.

## Evolution marking position *(teacher-guide evidence)*

The guide's Unit 1 keys handle the creation-versus-evolution question by instructing the teacher to have pupils decide between the Biblical notion and the scientific view on the evidence as presented — the official handling of a sensitive point, recorded as teacher evidence.
