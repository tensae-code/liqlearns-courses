## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Africa, Cradle of Humankind. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Africa and human evolution — the continent of first finds

Africa is presented as the centre of the human-origins evidence: the oldest australopithecine, from northern Chad (found 2001, 7–6 million years old); Ardipithecus ramidus fossils aged 4.4 million years from Afar (1994); and Australopithecus afarensis — Lucy, Dinkinesh — found at Hadar in Afar in 1974, about 3.18 million years old. Pupils map Africa's major archaeological sites and argue why East Africa matters for studying human origin.
