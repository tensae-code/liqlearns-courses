# The Stone Age and the Farming Turn

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Stone Age and the Farming Turn. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Stone Age — three ages and the farming turn

Tool-making distinguishes humans; the earliest tools were stone. The Stone Age divides into Palaeolithic (hunting and gathering), Mesolithic, and Neolithic — the age when plants and animals were domesticated and settled farming began. In Ethiopia the Neolithic is placed about 7,000 years ago: teff, dagussa, nug and enset were among the domesticated plants, pottery began, and Neolithic sites are named at Aksum, Lalibela, near Chercher in Hararghe and at Metehara.

## Early human culture — tools, fire and the Neolithic Revolution

Stone-tool ages (Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic), the control of fire, and the Neolithic Revolution — farming and animal domestication — as the turn from food gathering to food production.
