# Video script — Dating the Past — Calendars, Timelines and Periods

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

Look at the map. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Dating fixes when events happened; time is chunked into decade, century and millennium; chronology orders events by calendar.

The past is divided into periods (ancient, medieval, modern) and by dating systems; periodization is presented as a historian's tool, not a fact of nature, with different civilisations periodised differently.

Maps carry a large share of the teaching (21 map references counted): evolution centres, civilisations, expansion and movement maps recur in almost every unit; map-reading is a named learning outcome from Unit 1 onward.

The book's single timeline runs human origins → ancient civilisations → Ethiopia to the 13th century → medieval/early-modern world → Africa to 1500 → Africa and the outside world → Ethiopia 1270–1855 (units 7–8 overlapping the world chronology) → revolutions 1789–1815; pupils are expected to hold Ethiopian and world time side by side.

Remember it this way: Dating fixes when events happened; time is chunked into decade, century and millennium; chronology orders events by calendar.
