## Dating, chronology, calendars, timelines, periodisation

Dating fixes when events happened; time is chunked into decade, century and millennium; chronology orders events by calendar. The Gregorian calendar counts BC/AD from the birth of Christ; the Islamic calendar counts BH/AH from the Hijra of 622 AD; the Ethiopian calendar runs seven to eight years behind the Gregorian. A timeline draws events in order for quick visualisation, and periodisation labels stretches of the past — ancient, medieval, modern — after significant developments (worked examples: the Battle of Adwa dated 1 March 1896; the Renaissance Dam's start 2 April 2011).

## Periodization

The past is divided into periods (ancient, medieval, modern) and by dating systems; periodization is presented as a historian's tool, not a fact of nature, with different civilisations periodised differently.

## Chronology spine

The book's single timeline runs human origins → ancient civilisations → Ethiopia to the 13th century → medieval/early-modern world → Africa to 1500 → Africa and the outside world → Ethiopia 1270–1855 (units 7–8 overlapping the world chronology) → revolutions 1789–1815; pupils are expected to hold Ethiopian and world time side by side. — Whole book.
