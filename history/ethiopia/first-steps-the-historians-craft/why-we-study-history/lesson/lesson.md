# Why We Study History

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Why We Study History. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Why history matters

History is taught as knowing the past in order to understand the present and foresee future developments; it builds national and international understanding, democratic principles and nationalism, trains research skills (collecting and analysing information to reach conclusions), gives a sense of identity, and forms conscious citizens who take part in shaping their future.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
