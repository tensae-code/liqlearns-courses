Where there are no sources there is no history.

Sources are divided into primary and secondary, written and unwritten (oral tradition, archaeology, linguistics, artefacts); every historical statement must rest on evidence, and sources undergo external and internal criticism before use.

The guide's overviews fill out what the student book compresses: prehistory is studied through archaeology (digging and studying artefacts and fossils), palaeontology and anthropology (human society and how humans developed and related); "history vs story" is made explicit — both are narrative, but history deals with real people and events, a story may be imagined — with a worked fact/opinion example (a king's reign as fact, calling him an evil despot as opinion); and the evolution overview dates Homo sapiens to about 100,000 years ago and modern human beings to about 40,000 years ago, with hominids and Homo sapiens first appearing in Africa.
