# Ethiopian Historiography — Chronicles and Hagiographies as Sources — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons (2026-11-09 ruling: sub lessons only where a lesson genuinely has distinct sequential parts). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files.

Pass mark: Ethiopia's own writing tradition is taught as content: royal chronicles from the 14th century recording emperors' deeds almost uninterruptedly to the early 20th century, plus hagiographies (saints' lives) as a second source tradition — their strengths and their court-centred bias. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
