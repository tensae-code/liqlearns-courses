# Interpreting the Past — From Evidence to a Written History — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what is this lesson's one thing?
**Answer:** The past is endless, so historians select problems, then describe, analyse, evaluate and explain past events from primary and/or secondary sources; the work is systematic (rules, procedures, standards) and aims at objective truth, though avoiding bias is admitted to be difficult in practice.

**2.** What does the part "Interpretation, objectivity and the writing of history" teach?
**Answer:** After criticism comes interpretation and then writing: collecting evidence, criticising it, interpreting it and writing objectively, with modern ICT and digital imaging noted as changing the historian's tools; the abuse of history is warned against.
