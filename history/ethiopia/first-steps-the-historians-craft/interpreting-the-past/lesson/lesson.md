# Interpreting the Past — From Evidence to a Written History

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Interpreting the Past — From Evidence to a Written History. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Historical interpretation

The past is endless, so historians select problems, then describe, analyse, evaluate and explain past events from primary and/or secondary sources; the work is systematic (rules, procedures, standards) and aims at objective truth, though avoiding bias is admitted to be difficult in practice.

## Interpretation, objectivity and the writing of history

After criticism comes interpretation and then writing: collecting evidence, criticising it, interpreting it and writing objectively, with modern ICT and digital imaging noted as changing the historian's tools; the abuse of history is warned against.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
