# Lesson plan — How States Emerge

**Course:** History — Ethiopia · **Title:** First Steps: The Historian's Craft and Human Origins · **Lesson:** How States Emerge
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft and Human Origins (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sedentary Neolithic communities grew large and needed organisation beyond simple society: the lesson identifies the features of a state and the major theories and possible factors of state origin, which pupils must summarise — the bridge from farming villages to the civilisations of Unit 2.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft and Human Origins (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson has distinct sequential parts, one per part below; the parts run in order inside the one lesson, with no break between steps on the lesson page.
- The emergence of states
- Emergence of the state
- Content the guide adds — six marks of a civilisation, and a religion-first theory of the state
- State-formation comparison thread

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself, as Ethiopia's books themselves work, telling the past as stories to be asked about, placed and retold.
