# Fact or Opinion? Reading the Past Critically

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Fact or Opinion? Reading the Past Critically. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Fact, opinion and history as inquiry

History as a body of knowledge is an account of the past built on facts and evidence, not a novel or an opinion: a fact can be proved with supporting information, an opinion is a writer's unprovable viewpoint, and telling them apart by critical thinking is presented as one of the core skills history teaches.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
