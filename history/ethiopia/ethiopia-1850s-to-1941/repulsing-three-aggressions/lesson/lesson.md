# Repulsing Three Aggressions — Britain, Egypt, the Mahdists

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Repulsing Three Aggressions — Britain, Egypt, the Mahdists. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## External aggressions, 1871–1889 — Napier, Egypt and the Mahdists

Ethiopia's survival was tested in sequence: the British military expedition under Sir Robert Napier, arising from Tewodros's modernisation drive and his detention of envoys, which ended with Tewodros's death; Egypt's attempt to dominate the Nile basin and the country, repulsed decisively at Gundat (1875) and Gura (1876); the Hewett Treaty and its results; Italian encroachment in the north, repulsed through the 1880s; and the Mahdist invasion, which consumed Emperor Yohannes IV's life in 1889 — unity in defence of sovereignty is the lesson's theme.

## External relations, challenges and threats

Egypt's invasions (defeated at Gundet and Gura, 1875–76), the Mahdist clash, and Italy's advance from Massawa through the Treaty of Wuchale's fatal translation dispute to war.
