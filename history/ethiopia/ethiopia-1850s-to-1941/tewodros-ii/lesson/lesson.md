## Tewodros II — the first modern unifier

The making of modern Ethiopia ran on twin processes: unification of the northern and north-central regions and principalities, and territorial expansion into the southern half. Kassa Hailu of Quara, who rose serving his half-brother Dejjazmach Kenfu of Dembya, took the throne as Tewodros II (1855–1868), ended the Zemene-Mesafint order and attempted to unify and modernise the country — the first emperor of modern Ethiopia.

## Power rivalry and consolidating central government, 1855–1913

Tewodros II reunifies by force and falls at Maqdala (1868); Yohannes IV holds the north against Egypt, Mahdists and Italy and falls at Metemma (1889); Menelik II succeeds and builds the modern central state.

## Content the guide adds — Tewodros's military reforms in working detail *(teacher-guide evidence)*

The guide's activity feedback gives the reform programme the student book summarises: Tewodros reformed administration, religion and the military, the military reform being the most important and least ambivalent — in organisation, discipline and armament. He introduced the decimal command titles Yasr Aleqa (commander of ten), Yamsa Aleqa (commander of the hundred) and Yeshi Aleqa (commander of the thousand); he tried to replace the regional armies of the Zemene-Mesafint with a salaried national army; he manufactured firearms at Gafat (near Debre Tabor) with the help of European missionaries and artisans — about 35 cannons, including his famous mortar "Sebastopol"; and he tried to separate church and state, reducing church land and the number of priests.
