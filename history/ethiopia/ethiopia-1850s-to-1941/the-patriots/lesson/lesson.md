# The Patriots — Five Years the Occupation Never Won

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Patriots — Five Years the Occupation Never Won. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The patriotic resistance, 1936–41

Resistance began almost immediately after the Battle of Maychew and never let the occupation become effective, especially in rural northern, eastern and central Ethiopia — its slogan printed in Amharic in the book ("Stand! Rise up; you cannot sit idle while the nation is invaded"). It fell into two phases divided by the reprisals after the failed assassination attempt on Graziani in February 1937: first traditional battles led largely by upper-class nobles, then guerrilla warfare. Inside the towns worked the Yewust Arbegnoch ("inner patriots"), passing intelligence and secretly delivering weapons, medicine and food — Ethiopian women prominent among them, gathering information from the Italians, some arranging killings of Italian officials, others leading troops in battle or rallying soldiers after their husbands fell, Shewareged Gedle among the named examples — until liberation in 1941.
