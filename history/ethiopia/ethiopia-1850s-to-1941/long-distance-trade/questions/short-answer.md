# The Long-Distance Trade — Bonga, Amole and Maria Theresa Thalers — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what is this lesson's one thing?
**Answer:** The peoples and polities of southern, northern and eastern Ethiopia and the Horn were connected by two major trade routes starting from Bonga, capital of Kafa; Muslim traders known as Afkala and Jabarti (Jeberti) played the pivotal role; salt bars (amole) and Maria Theresa thalers served as the medium of exchange; and trade itself was the most important catalyst in relations between peoples and polities — routes, major market centres, trade items and people's interaction are the lesson's four contents.

**2.** What does the part "Long-distance trade and peoples' interaction in 19th-century Ethiopia" teach?
**Answer:** Caravan networks tie the regions together — Gondar, Harar, Jimma, the Red Sea and Sudan routes moving coffee, ivory, gold and slaves — making trade the era's great integrator before political unification.
