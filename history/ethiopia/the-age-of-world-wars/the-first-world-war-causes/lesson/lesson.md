## Do it first

Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The First World War — causes and alliances

The Great War (1914–18) involved most of Europe with Russia, the United States, the Middle East and other regions. Its root causes are taught as a system: mutual defence alliances (built since the 1870s to keep peace, they instead pushed the continent into war), nationalism, rivalry for empires and markets, an arms race and militarism — detonated by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand. The blocs: the Triple Entente/Allied Powers (France, Russia, Britain, the USA from 1917) against the Triple Alliance/Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria; Italy left its alliance). The lesson embeds a video on the war's causes.

## World War I and its settlement

Causes (alliances, imperial rivalry, Sarajevo), the trench war, and the Versailles settlement with the League of Nations — whose failure over Ethiopia in 1935 the pupils are positioned to judge from Unit 3.
