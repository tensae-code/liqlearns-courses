# What the First World War Changed — Empires Down, America and Communism Up

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: What the First World War Changed — Empires Down, America and Communism Up. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The First World War — course and consequences

The war ran to the Central Powers' defeat, and its consequences remade the world: empires destroyed, several new nation-states established, independence movements promoted in the European colonies, the United States driven to become a world power, and the conditions created for communism's rise in Russia and Hitler's in Germany; Versailles made Germany responsible for the outbreak and imposed loss of foreign territories, a reduced army and navy, and reparations. The war was not Europe's alone — it devastated the Middle East, Africa and Asia and spurred colonial peoples' desire for independence. — Printed pp. 86–91 (and summary, printed p.109).

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
