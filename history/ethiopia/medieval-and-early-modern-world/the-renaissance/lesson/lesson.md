# The Renaissance — Rebirth of Learning and Humanism

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Renaissance — Rebirth of Learning and Humanism. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Renaissance (Book 1 telling)

The Renaissance (14th–17th centuries) was the "rebirth" of learning: it began in urban Italy — an age of recovery from 14th-century disasters, with a new emphasis on individual ability — and spread to England, France, Germany, Spain and Holland. Its named works: Dante's Divine Comedy, More's Utopia, Cervantes' Don Quixote, Luther's German Bible, Shakespeare's plays; Leonardo's Last Supper and Mona Lisa, Michelangelo's Creation of Adam and statues of David and Moses; Vesalius in anatomy and Copernicus declaring the earth revolves around the sun. Its legacy is humanism and a turn from dominant religious framing toward human capacity.

## The Renaissance (Book 3 telling)

Italy's rebirth of classical learning: humanism, art (Leonardo, Michelangelo), the printing press, and the movement's spread north.
