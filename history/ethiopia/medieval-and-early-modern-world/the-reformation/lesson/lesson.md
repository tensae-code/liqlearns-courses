# The Reformation — Luther and the Splitting of the Church

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Reformation — Luther and the Splitting of the Church. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Reformation (Book 1 telling)

Martin Luther's religious reforms broke Western Christianity: Protestantism emerged from the revolt against Roman Catholic practice, with national forms — in Britain the Protestants' church became the Anglican church, Calvin's followers taking forms such as the Presbyterian — and the settlement after Europe's religious wars (the Peace of Westphalia) is set as a discussion of settling disputes by peaceful means.

## The Reformation (Book 3 telling)

Luther's 1517 protest over indulgences splits Western Christianity; Protestant churches, Catholic Counter-Reformation, and religious wars follow.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
