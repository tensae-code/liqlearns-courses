# Explorations and Discoveries — Why Europe Took to the Sea

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Explorations and Discoveries — Why Europe Took to the Sea. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Age of Explorations and Discoveries

In the 15th–16th centuries Europeans sailed to bypass the Ottoman blockade of the old routes to the Far East, encouraged by interest in long-distance trade and new inventions such as the compass, and sponsored by rulers seeking profit and access; the voyages' economic effect was huge profits from local trade networks, faster globalisation, and — with subsequent colonial conquest — faster development of capitalism.

## The Age of Exploration and the inception of globalization

Portuguese and Spanish voyages (Dias, da Gama, Columbus, Magellan), the Columbian exchange, and the first truly global trade web — told with its costs to the peoples reached.
