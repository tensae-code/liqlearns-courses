# History — Ethiopia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Ethiopia inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Ethiopia's own History textbooks —
the standalone History subject of Grades 9–12 (in Ethiopia,
Social Studies is the subject up to Grade 8; History is a
separate subject from Grade 9) — run one chronological sweep
twice over: the Grade 9 and Grade 11 books sweep from the
historian's craft and human origins to 1815, and the Grade 10
and Grade 12 books sweep from 1815 to the present and close
on indigenous knowledge and heritages. Inside each book,
world units and Ethiopia/Africa units alternate, so every
world pattern — states, feudalism, trade, revolution,
capitalism, world war — is then met again in Ethiopian form.
The ladder follows the books' own order exactly: method and
human origins first; the ancient world; Ethiopia and the Horn
to the 13th century; the medieval and early modern world;
Africa to 1500; Africa and the outside world to the 1880s;
Ethiopia century by century to the mid-19th century; the Age
of Revolutions; then the second sweep — capitalism,
nationalism and colonial Africa; Ethiopia to 1941; the world
wars; the world since 1945 with Ethiopia 1941–1991; Africa
since 1960 with Ethiopia since 1991; and last, as the books
themselves close, Ethiopia's indigenous knowledge systems
and heritages. Fifteen levels, in the books' own order. The
ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Ethiopia's own
Ministry of Education History textbooks for Grades 9–12 —
except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the
people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list, the holdings
table, and the acceptance reckoning.
