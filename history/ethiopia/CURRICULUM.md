# History — Ethiopia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Ethiopia inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Ethiopia's books in hand tell one
long national story, and the ladder follows it. The Social
Studies books for Grades 7–8 and the History books for Grades
9–12 carry the story itself — Punt, D'aamat and Aksum; the
Zagwe and the restored Solomonic kingdom beside the
sultanates; unification under Tewodros II, Yohannes IV and
Menilek II, crowned at Adwa in 1896; then occupation,
liberation, empire, revolution, the Derg, federation, and
Ethiopia's heritage and indigenous knowledge. The Economics
books for Grades 9–12 carry the same country's second story —
its economy: what development means, the three sectors, three
economic systems in a century, and the plans and enterprise
since 1991. Six levels, in the books' own order: ancient
Ethiopia; medieval Ethiopia; unification and Adwa; occupation
to today; the economy and its systems; and plans, strategies
and enterprise since 1991. The ladder is a recommended path,
never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Ethiopia's own
Social Studies, History and Economics books — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book contents
map, and the acceptance reckoning.
