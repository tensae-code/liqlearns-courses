# Video script — North Africa — Fatimids, Mamluk Egypt and the Funj

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
After the Arab conquest (Egypt 642 AD, then the whole coast to Morocco and into Spain by century's end) and an 11th-century revival of Islam with further Arab immigration, North Africa's great states of the period were Fatimid then Mamluk Egypt — the Turkish-origin Mamluks ruling 1250–1517, with Ethio-Egyptian relations full of conflict — and the loosely united Funj sultanate, which fought Christian Ethiopia over trade routes and lasted until Egypt conquered it in the 1820s.
