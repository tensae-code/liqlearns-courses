# North Africa — Fatimids, Mamluk Egypt and the Funj — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1.** After the Arab conquest (Egypt 642 AD, then the whole coast to Morocco and into Spain by century's end) and an 11th-century revival of Islam with further Arab immigration, North Africa's great states of the period were Fatimid then Mamluk Egypt — the Turkish-origin Mamluks ruling 1250–1517, with Ethio-Egyptian relations full of conflict — and the loosely united Funj sultanate, which fought Christian Ethiopia over trade routes and lasted until Egypt conquered it in the 1820s.
**Answer: True**

**2.** This lesson teaches: Islam in North Africa.
**Answer: False** — this lesson teaches: After the Arab conquest (Egypt 642 AD, then the whole coast to Morocco and into Spain by century's end) and an 11th-century revival of Islam with further Arab immigration, North Africa's great states of the period were Fatimid then Mamluk Egypt — the Turkish-origin Mamluks ruling 1250–1517, with Ethio-Egyptian relations full of conflict — and the loosely united Funj sultanate, which fought Christian Ethiopia over trade routes and lasted until Egypt conquered it in the 1820s.
