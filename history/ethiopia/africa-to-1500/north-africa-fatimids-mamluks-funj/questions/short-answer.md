# North Africa — Fatimids, Mamluk Egypt and the Funj — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what is this lesson's one thing?
**Answer:** After the Arab conquest (Egypt 642 AD, then the whole coast to Morocco and into Spain by century's end) and an 11th-century revival of Islam with further Arab immigration, North Africa's great states of the period were Fatimid then Mamluk Egypt — the Turkish-origin Mamluks ruling 1250–1517, with Ethio-Egyptian relations full of conflict — and the loosely united Funj sultanate, which fought Christian Ethiopia over trade routes and lasted until Egypt conquered it in the 1820s.

**2.** What does the part "North Africa — Mamluk Egypt" teach?
**Answer:** Mamluk Egypt: slave-soldier sultans ruling Egypt and Syria, Cairo's wealth and scholarship, and victory over Mongols and Crusaders.
