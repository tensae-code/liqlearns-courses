# Video script — Ancient India — Indus Cities, Hinduism and Buddhism

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The first Indian civilisation began in the Indus Valley (c.2500 BC, at its height c.1500 BC) with its own counting, measuring, weighing and writing systems, irrigation ditches and canals, trade reaching Mesopotamia, and artisans working copper, bronze and gold.

Look at the map. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

The first Indian civilisation began in the Indus Valley (c.2500 BC, at its height c.1500 BC) with its own counting, measuring, weighing and writing systems, irrigation ditches and canals, trade reaching Mesopotamia, and artisans working copper, bronze and gold.

The Indus Valley cities (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa) and then Vedic society, caste, and the birth of Hinduism and Buddhism.

The guide names Buddhism's founder, Siddhartha Gautama (c.563–483 BC), whom the student book's India section does not name; it glosses Mesopotamia as Greek for "land between rivers" and cuneiform as the world's first writing system; it calls Persia the "Land of the Aryans" and bounds the Persian Empire at over two hundred years; and it repeats Confucianism's ranked-order teaching in rule-form (parents rule children, men rule women, the educated rule common people).

Remember it this way: The first Indian civilisation began in the Indus Valley (c.2500 BC, at its height c.1500 BC) with its own counting, measuring, weighing and writing systems, irrigation ditches and canals, trade reaching Mesopotamia, and artisans working copper, bronze and gold.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
