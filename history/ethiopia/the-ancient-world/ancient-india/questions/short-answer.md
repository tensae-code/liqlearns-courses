# Ancient India — Indus Cities, Hinduism and Buddhism — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what is this lesson's one thing?
**Answer:** The first Indian civilisation began in the Indus Valley (c.2500 BC, at its height c.1500 BC) with its own counting, measuring, weighing and writing systems, irrigation ditches and canals, trade reaching Mesopotamia, and artisans working copper, bronze and gold.

**2.** What does the part "Content the guide adds — names and framings for Asia" teach?
**Answer:** The guide names Buddhism's founder, Siddhartha Gautama (c.563–483 BC), whom the student book's India section does not name; it glosses Mesopotamia as Greek for "land between rivers" and cuneiform as the world's first writing system; it calls Persia the "Land of the Aryans" and bounds the Persian Empire at over two hundred years; and it repeats Confucianism's ranked-order teaching in rule-form (parents rule children, men rule women, the educated rule common people).
