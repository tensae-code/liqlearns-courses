# Christianity — From Persecuted Sect to State Religion

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Christianity — From Persecuted Sect to State Religion. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The rise and spread of Christianity (Book 1 telling)

Christianity began in 1st-century Palestine on the teaching of Jesus, born at Bethlehem in Roman Judea; Rome first saw Christians as a threat and persecuted them (from Nero, AD 54–68). The turn came with Constantine: his conversion (312) and the Edict of Milan (313) granting toleration, and under Theodosius Christianity became the Empire's official religion — after which it spread across the world.

## The rise and spread of Christianity (Book 3 telling)

Christianity begins in Roman Judea, spreads through the empire despite persecution, is legalised under Constantine and becomes the state religion — the bridge into the medieval units.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
