## Mesopotamia — Sumer

Between the Tigris and Euphrates (modern Iraq, eastern Syria, southern Turkey) arose the earliest river-valley civilisation: not one unified kingdom but warring city-states, unlike Egypt. The Sumerians built the first cities, raised stepped mud-brick temples (ziggurats — the book pictures the Ziggurat of Ur), farmed fertile soil by flood control and irrigation, were ruled by kings holding divine authority as their city god's representative, and invented the plough, the potter's wheel, possibly bronze, cuneiform writing (c.3200 BC, a complex ideograph system, not an alphabet), and advances in mathematics, astronomy, medicine and metalwork.

## Mesopotamia

In the Tigris–Euphrates valley: Sumerian cities, cuneiform, Hammurabi's law code, and successive Babylonian and Assyrian empires.
