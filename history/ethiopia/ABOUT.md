# About the History — Ethiopia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Ethiopia's own
Ministry of Education History textbooks for Grades 9–12. The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from
us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons
are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Ethiopia inside the History folder
(one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

**Why this course is built only from the Grades 9–12 History
books:** in Ethiopia, Social Studies is the subject up to
Grade 8, and History is a separate, standalone subject in
Grades 9–12, with its own textbooks. The Social Studies
Grades 7–8 history chapters therefore stay in Social Studies,
where they belong, and feed no lesson here. An earlier
History — Ethiopia course built from Social Studies lessons
was withdrawn on 2026-11-23 for exactly that reason; this
course replaces it, built only from the standalone History
textbooks. No Social Studies material and no Economics
material stands anywhere in it.

The course as built has **fifteen levels**, following the
four student books' own alternating world–Ethiopia sweep:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: The Historian's Craft and Human
   Origins.** What history is, fact against opinion,
   historiography (Ethiopia's chronicles included), sources
   and how to test them, dating the past, human evolution
   and Ethiopia's fossils, the Stone Age and the farming
   turn, and how states emerge.
2. **Level 2 — The Ancient World.** Egypt, Nubia–Kush–Meroe,
   Mesopotamia, Persia, India, China, the Maya, the Inca and
   the Aztecs, Greece, Rome, and Christianity's rise.
3. **Level 3 — Ethiopia and the Horn to the End of the 13th
   Century.** The language families, the indigenous,
   Jewish, Christian and Muslim arrivals, the peopling of
   Ethiopia, pre-Aksumite states, Aksum and its decline,
   the Zagwe, the Sultanate of Shewa, Damot.
4. **Level 4 — The Medieval and Early Modern World,
   500–1789.** The Dark Age, the two Romes, Byzantium,
   feudal society, Islam's rise, the Ottomans, China's
   dynastic cycle, early capitalism, the explorations, the
   Renaissance, the Reformation, and the Scientific
   Revolution and Enlightenment.
5. **Level 5 — Africa to 1500.** The language families,
   North Africa's dynasties, Islam in North and West
   Africa, Ghana, Mali, Songhai, Kanem-Bornu and the Hausa
   states, Central and East Africa, Great Zimbabwe, the
   Trans-Saharan trade.
6. **Level 6 — Africa and the Outside World, 1500–1880s.**
   The post-1500 kingdoms (Benin, Oyo, Dahomey, Ashante,
   Kongo, the Zulu), Ottoman North Africa, the Portuguese
   contacts, slavery's older forms, the three slave trades,
   abolition and its cost, legitimate commerce, white
   settlement, explorers and missionaries.
7. **Level 7 — Ethiopia, 13th Century to the Mid-16th
   Century.** The 1270 Solomonic restoration, rist and
   gult, Amda Seyon, the Muslim sultanates and the Zeila
   caravans, the kingdom–Adal wars, the southern states,
   the population movements, the Oromo expansion, Gadaa,
   Moggasa and Guddifacha, and the egalitarian systems.
8. **Level 8 — Ethiopia, Mid-16th to Mid-19th Century.**
   The tato and kawo kingdoms, Sera and Woma, the enset
   peoples, the Gibe states, Benishangul, Harar, the Afar
   and Somali systems, Susenyos, Gondar, the Zemene-
   Mesafint, the Yejju, Shewa, and the European travellers.
9. **Level 9 — The Age of Revolutions, 1750s–1815.** The
   Industrial Revolution and industrial capitalism,
   France before and through 1789, Napoleon, American
   independence, Vienna 1815, and the language of
   Ethiopian rule.
10. **Level 10 — Capitalism, Nationalism and Colonial
    Africa, 1815–1914.** Capitalism and its Marxian
    critique, nationalism, Italy and Germany unified, the
    American Civil War, the Eastern Question, the Scramble
    and Berlin, the systems of colonial rule, the first
    resistances, and colonial rule's impacts.
11. **Level 11 — Ethiopia, Mid-19th Century to 1941.** The
    long-distance trade, cottage industries, Tewodros,
    Yohannes, Menilek, the making of the modern map, the
    three aggressions repulsed, Adwa, Boru Meda, the
    succession struggle, the 1931 constitution, the 1935
    invasion and the Patriots.
12. **Level 12 — The Age of World Wars, 1914–1945.** The
    First World War and what it changed, 1917 in Russia,
    the League, the Depression, Fascism and Nazism,
    militarist Japan, the Second World War.
13. **Level 13 — The World Since 1945 and Ethiopia,
    1941–1991.** The UN, recovery, the Cold War and its
    Asian wars, non-alignment, the Middle East, the Soviet
    bloc's dissolution — and at home: the restored empire,
    the 1955 constitution, land, the peasant revolts, the
    1960 coup, the student movement, 1974, the Derg, the
    Ogaden war, the Eritrea question, and 1991.
14. **Level 14 — Africa Since 1960 and Ethiopia Since
    1991.** Liberation region by region, apartheid,
    governing the new nations, the post-flag economies,
    the Cold War in Africa, Pan-Africanism and the OAU
    judged — and at home: the transition, the FDRE, the
    Nile question, development and democratisation, and
    Ethiopia the peacekeeper.
15. **Level 15 — Indigenous Knowledge and Heritages of
    Ethiopia.** Indigenous knowledge and how it behaves,
    its place in development, Mada'a and Dagu, Jaarsummaa,
    Sinqe and Gumaa, Shimgelina, Luwa and Seera, heritage
    and its three kinds, Ethiopia on the World Heritage
    List, and the closing glossaries.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Ethiopia's story:* "Start with the tools: history is an
  inquiry built from sources that must be tested, and the
  oldest human story stands on Ethiopian soil — Lucy and the
  Afar fossils. Then the ancient world rises on its rivers,
  and Ethiopia's own first states rise on the Red Sea road:
  Aksum's stelae and Adulis, the Zagwe's rock churches, the
  sultanates on the Zeila road. The medieval world turns —
  feudal Europe, Islam, the Ottomans — while Africa builds
  Ghana, Mali and Songhai on salt and gold, and Ethiopia
  fills with its own systems: rist and gult, Gadaa, Sera and
  Woma, tato and kawo. The slave-trade centuries scar the
  continent; Gondar rises and the princes divide the empire;
  revolution remakes France and industry remakes the world.
  Then the modern sweep: capitalism and nationalism, Berlin
  partitioning Africa — and Ethiopia answering at Adwa in
  1896, surviving the 1935 invasion by five years of
  patriotic resistance. Two world wars, the Cold War, the
  empire's fall in 1974, the Derg, 1991, the federal
  republic — and the course closes where the books close:
  on the knowledge Ethiopia's own peoples built to govern,
  judge, heal and remember."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Grades 9 and 11 (Books 1 and 3):** the same sweep told
  twice — method and human evolution first, then world and
  Ethiopia/Africa units alternating (ancient world;
  Ethiopia and the Horn; medieval and early modern world;
  Africa to 1500; Africa and the outside world; Ethiopia's
  states and systems; the Age of Revolutions). Grade 11
  tells it deeper and adds the Scientific Revolution and
  Enlightenment, the post-1500 African kingdoms and the
  abolition and effects of the slave trade. Every unit
  opens with outcomes and a Brainstorming question and
  closes with a summary and review; the Grade 9 book's
  closing Glossary defines Ethiopia's own vocabulary of
  rule (Abbaa Gadaa, rist, gult, gabbar, Tato, Kawo, Woma).
- **Grades 10 and 12 (Books 2 and 4):** the modern sweep,
  1815 to the present, told twice the same way — capitalism
  and nationalism; colonial Africa; Ethiopia to 1941; the
  world wars; the world since 1945; Ethiopia 1941–1991;
  Africa since 1960; Ethiopia since 1991 — and closing, in
  both tellings, on indigenous knowledge systems and the
  heritages of Ethiopia. The Grade 12 book organises its
  body in numbered Lessons opening with Competencies and
  guiding questions.
- **The teacher guides (Books 5–8):** edition-matched
  companions to the student books — the Grades 9–10 guides
  script every lesson the same way (competencies, overview,
  activities, stabilisation, evaluation, answers supplied);
  the Grades 11–12 guides add period budgets per unit,
  unit overviews, debate-led delivery and full answer keys.
  The Grade 12 guide's contents label all nine units
  correctly in body order, and so resolve the Grade 12
  student book's scrambled contents (see Honesty notes).
