# About the History — Ethiopia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Ethiopia's own
Social Studies, History and Economics books. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Ethiopia inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **six levels**:

1. **Level 1 — Ancient Ethiopia: Punt, D'aamat and Aksum.**
   The periods of Ethiopian history, Punt, the Addi-Seglemeni
   inscription, D'aamat and Yeha, the settlement of the
   peoples of Ethiopia and the Horn, the language families,
   and Aksum whole — its sources, economy, stelae, the coming
   of Christianity and of Islam's first hijra, and its
   decline.
2. **Level 2 — Medieval Ethiopia: Kingdoms and Sultanates.**
   The chronicles as sources, Zagwe and Lalibela, the
   Solomonic restoration and Amda Tsion, how the Christian
   highland kingdom was governed and traded, the sultanates
   one by one (Shewa, Ifat, Dahlak, Hadiya and Bale, Adal),
   Harar, the southern states, the Gadaa system and the other
   egalitarian orders, the Bete Israel, the Oromo expansion,
   the Adal wars, Gondar, Shewa's growth, and the Era of the
   Princes.
3. **Level 3 — Modern Ethiopia I: Unification and Adwa,
   1855–1936.** Tewodros II, Yohannes IV, the Egyptian and
   Mahdist invasions beaten back, Menilek II and the land
   order, Wuchale and its denunciation, the Adwa campaign and
   the victory of 1 March 1896, and the modern firsts that
   followed — closing with Haile Selassie's consolidation and
   the 1931 constitution.
4. **Level 4 — Modern Ethiopia II: Occupation, Revolution
   and Federation, 1935–Today.** The Fascist invasion, the
   Patriots, liberation in 1941, Eritrea's federation and
   thirty-year struggle, the 1955 constitution, the student
   movement and the peasant risings, the revolution of 1974,
   the Derg, the Ogaden war, 1991 and the transitional
   charter, the 1995 constitution and federal Ethiopia — and
   the books' own closing strands: heritage, Ethiopia on the
   World Heritage list, and indigenous knowledge and its
   peace-making ways.
5. **Level 5 — Ethiopia and Development I: The Economy and
   Its Systems.** Growth against development, the HDI and
   what it leaves out, the capability approach, sustainable
   development and the goals, Ethiopia's resource base and
   land story, agriculture, industry and services, GDP
   measured, and three economic systems in a century — the
   imperial plans, commercial farms and import substitution,
   then the Derg's socialist economy — with trade, transport,
   tourism and communication.
6. **Level 6 — Ethiopia and Development II: Plans,
   Strategies and Enterprise since 1991.** Structural
   adjustment and the 1990s scoreboard, SDPRP, PASDEP, ADLI,
   the uni-modal and bi-modal strategies, GTP I and II and
   what actually grew, the long view of the plans to the
   ten-year plan of 2021–2030, home-grown economic reform,
   industrial parks, education and health policy,
   decentralisation — and enterprise itself: the entrepreneur,
   creativity and innovation, feasibility and finance, forms
   of business organisation, social entrepreneurship,
   Ethiopia's own founders, and management, teamwork,
   productivity and cost-benefit analysis.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Ethiopia's story:* "Start in the deep past: Punt, D'aamat
  and Yeha, then Aksum — its stelae, its trade, the faiths
  arriving by land and sea. Then the medieval land whole:
  Lalibela's rock churches and the restored kingdom beside
  the sultanates and Harar, peoples governing themselves by
  Gadaa and by elders, the wars of Imam Ahmad, Gondar's
  castles, and the princes' era that scattered the crown.
  Then the remaking: Tewodros, Yohannes and Menilek drawing
  one Ethiopia together, invasion after invasion beaten
  back, and Adwa in 1896 — the victory that echoed worldwide.
  Then the hard century: Fascist occupation and the Patriots,
  liberation, empire and its critics, revolution, the Derg,
  and the federal republic of 1995 — closing with the
  heritage and the old peace-making knowledge the books
  refuse to let die. And because a country is also its
  living, the last two levels walk the economy the same books
  teach: what development really means, three systems in a
  hundred years, the plans from the first five-year plan to
  the ten-year plan, and the entrepreneurs — SoleRebels'
  founder among them — building what comes next."

## Contents map — combined books (parts named, not placed)

The two Social Studies books (Grades 7 and 8) are combined
books; only their Ethiopia-history parts are placed in this
course (Levels 1–3 in part, from the Grade 7 book's Chapter 4
and the Grade 8 book's Chapter 4, as each lesson's sources
file them). Their non-History parts, named as filed in the
Social Studies course, stand in that course's other titles
and feed no lesson here:

- **Social Studies Student Textbook, Grade 7:** the physical
  Earth and its characteristics; human evolution and world
  population; human and natural resources; the ancient
  civilisations and medieval history of the world; the
  ancient history of Africa; and contemporary global issues —
  the book's geography, population, resources, world-history
  and global-issues chapters, placed in the Social Studies
  world course under its Earth, people, resources, ancient-
  world, Africa and global-issues titles.
- **Social Studies Student Textbook, Grade 8:** the physical
  Earth and its surface forces; people and socio-economic
  activities; natural resources and socio-economic
  development; slavery and colonialism in the wider world;
  and the contemporary world and green-economy chapters —
  placed the same way in the Social Studies world course;
  only Chapter 4, Modern Ethiopian History, is placed here.

The History books for Grades 9–12 are History books
throughout, but they alternate Ethiopia units with world and
Africa units; the world and Africa units (for example the
Grade 9 book's world civilisations, the Grade 12 book's
colonial experience and post-1945 world) stand in the Social
Studies world course's world and Africa titles, not here —
only the Ethiopia units are placed in this course. The
Economics books for Grades 9–12 likewise carry general
economics units (demand and supply, market structures,
national income, money and the rest) that stand in the Social
Studies course's Microeconomics and Macroeconomics titles;
only their Ethiopia-economy and development units are placed
here, in Levels 5–6.
