# Video script — The Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade and the Triangular Trade

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Atlantic trade arose amid three West African circumstances: the fragmentation of the large states (Ghana, Mali, Songhai), the region's established role as a source of enslaved people for the Sahara traffic, and coastal states' willingness to exchange slaves for arms.

Look at the map. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

The Atlantic trade arose amid three West African circumstances: the fragmentation of the large states (Ghana, Mali, Songhai), the region's established role as a source of enslaved people for the Sahara traffic, and coastal states' willingness to exchange slaves for arms.

The Atlantic system at its scale: the triangular trade, the Middle Passage, plantation Americas, and the numbers carried across — taught as the unit's centre of gravity.

The guide's activity answers supply two causal claims the student book does not make: (1) Prince Henry's sponsored voyages were criticised for their rising expense and had to pay for themselves; his hopes of West African gold materialised only as some gold dust, but the slave trade proved profitable and soon became the basis of the sponsored voyages; (2) Africans who engaged in slave trading did so because they received guns and goods from Europeans, making it lucrative for both sides.

Remember it this way: The Atlantic trade arose amid three West African circumstances: the fragmentation of the large states (Ghana, Mali, Songhai), the region's established role as a source of enslaved people for the Sahara traffic, and coastal states' willingness to exchange slaves for arms.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
