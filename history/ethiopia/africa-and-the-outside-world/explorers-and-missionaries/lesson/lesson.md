## European explorers and missionaries

Europeans remained deeply ignorant of Africa's interior despite centuries of coastal trade; abolition of the slave trade revived their interest, and between the 1780s and 1870s explorers travelled inland — presented by historians as moved by scientific enquiry, adventure or fame, while their financiers were moved by future trading profit; much of Africa was mapped in the process. Catholic missionaries from Portugal had followed the first coastal penetration (late 15th–early 16th centuries) to convert African rulers, and in several cases missionaries promoted and shaped the coming of European colonialism.

## European explorers and missionaries, 1770–1870

Explorer and missionary penetration of the interior (Livingstone named among them) maps Africa for Europe and prepares the ground for partition.
