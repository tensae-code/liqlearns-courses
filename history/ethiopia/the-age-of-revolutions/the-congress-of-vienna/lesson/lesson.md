## The Congress of Vienna

After Napoleon's final defeat, virtually every European state sent delegates to Vienna (arriving from September 1814) to build a long-term peace: Austria's Metternich hosted, with Tsar Alexander I for Russia, Hardenberg for Prussia, Castlereagh (then Wellington) for Britain and Talleyrand for restored Bourbon France. Friction centred on Poland and Saxony and German borders; the Final Act (signed 9 June 1815, Spain refusing over Italy) redistributed territory — Poland largely to Russia, Prussia compensated on the Rhine to guard against France, Austria in Lombardy-Venice, a loose German confederation, Norway to Sweden, Piedmont absorbing Genoa, the Papal States restored — agreed free navigation of international rivers and diplomatic precedence, and gave the slave trade only a pious declaration. Its boundaries lasted over 40 years on the balance-of-power principle, but it ignored nationality and the wishes of inhabitants and built no organ to adapt its work to new forces.

## Peace coda

The book's final text closes Unit 9 not on Napoleon but on a stated moral: war is destructive in all its forms and peace is the precondition of industrialisation and democratisation — the curriculum's own closing value, preserved verbatim in intent as a summary placement.
