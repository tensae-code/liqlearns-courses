## Do it first

## The Industrial Revolution (first treatment)

The Industrial Revolution means machines replacing hand labour: a shift from farming and handicrafts to factory manufacturing, beginning in Great Britain in the 1780s and spreading over decades. Its power was steam — Savery (1698) and Newcomen (c.1705–1711) preceded James Watt's improved engine (1782–84), which was then applied to factory machinery and to transport, first steamboats on rivers. (The unit is the first of the book's two treatments; its effects are Unit 9's subject.)

## The Industrial Revolution — what changed

Beginning in Britain: steam, factories, coal and iron, new transport — why Britain first and what the factory system did to work.
