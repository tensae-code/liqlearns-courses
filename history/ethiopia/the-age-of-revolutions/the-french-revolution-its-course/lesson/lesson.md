# 1789–1799 — Rights of Man, Republic, Terror

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: 1789–1799 — Rights of Man, Republic, Terror. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The French Revolution — its course: Assembly, Rights, Terror

In sequence: the National Assembly formed; in August 1789 it declared the Rights of Man and of the Citizen; the 1791 constitution established a limited monarchy with voting restricted to tax-paying "active citizens" (the rest "passive"); the revolution radicalised into the Reign of Terror under the Jacobins and Robespierre, the guillotine executing counter-revolutionaries, Girondins and Danton's followers, alongside Jacobin reforms — until the revolutionary government itself was overthrown in 1799.

## The French Revolution — republic, Terror and reaction

The monarchy falls, the republic fights Europe, the Committee of Public Safety runs the Reign of Terror (1793–94), and reaction follows Thermidor.
