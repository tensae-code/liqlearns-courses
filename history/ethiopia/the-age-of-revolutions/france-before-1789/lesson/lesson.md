# France Before 1789 — Three Estates and a Breaking Order

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: France Before 1789 — Three Estates and a Breaking Order. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The French Revolution — the old order and its causes

Pre-revolutionary France was a feudal, oppressed society divided into three Estates: the First (clergy) and Second (nobility) owned vast land, held every privilege and paid no taxes; the Third — peasants and bourgeoisie together, the largest section — owned little land, held no rights and paid heavy taxes. Onto this injustice came Enlightenment ideas and the immediate example of the American war; the revolution opened when revolutionaries stormed the Bastille prison on 14 July 1789, and ran until Napoleon's 1799 overthrow of the revolutionary government.

## The French Revolution — causes and outbreak

France's old regime — estates, royal debt, bread prices — collapses in 1789: Estates-General, Tennis Court Oath, Bastille, Declaration of the Rights of Man.
