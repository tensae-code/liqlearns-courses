The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

Napoleon Bonaparte (born 1769 on Corsica; an army officer from his twenties, "the son of the revolution", famous from 1795 as the revolution's defender) seized power in 1799 and ruled France as a military dictatorship that nevertheless kept the Revolution's fruits, above all civil equality, and did great constructive work — without political democracy.

Napoleon's rise from revolutionary general to emperor: his reforms (tax, bank, schools, the Code), his conquests remaking Europe's map, and his fall — closing with the Congress of Vienna restoring the pre-1790 order's borders.

The guide's Napoleon overview adds the career steps and domestic programme the student book compresses: victory over Austria in Italy in 1796 made his name; the short-lived conquest of Egypt 1798–1801 followed; as ruler he made employment and promotion in government depend on ability, and expanded education, opening state secondary schools and universities.
