## Industrial capitalism in Europe — effects of the Industrial Revolution

The Industrial Revolution (begun in late-1700s Britain, spreading slowly worldwide) created industrial capitalism: politically and economically it matured into laissez-faire, liberalism, competition and a free-market economy, with Britain by the mid-19th century "the world's workshop". Socially it grew cities and created two new classes — the bourgeoisie, owning factories, machines, raw materials and finished goods, and the proletariat, selling labour for poorly paid wages under strict conditions — and with them strikes and labour protest. The unit summary places this revolution in a chain: the 17th-century English Revolution (a bourgeois revolution destroying feudal autocracy), the American war, and the French Revolution together built modern capitalist society. — Printed pp. 189–191 (summary printed p.207).

## Effects of the Industrial Revolution

Political, economic and social effects systematised: urbanisation, population growth, a middle class and a working class, trade unions, socialism's rise, and the push to imperialism for markets and raw materials.
