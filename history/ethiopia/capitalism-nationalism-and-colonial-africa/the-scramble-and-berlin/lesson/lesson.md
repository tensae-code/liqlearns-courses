## The Scramble for Africa and the Berlin Conference

Between 1884 and 1914 European nations took control of the continent in the Scramble, partitioning the largely unexplored interior into protectorates, colonies and "free-trade areas"; the Berlin Conference of 1884–1885 is the diplomatic machine of the partition, called to regulate colonial rivalries before they became wars among Europeans.

## Content the guide adds — Berlin's mechanics: who called it, who came, who was absent *(teacher-guide evidence)*

The guide's Scramble overview adds the conference's working facts: the Berlin Conference (1884–85) was also known as the "Congo Conference"; Portugal proposed it; fourteen European countries met in Berlin regarding the division of Africa; although the conference was about Africa, Africans were not invited nor involved; and of the fourteen, France, Germany, Great Britain and Portugal were the major players.
