# Before the Scramble — Africa Trading, Mostly Unruled

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Before the Scramble — Africa Trading, Mostly Unruled. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## General background to colonialism

Africa's commercial transition was driven by industrialising Western Europe's rising demand for industrial inputs: African exports broadened to vegetable and palm oils, groundnuts, gum, ivory, gold, hides and skins, rubber, cotton, copper and cash crops (cocoa, coffee, tea, tobacco). Before the 1880s only about 10% of Africa was ruled by foreign powers — nominal Turkish rule in much of North Africa, Omani rule in Zanzibar, the British in Sierra Leone, the French in Algeria, Boers and British in South Africa, the Portuguese in Mozambique — the baseline the Scramble then overturned.
