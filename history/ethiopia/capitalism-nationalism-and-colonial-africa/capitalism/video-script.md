# Video script — Capitalism — Features and Its Marxian Critique

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Capitalism, dominant in the West since feudalism's collapse, rests on private ownership of most means of production; the Industrial Revolution (begun in 18th-century England, then Belgium, France and the German states) transformed it — coal and steam replaced wind and water, the factory system replaced the domestic system of home workshops, and new socio-economic classes, above all industrial capitalists and factory workers, emerged from the tremendous economic transformation of Western Europe and the USA in the 19th century.

Capitalism is defined from mercantilism's womb: private ownership, market distribution, profit, laissez-faire — and the lesson explicitly includes the theoretical foundations of Marxian economics as the system's great critique.

The guide's overview states the system in class terms the student book leaves softer: capitalism is a socio-economic system in which resources are owned by a few rich private people and production is for profit; the factors of production are owned by the capitalists (the bourgeoisie), the proletariat sell their labour for wages, and there is complete free trade and competition with no restriction or interference by government — everything left to private enterprise.

Capitalism and Marxian economics are taught first so colonisation, the Russian Revolution and the Cold War are analysed with named concepts (imperialism, laissez-faire, Social Darwinism) rather than narrated bare.
