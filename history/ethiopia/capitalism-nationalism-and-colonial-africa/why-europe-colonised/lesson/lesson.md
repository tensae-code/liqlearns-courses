# Why Europe Colonised — Motives and the Civilising-Mission Claim

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Why Europe Colonised — Motives and the Civilising-Mission Claim. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The motives of European colonialism

The lesson weighs the motives — economic (raw materials and markets, assessed as the driving factors), strategic and ideological — and sets pupils to assess the role of missionaries on the eve of colonisation; the colonisers' self-justification, the "civilizing mission", is stated plainly as an asserted structure of ideas in which European practices were civilised and universal and non-European societies uncivilised and inferior — with Kipling's "Take up the White Man's Burden" printed in the book as the period document of that ideology.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
