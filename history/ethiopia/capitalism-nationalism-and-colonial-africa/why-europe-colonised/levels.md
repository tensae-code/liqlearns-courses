# Why Europe Colonised — Motives and the Civilising-Mission Claim — depth

This lesson is one whole lesson with no sub lessons (2026-11-09 ruling: sub lessons only where a lesson genuinely has distinct sequential parts). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files.

Pass mark: The lesson weighs the motives — economic (raw materials and markets, assessed as the driving factors), strategic and ideological — and sets pupils to assess the role of missionaries on the eve of colonisation; the colonisers' self-justification, the "civilizing mission", is stated plainly as an asserted structure of ideas in which European practices were civilised and universal and non-European societies uncivilised and inferior — with Kipling's "Take up the White Man's Burden" printed in the book as the period document of that ideology. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
