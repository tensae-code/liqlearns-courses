# Video script — The Eastern Question — Nationalism in the Balkans, Empires in Collision

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Eastern Question was the 19th- and early-20th-century diplomatic problem created by three forces together: rising nationalist feeling among the Balkan peoples, the declining Ottoman (Turkish) Empire, and the divergent interests of the Great Powers in the Near East.

Look at the map. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

The Eastern Question was the 19th- and early-20th-century diplomatic problem created by three forces together: rising nationalist feeling among the Balkan peoples, the declining Ottoman (Turkish) Empire, and the divergent interests of the Great Powers in the Near East.

The Ottoman Empire's decline becomes the "Eastern Question": great-power rivalry over its remains, Balkan nationalisms, and the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 as the fuse toward 1914.

The guide's presentation defines the Eastern Question explicitly as the Balkan peoples' liberation struggle against Ottoman Turkish rule plus the great-power politics accompanying it, and notes it remained unsolved even after World War I — a sharper formulation than the student text's, used as the teaching definition.

Remember it this way: The Eastern Question was the 19th- and early-20th-century diplomatic problem created by three forces together: rising nationalist feeling among the Balkan peoples, the declining Ottoman (Turkish) Empire, and the divergent interests of the Great Powers in the Near East.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
