# West Africa Resists — Samori Ture, Ahmadu Seku, Asante

## Do it first

## Early resistance in West Africa

African responses ranged from armed resistance to nonviolent forms — indigenous press, trade unionism, organised religion, associations, literature and art, mass action. West Africa's armed exemplars: Ahmadu Seku of the Tukulor Empire, who preferred diplomacy until French violations forced clashes, was defeated, exiled to Sokoto, and saw the French rule through his brother; Samori Ture (c.1830–1900), the Mandinka Muslim leader who united the region between the Niger sources and the Upper Volta basin and fought the French in protracted war from 1882 to 1898; the Asante kingdom under Asantehene Prempeh, defeated after bloody war as Britain completed the Gold Coast colony by 1900; and Nigeria, conquered by 1910 through divide-and-rule, superior weapons and African-recruited troops.

## African resistance against colonial expansion

Resistance is taught as a spectrum — primary armed resistance (Samori Touré, the Ashante, Ethiopian victory at Adwa cross-referenced), revolts such as Maji Maji, and later organised movements — with African agency at the centre.
