# Video script — Italy Unifies — Cavour, Garibaldi, and Unification from Above and Below

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Italy, broken into many smaller states sharing language, culture, history and religion, unified in the second half of the 19th century by different patriotic approaches working together; Cavour is named the architect of the united Italy for his central involvement in the unification process, the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861, and the new government inherited the challenge of tension between the industrialising north and the agrarian south (the exercises correct the reversed claim) and a weakened papacy.

Italy unifies through Piedmont-Sardinia's statecraft (Cavour), Garibaldi's volunteers and war with Austria, completed 1861–70 with Rome as capital.

The guide instructs that Italian unification be taught as the joint product of Piedmont-Sardinia's government measures "from above" and popular revolt "from below", and German unification taught by comparison with Italy — the analytical frame for student placements 5–6.
