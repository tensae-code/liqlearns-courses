# How Indigenous Knowledge Behaves — Oral, Local, Inherited

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: How Indigenous Knowledge Behaves — Oral, Local, Inherited. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Characteristics of indigenous knowledge

The lesson fixes the concept's marks (the review tests them): it is transmitted orally from generation to generation, provides problem-solving strategies for local communities, and is generated within a community in its location and culture — local, practised and inherited, not universal or written-first.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
