## Indigenous knowledge — what it is and what it does

Indigenous knowledge is the knowledge that helps a society judge which activities are compatible with its way of life; the knowledge local people use to make a living in a particular environment — "local knowledge" — passed down from generation to generation through oral tradition and practice. It shapes worldviews and guides social, economic, political and spiritual survival, and takes forms including indigenous laws, rituals, local classifications, management practices, decision-making processes, conflict-management practices and rights; its role in overall and sustainable development is the lesson's argument.

## Indigenous knowledge — definition and characteristic features

Indigenous knowledge is defined as communities' own tested knowledge of nature, farming, medicine, weather and social order, characterised as local, oral, experience-based and holistic — with Ethiopian examples (gadaa governance, traditional medicine, terracing, calendar) treated as knowledge, not folklore.
