# Hands-on — Ethiopia on the World Heritage List

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: The registered list is walked site by site: the Stelae of Aksum (inscribed 1980; symbol of the Aksumite kingdom's wealth and importance); the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela (one of the first sites ever listed, 1978); the Lower Valley of the Awash (1980; palaeo-anthropological research 300 km north-east of Addis Ababa in Afar); the Lower Valley of the Omo (1980; human and animal fossils and stone tools fundamental to studying human evolution); Tiya (36 monuments including 32 carved stelae, 90 km south of Addis Ababa) — with the remaining registered sites (the review adds Harar Jugol and Fiche-Chambalala to the pupil's map of them). Hold it in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the story as a row of scenes, one scene per part of the lesson, with the people, the place and the turn in each scene. Say each scene aloud as you draw it: who is there, what happens, what changes.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see its teaching at work — a story of the past being told, asked about, placed in time or acted on — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
