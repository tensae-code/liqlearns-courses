# Ethiopia on the World Heritage List

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Ethiopia on the World Heritage List. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Ethiopia's UNESCO-registered heritages

The registered list is walked site by site: the Stelae of Aksum (inscribed 1980; symbol of the Aksumite kingdom's wealth and importance); the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela (one of the first sites ever listed, 1978); the Lower Valley of the Awash (1980; palaeo-anthropological research 300 km north-east of Addis Ababa in Afar); the Lower Valley of the Omo (1980; human and animal fossils and stone tools fundamental to studying human evolution); Tiya (36 monuments including 32 carved stelae, 90 km south of Addis Ababa) — with the remaining registered sites (the review adds Harar Jugol and Fiche-Chambalala to the pupil's map of them).

## UNESCO-registered heritages of Ethiopia

The register is walked site by site: Aksum obelisks, Tiya stelae, Harar Jegol, Lalibela's rock churches, Konso's terraces, Gondar's castles, and the intangible inscriptions — Timket, Meskel, the Gada system, Fiche Chembelela, Irreecha discussed in the review — closing with preservation duties and Ethiopia's unregistered wealth.
