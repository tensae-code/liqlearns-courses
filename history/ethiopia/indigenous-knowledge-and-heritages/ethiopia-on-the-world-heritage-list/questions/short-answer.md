# Ethiopia on the World Heritage List — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what is this lesson's one thing?
**Answer:** The registered list is walked site by site: the Stelae of Aksum (inscribed 1980; symbol of the Aksumite kingdom's wealth and importance); the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela (one of the first sites ever listed, 1978); the Lower Valley of the Awash (1980; palaeo-anthropological research 300 km north-east of Addis Ababa in Afar); the Lower Valley of the Omo (1980; human and animal fossils and stone tools fundamental to studying human evolution); Tiya (36 monuments including 32 carved stelae, 90 km south of Addis Ababa) — with the remaining registered sites (the review adds Harar Jugol and Fiche-Chambalala to the pupil's map of them).

**2.** What does the part "UNESCO-registered heritages of Ethiopia" teach?
**Answer:** The register is walked site by site: Aksum obelisks, Tiya stelae, Harar Jegol, Lalibela's rock churches, Konso's terraces, Gondar's castles, and the intangible inscriptions — Timket, Meskel, the Gada system, Fiche Chembelela, Irreecha discussed in the review — closing with preservation duties and Ethiopia's unregistered wealth.
