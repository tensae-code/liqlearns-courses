# Hands-on — Three Kinds of Heritage — Nature, Practice, Thing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Three kinds are defined in UNESCO's frame: natural heritage (natural features, geological and physiographical formations, habitats of threatened species, and natural sites of outstanding scientific, conservation or beauty value); intangible heritage (practices, representations, expressions, knowledge and skills, with their instruments and artefacts — in the domains of oral traditions and expressions including language, performing arts, social practices, rituals and festive events, knowledge and practices concerning nature and the universe, and indigenous handicrafts); and tangible heritage (physical artefacts produced, maintained and transmitted across generations — artistic creations, buildings, historic places, monuments). Hold it in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the story as a row of scenes, one scene per part of the lesson, with the people, the place and the turn in each scene. Say each scene aloud as you draw it: who is there, what happens, what changes.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see its teaching at work — a story of the past being told, asked about, placed in time or acted on — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
