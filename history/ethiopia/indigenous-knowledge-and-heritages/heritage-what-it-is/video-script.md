# Video script — Heritage — What It Is and Why It Matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Heritage is what can be passed from generation to generation, conserved or inherited, with historic or cultural value: it can be a physical object, property, building or place that is owned and passed on — and just as truly a practice: language, culture, popular song, literature and dress help us comprehend who we are as much as artefacts and buildings.

Heritage is defined as inherited physical artefacts and intangible attributes; its values (historical, national-symbolic, socio-political) and the 1972 World Heritage Convention's types — cultural and natural — are laid out.

Ending a history course on indigenous knowledge and heritage preservation — rather than on the present government — is a deliberate curricular statement; the build preserves the order.

Activity 9.3's guided group task — identify tangible and intangible heritage sites in the pupils' own town, zone or region, with the teacher helping the identification — is a ready-made local-fieldwork lesson the student book only implies.
