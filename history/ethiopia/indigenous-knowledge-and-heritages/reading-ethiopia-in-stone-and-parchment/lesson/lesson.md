# Reading Ethiopia in Stone and Parchment

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Reading Ethiopia in Stone and Parchment. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## A tangible-heritage tour — manuscripts, temples, mosques, palaces, caves, walls

Beyond the UNESCO list the book tours Ethiopia's built and written heritage by kind: Ge'ez manuscripts (figured); temples — the Temple of Yeha, linked to South Arabia around 700 BC (figured); mosques; palaces (the review names Jimma Abba Jifar II's palace); houses and caves — the Sof Omar caves (figured); and the Halala Keela (Halala Kab) wall of Dawuro (figured).

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
