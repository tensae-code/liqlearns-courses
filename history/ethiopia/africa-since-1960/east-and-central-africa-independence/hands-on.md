# Hands-on — East Africa — Nyerere's Tanganyika, Obote's Uganda, Kenya's Hard Road

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Uganda attained independence in 1962 as a single entity under Prime Minister Milton Obote, through a coalition of the royalist Kabaka Yekka movement and Obote's Uganda People's Congress; Tanganyika won independence in 1961 under Julius Nyerere's TANU, Zanzibar in 1963, and the two federated in 1964 as Tanzania; Kenya's road was not smooth — its peasants had been dispossessed, and its struggle was the region's hardest; the former German colonies of Rwanda and Burundi, handed to other powers as League mandates, closed the section (Rwanda a republic at independence in 1962, Burundi a kingdom until November 1966). Hold it in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the story as a row of scenes, one scene per part of the lesson, with the people, the place and the turn in each scene. Say each scene aloud as you draw it: who is there, what happens, what changes.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see its teaching at work — a story of the past being told, asked about, placed in time or acted on — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
