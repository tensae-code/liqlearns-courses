# Video script — Pan-Africanism — From Manchester 1945 to the OAU and the AU

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Pan-Africanism — the idea that peoples of African descent have common interests and should be unified — originated in the New World in the 18th–19th centuries within the Black liberation struggle; at the Fifth Pan-African Congress (Manchester, October 1945) African delegates dominated for the first time and the focus became the liberation of colonised Africa.

Look at the map. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Pan-Africanism — the idea that peoples of African descent have common interests and should be unified — originated in the New World in the 18th–19th centuries within the Black liberation struggle; at the Fifth Pan-African Congress (Manchester, October 1945) African delegates dominated for the first time and the focus became the liberation of colonised Africa.

Pan-Africanism's idea-history culminates institutionally: the OAU founded in Addis Ababa (1963), its liberation and unity record, and its transformation into the African Union (2002).
