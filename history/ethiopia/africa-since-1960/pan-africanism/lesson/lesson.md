## Pan-Africanism and the OAU

Pan-Africanism — the idea that peoples of African descent have common interests and should be unified — originated in the New World in the 18th–19th centuries within the Black liberation struggle; at the Fifth Pan-African Congress (Manchester, October 1945) African delegates dominated for the first time and the focus became the liberation of colonised Africa. Its culmination was the OAU: pupils prepare the timeline of its founding, chart its main organs, and trace its replacement by the AU. The book's map shows the years of African countries' independence.

## Pan-Africanism — from the OAU to the African Union

Pan-Africanism's idea-history culminates institutionally: the OAU founded in Addis Ababa (1963), its liberation and unity record, and its transformation into the African Union (2002).
