# Hands-on — North Africa Frees Itself — Egypt to Algeria's Hard Road

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: The first breakthroughs came in the north: Egypt, a British protectorate from 1882, where Saad Zaghlul's nationalist delegation's demand to end the protectorate was backed by demonstrations and strikes across the country; Morocco, independent by negotiated settlement on 2 March 1956, its French and Spanish zones reunited; Tunisia, independent in 1956 under Habib Bourguiba's Neo-Destour Party; and Algeria, where a large settler population, organised in the "Secret Armed Organization" to block independence, forced the FLN under Ahmed Ben Bella into a bitter guerrilla war from 1954 to 1962, ended by agreement and referendum. Hold it in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the story as a row of scenes, one scene per part of the lesson, with the people, the place and the turn in each scene. Say each scene aloud as you draw it: who is there, what happens, what changes.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see its teaching at work — a story of the past being told, asked about, placed in time or acted on — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
