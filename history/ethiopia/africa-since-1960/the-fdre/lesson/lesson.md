## The 1995 constitution and the FDRE — ethnic federalism and its challenges

The 1995 constitution established the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia: nine regional states (and two city administrations in the summary's count) in a federation many commentators call "ethnic federalism" for its ethno-linguistic basis of state formation. The compact document — 106 articles in eleven chapters — prioritises rule of law, self-determination, popular (ethnic) sovereignty, inter-ethnic and inter-religious equality and gender equality, plus constitutional supremacy, fundamental rights, secularism and accountable, transparent government; parliamentarism through the House of Peoples' Representatives (five-year elected terms) and the House of Federation. Its stated objective is "unity in diversity"; its posed challenges are the threat of secession and fragmentation, extreme inter-state imbalances, state-building in impoverished and historically underserved states, executive power-sharing, capital-city choices, and the quest for more than one federal working language.
