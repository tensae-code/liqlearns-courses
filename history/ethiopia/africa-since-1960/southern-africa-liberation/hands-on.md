# Hands-on — Southern Africa — Liberation at Gunpoint, 1960s–1980

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud, in your own words: Southern Africa was the settler belt — British Southern Rhodesia, Northern Rhodesia, Nyasaland, Bechuanaland and Basutoland, South Africa itself (holding South West Africa/Namibia under mandate), and Portugal's Angola and Mozambique — and its 1960s–70s dominant theme was armed struggle: in Zimbabwe, ZANU under Robert Mugabe and ZAPU under Joshua Nkomo fought a long, bitter war that ended white minority rule, bringing majority rule in April 1980 with Mugabe as first prime minister and Rhodesia renamed Zimbabwe; in Namibia, SWAPO organised the long war against South African rule; the Portuguese colonies' independence was delayed until the mid-1970s. Hold it in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the story as a row of scenes, one scene per part of the lesson, with the people, the place and the turn in each scene. Say each scene aloud as you draw it: who is there, what happens, what changes.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see its teaching at work — a story of the past being told, asked about, placed in time or acted on — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
