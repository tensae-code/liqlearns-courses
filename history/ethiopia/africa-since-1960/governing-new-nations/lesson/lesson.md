# Governing New Nations — Parties, Coups, Openings

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Governing New Nations — Parties, Coups, Openings. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Politics in independent African states

One-party systems, military coups, the socialist and capitalist paths tried, and the democratic openings of the 1990s.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches, in your own words: One-party systems, military coups, the socialist and capitalist paths tried, and the democratic openings of the 1990s. Give one real example from the story. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Retell this lesson's story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Draw the story as a row of scenes, one scene per part of the lesson, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and note one place where its teaching connects to your own locality.
