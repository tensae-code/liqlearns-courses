# Rist and Gult — How the Christian Kingdom Held Its Land — depth

This lesson's parts are distinct and sequential, so they stand as its sub lessons (2026-11-09 ruling: sub lessons only where a lesson genuinely has distinct sequential parts). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files.

Pass mark: The restored kingdom was administered through land rights: rist was a hereditary, communal birthright claim to land, its peasant holders owing tribute (gibber) to the state in return for security; gult was a non-hereditary right to collect tribute, granted to an official as a substitute for salary, lasting only while he stayed loyal — and the bale-gult holder in turn kept law and order in his area. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
