# Kingdom Versus Adal — the Wars of Imam Ahmad, 1529–1559

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Kingdom Versus Adal — the Wars of Imam Ahmad, 1529–1559. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Christian kingdom and Adal, 1520s–1559 — the great war

The underlying conflict was monopoly of long-distance trade (great wealth, and the link between Ethiopia's peoples) plus territorial expansion: Ifat, defeated in 1332, lost control of the Zeila route to Amda Seyon, and its successors fought to recover independence and the route. The war's height ran 1529–1543 — Imam Ahmed's campaigns, with his decisive victory over Christian forces (the review fixes the battles of Shimbra Kure and Woina Dega) — and the sequence closes in 1559, when Emir Nur Mujahid defeated and killed Emperor Gelawdewos in the Awash valley yet could not exploit the victory because Oromo forces had reached the Harar area. — Printed pp. 126–130 (and printed p.138).

## Christian kingdom versus Adal — to the wars of the 1520s–40s

Rivalry over trade and territory hardens into the wars of Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi (Gragn), whose campaigns of 1529–43 shatter the highland kingdom before Portuguese musketeers help reverse them — told as outline: invasion, near-collapse, and the turning at Wayna Daga (1543).
