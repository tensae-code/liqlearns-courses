The underlying conflict was monopoly of long-distance trade (great wealth, and the link between Ethiopia's peoples) plus territorial expansion: Ifat, defeated in 1332, lost control of the Zeila route to Amda Seyon, and its successors fought to recover independence and the route.

Rivalry over trade and territory hardens into the wars of Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi (Gragn), whose campaigns of 1529–43 shatter the highland kingdom before Portuguese musketeers help reverse them — told as outline: invasion, near-collapse, and the turning at Wayna Daga (1543).
