# The Oromo Expansion — a Map Redrawn, Peoples Intermingled

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Oromo Expansion — a Map Redrawn, Peoples Intermingled. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Oromo expansion and integration — outcomes

The period's defining movement: expanding Oromo destroyed the old states of Damot, Bizamo and Ennarya; Damot and Gafat peoples were assimilated or pushed north into the Christian community; the Christian kingdom, weakened territorially and financially, shifted its centre from Shewa to Lake Tana and Gondar; Adal shrank to walled Harar. The movement's long outcomes were religious, ethnic and linguistic interaction and intermingling — intermarriage, changed abodes, changed cultures, and new identities.

## The Oromo population movement

From the mid-16th century the Oromo expand northward in waves organised by the *gadaa* generation system, transforming the demography and politics of the whole region — the book's major movement story.
