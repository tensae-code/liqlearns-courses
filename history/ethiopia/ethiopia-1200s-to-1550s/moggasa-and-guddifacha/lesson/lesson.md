## Moggasa and Guddifacha — adoption as integration

The Oromo assimilated non-Oromo people by two adoption mechanisms: Guddifacha, a family's adoption of a child, who then enjoyed rights equal to a biological child's; and Moggasa, the adoption of individuals or whole communities into an Oromo clan, whose members then shared everything equally, gaining protection and material benefit. The two institutions let peoples mix and live in peace, fed the expansion (adopted populations joined the next campaigns), spread the Gadaa system and Oromo language to neighbours, and worked both ways — the Oromo also adopted neighbours' cultures, including monarchical systems.
