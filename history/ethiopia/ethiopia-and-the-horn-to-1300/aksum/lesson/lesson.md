## The Aksumite kingdom — rise, trade and reach

Aksum emerged in the 1st century AD with its nucleus and political-religious centre at the city of Aksum, adorned with temples and monolithic stelae. Between the 4th and 6th centuries its kings expanded it beyond the Red Sea into southern Arabia, west to the Abay (Blue Nile), north to Eritrea's tip and south to northern Shewa. Its economy rested first on plough agriculture and tribute linking king and people through a demarcated social structure, then increasingly on Red Sea trade through the port of Adulis, which made it rich.

## The Aksumite Kingdom

Aksum's rise: Red Sea trade through Adulis, Ezana's conversion, coinage, Ge'ez script, stelae and obelisks, Kaleb's Himyar expedition, and the kingdom's long decline as trade routes shifted.
