# Before Aksum — Punt, Damat and the Early States

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Before Aksum — Punt, Damat and the Early States. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Pre-Aksumite states (Book 1 telling)

Aksum was not the first centralised polity of the region: principalities rose and fell before it — Punt, Hawlti-Melazo, Addi-Seglemeni, Metera, Kaskase and Da'amat are named — in a geographical setting tied to the Red Sea and the northern highlands, interacting with the world beyond.

## Pre-Aksumite states (Book 3 telling)

Before Aksum: Damat (Da'amat) and other Red Sea–linked polities of the northern highlands, with Sabaean contacts, temples and inscriptions such as Yeha.

## Content the guide adds — Addi-Seglemeni and the Habashat/Agazi migration *(teacher-guide evidence)*

The guide's answer to the pre-Aksumite activity gives a concrete find the student book only names: Addi-Seglemeni lies 10 km south-west of Aksum, where a stone slab was fetched and the oldest Ethiopian monumental inscription discovered; and it states that these sites were initiated by the Habashat and Agazi tribes who migrated into Ethiopia from Saudi Arabia (southern Arabia). It also answers the interaction question directly: Ethiopia sits at the centre of the Horn and sought formal relations with the outside world from ancient times, playing an active regional and global role even before Aksum.
