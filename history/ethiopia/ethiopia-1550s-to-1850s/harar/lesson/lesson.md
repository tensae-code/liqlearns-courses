# Harar — Walled City of Trade and Islamic Learning

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Harar — Walled City of Trade and Islamic Learning. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Eastern states — Harar

After Ifat's defeat in 1332, members of its ruling family retreated to Harar and established the Sultanate of Adal there c.1380; Harar became a centre of Islamic culture and political power controlling the trade routes from the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean coast, and from the late 16th to the 19th century the walled town was a major trade centre between coast and interior highlands and a seat of Islamic learning since the 14th century; its later Emirate was founded by Ali ibn Dawd.

## Harar

The walled city of Harar under its emirs: a commercial and Islamic scholarly city linking highlands, lowlands and the Gulf of Aden trade.
