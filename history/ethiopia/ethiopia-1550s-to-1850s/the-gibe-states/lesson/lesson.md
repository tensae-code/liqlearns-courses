# The Gibe States — From Gadaa to Kingdoms of Coffee and Islam

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: The Gibe States — From Gadaa to Kingdoms of Coffee and Islam. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Western states — the Oromo Gibe states

In Gibe and Wallaga the expanding Oromo gradually abandoned Gadaa and formed monarchies in the early 19th century: Limmu-Ennarya; Jimma, the strongest, popular under Abba Jifar I (r.1830–1855), resting on Islam's ideological support and commercial prosperity from coffee and the slave trade; Gomma, the earliest Gibe state to accept Islam, under rulers such as Oncho Jilcha; and Gera, known from c.1835 under the warrior-administrator Tullu Gunji, in conflict with Gomma. A shared mark of the Gibe states was conversion from indigenous religion to Islam in the first half of the 19th century; their kings' title was Moti, and Leqa Naqemte's ruler Kumsa Moroda and the Jeberti Muslim merchants belong to this western commercial world.

## The Gibe and Leqa Oromo states

In the west the Oromo monarchies of the Gibe valley and Leqa — Jimma the largest — grow rich on coffee, ivory and the slave trade, with Abba Jifar's Jimma as the type case.
