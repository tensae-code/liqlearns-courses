# The Gibe States — From Gadaa to Kingdoms of Coffee and Islam — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1.** In your own words, what is this lesson's one thing?
**Answer:** In Gibe and Wallaga the expanding Oromo gradually abandoned Gadaa and formed monarchies in the early 19th century: Limmu-Ennarya; Jimma, the strongest, popular under Abba Jifar I (r.1830–1855), resting on Islam's ideological support and commercial prosperity from coffee and the slave trade; Gomma, the earliest Gibe state to accept Islam, under rulers such as Oncho Jilcha; and Gera, known from c.1835 under the warrior-administrator Tullu Gunji, in conflict with Gomma.

**2.** What does the part "The Gibe and Leqa Oromo states" teach?
**Answer:** In the west the Oromo monarchies of the Gibe valley and Leqa — Jimma the largest — grow rich on coffee, ivory and the slave trade, with Abba Jifar's Jimma as the type case.
