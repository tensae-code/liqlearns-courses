# Shewa — the Kingdom That Grew Apart

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Shewa — the Kingdom That Grew Apart. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## The Kingdom of Shewa (Book 1 telling)

Around the end of the 17th or beginning of the 18th century the Shewan kingdom grew in the Manz region under its founder, the local chief Negasi: Shewa had been the Christian state's earlier political centre before pressure from Adal and the Oromo expansion shifted the capital to Lake Tana; Gondarine kings such as Iyassu I (r.1682–1706) sent expeditions to control it, but Shewa gradually detached itself — the kingdom that would matter most in the century after this book's period.

## The Kingdom of Shewa (Book 3 telling)

While the north fragments, Shewa grows under its own dynasty — Sahle Selassie expanding and enriching the kingdom that will later reunify Ethiopia.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read the story once for the turn — who wanted what, what changed, what came of it. Then read it again for the names and dates; they stick once the turn is clear.
