# Gumuz, Shinasha and the Gold Sheikhdoms of Benishangul

## Do it first

## Western peoples — Gumuz, Shinasha and the Benishangul sheikhdoms

Along the narrow lowland of the Ethio-Sudanese border lived Nilo-Saharan-speaking peoples in a gold-producing region known since Punt and associated with "silent commerce". The Gumuz practised small-scale shifting (slash-and-burn) cultivation; the Shinasha prized cattle and protected reserved forests — cutting their trees was forbidden, an environmental tradition fitted to their land. In Benishangul, interaction with Arabic-speaking merchants produced the Muslim sheikhdoms of Asosa, Benishangul and Khomosha, which — targets for Egypt in the 1820s and Sudanese-Mahdist penetration in the 1880s because of the region's gold — defended their independence.

## Benishangul-Gumuz sheikhdoms and the Gambella chiefs

The western borderlands: the sheikhdoms of Benishangul-Gumuz tied to Sudanese trade, and the local chiefs of the Gambella peoples.
