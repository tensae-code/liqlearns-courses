## Cushitic states and the Sera–Woma systems

From the mid-16th century Highland East Cushitic peoples — Hadiya, Kambata, Sidama, Tambaro, Kabena, Halaba, Gedeo, Konso, Burji — formed states relatively free of outside domination. Hadiya's traditional administration, Sera, had elected officers under an adila/garada; Sidama transferred power in Luwa ceremonies every eight years, its sub-tribes ruled by a woma (supreme judge) who together formed the woma-songo; Kambata, Christian since the 14th–15th centuries, was governed by elected woma leaders; Halaba's woma chaired the Ogate clan council of twelve judges ruling by traditional and Islamic law; Gedeo adopted a Gadaa-type system after an era of women rulers.

## Cushitic states under the sera-woma system; Gedeo, Konso, Gurage

Sidama-area states under the *sera-woma* order, then short studies of the Gedeo, the terraced Konso with their walled towns, and the Gurage chiefdoms.
