# Kafa, Yem and the Tato Kingdoms

## Do it first

Before you read, say out loud what you already know about this: Kafa, Yem and the Tato Kingdoms. Who was involved? Where did it happen? What changed? Keep those three questions — who, where, what turned — in mind as you read: this lesson is told as a story, in that shape.

## Southern states I — Kafa and Yem

After the Christian kingdom's decline, southern peoples built independent states. Kafa, south of the Gojeb and north of the Omo, known since at least the late 14th century in a rainy, forested, fertile land inhabited by peoples related to the Bench, was ruled by a king titled Tato whose insignia were twelve objects, many of gold; its capital was Bonga, with Anderecha as second seat; it was highly organised, influenced by but independent of the Christian highland state, and survived Oromo pressure. Yem, its neighbour, repulsed the Oromo in the Gibe region but came under growing pressure after Jimma Abba Jifar formed, with raids and counter-raids lasting into the late 19th century.

## Omotic states under the tato system

The Omotic kingdoms of the south-west (Kafa among them) under sacral *tato* kingship — Kafa's isolation, coffee forests and court.
