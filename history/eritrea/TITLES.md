# History — Eritrea course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

History belongs to the country: this course is Eritrea's own —
built from the Ministry of Education's Social Studies student
books for Grades 4–8 (Tigrinya for Grades 4–5, English for
Grades 6–8). Inside the course the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: the titles below sit inside their
levels, in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grade each book comes from lives only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the books': method first (what
history is, its sources, dating the past, heritage and the
museum), then ancient Eritrea told in strict order —
migrations, the Red Sea trading towns, the faiths, the Beja
kingdoms and the Bahri Negestat — with the wider world's
ladder of societies (slave, caste, feudal, Ottoman) climbed
beside it, then the coast powers and the resistance to each,
and last the whole modern chain: Italy, Britain, the parties,
the federation and its end, the thirty years' armed struggle,
and the referendum of 1993. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know the Past**:

- `first-steps/` — What History Is; From Evidence to a
  Written History; Find History in Your Own Locality; Dating
  the Past; Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters; Who
  Studies Prehistory.

**Level 2 — Ancient Eritrea and the Peoples to 1500**:

- `africa-to-1500/` — From Stone Tools to Farming; Eritrea:
  Early Migrations and Peoples; Ancient Eritrea: Adulis and
  the Red Sea Trade; Ancient Eritrea: Christianity and Geez
  Writing; The Beja Kingdoms and Dahlak; Eritrea: The Bahri
  Negestat; Eritrea: Nara and Kunama Societies; Eritrea:
  Medieval Highland Villages; North Africa: Fatimids,
  Mamluks and the Funj.

**Level 3 — The Wider World's Old Orders**:

- `ancient-world/` — How Slave Society Worked; Ancient
  Civilisations: India's Order and Gifts; Medieval World:
  The Dark Age and Feudalism; Medieval World: The Ottomans.

**Level 4 — Coast Powers, Invaders and Resistance, 1500–1900**:

- `africa-after-1500/` — Eritrea: Ottoman Coast and the
  Naibs; Eritrea: Portuguese-Turkish Rivalry and the Gragn
  Wars; Eritrea: Turkish Expansion and Bahri Negassi Yeshaq;
  Eritrea: Egyptian Expansion and Resistance; Eritrea:
  Abyssinian Expansion and Highland Resistance; Eritrea:
  Beni Amir Federation and the Funj; Eritrea: Samhar, Saho,
  Afar and the Naibs.

**Level 5 — Colony, Federation and Liberation, 1890–1993**:

- `africa-after-1500/` — Colonisation: The Road to the
  Scramble; Eritrea: Italian Expansion and Resistance;
  Eritrea: Italian Colonial Administration; Colonisation:
  Direct and Indirect Rule; Colonisation: Its Effects on
  Africa; Eritrea: British Military Administration; Eritrea:
  Political Parties and the UN Question; Eritrea: Federation
  with Ethiopia and Its End; Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the
  ELF; Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the EPLF and Liberation;
  Eritrea: Referendum and Independence 1993.
