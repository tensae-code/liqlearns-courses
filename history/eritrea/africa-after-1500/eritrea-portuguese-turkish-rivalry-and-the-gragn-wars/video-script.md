# Video script — Eritrea: Portuguese-Turkish Rivalry and the Gragn Wars

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Portuguese and Turks fought over the Red Sea trade while Imam Ahmad Gragn's wars tore through the highlands, weakening the old order the Bahri Negestat held together.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Two empires want the same sea. Portuguese and Turks fight over the Red Sea trade — its ports, its customs, its routes to India — while, inland, Imam Ahmad Gragn's wars tear through the highlands. The old order the Bahri Negestat held together is weakened from both sides at once: empire rivalry on the water, holy war in the mountains.

Remember it this way: Portuguese and Turks fought over the Red Sea trade while Imam Ahmad Gragn's wars tore through the highlands, weakening the old order the Bahri Negestat held together.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
