# Eritrea: Portuguese-Turkish Rivalry and the Gragn Wars

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Two empires want the same sea. Portuguese and Turks fight over the Red Sea trade — its ports, its customs, its routes to India — while, inland, Imam Ahmad Gragn's wars tear through the highlands. The old order the Bahri Negestat held together is weakened from both sides at once: empire rivalry on the water, holy war in the mountains.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: When two empires fight over your sea, the war at your door is only half yours — Gragn's wars were the other half.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Portuguese and Turks fought over the Red Sea trade while Imam Ahmad Gragn's wars tore through the highlands, weakening the old order the Bahri Negestat held together. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
