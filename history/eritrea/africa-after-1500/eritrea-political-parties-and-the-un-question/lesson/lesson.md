# Eritrea: Political Parties and the UN Question

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 9.3, the party years

Freed from Italy, Eritreans organise. The country's first political leaders step forward — Ibrahim Sultan, Woldeab Woldemariam, Ras Tesemma Asberom — and organise parties to demand Eritrea's own future. The question of the 1940s is asked in party offices and newspapers: who decides what Eritrea becomes?

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Three names to hold: Woldeab Woldemariam, Ibrahim Sultan, Ras Tesemma Asberom — the party years begin with them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 10.3, the party leaders

The Grade 5 book names the camps. Woldeab Woldemariam, Ibrahim Sultan and Ras Tesemma Asberom lead the independence parties of the 1940s against the unionists who want Eritrea joined to Ethiopia. The Four Powers cannot agree on Eritrea's fate — so the United Nations takes up the case. A small country's future becomes a world question.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 5.5, Political Movements During the British Period

The Grade 8 book starts the story a step earlier: Mahber Fiqri Hager, founded in 1941, seeds Eritrea's first parties. The Muslim League and the Liberal Progressive Party demand independence; Ethiopia builds the Unionist Party to pull the other way. And the Four Powers, deadlocked, hand Eritrea's future to the United Nations.
