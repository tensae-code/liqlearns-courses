# Video script — Eritrea: Ottoman Coast and the Naibs

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From 1557 the Turks held Eritrea's Red Sea coast for some 300 years, ruling through the Naib of the Belau family — who judged disputes, settled grazing rights and shared the port taxes — while the highlands stayed out of their grip.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

From 1557 the Turks hold the Red Sea coast for some 300 years — an astonishing span — but they hold it indirectly, ruling through the Naib family. The highlands stay out of their grip the whole time: an empire of the shore, ruling a coastline it could tax but a plateau it could not take.

The Grade 5 book repeats the measure of it: the Turks seize the coast in 1557 and hold it some 300 years through the Naibs, never mastering the highlands. Three centuries of foreign rule over the ports — and three centuries of highland independence running beside it.

The Grade 6 book shows how the arrangement actually worked. The Turks rule the coast through the Naib of the Belau family, who judges disputes, settles grazing rights and shares the port taxes with the Bahri Negestat. Coast and highlands, foreigner and local lord, divide the taxes and the judging between them — government by bargain.

Remember it this way: From 1557 the Turks held Eritrea's Red Sea coast for some 300 years, ruling through the Naib of the Belau family — who judged disputes, settled grazing rights and shared the port taxes — while the highlands stayed out of their grip.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
