# Eritrea: British Military Administration

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 10.3, British rule 1941–1952

In 1941 Britain defeats Italy, and Eritrea changes masters without gaining freedom. For eleven years Britain rules it as an occupied territory, while Eritreans — no longer under Italian law — begin to organise for their own future. The party years grow in the space between one empire's fall and the world's decision.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: An occupation that bans politics still teaches politics: Eritreans organised their first parties under the ban.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 6.2, British Colonial Administration (Occupation)

The Grade 8 book is blunter about the occupation. Britain wins at Keren in March 1941 and rules through a Military Administration that bans politics, protects Italian property, and strips factories out of the country — working, the book says, to keep Eritrea weak and divided, so that its west can be joined to Sudan. Eritrea's future is being bargained away while Eritreans are forbidden to bargain.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Britain defeated Italy in 1941 — at Keren in March — and ruled Eritrea as an occupied territory for eleven years: politics banned, Italian property protected, factories stripped, and plans made to join Eritrea's west to Sudan. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
