# Eritrea: Italian Expansion and Resistance

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 9.3, Italian rule

Italy arrives by purchase before it arrives by army: it buys Assab in 1869 and takes Massawa. In 1890 it names the colony Eritrea. But the land seizures meet resistance at once — Dejazmach Bahta Hagos rises against them in 1894, the first great name in a resistance that never entirely stops.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Dogali matters: Italy's road to a colony was not a walk — it was stopped in blood in 1887 and only reopened by famine and a king's death.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 10.3, Italian rule 1882–1941

The Grade 5 book widens the frame: Italy lands at Assab and Massawa and proclaims the colony of Eritrea in 1890, meeting resisters like Bahta Hagos — while Eritrean askari, colonial soldiers, are shipped to fight Italy's other wars. The colony takes Eritrea's land and Eritrea's young men at the same time.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 5.4, Italian Expansion and Eritrean Resistance

The Grade 8 book tells the taking step by step. Sapeto buys Assab in 1869; Italy takes Massawa in 1885; Ras Alula bloodies the invaders at Dogali in 1887. Then famine and the death of Yohannes open the road: the Treaty of Wuchale, and on 1 January 1890 the colony of Eritrea is proclaimed. A colony founded by purchase, checked by battle, and completed by hunger.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Italy bought Assab in 1869, took Massawa in 1885, was bloodied by Ras Alula at Dogali in 1887, then rode famine and Yohannes's death to the Treaty of Wuchale and proclaimed the colony of Eritrea on 1 January 1890 — and Eritreans like Dejazmach Bahta Hagos rose against the land seizures in 1894. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
