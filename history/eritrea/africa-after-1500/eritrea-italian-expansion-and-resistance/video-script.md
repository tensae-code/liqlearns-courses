# Video script — Eritrea: Italian Expansion and Resistance

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Italy bought Assab in 1869, took Massawa in 1885, was bloodied by Ras Alula at Dogali in 1887, then rode famine and Yohannes's death to the Treaty of Wuchale and proclaimed the colony of Eritrea on 1 January 1890 — and Eritreans like Dejazmach Bahta Hagos rose against the land seizures in 1894.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Italy arrives by purchase before it arrives by army: it buys Assab in 1869 and takes Massawa. In 1890 it names the colony Eritrea. But the land seizures meet resistance at once — Dejazmach Bahta Hagos rises against them in 1894, the first great name in a resistance that never entirely stops.

The Grade 5 book widens the frame: Italy lands at Assab and Massawa and proclaims the colony of Eritrea in 1890, meeting resisters like Bahta Hagos — while Eritrean askari, colonial soldiers, are shipped to fight Italy's other wars. The colony takes Eritrea's land and Eritrea's young men at the same time.

The Grade 8 book tells the taking step by step. Sapeto buys Assab in 1869; Italy takes Massawa in 1885; Ras Alula bloodies the invaders at Dogali in 1887. Then famine and the death of Yohannes open the road: the Treaty of Wuchale, and on 1 January 1890 the colony of Eritrea is proclaimed. A colony founded by purchase, checked by battle, and completed by hunger.

Remember it this way: Italy bought Assab in 1869, took Massawa in 1885, was bloodied by Ras Alula at Dogali in 1887, then rode famine and Yohannes's death to the Treaty of Wuchale and proclaimed the colony of Eritrea on 1 January 1890 — and Eritreans like Dejazmach Bahta Hagos rose against the land seizures in 1894.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
