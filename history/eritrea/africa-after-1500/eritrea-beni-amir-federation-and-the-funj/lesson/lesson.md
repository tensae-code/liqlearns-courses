# Eritrea: Beni Amir Federation and the Funj

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

A marriage makes a federation, and a murder unmakes a family. The Funj agent Ali Abu Gassim marries into the Belaw royal line — and is killed by them. His son, born after his death, returns with an army, defeats the Belaw, and unites some 21 tribes under the Diglal. The federation he builds is ordered in three castes: nabtab rulers, serfs and slaves — unity and hierarchy founded in the same act.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A federation built by one man's revenge still has to feed 21 tribes the next morning — the Diglal's order outlasted the quarrel that made it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Funj agent Ali Abu Gassim married into the Belaw royal line and was killed by them; his posthumous son returned with an army, defeated the Belaw and united some 21 tribes under the Diglal, in a society of nabtab rulers, serfs and slaves in three castes. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
