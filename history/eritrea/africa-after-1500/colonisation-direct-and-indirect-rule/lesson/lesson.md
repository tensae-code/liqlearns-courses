# Colonisation: Direct and Indirect Rule

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Italy's rule wears two faces. In the highlands it rules through appointed chiefs — mislene — salaried and closely controlled, direct rule in local dress. In the lowlands it keeps tribal leaders in place but strips them of power. Either way the decisions are Italy's: direct control wearing a traditional face, and a lesson in how to read any colonial government — follow the salary and the power of dismissal.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Ask of any chief under colonial rule: who pays his salary, and who can dismiss him? The answers tell you who really rules.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Italy ruled the highlands through salaried, closely controlled appointed chiefs (mislene) and the lowlands through tribal leaders kept in place but stripped of power — direct control wearing a traditional face. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
