# Eritrea: Italian Colonial Administration

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 10.3, life under Italy

Life under Italy is a system, not a series of accidents: separate schools for Eritrean and Italian children, Fascist propaganda posters on the walls, forced labour on the roads and on the great Massawa–Asmara cableway. Every part of the colony is arranged so that wealth and power flow to the coloniser.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A school cap is a policy you can read: schooling beyond grade 4 was banned because an educated colony is harder to rule.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 6.1, Italian colonial administration and the Racial Laws

The administration's own paperwork shows the design. Commissariati and municipalities run a colony whose aims shift — settler farms, then markets, then a springboard for the invasion of Ethiopia. And in 1937 the Racial Laws make the design explicit: Eritreans are barred from Italian streets and bars, and from schooling beyond grade 4.
