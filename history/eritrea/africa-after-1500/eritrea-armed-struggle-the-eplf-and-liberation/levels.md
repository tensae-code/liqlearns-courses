# Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the EPLF and Liberation — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 9.4, the National Struggle
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The youngest book tells the whole war in one sweep: the ELF begins the armed struggle under Hamid Idris Awate in 1961; the EPLF carries it through thirty years of war; and on 24 May 1991 Eritrea is liberated — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 10.4, the Armed Struggle 1961–1991
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Grade 5 book walks the war's great turns: Hamid Idris Awate opens the armed struggle in 1961; the fronts survive civil war between themselves, the huge offensives of 1977–78, the strategic retreat, and the Red Star campaign; then the tide turns — victory at Afabet in 1988, Massawa in 1990, and Asmara liberated on 24 May 1991. — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 7.2, The Eritrean People's Liberation Front
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The Grade 8 book follows the EPLF itself: three splinter groups unite as the EPLF by 1973; the front survives a civil war with the ELF, adopts the National Democratic Program as its political compass, takes the big towns in 1977, endures the Soviet-backed strategic retreat to the Sahil mountains — and fights through, year by year, to liberation — shown in the learner's own words and one real example.
