# Video script — Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the EPLF and Liberation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The EPLF, united from three splinter groups by 1973, survived civil war with the ELF, adopted the National Democratic Program, took the big towns in 1977, endured the Soviet-backed strategic retreat to Sahil and the Red Star campaign, won at Afabet in 1988 and Massawa in 1990, and liberated Asmara on 24 May 1991.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

The youngest book tells the whole war in one sweep: the ELF begins the armed struggle under Hamid Idris Awate in 1961; the EPLF carries it through thirty years of war; and on 24 May 1991 Eritrea is liberated. Thirty years from one man's first stand to the fall of Asmara.

The Grade 5 book walks the war's great turns: Hamid Idris Awate opens the armed struggle in 1961; the fronts survive civil war between themselves, the huge offensives of 1977–78, the strategic retreat, and the Red Star campaign; then the tide turns — victory at Afabet in 1988, Massawa in 1990, and Asmara liberated on 24 May 1991.

The Grade 8 book follows the EPLF itself: three splinter groups unite as the EPLF by 1973; the front survives a civil war with the ELF, adopts the National Democratic Program as its political compass, takes the big towns in 1977, endures the Soviet-backed strategic retreat to the Sahil mountains — and fights through, year by year, to liberation. Organisation, the book argues, is what turned survival into victory.

Remember it this way: The EPLF, united from three splinter groups by 1973, survived civil war with the ELF, adopted the National Democratic Program, took the big towns in 1977, endured the Soviet-backed strategic retreat to Sahil and the Red Star campaign, won at Afabet in 1988 and Massawa in 1990, and liberated Asmara on 24 May 1991.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
