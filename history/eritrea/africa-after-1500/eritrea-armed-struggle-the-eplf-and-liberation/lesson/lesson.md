# Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the EPLF and Liberation

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 9.4, the National Struggle

The youngest book tells the whole war in one sweep: the ELF begins the armed struggle under Hamid Idris Awate in 1961; the EPLF carries it through thirty years of war; and on 24 May 1991 Eritrea is liberated. Thirty years from one man's first stand to the fall of Asmara.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: The strategic retreat was not a defeat: the front gave up the towns to keep the army, and came back to win Afabet, Massawa and Asmara.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 10.4, the Armed Struggle 1961–1991

The Grade 5 book walks the war's great turns: Hamid Idris Awate opens the armed struggle in 1961; the fronts survive civil war between themselves, the huge offensives of 1977–78, the strategic retreat, and the Red Star campaign; then the tide turns — victory at Afabet in 1988, Massawa in 1990, and Asmara liberated on 24 May 1991.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 7.2, The Eritrean People's Liberation Front

The Grade 8 book follows the EPLF itself: three splinter groups unite as the EPLF by 1973; the front survives a civil war with the ELF, adopts the National Democratic Program as its political compass, takes the big towns in 1977, endures the Soviet-backed strategic retreat to the Sahil mountains — and fights through, year by year, to liberation. Organisation, the book argues, is what turned survival into victory.
