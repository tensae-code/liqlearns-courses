# Lesson plan — Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the EPLF and Liberation

**Course:** History — Eritrea · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the EPLF and Liberation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Colony, Federation and Liberation, 1890–1993 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The EPLF, united from three splinter groups by 1973, survived civil war with the ELF, adopted the National Democratic Program, took the big towns in 1977, endured the Soviet-backed strategic retreat to Sahil and the Red Star campaign, won at Afabet in 1988 and Massawa in 1990, and liberated Asmara on 24 May 1991.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 9.4, the National Struggle** — The youngest book tells the whole war in one sweep: the ELF begins the armed struggle under Hamid Idris Awate in 1961; the EPLF carries it through thirty years of war; and on 24 May 1991 Eritrea is liberated.
2. **Chapter 10.4, the Armed Struggle 1961–1991** — The Grade 5 book walks the war's great turns: Hamid Idris Awate opens the armed struggle in 1961; the fronts survive civil war between themselves, the huge offensives of 1977–78, the strategic retreat, and the Red Star campaign; then the tide turns — victory at Afabet in 1988, Massawa in 1990, and Asmara liberated on 24 May 1991..
3. **Unit 7.2, The Eritrean People's Liberation Front** — The Grade 8 book follows the EPLF itself: three splinter groups unite as the EPLF by 1973; the front survives a civil war with the ELF, adopts the National Democratic Program as its political compass, takes the big towns in 1977, endures the Soviet-backed strategic retreat to the Sahil mountains — and fights through, year by year, to liberation.
