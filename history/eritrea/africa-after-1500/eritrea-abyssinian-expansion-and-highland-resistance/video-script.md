# Video script — Eritrea: Abyssinian Expansion and Highland Resistance

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Abyssinian rulers raided and taxed the highlands in the 19th century — Ras Michael Sehul even deposed a Bahri Negassi in 1770 — and Eritreans remember both the exactions, the famine years of 1888–1892, and resisters like the Saho at Rub-Rubia and Woldemichael Solomon of Hazzega.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

From the south come raids instead of annexation. Abyssinian rulers raid and tax the highlands through the 19th century, and Eritreans remember both the exactions — the cattle and grain taken year after year — and the famine years of 1888–1892 that followed, when hunger did what armies had not.

The older book names the resistance. Tigrean and Abyssinian rulers raid Medri Bahri for tribute, plunder and coastal arms — Ras Michael Sehul even deposes a Bahri Negassi in 1770, treating the sea-lord as his own official. But the Saho beat Ras Alula's men at Rub-Rubia, and Woldemichael Solomon of Hazzega leads the highland resistance: raided, taxed, and never simply obedient.

Remember it this way: Abyssinian rulers raided and taxed the highlands in the 19th century — Ras Michael Sehul even deposed a Bahri Negassi in 1770 — and Eritreans remember both the exactions, the famine years of 1888–1892, and resisters like the Saho at Rub-Rubia and Woldemichael Solomon of Hazzega.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
