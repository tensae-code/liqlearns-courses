# Colonisation: Its Effects on Africa

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

What did Italian rule leave behind? Roads, railways and factories — real things, built for military and settler needs. Industrialisation serves the coloniser. And on the other side of the ledger, education is deliberately capped, designed to produce obedient, low-skilled subjects. The colony is developed and held down by the same hands, on purpose.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A railway is not a gift or a theft by itself: ask where it runs — from mine to port, or from town to town? Italy's ran for the army and the settlers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Italian rule built roads, railways and factories for military and settler needs, while education was deliberately capped to produce obedient, low-skilled subjects — industrialisation that served the coloniser, not the colony. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
