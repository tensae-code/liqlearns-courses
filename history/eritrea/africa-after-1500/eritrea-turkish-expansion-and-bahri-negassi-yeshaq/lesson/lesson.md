# Eritrea: Turkish Expansion and Bahri Negassi Yeshaq

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Ozdemir Pasha takes Massawa in 1557 and pushes for the highlands — and meets the Bahri Negassi Yeshaq. Yeshaq first bars the Turkish advance; then, calculating that Abyssinia is the greater threat, he allies with the Turks against Abyssinia's King Minas. In 1578 he is killed fighting Minas's successor, King Sertse-Dengel. After his death, Turkish rule shrinks back to the coast and the Naibs: the highlands were defended, at the price of their defender.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Yeshaq changed sides once, against his bigger enemy. Judge him by the map he was given, not by the map we wish he had.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ozdemir Pasha took Massawa in 1557 and pushed for the highlands; Bahri Negassi Yeshaq first barred him, then allied with the Turks against Abyssinia's King Minas, and was killed fighting King Sertse-Dengel in 1578 — after which Turkish rule shrank back to the coast and the Naibs. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
