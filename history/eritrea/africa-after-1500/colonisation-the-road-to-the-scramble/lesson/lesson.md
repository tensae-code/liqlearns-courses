# Colonisation: The Road to the Scramble

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

In 1869 the Suez Canal opens, and the Red Sea becomes the short road between Europe and Asia — a prize every empire wants a shore on. Britain sits in Aden and Egypt; France takes Djibouti. And Britain, preferring a weak Italy to a strong France on this coast, encourages Italy forward. Eritrea's coloniser is chosen, in part, in London.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A canal is a shortcut that moves empires: after 1869 every Red Sea shore became a coaling station somebody wanted.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Suez Canal's opening in 1869 made the Red Sea a prize: Britain in Aden and Egypt, France at Djibouti — and Britain encouraged Italy forward to keep France out. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
