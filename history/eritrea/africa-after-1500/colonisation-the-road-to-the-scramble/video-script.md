# Video script — Colonisation: The Road to the Scramble

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Suez Canal's opening in 1869 made the Red Sea a prize: Britain in Aden and Egypt, France at Djibouti — and Britain encouraged Italy forward to keep France out.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

In 1869 the Suez Canal opens, and the Red Sea becomes the short road between Europe and Asia — a prize every empire wants a shore on. Britain sits in Aden and Egypt; France takes Djibouti. And Britain, preferring a weak Italy to a strong France on this coast, encourages Italy forward. Eritrea's coloniser is chosen, in part, in London.

Remember it this way: The Suez Canal's opening in 1869 made the Red Sea a prize: Britain in Aden and Egypt, France at Djibouti — and Britain encouraged Italy forward to keep France out.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
