# Video script — Eritrea: Referendum and Independence 1993

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In April 1993, two years after liberation, Eritreans voted under international observation and 99.8 percent chose independence — formally declared on 24 May 1993, the day the flag of a sovereign Eritrea rose.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Liberation is confirmed at the ballot box. In April 1993 Eritreans vote under international observation, and 99.8 percent choose independence. On 24 May 1993 — exactly two years after liberation day — independence is declared, and Eritrea takes its place among the nations of the world.

The Grade 5 book keeps the two dates side by side: in April 1993 Eritreans vote 99.8 percent for independence, and on 24 May 1993 the flag of a sovereign Eritrea rises. The war won the country; the vote confirmed it; the flag announced it.

The Grade 8 book names the witnesses: the referendum of 23–25 April 1993 is held under UN, OAU and Arab League observation, two years after actual liberation on 24 May 1991. Over 99.8 percent choose independence, formally declared on 24 May 1993. No one, the observers certify, can say the choice was not Eritrea's own.

Remember it this way: In April 1993, two years after liberation, Eritreans voted under international observation and 99.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
