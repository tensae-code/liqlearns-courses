# Lesson plan — Eritrea: Referendum and Independence 1993

**Course:** History — Eritrea · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Eritrea: Referendum and Independence 1993
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Colony, Federation and Liberation, 1890–1993 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In April 1993, two years after liberation, Eritreans voted under international observation and 99.8 percent chose independence — formally declared on 24 May 1993, the day the flag of a sovereign Eritrea rose.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — Colony, Federation and Liberation, 1890–1993 (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 9.4, the referendum** — Liberation is confirmed at the ballot box.
2. **Chapter 10.4, referendum and independence** — The Grade 5 book keeps the two dates side by side: in April 1993 Eritreans vote 99.8 percent for independence, and on 24 May 1993 the flag of a sovereign Eritrea rises.
3. **Unit 8.1, The Referendum, 23–25 April 1993** — The Grade 8 book names the witnesses: the referendum of 23–25 April 1993 is held under UN, OAU and Arab League observation, two years after actual liberation on 24 May 1991.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the story in the names and dates → go back to the one turn: who wanted what, what changed, and what came of it. Name those three aloud, in order.
- Go back to the one thing: In April 1993, two years after liberation, Eritreans voted under international observation and 99.8 percent chose independence — formally declared on 24 May 1993, the day the flag of a sovereign Eritrea rose. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
