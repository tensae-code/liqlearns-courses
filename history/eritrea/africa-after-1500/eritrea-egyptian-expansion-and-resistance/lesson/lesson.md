# Eritrea: Egyptian Expansion and Resistance

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 10.3, the Egyptian advance

Egypt pushes south and east from the Sudan: Kassala in 1840, Massawa in 1846, Keren in 1874 — the Egyptian frontier climbing toward the highlands town by town. Until, in 1875–76, its highland invasion breaks. The advance that took thirty years to build is stopped in two campaigning seasons.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Count the rifles: 700 captured rifles meant 700 Eritrean fighters newly armed — a defeat that armed the resistance.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 5.2, Egyptian Expansion and Eritrean Resistance

The Grade 8 book tells the breaking in full. Muhammad Ali's Egypt swallows the Sudan, Kassala, the Barka and Keren under Munzinger — until Khedive Ismail's highland drive is broken at Gundet and Gura in 1875–76. And Woldemichael Solomon follows the retreating Egyptians and captures 700 rifles: the invasion leaves Eritrea armed with its enemy's own weapons.
