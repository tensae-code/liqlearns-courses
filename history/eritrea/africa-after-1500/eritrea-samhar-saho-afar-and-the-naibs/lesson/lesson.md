# Eritrea: Samhar, Saho, Afar and the Naibs

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The eastern lowlands govern themselves differently. The Saho and Afar tribes of Samhar and Dankalia have no master-serf relations: they rule themselves through elected councils of elders, chosen, not born. Only later does Samhar pass under the Naib family of Belau origin — the coast's tax-men reaching into a land that had governed itself by election.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: No masters does not mean no government: an elected council of elders is a government, chosen the lowlands' own way.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Saho and Afar of the eastern lowlands, with no master-serf relations, governed themselves through elected councils of elders — until Samhar passed under the Naib family of Belau origin. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
