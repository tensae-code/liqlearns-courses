# Video script — Dating the Past

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Events are placed in time by dates and durations — past, present and future, hours to millennia, decades, centuries and timelines — so the past can be told in its true order.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Before a story can be told in order, time itself must be measured. Past, present and future are separated, then time is counted in hours, days, weeks, months and years, with calendars to hold the count — and two big measures on top: the century, 100 years, and the millennium, 1,000 years.

The older book puts dates to work on Eritrea's own story: the liberation struggle ran 1961–1991, and Eritrea was liberated on 24 May 1991. Decades, centuries and millennia are laid out on hand-drawn timelines, so a learner can place an event, measure how long it lasted, and see what came before and after it.

Remember it this way: Events are placed in time by dates and durations — past, present and future, hours to millennia, decades, centuries and timelines — so the past can be told in its true order.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
