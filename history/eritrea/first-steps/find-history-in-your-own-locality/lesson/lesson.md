# Find History in Your Own Locality

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 1.2, historical places as sources

History begins at walking distance. Old churches, mosques and buildings in the learner's own town are read as evidence of who lived there and what happened — and Keren is the worked example, its old buildings telling who built the town and how it grew. A building is a document you can touch.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Start with one old building near you. Ask who built it, when, and what it has seen — that asking is history work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 6.3, the sites zone by zone

The next book widens the walk to the whole country: every zone of Eritrea holds its own ancient sites — ruins, inscriptions and old buildings in the Northern Red Sea, Gash-Barka, Debub and the rest. So the learner's own region is a history source to be visited and recorded, not just read about.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History is not only in faraway books: the old churches, mosques, ruins and buildings of your own town and zone are evidence you can visit, read and record. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
