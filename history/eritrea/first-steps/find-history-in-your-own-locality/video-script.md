# Video script — Find History in Your Own Locality

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
History is not only in faraway books: the old churches, mosques, ruins and buildings of your own town and zone are evidence you can visit, read and record.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

History begins at walking distance. Old churches, mosques and buildings in the learner's own town are read as evidence of who lived there and what happened — and Keren is the worked example, its old buildings telling who built the town and how it grew. A building is a document you can touch.

The next book widens the walk to the whole country: every zone of Eritrea holds its own ancient sites — ruins, inscriptions and old buildings in the Northern Red Sea, Gash-Barka, Debub and the rest. So the learner's own region is a history source to be visited and recorded, not just read about.

Remember it this way: History is not only in faraway books: the old churches, mosques, ruins and buildings of your own town and zone are evidence you can visit, read and record.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
