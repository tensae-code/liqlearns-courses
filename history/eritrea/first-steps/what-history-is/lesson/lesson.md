# What History Is

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The book opens with a definition a learner can test: history is the record of the past, and it is built only from facts. A fact is not a guess or a fine story — it is something a source can stand behind. So the historian's first habit is simple: for everything claimed about the past, ask what source tells us exactly what happened.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: When someone tells you a story about long ago, ask the historian's question: how do we know? A fact needs a source behind it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: History is the record of the past built only from facts, and facts come from sources that tell us exactly what happened. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
