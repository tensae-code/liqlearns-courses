# Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 6.3, Historical Sites in Eritrea

Heritage is introduced standing in front of real stones: Kohaito's stelae, Metera's ruins, and the sites of the Northern Red Sea, Gash-Barka and Debub — Eritrea's inherited evidence, named site by site across all six zones. What the past left behind is not decoration; it is proof, and it belongs to the learners.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Heritage is not only stone. A wedding custom your grandparents kept is heritage too — ask them to show you how it was done.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 7.1, What is Heritage?

The older book gives the word its full size: heritage is what is passed from the past to the present. Cultural heritage lives in weddings, dress and custom; natural heritage stands beside it in the land itself. Both are inherited, both can be lost, and both are the learner's to keep.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Heritage is what is passed from the past to the present — Eritrea's ancient sites zone by zone, and cultural heritage in weddings, dress and custom, kept beside natural heritage. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
