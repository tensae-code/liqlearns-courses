# Video script — Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Heritage is what is passed from the past to the present — Eritrea's ancient sites zone by zone, and cultural heritage in weddings, dress and custom, kept beside natural heritage.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Heritage is introduced standing in front of real stones: Kohaito's stelae, Metera's ruins, and the sites of the Northern Red Sea, Gash-Barka and Debub — Eritrea's inherited evidence, named site by site across all six zones. What the past left behind is not decoration; it is proof, and it belongs to the learners.

The older book gives the word its full size: heritage is what is passed from the past to the present. Cultural heritage lives in weddings, dress and custom; natural heritage stands beside it in the land itself. Both are inherited, both can be lost, and both are the learner's to keep.

Remember it this way: Heritage is what is passed from the past to the present — Eritrea's ancient sites zone by zone, and cultural heritage in weddings, dress and custom, kept beside natural heritage.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
