# Video script — Who Studies Prehistory

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The people who dig the past are archaeologists: the National Museum of Eritrea in Asmara keeps the country's finds, and archaeology works with its own toolkit of words — objects, features, relics and ecofacts.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

In Asmara stands the National Museum of Eritrea, keeping the country's finds — the objects dug from Eritrea's ground, gathered where learners can see them. Around the museum the chapter builds the toolkit of those who dig the past: its glossary defines archaeology itself, and archaeological objects, features, relics and ecofacts — the words a learner needs before asking who studies prehistory and how.

Remember it this way: The people who dig the past are archaeologists: the National Museum of Eritrea in Asmara keeps the country's finds, and archaeology works with its own toolkit of words — objects, features, relics and ecofacts.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
