# Medieval World: The Ottomans

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The empire arrives from the north. The Ottoman Empire, having taken Egypt, Sudan and Arabia, lands on the Eritrean coast in 1517 — and by 1557 holds Massawa and Hirghigo, the ports where the highlands' goods meet the sea. The Turks tax the port trade and hold the shore; the mountains behind it are another matter.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: An empire of ports lives on taxes, not territory: Massawa's customs house was worth more to the Ottomans than the highlands behind it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Ottoman Empire, having taken Egypt, Sudan and Arabia, landed on the Eritrean coast in 1517 and by 1557 held Massawa and Hirghigo, taxing the port trade. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
