# How Slave Society Worked

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The ladder of societies climbs to its hardest step. Once farming yields a surplus and private property appears, people split into classes for the first time. Debtors who cannot pay and prisoners taken in war become slaves — people with no rights, owned outright by slave-owners — while free farmers and craftsmen work beside them. Inequality is not natural in this telling; it is made, by surplus and by force.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Slavery began, in this telling, with two doors: debt you could not pay, and war you did not survive free.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: When farming produced a surplus and private property appeared, people split into classes for the first time: debtors and prisoners of war became slaves with no rights, owned by slave-owners beside free farmers and craftsmen. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
