# Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

Feudalism grows out of slavery's failure. Slaves with no stake in their work are unproductive, so they are given plots of land — and become serfs: bound to a lord's estate, owing labour and taxes, and sold with the land itself when the estate changes hands. It is one road by which feudalism appears: not freedom, but a new kind of bondage, tied to soil instead of a master.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A slave is owned as a person; a serf is bound to the land. The difference is small, and it changed the whole countryside.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Feudalism appears when unproductive slaves are given plots and become serfs — bound to a lord's estate, owing labour and taxes, and sold with the land itself. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
