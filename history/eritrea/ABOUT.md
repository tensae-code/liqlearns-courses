# About the History — Eritrea course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Eritrea's own
Ministry of Education Social Studies books. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Eritrea inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

The course as built has **five levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know the Past.** What
   history is, the sources and how they are weighed, history
   found in your own locality, dating the past, heritage, and
   the National Museum and archaeology.
2. **Level 2 — Ancient Eritrea and the Peoples to 1500.**
   Stone tools to farming, the migrations that made Eritrea's
   peoples, Adulis and the Red Sea trade, Christianity and
   Geez writing, the Beja kingdoms and Dahlak, the Bahri
   Negestat, the Nara and Kunama, the medieval highland
   villages, and the Funj reaching in from Sennar.
3. **Level 3 — The Wider World's Old Orders.** The Grade 7
   book's ladder of societies — slave society, India's caste
   order, feudalism — with the Ottoman arrival that Eritrea's
   own story meets next.
4. **Level 4 — Coast Powers, Invaders and Resistance,
   1500–1900.** Three hundred years of Turkish rule through
   the Naibs, the Portuguese-Turkish rivalry and Gragn's
   wars, Yeshaq's stand, Egypt's advance broken at Gundet and
   Gura, Abyssinian raids and highland resistance, the Beni
   Amir federation, and the self-governing Saho and Afar.
5. **Level 5 — Colony, Federation and Liberation,
   1890–1993.** The scramble, Italy's taking and rule of the
   colony, British occupation, the party years and the UN
   question, the federation and its dismantling, the ELF and
   the EPLF, liberation on 24 May 1991, and the referendum
   and independence of 1993.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Eritrea's story:* "Start with the tools: history is built
  from sources — elders' words, written documents, dug-up
  tools — weighed and dated, and the oldest evidence stands
  in your own town and in the National Museum. Then the land
  fills: wave after wave of peoples, stone-tool hunters
  becoming farmers, and ports — Adulis above all — growing
  rich on the Red Sea trade, until Christianity and Islam
  arrive by sea and the Bahri Negassi rules the highlands
  from Debarwa. The wider world climbs its ladder beside it:
  slave, caste, feudal. Then the coast is taken and held —
  Turks through the Naibs for 300 years, Egyptians broken at
  Gundet and Gura, Abyssinian raiders resisted — until
  Suez makes the Red Sea a prize and Italy takes the colony
  in 1890. Britain follows, then the parties, a federation
  dismantled piece by piece, and thirty years of armed
  struggle — Awate's first shot in 1961, Afabet, Massawa,
  Asmara free on 24 May 1991 — closing with the vote of
  April 1993: 99.8 percent for independence."

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **Grade 4 (Book 1):** a Tigrinya-medium book that teaches
  history twice over — first the tools (sources, family
  trees, calendars, centuries) and then, in its last chapter,
  the whole Eritrean story from stone tools to the 1993
  referendum, told with photographs of the real places and
  people.
- **Grade 5 (Book 2):** history anchored in place — the Red
  Sea's ancient trade, the archaeological sites of all six
  zones, and the National Museum — before the final chapter
  walks the whole national story period by period: ancient,
  medieval, modern, armed struggle.
- **Grade 6 (Book 3):** an integrated book that starts from
  method — sources first, then measuring time — and only then
  tells Eritrea's own story in strict order: migrations,
  ancient trading towns, Beja kingdoms, the Bahri Negestat,
  each unit closing with timeline and map-skills work.
- **Grade 7 (Book 4):** world history taught as a ladder of
  societies — primitive communal, slave, feudal — and then
  the ladder climbed at home: Eritrea's own traditional
  societies, lowland and highland, reconstructed from
  travellers' accounts that learners must grade as primary or
  secondary sources.
- **Grade 8 (Book 5):** the whole modern national story told
  as one chain of resistance — Turks, Egyptians, Abyssinians,
  Italians, British, federation, armed struggle — every
  invader met by named Eritrean resisters, from Bahri Negassi
  Yeshaq and Woldemichael Solomon to Hamid Idris Awate.
