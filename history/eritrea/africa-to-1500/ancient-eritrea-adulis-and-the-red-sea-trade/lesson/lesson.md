# Ancient Eritrea: Adulis and the Red Sea Trade

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 9.2, Ancient Eritrea

Ancient Eritrea announces itself in stone. The ruins of Adulis, Kohaito and Metera — stelae, temples, dams — prove a trading civilisation stood on this Red Sea coast in ancient times, building in stone and storing water while ships carried its goods across the sea.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Ports grow where ships must stop. Adulis stood on the road between Egypt, Arabia and India — that position was its wealth.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 5.2, Ancient Trade in the Red Sea

The sea trade itself is older still. Egyptian ships sail to the Land of Punt, and Eritrea's ports grow rich on the exchange: leopard skins, ivory, rhino horn and cowries go out, and goods from Arabia and India come in. The coast's wealth was made by ships, not by mines.

## Sub lesson 3 — Chapter 10.1, Ancient History of Eritrea

The Grade 5 book gives the trade its full span: four thousand years of Red Sea history — Punt expeditions, the port of Adulis, the ruins of Kohaito, Matara and Keskesse, stelae, inscriptions and a carved sphinx. Each ruin is a receipt: proof that merchants, sailors and kings did business here for millennia.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 8.1, Eritrean society in ancient times

And the towns behind the ports were real communities: farming and craft towns growing along the trade — Adulis, Kohaito, Matara, Keskesse — city-state communities that Queen Hatshepsut's Egypt already traded with as the Land of Punt. Trade did not just pass Eritrea by; it built towns that fed and supplied the ships.
