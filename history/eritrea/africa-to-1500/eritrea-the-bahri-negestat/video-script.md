# Video script — Eritrea: The Bahri Negestat

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From the 1300s a 'Lord of the Sea', the Bahri Negassi, ruled Medri Bahri from Debarwa with a Council of Elders' approval — until Gragn's wars shook the region and fighting among local chiefs hollowed his authority out from inside.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

In the medieval highlands a sea-lord rules a land of mountains. The Bahri Negassi rules Medri Bahri from Debarwa — the 'Lord of the Sea' whose land looks down to the coast. Then, in 1541, Gragn's wars shake the whole region, and the old highland order is tested by invasion.

The fuller telling starts two centuries earlier: from the 1300s the Bahri Negassi rises over the separate highland communities, ruling from Debarwa — but not alone. He governs with a Council of Elders' approval, while Abyssinian kings try to pull him under their control. His state is a bargain between a lord, his elders and a jealous neighbour.

The decline comes from inside. Districts grow out of groups of villages, each with its own chiefs, and fighting among the local chiefs hollows out the Bahri Negassi's authority. No foreign army ends the Bahri Negestat in this telling — it thins away as its own chiefs stop obeying.

Remember it this way: From the 1300s a 'Lord of the Sea', the Bahri Negassi, ruled Medri Bahri from Debarwa with a Council of Elders' approval — until Gragn's wars shook the region and fighting among local chiefs hollowed his authority out from inside.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
