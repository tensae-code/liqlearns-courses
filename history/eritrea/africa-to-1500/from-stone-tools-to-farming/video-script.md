# Video script — From Stone Tools to Farming

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
People changed step by step — stone tools, cave painting, hunting and gathering, then fire, the bow and arrow, metal tools and domesticated animals — until farming and surplus changed everything.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

The first Eritreans enter the story with stone in their hands. They shape stone tools, paint animals on cave walls, and live by hunting and gathering — taking what the land offers, moving as it moves — before settled peoples arrive. Everything later in Eritrea's story stands on these first tool-makers.

The wider book tells the same transformation as a ladder of inventions. Hunter-gatherers living on roots and fruit are changed step by step: fire, the bow and arrow, metal tools, domesticated animals — until farming and surplus change everything. A surplus can be stored, traded and fought over; society itself is rebuilt around it.

Remember it this way: People changed step by step — stone tools, cave painting, hunting and gathering, then fire, the bow and arrow, metal tools and domesticated animals — until farming and surplus changed everything.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
