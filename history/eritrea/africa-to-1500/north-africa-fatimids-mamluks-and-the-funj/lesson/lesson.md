# North Africa: Fatimids, Mamluks and the Funj

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

From Sennar, far up the Nile, the Funj Kingdom reaches east in the 16th century and pulls the Beja tribes between the Barka and the Sahil under its control. Eritrea's western lowlands are drawn into a Sudanese kingdom's orbit — proof that Eritrea's story was never only about the Red Sea; the Nile side pulled too.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Kingdoms reach as far as their agents can marry, tax and garrison — the Funj reached into Eritrea's lowlands without ever holding its highlands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the 16th century the Funj Kingdom of Sennar expanded and pulled the Beja tribes between the Barka and the Sahil under its control. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
