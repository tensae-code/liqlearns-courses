# Eritrea: Medieval Highland Villages

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

The medieval highland village fed itself and asked little of the world. Around Debarwa, self-sufficient villages grow wheat, barley, teff and sorghum, while churches and monasteries such as Debre Bizen hold land worked by tenants. Grain, faith and tenancy: that triangle organised highland life for centuries.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A village that grows four grains is hard to starve: wheat, barley, teff and sorghum ripen at different times and cover each other's failures.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Medieval highland villages around Debarwa fed themselves — wheat, barley, teff and sorghum — while churches and monasteries such as Debre Bizen held land worked by tenants. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
