# Video script — Eritrea: Early Migrations and Peoples

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Eritrea's peoples arrived in wave after wave — pygmoid first inhabitants, Nilotes, Kushite herders about 2000 BCE, Semites from Arabia about 1000 BCE, and later Beja, Bilen, Saho, Afar and Arab groups — and together they became the peoples of Eritrea.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

Eritrea was not settled once; it was settled again and again. Kushites, Semites and later Beja, Bilen, Saho and the rest move in wave after wave, each bringing their herds, languages and ways — and together, over thousands of years, they become the peoples of Eritrea.

The older book names the waves in order: pygmoid first inhabitants, then Nilotes, then Kushite herders about 2000 BCE, then Semites from Arabia about 1000 BCE bringing camels, horses and new crops — and later Beja, Arab Muslims in Dahlak and Afar groups complete today's peoples. Eritrea's diversity is not an accident; it is the sum of its migrations.

Remember it this way: Eritrea's peoples arrived in wave after wave — pygmoid first inhabitants, Nilotes, Kushite herders about 2000 BCE, Semites from Arabia about 1000 BCE, and later Beja, Bilen, Saho, Afar and Arab groups — and together they became the peoples of Eritrea.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
