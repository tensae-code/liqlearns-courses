# The Beja Kingdoms and Dahlak

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chapter 5.2, the faiths arrive by sea

The faiths arrive by sea. Christianity reaches Eritrea across the Red Sea about 350 CE; and in 615 CE the first Muslims, fleeing persecution, find refuge in Dahlak — planting Islam on the islands and the coast. Eritrea's shore was a refuge before it was a frontier.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Two faiths, one sea road: Christianity climbed to the highlands while Islam held the islands and the coast — the map still shows it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 10.2, Eritrea in the Medieval Age

By the medieval age the pattern has hardened into states: the Dahlak kingdom rules the islands, the Beja hold the western lowlands, and mosques and inscriptions mark a coast turned toward the Islamic world. Islands, lowlands and highlands now face different seas of faith.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 8.2, the Bejas and Islam

The Grade 6 book names the Beja order precisely: the Zenafij Beja push through the Barka Valley at the end of the 7th century and raise five kingdoms — Naqis, Baqlin, Bazin, Jarin and Qata — while Arab Muslims found the state of Dahlak and Islam spreads from the coast inland. Five small kingdoms, one island state: the lowlands governed themselves in their own way.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: From the sea the faiths arrived — Christianity about 350 CE and Islam, whose first believers found refuge in Dahlak in 615 CE — and the land ordered itself into the Dahlak kingdom on the islands and five Beja kingdoms in the western lowlands. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
