# Eritrea: Nara and Kunama Societies

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

In the Gash-Setit lowlands of the south-west, the Nara and the Kunama order life without kings. Kinship is the constitution and ancestor worship the faith: the Kunama know a person through the mother line — your mother's people are your people — and believe in the creator Anna. A society can be held together by family and reverence alone.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Not every society counts family the father's way: among the Kunama, the mother line is the line that names you.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Nara and the Kunama of the Gash-Setit lowlands ordered life by kinship and ancestor worship — the Kunama know a person through the mother line and believe in the creator Anna. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: retell the story aloud — people, place, the turn, what came of it.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story as a row of scenes, with the turn marked.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
