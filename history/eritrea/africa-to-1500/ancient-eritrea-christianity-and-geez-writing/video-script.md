# Video script — Ancient Eritrea: Christianity and Geez Writing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In the mid-4th century the Syrian monks Frumentius and Aedesius brought Christianity to the Aksumite world, the Sabean alphabet yielded to Geez, Aksumite coins changed their symbols, and by the 5th century the Bible was translated into Geez.

Look at the map. We are in Eritrea — its highlands, its Red Sea coast and its lowlands, and, where the story widens, in the lands around it. Watch where the story happens, and who is in it.

The coins tell the turn first. Aksumite coins change their symbols as Christianity comes in during the 4th century through Frumentius — a king's money carrying a new faith into every market. And across the sea, in 610–614 CE, the first Muslims reach Dahlak: two world faiths arrive in Eritrea within three centuries, both by water and by word.

The older book tells how the faith landed: South Arabian and Greek gods give way in the mid-4th century when the Syrian monks Frumentius and Aedesius bring Christianity. Writing turns with it — the Sabean alphabet yields to Geez — and by the 5th century the Bible is translated into Geez, giving the highlands a written sacred language of their own.

Remember it this way: In the mid-4th century the Syrian monks Frumentius and Aedesius brought Christianity to the Aksumite world, the Sabean alphabet yielded to Geez, Aksumite coins changed their symbols, and by the 5th century the Bible was translated into Geez.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
