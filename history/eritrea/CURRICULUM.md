# History — Eritrea course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Eritrea inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Eritrea's books in hand climb in
the country's own teaching order, and the ladder follows it.
The Grade 4 and 5 books (Tigrinya) teach the tools first —
sources, family trees, calendars, centuries — and anchor
history in place: the Red Sea's ancient trade, the
archaeological sites of all six zones, the National Museum.
The Grade 6 book starts again from method (sources first,
then measuring time) and tells Eritrea's own story in strict
order: migrations, ancient trading towns, Beja kingdoms, the
Bahri Negestat. The Grade 7 book climbs a ladder of societies
— primitive communal, slave, feudal — and climbs it at home,
reconstructing Eritrea's traditional societies from
travellers' accounts. The Grade 8 book tells the whole modern
national story as one chain of resistance: Turks, Egyptians,
Abyssinians, Italians, British, federation, armed struggle,
referendum. Five levels, in the books' own order: how we
know the past; ancient Eritrea and its peoples; the wider
world's old orders; the coast powers and the resistance to
them; and the colony-to-independence story whole. The ladder
is a recommended path, never a forced gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Eritrea's own
Ministry of Education Social Studies books — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the combined-book contents
map, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: How We Know the Past** — 6 lessons:
   What History Is; From Evidence to a Written History; Find
   History in Your Own Locality; Dating the Past; Heritage:
   What It Is and Why It Matters; Who Studies Prehistory.

2. **Level 2 — Ancient Eritrea and the Peoples to 1500** —
   9 lessons: From Stone Tools to Farming; Eritrea: Early
   Migrations and Peoples; Ancient Eritrea: Adulis and the
   Red Sea Trade; Ancient Eritrea: Christianity and Geez
   Writing; The Beja Kingdoms and Dahlak; Eritrea: The Bahri
   Negestat; Eritrea: Nara and Kunama Societies; Eritrea:
   Medieval Highland Villages; North Africa: Fatimids,
   Mamluks and the Funj.

3. **Level 3 — The Wider World's Old Orders** — 4 lessons:
   How Slave Society Worked; Ancient Civilisations: India's
   Order and Gifts; Medieval World: The Dark Age and
   Feudalism; Medieval World: The Ottomans.

4. **Level 4 — Coast Powers, Invaders and Resistance,
   1500–1900** — 7 lessons: Eritrea: Ottoman Coast and the
   Naibs; Eritrea: Portuguese-Turkish Rivalry and the Gragn
   Wars; Eritrea: Turkish Expansion and Bahri Negassi
   Yeshaq; Eritrea: Egyptian Expansion and Resistance;
   Eritrea: Abyssinian Expansion and Highland Resistance;
   Eritrea: Beni Amir Federation and the Funj; Eritrea:
   Samhar, Saho, Afar and the Naibs.

5. **Level 5 — Colony, Federation and Liberation,
   1890–1993** — 11 lessons: Colonisation: The Road to the
   Scramble; Eritrea: Italian Expansion and Resistance;
   Eritrea: Italian Colonial Administration; Colonisation:
   Direct and Indirect Rule; Colonisation: Its Effects on
   Africa; Eritrea: British Military Administration;
   Eritrea: Political Parties and the UN Question; Eritrea:
   Federation with Ethiopia and Its End; Eritrea: Armed
   Struggle — the ELF; Eritrea: Armed Struggle — the EPLF
   and Liberation; Eritrea: Referendum and Independence
   1993.

**Totals: 37 lessons, 70 placements** (Level 1: 6 lessons,
12 placements; Level 2: 9 lessons, 19 placements; Level 3:
4 lessons, 4 placements; Level 4: 7 lessons, 11 placements;
Level 5: 11 lessons, 24 placements — every placement from a
student book).
