# History in Popular Culture Film Games and Street Art

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

Braveheart's kilts never existed, Gladiator invents its emperor, Assassin's Creed rebuilds cities stone-perfect and invents the story inside them; Maus tells the Holocaust in mice and cats, Budapest's iron Shoes on the Danube say it without words — pop culture is a fine doorway into history and a dangerous place to stop.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of history in Popular Culture Film Games and Street Art — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: History in Popular Culture Film Games and Street Art. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
