# What History Is

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Introduction: what the "new age" (early-modern/modern era) is and how life, power and society changed as nations formed

Centuries of slow life give way to new states built on the idea that peoples sharing language and culture deserve their own country.

## Sub lesson 2 — Introduction to history

History begins as the Greek word istoria, inquiry; the book separates history (what happened) from historiography (how it is written), lays out the great periods from prehistory to the newest age, and ends with the old saying that history is life's teacher.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of what History Is — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: What History Is. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
