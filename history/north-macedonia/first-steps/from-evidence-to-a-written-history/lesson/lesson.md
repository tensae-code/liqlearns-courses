# From Evidence to a Written History

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Introduction: sources for the history of the Middle Ages

The medieval past survives in two kinds of trace — written sources (rulers' charters, chronicles that mix legend with fact, saints' lives) and material ones (coins, weapons, frescoes, fortresses) — and the historian must cross-examine both.

## Sub lesson 2 — Introduction: sources for the history of the new age, worked through Napoleon's entry into history as a source exercise

Pupils open a new era by reading what a conqueror's contemporaries wrote about him and learning to question it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Introduction: historical sources

The past speaks through material, written, oral and audiovisual traces, and the historian first asks whether a source is primary or secondary before trusting it.
