# Video script — From Evidence to a Written History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of from Evidence to a Written History — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

The medieval past survives in two kinds of trace — written sources (rulers' charters, chronicles that mix legend with fact, saints' lives) and material ones (coins, weapons, frescoes, fortresses) — and the historian must cross-examine both.

Pupils open a new era by reading what a conqueror's contemporaries wrote about him and learning to question it.

The past speaks through material, written, oral and audiovisual traces, and the historian first asks whether a source is primary or secondary before trusting it.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
