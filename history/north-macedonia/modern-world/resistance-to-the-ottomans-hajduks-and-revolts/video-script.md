# Video script — Resistance to the Ottomans Hajduks and Revolts

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of resistance to the Ottomans Hajduks and Revolts — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

As the empire weakens after Lepanto (1571) and taxes climb, resistance runs from the passive — fleeing the tax collector, shunning Ottoman courts, keeping the faith in song — to the armed: hajduk bands of twenty or thirty under an arambasha, sheltered by jatak helpers, gathering at St George's Day and dispersing at St Demetrius', led sometimes by women like Sirma and Rumena vojvoda; and open revolts like the thousand peasants of Prilep-Mariovo.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
