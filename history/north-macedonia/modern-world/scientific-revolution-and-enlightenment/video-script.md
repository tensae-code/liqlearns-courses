# Video script — Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of scientific Revolution and Enlightenment — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Gutenberg prints the Bible in 1455 and ideas outrun censors; Copernicus moves the Earth and Galileo's telescope confirms him — both persecuted by the Church; Machiavelli writes The Prince as a handbook of power, and Francis Bacon founds knowledge on observation and experiment, the road to Hobbes, Locke, Kant and Newton.

Reason is turned against absolute kings, and some kings try to rule with reason before it rules them out.

Experiments and print change daily life as much as revolutions change states.

Bacon teaches induction ("knowledge is power"), Galileo's telescope finds Jupiter's moons and earns him house arrest, Bruno burns for an infinite universe, Descartes doubts everything except thought itself, and Newton binds Kepler's ellipses and falling apples into one law of motion — while Maria Kirch discovers a comet and watches her husband take the credit.

Locke answers the kings — rights are natural, government is a contract, and a people may change it; Rousseau hands sovereignty to the citizens, Montesquieu splits power three ways, Voltaire defends the speech he hates, and enlightened despots like Frederick II and Catherine II borrow the ideas while keeping the crown.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
