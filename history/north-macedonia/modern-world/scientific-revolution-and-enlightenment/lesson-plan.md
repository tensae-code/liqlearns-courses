# Lesson plan — Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment

**Course:** History — North Macedonia · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of scientific Revolution and Enlightenment — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theme 3: the rise of science, technology and thought** — Gutenberg prints the Bible in 1455 and ideas outrun censors; Copernicus moves the Earth and Galileo's telescope confirms him — both persecuted by the Church; Machiavelli writes The Prince as a handbook of power, and Francis Bacon founds knowledge on observation and experiment, the road to Hobbes, Locke, Kant and Newton
2. **Theme 1: the Enlightenment and enlightened absolutism — thinkers attack unchecked rule, demand human rights, and rulers like Frederick II of Prussia pose as reforming monarchs** — reason is turned against absolute kings, and some kings try to rule with reason before it rules them out
3. **Theme 1: science and culture in Europe and the world, mid-17th to mid-19th century — Jenner's first vaccination (1796), the microscope, newspapers and public cultural life** — experiments and print change daily life as much as revolutions change states
4. **Theme 2: science and the new view of the world** — Bacon teaches induction ("knowledge is power"), Galileo's telescope finds Jupiter's moons and earns him house arrest, Bruno burns for an infinite universe, Descartes doubts everything except thought itself, and Newton binds Kepler's ellipses and falling apples into one law of motion — while Maria Kirch discovers a comet and watches her husband take the credit
5. **Theme 2: the Enlightenment** — Locke answers the kings — rights are natural, government is a contract, and a people may change it; Rousseau hands sovereignty to the citizens, Montesquieu splits power three ways, Voltaire defends the speech he hates, and enlightened despots like Frederick II and Catherine II borrow the ideas while keeping the crown

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the story of scientific Revolution and Enlightenment — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
2. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, what came of it — without looking back.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of the region in the story if you have one. Nothing else.
