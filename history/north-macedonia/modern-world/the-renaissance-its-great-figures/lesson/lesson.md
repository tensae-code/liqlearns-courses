# The Renaissance its Great Figures

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theme 3: the great figures

Dante writes the Divine Comedy in the people's Italian, Petrarch and Boccaccio follow; Leonardo paints the Mona Lisa, Michelangelo the Sistine frescoes and David, Raphael and Titian complete the school; Shakespeare and Cervantes carry it north and west — all floated on patrons like Florence's Medici.

## Sub lesson 2 — Theme 2: the great figures

Leonardo draws flying machines and paints the Mona Lisa, Michelangelo frees David from the marble and covers the Sistine ceiling, Raphael fills the School of Athens — and the new spirit travels north to Shakespeare's stage, Cervantes's knight and Erasmus's mocking pen.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of the Renaissance its Great Figures — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: The Renaissance its Great Figures. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
