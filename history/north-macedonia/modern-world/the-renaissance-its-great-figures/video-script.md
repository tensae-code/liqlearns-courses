# Video script — The Renaissance its Great Figures

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of the Renaissance its Great Figures — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Dante writes the Divine Comedy in the people's Italian, Petrarch and Boccaccio follow; Leonardo paints the Mona Lisa, Michelangelo the Sistine frescoes and David, Raphael and Titian complete the school; Shakespeare and Cervantes carry it north and west — all floated on patrons like Florence's Medici.

Leonardo draws flying machines and paints the Mona Lisa, Michelangelo frees David from the marble and covers the Sistine ceiling, Raphael fills the School of Athens — and the new spirit travels north to Shakespeare's stage, Cervantes's knight and Erasmus's mocking pen.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
