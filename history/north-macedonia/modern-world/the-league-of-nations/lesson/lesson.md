# The League of Nations

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

Wilson's League of Nations opens in Geneva in 1920 without America in it; it mediates, feeds refugees and fights the drug trade, the Washington Conference caps navies, Locarno guarantees borders, and in 1928 the world's states solemnly renounce war in the Briand-Kellogg Pact — and go on preparing it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of the League of Nations — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: The League of Nations. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
