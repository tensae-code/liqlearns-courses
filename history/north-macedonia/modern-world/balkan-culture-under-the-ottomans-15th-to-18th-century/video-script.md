# Video script — Balkan Culture under the Ottomans 15th to 18th Century

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of balkan Culture under the Ottomans 15th to 18th Century — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Monasteries at Lesnovo, Treskavec and Slepče keep Slavic letters alive in scriptoria; damaskini retell saints' lives in near-vernacular; printing happens abroad — Jakov of Kamena Reka in Venice, Žefarović's Stemmatographia (Vienna 1741) with its Macedonian coat of arms, Buzuku's Albanian Meshari (1555); cell schools and medreses (Skopje's Isa-beg) teach the two faiths; hidden cave churches ring Lake Ohrid while Ottoman towns take their lasting shape — čaršija and mahala, türbe, hamam, han and bezisten.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
