# Republic and Reign of Terror

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theme 1: from monarchy to republic, and the Reign of Terror and the Revolution's meaning

The republic that promised liberty guillotines its own children before the terror burns out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Theme 2: from monarchy to republic and Terror

The 1791 constitution keeps a king and a rich man's vote; the king's flight destroys trust, the republic is proclaimed, and Robespierre's Jacobins guillotine king, queen and finally each other — the corrupt Directory that follows is swept away by Napoleon's coup of 1799.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of republic and Reign of Terror — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Republic and Reign of Terror. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
