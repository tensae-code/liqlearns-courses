# The Long Road to Human Rights

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

The Universal Declaration of 1948 gives the world a rights charter, Europe adds its own court in 1959 — and in America Rosa Parks keeps her bus seat and Martin Luther King dreams aloud in Washington in 1963 until the laws begin to bend.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of the Long Road to Human Rights — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: The Long Road to Human Rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern happens in real life. Note it.
