# Women Win the Vote

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

Florence Nightingale remakes nursing in the Crimea's hospitals; suffragettes hunger-strike their way from New Zealand's first victory (1893) to America's (1920); Marie Curie takes two Nobel Prizes, Rosa Luxemburg binds the workers' cause to women's, Simone de Beauvoir names how society "makes" women — and a missionary from Skopje, Mother Teresa, serves the poorest of the poor.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of women Win the Vote — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Women Win the Vote. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the story of women Win the Vote — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where this story's pattern happens in real life. Note it.
