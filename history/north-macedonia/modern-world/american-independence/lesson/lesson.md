# American Independence

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theme 1: the creation of the United States — Britain taxes its colonies after costly wars, Jefferson drafts the Declaration that all men are created equal, independence is won and a constitution follows

Colonies taxed without a voice declare themselves a new nation and write its rules.

## Sub lesson 2 — Theme 2: the American War of Independence

Taxed without a seat in Parliament, Boston's Sons of Liberty dump the tea in the harbour disguised as Native Americans; London closes the port, the colonies meet at Philadelphia, and the first shots at Lexington and Concord turn a tax revolt into a war.

## Sub lesson 3 — Theme 2: the Declaration, the victory and the Constitution

Jefferson writes Locke into the Declaration of 1776; Saratoga brings France in, Yorktown ends it, and the Constitution of 1787 builds Montesquieu's three powers with checks and balances, crowned by a Bill of Rights — a freedom that still leaves slavery standing and women, like Molly Pitcher who served the guns at Monmouth, without the vote.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of american Independence — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: American Independence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
