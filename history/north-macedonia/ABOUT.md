# About the History — North Macedonia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — North Macedonia's own history books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns, marked exactly "Tip — from us, not from the books". Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for North Macedonia inside the History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade a book comes from appears only in this ABOUT and each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. Levels derive from the books' own progression as filed (see CURRICULUM.md).

The course as built has **five levels**: Level 1 The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans (51 lessons) · Level 2 The New Age: Revolution, Industry and the Balkan Awakening (32) · Level 3 Foundations and Ideas: First Civilisations, Faith and Power (22) · Level 4 A Connecting World: Empires, World Wars and Freedom Struggles (52) · Level 5 History in Today's World: Memory, Media and Identity (8). Totals: 165 lessons, 222 placements.

## The way of the books

North Macedonia's own way, as filed per book: the Middle Ages are told in five themes that swing between Europe and home — early medieval Europe, then the Balkans and Macedonia in the same centuries; developed/late medieval Europe, then the Balkans again; and finally Macedonia under the sultans — with parchment-style role-play inserts (a Frankish king's council, a "historical tournament", a researcher's travel notes from Rumelia) that make pupils argue the decisions themselves (Grade 7). World history and Balkan/Macedonian history alternate theme by theme in Grade 8, so pupils meet absolutism and revolution in Europe, then immediately their own Ottoman-era story; every lesson carries source extracts ("The past through sources"), a "Meet a historical figure" box and research challenges. Grade 10 is a thematic, not chronological, world survey in five themes — foundations, ideas and power, a connecting world, wars and freedom struggles, and history in today's world — that keeps returning to method (fact against interpretation, media, pop culture, identity, digital memory) and places Macedonia inside the Balkan, Byzantine and Ottoman stories, and on its own First World War front, rather than in a separate national narrative.
