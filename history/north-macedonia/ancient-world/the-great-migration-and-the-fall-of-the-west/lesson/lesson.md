# The Great Migration and the Fall of the West

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theme 1: the Great Migration of the peoples and the early European kingdoms

Climate change, hunger and the Huns push tribe after tribe across Rome's frontiers; Attila's host is broken at the Battle of the Nations in 451, Rome is sacked twice, and in 476 the last western emperor is deposed — on the ruins rise the kingdoms of Visigoths, Ostrogoths, Franks, Vandals and Anglo-Saxons, then Avars, Lombards and Slavs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Theme 3: the Great Migration of peoples

Cold, dry steppes and growing tribes set the dominoes falling; the Huns burst through the Gate of Peoples, Attila is checked at the Catalaunian Plains in 451, and Germanic kingdoms — Visigoth, Ostrogoth, Frankish, Vandal, Anglo-Saxon — inherit the West, with Avars, Lombards and Slavs following in the sixth century.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of the Great Migration and the Fall of the West — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: The Great Migration and the Fall of the West. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
