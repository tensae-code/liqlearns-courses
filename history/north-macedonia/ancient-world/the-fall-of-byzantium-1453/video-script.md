# Video script — The Fall of Byzantium 1453

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of the Fall of Byzantium 1453 — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Michael VIII Palaiologos wins Constantinople back in 1261, but the restored empire never recovers its trade or strength; civil wars invite the Ottomans in, and by the 1400s Byzantium is a city with an empire's memories — in 1453 Mehmed II's great army breaks the Theodosian walls and the last emperor, Constantine XI Dragash, dies in the defence; its scholars flee west, Moscow claims its mantle, and its fall pushes Europe to seek new sea roads.

Manzikert opens Anatolia to the Seljuks, the Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople itself in 1204, and though Nicaea restores the empire in 1261 it shrinks to a city — Mehmed II takes it in 1453 and the last emperor dies on the walls; what survives is law, letters and the mission of Cyril and Methodius, while Moscow dreams of being the Third Rome.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
