# Everyday Life in the Middle Ages

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

Nobles feast, play chess and joust in castle tournaments while peasants live by the sun and the church calendar and crowd to fairs on feast days; towns are filthy and plague kills a third of Europe in the 14th century; and women — subordinate in law — still leave marks as the poetess Kassia, the historian Anna Komnina, Christine de Pizan and Empresses Theodora and Irene.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of everyday Life in the Middle Ages — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Everyday Life in the Middle Ages. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
