# Capetian France and the Hundred Years War

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

Hugh Capet begins in 987 with little more than Paris; Philip II Augustus and Saint Louis build a real kingdom; when the Capetian line fails, England's king claims the throne and the Hundred Years War (1337–1453) begins — English longbowmen win the early battles, French cannon the last, and France emerges a great power (the book sets Joan of Arc as the pupils' own research).

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of capetian France and the Hundred Years War — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: Capetian France and the Hundred Years War. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
