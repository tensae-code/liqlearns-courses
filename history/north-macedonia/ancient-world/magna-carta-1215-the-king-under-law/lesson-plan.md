## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theme 3: medieval England** — Angles and Saxons, Alfred, then William of Normandy's conquest of 1066 make one kingdom; the Plantagenets hold half of France too — until King John's taxes and seizures drive his barons to force the Magna Carta on him in 1215: no free man imprisoned without lawful judgment, justice neither sold nor delayed — and England's first parliament follows
2. **Theme 2: England** — William wins Hastings in 1066 and makes every lord swear to the king himself; his descendant John squeezes the barons too hard and is forced at Runnymede to seal Magna Carta, and Edward I's Model Parliament of 1295 seats towns and counties beside the lords

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the story of magna Carta 1215 the King under Law — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
2. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, what came of it — without looking back.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of the region in the story if you have one. Nothing else.
