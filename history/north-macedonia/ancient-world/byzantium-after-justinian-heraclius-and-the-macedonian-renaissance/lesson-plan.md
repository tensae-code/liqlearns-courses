# Lesson plan — Byzantium after Justinian Heraclius and the Macedonian Renaissance

**Course:** History — North Macedonia · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Byzantium after Justinian Heraclius and the Macedonian Renaissance
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of byzantium after Justinian Heraclius and the Macedonian Renaissance — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theme 1: reforms and the empire's second rise** — with Avars at the walls, Heraclius rebuilds the state into soldier-farmer themes and Greek replaces Latin; three centuries later the Macedonian dynasty brings a Byzantine renaissance — Basil I, a peasant from the theme of Macedonia, seizes the throne, and Basil II extends the empire again
2. **Theme 2: Byzantium survives and renews itself** — Heraclius answers siege and collapse with soldier-farmer themes; the empire tears itself over icons, splits from Rome for good in 1054, flowers again under the Macedonian dynasty — then eats its own strength by granting pronoia lands to magnates

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
