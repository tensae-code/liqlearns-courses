# Video script — Byzantium after Justinian Heraclius and the Macedonian Renaissance

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of byzantium after Justinian Heraclius and the Macedonian Renaissance — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

With Avars at the walls, Heraclius rebuilds the state into soldier-farmer themes and Greek replaces Latin; three centuries later the Macedonian dynasty brings a Byzantine renaissance — Basil I, a peasant from the theme of Macedonia, seizes the throne, and Basil II extends the empire again.

Heraclius answers siege and collapse with soldier-farmer themes; the empire tears itself over icons, splits from Rome for good in 1054, flowers again under the Macedonian dynasty — then eats its own strength by granting pronoia lands to magnates.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
