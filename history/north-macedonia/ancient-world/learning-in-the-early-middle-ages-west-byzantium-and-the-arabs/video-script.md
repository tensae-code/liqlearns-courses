# Video script — Learning in the Early Middle Ages West Byzantium and the Arabs

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of learning in the Early Middle Ages West Byzantium and the Arabs — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Three worlds keep learning alive differently — western monks copy manuscripts in scriptoria and Charlemagne's court revives letters; Byzantium's Magnaura school teaches emperors' children, and its engineers wield Greek fire; the Arabs, freer of dogma, found algebra and trigonometry, adopt Indian numerals and zero, print Avicenna's Canon of medicine and carry paper and gunpowder from China toward Europe.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
