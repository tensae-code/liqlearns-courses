# The Crusades Two Centuries for Jerusalem

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Theme 3: the Crusades and Byzantium

After the Seljuks take Anatolia and Jerusalem, Urban II's call at Clermont (1095) sends eight crusades east; the first takes Jerusalem in 1099, but the Fourth, hijacked by Venice's Enrico Dandolo, sacks Christian Constantinople in 1204 and plants a Latin Empire — Acre, the last crusader port, falls in 1291, and the real winners are Venice and Genoa.

## Sub lesson 2 — Theme 3: the Crusades

Eight great expeditions under the popes' banner march to free Jerusalem between 1096 and 1270, mixing faith, land-hunger and profit, massacring Muslims, Jews and Orthodox on the way — and crusaders come home carrying the crafts, foods and irrigation of the Islamic cities they fought.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of the Crusades Two Centuries for Jerusalem — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: The Crusades Two Centuries for Jerusalem. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
