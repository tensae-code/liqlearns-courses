The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of medieval World the Dark Age and Feudalism — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Renaissance humanists name the age between antiquity and themselves the "middle" age; historians open it with the fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 and close it at the discovery of America (1492) or the English Revolution, dividing it into early, developed and late periods as feudalism rises and decays.

Western society stacks into a feudal hierarchy — the ruler grants fiefs to great nobles, they to lesser ones, each owing loyalty and war-service upward, while at the bottom free peasants slide into serfdom; Byzantium runs the opposite way, a centralised state of salaried officials, theme armies and free tax-paying farmers.

Land is power — kings grant fiefs to vassals who owe swords in return, serfs are bound to the soil, and "the vassal of my vassal is not my vassal" explains why early medieval kings command so little.
