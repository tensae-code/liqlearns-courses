# Lesson plan — Clovis Charlemagne and the Frankish Empire

**Course:** History — North Macedonia · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Clovis Charlemagne and the Frankish Empire
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of clovis Charlemagne and the Frankish Empire — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theme 1: the state of the Franks** — Clovis unites the Franks, accepts baptism at Reims and wins the Church for his dynasty; as Merovingian kings fade into "do-nothing" rulers, their mayors of the palace take power — Charles Martel stops the Arabs in 732, Pepin founds the Carolingian line and carves out the Papal State, and Charlemagne, crowned emperor in Rome in 800, builds an empire whose court school at Aachen sparks a Carolingian Renaissance — before Verdun splits it into France, Italy and Germany
2. **Theme 2: the Franks and the Holy Roman Empire** — Clovis's baptism wins the Church for the Franks; Charles Martel halts the Arabs, Pepin takes a crown, and Charlemagne's Aachen court sparks a renaissance before Verdun splits his empire three ways — Otto I's imperial coronation in 962 refounds an "empire" that is really a loose league of princes

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the story of clovis Charlemagne and the Frankish Empire — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
2. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, what came of it — without looking back.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of the region in the story if you have one. Nothing else.
