# Lesson plan — Medieval World the Ottomans

**Course:** History — North Macedonia · **Title:** The Ancient World · **Lesson:** Medieval World the Ottomans
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story of medieval World the Ottomans — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — The Middle Ages: Europe, the Balkans and Macedonia Under the Sultans — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theme 4: the Ottomans and their conquests in the Balkans** — Seljuk Turks fleeing the Mongols settle Anatolia in beyliks; Emir Osman unites them and his heirs cross at Gallipoli in 1352, take Adrianople as Edirne, destroy the Serbian and Bulgarian kingdoms between Maritsa (1371), Kosovo (1389) and Nicopolis (1396), and leave Macedonia wholly theirs when Thessalonica finally falls in 1430
2. **Theme 5: the Ottoman Empire — its organisation and army** — a centralised empire under an absolute sultan, advised by the divan and served by the grand vizier; sharia law in kadi courts; Rumelia and Anatolia as first eyalets; and an army of timar-holding sipahi cavalry plus the janissaries — Christian boys taken by the devshirme, raised as the sultan's own, some rising to grand vizier — at its height under Suleiman the Lawgiver, whose fleets and armies press Vienna
3. **Theme 2: the Ottoman rise** — Osman's small Anatolian beylik takes Bursa, crosses at Gallipoli into Europe, crowns itself with Constantinople in 1453 and peaks under Suleiman the Magnificent, who breaks Hungary at Mohács

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the story of medieval World the Ottomans — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.
2. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, what came of it — without looking back.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a simple map of the region in the story if you have one. Nothing else.
