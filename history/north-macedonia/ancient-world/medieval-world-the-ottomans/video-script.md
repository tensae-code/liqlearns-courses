# Video script — Medieval World the Ottomans

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The story of medieval World the Ottomans — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it.

Seljuk Turks fleeing the Mongols settle Anatolia in beyliks; Emir Osman unites them and his heirs cross at Gallipoli in 1352, take Adrianople as Edirne, destroy the Serbian and Bulgarian kingdoms between Maritsa (1371), Kosovo (1389) and Nicopolis (1396), and leave Macedonia wholly theirs when Thessalonica finally falls in 1430.

A centralised empire under an absolute sultan, advised by the divan and served by the grand vizier; sharia law in kadi courts; Rumelia and Anatolia as first eyalets; and an army of timar-holding sipahi cavalry plus the janissaries — Christian boys taken by the devshirme, raised as the sultan's own, some rising to grand vizier — at its height under Suleiman the Lawgiver, whose fleets and armies press Vienna.

Osman's small Anatolian beylik takes Bursa, crosses at Gallipoli into Europe, crowns itself with Constantinople in 1453 and peaks under Suleiman the Magnificent, who breaks Hungary at Mohács.

Watch the map as you listen: find where the story starts, follow what moves, and stop where the turn happens.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself.
