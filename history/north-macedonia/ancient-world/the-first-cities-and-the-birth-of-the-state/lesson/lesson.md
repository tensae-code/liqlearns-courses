# The First Cities and the Birth of the State

## Do it first

On a blank page, draw the map of this story as you imagine it — the places, the people, the road between them. Keep the page; the story will fill it in.

## The story

Surplus grain fills the first cities — Uruk with its temples and scribes — and strangers must now be ruled: the book weighs three theories of the state (conquest, contract, inequality) as Sargon of Akkad welds Mesopotamia into the first empire and Egypt gathers under Memphis, while ordinary people are paid in beer and play senet.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of the First Cities and the Birth of the State — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it — and name the lesson's key words: The First Cities and the Birth of the State. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: draw the story's map once from memory after you close the lesson; the places you cannot draw are the parts to read again.
