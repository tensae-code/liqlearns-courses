# History — Cuba course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Cuba inside the History folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Cuba's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance reckoning.

**How the ladder is derived:** Cuba's books tell the national story again and again, deeper each time, and set it inside the world story: first the primary national survey (to 1898, then the twentieth century to the Revolution in power), then the world story ancient-to-medieval and modern-to-contemporary, then the national story again at secondary and pre-university depth, with the Americas given a book of their own. The ladder follows that progression in the books' own order: each level is one arc of the country's telling, earliest first, and a lesson stands at the level of the earliest arc that teaches it; its later, deeper tellings are stocked into the same lesson. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced gate. History is taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.
