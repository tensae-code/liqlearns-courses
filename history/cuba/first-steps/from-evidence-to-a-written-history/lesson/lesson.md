# From evidence to a written history

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "From evidence to a written history" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Sources of historical knowledge: museum objects, documents, buildings, coins

A hero's weapon in a museum seems to speak; objects left by aborigines and fighters tell how they lived and thought

## Introductory activities: sorting true/false historical sources

Heroes' objects, old buildings and coins count as sources; a brand-new building and a modern map of Africa do not

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of From evidence to a written history as Cuba's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's story in your own words.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the story happens in real life. Note it.
