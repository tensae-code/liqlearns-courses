# What history is

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "What history is" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## Introductory chapter: what History of Cuba studies, national vs universal history

A child asks what their country's past is; the book answers that national history is the people's own life, heroes and customs from their origins

## General considerations + guiding ideas for teaching History of Cuba in primary school

A teacher is told no one can teach what they do not know; history class exists to form patriotic feeling and judgement, not to memorise a chronicle

## I.1 Importance of studying universal history; historical sources

A new secondary pupil asks why study a world that ended centuries ago; sources — objects, writings, images — are introduced as the evidence that makes the answer possible

## Discipline characterisation and general objectives for Modern/Contemporary history in Secundaria Básica

Teachers are told the subject's job: pupils who can explain, not just recite, how the modern world was made

## Intro 1.1 Importance of studying History of Cuba and its link with universal history

At secondary level the national story is explicitly tied to the world story pupils learned in Grades 7–8 — Cuba inside world time

## Didactic conception of the subject in the grade; introductory unit: approach to the formation of Cuban nationality

The teacher's whole course is one question — how did a colony's mixture become a nation that thinks for itself?
