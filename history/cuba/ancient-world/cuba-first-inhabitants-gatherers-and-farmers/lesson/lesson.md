# Cuba first inhabitants gatherers and farmers

## Do it first

Before you read: say what you already know about "Cuba first inhabitants gatherers and farmers" — a name, a place, a date, anything. Write it down. This lesson tells the story properly; at the end, check what you said against it.

## 1.1 First settlers, occupations and ways of life

Canoe migrants from South America cross the Antilles; gatherer-hunter-fisher groups live in caves while farmer-potter groups plant conucos, make casabe from yuca, and are led by a cacique and a behíque

## Ch.1 activities: comparing gatherer and farmer-potter groups, casabe-making, investigating aborigines in your own locality

Two ways of aboriginal life are set side by side in a table; the pupil then hunts for the aborigines who once lived in their own town

## Unit guidance, Part I Aboriginal community society (Intro unit + Unit 1)

Teachers are walked through presenting Cuba's oldest inhabitants with descriptions, anecdotes and stories suited to 10-year-olds

## Intro 1.1 (second part) Aboriginal communities in Cuba at the end of the 15th c.

The island's different aboriginal communities are surveyed again, now with the Amazon-to-Caribbean canoe expedition as evidence

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the story of Cuba first inhabitants gatherers and farmers as Cuba's own books tell it — who was in it, where it happened, what turned, and what came of it. Retell the story — who, where, what turned, and what came of it. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
