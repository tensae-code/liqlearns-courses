# History — Cuba course — ABOUT

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this course comes from government textbooks — Cuba's own History books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How the course is organised

One course for Cuba, organised by levels, never grades (see CURRICULUM.md). Lessons sit under title folders named for the placements' own units (`first-steps/`, `ancient-world/`, `modern-world/`, `africa-to-1500/`, `africa-after-1500/`). Every placement in the placements file stocks into exactly one lesson, with exactly one provenance row in that lesson's `lesson/sources.md`. Placements at `liqlearns-courses-work/expansion-history-2026-11-15/cuba-placements.md` (250 placements — the count of lines containing `story beats:`; the lines govern).

Records as filed: 27 records / 14 distinct books (duplicates: G5 student x3 routes, G7 x2, G8 student x2, G8 orientaciones x2, G9 x2, G10 Cuba x2, G10 América x2, G11 programa x2; and the Grade 10/11/12 Nivel Medio Superior records are ONE book, MD5-verified for 11/12) / read via grab.py (GitHub API), in-place staging files where on_disk, and the recorded CubaEduca source for G7 after the release grab truncated twice. The placements file files 15 book headers (Books 1–15); Book 13 is the duplicate record of Book 11 (see Honesty notes).

## The way of the books

Cuba's own way, as filed book by book: the national story alone, chronologically, with local history inside it from the first chapter (Grade 5, with its workbook mirroring the textbook chapter by chapter and its teacher guide threading Martí's and Fidel's thought through every unit); the twentieth century as frustrated independence → neocolonial republic → revolution (Grade 6); the world story in a Marxist stage frame, Egypt the typical model, the medieval worlds of Asia, Africa and America given their own part (Grade 7); the modern and contemporary world told from Latin America outward, to the fall of the USSR and the unipolar present (Grade 8, with its teacher guide as the map of the same arc); the full national survey again, deeper and more analytical, ending with the Revolution's first decades (Grade 9); the pre-university survey with its culture panoramas, running to the Special Period, beside a whole book for the Americas under Martí's "Nuestra América" (Grade 10); and pre-university guidance framed by one question — how a colony's mixture became a nation (Grade 11 teacher guide).
