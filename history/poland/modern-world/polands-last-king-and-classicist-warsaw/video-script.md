# Video script — Poland's Last King and Classicist Warsaw

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Stanisław August, Poland's last king, makes Warsaw a classicist capital of theatres, palaces and Łazienki park even as the partitions close in — a court culture flowering in the shadow of the state's destruction.

The style comes home to a king running out of country. Stanisław August, Poland's last king, is a true patron of the classicist age: he makes Warsaw a classicist capital — theatres, palaces, and Łazienki, his park of pavilions and water, the style named for him in Poland: the Stanisławowski style.

But the flowering happens in shadow. Even as Warsaw builds, the partitions close in — Russia, Prussia and Austria dividing the Commonwealth between them until the state itself is destroyed. A court culture reaches its height at the very moment the country it crowns is being erased from the map. Poland's classicism is therefore beautiful and heartbreaking at once: a capital dressing itself in Rome while Poland disappears.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
