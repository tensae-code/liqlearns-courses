# Hands-on — Symbolism and Munch: Art of Dreams and Anxiety

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: The century ends dreaming — Pre-Raphaelites and symbolists paint myths, dreams and death rather than the visible world, Rodin remakes sculpture from the inside, and Munch's Scream turns modern anxiety into an icon before expressionism has a name. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f a figure on a bridge with hands to its face under a swirling blood-red sky — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
