# Video script — Symbolism and Munch: Art of Dreams and Anxiety

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

This lesson is about one thing.
The century ends dreaming — Pre-Raphaelites and symbolists paint myths, dreams and death rather than the visible world, Rodin remakes sculpture from the inside, and Munch's Scream turns modern anxiety into an icon before expressionism has a name.

The century ends dreaming. Tired of painting the visible world — realists' stones, impressionists' light — the symbolists turn inward: myths, dreams and death are their subjects, painted in England by the Pre-Raphaelites with jewel-like intensity and across Europe by artists for whom a picture is a state of soul, not a view.

Sculpture turns too: Rodin remakes it from the inside, figures twisting out of rough stone as if thought itself were struggling into form. And in Norway, Munch paints The Scream — a figure on a bridge, hands to its face, the sky blood-red — turning modern anxiety into an icon, years before expressionism has a name for what he did. The visible world was not enough; the century closes by painting the invisible.
