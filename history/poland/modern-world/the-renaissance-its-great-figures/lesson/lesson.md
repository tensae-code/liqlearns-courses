# The Renaissance: Its Great Figures

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

Now the rebirth gets faces. The Renaissance is the first age of art told by great named masters, and four of them head this lesson. Michelangelo works like a force of nature — carving marble and painting ceilings with a power that still overwhelms. Leonardo da Vinci turns painting into a science of light and mind: every picture an investigation, every face a study of a soul.

Raphael balances it all — the power and the science composed into calm, harmonious scenes where nothing jars. And in Venice, Titian makes colour itself glow, painting with a richness of tone the earlier masters never reached. Sculpture and painting of the 15th and 16th centuries stand in this lesson under four names — the giants the next generation will feel it must escape or surpass.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Four names, four gifts: Michelangelo power, Leonardo science, Raphael balance, Titian colour.
