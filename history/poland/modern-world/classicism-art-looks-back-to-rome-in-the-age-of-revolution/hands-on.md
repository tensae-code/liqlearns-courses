# Hands-on — Classicism: Art Looks Back to Rome in the Age of Revolution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: Disgusted with Rococo frivolity, Europe digs up Pompeii and dresses revolution and empire alike in Roman virtue — temples for banks and parliaments, David's oath scenes for republics, and Napoleon's empire style for a new Caesar. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f a bank built like a Roman temple beside a painting of Roman heroes swearing an oath together — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
