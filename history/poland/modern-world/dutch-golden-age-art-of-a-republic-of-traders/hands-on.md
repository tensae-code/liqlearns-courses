# Hands-on — Dutch Golden Age: Art of a Republic of Traders

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: One Low Countries split in two — Catholic Flanders paints big and sensuous for church and court with Rubens, while the Protestant Dutch Republic, rich on trade, buys small pictures of its own homes, ships, landscapes and faces, and Rembrandt turns light inward. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f a Dutch interior with a family at a table, a small landscape and a ship painting on the wall, light falling from one window — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
