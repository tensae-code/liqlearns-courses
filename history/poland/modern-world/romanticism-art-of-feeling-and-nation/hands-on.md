# Hands-on — Romanticism: Art of Feeling and Nation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: Reason's age provokes a revolt of feeling — Goya paints war's madness and monsters of sleep, French romantics fill canvases with shipwrecks, revolutions and oriental dreams, and everywhere art becomes the voice of nations longing to be free. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f a shipwreck in a stormy sea with figures clinging to a raft, dramatic clouds above — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
