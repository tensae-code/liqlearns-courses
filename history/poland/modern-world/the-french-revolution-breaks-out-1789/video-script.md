# Video script — The French Revolution Breaks Out, 1789

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The volume's own timeline tells the age straight — Britain grows into an empire, Catherine rules Russia, America declares independence in 1776, France's revolution erupts in 1789, Napoleon crowns himself, and the Congress of Vienna closes the age in 1815 — the political storm inside which classicism's calm Roman lines were drawn.

The third volume opens with a timeline, and the timeline is a storm. Britain grows into an empire; Catherine rules Russia; across the Atlantic, America declares independence in 1776. Then, in 1789, France's revolution erupts — the Bastille, the rights of man, and in time the guillotine taking the king himself. Out of the storm rides Napoleon, who crowns himself emperor, until the Congress of Vienna closes the age in 1815 and redraws Europe's map.

Hold the storm in mind, because the art of the age — classicism, calm Roman lines, drawn straight and severe — was made inside it. Revolution and empire alike dressed themselves in Roman virtue while Europe shook. The next lesson takes up the style; this one keeps the weather it was made in.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
