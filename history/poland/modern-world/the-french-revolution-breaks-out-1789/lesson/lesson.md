# The French Revolution Breaks Out, 1789

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

The third volume opens with a timeline, and the timeline is a storm. Britain grows into an empire; Catherine rules Russia; across the Atlantic, America declares independence in 1776. Then, in 1789, France's revolution erupts — the Bastille, the rights of man, and in time the guillotine taking the king himself. Out of the storm rides Napoleon, who crowns himself emperor, until the Congress of Vienna closes the age in 1815 and redraws Europe's map.

Hold the storm in mind, because the art of the age — classicism, calm Roman lines, drawn straight and severe — was made inside it. Revolution and empire alike dressed themselves in Roman virtue while Europe shook. The next lesson takes up the style; this one keeps the weather it was made in.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Calm lines, drawn in a storm: learn the timeline first, and classicism will make sense.
