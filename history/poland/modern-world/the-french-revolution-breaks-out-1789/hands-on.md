# Hands-on — The French Revolution Breaks Out, 1789

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: The volume's own timeline tells the age straight — Britain grows into an empire, Catherine rules Russia, America declares independence in 1776, France's revolution erupts in 1789, Napoleon crowns himself, and the Congress of Vienna closes the age in 1815 — the political storm inside which classicism's calm Roman lines were drawn. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f a timeline banner with flags and dates — 1776, 1789, Napoleon's crown, 1815 — over a map of Europe — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
