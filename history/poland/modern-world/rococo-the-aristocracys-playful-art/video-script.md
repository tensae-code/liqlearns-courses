# Video script — Rococo: The Aristocracy's Playful Art

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
After the Sun King's death the aristocracy trades glory for pleasure — rooms shrink to salons, curves turn to shells and scrolls, and painting fills with silk, flirtation and garden parties on the eve of a revolution nobody at the party expects.

The Sun King dies, and the mood of Europe exhales. Glory is exhausting; pleasure is next. The aristocracy trades the vast halls of Versailles for small, intimate salons, and the Rococo style is born: curves that turn into shells and scrolls, pale colours, mirrors and gilt, everything light, asymmetrical and playful.

Painting follows the rooms: silk dresses, flirtation, garden parties, lovers in parks at golden hour. It is the most charming art in this whole course — and the most fateful, because it floats on the eve of a revolution nobody at the party expects. Within a generation the world of the salon will be swept away; the Rococo paints its last, loveliest summer, not knowing.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
