# Hands-on — Rococo: The Aristocracy's Playful Art

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: After the Sun King's death the aristocracy trades glory for pleasure — rooms shrink to salons, curves turn to shells and scrolls, and painting fills with silk, flirtation and garden parties on the eve of a revolution nobody at the party expects. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f an elegant salon with shell-and-scroll gilt decoration and ladies and gentlemen at a garden party beyond the windows — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
