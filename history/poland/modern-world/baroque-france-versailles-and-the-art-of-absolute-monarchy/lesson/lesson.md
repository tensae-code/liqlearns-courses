# Baroque France: Versailles and the Art of Absolute Monarchy

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

In France, art becomes government. Louis XIV, the Sun King, builds Versailles — a palace-city of halls, gardens and fountains arranged as a machine for displaying absolute power, where the court must live under the king's eye and every ceiling proclaims his glory. French Baroque takes a classicising turn: ordered, majestic, disciplined splendour.

And it conquers without armies. Versailles sets the taste of every court in Europe — kings everywhere copy its halls, its gardens, its manners. Across the Channel, England answers with its own restrained grandeur: Baroque, but held in check, dignity without Versailles' blaze. One king's palace becomes a continent's style book.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Versailles is not a home; it is a machine — art doing the work of government.
