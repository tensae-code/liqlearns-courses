# Hands-on — Baroque France: Versailles and the Art of Absolute Monarchy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: Louis XIV turns art into government — Versailles rises as a machine for displaying absolute power, French classicising Baroque sets the taste of every court in Europe, and England answers with its own restrained grandeur. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f Versailles with its long palace front, formal gardens and fountains stretching to the horizon — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
