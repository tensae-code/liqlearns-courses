# Video script — Realism: Painting Ordinary Life

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The realists turn their backs on heroes and gods and paint stone-breakers, funerals in provincial towns and peasants at work — a scandal at the salons, and a claim that ordinary life is worthy of the biggest canvases.

The realists make a scandalous claim: ordinary life deserves the biggest canvases. They turn their backs on heroes, gods and oriental dreams, and paint what is in front of them — stone-breakers hammering rocks by the road, a funeral in a provincial town, peasants at work in the fields, painted life-size, without flattery and without apology.

At the official salons this is a scandal: such subjects, in such sizes, with such honesty? But the claim stands and wins its place in history. Realism says the present day, ordinary people and unglamorous work are worthy of art's full seriousness — the doctrine the rest of modern painting will build on.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
