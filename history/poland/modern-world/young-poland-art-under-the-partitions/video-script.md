# Video script — Young Poland: Art Under the Partitions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Young Poland makes Kraków and Zakopane a capital of the spirit — highland cottages become a national style, stained glass and drama fuse in Wyspiański's visions, and a generation paints folklore and myth as the programme of a country that exists in culture before it exists on the map.

The course ends where its Polish story has been heading all along: a country that exists in culture before it exists on the map. Young Poland — the generation of the 1890s and 1900s — takes every new European current, impressionism, symbolism, secession and the rest, and turns them to one programme: folklore and myth, painted and built as the substance of a stateless nation.

Its capitals are Kraków and Zakopane, the highland town under the Tatras. There the highland cottage itself becomes a national style — the Zakopane style, carved timber and steep roofs proposed as Poland's own architecture. And above the generation stands Wyspiański, in whom stained glass and drama fuse: windows of saints and kings for Kraków's churches, plays that summon Poland's past onto the stage. A generation paints a country back into visibility — and within two decades of this volume's close, the map itself will follow.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
