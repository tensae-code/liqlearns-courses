# Video script — Baroque Rome: Art of the Counter-Reformation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Bernini makes marble fly and St Peter's Square embrace the crowds; Borromini bends walls like waves; Caravaggio drags saints into tavern light and shadow; and ceiling painters break the roof open onto heaven.

Rome's answer gets its makers. Bernini, the age's presiding genius, makes marble fly — drapery whirling, saints swooning — and shapes St Peter's Square into two great colonnades that embrace the crowds like arms. Beside him, his rival Borromini bends walls like waves, churches swelling and curving as if stone had become soft.

Painting answers in two directions at once. Caravaggio drags saints into tavern light and shadow — holy scenes played by rough, real people under one hard beam of light. And the illusionist ceiling painters break the roof open onto heaven, painting skies and ascending saints where the ceiling should be, so the church interior pours upward into glory. Marble flying, walls bending, saints in taverns, heaven through the roof: that is Baroque Rome at full stretch.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
