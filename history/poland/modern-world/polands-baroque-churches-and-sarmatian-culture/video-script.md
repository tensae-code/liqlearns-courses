# Video script — Poland's Baroque: Churches and Sarmatian Culture

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Commonwealth covers itself in Baroque churches built as fortresses of faith through wars with Sweden and the Ottomans, while the szlachta's Sarmatian culture — coffin portraits, oriental dress, country manors — gives Polish Baroque a face found nowhere else.

The styles come home to a country at war. Through wars with Sweden and the Ottomans, the Polish Commonwealth covers itself in Baroque churches — splendid, defiant buildings that stand as fortresses of faith on a fought-over land. Rome's heart-moving splendour, in Poland, also has to be brave.

And Polish Baroque has a face found nowhere else: Sarmatian culture, the world of the szlachta, the Polish nobility. The nobles believed themselves descended from the ancient Sarmatians, and lived it — oriental dress, shaved heads with a topknot, country manors, and the coffin portrait: a startlingly true painted face fixed to the coffin at a noble's funeral, so the dead man attends his own farewell. Faith's fortresses and the Sarmatian face: that is Poland's Baroque.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
