# Hands-on — Post-Impressionism: Four Rebels After the Impressionists

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: Four heirs break four different ways — Cézanne rebuilds the world in solid geometry, Gauguin flees to Brittany and Tahiti for the primitive, Van Gogh burns feeling into swirling colour and sells almost nothing, and Toulouse-Lautrec takes art to the cabaret poster. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f four panels — a solid geometric mountain, a Tahitian scene, a swirling starry sky and a bold cabaret poster — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
