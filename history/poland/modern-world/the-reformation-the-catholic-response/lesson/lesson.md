# The Reformation: The Catholic Response

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

The Baroque begins as an answer to a wound. The Reformation has split western Christianity, and the Catholic Church, wounded, decides to answer — not with arguments alone, but with splendour. Rome rebuilds itself as a stage set of faith: domes, fountains, colonnades and churches designed to overwhelm the senses and win back the wandering heart.

That is the Baroque order given to art: move the heart, not just teach the mind. Where the Renaissance balanced and measured, Baroque art will sweep, dazzle and embrace — saints in ecstasy, ceilings opening onto heaven, marble in motion. The historical background of this volume's second half is a church fighting for souls with beauty as its weapon.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A wounded church answers with splendour: art must move the heart.

## Say it to your companion
