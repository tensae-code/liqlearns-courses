# The Renaissance Spreads North: France, Germany and the Netherlands

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

Ideas travel. The Italian spark crosses the Alps, and every northern country catches it in its own way. French kings, home from Italian wars, build châteaux along the Loire — Renaissance palaces for hunting and pleasure. In Germany, Dürer marries Italian perspective to northern detail, the learned south wedded to the painstaking north in one man's prints and paintings.

The Netherlands fill their panels with merchants' worlds — interiors, objects, wealth observed hair by hair. In Spain, El Greco stretches souls heavenward in flame-like figures. And in Prague, the emperor Rudolf gathers artists, scholars and strange objects until his court becomes a cabinet of wonders for the whole continent. One spark, five northern fires.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: North of the Alps the Renaissance speaks with local accents: château, print, panel, flame and cabinet.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Italian spark crosses the Alps — French kings build Loire châteaux, Dürer marries Italian perspective to northern detail, Netherlandish painters fill panels with merchants' worlds, El Greco stretches souls in Spain, and Rudolf's Prague becomes a cabinet of wonders. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Dürer, cabinet of wonders. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
