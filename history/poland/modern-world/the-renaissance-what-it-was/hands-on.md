# Hands-on — The Renaissance: What It Was

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: Rich Italian city-states, proud of Rome's ruins under their feet, decide the Middle Ages were a long sleep — scholars dig out ancient books, bankers and popes pay for beauty, and art wakes up measuring the world with new eyes. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f an Italian city with a domed church and palace, ancient Roman ruins standing in the street before them — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
