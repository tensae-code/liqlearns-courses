# Mannerism: Italy's Restless Art After the Renaissance

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

What do you do when you are born after Michelangelo? The generation that follows the giants feels there is nothing left to perfect — balance has been achieved, and achieved things cannot be repeated, only escaped. So Mannerism escapes: bodies stretch longer than nature, poses twist into elegant spirals, and unease replaces calm — deliberately, skilfully, as a style.

The unease is not only in the pictures. Italy itself is invaded by foreign armies, and Rome is sacked — the centre of the Renaissance world plundered. Art that twists and stretches is the honest art of a country whose balance has been broken in life as well as in painting.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: After perfection, restlessness: stretched bodies for a sacked world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The generation after the giants feels there is nothing left to perfect — so it stretches bodies, twists poses and chooses elegant unease over balance, while Italy itself is invaded and Rome is sacked. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Mannerism, sack of Rome. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
