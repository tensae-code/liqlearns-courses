# Hands-on — The Industrial Revolution: Its Two Stages

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: Steam, iron and the factory remake the century's cities faster than any style can follow — engineers raise bridges, stations and exhibition halls of iron and glass while architects dress banks and parliaments in borrowed Gothic and Renaissance clothes. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f an iron-and-glass railway station with a steam train arriving, a Gothic-dressed parliament building behind it — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
