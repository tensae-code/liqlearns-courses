# The Industrial Revolution: Its Two Stages

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

The 19th century's real builder is not a style but a machine. Steam, iron and the factory remake the cities faster than any style can follow: populations explode, railways cut across countries, and a new kind of maker — the engineer — raises the age's true monuments: bridges, stations and exhibition halls of iron and glass, built by calculation, not by costume.

Architecture splits in two. The engineers build the future in naked iron; the architects, uneasy, dress the age's banks and parliaments in borrowed clothes — Gothic here, Renaissance there — the historicism the next lesson takes up. Two stages of industry drive it all: first steam and iron, then the still greater forces that follow. The century builds its body in iron and argues about its dress.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Iron body, borrowed clothes: engineers raise the real century while architects argue about its costume.
