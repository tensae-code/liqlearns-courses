# Video script — Poland's Renaissance: Kraków and the Jagiellonian Court

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Jagiellonian court makes Kraków a northern Florence — Italian architects rebuild Wawel with an arcaded courtyard, the Sigismund Chapel rises as the period's jewel, and Polish nobles' manors and town halls take up the new style — Poland's own Renaissance golden age.

The spark comes home, and Poland catches it royally. Under the Jagiellonian kings, Kraków becomes a northern Florence: Italian architects are brought to rebuild Wawel, the royal castle, giving it an arcaded courtyard — tiered arches in the Italian manner, on a Polish royal hill.

Beside the castle rises the period's jewel: the Sigismund Chapel, the royal burial chapel, counted among the purest Renaissance works north of the Alps. And the style does not stay at court — Polish nobles build manors in the new manner and towns raise town halls with Renaissance gables. A court, a chapel, a countryside of manors: this is Poland's own Renaissance golden age.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
