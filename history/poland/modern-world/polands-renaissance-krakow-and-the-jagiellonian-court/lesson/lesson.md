# Poland's Renaissance: Kraków and the Jagiellonian Court

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

The spark comes home, and Poland catches it royally. Under the Jagiellonian kings, Kraków becomes a northern Florence: Italian architects are brought to rebuild Wawel, the royal castle, giving it an arcaded courtyard — tiered arches in the Italian manner, on a Polish royal hill.

Beside the castle rises the period's jewel: the Sigismund Chapel, the royal burial chapel, counted among the purest Renaissance works north of the Alps. And the style does not stay at court — Polish nobles build manors in the new manner and towns raise town halls with Renaissance gables. A court, a chapel, a countryside of manors: this is Poland's own Renaissance golden age.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Kraków signs the Renaissance in Polish: an arcaded courtyard at Wawel, a jewel of a chapel beside it.
