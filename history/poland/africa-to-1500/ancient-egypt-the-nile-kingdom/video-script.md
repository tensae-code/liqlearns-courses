# Video script — Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Nile kingdom builds for eternity — pyramids and temples for gods and god-kings, statues carved to a strict canon so a pharaoh's face outlives him by three thousand years, and tomb paintings that pack a whole life for the journey after death.

Egypt builds for eternity. Along the Nile, a kingdom of god-kings raises pyramids — mountains of stone meant never to fall — and temples to its gods. Nothing here is built for a season; everything is built to outlast time itself.

The same will to last shapes the figures. Statues are carved to a strict canon, a fixed rule of proportions, so that a pharaoh's face stays true and can outlive him by three thousand years. And in the tombs, painters pack a whole life onto the walls — food, servants and boats — everything the dead would need for the journey after death. Egypt's art is its answer to death: build, carve and paint so well that nothing is ever really gone.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
