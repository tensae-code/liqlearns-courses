# History — Poland course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Poland inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** Poland's books in hand are the
three volumes of one art-history series for Polish art
schools, *Ars longa* — and the ladder follows the series'
own progression, which is the story of art told as the
cultural story of each period, every chapter opening with
its "Tło historyczne przemian w sztuce" (historical
background of the changes in art). Volume I climbs from
what art and heritage are, through prehistory and the
ancient civilisations, into the medieval world of faith and
stone — and the ladder splits it at the age's own turn: the
ancient world first, then faith and stone. Volume II is one
level whole: the Renaissance waking, spreading north and
coming home to Kraków, then the Baroque answer to the
Reformation — Rome, Spain, France, the Dutch Republic,
Rococo, and home again to Poland's Sarmatian Baroque.
Volume III closes the ladder: the revolutionary storm and
classicism, industry and its iron, the century's revolts of
feeling, realism and the salons, and at every turn the
Polish story inside the world's — a stateless nation keeping
itself visible in paint, from Stanisław August's Warsaw to
Matejko to Young Poland. Four levels, in the books' own
order. The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced
gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Poland's own
*Ars longa* art-history textbooks for its art schools —
except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the
people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — First Steps, Prehistory and the Ancient World** — 10 lessons: Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters; From Stone Tools to Farming; Prehistoric Art on Polish Lands; Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia; Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom; Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete; Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy; Greek Art: Temples, Statues and Painted Vases; The Etruscans: Rome's Teachers; Ancient Civilisations: Rome.

2. **Level 2 — Faith and Stone: The Medieval World** — 6 lessons: Christianity: From Persecuted Sect to State Religion; The Medieval World: Byzantium and Justinian; The Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism; Pre-Romanesque and Romanesque Art in Poland; Romanesque Art: Churches of Stone; Gothic Art: Cathedrals and Towns.

3. **Level 3 — Rebirth and Splendour: Renaissance, Baroque and Rococo** — 13 lessons: The Renaissance: What It Was; The Renaissance: Its Great Figures; Mannerism: Italy's Restless Art After the Renaissance; The Renaissance Spreads North: France, Germany and the Netherlands; Poland's Renaissance: Kraków and the Jagiellonian Court; The Reformation: The Catholic Response; Baroque Rome: Art of the Counter-Reformation; Baroque Spain: Art of a Golden-Age Court; Baroque France: Versailles and the Art of Absolute Monarchy; Dutch Golden Age: Art of a Republic of Traders; Rococo: The Aristocracy's Playful Art; Baroque and Rococo Across Central Europe; Poland's Baroque: Churches and Sarmatian Culture.

4. **Level 4 — Revolution, Industry and the Nation in Art: Classicism to Young Poland** — 14 lessons: The French Revolution Breaks Out, 1789; Classicism: Art Looks Back to Rome in the Age of Revolution; Poland's Last King and Classicist Warsaw; The Industrial Revolution: Its Two Stages; Historicism and Iron Architecture in the Industrial Century; Romanticism: Art of Feeling and Nation; Realism: Painting Ordinary Life; Academic Art: The Official Salons; Poland's Painters Under Partition: Matejko and History Painting; Impressionism: Paris Paints Modern Life; Post-Impressionism: Four Rebels After the Impressionists; Symbolism and Munch: Art of Dreams and Anxiety; Art Nouveau and Secession: A Total Style for 1900; Young Poland: Art Under the Partitions.

**Totals: 43 lessons, 43 placements stocked** (Levels: Level 1: 10, Level 2: 6, Level 3: 13, Level 4: 14).
