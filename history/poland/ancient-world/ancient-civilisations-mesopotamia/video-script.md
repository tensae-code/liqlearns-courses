# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Between two rivers, the Sumerians raise the first cities and ziggurats, the Assyrians carve palace walls with kings hunting and conquering, and Babylon rebuilds in glazed blue brick — three peoples, one river plain, each leaving its power written in art.

Between two rivers — the Tigris and the Euphrates — the world's first cities rise. The Sumerians build them, and above each city they raise a ziggurat, a great stepped tower climbing toward the gods. City, temple-tower and writing all begin on this one river plain.

Then the plain changes hands, and the art changes with it. The Assyrians, a warlike people, carve their palace walls with their kings hunting lions and conquering enemies — power shown stone by stone. Last comes Babylon, rebuilt in splendour, its gates and walls faced in glazed blue brick that still shines. Three peoples, one river plain — and each one leaves its power written in its art.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
