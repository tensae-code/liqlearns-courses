# Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

Between two rivers — the Tigris and the Euphrates — the world's first cities rise. The Sumerians build them, and above each city they raise a ziggurat, a great stepped tower climbing toward the gods. City, temple-tower and writing all begin on this one river plain.

Then the plain changes hands, and the art changes with it. The Assyrians, a warlike people, carve their palace walls with their kings hunting lions and conquering enemies — power shown stone by stone. Last comes Babylon, rebuilt in splendour, its gates and walls faced in glazed blue brick that still shines. Three peoples, one river plain — and each one leaves its power written in its art.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Read each people's art as its signature: Sumer builds up, Assyria carves its victories, Babylon glazes blue.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between two rivers, the Sumerians raise the first cities and ziggurats, the Assyrians carve palace walls with kings hunting and conquering, and Babylon rebuilds in glazed blue brick — three peoples, one river plain, each leaving its power written in art. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: ziggurat, Babylon. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
