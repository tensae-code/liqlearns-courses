# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Rome

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Rome takes Greek beauty and puts it to work — concrete, vaults and aqueducts for an empire, triumphal arches that turn victories into street theatre, and portrait busts so honest they keep every wrinkle of power.

Rome looks at Greek beauty and asks a Roman question: what work can it do? The answer is an empire built in concrete. With concrete, vaults and aqueducts, Rome carries water across valleys, roofs great halls and holds its vast state together in stone and mortar — Greek forms, put to work at an imperial size.

Victory, too, is put to work. The triumphal arch turns a general's win into street theatre: a permanent arch over the road, carved with the campaign, so the triumph marches on long after the parade has passed. And Rome's portrait busts are honest to the point of rudeness — every wrinkle of power kept, the face of a real ruler with nothing smoothed away. Beauty, engineering and truth-telling: that is Rome's art.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
