# Hands-on — Greek Art: Temples, Statues and Painted Vases

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say the lesson's one thing out loud: The Greeks hunt for perfect proportion — temples tuned like music, statues that step out of stiffness into living balance, and black-and-red vases that carry the myths and the games into every Greek home. Hold that in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the scene at the heart of this lesson — f a Greek temple with even columns, a marble statue standing in easy balance beside it, and a painted vase in front — and under your drawing write the one thing in your own words. Say each part aloud as you draw it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Note each time you see it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
