# The Medieval World: Byzantium and Justinian

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

When Rome's western half falls, its eastern half lives on — for a thousand years. This is Byzantium, and its great emperor is Justinian. In his capital, Constantinople, Justinian raises Hagia Sophia, Holy Wisdom: a church crowned by an enormous dome ringed with windows, so that the dome seems to float on light, hanging in the air above the worshippers.

Inside Byzantine churches, the walls themselves preach. Mosaics on grounds of gold show Christ, the Virgin and the saints in solemn stillness, and icons — painted holy images — carry the same vision into every church and home of the Orthodox world. Heaven, made visible in gold: that is Byzantium's gift, kept alive for a millennium.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Byzantium's sign is gold light: a floating dome above, shining saints around.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rome's eastern half lives on for a thousand years — Justinian's Hagia Sophia raises a dome that seems to float on light, and gold-ground mosaics and icons make heaven visible in every church of the Orthodox world. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: Byzantium, icon. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
