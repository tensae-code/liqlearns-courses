# Video script — Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Free city-states quarrel under the same gods — Athens invents democracy, Sparta trains for war, and the Greeks' beliefs in the Olympian family give every temple and statue its subject.

Greece is not one kingdom but many free city-states, quarrelling with each other under the same sky. Two of them show the two Greek ways. Athens invents democracy — the citizens themselves ruling, speaking and voting in the assembly. Sparta chooses the opposite road: a whole city trained for war, where life is drilled like an army camp.

Above both stands one shared world: the Olympian family of gods, Zeus and his household on Mount Olympus, whose stories every Greek knows. Those beliefs are not decoration — they give every temple its god and every statue its subject. To understand Greek art, first meet the quarrelling cities and the gods they shared.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
