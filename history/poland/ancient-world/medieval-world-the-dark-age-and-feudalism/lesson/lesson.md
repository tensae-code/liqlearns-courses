# The Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism

## Do it first

Think of a story about the past that you already know — from home, from school, from your street. Say out loud who was in it, where it happened, and what changed by its end. Keep that shape in mind: this lesson's story is told the same way — people, place, the turn, and what came of it.

## The story

After Rome falls, the West breaks apart. In place of one empire stand many kingdoms, ruled by warriors, in a feudal world of lords, oaths and castles. It would be easy for everything Rome knew to be lost — and the people who stop that happening are monks: in their monasteries, books are copied, building goes on and learning stays alive through the feudal centuries.

Twice, kings try to put the pieces back together. Charlemagne, greatest of the Frankish kings, tries to rebuild an empire and a culture at once — crowned emperor, gathering scholars, reviving building and writing. After him the Ottonians follow, renewing the empire in the German lands. Warriors and monks, a broken world, and two rebuildings: that is the West before the Romanesque dawn.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Two keepers of the flame: the monk copies the book, the emperor rebuilds the stage.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: After Rome falls the West breaks into kingdoms of warriors and monks; Charlemagne tries to rebuild an empire and a culture at once, the Ottonians follow, and monasteries keep books, building and learning alive through the feudal centuries. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: feudal, Charlemagne. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
