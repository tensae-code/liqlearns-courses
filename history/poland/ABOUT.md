# About the History — Poland course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — Poland's own
*Ars longa* art-history textbooks for its art schools
(liceum sztuk plastycznych). The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Poland inside the History folder (one
course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school
grade each book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is taught as
stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of it.

Poland teaches art history as a school subject in its own
right in its art schools, and this course is built exactly
as its books teach it: every chapter opens with a "Tło
historyczne przemian w sztuce" — historical background of
the changes in art — section, and the art itself is taught
as the cultural story of each period. Every world period
chapter closes by bringing the story home to Poland, and
the course keeps that shape.

The course as built has **four levels**:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps, Prehistory and the Ancient
   World.** What art and heritage are, the road from stone
   tools to farming, prehistory on Polish lands, and the
   ancient civilisations — Mesopotamia, Egypt, Crete and
   Mycenae, Athens, Greek art, the Etruscans and Rome.
2. **Level 2 — Faith and Stone: The Medieval World.**
   Christianity's road from the catacombs to the state
   church, Byzantium, the feudal West, and Romanesque and
   Gothic stone — with Poland's first stone churches inside
   the Western story.
3. **Level 3 — Rebirth and Splendour: Renaissance, Baroque
   and Rococo.** The Renaissance in Italy, its great
   figures, Mannerism, the spread north and home to
   Jagiellonian Kraków; then the Catholic answer to the
   Reformation — Baroque Rome, Spain, France, the Dutch
   Republic, Rococo, Central Europe, and Poland's Sarmatian
   Baroque.
4. **Level 4 — Revolution, Industry and the Nation in Art:
   Classicism to Young Poland.** The revolutionary timeline
   and classicism, Stanisław August's Warsaw, industry and
   historicism, Romanticism, Realism, the academies,
   Matejko's partitioned nation, and the road through
   Impressionism and Symbolism to Young Poland.

## The way of the books (their own shape, preserved)

- **The series pattern:** each volume opens every chapter
  with a "Tło historyczne przemian w sztuce" — historical
  background of the changes in art — section, and teaches
  the art itself as the cultural story of each period.
  This course follows that order exactly: the historical
  story first, told as story, in every lesson.
- **Bringing it home:** Tom I closes each world period by
  bringing the story home — prehistoric art on Polish
  lands, pre-Romanesque art in Poland, Romanesque art in
  Poland — so Polish learners meet each world style again
  in their own soil. Tom II keeps the pattern — the world
  styles first (Italy, then the other countries), then a
  full chapter bringing each age home to Poland.
- **The double story of Tom III:** partitioned Poland has
  no state, so its chapters tell a double story — the world
  styles, and then Polish artists under three foreign
  empires using art, above all Matejko's giant history
  canvases and the Young Poland movement, to keep a
  stateless nation visible to itself.
