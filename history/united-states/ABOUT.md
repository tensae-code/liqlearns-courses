# About the History — United States course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — here, the
United States' own OpenStax survey, *U.S. History*, held in
the library as an approved-private US book. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons
are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for the United States inside the History
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation
is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by
what they prove, and each level stands on the ones before
it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The
school grade the book comes from appears only in each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is
taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came
of it.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the one
book's own chapter order:

1. **Level 1 — contact and colonies.** The three worlds meet, empires plant ranked colonies, and britain's colonies bind tight while learning to think as one people (chapters 1–4) — 6 lessons.
2. **Level 2 — revolution and the early republic.** Protest discovers an american identity, independence is won, a federal republic is built, and the young nation survives its second war with britain (chapters 5–8) — 4 lessons.
3. **Level 3 — expansion, slavery and civil war.** Mills and markets transform the north, democracy widens and removes, the nation takes half of mexico, cotton and reform collide, the compromises break, and civil war destroys slavery before reconstruction rises and falls (chapters 9–16) — 8 lessons.
4. **Level 4 — industrial america and empire.** The west is taken, corporations rise on labour's back, cities swell with migration and immigration, populists revolt, progressives reform, and the flag crosses the oceans (chapters 17–22) — 6 lessons.
5. **Level 5 — world wars, depression and the new deal.** America enters the great war late and refuses its peace, booms and back-lashes through the jazz age, crashes in 1929, rebuilds under the new deal, and fights the second world war on two oceans (chapters 23–27) — 6 lessons.
6. **Level 6 — postwar america to the twenty-first century.** Suburbs and cold war fears, civil rights and the great society, vietnam and watergate, reagan and the culture wars, and the new century's terror, crisis and hope (chapters 28–32) — 5 lessons.

All thirty-five lessons stand on a **student book** and
count in the acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the book's own shape, walked in order):

- *One nation's widening freedom:* "Begin before 1492, with
  three worlds about to meet. Watch colonies planted and
  bound to an empire, then breaking from it to invent a
  republic. Follow the republic as it industrialises,
  expands, tears itself apart over slavery and rebuilds —
  then as it industrialises again into cities, corporations
  and an overseas empire. Stand with it through two world
  wars, a depression and a New Deal, and walk its postwar
  road: suburbs and Cold War fears, civil rights marching,
  Vietnam and Watergate, Reagan and the culture wars — to a
  new century of terror, crisis and hope."
