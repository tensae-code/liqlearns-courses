# Video script — Colonial Societies in the Americas

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Between 1500 and 1700 the newcomers built ranked colonial societies in the Americas: Spain ordered its empire by rank, the Dutch and French chased furs and empire, and England's settlers — Jamestown's tobacco planters and Massachusetts' Puritans — remade Native lands into colonies that transformed everyone they touched.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The empires did not just take land; they built new social orders on it. Spain's America was a ranked society, with place and power handed out by birth and origin.

Further north, the Dutch and the French chased a different wealth: furs. Their traders and forts followed the beaver deep into Native country, and empire grew along the trade routes.

England's settlers built two famous beginnings. At Jamestown, tobacco made planting pay. In Massachusetts, Puritans built godly towns. Both remade Native lands into colonies — and the remaking transformed everyone it touched: settlers, Native peoples and the land itself.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
