# Video script — Reconstruction, 1865–1877

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Reconstruction tried to remake the South: Lincoln's lenient restoration gave way to Congress's Radical Reconstruction — amendments, Black officeholders, a new South begun — then violence, exhaustion and the compromise of 1877 collapsed it, leaving the freedpeople to Jim Crow.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The war ended; the harder question began: what would freedom mean, and who would decide? Lincoln had planned a lenient restoration — bring the South back gently. After his death, Congress took the work harder in hand.

Radical Reconstruction remade the South by law: constitutional amendments abolished slavery, made citizens of the freedpeople and gave Black men the vote. Black officeholders sat in legislatures and in Congress itself — a revolution in American democracy.

Then came the counter-attack: violence against freedpeople and their allies, a North growing exhausted, and at last the compromise of 1877, which withdrew the federal troops. Reconstruction collapsed — and the freedpeople were left to the long night of Jim Crow.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
