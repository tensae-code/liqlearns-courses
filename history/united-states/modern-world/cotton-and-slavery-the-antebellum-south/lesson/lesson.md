# Cotton and Slavery: The Antebellum South

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

One machine changed the South. The cotton gin cleaned cotton fast — and that made growing cotton, and the slavery that grew it, more profitable than ever. Cotton became king.

The book follows the kingdom honestly: the economics of cotton exports that tied the South to world markets; and beneath the wealth, the lives of the enslaved — families, labour, resistance and survival — and of free African Americans living in a slave society's shadow.

Cotton also built a planter culture that defended slavery as a way of life, and it pushed outward: filibusters — private adventurers — hunted new slave states in the Caribbean, trying to grow the kingdom by conquest.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: The gin was small; its consequence was enormous — a machine that strengthened slavery instead of ending it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The cotton gin made slavery more profitable than ever, and cotton built a whole world: its economics, the lives of enslaved and free African Americans under it, the planter culture it raised — and the filibusters who hunted new slave states in the Caribbean. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
