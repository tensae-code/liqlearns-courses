# Video script — Cotton and Slavery: The Antebellum South

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The cotton gin made slavery more profitable than ever, and cotton built a whole world: its economics, the lives of enslaved and free African Americans under it, the planter culture it raised — and the filibusters who hunted new slave states in the Caribbean.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

One machine changed the South. The cotton gin cleaned cotton fast — and that made growing cotton, and the slavery that grew it, more profitable than ever. Cotton became king.

The book follows the kingdom honestly: the economics of cotton exports that tied the South to world markets; and beneath the wealth, the lives of the enslaved — families, labour, resistance and survival — and of free African Americans living in a slave society's shadow.

Cotton also built a planter culture that defended slavery as a way of life, and it pushed outward: filibusters — private adventurers — hunted new slave states in the Caribbean, trying to grow the kingdom by conquest.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
