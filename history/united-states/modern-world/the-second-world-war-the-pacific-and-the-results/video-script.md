# Video script — The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In the Pacific, island-hopping crossed the ocean at terrible cost until the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki ended the war — and opened the atomic age the rest of the book lives in.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The Pacific war was fought across an ocean of islands. America's strategy was island-hopping: seize one island, build its airfield, leap to the next — each leap paid for at terrible cost against defenders who fought to the last.

The leaps closed on Japan itself. Rather than invade the home islands, the United States used its new and terrible weapon: atomic bombs fell on Hiroshima and on Nagasaki.

Japan surrendered, and the war ended. But the bombs did more than end a war: they opened the atomic age — the age of weapons that can end cities in a flash, the age the rest of this book, and the world, now lives in.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
