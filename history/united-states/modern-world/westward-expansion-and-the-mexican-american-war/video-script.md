# Video script — Westward Expansion and the Mexican-American War

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
As the nation moved west — Lewis and Clark crossing the continent, Missouri's admission cracking the Union's balance, Texas breaking from Mexico — the Mexican-American War of 1846–1848 took half of Mexico, leaving the unanswerable question over every new acre: free soil or slave soil?

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The young nation could not sit still. Lewis and Clark crossed the continent to the Pacific and back, mapping a west that settlers soon followed.

Every new state shook the Union's balance. When Missouri asked to enter as a slave state, the quarrel cracked open: north and south counted Senate seats like soldiers, because each new state could tip the nation's future.

Texas broke away from Mexico and joined the United States; war followed. In the Mexican-American War (1846–1848), the United States took half of Mexico's land. Victory brought an unanswerable question that poisoned everything after it: would the new lands be free soil — or slave soil?

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
