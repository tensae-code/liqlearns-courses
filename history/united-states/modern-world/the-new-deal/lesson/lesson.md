# The New Deal

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

Franklin Roosevelt took office with the banks closed and the nation frightened — and moved at once. The First New Deal threw the federal government into the fight: banks reopened under new rules, jobs programmes put the unemployed to work, farm programmes steadied the countryside. The fall stopped.

Then Roosevelt went further. The Second New Deal built the floor the country still stands on: Social Security, a pension promise for old age; labour rights, protecting unions and wages; and great works programmes that built roads, dams and parks with public hands.

The change outlasted the emergency. The federal government had taken a new place in American life — insurer, employer, referee — and it has never given that place back.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: First stop the fall, then rebuild the floor — the two New Deals in one line.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Franklin Roosevelt's New Deal stopped the fall and then went further: the First New Deal's banks, jobs and farm programmes, then the Second New Deal — Social Security, labour rights, works programmes — remaking the federal government's place in American life for good. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
