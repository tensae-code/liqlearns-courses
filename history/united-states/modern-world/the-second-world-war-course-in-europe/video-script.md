# Video script — The Second World War: Course in Europe

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Fascism and Japanese expansion drew a still-isolationist America toward a war it did not seek; Pearl Harbor ended the argument, and American power landed in North Africa, Italy and Normandy on the road to Germany's defeat.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The 1930s darkened: fascism rose in Europe, and Japan expanded by conquest in Asia. America, remembering the last war, clung to isolation — but watched, and armed, and argued.

Pearl Harbor ended the argument in a morning. Japan's attack on the Pacific fleet brought the United States into the Second World War with a united fury.

The road to Germany's defeat ran west to east: American power landed first in North Africa, then fought up Italy's hard spine, then stormed ashore in Normandy on D-Day — the great invasion that opened the road through France to Germany itself.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
