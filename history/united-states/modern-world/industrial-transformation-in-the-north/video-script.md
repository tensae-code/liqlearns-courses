# Video script — Industrial Transformation in the North

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Between 1800 and 1850 the North transformed: mills rose in the Northeast, a vibrant capitalist republic of banks and markets followed, canals and railroads stitched it together in a transportation revolution — and wage labour sorted the North into new classes.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

In the Northeast, the mills rose first — water-powered factories gathering workers under one roof to spin and weave. Around them grew a vibrant capitalist republic: banks to lend, markets to sell in, towns to feed the mills.

Then the stitching: canals cut across the land and railroads raced over it — a transportation revolution that bound farms, mills and ports into one economy.

Work itself changed. More and more people no longer farmed their own land or owned their own tools; they sold their labour for wages. Wage labour sorted the North into new classes — owners, managers, and the growing ranks of workers.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
