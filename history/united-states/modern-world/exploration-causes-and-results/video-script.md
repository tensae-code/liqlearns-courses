# Video script — Exploration: Causes and Results

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Plants, animals, germs and people poured both ways across the Atlantic in the Columbian Exchange: potatoes and maize fed the Old World, smallpox emptied the New, and the hunger for labour built the Atlantic slave system.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

Once the ships kept crossing, the two worlds began to swap their living things. This two-way pour of plants, animals, germs and people is called the Columbian Exchange.

Eastward went foods the Old World had never known — potatoes and maize among them — and they fed growing populations across Europe, Africa and Asia.

Westward came horses, cattle and crops — and germs. Smallpox and other diseases emptied Native towns and peoples who had no defence against them. And as Native labour died and the new plantations and mines hungered for workers, that hunger for labour built the Atlantic slave system, carrying enslaved Africans across the ocean in chains.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
