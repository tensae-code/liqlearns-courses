# Exploration: Causes and Results

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

Once the ships kept crossing, the two worlds began to swap their living things. This two-way pour of plants, animals, germs and people is called the Columbian Exchange.

Eastward went foods the Old World had never known — potatoes and maize among them — and they fed growing populations across Europe, Africa and Asia.

Westward came horses, cattle and crops — and germs. Smallpox and other diseases emptied Native towns and peoples who had no defence against them. And as Native labour died and the new plantations and mines hungered for workers, that hunger for labour built the Atlantic slave system, carrying enslaved Africans across the ocean in chains.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Exchange sounds fair — hold both sides: food that fed millions one way, disease and slavery the other.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Plants, animals, germs and people poured both ways across the Atlantic in the Columbian Exchange: potatoes and maize fed the Old World, smallpox emptied the New, and the hunger for labour built the Atlantic slave system. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
