# Video script — America in the 1960s: Civil Rights and Great Society

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In the 1960s, Kennedy's promise and Johnson's Great Society met the civil rights movement marching on — sit-ins, freedom rides, Birmingham, the March on Washington — while a wider generation challenged the status quo on every front.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The 1960s opened in promise: Kennedy's youth and pledge of a new frontier. When he was killed, Johnson carried the promise into law as the Great Society — a war on poverty and a rebuilt nation.

But the decade's engine was in the streets. The civil rights movement marched: students at sit-ins refusing to move, freedom riders testing the roads, Birmingham's children facing dogs and hoses, and a quarter of a million people at the March on Washington demanding the promise be kept.

And the challenge widened beyond civil rights: a whole generation contested the status quo — on war, on women's place, on authority itself. America in the 1960s was a country arguing about its future, out loud.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
