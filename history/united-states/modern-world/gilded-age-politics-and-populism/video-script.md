# Video script — Gilded Age Politics and Populism

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Gilded Age politics ran on corruption and patronage, the tariff and gold questions divided the parties — and indebted farmers revolted in the Populist movement, as the 1890s closed in strikes and social unrest.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The age glittered on top and rotted underneath — gilded, not golden. Politics ran on patronage: government jobs handed out as party rewards, and corruption skimming the public purse.

The parties fought over two great questions of money: the tariff, taxing imports to shelter industry; and gold — whether the dollar should stand on gold alone, which squeezed debtors, or on silver too.

The squeezed answered. Indebted farmers, crushed between falling prices and fixed debts, revolted in the Populist movement — a people's party demanding the system serve the producers. The 1890s closed as the revolt spread: strikes in the cities, unrest everywhere.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
