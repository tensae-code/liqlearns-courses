# Atlantic World: Rival Empires and Faiths

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

Europe's churches split. The Reformation broke the old unity of western Christianity, and rival faiths now stood behind rival kingdoms.

The quarrel did not stay in Europe. It crossed the Atlantic with the ships: where Spain had claimed the new world alone, challengers now appeared.

The Dutch, the French and the English raided Spanish treasure, traded where Spain said no trade was allowed, and planted colonies of their own. The Atlantic world became a contest — of empires, and of faiths.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Spain claimed the ocean alone; this lesson is everyone else refusing to accept that claim.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Reformation split Europe's churches, and the split crossed the ocean: Dutch, French and English challengers raided, traded and planted colonies in the Atlantic world Spain had claimed alone. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
