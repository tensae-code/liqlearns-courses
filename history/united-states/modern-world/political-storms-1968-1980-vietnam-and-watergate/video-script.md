# Video script — Political Storms, 1968–1980: Vietnam and Watergate

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From 1968 to 1980 the storms broke: identity politics fractured the old coalitions, Vietnam spiralled down and divided the nation, and Watergate brought down a president — the seventies ended with trust in government in ruins.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The old coalitions cracked. From 1968, identity politics — movements organising around race, gender and identity — fractured the parties' old alliances and redrew the map of American politics.

Abroad, the storm was Vietnam: a war that spiralled downward year by year, televised nightly, dividing the nation between those who demanded its end and those who demanded victory, and ending in failure and withdrawal.

At home, the presidency itself fell. Watergate — a break-in, a cover-up, tapes — climbed until a president resigned rather than face removal. The seventies ended with the deepest casualty named plainly: trust in government, in ruins.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
