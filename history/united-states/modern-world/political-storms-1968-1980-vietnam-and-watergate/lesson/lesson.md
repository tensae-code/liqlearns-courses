# Political Storms, 1968–1980: Vietnam and Watergate

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

The old coalitions cracked. From 1968, identity politics — movements organising around race, gender and identity — fractured the parties' old alliances and redrew the map of American politics.

Abroad, the storm was Vietnam: a war that spiralled downward year by year, televised nightly, dividing the nation between those who demanded its end and those who demanded victory, and ending in failure and withdrawal.

At home, the presidency itself fell. Watergate — a break-in, a cover-up, tapes — climbed until a president resigned rather than face removal. The seventies ended with the deepest casualty named plainly: trust in government, in ruins.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: A fractured map, a failed war, a fallen president — the casualty list ends with trust itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: From 1968 to 1980 the storms broke: identity politics fractured the old coalitions, Vietnam spiralled down and divided the nation, and Watergate brought down a president — the seventies ended with trust in government in ruins. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
