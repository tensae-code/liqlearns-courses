# Video script — Antebellum Reform and Abolition

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Second Great Awakening fired a generation of reformers: utopian communes, temperance and health reform, the abolitionist assault on slavery — and at Seneca Falls in 1848, the first organised demand for women's rights.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

A wave of preaching — the Second Great Awakening — told ordinary people their souls, and their society, could be remade. A fired-up generation believed it, and set out to reform everything.

They built utopian communes to model a perfect world. They campaigned for temperance against drink, and for health reform. Above all, abolitionists launched an assault on slavery itself — in print, in pulpits and in politics — demanding not gradual comfort but an end to the sin.

And in 1848, at Seneca Falls, women organised their own demand: the first organised call for women's rights, modelled on the Declaration itself. Reform had found its next century.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
