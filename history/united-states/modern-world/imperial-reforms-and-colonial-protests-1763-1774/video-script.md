# Video script — Imperial Reforms and Colonial Protests, 1763–1774

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Britain's war debt brought new taxes — the Stamp Act first — and the colonies answered as one: the Sons and Daughters of Liberty boycotted, the Townshend Acts and the Boston Tea Party followed, the Coercive Acts punished Massachusetts, and the First Continental Congress discovered an American identity.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

Britain won its wars for empire — and was left with the bill. To pay the war debt, London reached into the colonies with new taxes, starting with the Stamp Act, a tax on paper itself.

The colonies refused together. The Sons and Daughters of Liberty organised boycotts of British goods; when the Townshend Acts taxed imports instead, the boycotts returned. In Boston, taxed tea went into the harbour — the Boston Tea Party.

London answered with punishment: the Coercive Acts closed on Massachusetts. But punishment did what taxes could not — it united the colonies. Delegates met in the First Continental Congress, and there, arguing and planning together, they discovered something new: an American identity.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
