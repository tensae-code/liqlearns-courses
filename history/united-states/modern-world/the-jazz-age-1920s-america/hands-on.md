# Hands-on — The Jazz Age: 1920s America

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Tell the story of this lesson out loud to yourself, in order — who, where, what turned, what came of it. Hold the turn in mind as you read.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: draw the map or the scene of this story, mark the place where it turned, and say each part aloud as you place it: The 1920s boomed: prosperity mass-produced cars, radio and movies, and a new generation flouted the old rules — while Prohibition, the Ku Klux Klan's revival and the Scopes trial answered with backlash, under a Republican ascendancy governing the boom.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see its mark — a name, a date, a flag, a story told by someone older — note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
