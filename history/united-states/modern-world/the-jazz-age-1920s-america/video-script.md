# Video script — The Jazz Age: 1920s America

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The 1920s boomed: prosperity mass-produced cars, radio and movies, and a new generation flouted the old rules — while Prohibition, the Ku Klux Klan's revival and the Scopes trial answered with backlash, under a Republican ascendancy governing the boom.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

After the war, the boom. Prosperity put the nation on wheels and wires: cars mass-produced until ordinary families owned them, radio voices in every parlour, movies remaking dreams.

A new generation flouted the old rules — in dress, dance, music and manners, the Jazz Age announced that the young would live differently.

But the decade had a second face. Prohibition banned drink and bred speakeasies and gangsters instead. The Ku Klux Klan revived, marching against the new America. At the Scopes trial, science and fundamentalist faith fought over the schools. Above it all, a Republican ascendancy governed the boom — and took the credit while it lasted.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
