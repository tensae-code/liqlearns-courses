# Video script — Urbanisation and Immigration, 1870–1900

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Between 1870 and 1900 America's cities swelled faster than they could govern themselves: the African American Great Migration and new European immigration remade them, reformers fought the chaos, and a new generation of writers and thinkers reflected a changed America.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The cities exploded. Factories pulled people in faster than houses, sewers, schools or honest governments could follow — growth with growing pains.

Two great movements remade the city. From the rural South, African Americans began the Great Migration northward, seeking work and escaping Jim Crow. Across the Atlantic came new immigration from Europe, in numbers that changed whole neighbourhoods' languages, foods and faiths.

Against the chaos stood the reformers — fighting corrupt machines, crowded tenements and dirty water. And a new generation of writers and thinkers held up a mirror: their books and ideas reflected an America that had become urban, industrial and mixed.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
