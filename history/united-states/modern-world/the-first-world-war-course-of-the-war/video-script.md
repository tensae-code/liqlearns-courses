# Video script — The First World War: Course of the War

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
America clung to isolation while Europe burned, until U-boats and the Zimmermann telegram pulled it in during 1917; a new kind of home front mobilised the whole nation — and Wilson went to Paris to make the peace, then lost the fight for the League of Nations at home.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

When Europe went to war in 1914, America chose isolation: the ocean would keep it safe, and most Americans wanted no part of the slaughter.

The war reached across the ocean anyway. German U-boats sank ships with Americans aboard, and the Zimmermann telegram — Germany's secret offer to Mexico — outraged the nation. In 1917 the United States entered the war.

Mobilisation built a new kind of home front: the whole economy, press and public turned toward the war, and American troops tipped the balance in Europe. Wilson then went to Paris to make a peace of his ideals, crowned by a League of Nations — and lost the fight for it at home, where the Senate refused to join.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
