# Video script — The American West, 1840–1900

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In the West of 1840–1900, homesteaders chased free land and gold and cattle built boom economies — while Chinese immigrants and Hispanic citizens were pushed to the margins, and the United States waged a generation-long assault on American Indian life and culture, from Sand Creek to Wounded Knee.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The West filled fast. Homesteaders chased the promise of free land; gold strikes raised boom towns overnight; cattle kingdoms spread across the plains. Boom economies made and broke fortunes in a season.

But the boom had margins, and people were pushed to them: Chinese immigrants who had built railroads and mined gold faced exclusion and violence; Hispanic citizens of the conquered Southwest lost land and standing in the nation that had absorbed them.

And over it all stands the darkest line of the chapter: a generation-long assault on American Indian life and culture — massacres and broken treaties from Sand Creek to Wounded Knee, where the wars of the West ended in 1890 with the killing of Lakota men, women and children.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
