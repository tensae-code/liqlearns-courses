# Postwar America and Cold War Fears

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

The veterans came home to a boom. Jobs, houses and babies multiplied: suburbs spread around the cities, televisions entered the living room, and the American Dream — a house, a car, a rising life — seemed open to millions.

But fear lived inside the prosperity. Abroad, the Cold War froze the world into two armed camps. At home, McCarthyism hunted supposed enemies within — hearings, blacklists, ruined careers on accusation.

And underneath it all, a third story gathered force: African Americans, who had served and worked through the war, pressed their struggle for civil rights with new strength. The next decade would bring it into the open.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Dream, fear, and a gathering struggle — three weathers in one sky.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: After 1945, veterans came home to the American Dream of suburbs and televisions while the Cold War froze foreign policy and McCarthyism hunted enemies at home — and underneath the prosperity, the African American struggle for civil rights gathered force. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
