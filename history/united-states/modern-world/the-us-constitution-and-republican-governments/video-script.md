# Video script — The US Constitution and Republican Governments

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Between 1776 and 1790 the new states learned to govern themselves: Paine's Common Sense killed the king in print, the states experimented with democracy and argued over how much change the Revolution really made — and the Constitutional Convention of 1787 built a federal republic strong enough to replace the failing Articles of Confederation.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

First, the king had to die in print. Thomas Paine's pamphlet Common Sense argued, in plain words everyone could read, that monarchy itself was the problem — and thousands who read it stopped being able to imagine a king over America.

Freed states then experimented. They wrote their own constitutions, tried democracy in different strengths, and argued honestly about how much change the Revolution really made — for women, for the enslaved, for ordinary men, the answers differed.

The first national frame, the Articles of Confederation, proved too weak to hold the union. So in 1787 the Constitutional Convention built something stronger: a federal republic, power shared between the nation and the states, under a written Constitution.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
