# Video script — The Tumultuous 1850s: Road to Civil War

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The 1850s broke the Union's compromises one by one: the Compromise of 1850 only paused the quarrel, the Kansas-Nebraska Act bled Kansas and birthed the Republican Party, Dred Scott told Black Americans they had no rights a court must respect, John Brown raided Harpers Ferry — and Lincoln's election in 1860 split the Union.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The decade opened with a patch: the Compromise of 1850 settled the quarrel for the moment. It only paused it.

Then Kansas bled. The Kansas-Nebraska Act let settlers vote slavery up or down, and pro- and anti-slavery men fought a small civil war there first. Out of that fight a new party was born — the Republicans, standing against slavery's spread. The Supreme Court then spoke in Dred Scott: Black Americans, it said, had no rights a court must respect.

John Brown tried to start a slave rising by raiding the arsenal at Harpers Ferry, and failed, and hanged — a hero to some, a terrorist to others. In 1860 the nation elected Abraham Lincoln, and the Union split.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
