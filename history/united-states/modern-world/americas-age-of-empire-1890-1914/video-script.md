# Video script — America's Age of Empire, 1890–1914

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Turner's frontier thesis and Mahan's sea-power argument pushed America outward: the Spanish-American War of 1898 won an overseas empire, the Open Door reached into China, and Roosevelt's Big Stick made the Caribbean an American lake.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

Two ideas pushed America outward. Turner argued the frontier had made America — and was now closed, so the nation's energy must find new frontiers. Mahan argued that sea power makes nations great, and great nations need navies, bases and trade routes.

In 1898 the argument became war. The Spanish-American War was short, and its prize was an overseas empire: Spain's islands, from the Caribbean to the Philippines, passed to the United States.

Then the reach widened: the Open Door notes demanded an open China for American trade, and Theodore Roosevelt's Big Stick — speak softly, carry force — made the Caribbean an American lake, guarded by the US Navy.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
