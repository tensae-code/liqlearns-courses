# Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

Portugal moved first, step by step: its sailors crept down the coast of Africa, harbour by harbour, until they rounded the continent and reached India by sea.

Columbus took a different road. Sailing for Spain, he crossed the Atlantic and reached the Americas — a world Spain had not known it was looking for.

Then came the conquest. Small bands of Spanish conquistadors toppled two great empires, the Aztec and the Inca. They did not do it by numbers: they had steel weapons, horses the Americas had never seen, diseases that emptied whole towns, and Native allies who had their own reasons to fight the empires that ruled them.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Remember the order: Portugal around Africa first, Columbus across the Atlantic second, the conquistadors third.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Portugal crept down Africa's coast and rounded it to India, Columbus sailed for Spain instead and reached the Americas, and the Spanish conquistadors then toppled the Aztec and Inca empires with steel, horses, disease and Native allies. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
