# Video script — The English Empire in America, 1660–1763

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
From 1660 to 1763 the English empire in America multiplied and tightened: the Restoration colonies grew, the Glorious Revolution reached across the ocean, slavery and consumer goods bound the colonies ever tighter to Britain — and the Great Awakening, the Enlightenment and the wars for empire taught colonists to think of themselves as one people.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

After 1660, England planted more colonies and drew the old ones closer. When the Glorious Revolution changed the ruler in London, its shock waves reached America too.

Two ropes bound the colonies to Britain. One was slavery, growing deeper in the colonial economy. The other was sweeter: consumer goods — British cloth, tea and wares that colonists bought eagerly and lived by.

But the same century taught the colonists something new. The Great Awakening's preaching and the Enlightenment's ideas crossed colony lines, and the wars for empire made colonists fight side by side. Slowly, people in thirteen separate colonies began to think of themselves as one people — a thought with a future.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
