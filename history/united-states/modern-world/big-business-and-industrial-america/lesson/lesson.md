# Big Business and Industrial America

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, do this: tell out loud, as far as you can, what you already think happened in this part of America's story — who was there, where it happened, and how it ended. Do not worry about being right. This lesson will give you the story whole, and at the end you will tell it again and hear how much has changed.

## The story

The age lit up — literally. Inventors like Thomas Edison turned electricity into light and a hundred devices into businesses. Inventions did not stay in workshops: they became industries, and industries became corporations of a size the world had not seen.

The giants were built on labour: millions working long hours in mines, mills and railroads. Labour answered back — organising unions and striking, sometimes at the cost of blood, for shorter hours and a living wage.

And a new consumer culture completed the circle: the corporations' products — sewn, canned, lit and ready-made — were sold to everyone, with advertising teaching the nation to want them.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Invent, incorporate, resist, consume — the age in four verbs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between 1870 and 1900, inventors like Edison lit a new age: inventions became industries and corporations grew to giant size — built on the backs of labour, whose strikes and unions answered back — while a new consumer culture sold the products to everyone. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood that this story helps explain. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: tell the story out loud once more, in order, and notice what you can now say that you could not say at the start.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the map or scene of the story, its turn marked, and one line under it saying what came of it.
3. Teach this lesson's story to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life — a name, a date, a monument, a story an older person tells. Note it.
