# Video script — American Independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The story is told the animated-map way: who, where, what turned.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In the war of 1775–1783, Britain's law-and-order strategy misfired, Washington's army survived the early years against the odds, and the war moved south to its decision at Yorktown — while soldiers, women, loyalists and the enslaved each fought their own revolution inside the war.

Look at the map. Here is the land this story walks.

The quarrel became a war in 1775. Britain planned to crush rebellion quickly — a law-and-order strategy, treating the rising as a disturbance to be policed. It misfired: the rebellion was a people, not a riot.

George Washington's army was outnumbered and outsupplied, and its first achievement was simply survival — staying in the field, year after year, so the cause stayed alive.

The war moved south, and there it ended: at Yorktown, the British army was trapped and surrendered. But the war was never only soldiers. Women kept farms, camps and the cause running; loyalists fought for the king they still served; and enslaved people seized the chaos to run toward freedom. Each fought their own revolution inside the war.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
