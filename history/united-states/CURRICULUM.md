# History — United States course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for the United States inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school
grade the book comes from appears only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** the United States' book in hand is one —
*U.S. History* (OpenStax, Rice University), a single national survey
from before 1492 to the Obama years, told as the widening — and
repeatedly contested — meaning of American freedom. Its own
progression is the ladder: the book's 32 chapters in order, grouped
the way the country's own story divides — contact and colonies;
revolution and the early republic; expansion, slavery and civil war;
industrial America and empire; world wars, depression and the New
Deal; and postwar America to the twenty-first century. One book,
six levels — the ladder stops where the book in hand stops.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — here, the United
States' own OpenStax survey held in the library as an
approved-private US book — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Contact and Colonies** — the three worlds meet, empires plant ranked colonies, and Britain's colonies bind tight while learning to think as one people (Chapters 1–4). 6 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (6 lessons): The Americas Before 1492; Exploration: Portugal and the Sea Routes; Atlantic World: Rival Empires and Faiths; Exploration: Causes and Results; Colonial Societies in the Americas; The English Empire in America, 1660–1763.

2. **Revolution and the Early Republic** — protest discovers an American identity, independence is won, a federal republic is built, and the young nation survives its second war with Britain (Chapters 5–8). 4 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (4 lessons): Imperial Reforms and Colonial Protests, 1763–1774; American Independence; The US Constitution and Republican Governments; The Early American Republic, 1790–1820.

3. **Expansion, Slavery and Civil War** — mills and markets transform the North, democracy widens and removes, the nation takes half of Mexico, cotton and reform collide, the compromises break, and civil war destroys slavery before Reconstruction rises and falls (Chapters 9–16). 8 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (8 lessons): Industrial Transformation in the North; Jacksonian Democracy; Westward Expansion and the Mexican-American War; Cotton and Slavery: The Antebellum South; Antebellum Reform and Abolition; The Tumultuous 1850s: Road to Civil War; The American Civil War: Union Against Slavery; Reconstruction, 1865–1877.

4. **Industrial America and Empire** — the West is taken, corporations rise on labour's back, cities swell with migration and immigration, Populists revolt, Progressives reform, and the flag crosses the oceans (Chapters 17–22). 6 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (6 lessons): The American West, 1840–1900; Big Business and Industrial America; Urbanisation and Immigration, 1870–1900; Gilded Age Politics and Populism; The Progressive Movement; America's Age of Empire, 1890–1914.

5. **World Wars, Depression and the New Deal** — America enters the Great War late and refuses its peace, booms and back-lashes through the Jazz Age, crashes in 1929, rebuilds under the New Deal, and fights the Second World War on two oceans (Chapters 23–27). 6 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (6 lessons): The First World War: Course of the War; The Jazz Age: 1920s America; The Great Depression, 1929; The New Deal; The Second World War: Course in Europe; The Second World War: The Pacific and the Results.

6. **Postwar America to the Twenty-First Century** — suburbs and Cold War fears, civil rights and the Great Society, Vietnam and Watergate, Reagan and the culture wars, and the new century's terror, crisis and hope (Chapters 28–32). 5 lessons, in one title: `modern-world/` (5 lessons): Postwar America and Cold War Fears; America in the 1960s: Civil Rights and Great Society; Political Storms, 1968–1980: Vietnam and Watergate; From Cold War to Culture Wars, 1980–2000; America in the Twenty-First Century.

**Totals: 35 lessons** (Level 1: 6 · Level 2: 4 · Level 3: 8 · Level 4: 6 · Level 5: 6 · Level 6: 5 — 35 placements, all from one student book).
