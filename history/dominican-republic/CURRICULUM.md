# History — Dominican Republic course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for the Dominican Republic inside the History folder, organised by
**levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they have proved
the level, not when a school calendar says so. The school grade
each book comes from appears only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation.

**How the levels derive:** the Dominican Republic's books in
hand are five combined Ciencias Sociales student books of
MINERD's "Libro Abierto" (Open Book) project — primary Grades
3, 5 and 6, then secondary Grade 10 (4° Secundaria) and Grade
12 (6° Secundaria), all in Spanish, all COMPLETE as filed. The
placements file's book order is the teaching order, earliest
books first. Inside that order, the ladder follows the eras as
the Dominican books themselves present them: the child's own
heritage and the historian's craft first (Level 1); humanity
before writing and the first civilisations (Level 2); Europe
transformed from feudalism to the revolutions (Level 3);
America, empire and the world wars (Level 4); the national
story from annexation to occupation (Level 5); and the
national story from dictatorship to the democratic transition
(Level 6). The ladder is a recommended path, never a forced
gate.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own MINERD "Libro Abierto" Ciencias Sociales books — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.
See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **First Steps: Heritage and the Historian's Craft** — the past handed to the youngest children as heritage — great events, heritage places, heroines and heroes, the patriotic symbols and beloved popular figures — together with the historian's own craft as the older books teach it: what history is, how its writing evolved, how sources are interrogated and criticised, and how the past is cut into ages, with America and the Dominican Republic given period lines of their own: The Dominican Republic: Our History, Heroes and Symbols · Heritage: What It Is and Why It Matters · From Evidence to a Written History · Dating the Past · What History Is · Historiography: The History of History Writing — 6 lessons.

2. **Before Writing and the First Civilisations** — the long age before writing read from tools, bones and the first humans becoming human; the peopling of an empty America; and the first civilisations on the great rivers of Asia and Africa — Mesopotamia, Egypt, India, China — with Greece's city-states, Athens' democracy and Rome's rise and division: Who Studies Prehistory? · The Peopling of the Americas · From Stone Tools to Farming · The First Civilisations of Asia and Africa · Ancient Civilisations: Greece and Crete · Ancient Civilisations: Rome · Hominisation and the First Humans · Ancient Civilisations: Mesopotamia · Ancient Egypt: The Nile Kingdom · Ancient Civilisations: India · Ancient Civilisations: China · Ancient Civilisations: Athens and Democracy · Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides — 13 lessons.

3. **Europe Transformed: Feudalism to Revolutions** — Europe remade step by step: feudalism's ladder after Rome, the Renaissance's rebirth, the causes of Europe's planetary expansion, the Reformation's split, England's revolution, the founding of the United States, capitalism stirring in feudalism's cracks, both industrial revolutions, the Enlightenment crossing the Atlantic, the French Revolution, and liberalism and socialism answering the factory age: The Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism · The Renaissance: What It Was · Modern Empires · Capitalism: Private Ownership, Factories, New Classes · The Industrial Revolution: What Changed, and Why England First · Liberalism and Socialism · The Scientific Revolution and the Enlightenment · The French Revolution Breaks Out, 1789 · Exploration: Causes and Results · The Reformation: Luther and Protestantism · The English Revolution · American Independence · The Industrial Revolution: Textiles, Steam and Rail · The Industrial Revolution: Its Two Stages — 14 lessons.

4. **America, Empire and the World Wars** — the hemisphere and the world it was pulled into: Columbus's collision of two worlds, the triangular slave trade and its rebellions, piracy and smuggling in colonial Santo Domingo, Latin America's independence and its liberal–conservative century, the United States swelling westward, modern empires and the scramble for Asia and Africa, both world wars with the Americas drawn in, Russia's two revolutions of 1917, fascism and Nazism, China's revolution of 1949, and the Soviet bloc's disintegration that ends the Cold War: Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan · The Atlantic Slave Trade: Scale, Coasts and the Gun Cycle · Piracy and Smuggling in Colonial Santo Domingo · Latin American Independence · The United States Expands · The Americas in the Twentieth Century · The First World War: Course of the War · The Second World War: Course in Europe · Colonisation: The Road to the Scramble · The First World War: Causes · 1917 Twice: February and October in Russia · Fascism and Nazism: Two Dictatorships Compared · The Second World War: Causes · The Chinese Revolution, 1949 · The End of the Cold War — 15 lessons.

5. **The Dominican Republic: Annexation, Caudillos and Occupation** — the national story's hard nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: the republic annexed to Spain in 1861 and re-won in the Restoration War, the red and blue caudillo parties trading power while the republic staggered, the debts of Heureaux and Cáceres bringing first US customs control and then the marine occupation of 1916–1924 and the resistance that ended it, and the brief elected modernisation under Vásquez that followed: The Dominican Republic: Annexation and Restoration · The Dominican Republic: Caudillos and Dictators · The Dominican Republic: The US Occupation, 1916–1924 · The Dominican Republic Between Occupation and Dictatorship — 4 lessons.

6. **Dictatorship, Revolution and the Democratic Transition** — the national story's hardest and proudest modern arc: thirty-one years of Trujillo — rise, contradictory modernisation and terror, isolation and assassination — the failed transition, Bosch's 1963 constitution and its overthrow, the April 1965 constitutionalist revolution and the US intervention, Balaguer's twelve neo-authoritarian years and their contradictions, the democratic transition of 1978–1996 and the turn to a service economy, Fernández's modernisation promise, and the ideas underneath it all — the liberal–conservative argument, Hostos and the school, and two centuries of constitutions from 1844 to 2015: The Trujillo Dictatorship · The Dominican Republic After Trujillo, 1961–1965 · The Dominican Republic: The April 1965 Revolution · The Dominican Republic: Balaguer and the Democratic Transition · Dominican Thinkers: Liberalism and Hostos · The Dominican Republic: Constitutional History — 6 lessons.

**Totals: 58 lessons** (Level 1: 6 · Level 2: 13 · Level 3: 14 · Level 4: 15 · Level 5: 4 · Level 6: 6 — 97 placements stocked, all on student books; 21 lessons carry sub lessons; no lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only — every book in this course is a student book, as filed in ABOUT.md).
