# Ancient Civilisations: Rome Divides

## Do it first

Think of one place, person or event from your own life in the Dominican Republic that connects to this lesson's story: The republic gave way to emperors, and the empire strained and divided. Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

Rome's second act is strain. The republic gave way to emperors — Augustus first, then a line of rulers good and disastrous — and the empire, grown too vast to hold from one throne, strained and divided into West and East. The Grade 10 book teaches the imperial phase as the necessary sequel to conquest: what an empire does to the republic that built it, and how even Rome could be too big to govern whole.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The republic gave way to emperors, and the empire strained and divided. Give one real example: An emperor's statue towers over a forum where republican debate once rang — and far away, the over-stretched empire splits into East and West. and name the lesson's key words: Roman Empire, Emperors, Division. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
