# Video script — The Medieval World: The Dark Age and Feudalism

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
After Rome fell, a rural, fragmented Europe organised itself around land and loyalty: castles, lords and peasants bound in the feudal ladder.

When Rome's empire fell in the West, Europe broke into pieces. The Grade 6 book introduces the Middle Ages and its stages: castles, lords and peasants ordering a fragmented Europe, each locality defending and feeding itself. It was a different civilisation from Rome's — rural, local, and held together by personal bonds instead of imperial law.
The Grade 10 book tells the same gestation from the start: after Rome, a rural, fragmented Europe organises itself around land and loyalty. With no emperor to protect them, people commend themselves to whoever can — and a whole society grows from those bargains.
That society has a name and a shape: the feudal system. Lords, vassals and serfs are bound in a ladder of obligations — land given for service, protection given for labour, each rung owing the one above and sheltering the one below. Learn the ladder and you can read a thousand years of European history.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the Dominican Republic.
