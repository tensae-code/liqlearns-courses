# Video script — From Evidence to a Written History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Before the past can be told, its traces must be interrogated: sources are questioned and criticised, and helper sciences are called in, before a claim is believed.

History begins with detective work. The Grade 5 book opens its history unit by teaching sources: the traces the past left behind — writings, objects, buildings, memories. Before telling any story, the class learns to interrogate those traces: who left this, when, why, and what might it be hiding? A history built on questioned sources is a history that can be trusted.
The Grade 10 book raises the same craft to a science. Sources are now criticised formally — tested for authenticity and bias — and the historian calls in helper sciences: archaeology for things, geography for places, chronology for dates, and others. Only after that criticism does a claim earn belief. The lesson's turn: evidence first, story second — in that order, always.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the Dominican Republic.
