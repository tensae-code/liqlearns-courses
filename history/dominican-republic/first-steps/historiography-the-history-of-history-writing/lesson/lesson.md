# Historiography: The History of History Writing

## Do it first

Think of one place, person or event from your own life in the Dominican Republic that connects to this lesson's story: From chronicles to scientific history, the way of writing the past keeps changing. Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

History has a history. After defining the science, the Grade 10 book follows its later evolution: from chronicles that listed events year by year, through great narrative histories, to scientific history that criticises sources and argues from evidence. The way of writing the past keeps changing because each age asks the past different questions. To know historiography is to read every history book — including this course's own books — with wiser eyes.

## Say it to your companion

Give one real example: A medieval chronicler lists kings year by year; a modern historian questions documents in an archive — the same past, written two different ways. and name the lesson's key words: Historiography, Chronicles. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
