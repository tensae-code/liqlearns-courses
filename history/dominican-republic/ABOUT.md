# About the History — Dominican Republic course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Ciencias Sociales books of the Ministerio de Educación
de la República Dominicana (MINERD), "Proyecto Libro Abierto"
(Open Book Project), in Spanish. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for the Dominican Republic inside the
History folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance. History is
taught as stories: who, where, what turned, and what came of
it. Legends and religious-history stories are told as the
books tell them, as plot summaries in our own words.

The course as built has **six levels**, following the books'
own progression as filed:

1. **Level 1 — First Steps: Heritage and the Historian's
   Craft.** The past as a Grade 3 child's inheritance —
   events, heritage places, heroines and heroes, symbols —
   and the craft of history itself: what it is, how its
   writing evolved, sources criticised, periods cut.
2. **Level 2 — Before Writing and the First Civilisations.**
   Prehistory, hominisation, the peopling of the Americas,
   and the river civilisations — Mesopotamia, Egypt, India,
   China, Greece, Athens and Rome.
3. **Level 3 — Europe Transformed: Feudalism to
   Revolutions.** The feudal ladder, the Renaissance,
   expansion's causes, the Reformation, the English and
   American and French revolutions, capitalism and both
   industrial revolutions, liberalism and socialism.
4. **Level 4 — America, Empire and the World Wars.** The
   collision of two worlds, the slave trade, colonial piracy,
   Latin American independence, US expansion, imperialism,
   both world wars, 1917, fascism, China 1949 and the Cold
   War's end.
5. **Level 5 — The Dominican Republic: Annexation,
   Caudillos and Occupation.** Annexation and Restoration,
   the red and blue caudillos, the road to the 1916–1924 US
   occupation and the resistance to it, and the Vásquez
   breathing space.
6. **Level 6 — Dictatorship, Revolution and the Democratic
   Transition.** Trujillo's thirty-one years, the failed
   transition and Bosch, April 1965, Balaguer's twelve years,
   the democratic transition, Hostos and the thinkers, and
   the constitutions from 1844 to 2015.

All 58 of the 58 lessons stand on **student books** and
count in the acceptance reckoning below; there are no
teacher guides in this course's filing, and no lesson stands
on teacher-guide evidence only.

## The way of the books

The Dominican books teach history twice over, and this
course keeps both tellings. Primary school opens with the
child's own ground and heritage (Grade 3), introduces
history as a science of sources and periods (Grade 5), and
bolts a full world sweep to a detailed national second half
(Grade 6). Secondary school then tells the world story
straight, through source analysis (Grade 10), and finishes
with almost pure national history — occupation, Trujillo,
the democratic struggles, Hostos and the constitutions —
taught with debates, memory interviews and round tables
(Grade 12). Where two books stock the same lesson, the
lesson keeps both parts in file order: the younger telling
and the older telling stand side by side, and the learner
meets the same story twice at two depths, as Dominican
children themselves do.
