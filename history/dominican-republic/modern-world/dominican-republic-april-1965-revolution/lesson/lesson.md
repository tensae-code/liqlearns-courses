# The Dominican Republic: The April 1965 Revolution

Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unidad 8 Historia dominicana desde 1965: Revolución de Abril de 1965

In April 1965 the argument over the fallen constitution turned to arms. The Grade 6 book tells the revolution: constitutionalist soldiers and civilians rose to restore Juan Bosch and the 1963 constitution, seized much of the capital, and faced the military establishment across the Ozama river. For a moment, the constitution had an army.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unidad 3 La Revolución Constitucionalista

The Grade 12 book names the movement by its own word — the Constitutionalist Revolution — and teaches its demand exactly: not a new regime of the gun, but the restoration of the 1963 constitution and its elected president. Civilians were armed and distributed weapons; a revolution fought for a document is a rare thing in history, and the book marks it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unidad 3 Intervención militar estadounidense y negociaciones

Then the United States intervened again. US troops landed — tens of thousands — and the revolution, contained and negotiated, was steered into elections instead of victory. The Grade 12 book teaches the intervention and the negotiations together: April's fighters did not win their constitution back, but they made a simple return to the old order impossible, and the elections of 1966 came out of their stand.
