# Exploration: Columbus, Vespucci and Magellan

## Do it first

Think of one place, person or event from your own life in the Dominican Republic that connects to this lesson's story: European ships reached America and two worlds collided, changing both forever. Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

At the end of the fifteenth century, European ships crossed the Atlantic and reached America — Columbus's voyages, the coasts Vespucci sailed and named, and Magellan's fleet that went on to circle the whole Earth. The Dominican book calls what followed a collision of two worlds: peoples who had never known of each other met, and neither world would ever be the same — new foods, new animals and new faiths one way; conquest, disease and a shattered world the other.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: European ships reached America and two worlds collided, changing both forever. Give one real example: Ships from Spain cross an ocean their maps left blank and land in a Caribbean world already full of peoples. and name the lesson's key words: Columbus, Vespucci, Magellan, Encounter of two worlds. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
