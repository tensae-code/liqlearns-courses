# 1917 Twice: February and October in Russia

## Do it first

Think of one place, person or event from your own life in the Dominican Republic that connects to this lesson's story: Twice in one year Russia rose — first against the tsar, then for the soviets. Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

Russia rose twice in 1917. In February, war, hunger and collapse brought crowds onto Petrograd's streets and the tsar — three hundred years of Romanov rule — fell in days. The provisional government that replaced him kept the war and lost the country; in October, the Bolsheviks seized power for the soviets, promising peace, land and bread. The Grade 10 book teaches the two revolutions separately because they are different events with different victors — the second devoured the first.

## Say it to your companion

Give one real example: In one year, two revolutions: crowds topple a three-hundred-year tsardom in February, and in October the soviets seize power in its place. and name the lesson's key words: Russian Revolution 1917, February and October. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
