# The Industrial Revolution: What Changed, and Why England First

## Do it first

Think of one place, person or event from your own life in the Dominican Republic that connects to this lesson's story: Factories and steam remade work, cities and the balance of the world — and it began in England. Write what the connection is, and how you could check it.

In the later eighteenth century, production itself changed nature. Factories gathered workers under one roof, steam replaced muscle and water as power, and the Grade 6 book follows the consequences: work, cities and the balance of the world are remade together. Industrialising first — England first — meant ruling the century; the book makes pupils see the revolution whole, not as a parade of inventions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Factories and steam remade work, cities and the balance of the world — and it began in England. Give one real example: A steam engine drives a row of looms in a mill town, while workers pour in from the countryside and chimneys multiply. and name the lesson's key words: Industrial Revolution, Factories, Steam. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
