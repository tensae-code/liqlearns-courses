# Video script — The Dominican Republic After Trujillo, 1961–1965

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
The tyrant fell, a council steered the country to elections, Juan Bosch won and wrote a democratic constitution — and was overthrown within months; democracy's first attempt collapsed into coups.

When the tyrant fell in 1961, nobody knew how to be a democracy. The Grade 6 book tells the transition: a Council of State steered the country through 1961–62, holding the ring between the fallen regime's family and the exiles returning, and prepared the first free elections in a generation.
The voters chose Juan Bosch. In 1963 his government produced a democratic constitution — social rights, limits on power, land and dignity written into law — and the Grade 6 book calls it his rise and fall in one breath: within months, in September 1963, the military overthrew him. A constitution, however democratic, needs defenders with more than paper.
The Grade 12 book sums the whole attempt bleakly: the democratic and revolutionary struggles of 1961–1965 begin with a failed transition and a coup order — triumvirate, resistance, conspiracy. Democracy's first attempt collapsed into coups, and the argument over Bosch's fallen constitution would soon be settled with guns.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the Dominican Republic.
