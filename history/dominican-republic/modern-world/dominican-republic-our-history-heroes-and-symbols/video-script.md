# Video script — The Dominican Republic: Our History, Heroes and Symbols

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
A nation's past is a treasure its children inherit: its great events, its heroines and heroes, its flag, shield and anthem, and the beloved figures who carry its culture.

The Dominican books begin with a gift. The great events of the country's past — the struggles, the foundings, the restorations — are not presented as a list to memorise but as a treasure handed down: this happened, it is yours, it forms part of who you are. A child who receives the past as heritage carries it differently from a child who only studies it: the story belongs to them before they can recite it.
The treasure also has faces. The nation's heroines and heroes — the women and men who founded, defended and restored the republic — are held up as an example of honour. Children are not only told what these people did; they are asked to honour the legacy: courage, sacrifice and love of country, carried forward as a duty.
Every day at school the treasure is sung and raised. The flag, the shield and the anthem are the patriotic symbols, and the book teaches them as identity: the flag's colours, the shield's Bible and cross, the anthem's words tell every Dominican child who they are and what their people have been through to be a nation.
And the culture has beloved carriers of its own. Popular figures — musicians, ballplayers, storytellers and artists the whole country knows — carry Dominican culture in music, sport and story. They are not in the constitutions, but they are in the people's heart, and the book names them as part of the inheritance too.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the Dominican Republic.
