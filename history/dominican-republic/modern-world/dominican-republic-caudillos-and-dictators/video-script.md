# Video script — The Dominican Republic: Caudillos and Dictators

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Red and blue caudillo parties traded power while the republic staggered.

After the Restoration, the republic was ruled by caudillos — strongmen with personal followings — organised in two rival parties the country knew by colours: the rojos (reds) and the azules (blues). Governments rose and fell in quick succession, none more famous than the six-year government that tried to hold power longer than the pattern allowed. The book's teaching is structural: while power belonged to men and colours instead of institutions, the republic staggered from revolution to revolution.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the Dominican Republic.
