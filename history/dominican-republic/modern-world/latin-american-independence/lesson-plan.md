# Lesson plan — Latin American Independence

**Course:** History — Dominican Republic · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Latin American Independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — America, Empire and the World Wars (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colony after colony broke from Spain and invented new republics — then spent a century fighting over what independence should mean, between liberals and conservatives.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — America, Empire and the World Wars (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unidad 6 América y el Caribe en el siglo XIX: Independencia de los pueblos de América Latina** — colony after colony breaks from Spain and invents new republics.
2. **Unidad 6 Revolución y restauraciones; liberales y conservadores** — the new nations split between liberals and conservatives and fight over the republic's soul.
3. **Unidad 5 La independencia en Hispanoamérica: antecedentes y procesos del siglo XIX** — Spanish America rises, and a continent of new nations is born in war.
4. **Unidad 5 América durante el siglo XIX: procesos revolucionarios latinoamericanos** — the new republics spend the century fighting over what independence should mean.
5. **Unidad 10 La independencia de América Latina** — Spanish and Portuguese America break free in a generation of wars.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Colony after colony broke from Spain and invented new republics — then spent a century fighting over what independence should mean, between liberals and conservatives.
2. Give one real example of it from the lesson: Across Spanish America, creole leaders raise armies, proclaim republics — and then argue for a hundred years over how those republics should be run.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the story itself and the learner's own life in the Dominican Republic, as the books themselves work.
