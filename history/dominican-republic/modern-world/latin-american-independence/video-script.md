# Video script — Latin American Independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
Colony after colony broke from Spain and invented new republics — then spent a century fighting over what independence should mean, between liberals and conservatives.

In the early nineteenth century, Spanish America rose. Colony after colony broke from Spain in wars of independence and invented new republics where kingdoms of the empire had stood. The Grade 5 book tells it as a continental story: peoples across America and the Caribbean claiming the right to rule themselves.
Winning independence was only the first fight. The new nations split between liberals, who wanted reform, rights and open trade, and conservatives, who wanted order, church and tradition — and they fought over the republic's soul in revolutions, restorations and civil wars. The Grade 5 book names this struggle as the century's pattern.
The Grade 6 book returns to the story with its causes: the Enlightenment's ideas, the example of other revolutions, and creole anger at being shut out of power in their own lands. Spanish America rises, and a continent of new nations is born in war — the book's own summary of the age.
And the Grade 6 book follows the new republics through the nineteenth century: revolutionary processes, caudillos, constitutions written and torn up. The century after independence was not peace but argument — over what independence should mean, fought out in every new nation.
The Grade 10 book closes the arc: Spanish and Portuguese America break free in a generation of wars, Brazil taking its own path beside the Spanish republics. A whole hemisphere changes its rulers within a few decades — one of the great turnings of modern history.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the Dominican Republic.
