# Video script — The Trujillo Dictatorship

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. History is told as a story here — animated-map style: who, where, what turned, and what came of it, in plain words.

---

This lesson tells one story.
For thirty-one years one man owned the country and crushed every right — rising from the occupation's constabulary, modernising contradictorily while opponents were jailed, exiled or killed, until isolation and sanctions ended with his assassination in 1961.

The Grade 6 book gives the regime in one clear line: for thirty-one years, 1930 to 1961, one man owned the country and crushed every right — human rights violated as a system, not an accident. Primary leavers meet Trujillo whole before any detail: a dictatorship measured in a human lifetime.
The Grade 12 book then tells the rise and consolidation: in 1930 the occupation's constabulary chief seized the state, fraud and force made him president, and Rafael Leónidas Trujillo built a personal regime — the army his, the party his, the country's wealth flowing through his family's hands. A dictatorship is not just a strong government; it is a state turned into one man's property.
The regime modernised — contradictorily. Roads, ports, monuments and a renamed capital rose; the Grade 12 book holds that building in one hand and the other hand's work in the other: opponents jailed, exiled or killed, a press gagged, a people watched. Modernisation under Trujillo was real, and so was the terror; the book refuses to let either cancel the other.
The end came in isolation. By the late 1950s the regime was sanctioned and shunned abroad, and at home the conspiracy grew. In May 1961, on a night road outside the capital, Trujillo was assassinated. Thirty-one years of one-man rule ended with gunfire, and a country that had forgotten free politics had to relearn it at once.

Now you. Say the story back, in your own words. Then go and do the lesson's Do It Yourself — today, at home, at school, in the Dominican Republic.
