## Why the common good matters

Mixed placement. The manners part: no one can live a good life separated alone — our private wants are often the same as others', and shared values create unity, a common feeling, satisfaction and happiness; standing for the common good also needs participation, cooperation, mutual support and citizens who take responsibility, and striving for it is called a patriotic virtue of citizens.

## Peace, law and rights stand or fall together

The civics part is explicit in the reading: without police, courts and a defence force there could be no peace, no rule of law and no respect for human rights — instead disorder, social turmoil and destruction would spread — and the chapter gives the common good a world scope: the peoples of the world must cooperate for world peace, a clean environment, equality and justice.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Act it, don't just say it. A value you have practised once in a real moment — at home, at school, in your street — is yours in a way a value you have only read about is not.
