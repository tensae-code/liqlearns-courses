# Keeping heritage safe — teach, record, watch, protect by law, belong

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's act once with what you already know: Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed. Keep what you did in mind — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

Protection means keeping a thing as it is — guarding its safety, original identity, size, shape, colour, character and meaning. The ways, in the book's order: first, awareness — teach the public what heritage is worth, what can harm it and how its safety is kept, including putting heritage into the school curriculum and teaching through radio and television; second, records — keep information on each heritage item, in digital and paper archives, saying what it holds, when and from what and why and by whom it was made; third, continuing and periodic monitoring of the heritage and its site's history and condition; fourth, legal frameworks that protect it; fifth, let local people visit for a small fee so they feel ownership of the heritage.
