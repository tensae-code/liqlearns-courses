# Hands-on — Keeping heritage safe — teach, record, watch, protect by law, belong

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Protection means keeping a thing as it is — guarding its safety, original identity, size, shape, colour, character and meaning. The ways, in the book's order: first, awareness — teach the public what heritage is worth, what can harm it and how its safety is kept, including putting heritage into the school curriculum and teaching through radio and television; second, records — keep information on each heritage item, in digital and paper archives, saying what it holds, when and from what and why and by whom it was made; third, continuing and periodic monitoring of the heritage and its site's history and condition; fourth, legal frameworks that protect it; fifth, let local people visit for a small fee so they feel ownership of the heritage.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
