## Why heritage matters

Heritage has political, economic and social benefits. First, it gives factual information about our history, civilisation and development, and builds Ethiopia's image worldwide: through it the country is known, in the book's claims, as the origin of mankind, a civilisation more than 5,000 years old, owner of an ancient civilisation with its own alphabet and calendar, and the one African country never colonised; knowing our past teaches our shared values, unity and differences, successes and failures. Second, economically: heritage attracts visitors and brings the country income, lifts hotels and transport, creates jobs for guides, drivers and museum workers, and draws foreign investors.

## Identity, history and livelihood

Third, socially: it gives the people a feeling of ownership and pride, promotes culture and creates work. A classroom activity compares the heritages pictured on the new Ethiopian 100-birr note.
