# Video script — Why heritage matters — who we are, and work for our people

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The lesson is text-first: this script says nothing the lesson text does not already teach.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Heritage has political, economic and social benefits. First, it gives factual information about our history, civilisation and development, and builds Ethiopia's image worldwide: through it the country is known, in the book's claims, as the origin of mankind, a civilisation more than 5,000 years old, owner of an ancient civilisation with its own alphabet and calendar, and the one African country never colonised; knowing our past teaches our shared values, unity and differences, successes and failures.

Heritage has political, economic and social benefits. First, it gives factual information about our history, civilisation and development, and builds Ethiopia's image worldwide: through it the country is known, in the book's claims, as the origin of mankind, a civilisation more than 5,000 years old, owner of an ancient civilisation with its own alphabet and calendar, and the one African country never colonised; knowing our past teaches our shared values, unity and differences, successes and failures. Second, economically: heritage attracts visitors and brings the country income, lifts hotels and transport, creates jobs for guides, drivers and museum workers, and draws foreign investors.

Third, socially: it gives the people a feeling of ownership and pride, promotes culture and creates work. A classroom activity compares the heritages pictured on the new Ethiopian 100-birr note.

Remember it this way: Heritage has political, economic and social benefits. Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
