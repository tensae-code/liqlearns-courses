# Hands-on — Kinds of heritage — things we can touch and things we carry in song

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
One activity has you research two intangible heritages from foods, drinks, dress styles and festival-day practices.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Heritage divides by its outer form and purpose into tangible and intangible. Tangible heritage is what can be seen and touched; it divides again into movable heritage — coins, writings, clothes and means of transport, which can be carried from place to place — and immovable heritage, whose base is tied to the land: monuments, castles, graves, mosques, churches, palaces and bridges.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
