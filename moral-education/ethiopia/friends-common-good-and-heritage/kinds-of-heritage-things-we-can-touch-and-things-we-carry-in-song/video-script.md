# Video script — Kinds of heritage — things we can touch and things we carry in song

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The lesson is text-first: this script says nothing the lesson text does not already teach.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Heritage divides by its outer form and purpose into tangible and intangible. Tangible heritage is what can be seen and touched; it divides again into movable heritage — coins, writings, clothes and means of transport, which can be carried from place to place — and immovable heritage, whose base is tied to the land: monuments, castles, graves, mosques, churches, palaces and bridges.

Heritage divides by its outer form and purpose into tangible and intangible. Tangible heritage is what can be seen and touched; it divides again into movable heritage — coins, writings, clothes and means of transport, which can be carried from place to place — and immovable heritage, whose base is tied to the land: monuments, castles, graves, mosques, churches, palaces and bridges.

Intangible heritage is glossed in the key words as songs, oral literature and religious festivals. One activity has pupils research two intangible heritages from foods, drinks, dress styles and festival-day practices.

Remember it this way: Heritage divides by its outer form and purpose into tangible and intangible. Tangible heritage is what can be seen and touched; it divides again into movable heritage — coins, writings, clothes and means of transport, which can be carried from place to place — and immovable heritage, whose base is tied to the land: monuments, castles, graves, mosques, churches, palaces and bridges.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
