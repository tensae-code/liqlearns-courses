# Hands-on — Honesty — no lying, no cheating, no stealing

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Honesty is defined as not even thinking of lying, cheating or stealing — the opposite of lying, which is knowingly saying what is false. The reading goes on in the guide's own adult register: stealing is taking what is not the thief's; cheating breaks the law and is kin to lying; lies differ in weight — from the "white lie" to lies that wreck social dealings — and the reading warns that even a lie told for a good end remains a lie, works through why people lie (to escape difficulty, or to gain from the lie), and notes that many pupils cheat or copy.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
