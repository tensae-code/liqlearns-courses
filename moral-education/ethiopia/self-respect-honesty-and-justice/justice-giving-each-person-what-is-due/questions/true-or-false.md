# Justice — giving each person what is due — true or false

Say true or false, and say why.

**1.** This statement is the lesson's one thing: Justice is defined as giving every person what is due to him or her. The reading places the idea in a long line — from Plato's Republic to John Rawls's Theory of Justice, justice is presented as the central pillar of morality — and distinguishes justice from fairness (ሚዛናዊነት): justice is chiefly the standard or measure of rightness, while fairness is the capacity to judge without putting one's own feeling or interest into the judgement.
**Answer: True** — it states the lesson's teaching in full.

**2.** A learner who lands on this lesson directly must finish every earlier level before starting it.
**Answer: False** — earlier levels are recommended first, never a forced gate; every lesson stands alone.

Difficulty: 1 = direct recall; 2 = applying the course's direct-entry rule.
