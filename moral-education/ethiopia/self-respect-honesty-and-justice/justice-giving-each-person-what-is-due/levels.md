# Justice — giving each person what is due — depth

This lesson's parts are distinct and sequential, so they stand as its sub lessons (2026-11-09 ruling: sub lessons only where a lesson genuinely has distinct sequential parts). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files.

Pass mark: Justice is defined as giving every person what is due to him or her. The reading places the idea in a long line — from Plato's Republic to John Rawls's Theory of Justice, justice is presented as the central pillar of morality — and distinguishes justice from fairness (ሚዛናዊነት): justice is chiefly the standard or measure of rightness, while fairness is the capacity to judge without putting one's own feeling or interest into the judgement. — shown in the learner's own words and in the lesson's closing act done in a real situation.
