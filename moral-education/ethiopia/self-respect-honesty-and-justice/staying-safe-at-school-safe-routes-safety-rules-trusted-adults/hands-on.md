# Hands-on — Staying safe at school — safe routes, safety rules, trusted adults

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Under the heading "staying safe" (1.2 in the body, 1.5 in the chapter-head contents) the guide prints a six-point reading, addressed to parents and school staff, summarised in substance: learn the school's emergency procedures and keep emergency plans and phone numbers where they are posted; know the routes children travel to and from school, including alternative routes if a road is blocked; know and follow the school's security rules — signing in when visiting, being accompanied inside buildings, carrying a visitor pass — and set children an example by following them; talk with your child about safety, say what to do if he or she feels unsafe (find a teacher, call 995 — the police number as printed in the reading — reach a trusted neighbour, and know how to reach you); tell school staff about a child's health conditions, food allergies, disability or bullying so staff can protect the child; and talk with school leaders about what you can contribute to safety, such as parents watching before and after school in coordination with neighbours.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
