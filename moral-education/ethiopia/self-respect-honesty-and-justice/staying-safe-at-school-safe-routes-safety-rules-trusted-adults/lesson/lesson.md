# Staying safe at school — safe routes, safety rules, trusted adults

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's act once with what you already know: Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed. Keep what you did in mind — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

Under the heading "staying safe" (1.2 in the body, 1.5 in the chapter-head contents) the guide prints a six-point reading, addressed to parents and school staff, summarised in substance: learn the school's emergency procedures and keep emergency plans and phone numbers where they are posted; know the routes children travel to and from school, including alternative routes if a road is blocked; know and follow the school's security rules — signing in when visiting, being accompanied inside buildings, carrying a visitor pass — and set children an example by following them; talk with your child about safety, say what to do if he or she feels unsafe (find a teacher, call 995 — the police number as printed in the reading — reach a trusted neighbour, and know how to reach you); tell school staff about a child's health conditions, food allergies, disability or bullying so staff can protect the child; and talk with school leaders about what you can contribute to safety, such as parents watching before and after school in coordination with neighbours.

## The school-safety reading

Teach the child-facing core: know the safe routes, follow the school's safety rules, and tell a trusted adult.
