# Staying safe at school — safe routes, safety rules, trusted adults — true or false

Say true or false, and say why.

**1.** This statement is the lesson's one thing: Under the heading "staying safe" (1.2 in the body, 1.5 in the chapter-head contents) the guide prints a six-point reading, addressed to parents and school staff, summarised in substance: learn the school's emergency procedures and keep emergency plans and phone numbers where they are posted; know the routes children travel to and from school, including alternative routes if a road is blocked; know and follow the school's security rules — signing in when visiting, being accompanied inside buildings, carrying a visitor pass — and set children an example by following them; talk with your child about safety, say what to do if he or she feels unsafe (find a teacher, call 995 — the police number as printed in the reading — reach a trusted neighbour, and know how to reach you); tell school staff about a child's health conditions, food allergies, disability or bullying so staff can protect the child; and talk with school leaders about what you can contribute to safety, such as parents watching before and after school in coordination with neighbours.
**Answer: True** — it states the lesson's teaching in full.

**2.** A learner who lands on this lesson directly must finish every earlier level before starting it.
**Answer: False** — earlier levels are recommended first, never a forced gate; every lesson stands alone.

Difficulty: 1 = direct recall; 2 = applying the course's direct-entry rule.
