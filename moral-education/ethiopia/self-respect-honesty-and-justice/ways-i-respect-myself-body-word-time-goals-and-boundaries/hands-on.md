# Hands-on — Ways I respect myself — body, word, time, goals and boundaries

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: The guide prints a numbered nineteen-point reading on respecting yourself, summarised here in substance: respect your own beliefs and values and do not change them to please anyone; respect your body — care for it, live healthily, make exercise a way of life; respect your surroundings — clean and beautify them; respect your wishes — share your real feelings openly; respect your word — be firm in honesty; respect your personal boundaries — know them and be able to say no; respect your fears — face them rather than fleeing; respect your failures — treat them as feedback and learn from them; respect your time — use it effectively; respect the advice of your elders; respect your weaknesses — take them as chances to grow; respect your goals and dreams — list them in order and start from the first; respect your feelings — do not stay in work or relationships that harm you; respect your opportunities — say yes to new experiences; put your own needs first before serving others' wishes; cut off people who wish you ill and use you; respect your actions — act with a good attitude; respect your responsibilities and accountability; and respect your thoughts — let them range, then train them toward the positive.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
