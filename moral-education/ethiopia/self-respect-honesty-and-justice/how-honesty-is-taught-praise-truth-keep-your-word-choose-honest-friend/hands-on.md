# Hands-on — How honesty is taught — praise truth, keep your word, choose honest friends

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: The guide prints ten ways, summarised in substance: (1) pay for truth with strong praise and hugs rather than rushing to punish or scold — praise builds trust; (2) teach children to speak truth and to keep silent rather than speak empty or false words; (3) speak the hard truth — a child whose friend cheats on a test should counsel the friend and, if refused, tell the teacher; (4) be the example — "a small habit of lying leads to a big habit of lying", and children who hear even a harmless white lie conclude lying is acceptable; (5) do not set children up to lie with trap questions — if you found the juice spilled on the sofa, say "wipe it up" instead of asking who did it; (6) take action when lying is caught — lying must have a consequence because it is wrong; (7) correct mistakes — ask why the child lied, explain how lying destroys trust and how hard trust is to regain, and show what else could have been done; (8) keep your word — teach children to treat their word as a sacred word; (9) care for others' property — borrowing is good, but what is borrowed must always be returned; (10) seek honest friends — "show me your friend and I will tell you who you are".

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
