# Hands-on — Cooperation — together for a common benefit

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You and a companion compare working together with working alone: together brings sharing new ideas and doing hard work easily, while working alone keeps its own uses — studying for an exam, doing simple tasks.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Cooperation is defined in the activity answers as a group of people working together in unity for a common benefit, formed of two or more people.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
