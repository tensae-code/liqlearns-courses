# Hands-on — Why healthy relationships keep us well

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: The benefits are listed in the answers: people with healthy social relationships and friends feel belonging and purpose; when people stand beside you in joy and in sorrow, your level of worry falls; a healthy social life refreshes the mind, raises the body's power to resist disease, and even lengthens life; friends support each other in hard times; it makes the value one person gives another grow; it helps people live in unity; it helps an individual's physical, social and other behaviours develop; and in general it improves a society's social, economic and political affairs.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
