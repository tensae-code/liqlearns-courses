# About the Moral Education — Ethiopia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government teacher guides —
Ethiopia's own Ministry of Education Moral Education teacher
guides for Grades 1–6. The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the guides'.

## This course stands on teacher guides alone — said plainly

This course is built from the six Moral Education teacher
guides **alone**. No Moral Education student book is in
hand for any grade — no student book for this subject
exists in any record we hold — and nothing in this course
dresses a guide up as a student book: every lesson's
`lesson/sources.md` carries its book as a teacher guide,
with its printed pages, marked "(teacher guide)", with the
plain line that the lesson stands on the teacher guide
alone.

The build stands on the owner's ruling of 2026-11-24:
teacher guides CAN be used to create courses — Ethiopian
teacher guides explain the student textbook, so a guide
carries the teaching. The lessons here are built from the
guides' own explanations, content summaries, teaching
notes and printed answer keys.

**The guides defer to a student book that is not in hand.**
Again and again the guides point at the student book for
the stories, the pictures and the activities' exact
wording. That student book's teaching reaches this course
only as the guides testify to it. Each guide's closing
syllabus is the only evidence of the missing student
course's shape, and the syllabuses stand in this ABOUT as
shape evidence (see "The syllabuses" below) — they are
never presented as student-book content.

**Why this is its own course:** Moral Education is a
standalone subject in Ethiopia's own curriculum, with its
own books for Grades 1–6. Under the owner's standing rule,
a subject that exists as its own book gets its own course
and is never silently folded into another course. Before
this build, these guides' teaching stood only as the named
opening levels (Levels 1–12, "morals and manners") and the
Level 13 civics strand of the Civics and Citizenship —
Ethiopia course; that course **stays exactly as it is**
(see the overlap note below) — this course is Ethiopia's
own Moral Education, beside it.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Ethiopia inside the Moral Education
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's
place is found by what they prove, and each level stands
on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never
a forced gate. The school grade each book comes from
appears only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance. The twelve levels follow the guides' own
progression across the six grades — two levels from each
grade's six chapters, in the books' own order (see
CURRICULUM.md). Lessons teach through their text first:
every picture's and GIF's teaching is also in the text, so
a lesson is fully learnable by listening to its text read
aloud.

**Both strands stand in this course.** The founding read
tagged every placement with its strand, because Moral
Education as Ethiopia teaches it in Grades 1–6 carries two:
the **manners/morals** strand (character, manners, values)
and the **constitution/civics** strand (human dignity as
the basis of rights, equality, children's and human
rights, the law and law-abidingness, responsibilities to
one's country and tax, patriotism). Both are part of the
Moral Education subject as its own books teach it, so both
stand here, in the guides' own places in the ladder; each
lesson's sources carry its strand tag, and the civics
strand stands in strength at Level 5 (equality and
rights), Level 7 (the Grade 4 guide's full human-rights
unit) and Level 11 (the Grade 6 guide's full
law-abidingness unit).

## The guides' ways — the course's shape (the six WAY placements)

Each guide's own shape is a placement in itself, and the
six together are this course's ladder:

- **Grade 1 — character built in widening circles: self,
  family, neighbours, school, community, culture.**
  Respecting oneself is the foundation — respecting others
  starts from respecting oneself — and the child's
  character is then guided by truth and honesty, fairness
  and justice, the common benefit and positive social
  relationships. Every chapter: outcomes first, then
  definitions, stories and examples, pair and group
  discussion, role-play and picture work, and
  observation-based continuous assessment with printed
  true/false keys.
- **Grade 2 — the individual built first, then turned
  outward.** Self-respect and staying safe first — the
  introduction states the foundation outright: respecting
  others begins from respecting oneself — then truth and
  honesty, justice, positive social interaction, standing
  for the common good, and cultural heritage last. Each
  chapter: outcomes, contents, a printed content-summary
  reading, discussion and action, and printed answers to
  closing questions.
- **Grade 3 — from what a human being is, outward.**
  Humanity and human dignity first — the book founds
  itself on a civics claim: human dignity is the basis of
  human rights, and all human beings are born free and
  equal in dignity and rights — then respecting others,
  equality, healthy social life, cooperation and teamwork,
  and multiculturalism last. Every lesson follows the
  guide's four-part rhythm: introduction, presentation,
  summary, assessment.
- **Grade 4 — character built topic by topic, rights
  taught as a child's own subject.** Self-worth first, then
  human rights — the book's one full civics unit — peace,
  ethics in everyday social activity, tolerance, and
  living with cultural diversity. Every unit: competencies
  up front, a short explanation, elicitation, group
  discussion, assessment, follow-up and printed answers to
  every activity.
- **Grade 5 — from duty at home to the whole connected
  world.** Responsibility, the culture of hard work and of
  reading, moral decision-making as a learnable skill with
  named steps, care for the environment, the ethics of
  social media, and cultural linkage. The civics strand
  appears where the first chapter turns outward —
  responsibilities to one's country, paying tax, respecting
  rules. The method never varies: competencies, a short
  explanation, elicitation and group work, printed answers
  to every activity.
- **Grade 6 — character reasoned out, then owed to the
  country.** The guide opens with a theory of morality
  itself (see "The subject's own foundation" below), then
  runs from the inside out: moral integrity,
  law-abidingness — the book's full civics unit — good
  behaviour, taking part in socio-economic life,
  patriotism, and peace with cooperation. Every session
  scripted identically, every unit closing with chapter
  answers.

**The course-intro picture** (the six guides): six widening
rings around one child — self, family, neighbours and
school, community and culture, the wide world of work,
decisions and screens, and the country — each ring labelled
with its grade's chapters. **Course-intro video ideas:**
one short script per grade walking its six chapters in
order, as the guides' own shapes (described slots; filming
is a later pass).

## The subject's own foundation (Grade 6, placement 27)

The Grade 6 guide's front matter states what Moral
Education is for, and it stands at this course's head as
the guides themselves state it: people are rational beings
whose character is formed, not inborn, so character can be
taught; morality's aims are to keep society from splitting
apart, to reduce human suffering, to help human
flourishing, to resolve conflicts justly, and to give
praise and blame, reward and punishment their proper
place; and the subject's purpose (preface, printed
p. VIII) is to raise a generation that can tell good from
bad and choose the good. (Teacher guide — evidence;
printed pp. I–II and VIII.)

## Overlap with Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia — noted, not merged

The Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia course holds these
same six guides' content as its named opening levels
(Levels 1–12, "morals and manners") and its Level 13
civics strand, built earlier inside that course's own
thematic ladder, alongside the Citizenship Education
books for Grades 7–10 and the older-generation Grade 11
course. That course **stays as-is — nothing in it was
deleted or edited by this build** (one wording note was
added to its ABOUT.md, saying this course now stands).
The overlap is real and is stated here and in
`moral-education/README.md`: the same Ethiopian guides feed
both courses — the Civics course by its citizen's ladder,
this course as Ethiopia's own standalone Moral Education
subject, in the guides' own grade-by-grade progression,
with both strands in their own places. Lesson titles
coincide where the thing taught is the same; the courses
are different shelves, and each lesson's sources say which
shelf it stands on.
