# Lesson plan — Law-abidingness — what law is for

**Course:** Moral Education — Ethiopia · **Level folder:** Level 11 — Integrity, the Law and Good Behaviour · **Lesson:** Law-abidingness — what law is for
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 11 — Integrity, the Law and Good Behaviour (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The grade's civics foundation: law-abidingness as readiness and commitment to obey one's country's laws, opened through the purposes of law — to make citizens know their rights and duties, to direct behaviour, and to secure citizens' equality.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 11 — Integrity, the Law and Good Behaviour (recommended, never forced).
**Strand:** constitution/civics — the strand the Grade 6 teacher guide itself gives this teaching (manners/morals, or constitution/civics where the guide teaches rights, law or the state inside Moral Education).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The lesson teaches through its text first: every picture's and GIF's teaching is also in the text, so the lesson is fully learnable by listening to its text read aloud.

Nothing but yourself — and, for the discussion and role-play acts, a companion or a small group. Nothing else.

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level (levels.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of a rule-book open, with three purpose ribbons — rights and duties, direction, equality → the student studies the picture and names what it shows, part by part, in the lesson's own words.
- VIDEO — a short explainer script, "why countries have laws" (made later) → the student listens and answers along. The lesson is fully learnable from its text alone; the video adds nothing the text does not teach.

Work the Do it first act again, slowly, saying each step aloud.
