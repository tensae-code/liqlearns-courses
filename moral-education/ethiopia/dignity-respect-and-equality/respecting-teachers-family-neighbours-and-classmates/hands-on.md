# Hands-on — Respecting teachers, family, neighbours and classmates

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The remaining named respects, each with its own small-group activity (2.8–2.11): respecting teachers — you express appreciation for teachers they see as good examples; respecting family — with exchange of experiences and the importance stated; respecting neighbours — for example showing respect in greeting, with class discussion of its importance and of building peaceful mutual interaction; and respecting classmates/friends — you show in practice, inside and outside the classroom, how much they respect each other, with the message that mutual respect shrinks disagreements between them.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: The remaining named respects, each with its own small-group activity (2.8–2.11): respecting teachers — pupils express appreciation for teachers they see as good examples; respecting family — with exchange of experiences and the importance stated; respecting neighbours — for example showing respect in greeting, with class discussion of its importance and of building peaceful mutual interaction; and respecting classmates/friends — pupils show in practice, inside and outside the classroom, how much they respect each other, with the message that mutual respect shrinks disagreements between them.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
