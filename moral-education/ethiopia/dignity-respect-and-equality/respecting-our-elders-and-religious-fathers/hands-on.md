# Hands-on — Respecting our elders and religious fathers

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two of the lesson's named respects get your own activities: respecting elders, with you naming the customary moral principles of elder-respect found in your society; and respecting religious fathers, where the activity answers note the things to be considered when religious fathers are respected (for example reconciliation/peace-making, እርቅ).

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Two of the lesson's named respects get their own activities: respecting elders, with pupils naming the customary moral principles of elder-respect found in their society; and respecting religious fathers, where the activity answers note the things to be considered when religious fathers are respected (for example reconciliation/peace-making, እርቅ).

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
