# Hands-on — Dignity first — where human rights come from

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Mixed placement, civics dominant: the guide states that respecting human dignity is the foundation for human rights, that human rights cannot be seen apart from human dignity, and that respecting dignity lays the basis for human-rights laws to protect and apply; the human-rights laws are quoted in the guide's own Amharic affirmation that all human beings are born free and are equal in dignity and in rights.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
