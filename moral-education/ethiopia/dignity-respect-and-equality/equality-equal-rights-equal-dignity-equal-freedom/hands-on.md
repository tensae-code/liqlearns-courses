# Hands-on — Equality — equal rights, equal dignity, equal freedom

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The manners part is that you must show equality in acts, not words.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Mixed placement, civics dominant: the guide's own definition is rights language — equality is having equal rights, dignity and freedom with others; using rights and benefits equally; carrying responsibilities equally; treating people equally without making race, religion or language a basis; and giving special honour to individuals and groups alike.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
