# Hands-on — Humanity — the human race, and the mark of being good

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You and a companion compare objects in the classroom with the human beings in it, and use the lesson's Figure 1.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: The lesson teaches two concepts of humanity. In the first sense it names the human race itself — humankind, distinct from the other animals: human beings are precious and irreplaceable (a thing can be replaced thing-for-thing; a human being cannot), they differ in race, height, sex and language, yet in humanity all human beings are equal, and no one is to be excluded for being different.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
