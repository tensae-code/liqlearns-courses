# Hands-on — How a respected person behaves

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You and a companion in groups of five discuss the traits, and show a respected person's character in role-play; teachers are told to name, as samples, good traits such as humility and honesty against bad ones such as rudeness/coarseness and fraud, so you learn to separate good character from bad, and to recognise your own human values (humility, honesty) and share them with classmates.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Character is a person's distinguishing mark, so a respected person's traits are that person's identifiers. Pupils in groups of five discuss the traits, and show a respected person's character in role-play; teachers are told to name, as samples, good traits such as humility and honesty against bad ones such as rudeness/coarseness and fraud, so pupils learn to separate good character from bad, and to recognise their own human values (humility, honesty) and share them with classmates.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
