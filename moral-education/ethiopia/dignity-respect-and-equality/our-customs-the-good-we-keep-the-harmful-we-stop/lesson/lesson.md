# Our customs — the good we keep, the harmful we stop

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's act once with what you already know: Take this lesson's teaching into one real situation today — at home, at school or in your neighbourhood: do what the lesson teaches, then say in one sentence what you did and what it changed. Keep what you did in mind — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

## Good and harmful cultural practices

Mixed placement inside Lesson 2's spread: the guide sorts cultural practices into good practices that are a basis for the country's development and change — respecting elders, idir and equb (the community's mutual-aid and rotating-savings associations), helping the needy, working together and hospitality — and harmful practices: female circumcision, under-age marriage, cutting the face in mourning, cutting the body with blades, wastefulness and abduction (ጠለፋ). The manners part is keeping the good customs; the civics part is that the harmful list is practices that injure people's bodies and rights, named in an equality chapter.

## Equality judged in custom

Teach the harmful list plainly and without pictures of the acts.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Act it, don't just say it. A value you have practised once in a real moment — at home, at school, in your street — is yours in a way a value you have only read about is not.
