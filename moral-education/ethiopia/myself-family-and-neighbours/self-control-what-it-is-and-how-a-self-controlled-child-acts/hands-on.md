# Hands-on — Self-control — what it is and how a self-controlled child acts

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You and a companion discuss the concept in groups with examples, and the key behaviours in pairs.

## Show it with real things
Carry the lesson's teaching into one real moment today — at home, at school or among your neighbours — and do the act the lesson teaches, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are doing, why, and what the lesson says it builds. Compare what happened against the lesson's one thing: Self-control is presented as the right moral behaviour a child develops and keeps. The teacher notes describe such children in concrete acts: respecting their parents, keeping themselves clean, helping their parents and others grow in ability, eating properly, and playing with friends safely and honestly.

## Check it for one day
For one day, watch for this lesson's teaching in real life around you — in your family, your classroom, your street. Each time you see it (or see it missing), note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
