# Lesson plan — Five steps to a moral decision

**Course:** Moral Education — Ethiopia · **Level folder:** Level 9 — Responsibility, Work and Deciding Well · **Lesson:** Five steps to a moral decision
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 9 — Responsibility, Work and Deciding Well (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The chapter's core procedure, taught as a fixed sequence: identify the problem; think of the options; identify each option's likely positive and negative outcomes; decide and follow through; review and evaluate what happened.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 9 — Responsibility, Work and Deciding Well (recommended, never forced).
**Strand:** manners/morals — the strand the Grade 5 teacher guide itself gives this teaching (manners/morals, or constitution/civics where the guide teaches rights, law or the state inside Moral Education).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The lesson teaches through its text first: every picture's and GIF's teaching is also in the text, so the lesson is fully learnable by listening to its text read aloud.

Nothing but yourself — and, for the discussion and role-play acts, a companion or a small group. Nothing else.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of the five steps as five stepping stones across a stream → the student studies the picture and names what it shows, part by part, in the lesson's own words.
- GIF — a child stepping stone to stone, pausing at the outcomes stone to look both ways — yes → the student watches, then describes the movement in words and does the act themselves.
- VIDEO — a worked-example script walking one decision through all five steps (made later) → the student listens and answers along. The lesson is fully learnable from its text alone; the video adds nothing the text does not teach.
