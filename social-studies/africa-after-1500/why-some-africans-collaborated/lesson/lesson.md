# Why some Africans collaborated

## Do it first

Not every African resisted: some peoples, weakened by disease and conflict or hoping for goods, protection or missionary favour, chose to work with the Europeans — Rwanda's P5 book names the Maasai and the Banyankole. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Weakened peoples — disease, rinderpest and conflict

The Maasai and the Banyankole, weakened by disease, rinderpest and conflict, choose to work with the Europeans. Collaboration, in this book, often begins from weakness, not from agreement.

[Picture slot — a picture of the world of this lesson as the book shows it: point to the picture and say what changed in this part of the story.]

## Sub lesson 2 — Hoping for goods and protection

Others hope for goods and protection. Working with the newcomers looks, from inside a hard moment, like a way to gain things and to be kept safe.

## Sub lesson 3 — Missionary favour

Others again seek missionary favour. The missions hold schools, medicine and influence, and standing well with them is one more reason a community works with the Europeans.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats: Weakened peoples — disease, rinderpest and conflict; Hoping for goods and protection; Missionary favour. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
