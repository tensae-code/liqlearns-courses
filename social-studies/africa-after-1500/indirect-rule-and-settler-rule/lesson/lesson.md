# Indirect rule and settler rule

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Indirect rule — governing through chiefs

Britain's contrasting design was **indirect rule**: govern **through existing African chiefs and institutions** rather than replacing them. The chief still sat in his court and judged by custom — but above him stood the British district officer, and the chief's authority now ran on the coloniser's licence. It was cheaper than direct rule and clothed foreign power in familiar faces. Compare it honestly with the last lesson: France set African authorities aside; Britain kept them — as instruments.

## Sub lesson 2 — Settler rule — land and power in settler hands

The hardest system was **settler rule**. Where white settlers came in numbers, they took the system into their own hands: **settler minorities held land and power directly** — the best land alienated to settler farms, political power reserved to the settler community. The book sets you to **sketch the colonial map and locate the white settlement areas** — do it, because that geography is a prophecy: the colonies where settlers held the land are exactly where, two generations later, liberation would have to be fought for hardest and longest. Unit 7 will collect that debt.
