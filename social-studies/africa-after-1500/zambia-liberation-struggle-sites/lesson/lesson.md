# Zambia — liberation struggle sites

## Do it first

Close your eyes and picture a freedom struggle. You might picture marches and speeches. Now picture something quieter: a rock, a tree, and a small township house with a number on its door. Before you read on, say why a movement would need places like these at all.

## Sub lesson 1 — A rock and a tree at Kabwe

Zambia's module teaches the liberation struggle through its places — the sites where the movement actually gathered.

At Kabwe are two of the oldest of them. The Mulungushi Rock of Authority is the rock where the nationalist movement held its great gatherings — a natural platform from which leaders addressed the people. Near it stands the Mukuyu Tree, another open-air meeting place of the struggle years.

A rock and a tree need no permission and no hall. They belonged to everyone who came — which is exactly why a movement that the colonial government watched and restricted made them its own.

## Sub lesson 2 — Houses, a statue and a shrine

In the townships, the struggle's places were ordinary houses, remembered now by their numbers. In Lusaka, Chilenje House No. 394 was Kenneth Kaunda's home from 1960 to 1962. Freedom House and Matero House No. 3144B in Lusaka held the movement's organising. At Kabwe, Bwacha House No. E1376 is remembered for one dated moment: on 8 March 1958, Kaunda was elected president of ZANC there.

In your mind's eye: the Zambian places and people named in this lesson, as the book tells it. Name one person or place and say what happened there.

Two further places carry the memory in public. The Freedom Statue stands as the struggle's memorial — its chained figure belongs to the next lesson's freedom fighters. And Isa ya Mwaamba at Choma is named among the key places of the liberation struggle. Together, rock, tree, houses, statue and shrine form what the module calls the places key in the liberation struggle: a map of freedom a learner can walk.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as two beats: the open-air places — the Mulungushi Rock of Authority and the Mukuyu Tree at Kabwe, where the movement gathered; and the houses and memorials — Chilenje House No. 394, Kaunda's home in 1960–62, Freedom House and Matero House No. 3144B, Bwacha House No. E1376 where Kaunda was elected ZANC president on 8 March 1958, the Freedom Statue, and Isa ya Mwaamba at Choma. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
