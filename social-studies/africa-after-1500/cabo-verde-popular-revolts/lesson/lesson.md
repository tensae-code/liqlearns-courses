# Cabo Verde — popular revolts

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of islands with no people on them at all — then people arriving by ship, building towns, rising in revolt, and at last proclaiming their own country. Which part of that long story do you think this lesson tells? Hold your guess; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Revolts of the 1800s

The poor of the islands rise again and again — Ribeira Grande 1811, the Engenhos 1822, Monte Agarro 1835, Achada Falcão 1841, Casa Martins 1847 — against taxes, hunger and abuse.

[Picture slot — a picture of a timeline with six flags planted on it — 1811, 1822, 1835, 1841, 1847, 1910 — the student reads the flags in order and says what the people rose against.]


## Sub lesson 2 — Ribeirão Manuel, 1910

Ribeirão Manuel 1910 — the rising the book carries into the 1900s, still against taxes, hunger and abuse.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory, the places and dates this lesson names.
2. Write who acted in this lesson, and what changed because of them.
3. Write two sentences explaining this lesson's contrast in your own words: again and again, for a hundred years: the poor rise against taxes, hunger and abuse — 1811, 1822, 1835, 1841, 1847, 1910.
