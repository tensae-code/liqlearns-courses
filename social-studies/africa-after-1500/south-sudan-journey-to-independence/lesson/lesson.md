# South Sudan — the journey to independence

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Marginalised and exploited by the north, the south needs change, and the biggest change is secession — South Sudan breaks from Sudan and becomes independent on 9 July 2011. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Change in our country

Marginalised and exploited by the north, the south needs change, and the biggest change is secession — South Sudan breaks from Sudan and becomes independent on 9 July 2011

## Sub lesson 2 — Journey to independence for South Sudan

1947 Juba Conference demands separation and is rejected; 1955 Torit Mutiny opens the Anya-nya 1 war under Joseph Lagu; 1972 Addis Ababa gives semi-autonomy; 1983 Nimeiri tears it up and John Garang's SPLM/A fights to the 2005 Naivasha peace; a 99% referendum vote leads to independence in 2011 with Salva Kiir as first president

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Comparison between change in South Sudan and change in South Africa

Both countries won change through armed struggle and bloodshed, and both through a long chain of meetings and signed agreements lasting many years

## Sub lesson 4 — South Sudan in the 20th century: separation policy

Britain's Milner Report of 1920 proposes dividing Sudan, and the Closed District Ordinance of 1922 seals the south off — northern staff and traders excluded, southerners barred from northern work and education, Arab dress and names prohibited

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39256 — north and south panels with a sealed fence between]

## Sub lesson 5 — The Juba Conference of 1947

Southern chiefs and officials, asked their opinion for the first time under the condominium, refuse Khartoum's Legislative Assembly on day one and accept it on day two after Judge Shingeiti's persuasion — Clement Mboro's reversal shows their unpreparedness; unity is postponed, but southern nationalism is born

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39257 — two panels of chiefs — DAY ONE NO, DAY TWO YES]

## Sub lesson 6 — Anya-Nya Movement (1955-1972)

Ex-Torit soldiers attack at Pamoju with knives and sticks and capture their first firearms; Joseph Lagu joins in 1963 and gives the movement weight; Abboud expels all missionaries in 1964; factions split along ethnic lines (ALF, SSPG) until Lagu unites the South Sudan Liberation Front in 1970 and forces talks

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39258 — two panels — stick fighters and a captured rifle, Lagu with one flag]

## Sub lesson 7 — Addis-Ababa Agreement

The 1972 agreement makes the south one autonomous region with its own assembly and High Executive Council, promises Abyei a referendum and integrates Anya-Nya fighters, but keeps oil and economic planning in Khartoum — welcomed by a war-weary south, denounced in the north, and plagued by rivalry from its first moment

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39259 — a signing table, a ONE REGION panel, an oil barrel marked north]

## Sub lesson 8 — The SPLM/A struggle for independence (1983-2005)

Battalion 105 at Bor refuses transfer north in 1983 and is attacked; 2,500 soldiers gather at Bilpham; Akuot Atem's secession manifesto is rejected by Ethiopia, so John Garang writes the manifesto for a socialist, united, secular Sudan that wins Ethiopian support; the struggle runs to the CPA, whose challenges, achievements and effects on South Sudanese pupils assess

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39260 — three soldiers in a column, Garang writing a paper]

## Sub lesson 9 — Development in Sudan since 1965 and in South Sudan since 2011

Political, economic and social development in Sudan from 1965, then in independent South Sudan from 2011, with post-independence challenges and the importance of peace
