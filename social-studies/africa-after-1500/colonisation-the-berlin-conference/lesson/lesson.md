# Colonisation — the Berlin Conference

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## A race that needed rules

Two racers sped the Scramble up: **King Leopold of Belgium** entering the rivalry, and **Germany** entering it too. The race **bred conflict among Europeans** themselves — and to **settle it peacefully**, the **Berlin Conference of 1884–85** was called, on the **colonial ordering of Africa**. It was **chaired under Otto von Bismarck**, and **fourteen European countries** met. Notice who was not in the room: no African ruler, no African voice. Africa was the subject of the conference, not a party to it.

## The General Act and Article 34

On **February 25, 1885** (as printed), the participants signed the **Berlin General Act**. Its **Article 34**, as the book gives it, laid down the rule of the race: **any European state taking a colony in Africa had to notify the other signatories**, who could **approve, reject or counter-claim** the holding. Colonisation by paperwork: claim it, announce it, and the other powers answer. With the rules written, the partition of a continent could proceed at full speed — the next lesson watches it happen.
