# Lesson plan — Rwanda — the road to independence

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Rwanda — the road to independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Rwanda, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Berlin hands Rwanda to Germany, which rules through the king; King Rudahigwa reforms, demands independence in 1954 and dies in 1959; five parties fight over the future, and on 1 July 1962 Rwanda becomes independent under Grégoire Kayibanda.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Berlin and German rule through the king** — The Berlin Conference of 1884–85 hands Rwanda to Germany, which rules through the king.
2. **Rudahigwa — reforms and the demand for independence** — King Rudahigwa abolishes Ubuhake and Uburetwa, opposes tribal identity cards and in 1954 demands independence.
3. **Parties, 1959 chaos — and independence, 1 July 1962** — MSM, APROSOMA, UNAR, RADER and MDR-PARMEHUTU fight over the future.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
