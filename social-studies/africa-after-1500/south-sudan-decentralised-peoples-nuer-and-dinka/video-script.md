# Video script — South Sudan — the Nuer and Dinka, peoples without kings

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as South Sudan's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A decentralised people of tribes and lineages whose leopard-skin religious chiefs settle disputes; cattle are wealth and prestige, raiding the Dinka is a duty blessed by women's rituals, and Kwoth is honoured through spirits, ghost marriages and marriages completed only at the third child.

A decentralised people of tribes and lineages whose leopard-skin religious chiefs settle disputes; cattle are wealth and prestige, raiding the Dinka is a duty blessed by women's rituals, and Kwoth is honoured through spirits, ghost marriages and marriages completed only at the third child.

South Sudan's largest people, the Jieng, are decentralised pastoralists led by ritual chiefs, masters of the fishing spear; boys are scarified into warrior age-sets, name themselves after their cattle's colours, wrestle, and trade cattle, grain and ivory with the Nuer.
