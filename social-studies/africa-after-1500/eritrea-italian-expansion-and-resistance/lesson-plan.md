# Lesson plan — Eritrea — Italian expansion and resistance

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Eritrea — Italian expansion and resistance
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Eritrea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Italy bought Assab in 1869, took Massawa, and in 1890 named its colony Eritrea — but Eritreans resisted from the start, and Dejazmach Bahta Hagos rose against the land seizures in 1894.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Italian rule begins** — Italy buys Assab in 1869, takes Massawa, and in 1890 names the colony Eritrea — but Dejazmach Bahta Hagos rises against the land seizures in 1894 (Italian rule begins)
2. **Italian rule 1882–1941** — Italy lands at Assab and Massawa, proclaims the colony of Eritrea in 1890, and meets resisters like Bahta Hagos — while Eritrean askari are shipped to Italy's other wars (Italian rule 1882–1941)
3. **Italian expansion and Eritrean resistance** — Sapeto buys Assab in 1869, Italy takes Massawa in 1885, is bloodied by Ras Alula at Dogali in 1887, then rides famine and Yohannes's death to the Treaty of Wuchale and proclaims the colony of Eritrea on 1 January 1890 (Italian expansion and Eritrean resistance)

2. Answer, in their own words: who were the askari, as the Grade 5 book tells it?
