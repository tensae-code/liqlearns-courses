# Egypt — the Urabi Revolution and the British occupation — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Urabi Revolution and the start of the British occupation
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Tawfiq restores the foreign Dual Control and Riyad Pasha's absolutism; Egyptian officers, passed over for Turks by war minister Uthman Rifqi, rally to Ahmed Urabi — freed by his own soldiers at Qasr al-Nil in February 1881, he presents the nation's demands at Abdeen on 9 September 1881: the ministry dismissed, an army of 18,000, an elected chamber; two Anglo-French joint notes and a British ultimatum over Alexandria's forts follow

## Sub lesson 2 — Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Mustafa Kamel founds the newspaper al-Liwa in 1900 and the National Party in 1907 and campaigns for an Egyptian university, opened in 1908; the Dinshaway hangings of 1906 shame Britain before Europe — Cromer resigns and Saad Zaghloul becomes a minister; Muhammad Farid opens free night schools for workers, helps found unions, defeats the forty-year extension of the Suez concession in 1910, and dies in Germany in 1919 still pleading Egypt's cause

## Sub lesson 3 — The national movement and the Urabi Revolution
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Sharif Pasha's Chamber of December 1881 fights for the budget; the first joint note of January 1882, al-Barudi's "Ministry of the Revolution" with Urabi at the War Ministry, the second note of May 1882, the Alexandria riot of 11 June and the bombardment follow; Urabi defies his dismissal, holds Kafr al-Dawwar and falls at Tell el-Kebir, where the officer Muhammad Ubayd is martyred; Cairo is occupied in September 1882 and the leaders exiled to Ceylon — and the book itself lists why the revolution failed

## Sub lesson 4 — The national struggle against the British occupation
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — under the occupation the British Agent rules, the army is capped at six thousand men under an English commander, cotton is king and education is starved; Mustafa Kamel rebuilds the movement — al-Liwa in 1900, the National Party in 1907, the Egyptian University in 1908, and Dinshaway in 1906 turned against Britain before Europe — and Muhammad Farid follows with free night schools, workers' unions and the defeat of the canal-extension scheme
