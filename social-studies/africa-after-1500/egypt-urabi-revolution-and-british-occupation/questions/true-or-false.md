# Egypt — the Urabi Revolution and the British occupation — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Tawfiq restores the foreign Dual Control and Riyad Pasha's absolutism; Egyptian officers, passed over for Turks by war minister Uthman Rifqi, rally to Ahmed Urabi — freed by his own soldiers at Qasr al-Nil in February 1881, he presents the nation's demands at Abdeen on 9 September 1881: the ministry dismissed, an army of 18,000, an elected chamber; two Anglo-French joint notes and a British ultimatum over Alexandria's forts follow.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The Urabi Revolution and the start of the British occupation

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Prep9 T1 "Egypt and the Continents of the New World"* is about Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid.
**Answer: False** — it is about The Urabi Revolution and the start of the British occupation

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Mustafa Kamel founds the newspaper al-Liwa in 1900 and the National Party in 1907 and campaigns for an Egyptian university, opened in 1908; the Dinshaway hangings of 1906 shame Britain before Europe — Cromer resigns and Saad Zaghloul becomes a minister; Muhammad Farid opens free night schools for workers, helps found unions, defeats the forty-year extension of the Suez concession in 1910, and dies in Germany in 1919 still pleading Egypt's cause.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Prep9 T1 "Egypt and the Continents of the New World"* is about The national movement and the Urabi Revolution.
**Answer: False** — it is about Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Sharif Pasha's Chamber of December 1881 fights for the budget; the first joint note of January 1882, al-Barudi's "Ministry of the Revolution" with Urabi at the War Ministry, the second note of May 1882, the Alexandria riot of 11 June and the bombardment follow; Urabi defies his dismissal, holds Kafr al-Dawwar and falls at Tell el-Kebir, where the officer Muhammad Ubayd is martyred; Cairo is occupied in September 1882 and the leaders exiled to Ceylon — and the book itself lists why the revolution failed.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The national movement and the Urabi Revolution

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Prep9 full-year "Geography of the World and Modern & Contemporary History of Egypt"* is about The national struggle against the British occupation.
**Answer: False** — it is about The national movement and the Urabi Revolution

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Under the occupation the British Agent rules, the army is capped at six thousand men under an English commander, cotton is king and education is starved; Mustafa Kamel rebuilds the movement — al-Liwa in 1900, the National Party in 1907, the Egyptian University in 1908, and Dinshaway in 1906 turned against Britain before Europe — and Muhammad Farid follows with free night schools, workers' unions and the defeat of the canal-extension scheme.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The national struggle against the British occupation

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *Prep9 full-year "Geography of the World and Modern & Contemporary History of Egypt"* is about Chapter 3: Egypt from the Urabi Revolution until the First World War.
**Answer: False** — it is about The national struggle against the British occupation

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** The revolution, the occupation it fails to prevent, and the national movement's long rebuilding under British rule down to 1914.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Chapter 3: Egypt from the Urabi Revolution until the First World War

**10. (Sub lesson 5)** This part of *Sec12 "History of Egypt and the Arabs, Modern and Contemporary"* is about The Urabi Revolution and the start of the British occupation.
**Answer: False** — it is about Chapter 3: Egypt from the Urabi Revolution until the First World War
