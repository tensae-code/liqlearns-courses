# Lesson plan — Egypt — the Urabi Revolution and the British occupation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Egypt — the Urabi Revolution and the British occupation
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Egyptian officers rally to Ahmed Urabi against foreign control and discrimination; Britain bombards Alexandria and occupies Egypt in 1882 — and Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid rebuild the national movement under occupation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Urabi Revolution and the start of the British occupation** — Tawfiq restores the foreign Dual Control and Riyad Pasha's absolutism; Egyptian officers, passed over for Turks by war minister Uthman Rifqi, rally to Ahmed Urabi — freed by his own soldiers at Qasr al-Nil in February 1881, he presents the nation's demands at Abdeen on 9 September 1881: the ministry dismissed, an army of 18,000, an elected chamber; two Anglo-French joint notes and a British ultimatum over Alexandria's forts follow
2. **Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid** — Mustafa Kamel founds the newspaper al-Liwa in 1900 and the National Party in 1907 and campaigns for an Egyptian university, opened in 1908; the Dinshaway hangings of 1906 shame Britain before Europe — Cromer resigns and Saad Zaghloul becomes a minister; Muhammad Farid opens free night schools for workers, helps found unions, defeats the forty-year extension of the Suez concession in 1910, and dies in Germany in 1919 still pleading Egypt's cause
3. **The national movement and the Urabi Revolution** — Sharif Pasha's Chamber of December 1881 fights for the budget; the first joint note of January 1882, al-Barudi's "Ministry of the Revolution" with Urabi at the War Ministry, the second note of May 1882, the Alexandria riot of 11 June and the bombardment follow; Urabi defies his dismissal, holds Kafr al-Dawwar and falls at Tell el-Kebir, where the officer Muhammad Ubayd is martyred; Cairo is occupied in September 1882 and the leaders exiled to Ceylon — and the book itself lists why the revolution failed
4. **The national struggle against the British occupation** — Under the occupation the British Agent rules, the army is capped at six thousand men under an English commander, cotton is king and education is starved; Mustafa Kamel rebuilds the movement — al-Liwa in 1900, the National Party in 1907, the Egyptian University in 1908, and Dinshaway in 1906 turned against Britain before Europe — and Muhammad Farid follows with free night schools, workers' unions and the defeat of the canal-extension scheme
5. **Chapter 3: Egypt from the Urabi Revolution until the First World War** — The revolution, the occupation it fails to prevent, and the national movement's long rebuilding under British rule down to 1914
