# Egypt — the Urabi Revolution and the British occupation

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Egyptian officers rally to Ahmed Urabi against foreign control and discrimination; Britain bombards Alexandria and occupies Egypt in 1882 — and Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid rebuild the national movement under occupation. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Urabi Revolution and the start of the British occupation

Tawfiq restores the foreign Dual Control and Riyad Pasha's absolutism; Egyptian officers, passed over for Turks by war minister Uthman Rifqi, rally to Ahmed Urabi — freed by his own soldiers at Qasr al-Nil in February 1881, he presents the nation's demands at Abdeen on 9 September 1881: the ministry dismissed, an army of 18,000, an elected chamber; two Anglo-French joint notes and a British ultimatum over Alexandria's forts follow.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mustafa Kamel and Muhammad Farid

Mustafa Kamel founds the newspaper al-Liwa in 1900 and the National Party in 1907 and campaigns for an Egyptian university, opened in 1908; the Dinshaway hangings of 1906 shame Britain before Europe — Cromer resigns and Saad Zaghloul becomes a minister; Muhammad Farid opens free night schools for workers, helps found unions, defeats the forty-year extension of the Suez concession in 1910, and dies in Germany in 1919 still pleading Egypt's cause.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — The national movement and the Urabi Revolution

Sharif Pasha's Chamber of December 1881 fights for the budget; the first joint note of January 1882, al-Barudi's "Ministry of the Revolution" with Urabi at the War Ministry, the second note of May 1882, the Alexandria riot of 11 June and the bombardment follow; Urabi defies his dismissal, holds Kafr al-Dawwar and falls at Tell el-Kebir, where the officer Muhammad Ubayd is martyred; Cairo is occupied in September 1882 and the leaders exiled to Ceylon — and the book itself lists why the revolution failed.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39104 — shell bursts over Alexandria's forts]

## Sub lesson 4 — The national struggle against the British occupation

Under the occupation the British Agent rules, the army is capped at six thousand men under an English commander, cotton is king and education is starved; Mustafa Kamel rebuilds the movement — al-Liwa in 1900, the National Party in 1907, the Egyptian University in 1908, and Dinshaway in 1906 turned against Britain before Europe — and Muhammad Farid follows with free night schools, workers' unions and the defeat of the canal-extension scheme.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.
