# Libya's struggle against Italy — 1911 to independence

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From the first battles of 1911 at Abu Meliana and al-Shatt, Libyans resist Italy for twenty years through desert war and Italian detention camps until independence comes after the second war. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why Italy invaded, and how it prepared

Cheated of Tunisia in 1881, Italy wins Europe's consent, then creeps in peacefully — Banco di Roma branches in Tripoli and Benghazi, schools, clinics, doctors — before an ultimatum over the Edirne ship affair gives Istanbul 24 hours; war is declared on 29 September 1911

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39174 — a bank building, a small church, an ultimatum panel]

## Sub lesson 3 — The invasion and the Treaty of Ouchy

The fleet shells Tripoli on 3 October 1911 against barely 7,000 Ottoman troops, and the garrison refuses Istanbul's surrender order; at Ouchy on 18 October 1912 the Sultan withdraws — but his firman grants Libyans self-rule, so Libyans reject the treaty and fight on: Derna's Friday battle in May 1913, Janduba, and Mahruqa in Fezzan

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39175 — a warship shelling the walls, the Ouchy panel crossed out]

## Sub lesson 4 — After the war: the Tripolitanian Republic

At Misrata on 16 November 1918 the leaders — Baruni, al-Murayyid, al-Suwayhili, Bilkhayr — proclaim the Tripolitanian Republic; the 1919 Suani Ben Yadam peace wins a Fundamental Law of rights, and at Gharyan in 1920 the leaders resolve that only unity under one elected leader can save the country

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39176 — four turbaned leaders at a table under a banner]

## Sub lesson 5 — The jihad resumes: Graziani and Omar Mukhtar

Fascist Italy tears up the agreements in 1922 and Graziani grinds through the Western Mountain — Khalifa ibn Askar is hanged after a show trial — while in the Green Mountain Omar Mukhtar, Senussi sheikh since 1902 and veteran of the Chad fighting, takes command in 1923 and defeats the Italians at Bir Bilal and Marsa Brega

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39177 — a white-bearded sheikh on a green ridge, a tank below]

## Sub lesson 6 — The liberation of Libya

In Cairo in 1940 the exiles form a provisional government and a Libyan army that grows to 14,000 men; after El Alamein on 23 October 1942 Montgomery's advance frees Benghazi on 20 November and enters Tripoli on 23 January 1943 — Italian rule is over

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39178 — soldiers and armour entering a city gate, 1943]

## Sub lesson 7 — Military administration and the partition plan

Britain rules Cyrenaica and Tripolitania separately and France holds Fezzan; the Bevin–Sforza plan of 1948 to split Libya among the three powers leaks, the streets explode in protest, a four-power committee hears one demand everywhere — independence — and the Arab League carries Libya's case to the United Nations

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39179 — a three-way split map above a protesting crowd]

## Sub lesson 8 — Independence

On 21 November 1949 the UN votes Libya independent by 1 January 1952; the Committee of Sixty writes a constitution of 213 articles and chooses a federal monarchy under King Idris; at ten in the morning on 24 December 1951 independence is proclaimed at al-Manar Palace in Benghazi

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39180 — a crowned king, a palace, the red-black-green flag]

## Sub lesson 9 — Independent Libya

The United Kingdom of Libya builds its institutions — a parliament elected in February 1952, a Senate of twenty-four — and takes its seat in the Arab League and the United Nations

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39181 — a parliament building, a Senate panel, two flags]

## Sub lesson 10 — Italy prepares and invades Libya

Beaten to Tunisia in 1881, Italy prepares Libya quietly — Banco di Roma branches, Italian schools, study missions — wins Britain's and France's consent, then shells Tripoli and lands on 5 October 1911 against barely 7,000 Ottoman troops, meeting a coast-wide resistance of named battles from Abu Meliana and al-Shatt to al-Hawari

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39182 — two panels — a quiet bank, then a shelled coast]

## Sub lesson 11 — Ouchy rejected, and the victory of al-Qardabiya

Libyans refuse the 1912 Treaty of Ouchy that abandons them to Italy; unity hardens, and at al-Qardabiya on 28 April 1915 Safi al-Din al-Senussi, Ramadan al-Suwayhili, Salih al-Atyush and Ahmad Sayf al-Nasr destroy Colonel Miani's column — Italy falls back on the defensive

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39183 — fighters on a dune above a broken column]

## Sub lesson 12 — The Ajdabiya government and the Tripolitanian Republic

Idris al-Senussi governs Cyrenaica from Ajdabiya and wins the Akrama agreement of 1917 — prisoners exchanged, Senussi zawiyas reopened, seized property restored — while the western leaders proclaim the Tripolitanian Republic on 18 November 1918, the Arab homeland's first republic, with its governing council, shura council and scholars' assembly

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39184 — two panels — Idris at Ajdabiya, the Republic council]

## Sub lesson 13 — The resistance resumes: Omar Mukhtar

Italy breaks its word after the Gharyan conference pledges allegiance to Idris in 1922 and storms Misrata; in the east, after Idris's exile, Omar Mukhtar — born in 1862 at al-Butnan, a desert sheikh who had memorised the Quran — leads the renewed jihad from Bir Bilal and Brega, and the struggle runs to 1931

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39185 — an old sheikh and his men on a green slope]

## Sub lesson 14 — The Fascist era in Libya

Fascist rule wars on the Libyan personality itself — gatherings and clubs banned, education strangled, Libya cut off from the Islamic world and its history distorted, fertile land seized by force for Italian settlers — and the resistance answers by moving abroad, to committees in Damascus, Egypt and the Levant

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 15 — Liberation and independence

At Alexandria in October 1939 the exiles rally to Idris, and the Cairo conference of 9 August 1940 raises the Libyan Arab Forces — 14,000 men who advance with the Allies from Egypt — and after the years of military administration and the United Nations road, the independent Libyan Kingdom is proclaimed

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.
