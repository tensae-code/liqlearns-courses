# The Atlantic slave trade — scale, coasts and the gun cycle — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The first captives crossed the Atlantic in —
a) 1492  b) 1532  c) 1630  d) 1807
**Answer: b) 1532** — 1532.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Numbers rose steeply after the 1630s as these entered the trade —
a) The Dutch, French and English  b) Only the Portuguese  c) The Germans and Italians  d) The Americans
**Answer: a) The Dutch, French and English** — The Dutch, French and English entered after the 1630s.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The 16th century's main source coast was —
a) Angola  b) Senegal  c) The Slave Coast  d) Mozambique
**Answer: b) Senegal** — Senegal was the 16th century's main source.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Angola supplied captives —
a) Only in the 16th century  b) From the 16th to the 19th century  c) Only in the 18th century  d) After 1807
**Answer: b) From the 16th to the 19th century** — Angola, 16th to 19th centuries — the longest run.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Europeans supplied chiefs with firearms so the chiefs would —
a) Defend the coast  b) Make war and deliver captives  c) Hunt animals  d) Guard ports
**Answer: b) Make war and deliver captives** — Guns exchanged for slaves: war produced captives.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Across 300 years, the number sold into slavery was —
a) 2 million  b) 5 million  c) More than 10 million  d) 100 million
**Answer: c) More than 10 million** — More than 10 million sold — excluding 2 million who died on the voyage.
**7. (Sub lesson 4)** What is Middle Passage?
a) the three-legged Atlantic system of goods, captives and produce
b) the Atlantic crossing of the enslaved, at the system's heart
c) a detail belonging to a different lesson in this course
**Answer: b) the Atlantic crossing of the enslaved, at the system's heart**
