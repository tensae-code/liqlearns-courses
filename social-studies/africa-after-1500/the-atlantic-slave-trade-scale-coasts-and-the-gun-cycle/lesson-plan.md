# Lesson plan — The Atlantic slave trade — scale, coasts and the gun cycle

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** The Atlantic slave trade — scale, coasts and the gun cycle
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12 — Africa after 1500 (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Across **300 years**, more than **10 million Africans** were sold into slavery — fed by the **guns-for-slaves cycle** along the coast from **Senegal to Angola**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met Africa after 1500 in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The first crossings — and the great rise** — The first captives crossed in 1532; numbers stayed small for a century, then rose steeply after the 1630s.
2. **The coasts that supplied captives** — Senegal in the 16th century, Angola from the 16th to the 19th, the Slave Coast, and at the peak the whole coast Senegal to Angola.
3. **The guns-for-slaves cycle** — Europeans supplied firearms to rival chiefs to make war; whatever a war's cause, its result was people delivered into slavery.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the trade's scale: 300 years, more than 10 million sold, 2 million dead on the voyage — and the 1532 and post-1630s dates.
2. Name the supplying coasts and their centuries.
3. Explain the guns-for-slaves cycle, with the Kongo–Ndongo example.
