# Zambia — primary resistance in Northern Rhodesia

## Do it first

Imagine an official arrives in your village with two demands: pay a tax in money you do not use, and send your young men away to work for strangers. Before you read on, say in your mind: what could a village do? Zambia's first answer was simple — no.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saying no to the tax and the labour draft

Zambia's History module teaches the colonial past as a two-stage resistance story. The first stage is called primary resistance: local, chief-led, village by village.

It began with refusal. In 1907 the Lunda under Kasanza, at Mwinilunga, refused the colonial tax. In 1909 the Gwembe Tonga refused it too. The tax was not only money — paying it meant accepting that a foreign power ruled you.

The second demand was labour. Colonial rule drafted men for forced labour, away from their homes and fields. The Namwanga and the Bisa refused the draft — another quiet, dangerous no.

## Sub lesson 2 — Armed resistance, land and the chiefs

Some refusals were armed. In 1897 the Ngoni under Mpezeni I resisted with arms. In the Luapula Valley, Mwata Kazembe resisted too. These were chief-led stands, fought by peoples defending their own country.

And what they defended was wider than land alone, though land was at the heart of it. They defended the authority of their chiefs against a power that wanted to rule over them. They defended chitemene, their own way of farming the land. And they defended independent churches — worship led by their own people, free of the mission and the administration.

The armed stands were defeated. But the module's point is that the refusal itself survived defeat — the memory of saying no became the ground the later, organised resistance grew from.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as two beats: the quiet refusals — the Lunda under Kasanza refusing the tax at Mwinilunga in 1907, the Gwembe Tonga in 1909, the Namwanga and Bisa refusing forced labour; and the armed stands — the Ngoni under Mpezeni I in 1897 and Mwata Kazembe in the Luapula Valley — defending land, chiefs' authority, chitemene farming and independent churches. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
