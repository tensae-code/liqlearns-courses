# Video script — Zambia — primary resistance in Northern Rhodesia

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: village by village, chiefs and farmers say no, as Zambia's Grade 8 History Teaching Module tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

When Britain ruled Northern Rhodesia — the country we now call Zambia — the first resistance did not come from political parties. It came from villages.

At Mwinilunga in 1907, the Lunda people under Kasanza refused to pay the colonial tax. In 1909 the Gwembe Tonga refused too. The Namwanga and the Bisa refused the forced labour draft — they would not be taken from their homes to work for the rulers.

Some refusals turned to arms. In 1897 the Ngoni under Mpezeni I fought. In the Luapula Valley, Mwata Kazembe resisted. Underneath every stand was the same defence: their land, the authority of their chiefs, their chitemene way of farming, and their own independent churches.

Even in defeat, the refusal stayed alive. Zambia calls this the primary resistance — the first no, spoken village by village.
