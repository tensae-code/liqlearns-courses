## Sub lesson 1 — The Scramble — a continent taken in thirty years

Now watch the map move — fast. **Between 1884 and 1914**, European nations **took control of the continent** in the rush remembered as the **Scramble for Africa**. The largely unexplored interior was **partitioned** — cut up into **protectorates, colonies and 'free-trade areas'** — often by men drawing lines on maps of lands they had never seen. In the GIF, the colours spread year by year from the 10% baseline of the earlier lesson until, by 1914, nearly the whole map has changed hands. Thirty years. That is the Scramble's measure.

## Sub lesson 2 — Berlin 1884–85 — fourteen flags, no Africans (with teacher guide evidence)

A rush like that threatened the rushers: European rivals kept colliding over the same lands. So the partition got a diplomatic machine — the **Berlin Conference of 1884–1885**, called **to regulate colonial rivalries before they became wars among Europeans**.

The teacher's guide adds the conference's working facts, and they are worth memorising as a row of chairs. Berlin was also known as the **'Congo Conference'**. **Portugal proposed it.** **Fourteen European countries** met in Berlin regarding the division of Africa. **France, Germany, Great Britain and Portugal** were the major players. And the fact the whole lesson turns on: although the conference was about Africa, **Africans were not invited nor involved**. Fourteen flags around the table — and an empty row where the continent's own peoples should have sat.
