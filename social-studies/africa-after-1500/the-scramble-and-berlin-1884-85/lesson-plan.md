# Lesson plan — The Scramble and Berlin 1884–85 — a continent partitioned on paper

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** The Scramble and Berlin 1884–85 — a continent partitioned on paper
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Africa and the Colonial Experience 1880s–1960s (Grade 10 History).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Between **1884 and 1914** Europe seized a continent in the **Scramble for Africa**, partitioning the interior into protectorates, colonies and 'free-trade areas' — and the **Berlin Conference of 1884–85**, called (the guide adds) by **Portugal**, with **fourteen countries** present and **no Africans invited**, was the partition's diplomatic machine.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the motives lesson — the learner knows why Europe came; now how the taking was organised. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Scramble — a continent taken in thirty years** — Explain that between 1884 and 1914 European nations took control of the continent, partitioning the largely unexplored interior into protectorates, colonies and free-trade areas.
2. **Berlin 1884–85 — fourteen flags, no Africans (with teacher guide evidence)** — Explain Berlin as the conference called to regulate colonial rivalries before they became wars among Europeans; add the guide's mechanics: also called the Congo Conference, proposed by Portugal, fourteen European countries, Africans not invited, France, Germany, Britain and Portugal the major players.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the Scramble's dates and what the interior was partitioned into.
2. Say what the Berlin Conference was called to do.
3. Recite the guide's Berlin mechanics: Congo Conference, Portugal proposed, fourteen countries, major players, no Africans invited.

## If the learner is stuck
- Learner imagines Berlin conquered Africa by itself → Berlin regulated the taking; armies, treaties and companies did the seizing on the ground. The conference's work was to stop Europeans fighting each other over the spoils.
