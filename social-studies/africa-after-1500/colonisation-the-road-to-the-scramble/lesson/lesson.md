# Colonisation — the road to the Scramble

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Coastal beginnings

European colonisation of Africa **opened with the Portuguese** and their **15th-century coastal voyages**. The **Dutch, English, French and Spanish followed**. For centuries Europe mostly clung to the coast — forts, stations, trade. What changed everything was the **19th century**: after it, Africa drew Europe as a **source of industrial raw materials**, a **market for its goods** and a **reservoir of cheap labour**. A continent once traded with at its edges was now wanted whole.

## Sub lesson 2 — The push inland — and the Scramble

**Explorers mapped** Africa's natural wealth, her **navigable rivers** and her **peopled market centres**, and **traders pushed from coastal stations to control the interior trade routes**. The book's stepping-stones: **France's small Senegal colony of the 1860s**; **Britain's defeat of Ashanti with Fante help in 1874**; and the **Dutch settlers of South Africa**, who **trekked inland after Britain took Cape Town in 1814** to escape British control. By the **mid-19th century France was moving on North Africa**. Then came the decisive stage, in the **1880s — the "Scramble for Africa"**.
