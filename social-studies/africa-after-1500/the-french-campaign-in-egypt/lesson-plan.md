# Lesson plan — The French Campaign in Egypt, 1798–1801

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** The French Campaign in Egypt, 1798–1801
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Napoleon's invasion of 1798 meets Egyptians resisting in Cairo's two revolts, fails before Acre, and ends in evacuation in 1801 — but its scholars write the Description de l'Égypte and find the Rosetta Stone, and the invasion wakes Egypt's national spirit.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The French Campaign on Egypt, 1798–1801** — Napoleon lands and Egyptians resist in Cairo's two revolts; the Levant campaign ends in the Jaffa massacre and failure before Acre; Kléber is assassinated by the Azhar student Sulayman al-Halabi in 1800, Menou dreams of a colony, and Britain and the Ottomans force the evacuation of September 1801 — but the campaign's scholars found the Scientific Institute, write the Description de l'Égypte and discover the Rosetta Stone in 1799, and the invasion wakes Egypt's national spirit
2. **Chapter 1: The French Campaign on Egypt and the Levant, 1798–1801** — France dreams of Egypt for a century first — Louis XIV in 1672 and Louis XV in 1769 both reach for her to wound Dutch and British trade; in 1798 the causes ripen — cut the Cape route, strike England's Indian empire, replace the lost American colonies with an eastern empire; and the Egypt it strikes is an Ottoman province of tax-farmers and decayed crafts, two societies — Turkish-Mamluk rulers and Egyptian ruled — governed by wali, diwan and beys
