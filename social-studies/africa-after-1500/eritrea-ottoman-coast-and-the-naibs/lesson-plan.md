# Lesson plan — Eritrea — the Ottoman coast and the Naibs

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Eritrea — the Ottoman coast and the Naibs
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Eritrea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From 1557 the Turks held Eritrea's Red Sea coast for some 300 years, ruling through the Naib family of the coast while the highlands stayed out of their grip.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Turkish rule of the coast** — from 1557 the Turks hold the Red Sea coast for some 300 years, ruling through the Naib family while the highlands stay out of their grip (Turkish rule of the coast)
2. **Modern history: the Turks** — the Turks seize the coast in 1557 and hold it some 300 years through the Naibs, never mastering the highlands (Modern history: the Turks)
3. **Turkish rule through the Naibs** — the Turks never take the highlands, so they rule the coast through the Naib of the Belau family, who judges disputes, settles grazing rights and shares the port taxes with the Bahri Negestat (Turkish rule through the Naibs)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
