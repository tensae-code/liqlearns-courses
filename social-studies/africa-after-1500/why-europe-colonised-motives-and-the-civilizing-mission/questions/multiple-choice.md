# Why Europe colonised — motives and the civilizing-mission claim — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The motives assessed as the driving factors of colonialism were —
a) Strategic  b) Economic — raw materials and markets  c) Religious only  d) Scientific curiosity
**Answer: b) Economic — raw materials and markets** — Economic motives are assessed as the driving factors.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Beside the economic motives, the lesson weighs —
a) Strategic and ideological motives  b) Only weather  c) Sport and exploration alone  d) African invitations
**Answer: a) Strategic and ideological motives** — Strategic and ideological motives sit beside the economic.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Pupils are set to assess, on the eve of colonisation, the role of —
a) Railways  b) Missionaries  c) Newspapers  d) Banks
**Answer: b) Missionaries** — The lesson sets pupils to assess the role of missionaries.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The 'civilizing mission' claimed that European practices were —
a) One option among equals  b) Civilised and universal  c) Inferior to African ones  d) Irrelevant
**Answer: b) Civilised and universal** — European practices were asserted as civilised and universal.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** In the same claim, non-European societies were held to be —
a) Uncivilised and inferior  b) More advanced  c) Equal partners  d) Unknowable
**Answer: a) Uncivilised and inferior** — Non-European societies were asserted to be uncivilised and inferior.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Kipling's 'White Man's Burden' appears in the book as —
a) A poem to memorise with pride  b) The period document of the civilizing-mission ideology  c) An African protest song  d) A trade manual
**Answer: b) The period document of the civilizing-mission ideology** — It is printed as the period document of that ideology.
