# Lesson plan — Why Europe colonised — motives and the civilizing-mission claim

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Why Europe colonised — motives and the civilizing-mission claim
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Africa and the Colonial Experience 1880s–1960s (Grade 10 History).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Europe colonised Africa for **motives weighed in the lesson — economic first**: raw materials and markets were the driving factors, with strategic and ideological motives beside them; the colonisers' self-justification, the **'civilizing mission'**, claimed European practices were civilised and universal and non-European societies uncivilised and inferior — Kipling's poem is its printed document.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands between the 'before' picture and the Scramble itself — the learner knows the baseline; now the reasons the baseline was destroyed. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Motives weighed — economics first** — Weigh the motives of colonialism: economic (raw materials and markets) as the driving factors, with strategic and ideological motives beside them, and the role of missionaries assessed.
2. **The 'civilizing mission' — and Kipling's document** — State the civilizing-mission claim plainly as an asserted structure of ideas (European = civilised and universal; non-European = uncivilised and inferior) and identify Kipling's 'White Man's Burden' as its period document, printed in the book.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

2. State the civilizing-mission claim in the book's plain terms.
3. Explain how Kipling's poem works in the lesson: as a document of the colonisers' ideology.
