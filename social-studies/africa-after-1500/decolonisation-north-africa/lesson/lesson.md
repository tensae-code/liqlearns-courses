# Decolonisation — North Africa

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Egypt, Libya, Sudan

**Organised national struggle turned decisive after the Second World War** — long struggle experience, education and over-exploitation pushing Africans to **coordinated political organisation**; nationalism grew **first in the north and south**. **Egypt:** **Saad Zaghlul's Wafd Party** won **self-government declared by Britain in 1922**, though not full freedom, and **after Wafd's victory Egypt regained its independence in 1936** (as printed). **Libya:** an Italian colony **freed of Italy by the war**, placed by the **UN under British trusteeship to 1949**, **independent in 1952** (as printed). **Sudan:** the **Al-Ashigga and Al-Umma parties** led; under joint British-Egyptian rule Sudan received a **three-year transition in February 1953**, **elected a parliament that year**, the **last British troops left in 1955**, and **independence was declared in January 1956**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Morocco and Tunisia

**Morocco**, split between France and Spain: the **Moroccan National Front (1947)** and **Istiqlal Party (1928)** led, with **Sultan Muhammad V** backing the demand for full independence; an **army was raised and guerrilla war fought**, the **Rif and Atlas peoples joining in 1955**, until **France signed Moroccan independence in 1956** and **Spain freed its zone** — the Sultan becoming **first king of a united Morocco**. **Tunisia:** **Habib Bourguiba's Neo-Destour** and **Muhammad Inafa's Communist Party** led; **armed struggle 1952–54** forced France to negotiate, and at **independence in 1956 Bourguiba became first president**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Algeria — the bitter struggle

**Algeria** was the hardest. The **Communist Party led from before 1945** against the **white settlers ("colons")** who tried to frustrate it. Algerians founded the **National Liberation Front (FLN)** and fought a **bitter struggle from 1954 to 1962**, setting up a **provisional government in exile in Cairo**. **France agreed to a referendum in 1961**; the people **chose independence**, and FLN leader **Ahmed Ben Bella became first head of state in 1962**. Why was Algeria's struggle bloodier? The settlers were rooted there — the assessment questions return to exactly that.
