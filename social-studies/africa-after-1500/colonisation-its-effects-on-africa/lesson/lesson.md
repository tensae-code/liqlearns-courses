# Colonisation — its effects on Africa

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## The book's verdict — four harms

After the taking and the ruling, the book stops and judges. **What did colonial rule do to Africa?** Its verdict comes in **four harms**. **First, it weakened Africa's economy and culture** — the working and the meaning of African life both bent under foreign rule. **Second, it exposed her wealth to plunder** — the resources Europe had come for flowed outward. **Third, it forced division and the acceptance of an alien culture** — peoples split, and a foreign way pressed on them as the price of advancement.

## The border that outlived the empire

The **fourth harm** is drawn on every modern map: **its wrongly drawn borders became a cause of conflict between states**. Borders ruled at Berlin and after cut through peoples and joined strangers — and when the flags changed at independence, the lines stayed. States inherited quarrels drawn for them in Europe. Remember this harm when the next lessons reach independence: the new nations won their freedom inside borders they had not chosen.

Tell your companion this lesson's story in your own words: The chapter's verdict in four harms: a weakened economy and culture, wealth plundered, forced division and an alien culture — and wrongly drawn borders that became a cause of conflict between states.
