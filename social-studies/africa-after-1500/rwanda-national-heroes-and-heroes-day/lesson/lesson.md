# Rwanda — national heroes and Heroes Day

## Do it first

Rwanda remembers its heroes every 1 February: heroes who ended the genocide, freed the country from bad rule and promoted unity — Rudahigwa, Rwagasana, and Sister Félicité Niyitegeka, who refused to abandon Tutsi families. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — What Rwanda's heroes did

Rwanda's heroes end the genocide, free the country from bad rule and promote unity. That is the contribution the P6 book asks pupils to learn: a hero is measured by what they did for the country and its people together.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rudahigwa and Rwagasana

Rudahigwa is remembered for independence, and Rwagasana against divisionism. The guide's Heroes Day answers pair each name with its deed, so pupils match the person to what they actually did.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sister Félicité Niyitegeka

Sister Félicité Niyitegeka refused to abandon Tutsi families. In Rwanda's telling she stands for the hero who will not leave people behind, even when leaving would be safer.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats: What Rwanda's heroes did; Rudahigwa and Rwagasana; Sister Félicité Niyitegeka. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
