# Libya under the Ottomans and the Karamanlis

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Spain seizes Tripoli in 1510 and hands it to the Knights of St John, who oppress it; the people appeal to Suleiman the Magnificent, and in August 1551 Sinan Pasha's fleet takes the city — Murad Agha becomes the first Ottoman wali and builds his mosque and school. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Ottomans come to Libya

## Sub lesson 2 — The first Ottoman era, 1551–1711

Libya joins a great Islamic empire; Andalusian refugees fleeing Spain settle and rebuild Derna, and by the mid-1600s Ottoman authority reaches Benghazi, Awjila and southern Cyrenaica

## Sub lesson 3 — Ahmad Pasha al-Karamanli

The Kouloughli officer Ahmad Karamanli takes Tripoli in 1711 with the people's backing and the Sultan confirms him as Pasha in 1714; over 34 years he unifies the country to Fezzan, rebuilds the fleet and shipyard, secures the caravan roads and patronises Ibn Ghalbun, who writes Libya's history

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39164 — a turbaned Pasha, a ship on the stocks, a camel]

## Sub lesson 4 — Muhammad and Ali Pasha: Tripoli treats with Europe

Muhammad Pasha's growing fleet wins a 28-article treaty from England in 1751 and Venice signs in 1756 — Tripoli deals with Europe as a state in its own right — until plague, debt and family feuds let the Sultan's man Ali Burghul seize the city in 1793

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39165 — a treaty signed between the Pasha and an envoy]

## Sub lesson 5 — Yusuf Pasha and the fall of the Karamanlis

Yusuf Pasha from 1795 restores order and taxes the sea lanes, then defies America — war in 1801, the frigate Philadelphia taken in Tripoli harbour in 1804, peace in 1805; wars with Sweden and the Italian states end in treaties, but debt and revolt close the dynasty in 1835

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39166 — a frigate struck through in Tripoli harbour]

## Sub lesson 6 — The second Ottoman era, 1835–1912

Direct rule returns; the Vilayet Law divides Libya into five sanjaks, councils sit in Tripoli from 1869, Ghuma al-Mahmudi resists the centre from the Western Mountain until 1858, and the province gains printing, the newspaper Tarabulus Gharb in 1866 and elected municipalities

## Sub lesson 7 — Libya on the eve of the Ottomans

Hafsid Tripoli decays into chaos and Spain storms its port in 1510; the Knights of St John then hold the walled city from 1530, while Cyrenaica obeys its tribal sheikhs and Fezzan its Awlad Muhammad rulers at Murzuq

## Sub lesson 8 — The first Ottoman era, 1551–1711

Libya becomes an Ottoman province in 1551; walis such as the Saqizlis raise mosques, schools and fortresses — though the era's later years slide into corruption and disorder as the empire weakens after Suleiman

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39169 — a mosque, a school under scaffolding, a fortress]

## Sub lesson 9 — Ahmad Pasha al-Karamanli

Chosen wali in 1711 amid chaos, Ahmad Karamanli ends the revolts, founds Libya's navy and its Tripoli shipyard, secures the desert caravan trade and builds his great mosque — his family rules for over a century

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 10 — Yusuf Pasha, America, and the dynasty's fall

The Karamanli fleet makes Europe pay tribute until Yusuf Pasha defies the United States — war on 14 May 1801, and the frigate Philadelphia captured in Tripoli harbour in 1804 — but debt, tax revolts and France's landing in Algeria undo the dynasty, and direct Ottoman rule returns on 25 May 1835

Reforming walis govern the province — Ali Rida Pasha applies modern laws, trims the foreigners' privileges and builds Tripoli's clock tower; Ahmad Rasim Pasha, the longest in office, founds the strangers' hospital at Bab al-Bahr
