# Libya under the Ottomans and the Karamanlis — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**Answer: Spain seizes Tripoli in 1510 and hands it to the Knights of St John, who oppress it; the people appeal to Suleiman the Magnificent, and in August 1551 Sinan Pasha's fleet takes the city — Murad Agha becomes the first Ottoman wali and builds his mosque and school (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: Libya joins a great Islamic empire; Andalusian refugees fleeing Spain settle and rebuild Derna, and by the mid-1600s Ottoman authority reaches Benghazi, Awjila and southern Cyrenaica (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: Ahmad Pasha al-Karamanli.
**Answer: The Kouloughli officer Ahmad Karamanli takes Tripoli in 1711 with the people's backing and the Sultan confirms him as Pasha in 1714; over 34 years he unifies the country to Fezzan, rebuilds the fleet and shipyard, secures the caravan roads and patronises Ibn Ghalbun, who writes Libya's history (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: Muhammad Pasha's growing fleet wins a 28-article treaty from England in 1751 and Venice signs in 1756 — Tripoli deals with Europe as a state in its own right — until plague, debt and family feuds let the Sultan's man Ali Burghul seize the city in 1793 (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**Answer: Yusuf Pasha from 1795 restores order and taxes the sea lanes, then defies America — war in 1801, the frigate Philadelphia taken in Tripoli harbour in 1804, peace in 1805; wars with Sweden and the Italian states end in treaties, but debt and revolt close the dynasty in 1835 (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
