## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Ottomans come to Libya** — Spain seizes Tripoli in 1510 and hands it to the Knights of St John, who oppress it; the people appeal to Suleiman the Magnificent, and in August 1551 Sinan Pasha's fleet takes the city — Murad Agha becomes the first Ottoman wali and builds his mosque and school
2. **The first Ottoman era, 1551–1711** — Libya joins a great Islamic empire; Andalusian refugees fleeing Spain settle and rebuild Derna, and by the mid-1600s Ottoman authority reaches Benghazi, Awjila and southern Cyrenaica
3. **Ahmad Pasha al-Karamanli** — the Kouloughli officer Ahmad Karamanli takes Tripoli in 1711 with the people's backing and the Sultan confirms him as Pasha in 1714; over 34 years he unifies the country to Fezzan, rebuilds the fleet and shipyard, secures the caravan roads and patronises Ibn Ghalbun, who writes Libya's history
4. **Muhammad and Ali Pasha: Tripoli treats with Europe** — Muhammad Pasha's growing fleet wins a 28-article treaty from England in 1751 and Venice signs in 1756 — Tripoli deals with Europe as a state in its own right — until plague, debt and family feuds let the Sultan's man Ali Burghul seize the city in 1793
5. **Yusuf Pasha and the fall of the Karamanlis** — Yusuf Pasha from 1795 restores order and taxes the sea lanes, then defies America — war in 1801, the frigate Philadelphia taken in Tripoli harbour in 1804, peace in 1805; wars with Sweden and the Italian states end in treaties, but debt and revolt close the dynasty in 1835
6. **The second Ottoman era, 1835–1912** — direct rule returns; the Vilayet Law divides Libya into five sanjaks, councils sit in Tripoli from 1869, Ghuma al-Mahmudi resists the centre from the Western Mountain until 1858, and the province gains printing, the newspaper Tarabulus Gharb in 1866 and elected municipalities
7. **Libya on the eve of the Ottomans** — Hafsid Tripoli decays into chaos and Spain storms its port in 1510; the Knights of St John then hold the walled city from 1530, while Cyrenaica obeys its tribal sheikhs and Fezzan its Awlad Muhammad rulers at Murzuq
8. **The first Ottoman era, 1551–1711** — Libya becomes an Ottoman province in 1551; walis such as the Saqizlis raise mosques, schools and fortresses — though the era's later years slide into corruption and disorder as the empire weakens after Suleiman
9. **Ahmad Pasha al-Karamanli** — chosen wali in 1711 amid chaos, Ahmad Karamanli ends the revolts, founds Libya's navy and its Tripoli shipyard, secures the desert caravan trade and builds his great mosque — his family rules for over a century
10. **Yusuf Pasha, America, and the dynasty's fall** — the Karamanli fleet makes Europe pay tribute until Yusuf Pasha defies the United States — war on 14 May 1801, and the frigate Philadelphia captured in Tripoli harbour in 1804 — but debt, tax revolts and France's landing in Algeria undo the dynasty, and direct Ottoman rule returns on 25 May 1835
11. **The second Ottoman era, 1835–1912** — reforming walis govern the province — Ali Rida Pasha applies modern laws, trims the foreigners' privileges and builds Tripoli's clock tower; Ahmad Rasim Pasha, the longest in office, founds the strangers' hospital at Bab al-Bahr
