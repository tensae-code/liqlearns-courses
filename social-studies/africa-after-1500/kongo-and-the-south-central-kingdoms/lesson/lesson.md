# Kongo and the South-Central kingdoms

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kongo's Christian kings

The kingdom of Kongo adopted Christianity early and voluntarily: its kings corresponded with Portugal as fellow monarchs, built churches, and sent embassies to Europe.

For generations Kongo dealt with Portugal as a partner. The poison in the partnership was the slave trade, which drained Kongo's people and corroded its kings' authority from within.

## Sub lesson 2 — Angola's kingdoms

South of Kongo, the kingdoms of Angola — Ndongo among them, famous for Queen Nzinga's resistance and diplomacy — faced Portuguese pressure from the coastal colony at Luanda.

Their wars and negotiations with the Portuguese run through the whole period: African statecraft under mounting external strain.

## Sub lesson 3 — Luba and Lunda successor states

In the savanna interior, the Luba and Lunda worlds generated successor states — kingdoms carrying their titles, regalia and trade connections outward across South-Central Africa.

A political technology travelling: kingship itself, exported from people to people.
