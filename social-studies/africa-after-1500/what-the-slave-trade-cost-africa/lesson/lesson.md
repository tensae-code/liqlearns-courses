# What the slave trade cost Africa

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — People — the first cost

The trades took people in their working and child-bearing years, and took more men than women to the Atlantic. Regions were drained, populations distorted in age and sex, and growth stalled where the raiding was heaviest.

Behind every number in this lesson stands a community that lost its young.

## Sub lesson 2 — War and the gun cycle

Guns came in payment for captives; captives were taken to buy guns. States armed, wars multiplied, and peoples fortified, fled or militarised to survive the cycle.

Political fragmentation followed: big stable states were hard to keep where slaving rewarded the raider.

## Sub lesson 3 — The long bill

The economic bill ran longest: labour lost, trades skewed toward human exports, and development distorted for centuries — the underdevelopment later colonialism inherited and deepened.

Historians still argue the totals. They do not argue the direction: the trade cost Africa people, peace and time.
