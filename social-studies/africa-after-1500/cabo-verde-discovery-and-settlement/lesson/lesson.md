# Cabo Verde — discovery and settlement

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of islands with no people on them at all — then people arriving by ship, building towns, rising in revolt, and at last proclaiming their own country. Which part of that long story do you think this lesson tells? Hold your guess; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Discovery of the islands

In the age of European expansion, Portuguese sailors find the uninhabited islands; the book asks who really "discovered" land no one lived on.

[Picture slot — a picture of a sailing ship approaching a dry, empty island shore, with no houses or people on the land — the student says who found the islands and why the book asks who really "discovered" land no one lived on.]


## Sub lesson 2 — Occupation and peopling of the islands

Settling empty, dry islands is hard; Europeans and Africans (many enslaved) people Santiago and Fogo first, then the remaining islands, farming cotton and maize, raising cattle, fishing and trading.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson as its beats: Discovery of the islands — In the age of European expansion, Portuguese sailors find the uninhabited islands; the book asks who really "discovered" land no one lived on.; Occupation and peopling of the islands — Settling empty, dry islands is hard; Europeans and Africans (many enslaved) people Santiago and Fogo first, then the remaining islands, farming cotton and maize, raising cattle, fishing and trading.. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory, the places and dates this lesson names.
2. Write who acted in this lesson, and what changed because of them.
3. Write two sentences explaining this lesson's contrast in your own words: empty islands, hard to settle: no one lived there, the land was dry, and peopling it took Europeans and Africans together, island by island, Santiago and Fogo first.
