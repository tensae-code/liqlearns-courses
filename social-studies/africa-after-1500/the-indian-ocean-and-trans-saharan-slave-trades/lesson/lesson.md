# The Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan slave trades

## Do it first

At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — The older trades east and north

Long before the Atlantic trade peaked, enslaved Africans were carried east and north: across the Sahara to North Africa, across the Red Sea to Arabia, and across the Indian Ocean to Persia and beyond.

These trades ran for many centuries. Their scale in any one year was smaller than the Atlantic's peak — their total span of time was far longer.

## Sub lesson 2 — Routes and destinations

Caravans crossed the desert in stages between wells; dhows rode the monsoon to the Gulf and India. The enslaved worked as soldiers, servants, sailors, pearl-divers and labourers across the Islamic world and the ocean rim.

Different routes, the same fact: people made into property, moved by other people's profit.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why this lesson stands before the Atlantic

Placing the eastern trades first corrects two errors: that slavery in Africa began with Europeans, and that the Atlantic was the only ocean of enslavement.

It also sharpens the Atlantic's true distinction — not that it existed, but its industrial scale, its racial plantation system, and its speed.
