# The Atlantic slave trade — how it ended — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Sugar became cheap because —
a) Fewer people ate it  b) Expanded production glutted the market  c) Ships stopped sailing  d) Prices were fixed by law
**Answer: b) Expanded production glutted the market** — Expanded sugar production glutted the market and cheapened sugar.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** After the Industrial Revolution, machines in Britain and France —
a) Raised slave prices  b) Flooded the market further  c) Ended farming  d) Banned sugar
**Answer: b) Flooded the market further** — Modern machines flooded the market further.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Planters' profits fell also because —
a) West African traders and chiefs raised slave prices  b) Wages rose in Europe  c) Ships sank  d) Land ran out
**Answer: a) West African traders and chiefs raised slave prices** — Rising slave prices squeezed the planters.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Britain outlawed slavery and the slave trade in —
a) 1750  b) 1807  c) 1885  d) 1957
**Answer: b) 1807** — 1807.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Olaudah Equiano and Ottobah Cugoano helped end the trade by —
a) Leading a navy  b) Exposing its horror in their writings  c) Buying captives free  d) Signing the Berlin Act
**Answer: b) Exposing its horror in their writings** — The two ex-slaves exposed the trade's horror in writing (names as printed).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** In Saint-Domingue (Haiti) the slaves rose in —
a) 1791  b) 1807  c) 1532  d) 1885
**Answer: a) 1791** — 1791 — 400,000 slaves rose and killed their white overseers.
**7. (Sub lesson 3)** What is 1807?
a) the organised campaigns that turned opinion against the trade
b) a detail belonging to a different lesson in this course
c) Britain's abolition of its slave trade
**Answer: c) Britain's abolition of its slave trade**
