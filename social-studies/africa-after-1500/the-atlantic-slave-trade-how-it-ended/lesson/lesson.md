# The Atlantic slave trade — how it ended

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — The trade stops paying

Trades end when the money ends. In the **18th century, expanded sugar production glutted the market and cheapened sugar**. After the **Industrial Revolution**, **modern machines in Britain and France flooded the market further**. Meanwhile **West African slave-traders and chiefs raised slave prices**. Planters' **profits fell** until Europeans concluded the trade **no longer paid**. The moral argument mattered — but the book's own chain is economic first: glut, machines, rising prices, falling profit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Britain acts — and Africa resists

From the **1800s Europe moved to stop the trade**. **Britain outlawed slavery and the slave trade in 1807** and set an **anti-slavery naval force to patrol the West African coast**. And Africans themselves helped end it. Two **ex-slaves from West Africa, Olaudah Equiano and Ottobah Cugoano** (as printed), **exposed the trade's horror in their writings**. And captives carried to America and the Caribbean **never stopped rebelling**: in **French Saint-Domingue (Haiti)**, with **400,000 slaves**, the slaves **rose in 1791 and killed their white overseers**. The trade was ended by law, by profit — and by the enslaved themselves.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion this lesson's story in your own words: The trade ended when it stopped paying — cheap sugar, machines, rising prices — and when Britain outlawed it in 1807 and Africans resisted, in writing and in revolt.
