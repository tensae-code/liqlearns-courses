## Sub lesson 1 — Taxes, forced labour — and a prophet

Cross now to East Africa, to German **Tanganyika**. The grievances were the daily ones of colonial rule at its harshest: **high taxation and forced labour — compulsory cotton-growing and road-building** laid on the people. Into that anger stepped the prophet **Kinjikitle Ngwale**, whose medicine — the **maji**, sacred water said to protect against bullets — did what no chief could do alone: it **united peoples across the south** in one movement. Grievance supplied the fuel; the prophet supplied the unity.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rising of 1905–1907 — and the pattern of a continent

The **Maji-Maji uprising of 1905–1907** became **the most significant African challenge to German colonial rule**. Remember the scale of that rule: Germany held four African colonies — **Tanganyika, Togo, Cameroon and Namibia** — and it was in Tanganyika that the challenge rose highest.

The unit's southern and northern sections complete the continental survey on the same pattern you have now seen three times: **primary resistance first** — the wars of Samori, Asante, Maji-Maji — **then the organised movements of the colonial period proper**, the parties and movements that would one day end colonial rule itself. The story of resistance does not stop in 1907; it changes its form.
