## Slavery beyond Africa — and a word that tells the story

When you hear the word slavery, do not picture one continent only. The book opens this chapter by widening the view: **slavery is not tied to race, skin colour or Africa alone**. In **Europe**, war captives of different peoples were sold as slaves. For long ages, **Slavs from eastern and central Europe** were sent as slaves to the **Middle East and North Africa** — so many of them that the European word **"slave" itself derives from "Slav"**. The name of a people became the name of the condition. That is how wide and how old this practice was, long before any ship crossed the Atlantic.

## Africa's older trade — growing from the 9th century

Africa knew the trade early too. **From ancient times, sub-Saharan Africa also sent slaves toward the Mediterranean.** That trade **grew from the 9th century** — but it **reached its height only after the Age of Exploration began**, when European ships opened the Atlantic route. Hold the order clearly: an old, wide practice first; a Mediterranean trade growing for centuries; then, after 1500, something far larger. The next lessons tell how that larger trade began, and why.
