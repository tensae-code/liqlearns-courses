# South Sudan — the Turkiyya and the Mahdiyya — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Turko-Egyptian rule in Sudan
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Muhammad Ali invades in 1820 with cannon to build a slave army and seize slaves, gold, ivory and timber; about 30,000 Sudanese are conscripted in 1820-21 and many die on the road to Aswan; Khartoum is founded, Sudan is taxed through provinces, and the south supplies the slaves

## Sub lesson 2 — Collapse of Turko-Egyptian rule and Sudanese response
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — weak successors, debt, anti-slavery campaigns that anger merchant traders, ivory violence and heavy taxes hollow out the Turkiyya, while South Sudanese resist forts and garrisons until disease and defection force their abandonment

## Sub lesson 3 — Mahdist Revolution (1881-1889)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Muhammad Ahmad, a Dongola boat-builder's son, declares himself Mahdi in June 1881 against foreign rulers, corrupt government and brutal taxes; his Ansar destroy Hicks's army at Shaykan in 1883 and Khartoum falls, ending the Turkiyya

## Sub lesson 4 — Mahdist administration and decline
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Mahdiyya imposes Sharia and zakat in place of colonial taxes, models itself on the Prophet's Medina under the Mahdi and his successor Khalifa Abdullahi, then declines
