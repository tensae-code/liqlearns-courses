# Video script — South Sudan — the Turkiyya and the Mahdiyya

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as South Sudan's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Muhammad Ali invades in 1820 with cannon to build a slave army and seize slaves, gold, ivory and timber; about 30,000 Sudanese are conscripted in 1820-21 and many die on the road to Aswan; Khartoum is founded, Sudan is taxed through provinces, and the south supplies the slaves.

Muhammad Ali invades in 1820 with cannon to build a slave army and seize slaves, gold, ivory and timber; about 30,000 Sudanese are conscripted in 1820-21 and many die on the road to Aswan; Khartoum is founded, Sudan is taxed through provinces, and the south supplies the slaves.

Weak successors, debt, anti-slavery campaigns that anger merchant traders, ivory violence and heavy taxes hollow out the Turkiyya, while South Sudanese resist forts and garrisons until disease and defection force their abandonment.

Muhammad Ahmad, a Dongola boat-builder's son, declares himself Mahdi in June 1881 against foreign rulers, corrupt government and brutal taxes; his Ansar destroy Hicks's army at Shaykan in 1883 and Khartoum falls, ending the Turkiyya.

The Mahdiyya imposes Sharia and zakat in place of colonial taxes, models itself on the Prophet's Medina under the Mahdi and his successor Khalifa Abdullahi, then declines.
