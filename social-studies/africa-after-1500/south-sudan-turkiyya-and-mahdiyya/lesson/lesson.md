# South Sudan — the Turkiyya and the Mahdiyya

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Muhammad Ali invades in 1820 with cannon to build a slave army and seize slaves, gold, ivory and timber; about 30,000 Sudanese are conscripted in 1820-21 and many die on the road to Aswan; Khartoum is founded, Sudan is taxed through provinces, and the south supplies the slaves. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 2 — Collapse of Turko-Egyptian rule and Sudanese response

Weak successors, debt, anti-slavery campaigns that anger merchant traders, ivory violence and heavy taxes hollow out the Turkiyya, while South Sudanese resist forts and garrisons until disease and defection force their abandonment

## Sub lesson 3 — Mahdist Revolution (1881-1889)

Muhammad Ahmad, a Dongola boat-builder's son, declares himself Mahdi in June 1881 against foreign rulers, corrupt government and brutal taxes; his Ansar destroy Hicks's army at Shaykan in 1883 and Khartoum falls, ending the Turkiyya
