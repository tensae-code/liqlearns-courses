# Lesson plan — Egypt between the Mamluks and the Ottomans

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Egypt between the Mamluks and the Ottomans
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** At Marj Dabiq in 1516 and al-Raydaniyya in 1517 the Mamluks fall to Selim's Ottomans, Egypt becomes an Ottoman province, and four stagnant centuries follow.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Egypt between the Mamluks and the Ottomans** — Three powers collide over the Islamic world — a Mamluk state ruined by the Cape of Good Hope route of 1498, the fleet's destruction at Diu in 1509 and its own infighting; Safavid Persia; and Selim's Ottomans, fresh from Chaldiran in 1514; at Marj Dabiq in 1516 and al-Raydaniyya in 1517 the Mamluks fall, Egypt becomes an Ottoman province and the caliphate's seat moves to the Ottoman state — four stagnant centuries follow, broken only by Ali Bey al-Kabir's bid for independence in 1768
