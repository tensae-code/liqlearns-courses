# Lesson plan — Eritrea — political parties and the UN question

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Eritrea — political parties and the UN question
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Eritrea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** After Britain replaced Italy in 1941, Eritrea's first political leaders — Ibrahim Sultan, Woldeab Woldemariam and Ras Tesemma Asberom — organised parties to demand the country's own future, until the deadlocked Four Powers handed Eritrea's case to the United Nations.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The party years** — after Britain replaces Italy in 1941, Eritrea's first political leaders — Ibrahim Sultan, Woldeab Woldemariam, Ras Tesemma Asberom — organise parties to demand the country's own future (The party years)
2. **The party leaders** — Woldeab Woldemariam, Ibrahim Sultan and Ras Tesemma Asberom lead the independence parties of the 1940s against the unionists, as the Four Powers fail and the UN takes up Eritrea's case (The party leaders)
3. **Political movements during the British period** — Mahber Fiqri Hager (1941) seeds Eritrea's first parties — the Muslim League and the Liberal Progressive Party demand independence while Ethiopia builds the Unionist Party — and the Four Powers, deadlocked, hand Eritrea's future to the United Nations (Political movements during the British period)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
