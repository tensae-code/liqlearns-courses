# Zimbabwe — the Second Chimurenga and independence

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Thirteen years of war — ZANU and ZAPU's armies, ZANLA and ZIPRA, opening fire at Chinhoyi in 1966 over land, taxes and unequal schooling — end at Lancaster House in 1979, and independence comes on 18 April 1980: a people who had no say now choose their leaders, control their resources and keep their own identity. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Second Chimurenga/Umvukela, 1966–79 (Grade 6)

The second war opens at Chinhoyi in 1966, where ZANU and ZAPU's armies — ZANLA and ZIPRA — open fire. Thirteen years of war follow, fought over land, taxes and unequal schooling. It ends not only in the bush but at a table: Lancaster House, 1979 — and independence comes on 18 April 1980.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Independence and self-governance (Grade 7)

At Grade 7, 1980 is re-read as a definition. Independence means this: a people who had no say under colonial rule now choose their leaders, control their resources and keep their own identity. Anything less is not yet independence.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: the second chimurenga/umvukela, 1966–79; independence and self-governance. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
