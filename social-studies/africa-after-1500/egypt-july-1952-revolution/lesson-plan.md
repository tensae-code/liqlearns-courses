# Lesson plan — Egypt — the 23 July 1952 Revolution

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Egypt — the 23 July 1952 Revolution
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The 23 July 1952 Revolution abolishes feudalism, makes education free, builds the High Dam, gives women the vote, and abroad co-founds the Non-Aligned Movement and backs Palestine, Sudan and Algeria.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The 23 July 1952 Revolution (Term 2)** — The revolution abolishes feudalism — agrarian reform from September 1952 caps ownership at fifty feddans — makes education free at every stage, builds the High Dam and Helwan's steelworks and gives women the vote in 1956; abroad it backs the independence of Sudan and Algeria and the rights of Palestine, breaks the arms monopoly with the Czech deal, co-founds the Non-Aligned Movement at Bandung in 1955, and unites with Syria in 1958
