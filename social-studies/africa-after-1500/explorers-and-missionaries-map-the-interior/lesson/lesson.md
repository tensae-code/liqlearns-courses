# Explorers and missionaries map the interior

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Europe turns inland

Between 1770 and 1870, European explorers pushed into Africa's interior — tracing the Niger, crossing to the lakes, following the Zambezi. David Livingstone, missionary and explorer together, is the name the book sets among them.

Their maps filled Europe's blank spaces with rivers, lakes and peoples — knowledge gathered at real cost and real courage.

## Sub lesson 2 — Missionaries beside explorers

Missionaries came to preach and stayed to translate, teach and heal — building schools and clinics, learning languages, and writing the first books in many of them.

Africans received, resisted and reshaped missions for their own purposes; conversion was never simply done to passive hearers.

## Sub lesson 3 — Knowledge that prepared partition

The explorers' and missionaries' reports did double work: they informed Europe — and they prepared its ambitions. Within a generation of the great journeys, the Scramble divided the mapped interior among empires.

That is this unit's sombre close: the map-makers were not the conquerors, but the conquerors used the maps.
