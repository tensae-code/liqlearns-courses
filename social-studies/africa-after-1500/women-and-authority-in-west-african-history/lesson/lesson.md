# Women and authority in West African history

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Long before colonial patriarchy, women in West Africa hold spiritual and political authority, at times over men — and Ahebi Ugbabe rises to become warrant chief and king, the only female king in British Africa. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Women hold spiritual authority

Long before colonial patriarchy, women hold spiritual authority in precolonial West Africa. Their power is real, recognised, and part of how their societies are ordered.

## Sub lesson 2 — Women hold political authority — at times over men

Women hold political authority too — at times over men. Female power in precolonial West Africa is not an exception hidden at the edge; it stands inside the political order itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ahebi Ugbabe — the female king

In colonial Nigeria, Ahebi Ugbabe rises to become warrant chief and king — the only female king in British Africa. Her story carries the older pattern of female authority into the colonial age, in a form no one could ignore.
