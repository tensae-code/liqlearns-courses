# Lesson plan — Women and authority in West African history

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Women and authority in West African history
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Gambia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Long before colonial patriarchy, women in West Africa hold spiritual and political authority, at times over men — and Ahebi Ugbabe rises to become warrant chief and king, the only female king in British Africa.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Women hold spiritual authority** — Long before colonial patriarchy, women hold spiritual authority in precolonial West Africa. Their power is real, recognised, and part of how their societies are ordered.
2. **Women hold political authority — at times over men** — Women hold political authority too — at times over men. Female power in precolonial West Africa is not an exception hidden at the edge; it stands inside the political order itself.
3. **Ahebi Ugbabe — the female king** — In colonial Nigeria, Ahebi Ugbabe rises to become warrant chief and king — the only female king in British Africa. Her story carries the older pattern of female authority into the colonial age, in a form no one could ignore.
