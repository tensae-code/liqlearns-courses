# Cabo Verde — end of the slave society

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of islands with no people on them at all — then people arriving by ship, building towns, rising in revolt, and at last proclaiming their own country. Which part of that long story do you think this lesson tells? Hold your guess; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Slavery is abolished

Slavery is abolished — the slave-owning society ends.

In your mind's eye: broken chains laid on a table beside farming tools and a school book. Say what ended and what had to be remade.

## Sub lesson 2 — Society remade without slavery

In the second half of the 1800s, Cabo Verdean society, culture and economy are remade without slavery.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson as its beats: Slavery is abolished — Slavery is abolished — the slave-owning society ends.; Society remade without slavery — In the second half of the 1800s, Cabo Verdean society, culture and economy are remade without slavery.. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory, the places and dates this lesson names.
2. Write who acted in this lesson, and what changed because of them.
3. Write two sentences explaining this lesson's contrast in your own words: a society remade: slavery ends, and in the second half of the 1800s work, culture and daily life have to be built again without it.
