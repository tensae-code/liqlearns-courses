Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 1 Teacher's Guide): the HBC spiral repeats ECD's identity → nation → heritage arc a year on with named content: Zimbabwe's 10 provinces and ethnic groups (Ndau of Chipinge, Tonga of Binga, Chewa of Hwange), the flag's five colours, Great Zimbabwe and Victoria Falls as tangible heritage, and Ubuntu/Unhu/Vumunhu named as the value system heritage carries. That way is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 2 Teacher's Guide): Grade 2 adds the nation's calendar to the symbols — Independence Day and Heroes Day with named heroes and heroines (Josiah Tongogara, Herbert Chitepo, John Nkomo, Sally Mugabe, Ruth Chinamano) buried at the National Heroes Acre, visited on an educational tour. That way is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 4 Teacher's Guide): Grade 4 is where Zimbabwe's deep past enters — the Khoisan first, then the Bantu migration of about 2,000 years ago with iron and farming — taught by group research and presentation, and immediately cashed out as heritage: the languages, stone cities and plant medicines modern Zimbabwe still lives on. Note: the guide's own answer key says the Bantu “migrated from central Africa in Kenya” — a garbled origin line kept as printed. That way is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 5 Teacher's Guide): Grade 5 Topic 1 is titled outright “Identity: Family history and local heritage”, and Topic 2 becomes narrative history: four pre-colonial states compared in tables (time, place, leadership, economy, religion, decline), then first European contact debated as a two-team poster argument — the guide explicitly teaches “balanced perspectives when studying history”. That way is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing.

Zimbabwe's own way (PlusOne Social Science Grade 6 Teacher's Guide): Grade 6 Topic 2 is a full national narrative in four acts — colonisation by treaty trickery and the Pioneer Column, the First Chimurenga of 1896–97, the Second Chimurenga of 1966–79, and the symbols-and-holidays settlement that follows independence — with spirit mediums given a named role in both wars. That way is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing.
