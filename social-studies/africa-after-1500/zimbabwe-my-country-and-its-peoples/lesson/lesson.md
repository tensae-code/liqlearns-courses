# Zimbabwe — my country and its peoples

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Zimbabwe is learned as a gathering of distinct peoples — Harare its capital, ten provinces, and each people known by where it lives, the tongue it speaks, its dances and its customs: the Ndau of Chipinge, the Tonga of Binga, the Chewa of Hwange and many more. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — My country and its ethnic groups (Grade 1)

Zimbabwe is named as home, with Harare its capital and ten provinces. Then its peoples are introduced one by one: the Ndau dancing muchongoyo at Chipinge, the Tonga fishing at Binga, the Chewa dancing Gule Wamkulu at Hwange — a country learned as its peoples.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethnic groups and their languages (Grade 2)

The peoples lesson returns, now with languages attached. Each group is known by two things together: where it lives, and the tongue it speaks — place and language, the twin name-tags of a people.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39385 — three figures with speech tags and place-and-language labels]

## Sub lesson 3 — Customs of different cultural groups (Grade 4)

Each cultural group in Zimbabwe keeps its own customs. The lesson lines them up side by side — custom next to custom — until difference itself becomes the national picture: one country, many ways of living.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39386 — four custom panels — a drum, a pot, a dancer, a woven pattern]

## Sub lesson 4 — The different languages of Zimbabwe (Grade 5)

Now the country's many tongues are counted and mapped. Language itself becomes the census of Zimbabwe's peoples: count the languages and you have counted the nation, people by people.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39387 — a map panel dotted with speech bubbles and a tally row]

## Sub lesson 5 — Ethnic groups and their cultures (Grade 6)

At the top of the cycle the peoples of Zimbabwe are paraded group by group, each with its culture. The nation is learned, finally and fully, as a gathering of distinct peoples — no people a footnote, each one a chapter.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 39388 — six figures paraded, each standing on its own chapter block]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: my country and its ethnic groups; ethnic groups and their languages; customs of different cultural groups; the different languages of zimbabwe; ethnic groups and their cultures. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
