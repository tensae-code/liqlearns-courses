# Contemporary Africa's agenda — the colonial hangover and after

## Do it first

A hangover is what the morning after a long intoxication feels like. The book borrows the word for something precise: states formally free for decades, still tied — commercially, militarily, habitually — to the capitals that once ruled them. This lesson lists the whole morning-after agenda, item by item.

## Sub lesson 1 — The colonial hangover — ties that outlasted the flag

The unit's closing diagnosis begins with a metaphor the book defines by use: the "colonial hangover". The newly independent states — themselves products of colonialism, their borders, economies and administrations drawn by the departed powers — kept stronger ties with their former colonial masters than independence's promise implied: trade, currency, military and cultural ties that continued the old orientation under new flags.

The named case is France's neo-colonial grip on its former sub-Saharan colonies — influence maintained after formal empire ended, the textbook example of neo-colonialism in the book's Africa. The hangover's point is structural, not personal: ties built over generations of rule do not dissolve at a flag-raising, and recognising them is the first step of the agenda that follows.

## Sub lesson 2 — The agenda list — boundaries to climate

The agenda the book lists is long because the inheritance was deep. Contested boundaries: peoples of one ethnic group shared between states by the colonial map, their homelands cut by lines drawn in Berlin — a standing invitation to dispute. Ethnicity and ethnic conflicts within the artificial states. Corruption, nepotism and client-patron relations — public office treated as family or factional property — hollowing the states the liberation generation built.

And the material pressures beneath the politics: poverty and unemployment for the majority the flags were raised for; population pressure on land, schools and jobs; and environmental and climatic problems — the droughts of the last lesson returning as a permanent condition to govern through. The unit ends not with a verdict but with an agenda: this is the list contemporary Africa works on, and the learner inherits it as a citizen, not only as a student.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the hangover defined with its case, then the agenda list complete — all of it, in order.
