# Lesson plan — Contemporary Africa's agenda — the colonial hangover and after

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Contemporary Africa's agenda — the colonial hangover and after
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — the post-liberation unit's close; Grade 10 Unit 7 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the newly independent states kept stronger ties with their former colonial masters — the "colonial hangover", France's neo-colonial grip the named case — and face contested boundaries, ethnicity and ethnic conflict, corruption, poverty, population pressure and environmental problems.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole Africa Since 1960 unit — this lesson is its agenda of unfinished business. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The colonial hangover — ties that outlasted the flag** — newly independent states, products of colonialism, keeping stronger ties with former masters; France's neo-colonial grip on its former sub-Saharan colonies the named case.
2. **The agenda list — boundaries to climate** — contested boundaries splitting peoples between states, ethnicity and ethnic conflicts, corruption, nepotism and client-patron relations, poverty, unemployment, population pressure, environmental and climatic problems.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: After the flag — debt, drought and the integration answer. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the colonial hangover and name its cited case.
2. List the agenda: boundaries, ethnicity, corruption and client-patron relations, poverty and unemployment, population, environment.
3. Say why the book calls these products of colonialism.
