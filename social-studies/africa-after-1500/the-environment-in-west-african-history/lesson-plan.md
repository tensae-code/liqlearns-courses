# Lesson plan — The environment in West African history

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** The environment in West African history
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Gambia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Climate, disease and landscape shape states and trade in West Africa as surely as kings do — the environment is an actor in the region's history, not just its setting.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Climate shapes history** — Climate shapes West African history. Rain and dryness set what can grow, where people can live, and how states rise and struggle.
2. **Disease shapes history** — Disease shapes history too. Sickness limits where people and animals can go, and bends the paths of settlement and power.
3. **Landscape shapes states and trade** — Landscape shapes states and trade as surely as kings do. Rivers, savanna and forest decide where towns grow and which routes trade can take — the land itself helps write the story.
