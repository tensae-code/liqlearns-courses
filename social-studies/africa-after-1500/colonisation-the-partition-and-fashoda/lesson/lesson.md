# Colonisation — the partition and Fashoda

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — The north already held — the west consolidated

When Berlin closed, **northern Africa was already in European hands**: **Egypt taken by Britain in 1882; Algeria and Tunisia under France**. After the conference **France consolidated West Africa**, taking **Dahomey, Ivory Coast and Guinea**. **Britain, Germany and Belgium competed likewise** across the continent. In a single generation, the map of Africa was recoloured almost entire.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fashoda — and Germany's fast expansion

In the Sudan the race nearly turned to war. The **joint British-Egyptian army under Lord Kitchener defeated the Mahdists at Omdurman in 1898** — and **a week later** word came that **Marchand's French force had reached Fashoda**, up the Nile. Two empires stood face to face: the **Fashoda crisis**. It was **settled peacefully by diplomacy**, and **from 1898 Sudan lay under joint British-Egyptian rule**. Meanwhile **Germany expanded fast**, controlling **South-West Africa (today's Namibia)** and **Tanganyika**.
