# Lesson plan — Colonisation — the partition and Fashoda

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Colonisation — the partition and Fashoda
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12 — Africa after 1500 (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** After Berlin the partition raced to completion — France in West Africa, Germany in the south-west and east — until Britain and France faced off at **Fashoda in 1898** and settled it by diplomacy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met Africa after 1500 in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The north already held — the west consolidated** — When Berlin closed, Egypt, Algeria and Tunisia were already taken; France then consolidated West Africa.
2. **Fashoda — and Germany's fast expansion** — Kitchener took Omdurman in 1898; a week later Marchand was at Fashoda — a crisis settled peacefully; Sudan passed under joint rule, and Germany took South-West Africa and Tanganyika.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say which northern lands were already held when Berlin closed, and which West African lands France then took.
2. Tell the Fashoda story: Omdurman, Marchand, a peaceful settlement, joint rule from 1898.
3. Name Germany's two fast gains.
