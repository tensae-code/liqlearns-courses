# Lesson plan — Problems of independent West African states

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Problems of independent West African states
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Gambia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Independence in West Africa opens with schools, roads and clinics — then coups, debt and one-party rule shadow the promise the new states began with.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The promise — schools, roads and clinics** — Independence opens with building: schools, roads and clinics. The new states set out to give their people the things colonial rule had not.
2. **Coups and one-party rule** — Then the promise is shadowed. Coups overthrow governments, and one-party rule narrows who gets to decide — the freedom won at independence is squeezed from inside.
3. **Debt shadows the promise** — Debt shadows the promise too. The new states owe, and owing limits what they can build — the schools, roads and clinics of the first hope are harder to pay for.
