# Lesson plan — Egypt — from the protectorate to the July 1952 Revolution

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Egypt — from the protectorate to the July 1952 Revolution
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From the 1919 revolution and Saad Zaghloul through the 1936 treaty and the tanks at Abdeen in 1942, Egypt's struggle for full independence ends with the monarchy's fall to the July 1952 Revolution.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Egypt from the British protectorate to the July 1952 Revolution** — The 1919 revolution and Saad Zaghloul, the declaration of 28 February 1922 and the constitutions of 1923 and 1930, with Egyptian women marching in 1919; the 1936 treaty turns occupation into alliance; British tanks surround Abdeen Palace on 4 February 1942 to impose a ministry; Nahhas abrogates the treaty in 1951, the Ismailia police battle of 25 January 1952 and the Cairo Fire follow — and the monarchy falls to the July Revolution
2. **Chapter 4: Egypt after the First World War** — From the 1919 revolution through the treaty age to the eve of 1952 — Egypt's interwar struggle for full independence
