# Benin, Oyo, Dahomey and Ashante

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Benin — bronzes and walls

The kingdom of Benin, in the forest of today's Nigeria, cast bronze plaques and heads of a quality Europe admired, and ringed its capital with enormous earthwork walls.

Benin traded with the Portuguese from the 1480s — pepper, ivory, cloth — as a sovereign power setting its own terms for centuries.

## Sub lesson 2 — Oyo and Dahomey — cavalry and palace

North of the forest, Oyo built a cavalry empire on the savanna edge, its horsemen projecting power where horses could live. Dahomey, by contrast, was a tight palace state — a king, a court, and an army famous in every account.

Two different answers to the same question: how to hold power where forest meets grassland.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ashante and the Golden Stool

The Ashante union was bound together by the Golden Stool — not a throne to sit on but the sacred soul of the nation, descended, the tradition says, from the sky for the first Asantehene.

A symbol doing constitutional work: whoever understands the Stool understands why Ashante held together — and why it fought so hard when outsiders threatened it.
