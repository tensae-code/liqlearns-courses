# The Atlantic slave trade — why Africa

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — America needed hands

Having found **America**, Europeans saw its **farm and mining wealth** — and wealth underground and in the fields is useless without hands to work it. They needed **much labour**. First they tried **enslaving America's own peoples** — the people the book calls the **"Red Indians"** — but under **European torment and new diseases** those peoples **could not supply** the labour the farms and mines demanded. So the Europeans looked across the Atlantic for another source of workers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why Africa could supply

They turned to **Africa**, and the book names the reasons plainly. **First, climate:** Africans lived in a **tropical climate like the New World's**, so they could work in its heat. **Second, cost.** **Third, supply:** **African chiefs' readiness to sell war captives and others** encouraged the trade — there were sellers ready at the coast. And where did most captives go? **To the Americas** — to **Brazil's coffee and sugar production**, the **Caribbean's sugar**, and the **cotton and tobacco farms of the southern North America**. Picture the triangle: ships and goods out of Europe, captives out of Africa, and sugar, cotton and tobacco out of America.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion this lesson's story in your own words: Europeans, having found America, needed labour for its farms and mines — and turned to Africa for named reasons: a matching tropical climate, cost, and chiefs ready to sell captives.
