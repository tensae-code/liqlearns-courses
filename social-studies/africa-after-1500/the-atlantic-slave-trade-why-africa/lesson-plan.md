# Lesson plan — The Atlantic slave trade — why Africa

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** The Atlantic slave trade — why Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12 — Africa after 1500 (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Europeans, having found **America**, needed labour for its farms and mines — and turned to **Africa** for named reasons: a matching tropical climate, cost, and chiefs ready to sell captives.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has met Africa after 1500 in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **America needed hands** — Europeans found America's farm and mining wealth and needed much labour; enslaving America's own peoples could not supply it.
2. **Why Africa could supply** — Africans lived in a tropical climate like the New World's; cost; and African chiefs' readiness to sell war captives and others encouraged the trade.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why enslaving America's own peoples could not supply the labour needed.
2. Give the book's three reasons Europe turned to Africa.
3. Name where most captives went and what they worked on.
