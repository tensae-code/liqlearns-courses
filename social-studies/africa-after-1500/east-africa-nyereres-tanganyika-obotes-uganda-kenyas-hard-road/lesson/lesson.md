# East Africa — Nyerere's Tanganyika, Obote's Uganda, Kenya's hard road

## Do it first

Three neighbours, three roads. One walks to freedom behind a schoolteacher's party; one negotiates a coalition with a kingdom; one bleeds for a decade because its best land was taken. East Africa contains the whole argument of this unit inside one region.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tanganyika and Uganda — the negotiated roads

Tanganyika won independence in 1961 under Julius Nyerere's TANU — the party that organised the territory into a single national movement and negotiated the transfer with Britain. Nyerere, the teacher-statesman, became the region's emblem of the organised, non-violent road: a movement so complete that independence arrived as an appointment kept.

Uganda attained independence in 1962 as a single entity under Prime Minister Milton Obote, through a coalition of the royalist Kabaka Yekka movement — the Buganda kingdom's party — and Obote's Uganda People's Congress: a negotiated unity of kingdom and commoners' party. Zanzibar followed in 1963, and in 1964 Tanganyika and Zanzibar federated as Tanzania — the union that made Nyerere's state the region's anchor. The former German colonies of Rwanda and Burundi close the section: Rwanda a republic at independence in 1962, Burundi a kingdom until November 1966.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kenya — the hard road

Kenya's road was not smooth, and the book gives the reason plainly: its peasants had been dispossessed. Kenya was a settler colony — its best highland land reserved for white farmers, its African majority pushed onto reserves and into wage labour on land that had been theirs. A movement for independence in such a country is also a movement for the land itself, and settler colonies, as the unit's pattern warns, do not negotiate their own dispossession away.

So Kenya's struggle was the region's hardest: the dispossession radicalised the politics, the settlers' presence hardened the colonial state, and liberation came through conflict rather than conference. Set Tanganyika's appointment-kept independence beside Kenya's bitter road and the unit's whole law stands visible in one region: organisation wins where there is no settler wall; where the wall stands, the road runs through it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four dates with their parties, then Kenya's one-word difference: dispossession.
