# Lesson plan — Zambia — secondary resistance and the road to independence

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Zambia — secondary resistance and the road to independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Zambia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In the second, "secondary" stage, Zambian resistance grew from village refusals into welfare associations, a banned religious movement, Copperbelt strikes, national political parties and UNIP's Cha Cha Cha campaign — until the colonial government conceded independence in 1964.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Zambia's own two-stage telling of resistance and on the names, places and dates the Zambian module itself names. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **From welfare societies to the Copperbelt strikes** — welfare associations grow from the 1920s, the Watchtower movement spreads and is banned in 1935, and Copperbelt workers strike in 1935 and 1940
2. **Parties, the Federation fight and Cha Cha Cha** — the Northern Rhodesia African Congress of 1948 becomes the ANC in 1951, a delegation fights the 1953 Federation in London, boycotts and pass-burning follow, and UNIP's Cha Cha Cha campaign of 1961 pushes the country to independence in 1964

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Colonisation — direct and indirect rule. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what Zambia's module means by secondary resistance.
2. Name the welfare associations, the Watchtower movement and the Copperbelt strikes of 1935 and 1940.
3. Follow the parties: the 1948 Congress, the ANC of 1951 and the fight against the 1953 Federation.
4. Say what Cha Cha Cha was, and in what year independence came.
