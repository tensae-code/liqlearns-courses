# Video script — Zambia — secondary resistance and the road to independence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: resistance grows from village refusals into unions, parties and nationwide campaigns, as Zambia's Grade 8 History Teaching Module tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The first resistance in Northern Rhodesia was spoken village by village. The second resistance learned to organise.

From the 1920s, Africans formed welfare associations to speak for their communities. The Watchtower movement spread its own message of defiance — so threatening to the rulers that they banned it in 1935. That same year, and again in 1940, the Copperbelt's miners struck, and the whole colony felt it.

Then came the parties. In 1948 the Northern Rhodesia African Congress was formed; in 1951 it became the ANC. When Britain imposed the Federation in 1953, a delegation went to London to fight it, and at home people boycotted elections and burned their passes.

At the end came UNIP and its Cha Cha Cha campaign of 1961 — a nationwide storm of protest. The colonial government could no longer hold on. In 1964, Northern Rhodesia became independent Zambia.
