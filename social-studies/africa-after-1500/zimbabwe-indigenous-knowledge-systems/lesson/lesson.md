# Zimbabwe — indigenous knowledge systems

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: The knowledge that kept Zimbabwe's communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft learned without books, and food kept by drying, smoking and storing — is named in Zimbabwe's lessons as a system in its own right: Indigenous Knowledge Systems, living technology worthy of the classroom. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Indigenous Knowledge Systems named (Grade 4)

The knowledge that kept communities alive — healing, farming, weather and craft, all learned without books — is named as a system in its own right. It is worthy of the classroom: not leftovers of the past, but a working body of knowledge with its own name, IKS.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Preserving food — the old ways beside the new (Grade 7)

The old food-keeping knowledge — drying, smoking, storing — is set beside today's methods and judged on its own merits. IKS appears here as living technology: the question is not whether it is old, but whether it works.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: indigenous knowledge systems named; preserving food — the old ways beside the new. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
