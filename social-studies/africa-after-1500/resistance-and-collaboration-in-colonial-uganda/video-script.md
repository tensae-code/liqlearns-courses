# Video script — Resistance and collaboration in colonial Uganda

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Uganda's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Kabaka Mwanga, Omukama Kabalega and Chief Awich resist while Ankole's premier Nuwa Mbaguta extends British rule and builds roads and schools; in 1907 the Banyoro rise in the Nyangire ("I have refused") rebellion against Baganda chiefs ruling them for the British.
Kakungulu plants Baganda agents as chiefs across the east and north, helps Colonel Colville capture Mwanga and Kabalega in Lango in 1899, and tries to push British rule into Karamoja.
The class sorts the names into two columns — collaborators Kaggwa, Kakungulu, Mbaguta, Mumia; resisters Mwanga, Kabalega, Chief Awich.
Africans react to foreign rule in two directions, and the newly schooled generation finds the natural resources suddenly hard to exploit without factories.
Collaborators hope for guns and rewards; resisters rise over land, imposed culture and treaties they were cheated in translation — and lose, the book says, to weak economies, internal wars, missionary teaching, and betrayal like the Jumbe Magoya's.
Muteesa I collaborates for firearms against his western enemies, Nabongo Mumia and Laibon Lenana calculate likewise, while Mwanga resists at once; Kakungulu then extends British rule over the rest of Uganda for them.
Resistance ends in destroyed villages and food stores, white brutality in Bunyoro and Hehe land, and a lost confidence in the traditional leaders and medicine men who "falsely led the masses".
Buganda collaborates because Bunyoro has resisted; Toro collaborates because Ankole has; and Kakungulu exports the whole Kiganda system of administration into eastern Uganda.
Toro and Buganda collaborate to win British guns against hostile neighbours; others reject and fight — the lesson makes the learner argue both columns.
