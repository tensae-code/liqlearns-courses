# Resistance and collaboration in colonial Uganda — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Kabaka Mwanga, Omukama Kabalega and Chief Awich resist while Ankole's premier Nuwa Mbaguta extends British rule and builds roads and schools; in 1907 the Banyoro rise in the Nyangire ("I have refused") rebellion against Baganda chiefs ruling them for the British.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The Road to Independence — Resistance and Collaborators

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Excel Standard Social Studies, Pupil's Book 5 — First Edition* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about The Road to Independence — Resistance and Collaborators

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Kakungulu plants Baganda agents as chiefs across the east and north, helps Colonel Colville capture Mwanga and Kabalega in Lango in 1899, and tries to push British rule into Karamoja.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Semei Kakungulu

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Excel Standard Social Studies, Pupil's Book 6 — First Edition* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Semei Kakungulu

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The class sorts the names into two columns — collaborators Kaggwa, Kakungulu, Mbaguta, Mumia; resisters Mwanga, Kabalega, Chief Awich.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Reactions to colonial rule in East Africa

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Excel Standard Social Studies, Pupil's Book 6 — First Edition* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Reactions to colonial rule in East Africa

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Africans react to foreign rule in two directions, and the newly schooled generation finds the natural resources suddenly hard to exploit without factories.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The Road to Independence

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *Home-Study Learning — Primary 6 — Social Studies* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about The Road to Independence

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** Collaborators hope for guns and rewards; resisters rise over land, imposed culture and treaties they were cheated in translation — and lose, the book says, to weak economies, internal wars, missionary teaching, and betrayal like the Jumbe Magoya's.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Response to the Establishment of Colonial Rule

**13. (Sub lesson 7)** Resistance ends in destroyed villages and food stores, white brutality in Bunyoro and Hehe land, and a lost confidence in the traditional leaders and medicine men who "falsely led the masses".
