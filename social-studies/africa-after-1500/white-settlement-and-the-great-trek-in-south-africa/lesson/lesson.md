# White settlement and the Great Trek in South Africa

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Settlement at the Cape

Dutch settlers came to the Cape in the 1600s to farm and supply ships; Britain later took the colony. Settler farming spread eastward, pressing on Khoikhoi and then Xhosa lands in a long frontier of conflict over grazing, cattle and soil.

Here, unlike the trading coasts, Europeans came to stay and to take land — the pattern that made South Africa's history distinct.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Great Trek

In the 1830s many Boer farmers — Dutch-descended settlers — loaded wagons and trekked inland, leaving British rule to found their own republics beyond the colony's reach.

The Trek is remembered by its descendants as a founding exodus. On the ground it was also an armed migration into lands African peoples held.

## Sub lesson 3 — Conflict over land

Trekker republics and African kingdoms — Zulu, Ndebele, Sotho and others — fought and negotiated over the interior for decades. Land taken then stayed taken; the map of South African landholding was drawn in these years.

Remember this lesson when apartheid's land laws arrive later in the course: their roots are here.
