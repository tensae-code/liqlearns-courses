# Shaka and the Zulu Kingdom

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shaka's military revolution

In southern Africa, Shaka transformed Nguni warfare: close-order fighters with short stabbing spears and great shields, drilled regiments, and tactics that closed with the enemy instead of skirmishing at a distance.

With that army he built the Zulu kingdom out of many small chiefdoms — by conquest, absorption and fear — in little more than a decade.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building a kingdom

The Zulu kingdom was more than an army: conquered peoples were incorporated, regiments doubled as labour and loyalty schools, and the king's homesteads anchored a new political geography.

State-building at speed — the same vocabulary of territory, tribute and legitimacy this course uses everywhere.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Mfecane — a subcontinent reshaped

Shaka's wars set peoples moving across southern Africa — fleeing, conquering, founding new states far away. This upheaval, the Mfecane, reshaped the whole subcontinent's map and peoples.

Later arrivals — traders, settlers, colonisers — met a land already transformed by an African revolution.
