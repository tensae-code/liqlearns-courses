# Before the Scramble — Africa trading, mostly unruled

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — A trading continent answers new demand

Open the map about 1880, and resist the picture the Scramble will soon paint. Africa is not empty, and it is not isolated. Industrialising Western Europe is hungry for **industrial inputs**, and Africa's commerce answers: the exports broaden to **vegetable and palm oils, groundnuts, gum, ivory, gold, hides and skins, rubber, cotton, copper**, and **cash crops — cocoa, coffee, tea, tobacco**. Caravans and coastal markets move it all. This is the age of 'legitimate trade' — African producers and merchants trading with the world, mostly on their own ground.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 10% baseline

Now fix the number the whole unit stands on. **Before the 1880s, only about 10% of Africa was ruled by foreign powers.** The footholds were real but small: **nominal Turkish rule in much of North Africa, Omani rule in Zanzibar, the British in Sierra Leone, the French in Algeria, Boers and British in South Africa, the Portuguese in Mozambique**. Find each dot on the picture. Everything between the dots — nine-tenths of a continent — was ruled by Africans. That is the baseline. The Scramble's speed can only be measured against it.
