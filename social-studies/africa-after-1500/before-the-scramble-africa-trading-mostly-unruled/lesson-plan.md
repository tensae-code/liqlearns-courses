# Lesson plan — Before the Scramble — Africa trading, mostly unruled

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa After 1500 · **Lesson:** Before the Scramble — Africa trading, mostly unruled
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Africa and the Colonial Experience 1880s–1960s (Grade 10 History).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Before the 1880s, Africa **traded with the world** — oils, groundnuts, ivory, gold, rubber, cotton and cash crops flowing out to industrialising Europe — while only about **10% of the continent was ruled by foreign powers**: the baseline the Scramble then overturned.
**This lesson's own level:** it opens Unit 2 in this title — the learner has followed Africa's earlier story in this title; now the calm before the partition. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A trading continent answers new demand** — Explain that industrialising Europe's demand for industrial inputs broadened African exports: vegetable and palm oils, groundnuts, gum, ivory, gold, hides and skins, rubber, cotton, copper and cash crops.
2. **The 10% baseline** — State that before the 1880s only about 10% of Africa was ruled by foreign powers, and locate the footholds: Turkish North Africa, Omani Zanzibar, British Sierra Leone, French Algeria, Boers and British in South Africa, Portuguese Mozambique.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the goods Africa's broadening exports carried to industrialising Europe.
2. State the pre-1880 baseline: about 10% of Africa under foreign rule.
3. Locate the six foreign footholds named in the book.
