# Video script — Zambia — nationalists of the independence struggle

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: a teacher, a king's son, a jailed party leader and two fearless women organisers carry one demand to the presidency in 1964, as Zambia's Grade 8 History Teaching Module tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Zambia teaches its independence through names — and it teaches learners to honour every one of them.

First, the leaders. Dauti Yamba. Godwin Mbikusita Lewanika. Harry Mwaanga Nkumbula, who led the Congress and the ANC. Kenneth Kaunda, who carried the struggle to the presidency in 1964, and Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe beside him. And the women who organised a nation: Julia Mulenga Nsofwa, known as Mama Chikamoneka, and Chibesa Kankasa, Mama Kankasa. With them, Mainza Chona. One demand ran through all their work: majority rule.

Behind the famous names stand the others. John Zanco Mpundu Mutembo — you have seen him even if you never knew his name: he is the man breaking his chains on the Freedom Statue, and on the currency. Moses Chinyama Luneta, Lewis Changufu, Nalumino Mundia, Reuben Kamanga, Sikota Wina, Justin Chimba, Grey Zulu, Aaron Milner — and the Mposa Mabwe stone throwers of Kitwe, boys whose courage and arrests sped the British toward the exit.

A teacher, a king's son, a jailed party leader, two fearless women, and stone-throwing boys: that is who carried Zambia to 1964.
