# Zambia — nationalists of the independence struggle

## Do it first

Think of the money in your pocket, or a statue you have passed. Zambia puts one of its freedom fighters in both places — and most people walk past without knowing his name. Before you read on, say why a country would teach its children the names, not just the dates.

## Sub lesson 1 — The leading nationalists

Zambia's module names the leading nationalists of the independence struggle one by one, and teaches learners to honour them by name.

Dauti Yamba and Godwin Mbikusita Lewanika stand among the founders of organised nationalism. Harry Mwaanga Nkumbula led the Congress that became the ANC. Kenneth Kaunda carried the struggle through UNIP to the presidency at independence in 1964, with Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe beside him. Mainza Chona stands in the same leading company.

And the module insists on the women. Julia Mulenga Nsofwa, known across the country as Mama Chikamoneka, and Chibesa Kankasa, Mama Kankasa, were organisers of the first rank — the movement's demand reached homes and markets because women like them carried it there. A teacher, a king's son, a jailed party leader and two fearless women organisers carry one demand — majority rule — from welfare societies to the presidency in 1964.

## Sub lesson 2 — The other freedom fighters

Behind the famous names, the module names the others — and first among them a man every Zambian has seen. John Zanco Mpundu Mutembo is the man on the Freedom Statue, breaking his chains, and his figure appears on the currency too.

With him stand Moses Chinyama Luneta, Lewis Changufu, Nalumino Mundia, Reuben Kamanga, Sikota Wina, Justin Chimba, Grey Zulu and Aaron Milner — youth militants and union men of the struggle. And the module remembers even the boys: the Mposa Mabwe stone throwers of Kitwe, whose stone-throwing, arrests and courage sped the British toward the exit. The lesson the names teach together is that independence was not one man's gift — it was carried by leaders, organisers, fighters and children alike.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as two beats: the leading nationalists — Dauti Yamba, Godwin Mbikusita Lewanika, Harry Mwaanga Nkumbula, Kenneth Kaunda, Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe, Julia Mulenga Nsofwa (Mama Chikamoneka), Chibesa Kankasa (Mama Kankasa) and Mainza Chona, carrying the demand for majority rule to 1964; and the other freedom fighters — John Zanco Mpundu Mutembo of the Freedom Statue, Luneta, Changufu, Mundia, Kamanga, Wina, Chimba, Zulu, Milner, and the Mposa Mabwe stone throwers of Kitwe. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
