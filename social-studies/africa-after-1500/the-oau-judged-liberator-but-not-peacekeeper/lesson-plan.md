# Lesson plan — The OAU judged — liberator, but not peacekeeper

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** The OAU judged — liberator, but not peacekeeper
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — the liberation unit's verdict lesson; Grade 10 Unit 7 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the book's double verdict on the OAU: an irreplaceable role in liberating Africa from European domination and white minority rule — and a failure to bring the peace, prosperity, security and stability the continent needed, for which it was dissolved at the 21st century's opening and replaced by the African Union.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Pan-Africanism — from Manchester 1945 to the OAU, the founding this lesson judges. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The credit side — liberator** — Pan-Africanism and the OAU's irreplaceable role in ending European domination and white minority rule.
2. **The debit side — and the successor** — the failure to bring peace, prosperity, security and stability; dissolution at the beginning of the 21st century and replacement by the African Union.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Pan-Africanism and OAU lesson in this title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the OAU's credited achievement in the book's words.
2. State its failure in the book's four nouns.
3. Give the outcome: dissolution and the AU.
