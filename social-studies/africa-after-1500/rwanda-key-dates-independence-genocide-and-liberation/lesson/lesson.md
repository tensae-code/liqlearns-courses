# Rwanda — key dates: independence, genocide and liberation

## Do it first

Three dates carry modern Rwanda on a child's timeline: 1962, when the country gains independence; 1994, the Genocide against the Tutsi and then Liberation on 4 July; and 2016, when Rwanda hosts the African Union summit. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1962 — independence

In 1962 Rwanda gains independence. On the P3 timeline this is the first of the country's own marked years: the year Rwanda stops being ruled from outside and becomes its own country.

[Picture slot — a picture of the world of this lesson as the book shows it: point to the picture and say what changed in this part of the story.]

## Sub lesson 2 — 1994 — genocide, then Liberation on 4 July

In 1994 comes the Genocide against the Tutsi. The same marked year carries its ending: Liberation on 4 July 1994, the day the genocide is stopped and the country is freed.

## Sub lesson 3 — 2016 — Rwanda hosts the African Union summit

In 2016 Rwanda hosts the African Union summit. Placed after 1962 and 1994 on the same line, it shows the timeline running forward: a country once colonised, then torn by genocide, now hosting Africa's leaders.
