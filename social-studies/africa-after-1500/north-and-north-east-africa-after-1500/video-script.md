# Video script — North and North-East Africa after 1500

Story-style script in the animated-map spirit: the map opens, peoples and armies move, routes light up. Super-simple voice; made later, when videos are made.

---

[Scene 1 — The Nile valley after 1500. The map and the scene build as the voice speaks.]
North-East Africa after 1500 was a valley of sultanates. Watch the names appear one by one: Funj sultanate, Sennar, riverain. This is the part of the story to hold.

[Scene 2 — Ethiopia's neighbours in a new age. The map and the scene build as the voice speaks.]
Around Ethiopia, the pieces of the medieval board rearranged: the old sultanates weakened, Egypt pressed from the north, and the Red Sea became an Ottoman lake.

Nothing here is yet colonial in the later sense — these are African and regional powers contesting the valley among themselves.. Watch the names appear one by one: regional powers, Red Sea, board. This is the part of the story to hold.

[Scene 3 — Ottoman North Africa. The map and the scene build as the voice speaks.]
Along the Mediterranean, the Ottoman Empire took Egypt and the Maghreb coast, holding North Africa within its system for three centuries — ruled through deputies, taxed for Istanbul, yet locally governed day by day.

So the north of the continent faced east, toward the Ottoman world, long before it was made to face Europe's empires.. Watch the names appear one by one: Ottoman North Africa, deputies, Istanbul. This is the part of the story to hold.


[Grade 11 depth scene — Ottoman North Africa in depth. The animated map carries the story as the voice speaks.]
The Grade 11 book's single page on this subject still fixes the fact: Ottoman rule extended over Egypt and the Maghreb coast, making North Africa part of the empire for three centuries — garrisoned, taxed and governed in Istanbul's name, while its cities' daily life ran on local rails. The names to hold: Ottoman rule, Maghreb coast.
