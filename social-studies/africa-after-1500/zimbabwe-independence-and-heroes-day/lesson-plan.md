# Lesson plan — Zimbabwe — Independence Day and Heroes Day

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Zimbabwe — Independence Day and Heroes Day
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Zimbabwe, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Zimbabwe's year is re-founded in holidays: on 18 April the country marks the 1980 break from colonial rule, and on Heroes Day it names those who died for that freedom — Tongogara, Chitepo, Nkomo, Sally Mugabe, Ruth Chinamano — laid to rest at the National Heroes Acre.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **National events and celebrations (Grade 2)** — On 18 April the country marks the 1980 break from colonial rule — Independence Day. (Topic 2, Unit 6 National events and celebrations)
2. **National events — the district joins in (Grade 3)** — The national calendar returns at Grade 3 with the district added. (Topic 2, Unit 6 National events / celebrations)
3. **The five holidays of the settlement (Grade 6)** — The year is re-founded in holidays: Independence Day, Heroes Day, Defence Forces Day, Unity Day — 22 December 1987 — and Workers Day. (Topic 2, Unit 10 National symbols and celebrations — the five holidays)
