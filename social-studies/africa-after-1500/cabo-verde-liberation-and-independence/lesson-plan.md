# Lesson plan — Cabo Verde — liberation and independence

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Cabo Verde — liberation and independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Cabo Verde, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** after remote roots of resistance, the PAIGC under Amílcar Cabral first tries negotiation with Portugal, then, refused, launches the armed struggle in Guinea — until Portugal's Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974 ends the war, and on 5 July 1975 Cabo Verde's independence is proclaimed, in unity with Guinea-Bissau.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: negotiation refused, struggle, then freedom: Cabral's PAIGC talks first, fights when refused, and independence comes on 5 July 1975. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the PAIGC, its leader Amílcar Cabral, and the order — negotiation first, then armed struggle in Guinea.
2. Give both dates — 25 April 1974 and 5 July 1975 — and say independence was proclaimed in unity with Guinea-Bissau.
