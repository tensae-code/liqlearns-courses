# Lesson plan — Egypt and Sudan under Muhammad Ali and his successors

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Egypt and Sudan under Muhammad Ali and his successors
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Muhammad Ali drives into Sudan to widen Egypt's borders, and Khedive Ismail pushes the flag to the great lakes and the Somali coast — until debt and foreign officials hollow the project out.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Egypt's civilisational role in Africa in Muhammad Ali's era** — Muhammad Ali drives into Sudan to widen Egypt's borders, crush the Mamluks who fled there and recruit soldiers; Ismail Pasha takes Sennar in 1821, his father inspects the gold country in 1838, and Salim Bey sails the White Nile toward the equatorial lakes; Khartoum rises as a capital with shipyard, hospital and factories and Sudanese students come to al-Azhar — until the Treaty of London in 1840 and the firman of 1841 fence the expansion in
2. **Egypt's role under Muhammad Ali's successors** — Khedive Ismail pushes the Egyptian flag to Lakes Albert and Victoria and the Somali coast, maps the continent in the Khedivial map of 1877, and builds schools, railways, telegraph lines and the Khartoum post office — but debt, crisis and foreign officials such as Gordon hollow the project out and open the road to Britain
