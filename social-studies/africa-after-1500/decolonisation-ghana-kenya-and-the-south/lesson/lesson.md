# Decolonisation — Ghana, Kenya and the south

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Ghana first — Nkrumah and the CPP

**Ghana** led the southward wave. The **United Gold Coast Convention (1947)** led the struggle, **Kwame Nkrumah among its leaders**. In **1948 Nkrumah founded the mass-based Convention People's Party** (as printed), using **strikes, demonstrations and boycotts of British goods despite imprisonment**. Britain agreed to negotiate; Ghana won **self-government in 1951** and in **1957 became the first sub-Saharan African country independent**, **Nkrumah its first leader**. First — that word is Ghana's place in this story, and the next lesson shows why it mattered for the whole continent.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kenya — land was the spark

**Kenya:** here **land seizure was the nationalism's spark** — a **1901 British law let Kenyan land be taken for white settlers**. The **Kikuyu, hardest hit**, organised the **Young Kikuyu Association under Harry Thuku** (as printed), demanding **protest and reform**; after a **long struggle Kenya won independence in 1963**. And the **others followed** Ghana: **Nigeria 1960, Sierra Leone 1961, Gambia 1965**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The south — the longest struggle

In the **south the struggle was widest and longest**. In **South Africa colonial rule ended only in 1994**, with the **anti-apartheid leader Nelson Mandela the first Black president**. From Accra 1957 to the South African vote of 1994 is thirty-seven years — one wave of freedom, breaking at very different speeds on different shores.
