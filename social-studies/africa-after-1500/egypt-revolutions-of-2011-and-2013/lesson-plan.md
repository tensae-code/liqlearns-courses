# Lesson plan — Egypt — the revolutions of 2011 and 2013

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Egypt — the revolutions of 2011 and 2013
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Rigged elections, the emergency law and rising prices ignite the revolution of 25 January 2011 in Tahrir Square; two years later millions fill the squares again on 30 June 2013 and the roadmap removes President Morsi.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Our two revolutions: 25 January 2011 and 30 June 2013 (Term 2)** — The rigged elections of 2010 that gave the ruling party ninety-seven per cent, the emergency law in force since 1981, rising prices and jobless graduates ignite a revolution organised through social media; for eighteen days Tahrir Square holds, popular committees guard the streets, the army protects the installations, and on 11 February 2011 President Mubarak steps down
2. **Chapter 8: The revolutions of 25 January 2011 and 30 June 2013** — The army council dissolves parliament in February 2011, the Freedom and Justice Party sweeps the elections, and Muhammad Morsi wins the presidency with fifty-one per cent; his November 2012 declaration seizes wider powers, his party fills the state's posts, debt and prices climb — until the call goes out for early elections by 30 June 2013, General al-Sisi gives one week for reconciliation, millions fill the squares on 30 June, a forty-eight-hour ultimatum runs out, and on 3 July the roadmap removes Morsi; the Rabia sit-in is dispersed in August, a new constitution passes in January 2014, and al-Sisi is elected president
