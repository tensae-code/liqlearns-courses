# Egypt — the revolutions of 2011 and 2013 — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Our two revolutions: 25 January 2011 and 30 June 2013 (Term 2)
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the rigged elections of 2010 that gave the ruling party ninety-seven per cent, the emergency law in force since 1981, rising prices and jobless graduates ignite a revolution organised through social media; for eighteen days Tahrir Square holds, popular committees guard the streets, the army protects the installations, and on 11 February 2011 President Mubarak steps down

## Sub lesson 2 — Chapter 8: The revolutions of 25 January 2011 and 30 June 2013
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the army council dissolves parliament in February 2011, the Freedom and Justice Party sweeps the elections, and Muhammad Morsi wins the presidency with fifty-one per cent; his November 2012 declaration seizes wider powers, his party fills the state's posts, debt and prices climb — until the call goes out for early elections by 30 June 2013, General al-Sisi gives one week for reconciliation, millions fill the squares on 30 June, a forty-eight-hour ultimatum runs out, and on 3 July the roadmap removes Morsi; the Rabia sit-in is dispersed in August, a new constitution passes in January 2014, and al-Sisi is elected president
