# Zimbabwe — my country, its anthem and its flag — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In Zimbabwe's first lessons, the red of the flag stands for —
a) the blood heroes lost to make the country free  b) the colour of the soil  c) the sunset over the land
**Answer: a) the blood heroes lost to make the country free** — red is taught as the blood of the heroes who made the country free

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** When the national anthem is sung, the children —
a) stand at attention  b) sit and talk  c) run outside to play
**Answer: a) stand at attention** — the anthem is the country's special song and children stand at attention for it

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** In the Grade 1 rhyme, the white of the flag stands for —
a) peace  b) snow on the mountains  c) clean uniforms
**Answer: a) peace** — white is sung as peace

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The national school pledge turns the flag's story into —
a) a daily promise of hard work, honesty and unity  b) a race at sports day  c) a shopping list
**Answer: a) a daily promise of hard work, honesty and unity** — the pledge is a daily promise of hard work, honesty and unity

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** At ECD B, the flag's colours are —
a) learned on their own, as national colours  b) painted over with new colours  c) only for teachers to know
**Answer: a) learned on their own, as national colours** — national colours are their own lesson at ECD B

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** The red of the national colours carries the story of —
a) freedom, bought with heroes' blood  b) the national football team  c) harvest time
**Answer: a) freedom, bought with heroes' blood** — red carries the freedom story

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** At Grade 2, anthem, symbols and pledge are taught as —
a) one kit of belonging  b) three separate exams  c) things only adults use
**Answer: a) one kit of belonging** — they are gathered as one kit of belonging

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** The child knows the nation's story when she can —
a) carry it in her own voice, reciting and singing it  b) copy it once into a book  c) hear it on the radio
**Answer: a) carry it in her own voice, reciting and singing it** — the story is carried in the child's own voice

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** At Grade 3, the national symbols must be —
a) explained, not just recognised  b) drawn without colours  c) kept secret from visitors
**Answer: a) explained, not just recognised** — each symbol carries a piece of the country's story and must be explained

**10. (Sub lesson 5)** Each national symbol carries —
a) a piece of the country's story  b) a price tag  c) a sports score
**Answer: a) a piece of the country's story** — the symbols are explained as pieces of the national story

**11. (Sub lesson 6)** The national school pledge matters because it —
a) binds the schoolchild to the country's story, daily  b) wins prizes for the school  c) ends the school day early
**Answer: a) binds the schoolchild to the country's story, daily** — it is a daily spoken promise binding the child to the country's story

**12. (Sub lesson 6)** The pledge is spoken —
a) every day  b) once at graduation  c) only on Independence Day
**Answer: a) every day** — it is a daily promise

**13. (Sub lesson 7)** After independence, the Zimbabwe bird and coat of arms appear on —
a) money and documents  b) only museum walls  c) school uniforms only
**Answer: a) money and documents** — they are placed on money and documents so the state is seen every day

**14. (Sub lesson 7)** The symbols after the war make the new state —
a) visible every day, in every hand  b) hidden until holidays  c) known only to soldiers
**Answer: a) visible every day, in every hand** — the settlement is symbolic — the state is seen daily
