# Video script — Zimbabwe — my country, its anthem and its flag

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Zimbabwe's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A small child learns that her country is Zimbabwe. She stands at attention for its special song, the national anthem, and she learns to read the flag like a story: green for the land, gold for the minerals under it, red for the blood the heroes lost to make the country free, and black for its people.

A year on, the flag's five colours are sung as a rhyme — red for the blood of those who fought for freedom, white for peace — and the morning pledge turns that story into a daily promise: hard work, honesty and unity, spoken by the child herself.

The anthem and the flag return, and now the colours step out of the flag to be learned on their own. The freedom story in the red and the peace in the white become the child's first national vocabulary — words for her country that she carries everywhere.

Anthem, symbols and pledge are gathered as one kit of belonging. They are recited and sung again and again until the nation's story is no longer the teacher's story — it is carried in the child's own voice.

Now the symbols are revisited as a set to be explained, not just recognised. Each symbol must give up its piece of the country's story: the child who once only pointed at the flag must now say what it means.

The pledge is now studied for why it matters. It is a daily spoken promise that binds the schoolchild to the country's story — said every morning, it ties one child's day to the whole nation's past.

After the war, the settlement is symbolic. The flag, the Zimbabwe bird and the coat of arms appear on money and on documents, so the state the people fought for is seen every day, in every hand.
