# Lesson plan — Rwanda — the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa after 1500 · **Lesson:** Rwanda — the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa after 1500 — built in the History expansion (Rwanda, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colonial rule turns social classes into ethnic groups with identity cards; divisionism, impunity, the Akazu, Hutu Power, Kangura and RTLM build the hatred that explodes in April 1994 — planned, executed by militia, stopped by the RPF, and answered with unity, healing and Abunzi reconciliation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Meaning and causes — classes made ethnic, hatred built** — Colonial rule turns social classes into ethnic groups with identity cards.
2. **Planning and execution — and how it was stopped** — The Akazu plan a 'final solution': lists of 'traitors', imported machetes, and the Interahamwe and Impuzamugambi militia.
3. **Ideology, denial, prevention — and the consequences counted** — Genocide ideology and denial are crimes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
