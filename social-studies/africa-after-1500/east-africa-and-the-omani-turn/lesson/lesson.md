# East Africa and the Omani turn

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Swahili coast under Portuguese pressure

After 1500 the Portuguese seized the Swahili coast's trade by force — bombarding cities, building forts like Fort Jesus at Mombasa, and taxing the monsoon commerce that had enriched the coast for centuries.

The city-states resisted, endured and adapted; Portuguese power on the coast was real but thin, resting on ships and strongpoints.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Omani turn

In time the coast's balance swung to Oman: Omani Arabs expelled the Portuguese from Mombasa and built their own maritime power, until an Omani sultan ruled from Zanzibar over the coast's trade.

The turn matters because it was not European: an Arab-African power, rooted at Zanzibar, ran the western Indian Ocean in the age before the Scramble.

## Sub lesson 3 — Inland peoples and their states

Behind the coast, inland peoples built and rebuilt their own states and trade networks, feeding ivory and other goods to whichever power held the ports.

Coast and interior were one economy with two stages — never read the forts without the caravans behind them.
