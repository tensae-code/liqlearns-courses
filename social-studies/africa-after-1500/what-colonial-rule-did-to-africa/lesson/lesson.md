# What colonial rule did to Africa — political, economic, social

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Political — lines on the map, thrones emptied

First register: the **political**. Colonialism left behind **artificial states and boundaries** — the straight lines of the partition maps, drawn with no reference to the peoples inside them: one people cut between two states, old enemies locked inside one. And within those lines, **indigenous authorities were destroyed** — kings deposed, chiefs reduced to instruments, the political systems of centuries dismantled or hollowed out. The picture's red lines crossing one people's homeland are this register drawn in one image.

## Sub lesson 2 — Economic — extraction, not development

Second register: the **economic** — and here the book's summary gives its bluntest verdict. Colonial economic policies **extracted rather than developed**: Africa was **weakened economically**, its **resources and labour serving the coloniser** — mines and plantations organised to ship wealth out, not to build the colonies up. In the summary's own judgement, colonial economic policies **destroyed rather than helped the colonies**. Hold the sentence exactly; Unit 7's story of post-independence economies begins from this wreckage.

## Sub lesson 3 — Social and cultural — a way of life broken

Third register: the **social and cultural**. Under colonial rule, **indigenous lifestyles and culture were destroyed** — languages pushed out of schools by assimilation and its cousins, customs suppressed or reshaped by foreign law and mission, communities reorganised around mines and labour migration. The summary's phrase is that Africa was **weakened culturally**, as it had been weakened politically and economically. Three registers, one verdict: the colonial period did not merely rule Africa — it unmade and remade it, on the coloniser's terms.
