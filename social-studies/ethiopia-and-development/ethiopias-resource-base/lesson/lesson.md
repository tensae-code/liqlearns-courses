# Ethiopia's resource base

## Do it first

A country has the largest livestock herd in Africa, rivers that could make about 30,000 MW of electricity, and land where 35% can be farmed — yet it is still poor. Which of these riches is real wealth already, and which is only a promise? Say your answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — People and land

Ethiopia's population is the second largest in Africa after Nigeria, growing 2.6% a year in the 2007 Census, with 84–89% of people living in rural areas. The young-age share fell from 49.8% in 1984 to 45% in 2007. Of the land, 35% is arable; much of the rest is pastoralist country. Rapid population growth carries costs: fewer jobs, falling wages, and pressure on housing, health and services.

## Sub lesson 2 — Animals, forests and energy

Ethiopia holds the largest livestock herd in Africa, held back by human and natural problems. Forest cover collapsed from about 30% of the land in the late 19th century to about 3% by 2011. Energy is called the most promising resource: hydroelectric potential of about 30,000 MW, plus high solar potential — power the rivers could make if dams and lines are built.

## Sub lesson 3 — Minerals — the unknown resource

Minerals are the least-known part of the resource base: their quantity and quality are still poorly known, with exploration under way in fuels, gold and precious stones. A resource that has not been measured cannot yet be planned on — which is why exploration itself is the first step in turning this promise into wealth.
