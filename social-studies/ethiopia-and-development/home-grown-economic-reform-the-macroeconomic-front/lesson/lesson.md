# Home-grown economic reform — the macroeconomic front

## Do it first

Growth built on borrowed public money brought inflation, few jobs and debt danger. A reform drafted at home — not imported — proposes to change the engine. From what, to what?

## Sub lesson 1 — Why reform, and whose

The home-grown economic reform answers the GTP model's limits: growth driven by debt- and aid-financed public infrastructure brought inflation, low productivity, too few jobs and debt vulnerability. Domestically initiated, it aims to sustain rapid growth in a stable macro environment with less debt and adequate jobs, shifting the engine from public to private and from demand to supply.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fiscal and financial fronts

Fiscal reform: modernised, fair and transparent tax administration, disciplined expenditure and public-debt management, auditing and project appraisal so budgets are spent as intended, action against the informal sector and contraband. Financial-sector reform moves toward market-based interest and exchange rates, capital markets, financial inclusion and a stronger National Bank.

## Sub lesson 3 — Exports, remittances and debt

Export diversification and value addition; remittance incentives through formal banking and quality foreign direct investment; and external-debt policy — fewer commercial loans, more concessional borrowing, rescheduling of what exists. Less debt, better debt, and more foreign earnings to service it.
