# Agriculture in the Ethiopian economy

## Do it first

One sector feeds the country, supplies its factories, earns most of its foreign money and employs most of its people — and shrinks as a share of GDP every decade. Is the shrinking good news or bad? Say your answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The headline position — two dated pictures

Agriculture's position must be read with its dates on. The NBE 2019/20 shares show agriculture falling from 40% to 33% of GDP across 2014/15–2019/20, industry rising 21% to 29% and services at about 40%. The book's features list quotes an older picture: about 45% of GDP, over 80% of exports, about 85% of employment and about 70% of industry's raw materials. Both are taught, dated, never blended.

## Sub lesson 2 — Problems and roles

Agriculture's problems: drought, soil degradation, overgrazing and overpopulation, land fragmentation, backward practices, poor marketing and roads, and rising input prices. Its roles run the other way: food and raw materials for industry, through backward and forward linkages; a source of capital for the state — with the Derg's extraction of the farm surplus for cities and the army, used ineffectively, as the cautionary case; the sector whose swings drive the whole economy; and the employer of over 80% that still cannot absorb the surplus labour.

## Sub lesson 3 — The internal structure — 65/25/10

Inside the sector, the proportions are crops about 65% of agricultural GDP, livestock about 25%, and forestry and fishery about 10%. Every sector figure in Unit 7 stands on this split: when a drought hits crops, it hits nearly two-thirds of the sector at once — the arithmetic behind agriculture's economy-wide swings.

## Going further — Older evidence — agriculture's problems and remedies (2011 guide)

An older guide splits agriculture's problems by cause. Human-made (policy) problems: land fragmentation, poor rural infrastructure (transport, markets, price information), shortage of credit facilities, lack of effective land-ownership entitlement, land degradation, and traditional practices. Nature-made problems: above all unpredictable weather and drought. Its keyed remedies match the causes: a land-tenure policy suiting the great majority of farmers, expanded infrastructure, organised farmer access to credit, and afforestation and reforestation schemes.
