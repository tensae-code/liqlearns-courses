# ADLI — Agricultural Development-Led Industrialization

## Do it first

A poor, farm-based country wants industry. One strategy says: build factories first. Another says: make farms productive first, and let farming pull industry up. Which did Ethiopia choose in 1994 — and why might it be right?

## Sub lesson 1 — The reversal of 1991–94

From 1991 the EPRDF reversed the Derg's strategy: agricultural price controls removed, state-farm assets privatised, structural adjustment adopted. In 1994 the home-born Agricultural Development-Led Industrialization strategy — ADLI — made agriculture the engine.

## Sub lesson 2 — Food self-sufficiency through productivity

ADLI aimed at food self-sufficiency through higher productivity, via three thrusts: expanding agricultural technologies, investing in agricultural infrastructure and inputs, and boosting rural non-farm sectors.

## Sub lesson 3 — The supporting system

Around ADLI sat roads, telecommunications and electricity, rural TVET, water development, microfinance, cooperative restructuring, and the PADETES extension system carrying fertiliser, seed, credit and advice to smallholders. ADLI remained the guiding pillar under every successor plan in this unit.
