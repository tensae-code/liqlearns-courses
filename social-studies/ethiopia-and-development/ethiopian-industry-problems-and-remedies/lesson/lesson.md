# Ethiopian industry — problems and remedies

## Do it first

Four gears drive an industrial sector — money, markets, technology and inputs. Imagine each gear jammed in turn and write what the factory experiences, before the lesson lists the four problem families and their remedies.

## Sub lesson 1 — Finance and marketing problems

Four problem families jam the sector. Finance: agriculture generates too little surplus, industries are import-dependent, savings are low and collateral demands are high. Marketing: weak domestic demand from subsistence-level purchasing power, missing market information, cheap-import competition, and consumer bias against local products.

## Sub lesson 2 — Technology and inputs problems

Technology: little local support, technology not based on local raw materials, and capital-intensive methods ill-suited to a labour-surplus economy. Inputs: costly, scarce foreign exchange for imported inputs — plus skills and work-ethics gaps across the sector.

## Sub lesson 3 — Remedies

The remedies: macroeconomic stability, a functioning regulated financial sector, dependable infrastructure, skilled human resources, an efficient civil service and legal framework, and industrial zones with full infrastructure — with improving the ease of doing business named a reform priority as growth shifts toward the private sector.
