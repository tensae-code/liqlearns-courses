# Video script — Education policy in Ethiopia — three regimes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Pre-1974, the Imperial government adopted no real education strategy. Its Education Sector Review recommended universal primary education and ruralised curricula — but was only published in February 1974, months before the regime fell.

The Derg closed the university and senior secondary schools in early 1975, deploying some 60,000 students and teachers to the rural 'zemecha' campaign, and nationalised private schools. Its objectives: education for socialist consciousness, production, and science and research — while spreading schools to neglected small towns and rural areas.

After 1991 enrolment ratios rose and the Education Sector Development Programme organised the work into six components: primary education, secondary education, technical-vocational training, teacher training, tertiary education, and institutional capacity building. Standing problems: relevance, quality, access and equity, regional and rural–urban and gender disparities, weak facilities and teacher training, overcrowded classes, textbook shortages and high dropout. Remedies centre on community participation, materials, narrowing inequalities, inspecting private provision, and better-paid, better-trained teachers.
