# After the reform — Ethiopia's 1990s scoreboard

## Do it first

A reform program ends. Before reading its scoreboard: which single line would you check first to call it a success — the deficit, investment, or saving? Say why.

## Sub lesson 1 — The diagnosis before the reform

The pre-history is stated as diagnosis: 1960–1992 growth was dismal because of rigid macroeconomic policies, protracted war and recurrent drought — drought and famine struck at least nine times in the period and, in a rain-fed economy, mattered most. Any scoreboard for the reform years is read against that baseline.

## Sub lesson 2 — Investment after the reform

Post-reform, approved investment projects rose rapidly: approvals totalling 41.5 billion birr for 1992/93–1998/99, with 1,491 projects actually commencing operations for 8.8 billion birr. The gap between approval and commencement is itself a scoreboard line — permission is not production.

## Sub lesson 3 — The deficit verdict — and the saving exception

The key's verdict on public finance: the public-sector deficit declined throughout except 1993/94 and 1998/99 — the Ethio-Eritrea war year, with the highest recurrent expenditure — so on deficit reduction the programme counts as a success. Demand-side indicators improved across the board except Gross Domestic Saving, and gross capital formation's share rose on the private-sector enabling environment and resource mobilisation.
