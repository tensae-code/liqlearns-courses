# Bi-modal strategy — small farms and big farms together

## Do it first

Now run both tracks at once: help the small farms AND build a large commercial farm sector beside them. What could go right — and what could go badly wrong for the small farmers? Argue it before the lesson weighs the dual track.

## Sub lesson 1 — The dual track

The bi-modal strategy is the dual-track alternative, practised as in Mexico and Colombia: intensify small farms and simultaneously build a strong commercial sector, in which entrepreneurial farmers may accumulate land while capital and technology flow in, and large-scale diversification is promoted.

[Picture 24943 — a split landscape — small plots on one side, one large mechanised commercial farm on the other]

## Sub lesson 2 — What the second track adds

The commercial track brings what smallholdings struggle to supply: accumulated land, capital, technology and large-scale diversification into new products — production the small-farm track alone may never reach.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its costs — the mirror of uni-modal's gains

The costs are the mirror of uni-modal's gains: rural differentiation into agricultural capitalists and landless labourers, eviction of poor peasants, loss of land for the majority, and few off-farm jobs to catch those displaced.
