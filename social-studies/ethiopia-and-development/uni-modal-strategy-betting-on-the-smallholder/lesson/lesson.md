# Uni-modal strategy — betting on the smallholder

## Do it first

Should a poor country pour its farm support into millions of tiny farms, or into a few big ones? Take the smallholder side first: what would you give every small farm, and what would you refuse to do to the farmers?

## Sub lesson 1 — The strategy — intensify the small farm

The uni-modal strategy transforms agriculture by intensifying small-scale peasant farms: peasants keep their own land and family labour — as in Japan, Thailand and China — while divisible modern innovations spread within the existing smallholding structure, with improved seeds and fertiliser supplied on revolving credit and a food-crop focus for food security.

[Picture 24942 — many small farms each receiving the same seed-and-fertiliser package]

## Sub lesson 2 — Its strengths

It is pro-poor, protects peasants from eviction, reduces rural poverty and reduces out-migration — development that reaches the many small farms where most rural people actually live.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its weaknesses

It neglects high-value market crops, market and weather information, and infrastructure — and tiny holdings cannot take large-scale inputs, which caps how far intensification alone can go.
