# Agriculture first or industry first

## Do it first

A government can push one sector first. Western economists long said: factories first. Ethiopia chose: farms first. Before reading, pick a side and give your strongest reason — you will be asked to defend the other side too.

## Sub lesson 1 — The industry-first case

Western economists long prioritised industry, seeing it as the sector with more opportunities to lead development — factories raise output per worker, earn foreign exchange and pull new skills into the economy. On this view, a country develops by industrialising, and agriculture follows behind the factories.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why that strategy fails where farming dominates

The guide's answer is about fit: a strategy that leads with industry cannot succeed where agriculture dominates output, exports and employment. If most people farm, most income is farm income, and most exports are farm goods, then raising farm productivity moves the whole economy — while factories wait for customers the farms cannot yet feed or pay.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ethiopia's settlement — ADLI

So Ethiopia prioritised agriculture — which is why ADLI, Agricultural Development-Led Industrialization, is the country's functional strategy: develop agriculture first, and let its rising incomes and surpluses lead industrialisation behind it. The activity key concedes both sides can argue their case, but lands on agriculture first, on Ethiopia's objective realities.
