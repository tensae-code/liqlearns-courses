# Video script — Imperial-era agriculture — commercial farms and packages

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Imperial policy prioritised industry but set agriculture production targets for cereals, cotton, cattle and coffee. The promised land reform was ignored — which the book blames for the plan's failure: output was demanded from a land system left untouched.

The third five-year plan's first route was large-scale mechanised commercial farms, encouraged with credit, five-year tax holidays on investments above 200,000 birr, cheap land and duty-free machinery. They reached only about 5% of agricultural output before nationalisation into state farms in 1975.

The second route was package projects meant to repeat India's Green Revolution: comprehensive packages of inputs, credit and know-how through CADU, WADU and the Adaa project. They raised participants' incomes but at high cost and with increased tenant evictions — and were followed by the cheaper, thinner minimum package programme.
