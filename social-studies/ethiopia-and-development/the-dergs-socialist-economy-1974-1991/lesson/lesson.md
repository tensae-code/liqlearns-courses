# The Derg's socialist economy, 1974–1991

## Do it first

A new regime nationalises the land, fixes grain prices below market and orders farmers to deliver quotas. Predict output, markets and morale — phase by phase, before the record is read.

## Sub lesson 1 — The socialist turn

The Derg nationalised rural land and productive assets, abolished tenancy, put commercial farms under state control, pushed collectivisation through resettlement and villagisation, held an overvalued currency, and let the Agricultural Marketing Corporation fix grain prices below market with compulsory quotas.

## Sub lesson 2 — Phases one and two — dislocation, then recovery

1974–78 brought dislocation: GDP +0.4% a year under nationalisation, instability and military spending. 1978–80 brought recovery under the Zemecha campaign: GDP +5.7%, manufacturing +18.9%. The system's best years came early and briefly.

## Sub lesson 3 — Phases three and four — setback and the ten-year plan

1980–85 was setback: drought, stagnant manufacturing, foreign-exchange shortage, defence costs. 1985–90 ran a ten-year perspective plan (1984–94) leaning on cooperatives and state farms, with GDP +5%, undercut by the 1984/85 drought's lingering effects. Seventeen years end where the next lesson's reversal begins.
