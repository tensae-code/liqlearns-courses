# Agriculture under the Derg — nationalisation and collectivisation

## Do it first

A new regime nationalises all rural land, bans private farm investment, fixes grain prices low and orders peasants into collectives. Predict the countryside's reply before the book delivers its verdict on the whole design.

## Sub lesson 1 — Land and farms under state control

The Derg recentred farm strategy on central planning: rural land was nationalised and distributed, commercial farms put under state control, tenancy abolished, and private investment in agriculture prohibited.

[Picture 24945 — state farm and cooperative signboards over fields, a grain-quota truck being loaded at a fixed price]

## Sub lesson 2 — Collectives, quotas and controls

Peasants were involuntarily collectivised into associations and cooperatives, with forced villagisation and resettlement. The state controlled input and output markets, demanded forced grain-quota deliveries at low fixed prices, and restricted moving produce between regions.

## Sub lesson 3 — The verdict — and the projects' path

The package projects were folded through ARDU and BARDU into PADEP. The book's verdict is blunt: these interventions were the primary reason the agricultural sector failed to develop in the period.
