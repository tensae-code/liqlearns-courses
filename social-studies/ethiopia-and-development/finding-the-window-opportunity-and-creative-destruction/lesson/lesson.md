# Finding the window — opportunity and creative destruction

## Do it first

A town queue forms daily for a service nobody provides. Is that an opportunity — or just a queue? What else must be true, and for how long, before a founder should bet savings on it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Ideas are not opportunities

Not every idea is an opportunity: an entrepreneurial opportunity exists where identifiable consumer demand meets the possibility of satisfying it, judged after developing an opportunity-seeking mind-set and researching. Opportunities come by deliberate search, by chance, and most often by recognising a problem and working to solve it. The guide adds (teacher guide — evidence): most good opportunities result from the entrepreneur being alert to possibilities, and fruitful sources include consumers and business associates.

## Sub lesson 2 — Judging the window

The guide's evaluation criteria (teacher guide — evidence): understand the cause of the opportunity, since market size and time dimension may differ from what was anticipated; market size and the length of the window are the primary factors determining risk and reward; the capital amount forms the basis of return, weighed against subsequent opportunities; and the opportunity must fit the entrepreneur's personal skills and goals, with time and effort committable.

## Sub lesson 3 — Creative destruction — Schumpeter's five routes

Schumpeter is introduced: entrepreneurial innovation is the disruptive force 'creative destruction' that sustains growth while destroying established firms and interrupting jobs. His five routes to new opportunity: a new market for an existing product; a new, cheaper source of resources; existing technology producing an old product in a new way; existing technology producing a new product; new technology producing a new product.
