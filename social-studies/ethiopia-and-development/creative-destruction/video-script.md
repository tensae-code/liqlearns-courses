The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Creative destruction is Schumpeter's argument that capitalism develops through imbalance, not equilibrium: new ideas make products obsolete, close companies and cost jobs, yet are necessary for better solutions. The destruction is real and so is the creation — the same wave carries both, which is why the process cannot simply be stopped without stopping progress.

Nokia and Kodak are the cautionary pair: once leaders, both failed to see their industries change — Kodak over film in a digital age, Nokia over handsets in the smartphone age. Leadership is no protection; the wave does not ask who used to be biggest. The gallery of innovators is the human face of the same force, seen from the winning side.

The book's gallery names eleven: Steve Jobs, Marie Curie, Elon Musk, Ann Kiessling, Nikola Tesla, Amanda Jones, Thomas Edison, Grace Hopper, Leonardo da Vinci, Josephine Cochrane and Alexander Graham Bell — each tied to their field. Read as a set, they span centuries and sciences: innovation is a human habit, not one industry or one era.
