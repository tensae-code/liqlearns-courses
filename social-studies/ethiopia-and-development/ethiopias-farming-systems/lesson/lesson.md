# Ethiopia's farming systems

## Do it first

Three farms: a family plot under 2 hectares growing crops and keeping animals; a herding family moving camels across dry lowland; a vast mechanised estate in the Rift Valley. Which feeds Ethiopia most? Say your answer first — it surprises most people.

## Sub lesson 1 — Smallholders — the dominant producer

The smallholder system is the dominant producer: over 90% of production on about 95% of cropped area, in mixed crop-and-livestock farming by some 10.58 million households averaging five people, over 86% of them cultivating under 2 hectares. Ethiopia eats, overwhelmingly, from family plots smaller than two football pitches.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pastoralism — herding the dry half

The pastoral system herds across about 40% of the land — the arid and semi-arid lowlands below 1,500 m — holding about 20% of the cattle, 25% of the sheep, 73% of the goats and about a million camels for some 5.5 million people. It produces live-animal exports and shares land with wildlife in Omo, Gambella and Awash, against chronic food shortage and historically little government support.

## Sub lesson 3 — Commercial and state farms

Commercial and state farms were introduced under the third five-year plan (1968–73) in Awash and the Rift Valley, confiscated in 1974, and run through state-farm ministries — pampered but inefficient and corrupt. They shrank to about 5% of cropland and under 10% of production; only 13 state farms remain, on 156,040 hectares, against 1,148 agricultural investment permits issued in 1992/93–97/98, 508 of them operational.

## Going further — Older evidence — the three farming systems in numbers (2011 guide)

An older guide gives the farming systems their numbers. Agriculture's GDP share fell from 76% in the early 1960s to 45% in 2003/04, and its growth ran below population growth. Inside the sector: crop production supplies about 65% of sector GDP, livestock 25%, allied activities (forestry, fishery) 10%. The smallholder system dominates — 90% of production, about 95% of cropped area, mixed farming, over 86% of its households cultivating under 2 hectares. The pastoral nomadic system covers about 40% of the country's area, arid and semi-arid, cattle-centred, with settlement planned around Awash-basin irrigation. Modern commercial farming began in the late 1960s, was nationalised with the rural-land reform of February 1975, and revived after 1991 — yet only 26.8% of the investment permits issued in 1992/93–1997/98 became operational; state farms hold about 5% of cropland and are being privatised except for strategic types.
