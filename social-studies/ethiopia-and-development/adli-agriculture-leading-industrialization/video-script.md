# Video script — ADLI — agriculture leading industrialization

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The Western paradigm treated agriculture as passive: a storehouse of workers and food to be released while industry — with its technology and linkages — led development and took priority. Under that reading, farming's job was to wait, feed and empty into the factories.

Since the 1970s economists have recast agriculture as a leading, dynamic sector, without which industry weakens: no food security, no raw materials, no rural purchasing power, no export earnings to fund industrial imports. The farm is not the factory's servant but its foundation and its market at once.

That recasting is Ethiopia's chosen strategy: ADLI, Agricultural Development-Led Industrialization — agriculture develops first, and industrialisation is led by, and built on, that development. Pupils debate the strategy from both sides and then evaluate it: the unit's figures on agriculture's shares, problems and systems are the evidence both sides must use.

Two sides. Side one — industry first: factories bring technology, linkages and high productivity; farms are passive, waiting to release workers. Side two — agriculture first: farms feed the cities, supply the factories, earn the foreign exchange and give millions of rural buyers income to spend. Ethiopia chose side two. Its name: ADLI — Agricultural Development-Led Industrialization. Now judge the choice with the sector figures you know.

The Grade 11 book restates ADLI from 1994: raising smallholder productivity and industrialising on domestic raw materials with labour-intensive technology, agriculture driving the rest of the economy through forward and backward linkages. The PADETES extension system delivered fertiliser, seed and credit packages from 1994/95.

The SDPRP (2002) added extension strengthening, farmer training centres, water harvesting, cooperatives and micro-finance. PASDEP added farm-to-market roads, irrigation dams and export-crop packages for spices, cut flowers, fruit and vegetables. Recent priorities under the current administration: irrigation at all scales, input financing, crop and livestock productivity, mechanisation, post-harvest loss reduction, research-based food security and agro-processing under the Home-Grown Economic Reform Agenda.

The teacher guide's model answer adds the mechanism (teacher guide — evidence): ADLI raises smallholder productivity through allocative efficiency — using existing land, labour and capital better through improved agronomic practices — and technical efficiency — adding resources, essentially capital, to introduce biological, chemical or mechanical technology. At programme level it couples the rural-centred agricultural package strategy with urban living standards, an effective education strategy, and a preventive and primary health care strategy.

## Grade 11 deepening from the Grade 10 teacher guide extension

The teacher guide instructs teachers to conclude its Activity 7.6 discussion with a verdict the student book never makes: in the guide's own words, 'ADLI is not a success — that is why we proceeded to Industrial parks development without agriculture developing.' Read it for what it is: the official guide's evaluative judgement on the strategy, offered to teachers as a conclusion. A learner should be able to report the verdict, name whose verdict it is, and still weigh the strategy's record for themselves. The same key describes the typical Ethiopian farm behind the strategy as around 0.5 hectares of rain-fed subsistence cultivation.
