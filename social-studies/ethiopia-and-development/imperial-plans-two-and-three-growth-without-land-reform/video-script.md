# Video script — Imperial plans two and three — growth without land reform

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The second plan (1962–1967) pushed agro-industry: large commercial farms for cotton, coffee and sugar — over the smallholders who grew about 80% of cereals. The economy's majority was planned around, not for.

The third plan (1968–1973) swung to agriculture and peasant farming against rising food shortage, with integrated rural development for inputs and rural health. The direction finally matched the population — a generation late.

Growth targets (4.3%, then 6.0%) were never properly evaluated for lack of personnel; per-capita GDP grew about 4% a year in the 1960s and manufacturing growth rose from 1.9% (1960/61) to 4.4% (1973/74) — yet most lives did not improve. The book blames the feudal land-tenure system that denied peasants rights over land and resources, the grievance that fed the 1974 revolution.
