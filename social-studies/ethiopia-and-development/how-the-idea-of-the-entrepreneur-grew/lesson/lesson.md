# How the idea of the entrepreneur grew

## Do it first

Five portraits across five centuries, one word — entrepreneur — changing meaning each time. Guess which century made him a risk-taker, and which made him an inventor, before reading.

## Sub lesson 1 — Manager and contractor

In the ancient period the entrepreneur was a manager of large projects using others' resources. In the 17th century he became a contractor supplying the government at a fixed price — keeping profit or loss from the contract.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cantillon's risk-taker

In the 18th century Richard Cantillon defined the entrepreneur as a risk-taker — buying at known prices to sell at unknown prices. With him, the entrepreneurial role is separated from the capital-providing role: risk, not money, becomes the defining act.

## Sub lesson 3 — Organiser, then innovator

In the late 19th–early 20th century the entrepreneur is an organiser operating an enterprise for personal gain. By the mid-20th century he is an innovator and inventor — the meaning the modern course builds on.
