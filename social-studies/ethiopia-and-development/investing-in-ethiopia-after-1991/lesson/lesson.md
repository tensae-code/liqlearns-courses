# Investing in Ethiopia after 1991 — proclamations and thresholds

## Do it first

A foreign investor needed $500,000 to enter. The threshold falls to $100,000. Before reading: who walks through a gate that gets five times wider — and which gates stayed shut on purpose?

## Sub lesson 1 — The first measure — Proclamation No. 15/1992

The Transitional Government's first economic measure was Investment Proclamation No. 15/1992, encouraging domestic and foreign investment with incentives such as land provision and duty-free imports, while reserving some areas from private investors: defence, electrical engineering works, air and marine transportation, and postal and telecommunications.

## Sub lesson 2 — The threshold steps down

Later reform cut the minimum capital for foreign investors from US$500,000 to US$100,000 and lifted the 25% cash-deposit requirement. A threshold is a filter: lowering it admits smaller investors, while the reserved areas keep the state's hand on the sectors it named.

## Sub lesson 3 — Proclamation No. 375/2003

Proclamation No. 375/2003 aimed to widen foreign participation and make investment administration transparent and efficient. A foreign investor may operate as a sole proprietor with full equity, or jointly with domestic investors or the government, with minimum capital of US$100,000 per project — US$50,000 in engineering, architecture, accounting and audit, and business-management consultancy projects.
