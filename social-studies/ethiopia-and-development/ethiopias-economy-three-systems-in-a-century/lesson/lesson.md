# Ethiopia's economy — three systems in a century

## Do it first

One country, three systems in a hundred years: markets under kings, commands under the Derg, markets again after 1991. What stays the same for a farmer through all three — and what flips each time? Say your answers first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Markets under the monarchy

Ethiopia's frame opens with a market economy under the feudal monarchy, running to 1974. Land, rank and custom ordered the old system, but production and trade moved through markets — buying, selling and private holding within a feudal shell.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Derg's command economy

From 1974 to 1991 the Derg ran a command economy: the state owned industries, banks and insurance, and directed production from above. The book's verdict is plain: it failed. Command replaced the market's signals with orders, and the economy it commanded did not deliver.

## Sub lesson 3 — Market-oriented from 1991

From 28 May 1991 the EPRDF built a market-oriented economy: privatisation of state holdings, promotion of agricultural exports, and deregulation. The third system keeps a strong state role but restores markets as the economy's engine — the setting for every figure in the rest of Unit 7.
