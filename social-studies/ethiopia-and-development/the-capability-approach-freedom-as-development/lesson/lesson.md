# The capability approach — freedom as development

## Do it first

A wealthy man fasts by choice; a poor farm-worker eats the same little because there is no more. Their plates match — does their development? Decide what differs before Sen's approach names it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Functionings — what people actually do

Amartya Sen's approach judges development by what people are able to be and do. Functionings are what a person actually does with the means at hand — being nourished, being educated, marrying, travelling, participating — the achieved states and activities of a life.

[Picture 24937 — two figures eating the same small meal, one with an open door of choices behind him, the other with the door locked]

## Sub lesson 2 — Capabilities — the real freedoms

Capabilities are the real freedoms to be and do what a person has reason to value — the set of functionings genuinely open to them, whether or not each is chosen. Development, on this approach, is the expansion of those freedoms.

## Sub lesson 3 — Conversion factors — and the fasting contrast

Personal, social and environmental conversion factors decide whether means become capabilities: the same income or goods convert differently in different bodies, societies and places. The book's contrast makes it plain: a wealthy man fasting by religious choice and a poor woman farm-worker who cannot feed her family may eat the same little — but his capability to be nourished is far greater than hers, because his hunger is chosen and hers is not.
