# Video script — Services in the Ethiopian economy

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The service sector's features: 45.1% of GDP in 2008/09 with only about 10% of the population engaged in it — its large share coming mostly from government employment — and strong recent gains in health, trade, tourism, banking and insurance since the early-1990s reforms. High value per worker, not many workers, is the sector's signature.

Services contribute output — passing agriculture in 2008/09 and 2009/10 at about 45% and 46% of GDP; employment — second after agriculture, with health and education at 1.4% of the labour force and transport and communication at 0.5%, pushed by infrastructure, private investment, services growth and horticulture; and foreign exchange — service exports rising from $261 million in 1980 to $348 million in 1998 and nearly $2 billion in 2008/09 from Ethiopian Airlines, Ethiopian Shipping Lines, tourism and finance — more than the $1.5 billion from goods.

The components are classified three ways. By the National Income Account: distributive services (trade, hotels and restaurants, transport and communication, banking and insurance, public administration and defence) and other services — services rising from 41.1% of GDP in 2002/03 to 46% in 2009/10 as agriculture fell to 42%. By customer: consumer-oriented, business-oriented or mixed. By activity: physical (car repair, hairdressing, cooking), intellectual (education and training) and aesthetic (museums, theatre, music).
