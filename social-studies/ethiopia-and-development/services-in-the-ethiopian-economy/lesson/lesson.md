# Services in the Ethiopian economy

## Do it first

An airline earns more foreign money than all the country's factories' goods combined, yet only one worker in ten works in services. How can the smallest workforce carry the largest GDP share? Say your idea first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Features — big share, few hands

The service sector's features: 45.1% of GDP in 2008/09 with only about 10% of the population engaged in it — its large share coming mostly from government employment — and strong recent gains in health, trade, tourism, banking and insurance since the early-1990s reforms. High value per worker, not many workers, is the sector's signature.

## Sub lesson 2 — Three contributions

Services contribute output — passing agriculture in 2008/09 and 2009/10 at about 45% and 46% of GDP; employment — second after agriculture, with health and education at 1.4% of the labour force and transport and communication at 0.5%, pushed by infrastructure, private investment, services growth and horticulture; and foreign exchange — service exports rising from $261 million in 1980 to $348 million in 1998 and nearly $2 billion in 2008/09 from Ethiopian Airlines, Ethiopian Shipping Lines, tourism and finance — more than the $1.5 billion from goods.

## Sub lesson 3 — Components, classified three ways

The components are classified three ways. By the National Income Account: distributive services (trade, hotels and restaurants, transport and communication, banking and insurance, public administration and defence) and other services — services rising from 41.1% of GDP in 2002/03 to 46% in 2009/10 as agriculture fell to 42%. By customer: consumer-oriented, business-oriented or mixed. By activity: physical (car repair, hairdressing, cooking), intellectual (education and training) and aesthetic (museums, theatre, music).

## Going further — Older evidence — the three sectors and the service sector in the 2011 guide

An older guide, from 2011, opens its course with the three sectors at a glance: for seventy to eighty years agriculture has been Ethiopia's leading contributor to GDP, export earnings and employment; industry is small and young, in three sub-sectors (large- and medium-scale manufacturing, construction, and small-scale and handicraft manufacturing); and services stand second to agriculture in GDP contribution but posted the highest growth of any sector between 1974/75 and 2005/06 — health, banking and finance, transport and communication, trade, hotels and restaurants, education, domestic and other services, public administration and defence. The same guide's later unit gives the services rise in figures: 40.8% of GDP in 2006/07 rising to 45.1% in 2008/09, about 45% and 46% for 2008/09 and 2009/10 against agriculture's falling share; the second-largest employer after agriculture; and an important foreign-exchange source — airlines, shipping, tourism, banking and insurance.
