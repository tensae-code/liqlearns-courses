# Communication — the village telephone effect

## Do it first

A farmer is about to sell his crop at the village price. One phone call could tell him the town price first. Before reading: list everything else that one telephone in a village quietly changes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Communication as development infrastructure

Communication — telecommunications, postal services and media — is treated as development infrastructure, not a luxury. Basic telephone access in a village lets farmers learn prices for their crops and livestock before they sell — information that changes what a harvest earns without changing the harvest itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — What one village telephone changes

The effects run past prices: a village telephone improves local administration, encourages trade and small business, and facilitates health, education and agricultural-extension services. One connection carries market information, government, and advice in both directions — which is why a single line can matter out of proportion to its cost.

## Sub lesson 3 — Counting communication performance

Performance is counted in fixed telephone lines, digital lines, mobile subscribers and internet subscribers; postal performance covers domestic and international parcels and EMS. The counts are access counts — they measure how far the infrastructure reaches, which is the sector's whole point.
