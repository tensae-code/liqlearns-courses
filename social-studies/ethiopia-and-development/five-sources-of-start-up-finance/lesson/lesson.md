# Five sources of start-up finance

## Do it first

A founder needs Birr 200,000. Five doors stand in a row: her own savings, her uncle, a partner, a wealthy angel, a venture firm. In what order should she knock — and what does each door ask in return?

## Sub lesson 1 — Your own pocket first

Five sources, in the order an entrepreneur should work through them: personal savings first — the rule of thumb is at least half the start-up funds from your own pocket. Your own money at risk is the proof every later source looks for.

[Picture 24091 — a five-step funding staircase from own savings up to venture capital]

## Sub lesson 2 — Friends, relatives, partners

Then friends and relatives — told honestly about opportunities and risks. Then partners, taken on to expand capital: more money, shared ownership. Honesty at the second step protects both the money and the relationship.

## Sub lesson 3 — Angels and venture capital

Then angels — wealthy private investors, often entrepreneurs themselves, backing start-ups for a share of the risk. Then venture capital firms — organisations investing for high return and high growth potential, providing start-up, development or expansion funds.
