# Transport — roads, rail, air and shipping in Ethiopia

## Do it first

One mode carries more than 90% of a landlocked country's passengers and freight. Before reading: guess the mode — and then think about what that dependence costs when it fails.

## Sub lesson 1 — Roads carry the country

Roads carry over 90% of Ethiopia's passenger and freight movement and are the government's top public-investment priority. Road performance is judged by the network stock in kilometres, road density per 1,000 km², the share of land more than 5 km from an all-weather road, and average time to reach one — access measured in distance and time, not only in asphalt.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rail and air — the classic links

The century-old Ethio-Djibouti Railway remains the classic link to the outside world. Ethiopian Airlines was founded on 29 December 1945 by Emperor Haile Selassie with TWA's assistance and began operations on 8 April 1946 with weekly Addis Ababa–Cairo flights on five Douglas DC-3s — a service born, from its first week, as an international link.

## Sub lesson 3 — Shipping — a landlocked country's ships

Ethiopian Shipping Lines carries commercial cargo on international routes even though the country has no seacoast of its own. Its performance is measured by cargo lifted, manpower, revenue, profit and dry-cargo market share — the same business gauges as any carrier, run from a landlocked home.
