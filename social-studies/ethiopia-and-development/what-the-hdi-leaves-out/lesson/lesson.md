# What the HDI leaves out

## Do it first

Two countries post the same HDI. In one, income is shared evenly; in the other, a small group holds nearly all of it. The index cannot tell them apart — start the list of what it misses before the lesson gives four criticisms.

## Sub lesson 1 — Equal weights for unlike things

The index weights unlike things equally, implying a year of life has an economic value that varies with a country's income — the same year counts differently depending on where it is lived, which many readers find hard to defend.

## Sub lesson 2 — Overlap — and what is ignored

Its components correlate with each other: education drives income, so the index partly counts the same advance twice. And it ignores inequality, poverty and gender disparity — a high average income can hide marginalised groups behind the average.

## Sub lesson 3 — The ceiling at 1

Because scores are capped between 0 and 1, already-rich countries have almost no room to register further income gains: near the ceiling, real progress barely moves the score, so the index says least exactly where incomes are highest.
