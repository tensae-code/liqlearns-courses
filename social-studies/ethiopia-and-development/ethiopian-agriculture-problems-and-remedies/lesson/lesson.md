# Ethiopian agriculture — problems and remedies

## Do it first

Stand in a rain-fed highland field in a dry year, on a shrinking plot, far from any road, with no credit and insecure rights to the land. Count the problems stacked against that farmer before the lesson sorts them — nature's and people's.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nature's problem — drought

The natural problems centre on recurrent drought in a rain-dependent highland agriculture: when the rains fail, a sector built on rainfall has no second source of water to fall back on.

## Sub lesson 2 — Human-made problems

The human-made list: land fragmentation and over-cultivation on shrinking holdings; poor rural infrastructure — about 75% of farm households far from transport and communication lines; lack of credit; insecure land-ownership entitlement; erosion and land degradation; traditional practices and backward technology; and inadequate rural markets.

## Sub lesson 3 — Remedies

The remedies answer problem for problem: irrigation from streams, lakes and stored rainwater; afforestation; drought-resistant crops in drought-prone areas; secure land rights; stronger extension services; farmer literacy for price and technique information; and rural infrastructure.
