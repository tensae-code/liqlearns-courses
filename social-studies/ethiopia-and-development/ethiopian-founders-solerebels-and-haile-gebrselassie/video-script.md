# Video script — Ethiopian founders — SoleRebels and Haile Gebrselassie

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bethlehem Tilahun Alemu founded SoleRebels at 25, straight after college, making shoes from recycled tyres. Forbes named it in 2014 the fastest-growing brand in Africa — waste material, local skill and an export market, combined by one founder's eye.

Haile Gebrselassie, the retired running champion, turned businessman: owner of Marathon Motors, which assembles Hyundai vehicles and rolled out its first electric car. His investments reach across hospitality, entertainment, real estate, agriculture and technology.

Together the cases employ over 3,000 people (Haile's investments alone) and carry the unit's definitions on real shoulders: an opportunity spotted, resources arranged, risk taken. Entrepreneurship in Ethiopia is not an imported story — these are its local chapters.

Case story one: Bethlehem Tilahun Alemu, at twenty-five, straight after college, founds SoleRebels — shoes made from recycled tyres — named by Forbes in 2014 the fastest-growing brand in Africa. Case story two: Haile Gebrselassie, the retired running champion, builds Marathon Motors, which assembles Hyundai vehicles and rolls out its first electric car, with investments employing over three thousand people.
