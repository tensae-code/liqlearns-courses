# The service sector

## Do it first

Walk down one market street and name everything being sold that you cannot carry home in a bag — the haircut, the lesson, the bank account, the bus ride. You have just inventoried a whole sector. Sort your list before the lesson classifies services three ways.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the sector contains

The service sector spans trade, hotels and restaurants, transport and communication, education, banking and insurance, public administration and defence, health and other services — everything produced that is not a farmed or manufactured thing.

[Picture 24952 — a market street of services — bank, clinic, school, garage, theatre — sorted under the three activity labels]

## Sub lesson 2 — Whom services serve

Its industries are consumer-oriented, business-oriented or mixed: a haircut serves a consumer, freight serves a business, and a bank serves both at once.

## Sub lesson 3 — Three kinds of activity

Its activities divide into physical (repairing cars, hairdressing, cooking), intellectual (education and training) and aesthetic (museums, theatre, music) — services done to things, to minds, and to the senses.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the service sector to your companion in your own words: what it is, the one fact you would never want to forget from it, and one example from your own surroundings or from Ethiopia. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
