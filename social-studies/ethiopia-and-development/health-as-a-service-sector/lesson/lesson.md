# Health as a service sector

## Do it first

Most of the sickness in a poor country comes from diseases that can be prevented. Before reading: if you ran the health service, would you spend first on hospitals, or on prevention? Give your reason.

## Sub lesson 1 — The post-1991 rebuild

Post-1991 Ethiopia rebuilt health policy: a National Health Policy in 1993 and a rolling 20-year Health Sector Development Plan from 1997, in its third phase (HSDP III) in the guide's time. Its focuses: decentralising the system, developing preventive, promotional and curative care together, guaranteeing access for all population segments, and promoting private and NGO participation.

## Sub lesson 2 — How health performance is measured

Performance is measured by the infant mortality rate (IMR), child and maternal mortality, life expectancy at birth, fertility and crude birth and death rates, and access to safe water. The disease burden is dominated by malaria, nutritional deficiency, diarrhoea and AIDS — largely preventable conditions, which is why prevention carries so much weight in the policy.

## Sub lesson 3 — Problems and remedies

The keyed problems: limited and regionally uneven physical access, inadequate budgetary allocation and low management quality, and low quality and low resource-utilisation capacity. The remedies mirror them: expanded programmes, family planning at every delivery level, reproductive-health education and expanded personnel training.
