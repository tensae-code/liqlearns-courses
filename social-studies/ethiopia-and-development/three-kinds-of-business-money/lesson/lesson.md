# Three kinds of business money — permanent, working, asset finance

## Do it first

Three needs land at once: money to open the business itself, money for this month's stock and wages, money for a delivery vehicle lasting eight years. Should all three be the same kind of loan? Say why not.

## Sub lesson 1 — Permanent capital

Permanent capital is share investment or partners' personal loans funding start-up costs and major developments. It is rewarded by dividends or capital gain when shares are sold, and repaid only when the firm can afford it — the patient money of the business.

## Sub lesson 2 — Working capital

Working capital is short-term finance for raw materials and running costs, varying by business type — sometimes suppliers effectively finance the start-up by waiting to be paid. It is the business's weekly breathing money.

## Sub lesson 3 — Asset finance — and the matching test

Asset finance is medium- to long-term loans, typically 3–10 years or more, matched to the asset's useful life, for plant, machinery, vehicles and buildings. Cash-flow sufficiency and matching finance to the enterprise's stage are the critical tests for all three kinds.
