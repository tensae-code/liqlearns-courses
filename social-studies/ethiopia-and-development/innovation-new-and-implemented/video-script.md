# Video script — Innovation — new and implemented

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Innovation is defined by the OECD's Oslo Manual: a new or improved product or process, significantly different from what came before, that is made available to potential users or brought into use. It compresses to two elements — creativity plus implementation. An idea never implemented is not yet innovation; the unit also promises the cost-benefit analysis that judges implementations, in the final lessons.

The Oslo Manual (2018) names four types. Organisational: new business practices, workplace organisation or external relations — the first four-day weeks and work-from-home firms. Process: new production or delivery methods — SaaS support desks, data-driven hotels. Product: new or improved goods or services — Lego's new brick materials, the first electric vehicles; a product innovation need not improve everything and need not sell, but routine fixes and seasonal changes do not count. Marketing: new design, packaging, pricing or promotion — Häagen-Dazs' waste-free container.

Eco-innovation stands as the fifth type: innovation driving progress toward the UN's 2030 sustainable-development goals — cutting production's environmental impact and using resources responsibly across the whole life cycle. It pays back in competitiveness, markets and reputation: cold-water detergents, IKEA's electric fleet, paper straws and supplier sustainability demands are the running examples.

## Grade 11 deepening from the Grade 10 teacher guide extension

The teacher guide grounds the innovation types in Ethiopia's own programmes. BPR — business process re-engineering — and KAIZEN are classified as process innovations: new ways of organising the work itself, carried through Ethiopia's public sector and manufacturing. The DRONE is classified as a product innovation: a new good entering the market. The guide's business-forms key is equally local: small shops as sole ownerships, two-to-three-owner businesses as partnerships, banks and insurance companies as corporations.
