# Home-grown economic reform — sectoral and structural fronts

## Do it first

Four sectors get named for reform — a farm sector, and three you might not expect in an Ethiopian plan. Guess the four before the list opens.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sectoral reforms — four fronts

Sectoral reforms target agriculture (research, private participation, irrigation at all scales, inputs and finance, livestock productivity, less post-harvest loss, import-substituting crops), mining (reformed law and promoted geological data to draw investors), tourism (destinations, infrastructure, private roles) and ICT.

## Sub lesson 2 — Structural rules — lead without crowding out

Structural reforms keep government as leader but forbid crowding out: intervention only where failure is proven and capacity exists, public projects handable to private investors transparently, and support by merit, not political proximity.

## Sub lesson 3 — The long structural list

Coordinated transport and logistics (corridors, dry ports), import substitution for big foreign-exchange drains (wheat, edible oil, food items), industrial parks at full capacity and ease-of-doing-business reforms, privatisation of large state enterprises and liberalisation of priority sectors, WTO accession and AfCFTA ratification, free movement of skilled labour, a stronger civic society and media, and diversified partnerships and finance.
