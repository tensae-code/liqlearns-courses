# Video script — Cost-benefit analysis

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Cost-benefit analysis weighs the sum of an action's benefits against its costs — including dollar values for intangibles and for opportunity cost, the value of what is given up. Businesses, individuals and governments all use it — Macy's debt repurchase is the book's corporate example — and it is strongest for long-term decisions. Its partner measure: NPV restates future benefits and costs at present value inside the CBA, and a positive NPV marks a good investment.

The procedure runs in three steps. Step 1: compile complete lists of costs and benefits, obvious and intangible. Step 2: give every item a monetary value — the book's $500 software-installation example prices even the cost of employees' lost working hours. Step 3: set up the benefit/cost (b/c) ratio and compare — proceed if benefits outweigh costs.

The same equation runs across rival options to compare them side by side: each option's benefits and costs, valued the same way, reduced to its ratio. The comparison disciplines the choice — an option can clear the proceed bar and still lose to a rival whose benefits outweigh its costs by more. Judged this way, the innovation unit's ideas meet the feasibility unit's tests.

Three steps. Step one: list every cost and every benefit — the obvious ones and the intangible ones. Step two: give every item a money value; in the software case, price the lost working hours too. Step three: build the benefit-over-cost ratio. If benefits outweigh costs, proceed. Run the same equation on the rival option and compare the two ratios side by side.
