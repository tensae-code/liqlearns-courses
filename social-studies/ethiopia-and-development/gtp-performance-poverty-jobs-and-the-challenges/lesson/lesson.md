# GTP performance — poverty, jobs and the challenges

## Do it first

Poverty falls, incomes double — and inequality edges up while city youth cannot find work. Can all four be true of the same decade? Read the figures, then judge.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saving, income and poverty

Domestic saving rose from 17.3% to 20.9% of GDP and GDP per capita from $389 to $1,080 (average +10.7% a year). The poverty headcount fell from 29.6% (2009/10) to 23.5% (2014/15) — rural poverty 30.4% to 25.6%, urban 25.7% to 14.8% by 2020.

## Sub lesson 2 — The other lines — Gini and jobs

The Gini edged up from 0.30 to 0.33 (rural 0.37→0.38, urban 0.27→0.28). Urban unemployment rose from 18% to 18.7%, with youth at 25.7% and urban women at 26.1% against men's 12.2% (2019/20). Investment's GDP share, 38.4% at its 2015/16 peak, fell through political instability and COVID-19 to 30.8%.

## Sub lesson 3 — The book's own challenge list

The challenges, in the book's words: growth that did not create enough jobs or equity or structural transformation; external debt distress; uncompetitive exports concentrated in a few unprocessed agricultural goods; and project mismanagement, resource wastage and corruption without accountability systems.
