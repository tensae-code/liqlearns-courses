# Video script — Real vs nominal GDP and the deflator

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Nominal GDP values output at current prices — price rises alone can lift it. Real GDP values output at base-year prices, so only quantity changes move it. In the base year itself the two are equal by construction: current prices and base prices are the same prices that year.

The GDP deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100, and it is always 100 in the base year. The inflation rate is read from the deflator's percentage change: a deflator climbing from 131 to 148 means prices rose about 13% between those years. The deflator thus turns the two GDPs into a price measure.

Table 7.2 works it fully: kitfo and chumbo prices and quantities for 2010–2012 give nominal GDP of Birr 27,500, 62,000 and 100,000; real GDP of 27,500, 47,500 and 67,500; and deflators of 100, 131 and 148. Check one: 62,000 ÷ 47,500 × 100 ≈ 131. Quantities grew — real GDP rose — and prices grew faster, which the deflator's climb records.

Compute with the table. Kitfo and chumbo, three years. Nominal GDP: 27,500, then 62,000, then 100,000 birr. Real GDP at base-year prices: 27,500, then 47,500, then 67,500. The deflator is nominal divided by real, times 100: 100 in the base year, about 131 next, about 148 last. Inflation from the deflator: 131 to 148 is a rise of about 13 percent.
