# Ethiopia's development plans — the long view

## Do it first

Three governments in a row each wrote a plan for the whole economy. One built roads, one put the commanding heights under the state, one bet on farms and food security. Before reading: what should a plan do first in a poor farming country? Say your answer first.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Imperial plans

After 1941 the economy changed little beyond road building and bureaucracy. Then came three Imperial five-year plans: the first launched at the end of 1957, the second running 1962–67, and the third lasting to 1974. The answer key names Bole International Airport and the Addis Ababa–Assab road as plan-era projects — the era built connections first.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Derg's ten-year perspective plan

The Derg replaced the five-year plans with a socialist ten-year perspective plan. Its mark was state control: the commanding heights of the economy — the big, decisive sectors — were placed under state control, and planning served that control rather than private farming and trade.

## Sub lesson 3 — SDPRP and PASDEP

Under the EPRDF came poverty-reduction plans. SDPRP in 2002 rested on ADLI, food security, decentralisation and empowerment, capacity building and good governance. PASDEP in 2005/06 aimed at the Millennium Development Goals through private-sector development. The key lists the era's challenges plainly: poor rainfall, the 2008/09 global financial crisis, and donor fatigue.
