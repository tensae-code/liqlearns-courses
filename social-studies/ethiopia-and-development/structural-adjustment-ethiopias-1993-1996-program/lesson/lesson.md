# Structural adjustment — Ethiopia's 1993–1996 program

## Do it first

By 1991 an economy faces near-collapse: high inflation, negative interest rates, debts it cannot carry. Before reading: would you cut, switch, or rebuild first — and what does each of those words even mean for a whole country?

## Sub lesson 1 — Why Ethiopia took the program

The need was collapse: the 1974/75 turn from a mixed economy to a totally controlled one pushed the economy into decline, and by 1991 it faced near-collapse with unsustainable internal and external imbalances — high inflation, negative interest rates, and an unviable debt ratio. Ethiopia's Structural Adjustment Program ran 1993–1996 with IMF, World Bank, African Development Fund and donor support.

## Sub lesson 2 — What structural adjustment means

The program is defined as the reorganisation of institutions, economic activities and the whole social system along market lines. Its objectives: revive growth, reduce macroeconomic distortion, improve efficiency and resource allocation, and expand productive capacity — a definition wider than budgets: the rules of the economy themselves were the object.

## Sub lesson 3 — The three components

The program had three components: expenditure-reducing policies, expenditure-switching policies, and institutional policy reform. In practice that paired stabilisation — macro balance, lower inflation, budget control — with supply-side adjustment that pushed export-crop production through real exchange-rate depreciation and incentives.
