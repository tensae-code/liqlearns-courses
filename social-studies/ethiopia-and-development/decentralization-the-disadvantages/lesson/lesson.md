# Decentralization — the disadvantages

## Do it first

A town generous to its poor watches rich taxpayers quietly move away and poor families stream in. Its generosity is eating itself. That is one of four disadvantages — predict the others.

## Sub lesson 1 — Spillovers and lost scale

Local decisions spill over: externalities cross borders between jurisdictions — one town's pollution or school quality lands on its neighbours. And scale economies are lost when every community builds its own library or runs its own tax office.


[Picture 25301 — a generous town's welcome sign, firms packing to leave]
## Sub lesson 2 — Mispriced tax bases

Local tax systems misprice bases: capital, inelastic nationally, is elastic locally — it can simply move — so localities under-tax it to attract investment. What is rational for one town is inefficient from the national view.

## Sub lesson 3 — Redistribution that self-destructs

Local redistribution self-destructs: a community generous to the poor attracts more poor residents and repels the rich taxpayers who fund it, until the programme collapses. Stabilisation, most economists agree, belongs to the centre for the same reason — it must cover those who cannot walk away from it.
