# Who owns the land? Ethiopia's land story

## Do it first

A farmer holds a paper that lets her farm her plot for life — but she cannot sell it or mortgage it. Does she own the land? Hold the question; Ethiopia has answered it three different ways in fifty years.

## Sub lesson 1 — Before 1974 — feudal holdings

Before 1974, land sat in inequitable feudal holdings: a few held much, and those who tilled it held little or none. Land access followed birth and favour, not the plough.

## Sub lesson 2 — 1975 — Land to the Tiller

In 1975 the feudal system was removed under the slogan 'Land to the Tiller' — but the outcome was state ownership rather than ownership by the tillers. The landlord fell; the state took his chair.

## Sub lesson 3 — From 1991 — holding rights and leases

From 1991 the state kept ownership of all urban and rural land and natural resources, while rural peasants and pastoralists hold lifetime 'holding' rights covering everything except sale and mortgage, and urban residents get residential land on 99-year leases. The book criticises the arrangement: restricted redistribution makes rural land access almost non-existent, and expropriation compensation is judged insufficient; more access, freer holding rights and fair compensation are named as the improvements needed.
