# Bimodal strategy — smallholders and commercial farms together

## Do it first

Now the opposite bet: improve the small farms AND build big commercial farms at the same time, with the big farms as the principal engine. Before reading: what does each mode give the country that the other cannot?

## Sub lesson 1 — Two modes at once

The bimodal strategy is the dualistic alternative: modernise smallholder farms and simultaneously promote large-scale commercial farming by private entrepreneurs. Both modes run under one policy — the small farm is not abandoned, but it is no longer the only road.

[Picture 25504 — A smallholder plot and a mechanised estate side by side under one policy banner]

## Sub lesson 2 — The commercial sector is principal

Inside the bimodal pair, the commercial sector is strong and principal — the leading engine — while smallholders are intensified beside it. That ordering is the strategy's signature: two modes, but not two equals. Private entrepreneurs run the commercial farms.

## Sub lesson 3 — The clean contrast, and the exemplars

The content-check key states the contrast cleanly: unimodal transforms through intensifying small peasant farms alone; bimodal intensifies both small and commercial farms. The guide's bimodal exemplars are Mexico and Ecuador — keep the pairs straight: China and Thailand for unimodal, Mexico and Ecuador for bimodal.
