# Lesson plan — Daily life in ancient Africa

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Daily life in ancient Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa to 1500 — built in the History expansion (Djibouti, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ancient Africans build homes and towns, live in families, eat from their land and wear their identity — daily life is where civilisation is lived.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Homes and towns** — ancient Africans build homes and towns, and the town changes how people live together (UA2 Habitat et urbanisation dans l'Afrique ancienne)
2. **The family** — the family is the first community every child belongs to and learns from (UA2 La famille dans l'Afrique ancienne)
3. **Food** — what ancient Africans grew, hunted and cooked decides how their societies thrive (UA2 L'alimentation dans l'Afrique ancienne)
4. **Dress, ornaments and art** — dress, ornaments and art carry each civilisation's identity on the body (UA2 L'habillement, parures et arts dans l'Afrique ancienne)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
