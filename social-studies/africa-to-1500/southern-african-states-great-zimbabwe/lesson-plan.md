# Lesson plan — Southern African States — Great Zimbabwe

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Southern African States — Great Zimbabwe
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Great Zimbabwe — 'stone enclosure' in Shona — raised walls up to 10 metres high between 1200 and 1450 as its state's symbol of power, and was succeeded by Mwene Mutapa.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Kongo lesson before it in this title — the learner moves south to the stone city. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The stone city** — local people building stone kraals for cattle and rich men's houses; 'Zimbabwe' meaning stone enclosure in Shona; walls up to 10 m high built between 1200 and 1450; the enclosure the state's symbol of power and honour, still Zimbabwe's heritage emblem.
2. **Mwene Mutapa succeeds** — Great Zimbabwe succeeded by Mwene Mutapa, Mwene being the ruler's title; a Mutapa city from the 11th–15th centuries; fallen under Portuguese administration in 1629 as the book states.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Central African States — Kongo. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what 'Zimbabwe' means in Shona, and what local people built in stone.
2. Give the Great Enclosure's building dates and wall height, and what the enclosure symbolised.
3. Tell the succession: Mwene Mutapa, its ruler's title, and the book's 1629 ending.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads Zimbabwe as only a modern country's name → in this lesson it is a Shona WORD first: stone enclosure — the building gave the state, and later the country, its name.
- Thinks the walls were a fort only → the book calls the enclosure the state's symbol of power and honour, and still Zimbabwe's heritage emblem.
