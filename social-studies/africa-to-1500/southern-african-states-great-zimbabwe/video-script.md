# Video script — Southern African States — Great Zimbabwe

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

In Shona, Zimbabwe means stone enclosure.
So they built in stone — kraals for cattle,
houses for the rich —
and walls, walls ten metres high.

Between 1200 and 1450 the stones rose.
The Great Enclosure: power you could see,
honour you could touch.

Mutapa followed, under its Mwene.
States fall. In 1629 the Portuguese came.
But the stone city still stands —
and a whole country carries its name.

---

Grade 9 story addition (Ethiopia Wave 4):

Villages dot the southern map. Slowly — the Grade 9 word — they draw together.

Stone walls climb at Great Zimbabwe; gold starts its walk to the coast.

1450: Rozwi kings raise Mwene Mutapa. And on the grasslands, Khoi-Khoi herds grow.

[Grade 11 depth scene — Great Zimbabwe in depth — stone city, gold trade, Mutapa. The animated map carries the story as the voice speaks.]
Great Zimbabwe receives its Grade 11 fullness: a stone city raised without mortar by the Shona, centre of a gold trade flowing to Sofala on the coast, its enclosures mapping a court's order in granite. The names to hold: Great Zimbabwe, Sofala.
