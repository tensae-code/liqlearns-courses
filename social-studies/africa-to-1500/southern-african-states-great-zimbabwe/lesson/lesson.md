# Southern African States — Great Zimbabwe

## Do it first

Most states in this title are remembered for gold, taxes or kings. This one is remembered for a wall.

Before you read: what does it say about a people when their greatest monument is not a tomb or a temple, but an enclosure — a wall built around their cattle and their rulers? Keep the question open.

## Sub lesson 1 — The stone city

In **Shona**, the local language, **"Zimbabwe" means stone enclosure**. Local people built **stone kraals for cattle** and **rich men's houses** in stone — and between **1200 and 1450** they raised walls **up to 10 metres high**.

[Picture 11310 — Great Zimbabwe — the stone city]

The greatest of these, the **Great Enclosure**, stood as the state's **symbol of power and honour** — and it is **still Zimbabwe's heritage emblem** today: the modern country carries the stone city's name and sign.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mwene Mutapa succeeds

Great Zimbabwe was **succeeded by Mwene Mutapa** — **Mwene being the ruler's title**, as Manikongo was Kongo's. A **Mutapa city** stood from the **11th to the 15th centuries**, and the state **fell under Portuguese administration in 1629**, as the book states.

Stone outlasted the state: the walls of 1200–1450 still stand as the emblem, long after Mutapa's fall.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 9 book's south — consolidation, Mutapa and the Khoi-Khoi

The Grade 9 book widens this lesson's frame. Southern states formed **more slowly**, from **independent villages consolidating from the 11th century**. The Shona built **Great Zimbabwe from about 1000 AD** — its great walls and tower in the **13th–14th centuries** — heart of a trading empire based on **cattle, crops and gold traded to the Indian Ocean coast, 11th–15th centuries**.

After **1450** the **Rozwi clan kings** founded the **Mwene Mutapa empire**, later **conquered by the Portuguese in the late 16th century**. And beside the stone cities, the **Khoi-Khoi** shifted about **1000 AD from hunting to sheep and cattle herding**, expanding from about **1300** — the pastoral thread of the same southern story.


## Say it to your companion

Explain the name first — what Zimbabwe means, in whose language. Then give the wall's height and dates, what the enclosure symbolised, and the successor's name and title. Your companion repeats the chain back without looking.
