# Carthage and the Punic Wars

## Do it first

Build a city in your mind on a sea coast, where ships from every shore must call. Fill its harbour with goods. Ring it with the strongest wall you can imagine.

Now ask: what could bring such a city down? Hold the question — a rival across the sea is already looking at it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The trading city

**Carthage** was founded in **814 BC** by the **Phoenicians**, near **today's Tunis** on the Mediterranean. It grew into a **rich trading centre**, ringed by a wall **12 metres high and 9 metres thick** — a wall you could walk a cart along.

In form, a **people's council** ruled Carthage. In fact, the book says plainly, **real power lay with a few strong men**. Hold both halves together: the council's name, and the strong men's power.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Punic Wars

Across the sea stood **Rome**. Rome's **wish for Sicily** — the island between them — brought the two powers to war: the **three Punic Wars, 264–146 BC**.

Twice Carthage survived. The **third war destroyed it**: Carthage was ended and its land made a **Roman province**. The rich walled city that Phoenicians founded in 814 BC stood, in all, nearly seven centuries — and war with Rome closed it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell Carthage's story in two breaths: the city (who, when, where, how walled, who really ruled) and the fall (who wanted Sicily, how many wars, what the third one did). Your companion listens for the two wall numbers and the war dates.
