# Ancient Egypt — What Egypt Gave

## Do it first

Shut your eyes and list everything you used this morning that somebody, long ago, had to invent: a way of writing things down, a way of counting days, a way of healing, a way of measuring land.

Keep the list open. One ancient land claims a surprising share of it — and prints its claim as a table.

## Sub lesson 1 — Writing, medicine, mathematics

**Writing.** Egypt gave **hieroglyphics**, written on **papyrus**. This was no decoration: the writing served **crop and tax records, science, administration and diplomacy** — the paperwork of a whole kingdom.

**Medicine.** Egypt's medicine included **hospital inpatient care** — the sick kept and treated in a place of healing — a knowledge of the body's bones (the book prints **360 bones**), and the **chemicals of mummy-making**, which preserved the dead.

**Mathematics.** Egypt's **geometry and arithmetic** were working tools, used for building **dams and canals** — measuring water and land so the Nile could be governed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Crafts, architecture, calendar

**Crafts.** Egyptian hands made **pottery, jewellery, and religious carving in metal and wood**.

**Architecture.** Egypt built **pyramids** — the **Great Pyramid of Giza** is named — **temples such as Karnak**, and the **canals and dams** its mathematics measured.

**The calendar.** And Egypt gave the world the calendar of **12 months and 365 days** — the frame your own year still hangs on.

Six gifts in the book's table: writing, medicine, mathematics, crafts, architecture, calendar. A farming kingdom on one river left tools the whole world still uses.

## Say it to your companion

Name the six gifts without looking. Your companion then picks any two, and you must give the example inside each — papyrus for writing, Karnak for architecture, 365 for the calendar, and so on.

## Do It Yourself

1. Copy the book's table as six boxes on one page, one gift per box, with its examples inside.
2. Circle the gift your own life used today. Write one sentence saying how.
3. Write the calendar's two numbers large: 12 months, 365 days.
