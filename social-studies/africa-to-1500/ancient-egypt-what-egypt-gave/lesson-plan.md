# Lesson plan — Ancient Egypt — What Egypt Gave

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Ancient Egypt — What Egypt Gave
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Egypt's printed table of gifts to the world — writing, medicine, mathematics, crafts, architecture and the 365-day calendar.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Ancient Egypt — the Nile Kingdom, the lesson just before it in this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Writing, medicine, mathematics** — hieroglyphics on papyrus for records, science, administration and diplomacy; hospital care and mummy-making knowledge; geometry and arithmetic for dams and canals.
2. **Crafts, architecture, calendar** — pottery, jewellery and carving; pyramids, temples like Karnak, canals and dams; the 12-month, 365-day calendar.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Ancient Egypt — the Nile Kingdom — it builds the kingdom whose gifts this lesson lists. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List Egypt's six contributions from the book's table, with one example inside each.
2. Say what hieroglyphics were written on, and three jobs the writing served.
3. Give the calendar Egypt gave the world: its months and its days.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the calendar's numbers → 12 months, 365 days — twelve like the months you know, 365 like the days you live.
- Thinks hieroglyphics were only pictures for temples → the book lists workaday uses: crop and tax records, science, administration, diplomacy.
