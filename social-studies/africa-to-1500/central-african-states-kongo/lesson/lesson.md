# Central African States — Kongo

## Do it first

Far south of the Niger bend, another great river — the Zaire — runs to the Atlantic. Along its lower course the land is fertile, the crafts are rich, the trade is strong.

Villages farm that land, each under its own ways. What would it take for villages like those to become one kingdom? Watch for the year, the place and the title in this short lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Bakongo villages

On the **lower Zaire river**, in a **fertile, craft-rich, trade-strong land**, the **Bakongo farming villages** had spread to the lower Zaire **by about 1300**.

Their faith centred on a **land spirit**, served by priests called **manikabunga**. Land gave the food; the spirit guarded the land; the priests served the spirit — village life held together around all three.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mbanza Kongo and the Manikongo

About **1400** the villages **united at Mbanza Kongo** — the kingdom's **political and cultural centre**. Its king carried the title **Manikongo**.

[Picture 11309 — the Kongo kingdom]

The kingdom grew with the land's strength: **by about 1580 it stretched from the Atlantic to the Kwango river** — ocean to inland river, one kingdom across the whole lower Zaire country.

## Say it to your companion

Tell Kongo's story with its two dates kept apart: villages spread by about 1300; united about 1400. Your companion then asks for the centre's name, the king's title, and the 1580 stretch — answer all three.
