# Lesson plan — West African Empires — Ghana

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** West African Empires — Ghana
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ancient Ghana — built by Soninke farmers between the southern gold lands and the northern desert caravans — grew great by taxing the gold trade at Koumbi Saleh, and fell to rivals, degradation and invasion by 1235.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Carthage lesson before it in this title — trade making and breaking states is the thread. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Gold between desert and forest** — Soninke farmers in today's south-east Mauritania and Mali; between southern gold producers and northern camel caravans; kings taxing gold at Koumbi Saleh; by 1000 AD, Senegal valley to the Niger bend.
2. **Islam, rivals and the end** — Soninke traders West Africa's first Muslims; rulers accepting Islam in the 11th century; Mandinka pressure, resource degradation and Almoravid invasion; Koumbi Saleh taken in 1235.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Carthage and the Punic Wars. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say who founded Ghana, where it sat, and how its kings grew rich.
2. Name Ghana's capital and the empire's reach by 1000 AD.
3. Tell how Islam came to Ghana's traders and rulers, and the three pressures that ended the empire.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts Ghana where today's Ghana country is → the book places ancient Ghana in today's south-east Mauritania and Mali — same name, different land.
- Thinks Ghana mined its own gold → its wealth was the TAX on gold passing between the southern producers and the northern caravans.
