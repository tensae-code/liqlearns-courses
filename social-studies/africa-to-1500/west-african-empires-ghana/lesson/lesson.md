# West African Empires — Ghana

## Do it first

Imagine a market town standing between a gold field and a desert road. Every lump of gold travelling north must pass your gate; every salt-laden camel travelling south must pass it too.

What would you charge — and what would that charge make you, over two hundred years? You have just built Ghana's idea.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gold between desert and forest

**Ancient Ghana** was founded by the **Soninke farmers** in **today's south-east Mauritania and Mali**. Its position was its fortune: it sat **between the southern gold-producing peoples and the northern camel caravans**.

From **Koumbi Saleh**, its capital, Ghana's kings **taxed the gold trade** — and the tax built a great empire, reaching **by 1000 AD from the Senegal valley to the Niger's great bend**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Islam, rivals and the end

Trade brought faith with it. The **Soninke traders were West Africa's first Muslims**; in the **11th century, Ghana's rulers accepted Islam** — improving ties with Muslim merchants, but breeding **rivals for the gold monopoly**.

The end came by three pressures together: **Mandinka pressure, resource degradation, and Almoravid invasion**. In **1235** the **Mandinka chief took Koumbi Saleh** — and Ghana was finished.

## Say it to your companion

Tell Ghana's story as position → tax → faith → fall. Your companion must catch the capital's name, the reach by 1000 AD, and the year the capital fell. Then swap roles.
