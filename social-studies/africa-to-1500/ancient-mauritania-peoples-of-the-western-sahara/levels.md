# Ancient Mauritania — peoples of the Western Sahara — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lesson 3: Ancient Mauritania — settlement and peoples
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — farming and herding begin in the Neolithic (7000–2000 BC); as the Sahara dries, Garamantes, Bafour and Berbers migrate in — Sanhaja Berbers in the 3rd century AD settle the ponds and wadis, the Bafour hold Adrar from Azuggi behind trained dogs until the Almoravids come, Phoenician sailors trade down the Atlantic coast from 1500 BC, and Sudanic groups hold the south

## Sub lesson 2 — Lesson 3: Ancient Mauritania — culture, economy and chiefs
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — rock engravings at al-Ghallawiya in Adrar, engraved ostrich eggs and pottery mark the culture; the first Berbers bring the wheeled chariot, horse, metal, writing and finally the camel (1st century AD), which opens the desert to caravan trade Herodotus already called silent; with no strong states, local chiefs manage water, grazing and disputes
