# Peoples of East Africa — Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite migrations

## Do it first

East Africa is settled side by side by peoples of different origins: Bantu farmers, Nilotes who came down the Nile, and Hamite and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists from Ethiopia — and people still move, for trade, work and safety. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bantu farmers and Nilotes from the Nile

Bantu farmers settle across East Africa, and Nilotes come down the Nile. Two great streams of people, with different languages and ways of life, arrive in the same region.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hamites and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists from Ethiopia

Hamites and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists come from Ethiopia, herding peoples alongside the farmers. East Africa's map of peoples is therefore a meeting, not a single origin.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why people move — immigrants and emigrants

People move for trade, work and safety, becoming immigrants in one country and emigrants from another, changing both places. The old migrations and today's movement are taught as one human habit.
