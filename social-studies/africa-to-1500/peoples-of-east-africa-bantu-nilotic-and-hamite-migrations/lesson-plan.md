# Lesson plan — Peoples of East Africa — Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite migrations

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Peoples of East Africa — Bantu, Nilotic and Hamite migrations
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa to 1500 — built in the History expansion (Rwanda, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** East Africa is settled side by side by peoples of different origins: Bantu farmers, Nilotes who came down the Nile, and Hamite and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists from Ethiopia — and people still move, for trade, work and safety.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bantu farmers and Nilotes from the Nile** — Bantu farmers settle across East Africa, and Nilotes come down the Nile.
2. **Hamites and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists from Ethiopia** — Hamites and Nilo-Hamite pastoralists come from Ethiopia, herding peoples alongside the farmers.
3. **Why people move — immigrants and emigrants** — People move for trade, work and safety, becoming immigrants in one country and emigrants from another, changing both places.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
