# Lesson plan — Kush and Meroe

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Kush and Meroe
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** south of Egypt, Kush — centred first at Napata — grew rich on trade, ruled Egypt itself for a time, and moved its capital south to Meroe, where it declined from the 4th century AD.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the two Egypt lessons before it in this title — Kush is met first as Egypt's southern neighbour and trader. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Kush and Napata** — Kush in Egyptian records about 2000 BC for today's Sudan; close trade; Egyptian culture and gods taken up; Napata's strength; five Kushite kings ruling Egypt in the 8th century BC.
2. **Meroe — the capital moves south** — driven from Egypt by Assyria about 670 BC, the kings found Meroe south of Khartoum; still Egyptian in language and gods; the guide's peak — its own writing, iron work and Horn trade; decline from the 4th century AD, ending about 350 AD at Ezana of Aksum's hands (guide).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Ancient Egypt — What Egypt Gave. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: Napata, Meroe and Aksum's place in the ending are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say where 'Kush' is first found, what land it names, and how Egypt and Kush traded and shared culture.
2. Tell how five Kushite kings ruled Egypt itself in the 8th century BC, and how Assyria drove them out about 670 BC (guide spine).
3. Describe Meroe: where it stood, its language and gods, its trade, and the internal and external causes of its decline.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts Meroe north of Napata → the capital moved SOUTH, away from Egypt, after the Assyrian blow — the picture's river runs Egypt → Napata → Meroe.
- Mixes Kush's end with Meroe's end → Kush's rule over Egypt ended about 670 BC; the Meroe kingdom itself declined centuries later, from the 4th century AD.
