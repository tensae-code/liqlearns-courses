# Kush and Meroe

## Do it first

Egypt was not alone on the Nile. Down the river to the south lay another rich land that Egypt traded with, borrowed from, fought — and was once ruled by.

Guess before you read: could Egypt's southern neighbour ever have sat on Egypt's own throne? The answer is yes — five times over.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kush and Napata

The name **"Kush"** is first found in **Egyptian records about 2000 BC**, for the land of **today's Sudan**. Egypt **traded closely** with Kush, and Kush **took Egyptian culture and gods** as its own.

Centred at **Napata**, Kush was **strong and rich to about 750 BC**. Then came the reversal no Egyptian expected: in the **8th century BC, five Kushite kings in succession ruled Egypt itself**.

Kush built greatly: **over 200 pyramids**, statues, temples, palaces and royal baths. Its trade ran wide — **gold, ivory and ebony** sent out to **Rome and Arabia**, against **olive oil, incense, timber and bronze** brought in.

## Sub lesson 2 — Meroe — the capital moves south

The rule over Egypt ended by force: **Assyria drove the Kushite kings out** — the guide's spine dates the blow to **about 670 BC**, when Napata itself was destroyed — and the kings **moved south and founded Meroe, south of Khartoum**.

Meroe stayed **Egyptian in language (hieroglyphics) and gods**. In the guide's telling, the Meroitic age was the kingdom's peak: Meroe **monopolised the trade of the Horn of Africa**, developed its **own art and architecture**, used **its own language**, invented **its own writing**, worshipped **its own gods**, and was known for its **iron work**.

The decline came from the **4th century AD**, by causes inside and outside: inside — **falling farm output, poorer charcoal and iron quality, and spreading desert**; outside — **Aksum's invasion**. The guide keys the end to **about 350 AD, at the hands of King Ezana of Aksum**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Grade 11 depth: Kush in depth — Kerma, Napata, Meroe

The Grade 11 depth gives Kush its full three-capital arc: Kerma, the early southern kingdom; Napata, from which Kush's kings ruled Egypt itself as its 25th Dynasty; and Meroe, the southern retreat that became an iron-working power with pyramids of its own and a script of its own. Kush was never Egypt's shadow that grew — it was a Nile civilisation in its own right, older in its southern roots than its northern fame.

## Say it to your companion

Tell the whole arc in four steps: Kush named in Egypt's records; Napata strong; five kings on Egypt's throne; the move to Meroe and the long decline. Your companion checks each step against the lesson, then names one internal and one external cause of the end.
