# Africa, cradle of humankind — Chad, Afar and Lucy

## Do it first

Every human being alive descends from Africa. Not as a slogan — as fossils. A skull in Chad, seven million years old. A skeleton in Ethiopia's Afar, walking upright three million years before us. This lesson visits the three finds that anchor the human story on this continent.

## Sub lesson 1 — Chad and the Middle Awash — the oldest finds

Africa is the centre of the human-origins evidence, and its oldest chapter opens in the north. In **northern Chad**, a find made in **2001** gave the **oldest australopithecine** then known — dated **7 to 6 million years** ago, from the time when the human line and the ape lines were newly parted.

[Picture 22509 — a map of Africa with the Chad, Hadar and Middle Awash sites marked, and Lucy's skeleton]

Ethiopia's **Afar** region holds the next anchors. At **Aramis in the Middle Awash**, **Ardipithecus ramidus** fossils aged **4.4 million years** were found in **1994** — a creature of the woodlands, from the team-work of Tim White's researchers in the Middle Awash. Two finds, two countries, one pattern: the deeper the diggers go in Africa, the older the family story becomes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lucy — Dinkinesh of Hadar

In **1974**, at **Hadar in the Afar**, Donald Johanson's team found the fossil that made Ethiopia's Afar famous across the world: **Australopithecus afarensis** — **Lucy**, called **Dinkinesh** in Amharic, 'you are wonderful'. She is dated about **3.18 million years** old, and her skeleton showed what the species was: small-brained, yet walking **upright** — bipedalism, the hominid mark, millions of years before big brains.

The book sets pupils two tasks with her: map Africa's major archaeological sites, and argue **why East Africa matters** for studying human origin. The map answers itself — Chad, the Middle Awash, Hadar: when the class plots them, the cradle draws itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell the whole story of Africa, cradle of humankind back to your companion, part by part, in order — names, dates and places included. Your companion listens with the lesson closed and stops you at any name or date they cannot place; open the lesson together and repair that part.
