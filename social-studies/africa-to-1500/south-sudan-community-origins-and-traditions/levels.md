# South Sudan — community origins and traditions — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Origin of a community
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Dinka sing that they came from the black mud of the Nile and their god Nhialic, and every child asks parents how their own community started

## Sub lesson 2 — Community rituals, ceremonies and festivals
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — rituals that cleanse a person or society, ceremonies and festivals are passed generation to generation as the community's living tradition

## Sub lesson 3 — Common practices of African communities in the past
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — children study pictures of traditional practices, name their community's name for God and the sacrifices offered, and list practices they still know

## Sub lesson 4 — Relationship between ceremonies today and those from the past
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — today's ceremonies are matched against past ones so children see which traditions carried through and which changed

## Sub lesson 5 — Stories of origin
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the teacher reads where Garang and Abuk came from, classes discuss Bible and Koran creation stories, and other peoples say they came from the moon, a superior being, or the mountains

## Sub lesson 6 — How stories of origin have changed our lives today
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — children ask parents about other peoples in their state and their origin stories, and judge how those beginnings still shape life today
