# Video script — South Sudan — community origins and traditions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as South Sudan's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The Dinka sing that they came from the black mud of the Nile and their god Nhialic, and every child asks parents how their own community started.

Rituals that cleanse a person or society, ceremonies and festivals are passed generation to generation as the community's living tradition.

Children study pictures of traditional practices, name their community's name for God and the sacrifices offered, and list practices they still know.

Today's ceremonies are matched against past ones so children see which traditions carried through and which changed.

The teacher reads where Garang and Abuk came from, classes discuss Bible and Koran creation stories, and other peoples say they came from the moon, a superior being, or the mountains.

Children ask parents about other peoples in their state and their origin stories, and judge how those beginnings still shape life today.
