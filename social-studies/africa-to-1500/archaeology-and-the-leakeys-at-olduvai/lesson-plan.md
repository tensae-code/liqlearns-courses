# Lesson plan — Archaeology and the Leakeys at Olduvai

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Archaeology and the Leakeys at Olduvai
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa to 1500 — built in the History expansion (Uganda, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** History before writing is dug, not read — Dr Louis Leakey comes with his wife Mary and finds the oldest skull of man at Olduvai Gorge in 1959; museums keep the artefacts safe
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sources of history and famous archaeologists in East Africa** — history before writing is dug, not read — Dr Louis Leakey comes with his wife Mary and finds the oldest skull of man at Olduvai Gorge in 1959; museums keep the artefacts safe
2. **The Cradle of Human Race in East Africa** — history's sources are named — oral tradition, linguistics, anthropology, archaeology — fossils are dug by excavators at named sites, and East Africa is taught as mankind's cradle
3. **Finding Out about Our Past** — the past is found four ways — oral, written, linguistics, archaeology — and the chapter's own integration task buries the lesson in a school building site, where digging a hall's foundation turns up the evidence
4. **Finding Out about Our Past (guide)** — the guide's chapter summary has learners appreciate their origin, locate the historical sites in their own communities, and respect one another's cultures

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
