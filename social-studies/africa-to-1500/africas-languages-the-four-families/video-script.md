# Video script — Africa's Languages — the Four Families

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Africa speaks in thousands of tongues.
But tongues have mothers.

Four great families hold them.
Afro-Asiatic in the north and the Horn.
Nilo-Saharan across the Sudan belt.
Niger-Congo — west, centre, east, south.
Khoisan, the smallest, in the south-west.

And Madagascar? An island apart.
Its family is Austronesian, from across the sea.

The most spoken of all: Swahili.
Then Hausa. Then Yoruba.

One family — five branches.
Semitic. Cushitic. Berber. Chadic. Omotic.
That family is Afro-Asiatic — the family of the Horn.

---

Grade 9 story addition (Ethiopia Wave 4):

The map of Africa blooms with voices — a thousand languages.

They sort into four great families, colour by colour, coast to coast.

And along the edges, newer tongues arrive by ship and caravan. The map keeps listening.

[Grade 11 depth scene — Africa's languages in depth — the frame of a continent. The animated map carries the story as the voice speaks.]
The Grade 11 book opens its Africa with this map for a reason: the continent's languages classified — Niger-Congo, the giant; Afro-Asiatic, spanning the north and Ethiopia; Nilo-Saharan; and Khoisan, oldest-sounding of all — and distributed across the land. The names to hold: Niger-Congo, Khoisan.
