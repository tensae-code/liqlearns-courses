# Lesson plan — Africa's Languages — the Four Families

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Africa's Languages — the Four Families
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's many languages group into four mother families — Afro-Asiatic, Nilo-Saharan, Niger-Congo and Khoisan — plus a fifth, Austronesian, on Madagascar; the most spoken are Swahili, then Hausa, then Yoruba.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the titles before it in this course — the learner has met Africa on the map and Ethiopia's own peoples. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The four families — and a fifth on Madagascar** — scholars group Africa's languages into four mother families, with Austronesian standing apart on Madagascar.
2. **The most-spoken languages, and the Afro-Asiatic branches** — Swahili first, Hausa second, Yoruba third; Afro-Asiatic's five branches — Semitic, Cushitic, Berber, Chadic, Omotic — with the Horn's languages placed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four mother language families of Africa, and the fifth family found on Madagascar.
2. Rank the three most-spoken languages the book names: Swahili, Hausa, Yoruba.
3. List the five branches of Afro-Asiatic and place Amharic, Somali, Oromo, Tigrinya and Hausa in their branches.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up a family and a language → a family is the mother group holding many languages; Swahili is one language, Niger-Congo is a family.
- Forgets Madagascar → the island stands apart in the picture on purpose: its family, Austronesian, is not one of the mainland four.
