# Africa's Languages — the Four Families

## Do it first

How many languages are spoken in your own town or village? Name every one you can — the language of home, the language of the market, the language of school.

Now think bigger. A whole continent speaks thousands of languages. How could anyone hold them in order? Try grouping the languages you know into families — languages that are kin, born from one mother tongue long ago.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four families — and a fifth on Madagascar

Scholars group Africa's many languages into **four mother families**:

- **Afro-Asiatic** — across the north and the Horn of Africa.
- **Nilo-Saharan** — across the Sudan belt and the lands around it.
- **Niger-Congo** — the great family of west, central, east and southern Africa.
- **Khoisan** — the smallest family, in the south-west.

[Picture 11300 — Africa's language families]

And one more stands apart. On the island of **Madagascar**, the languages belong to a fifth family: **Austronesian**. The island's tongues are kin to languages across the ocean, not to the mainland four — that is why the picture marks Madagascar separately.

## Sub lesson 2 — The most-spoken languages, and the Afro-Asiatic branches

Of all Africa's languages, the book names the most spoken in order:

1. **Swahili** — first.
2. **Hausa** — second.
3. **Yoruba** — third.

Now look inside one family. **Afro-Asiatic** — the family of the Horn, where Ethiopia stands — holds five branches: **Semitic, Cushitic, Berber, Chadic and Omotic**.

The Horn's own languages sit inside it: **Amharic, Somali, Oromo and Tigrinya** are Afro-Asiatic tongues of the Horn. And **Hausa** — the second most-spoken language on the continent — belongs to Afro-Asiatic too, in its **Chadic** branch. A language can be famous in two lists at once: Hausa is both a most-spoken language and a Chadic branch language.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 9 survey — over a thousand languages, four super-families

The Grade 9 book surveys the whole continent. **Over 1,000 interrelated languages** are spoken across Africa, classified by **shared vocabulary** into **four super-families: Congo-Kordofanian (Niger-Congo), Afro-Asiatic, Nilo-Saharan and Khoisan**.

One caveat rides with the count: **not all languages now spoken in Africa are native to it** — the book names **Arabic, English, French, Spanish and Portuguese** as arrivals. Set this continental frame beside the earlier grades' detail: four great families, a thousand tongues, and a margin of imported languages along the coasts and in the cities.


## Sub lesson 4 — Grade 11 depth: Africa's languages in depth — the frame of a continent

The Grade 11 book opens its Africa with this map for a reason: the continent's languages classified — Niger-Congo, the giant; Afro-Asiatic, spanning the north and Ethiopia; Nilo-Saharan; and Khoisan, oldest-sounding of all — and distributed across the land. States, trades and migrations in every following lesson walk on this linguistic ground; the families are the frame, the empires the pictures.
