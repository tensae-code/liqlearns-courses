# Egypt — the Tulunids and Ikhshidids

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Ahmad ibn Tulun gathers Egypt's reins in 868 and wins her people's love with justice and generosity — the first independent Egyptian state of the Islamic age, followed by al-Ikhshid's. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Egypt in the Tulunid and Ikhshidid ages

Ahmad ibn Tulun, the Turkish officer who came as a deputy in 868, gathers Egypt's reins, wins her people's love with justice and generosity, and stretches his state into the Levant as its shield; his son Khumarawayh defeats the caliphate's armies and wins a thirty-year grant of Egypt and the Levant — the first independent Egyptian state of the Islamic age, followed by Muhammad ibn Tughj al-Ikhshid's.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.
