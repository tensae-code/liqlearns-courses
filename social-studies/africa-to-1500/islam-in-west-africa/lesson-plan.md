# Lesson plan — Islam in West Africa

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Islam in West Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Africa to 1500 — built in the History expansion (Gambia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Islam in West Africa reshapes law, learning and kingship without erasing older ways — the new faith changes West African societies, and older ways continue beside it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Law reshaped** — The new faith reshapes law in West African societies. How people judge, rule and settle questions of right and wrong changes as Islam takes root.
2. **Learning reshaped** — The new faith reshapes learning too. West African scholarship and teaching are changed by Islam's arrival and growth.
3. **Kingship reshaped — and older ways remain** — Kingship is reshaped by the new faith — but older ways are not erased. The book teaches the change and the continuity together: a society can take a new faith and still keep much of what it was.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
