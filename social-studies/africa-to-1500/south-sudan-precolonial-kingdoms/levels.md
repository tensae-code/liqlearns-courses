# South Sudan — its pre-colonial kingdoms — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pre-Colonial Kingdoms
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — before Europeans, South Sudan's main kingdoms were Azande, Otuho, Chollo (Shilluk) and Anyuak, while Moru-Madi, Luo and Atker had no kings but chiefs, clan heads and family heads

## Sub lesson 2 — Features of kingdoms in South Sudan
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — a king heads the kingdom, warriors defend it, a council of elders advises, subjects obey, and each kingdom keeps its own artwork and economic life

## Sub lesson 3 — The Kingdoms of South Sudan: Shilluk
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Shilluk kingdom on the White Nile, capital Fashoda, was founded in the mid-fifteenth century by its first ruler, the demi-god Nyikang, and its people sing of the Reth and their titles

## Sub lesson 4 — The Shilluk Kingdom (1490-1865)
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Nyikang quarrels with his relatives, migrates and founds a sacred monarchy on the White Nile at Fashoda; every reth embodies Nyikang's spirit, wins his throne in a mock battle against the effigy's army, rules Gar and Lak through chiefs, and the kingdom declines under Ottoman attacks and British colonisation

## Sub lesson 5 — The Azande Kingdom
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Vungara dynasty's fierce warriors conquer much land; dead kings' sons fight for the seat and losers found new kingdoms east and north; the Zande work iron, farm millet and sorghum, and order justice through belief in mangu, witchcraft in the belly, as Evans-Pritchard recorded in 1937

## Sub lesson 6 — The Anyuak (Anywaa) Kingdom
Sub lesson 6 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — a federation of river villages along the Sobat, Baro and Akobo, each with its Nyiya king, feuds over the Ocwok royal throne and bead until the British make Agada Akway king of kings; his line reaches the 23rd king, Adongo Agada, and the people farm, fish, smith iron and pan gold for demuy beads
