## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pre-Colonial Kingdoms** — before Europeans, South Sudan's main kingdoms were Azande, Otuho, Chollo (Shilluk) and Anyuak, while Moru-Madi, Luo and Atker had no kings but chiefs, clan heads and family heads
2. **Features of kingdoms in South Sudan** — a king heads the kingdom, warriors defend it, a council of elders advises, subjects obey, and each kingdom keeps its own artwork and economic life
3. **The Kingdoms of South Sudan: Shilluk** — the Shilluk kingdom on the White Nile, capital Fashoda, was founded in the mid-fifteenth century by its first ruler, the demi-god Nyikang, and its people sing of the Reth and their titles
4. **The Shilluk Kingdom (1490-1865)** — Nyikang quarrels with his relatives, migrates and founds a sacred monarchy on the White Nile at Fashoda; every reth embodies Nyikang's spirit, wins his throne in a mock battle against the effigy's army, rules Gar and Lak through chiefs, and the kingdom declines under Ottoman attacks and British colonisation
5. **The Azande Kingdom** — the Vungara dynasty's fierce warriors conquer much land; dead kings' sons fight for the seat and losers found new kingdoms east and north; the Zande work iron, farm millet and sorghum, and order justice through belief in mangu, witchcraft in the belly, as Evans-Pritchard recorded in 1937
6. **The Anyuak (Anywaa) Kingdom** — a federation of river villages along the Sobat, Baro and Akobo, each with its Nyiya king, feuds over the Ocwok royal throne and bead until the British make Agada Akway king of kings; his line reaches the 23rd king, Adongo Agada, and the people farm, fish, smith iron and pan gold for demuy beads
