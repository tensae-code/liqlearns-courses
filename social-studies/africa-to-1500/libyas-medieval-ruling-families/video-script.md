The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Libya's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Inside what is now Libya, local families and dynasties govern the coast and the oases between the great empires, keeping Libyan towns alive between Fatimids, Zirids and Hafsids.

Harun al-Rashid appoints Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab over Ifriqiya in 800; his line builds al-Abbasiyya and a great fleet, takes Sicily in 827 under the judge Asad ibn al-Furat, and makes the Maghreb flourish — until the Fatimids end the state in 909.

The Fatimid call rises in Ifriqiya, destroys the Aghlabids, then takes Egypt and founds Cairo, ruling the southern Mediterranean.

Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi founds the Fatimid state in North Africa in 909 and builds Mahdia; al-Mu'izz makes Tripoli's port his naval base for the Sicily jihad, Jawhar takes Egypt in 969 and founds Cairo and al-Azhar — then the Banu Hilal invasion ruins Ifriqiya and the state fades away in 1171.
