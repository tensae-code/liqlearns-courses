# East African Coast — Azania and Zanj

## Do it first

Stand on a coast in your mind: dhows arriving with the season's wind, markets at the water's edge, goods moving between inland peoples and overseas sailors.

This coast had two old names before it had the one you will meet next lesson. Learn both here — and notice what each name is really describing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Azania

**Azania** was the **East African coast** — its **exact extent is uncertain**, and the lesson keeps it that way. Its societies held **towns, ports and markets**, and traded **ivory** with the **Himyarites of South Arabia**, who **brought iron goods and weapons** in return.

## Sub lesson 2 — Zanj

**Zanj** was the coast **from the Horn to Mozambique**, ruled by **kings including market-centre rulers** — **wakilimi**, as the book prints the word.

Its people were **mostly Bantu-speaking, of Arab and Persian descent as the book presents them**, **herding cattle**, with **traditional beliefs** — **Maklanjaru**, as printed — and **planet, animal and mineral gods**.

[Picture 11311 — Azania and Zanj — the East African coast]

Two names, one coast: Azania the trading shore of towns and ivory, Zanj the long kings' coast running south to Mozambique. The city-states of the next lesson grow out of this shore.

## Say it to your companion

Describe the coast twice: once as Azania (towns, ports, markets, ivory out, iron goods in), once as Zanj (Horn to Mozambique, market-centre kings, cattle herding, beliefs as printed). Your companion must catch wakilimi and Maklanjaru exactly as printed.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the coast from the Horn to Mozambique as one long curve. Label the whole curve: Azania and Zanj.
2. Write Azania's trade as two arrows: ivory out to South Arabia; iron goods and weapons in.
3. Write Zanj's two 'as printed' words carefully: wakilimi (market-centre rulers), Maklanjaru (traditional beliefs).
