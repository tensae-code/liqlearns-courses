# How Africa's regions traded with each other

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Four great exchange systems

Close the Africa-to-1500 unit by drawing its map whole. Four exchange systems webbed the continent: the trans-Saharan caravans north–south; the Nile valley's river road; the Red Sea crossing to Arabia; and the Indian Ocean's monsoon trade down the east coast.

Gold, salt, ivory, cloth, faiths and ideas moved along all four — Africa's regions were never sealed boxes.

## Sub lesson 2 — What moved, and why it mattered

Salt went south where people sweated and lacked it; gold went north where kings coined and coveted it; ivory and slaves moved to the coasts; books and beliefs travelled with the merchants.

Trade did more than enrich: it built the Sudanic empires, the Swahili cities and Aksum's successor states, and it carried Islam and Christianity into new lands.

## Sub lesson 3 — The continent as one story

This is the unit's verdict: study Africa's regions together, because Africans did. The routes explain the states; the states guarded the routes; the faiths rode with the goods.

Keep this map in mind — the next unit watches outsiders arrive at its edges, and everything changes.
