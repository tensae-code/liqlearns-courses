# Video script — West African Empires — Mali and Mansa Musa

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This is the story of a king and his gold.
(Summary — the pilgrimage told as story, never the book's own words.)

Sundiata built Mali in 1235.
Two generations on, its king was Mansa Musa —
ruler of the gold lands of the upper Niger.

A Muslim king must, if he can, walk to Mecca.
So in 1324 Mansa Musa set out — through Cairo, on to Mecca.
And everywhere he passed, he gave gold.
To Egypt's rulers: gold.
To Mecca's rulers: gold.
Not counted in coins — given lavishly, like water.

Cairo talked. Mecca talked.
Ships carried the story across the sea:
far in the west there is a king
whose land grows gold like grain.

At home stood Timbuktu — gold in its markets,
scholars in its schools.
Mali's harvests fed it: millet, sorghum, rice from Niani's rains,
grain from the state farms, gold from its ground.

But no empire stands forever.
Kings quarrelled over the throne.
Tributaries walked away. The Mossi struck.
The Tuaregs took Timbuktu itself.

The gold road remained.
The empire of Mansa Musa did not.
