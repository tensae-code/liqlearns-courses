# Lesson plan — West African Empires — Mali and Mansa Musa

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** West African Empires — Mali and Mansa Musa
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Mali rose when Sundiata Keita united the upper Niger in 1235, and won the world's attention when Mansa Musa carried its gold on pilgrimage to Mecca (1324–1325) while Timbuktu shone for gold and learning.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on West African Empires — Ghana, the lesson just before it in this title — Mali is Ghana's successor on the same gold ground. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Sundiata and the founding** — the Mandinka under Sundiata Keita breaking free of Ghana and uniting the upper Niger peoples in 1235; Mali's rulers Muslim before the state was founded.
2. **Mansa Musa's pilgrimage** — the 1324–1325 pilgrimage through Cairo to Mecca, gold given lavishly to Egypt's and Mecca's rulers; Timbuktu's world fame for gold trade and Islamic learning.
3. **Wealth, farming and the break-up** — Niani's rain growing millet, sorghum and rice; state farms worked by slaves growing grain; gold the other revenue; succession struggles, tributaries breaking away, Mossi strikes, Tuaregs taking Timbuktu.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: West African Empires — Ghana. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell how Sundiata Keita founded Mali in 1235 and what its rulers' faith was.
2. Retell Mansa Musa's pilgrimage (1324–1325): the route, the gold-giving, and what it did for Mali's name.
3. Say how Mali ate and earned — Niani's rains, state farms, gold — and the three late-14th-century blows that broke it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Dates Sundiata and the fall of Ghana apart → both are 1235 in the book: the year Koumbi Saleh fell is the year Mali rose.
- Thinks the pilgrimage was only religion → it was also display: the gold given in Cairo and Mecca is what made the world notice Mali.
