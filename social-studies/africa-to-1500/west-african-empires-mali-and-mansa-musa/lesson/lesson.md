# West African Empires — Mali and Mansa Musa

## Do it first

Ghana has fallen — its capital taken in 1235. The gold trade still flows. Who picks up an empire from a fallen one?

And a second question to carry: if you ruled the richest gold land on earth, how would you make the whole world know your name? One Malian king's answer fills this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sundiata and the founding

In **1235** the **Mandinka under Sundiata Keita broke free of Ghana** and **united the upper Niger peoples** — the empire of **Mali** was founded. Its rulers were **Muslim before the state was founded**: Islam in Mali came before the empire, not after it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mansa Musa's pilgrimage

The world's attention came with a journey. In **1324–1325, Mansa Musa** made his **pilgrimage through Cairo to Mecca**, **giving gold lavishly to Egypt's and Mecca's rulers** as he passed. A king who could give gold away like that, city after city — the world had to ask who he was, and where such wealth came from.

[Picture 11307 — Mansa Musa's pilgrimage route]

Meanwhile **Timbuktu won world fame** with a double name: a centre of the **gold trade**, and a centre of **Islamic learning**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Wealth, farming and the break-up

Mali fed itself well. At **Niani**, the capital, **better rain grew millet, sorghum and rice**; **state farms worked by slaves grew grain**; and **gold was the other revenue** beside the farms.

The break-up came late in the **14th century**: **succession struggles** at the top, **tributaries declaring independence**, **Mossi states striking at its heart**, and **Tuaregs taking Timbuktu**. The empire of Sundiata and Mansa Musa came apart piece by piece.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Grade 9 book's Mali — the Niger, the pilgrimage, Timbuktu

The Grade 9 sweep of Mali runs on three rails. **Islam spread widely along the Niger** — with its language, laws and customs travelling the river. Its greatest king, **Mansa Musa (1312–1337)**, made his **famous pilgrimage to Mecca** — the journey that announced Mali's wealth to the world.

And **Timbuktu became one of the most important centres of Islamic learning and culture** — a desert-edge city of mosques, manuscripts and scholars, grown rich where caravan met river. The book pictures a cartoon of Mansa Musa; hold beside it this lesson's earlier parts — Sundiata's founding and the empire's break-up — and Mali's arc stands complete.
