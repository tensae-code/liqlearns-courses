# East African Coast — the Swahili Cities

## Do it first

The coast of the last lesson — Azania, Zanj — held towns, ports and kings. Now watch those ports grow into cities that trade with half the world.

Before you read, name what a port city needs to grow rich: a safe harbour, goods the world wants, and ships that come back every year. The Swahili cities had all three — for three hundred years.

## Sub lesson 1 — The coast people

The **Swahili** are the **Arab-Bantu mixed coastal people** — **"Swahili" means coast people** — living **from Somalia to Mozambique**. Their language became **East Africa's working language**, and it is **today Tanzania's national language**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five cities and their trade

Between **1200 and 1500** the coast raised named **city-states**: **Kilwa, Lamu, Malindi, Mombasa and Zanzibar**.

[Picture 11312 — the Swahili city-states, 1200–1500]

Their dhows traded **gold, ivory and slaves** with **Persia, China and Indonesia** — a coast in Ethiopia's own region speaking to Asia across the ocean, centuries before Europeans arrived in these waters in force.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Portuguese seizure

The ending came from the sea. The **Portuguese seized the coast (1500–1600)** and **destroyed the trade routes and the civilisation** the cities had built. The Swahili language and people remained — the city-states' trading world did not.

## Say it to your companion

Name the five cities down the coast in order without looking, then the three goods and the three far lands. Your companion closes the story: who seized the coast, when, and what they destroyed.
