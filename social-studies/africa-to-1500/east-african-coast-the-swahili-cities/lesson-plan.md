# Lesson plan — East African Coast — the Swahili Cities

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** East African Coast — the Swahili Cities
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Swahili — the Arab-Bantu 'coast people' from Somalia to Mozambique — raised the city-states of Kilwa, Lamu, Malindi, Mombasa and Zanzibar (1200–1500), trading gold, ivory and slaves as far as China, until the Portuguese seized the coast.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on East African Coast — Azania and Zanj, the lesson just before it in this title — the city-states grow out of that coast. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The coast people** — Swahili — the Arab-Bantu mixed coastal people, 'Swahili' meaning coast people — from Somalia to Mozambique; the language East Africa's working language and today Tanzania's national language.
2. **The five cities and their trade** — city-states of 1200–1500: Kilwa, Lamu, Malindi, Mombasa, Zanzibar; trading gold, ivory and slaves with Persia, China and Indonesia.
3. **The Portuguese seizure** — the Portuguese seizing the coast (1500–1600) and destroying the trade routes and the civilisation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: East African Coast — Azania and Zanj. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say who the Swahili are, what their name means, and what their language became.
2. Name the five city-states of 1200–1500 and their trade: goods, and the far lands they traded with.
3. Tell how the Portuguese seizure (1500–1600) ended the cities' world.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads 'Swahili' as only a language → in this lesson it is first a PEOPLE — the coast people — whose language then became the region's working language.
- Thinks the cities fell to a local rival → the book names outsiders from across the sea: the Portuguese, 1500–1600.
