# Lesson plan — Ancient Egypt — the Nile Kingdom

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Ancient Egypt — the Nile Kingdom
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Egypt grew along the Nile in provinces called nomes — 22 in Upper (southern) Egypt, 20 in Lower (northern) — until Menes united them about 3000 BC and founded the first dynasty; its Pharaohs were held divine.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the two language lessons before it in this title — the learner has placed Egypt's land and peoples. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before

(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Nomes, Menes and the Pharaoh** — the Nile valley's provinces — 22 Upper, 20 Lower — united by Menes about 3000 BC; the divine Pharaoh, his officials, and a farming people.
2. **Dynasties, invasion — and the guide's skeleton (teacher guide — evidence)** — 31 dynasties, some foreign-founded; the Hyksos invasion about 1640 BC and its ending about 1550 BC; the guide's Old/Middle/New Kingdoms, three classes, vizier and nomarch.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Africa's Languages — the Families in Detail. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: nomes, Pharaoh and dynasty are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a nome was, and how many nomes Upper and Lower Egypt held.
2. Tell how Menes united Egypt about 3000 BC and founded the first dynasty, and how many dynasties Egypt had in all.
3. Describe the Pharaoh's place, the officials who served him, and how most Egyptians lived.
4. Tell the Hyksos story: the invasion about 1640 BC, what it brought, and Ahmose I's expulsion about 1550 BC.
5. Name the guide's three kingdoms and three classes, and the vizier and the nomarch (teacher guide — evidence).

## If the learner is stuck
- Swaps Upper and Lower → Upper is the SOUTH (the river's upper course), Lower is the north — the picture's river runs from Upper at the bottom to Lower at the top.
- Thinks the Pharaoh was only a king → the book holds him divine: an earthly representative of the gods, not just a ruler.
