# Ancient Egypt — the Nile Kingdom

## Do it first

Look at a map of Egypt. One river — the Nile, the Abay — runs the whole length of the land, and nearly everything green holds to its banks.

If you had to rule this long thin land, how would you divide it so it could be governed? Draw your divisions. The Egyptians' answer had a name: the nome.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nomes, Menes and the Pharaoh

Along the Nile valley the Egyptians lived in administrative provinces called **nomes** — **22 in Upper Egypt**, the southern land, and **20 in Lower Egypt**, the northern land. Upper and Lower sound backwards until you follow the river: Upper Egypt is upstream, in the south; Lower Egypt is downstream, in the north where the river meets the sea.

[Picture 11302 — Ancient Egypt — the Nile kingdom]

About **3000 BC**, a king named **Menes** united the nomes and founded the **first dynasty** — the first ruling house of a single Egypt.

At Egypt's head stood the **Pharaoh**, held **divine** — an earthly representative of the gods. He was served by **commanders, tax officials and priests**. Below him, society was **mostly farmers**, growing **wheat, barley, onions, flax and vegetables** by Nile water, and keeping **cattle and goats** and **fishing** — with **scribes, merchants, craftsmen and slaves** among them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dynasties, invasion — and the guide's skeleton

Egypt had **31 dynasties** in all. Not all were Egyptian-born: some were founded by invading **Nubians, Syrians, Persians and Romans**.

The great break came with the **Hyksos invasion, about 1640 BC**, which broke the civilisation — yet brought gifts with it: the **horse in war** and **bronze-working**. About **1550 BC**, **Ahmose I expelled the Hyksos** and Egypt stood whole again.

A teacher's skeleton helps hold the long story straight. Egyptian history is divided into **three periods — the Old Kingdom, the Middle Kingdom and the New Kingdom** — and its society into **three classes: upper, middle and lower**. The administration had its own named officers: the **vizier**, the chief official — the prime minister — appointed by the Pharaoh to run the government, and the **nomarch**, the appointed official who administered a nome. Egypt itself was called **"the gift of the Nile"** — the river gave irrigation water, and transport and communication besides.

## Say it to your companion

Tell Egypt's story as three sentences: the nomes and Menes; the Pharaoh and his farming people; the Hyksos break and Ahmose's rescue. Your companion then asks: who was the vizier? Who was the nomarch? Answer both.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the Nile as one long line. Mark Lower Egypt (20 nomes) at the north end and Upper Egypt (22 nomes) at the south end.
2. Write Menes' date — about 3000 BC — at the joining point, and the two Hyksos dates, about 1640 BC and about 1550 BC, on a small timeline.
3. Write the court's ladder: Pharaoh at the top, then vizier, then nomarch over his nome.
