# Egypt from the Arab conquest to the independent states

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: For two centuries after the Arab conquest Egypt is a province governed from abroad — until fast-changing governors and Turkish amirs teach her to dream of ruling herself. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Egypt from the Islamic conquest until the rise of the independent states

For two centuries Egypt is a province governed from abroad — under the Umayyads Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan, the best and justest of her governors, builds the Nilometer of the year 69 AH and makes Helwan his seat; under the Abbasids the governors change so fast, farmed out to Turkish amirs and their deputies, that Egypt learns to dream of ruling herself.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Egypt from the Islamic conquest until the rise of the independent states. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
