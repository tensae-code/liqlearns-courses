# West African Empires — Songhai

## Do it first

Two empires have risen and fallen on the Niger bend in this title — Ghana taxed, Mali shone. The river bends on.

If you were the next people of the bend, what would you copy from Mali — and what would you fix? Name one of each before you read. Songhai's two great kings split your answer between them: one conquered, one organised.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sunni Ali — the conqueror

As Mali weakened, the **Songhai of the Niger bend** rose. **Sunni Ali (1464–1492)** made Songhai a great empire **by conquest**: he drove the **Tuaregs out of Timbuktu** and the **Mossi off the bend**, and at his death he left **an empire covering all Mali**.

[Picture 11308 — Songhai — empire of the Niger bend]

## Sub lesson 2 — Askia Muhammad — the builder

**Askia Muhammad** founded the **Askia dynasty**. A **devout Muslim**, he **expanded the state** and **built strong administration**; he **used Islam to unify** the empire and **posted Sharia judges empire-wide to keep peace**. He **subdued the Muslim Tuaregs** and **raised great learning centres in Timbuktu and Jenne** — the bend's cities shone again, now under Songhai.

## Sub lesson 3 — The fall

Late in the **16th century** the pressures gathered: **succession struggles**; new **eastern rivals — Bornu, the Hausa city-states and the Tuareg sultanate**; and **Europeans diverting the gold trade to the coast**, draining the bend's wealth seaward.

Then the blow: **Morocco's invasion of 1591** ended Songhai — the third and last of the Niger bend's great empires in this title.

## Say it to your companion

Split the empire between its two kings: one sentence for Sunni Ali the conqueror, one for Askia Muhammad the builder. Then close with the fall: the rivals, the diverted gold, and 1591. Your companion checks every name against the lesson.
