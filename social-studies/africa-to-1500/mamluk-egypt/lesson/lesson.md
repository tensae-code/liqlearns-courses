# Mamluk Egypt

## Do it first

At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Slave-soldiers who became sultans

Mamluk Egypt was ruled by a remarkable caste: soldiers bought as slaves, trained as elite cavalry, freed into power — until the soldiers' own commanders took the throne itself. For over two and a half centuries, Mamluk sultans ruled Egypt and Syria.

A state run by men who began as property sounds impossible; Cairo made it a system, renewed by each generation of recruits.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cairo — wealth and scholarship

Under the Mamluks, Cairo grew rich on the trade between the Indian Ocean and the Mediterranean, and splendid with mosques, schools and hospitals. Scholars and craftsmen gathered where the money and the patronage were.

Egypt's old role — the hinge between seas — did not end with the pharaohs. The Mamluks inherited the hinge and taxed it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Victors over Mongols and Crusaders

Twice the Mamluks stopped invasions others could not: they defeated the Mongols, whose advance had seemed unstoppable, and they finished the Crusader presence in the Levant.

That double victory fixed their fame in the Islamic world — and kept Egypt independent while surrounding lands fell.
