# Africa's Languages — the Families in Detail

## Do it first

Last lesson sorted Africa's languages into four families. This time, open three of the families up and look inside.

Before you read on: which family do you think holds the most people? Which holds the fewest? Which one would you most like to hear spoken aloud? Hold your guesses — the lesson answers all three.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nilo-Saharan

The **Nilo-Saharan** family runs across a broad belt: **Sudan, South Sudan, Chad, southern Egypt, northern Tanzania, Nigeria, Kenya, DR Congo, Ethiopia and Niger**.

Inside it stand named branches — **Songhay, Koman and Chari-Nile** — and named languages: **Berta, Fur, Kunama, Gumuz and Kadu**. Several of those names are Ethiopia's own neighbours and peoples — this family reaches right into the Horn's western edge.

## Sub lesson 2 — Niger-Congo

The **Niger-Congo** family is, in the book's telling, **the world's largest by speakers**. Its lands cover **west, central, east and southern Africa** — more of the continent than any other family.

Its named languages include **Igbo, Fula, Shona, Zulu, Bantu and Yoruba**. Yoruba carries a second honour from the last lesson: it is the third most-spoken language in the book's ranking.

## Sub lesson 3 — Khoisan — the click family

The **Khoisan** family is named from two names joined: **Khoikhoi + San**. It is **the oldest and the smallest** of the families, spoken in **Namibia and Botswana**.

And it is famous for a sound no other family makes its own: **click sounds, made by drawing air in**. Where other tongues push air out to speak, the Khoisan languages click the air inward — hear one word and you know the family at once.
