# Lesson plan — Africa's Languages — the Families in Detail

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Africa to 1500 · **Lesson:** Africa's Languages — the Families in Detail
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Africa to 1500 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** each family up close — Nilo-Saharan's branches and lands, Niger-Congo the largest family with its named tongues, and Khoisan, the oldest and smallest, famous for its click sounds.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Africa's Languages — the Four Families, the lesson just before it in this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Nilo-Saharan** — Songhay, Koman and Chari-Nile branches; Berta, Fur, Kunama, Gumuz and Kadu languages; a belt of lands from southern Egypt to northern Tanzania.
2. **Niger-Congo** — the world's largest family in the book's telling, across west, central, east and southern Africa: Igbo, Fula, Shona, Zulu, Bantu, Yoruba.
3. **Khoisan — the click family** — named from Khoikhoi + San; the oldest and smallest family, in Namibia and Botswana, marked by click sounds made by drawing air in.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Africa's Languages — the Four Families — it names the families this lesson opens up. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Nilo-Saharan's branches and lands, and five of its languages.
2. Say where Niger-Congo is spoken, why the book calls it the largest, and name six of its languages.
3. Say what Khoisan's name is made of, where it is spoken, and what its click sounds are.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts Yoruba in the wrong family → Yoruba is Niger-Congo (and third most-spoken overall — a language can hold both facts).
- Thinks clicks are made by blowing air out → the book says the opposite: the clicks are made by drawing air in.
