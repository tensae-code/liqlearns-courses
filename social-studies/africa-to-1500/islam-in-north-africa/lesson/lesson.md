# Islam in North Africa

## Do it first

Before you read anything: look at the title of this lesson and write down, from anything you already know, one thing you expect its story to contain — a name, a place, a date. Keep your line. At the end you will check it against the story itself: what you expected, what surprised you, and what you can now prove.

## Sub lesson 1 — Conquest opens the door

Islam first crossed North Africa by conquest, as Arab armies carried the new faith westward along the Mediterranean coast in the generation after the Prophet. Cities changed rulers; garrisons became towns.

Conquest explains the speed. It does not explain the depth — that came slower, and by other means.

## Sub lesson 2 — Trade and scholars do the deep work

The faith rooted itself by caravan and classroom: merchants carried Islam along the trade routes, and scholars and holy men taught it in the towns of the Maghreb and beyond.

By that road Islam crossed the Sahara too, linking North Africa to the gold lands of West Africa — the connection the next lessons trade on.

## Sub lesson 3 — A reshaped region

Islam remade North Africa's law, learning, architecture and alliances, and bound the region into the wider Islamic world from Spain to Arabia.

Yet it arrived in a land with older peoples and tongues — the Berber peoples above all — whose history runs through the Islamic centuries, not under them.
