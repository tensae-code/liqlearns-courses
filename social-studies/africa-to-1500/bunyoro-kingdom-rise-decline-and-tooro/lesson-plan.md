## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bunyoro and Tooro kingdoms** — Bunyoro, once the region's great power, declines because it is too big for one ruler, torn by internal conflict and pressed by rising neighbours — Tooro and Buganda — before the colonialists arrive
2. **Bunyoro kingdom** — Bunyoro grows under Omukama Kamurasi, and his son Kabalega inherits a kingdom whose districts the book maps onto today's Hoima, Masindi and Buliisa
3. **Bunyoro shrinks** — Bunyoro Kingdom is reduced in size, and Toro breaks away from it — the old empire's map redrawn by secession
4. **Bunyoro Kingdom** — the Batembuzi demi-gods vanish around 1200 AD, the Chwezi follow, and a centralised kingdom grows under the hereditary Omukama — provinces, tax-collecting chiefs — so strong it resists colonisation until Kabaleega is forced into asylum in 1896
