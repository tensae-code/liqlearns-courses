# Video script — Bunyoro — rise, decline and Tooro

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Uganda's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bunyoro, once the region's great power, declines because it is too big for one ruler, torn by internal conflict and pressed by rising neighbours — Tooro and Buganda — before the colonialists arrive.
Bunyoro grows under Omukama Kamurasi, and his son Kabalega inherits a kingdom whose districts the book maps onto today's Hoima, Masindi and Buliisa.
Bunyoro Kingdom is reduced in size, and Toro breaks away from it — the old empire's map redrawn by secession.
The Batembuzi demi-gods vanish around 1200 AD, the Chwezi follow, and a centralised kingdom grows under the hereditary Omukama — provinces, tax-collecting chiefs — so strong it resists colonisation until Kabaleega is forced into asylum in 1896.
