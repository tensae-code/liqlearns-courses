Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka G10 Tamil TG) (teacher guide — evidence): a 90-period competency syllabus (units 10.1–10.13, printed in full) with an expanded-content section and a timed activity set (செயற்பாடு) for every unit — quality inputs, process steps and assessment criteria — plus annexed readings (a poem on Earth and life, a 2004-tsunami reading) and term-test blueprints; the non-content parts (teaching-learning methods, pp. 18–23, and assessment, pp. 325–337) are pedagogy, named here but not placed. That evidence is stocked here as the way Sri Lanka teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Sri Lanka is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 11 geo-5as-lm): The senior Letters cycle re-runs physical geography at full depth in four units — Planet Earth, Climate, the Effects of Climate, the Earth (structure, erosion, coasts) — with a class project closing Units 1 and 4.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 10 Geography (Dari)): the upper-secondary pattern changes from continent tours to themes studied in depth through Afghanistan itself: a full map-skills chapter (map types, scale, colour, projection, satellite photos), Afghanistan's climate and each of its river basins one by one (Amu, Kabul, Helmand–Sistan, Harirud, the closed basins), an environment-protection chapter aimed at Kabul's air and streets, a natural-hazards chapter, then earth-and-space and atmosphere chapters.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 10 G10 Geography of Man and the Environment): secondary geography is thematic, not regional — man and space, man representing his geographic field (remote sensing, projections, maps), man organising it (rural/urban), climate and water, the dynamic Earth, and the great environmental systems.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 6 Geography 6): world physical Geography taught through Pakistan's own land — its fault lines and 1935/2005 earthquakes, Khewra salt and Thar coal among the rocks, K2 and the Karakoram among the mountains, Pakistan's four climatic regions, its six forest types from mangrove to alpine, and the Indus flood as the country's defining disaster.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 10 LY G10 Principles): Secondary geography opens as a two-part systematic course — physical geography first (astronomy, surface-shaping factors, climate, vegetation and wild animals), then human geography (population and settlement, economic resources) — and closes with a whole book on spatial-data technology: remote sensing, GPS and GIS, then the history of maps from Babylon and Ibn Hawqal.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 5 G5 Geography): first Geography year built as a single Earth-systems arc — planet, envelopes (lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere/biosphere), human society, then a closing "Protecting the Earth" chapter; every paragraph opens with "you will be able to" outcomes and chapters close with evaluation tests, plus two practical applications (map work; a week-long weather calendar).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 4 np-g4): Grade 4 "Our Earth" moves to the district (Kanchanpur read on a map), the origin and structure of the Earth, and Nepal's two neighbours India and China; resources and traffic rules sit inside the Civic Awareness unit.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 5 TG np-g5tg) (teacher guide — evidence): Guide to the new-curriculum Grade 5 book (companion to the trial-edition line, not to the older English np-g5): its "Hamro Prithvi" unit teaches the solar system and Earth's structure, then Nepal's natural form, climate, vegetation, population and social life, local disasters, and map-making — learners draw their own province's map showing their district and local level and study the Nepal map. Economic unit works from local economic activities and locally produced goods (and the markets that sell them) up to the country's major economic activities across Himalayan, Hilly and Terai regions. That evidence is stocked here as the way Nepal teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Nepal is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 6 np-g6): Grade 6 has a full "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth) unit — Earth's introduction, latitude/longitude, outer-layer structure, then Nepal itself: geographical description, climate, rivers/glaciers/lakes, natural vegetation, natural resources and map study, closing with disaster management; a population unit follows (demography, the census). Economic activities are organised by region and resource: agriculture and industry, services and trade, tourism, and economic resources (land and water; forest, minerals and people).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 8 np-g8): this is the old kingdom-era standalone Geography volume — Nepal is taught through its 14 zones (anchal), with full regional accounts of Bagmati, Narayani, Mechi and Mahakali zones (position, environment, resources, development, chief towns, each with maps); Asia is the one continent studied in depth; world economic geography is built on "environment and man" (geographical control). Chapters marked ** in the contents (Earth's surface/interior; the population chapter) were prescribed only for certain school types.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 3): a systematic geomorphology course — rocks and Earth's interior first, then isostasy, continental drift and plate tectonics, then internal agents (folds, faults, earthquakes, volcanoes in four lessons), then external agents and the modelled landscapes of hot, temperate, cold and coastal environments, closing with the geological cycle.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G9 Geography T1): From Grade 9 Geography stands alone as its own book: the Earth's rock and water spheres first, then the geography of the Arab homeland — its natural features, its human features and its challenges.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 4 Estudios Sociales G4): Units I–V are a full national Geography of Nicaragua — coordinates, time zones and seasons first, then the country's position, borders, political division and hydrography, and a closing landscape unit on climate, natural resources, relief by region, earthquakes and hurricanes; the second half of the book is national History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI first settlers' history and culture (printed pp. 120–137, History, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII European presence and colonisation (printed pp. 140–164, History); Unit VIII Independence and the National War (printed pp. 168–183, History); Unit IX Nicaragua 1857–1909 (printed pp. 186–197, History); Unit X Nicaragua in the 20th century (printed pp. 200–221, History).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 10 Geography): Class X pairs deep physical theory (Big Bang, plate tectonics, the making of the Himalayas) with an applied Bhutan block — climate and vegetation zones of Bhutan, Bhutanese agriculture's challenges and advancement, industries and their distribution, and a Bhutan disaster chapter that adds Education in Emergency.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 11 Geography): The Advanced level (XI–XII) is full academic geography — map projections, remote sensing and GIS, insolation and pressure belts, climate classification — with Bhutan applied chapters on agriculture, manufacturing and tourism, the last taught on Bhutan's own sustainability principle ("high value, low volume" in the Bhutan Tourism Monitor data the book uses).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Geography student): grade-10 Geography is a methods-first course — it opens with the science's history, branches and GIS/GPS/aerospace methods, works through the lithosphere with a full natural-disasters lesson, and closes with resources measured by endowment; Armenia's own rivers fill the hydrology tables and Armenia's place in modern geopolitics closes the political-map theme.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 OM Geografía G5) (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher guide teaches the same four units as the programme/student book but adds Cuba-specific complementary materials no other country set carries: the Tarea Vida state climate-change plan as classroom content, project-based-learning orientation, and a long section on mastering the changing world political map through country cases (Congo, Belarus, the Yugoslav successor states). Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 Geografía G5): The first Geography book a Cuban child meets opens with the history of geography itself (cuneiform maps, Hecataeus), builds all map skills before any content, and ends the planet tour with world population, the world political map and world heritage — with Cuba's own places (Llanura del Cauto, Trinidad) woven in as examples throughout.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).
