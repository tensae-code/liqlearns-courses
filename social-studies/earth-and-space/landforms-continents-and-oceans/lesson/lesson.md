# Landforms, Continents and Oceans

## Do it first

Every shape on the Earth's surface — every hill, valley and plain — was made by forces. Two families of forces do the work.

## Sub lesson 1 — Landforms: how the Earth's face is made

Internal forces come from inside the Earth: folding, faulting and volcanism push the surface up and crack it open. External forces work from outside: erosion, weathering and deposition wear it down and lay the worn material somewhere new.

The chief features to name: continents, oceans, seas, lakes, the lower atmosphere, the living surface layer, and human-made landscapes. And on the continents themselves, four great shapes: plateaus, plains, mountains and rift valleys.

## Sub lesson 2 — The seven continents and five oceans

Now rank the world. The seven continents, by area: Asia is largest at 44,810,880 km², and Africa is second at 30,335,000 km²; the list runs down through North America, South America, Europe and Antarctica to Australia, smallest at 8,289,000 km².

The five oceans: the Pacific is by far the largest at 165,384,000 km², and the Arctic is the smallest at 14,056,000 km², with the Atlantic, Indian and Southern oceans between them.

Do the ranking yourself from the map and the table: name all seven continents and all five oceans, and put each set in order, largest to smallest. That ordering — not the bare list — is the skill.
