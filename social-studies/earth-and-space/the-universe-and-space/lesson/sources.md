Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 10): This is the physical-geography year: the universe and Earth's layers, continental drift with volcanoes and earthquakes — including earthquake-prevention measures as a group task — water and winds across the continents, and a closing unit on political and economic geography that treats borders, geopolitical order and international climate agreements as geography, with hurricane-season citizen responsibility as the water unit's group activity.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado06 Secundaria Activa): Grade 6 Secundaria Activa starts from the universe and the Earth as a "living machine" (rocks and wrinkles, water, air), then follows humans evolving, migrating from Africa to America and adapting to each continent's environment — geography as the stage for all the history that follows.

India's own way (Grade 6 The Earth Our Habitat): The first Geography year goes planet → globe → motions → maps → Earth's domains → landforms, and only then turns home: the last two chapters are India itself (location, neighbours, physical divisions, monsoon climate, vegetation and wildlife), so world basics are immediately applied to the home country.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 1 Social Studies G1): the Grade 1 book starts the same widening-rings series (Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland); Geography enters through street-crossing safety, then the environment unit — nature and the farm, the four friends (the seasons), beautiful nature, water "more precious than gold", a precious treasure, and a journey into space — and closes with the homeland's antiquities and pictures from across Syria.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 10 Geography (Dari)): the upper-secondary pattern changes from continent tours to themes studied in depth through Afghanistan itself: a full map-skills chapter (map types, scale, colour, projection, satellite photos), Afghanistan's climate and each of its river basins one by one (Amu, Kabul, Helmand–Sistan, Harirud, the closed basins), an environment-protection chapter aimed at Kabul's air and streets, a natural-hazards chapter, then earth-and-space and atmosphere chapters.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 10 G10 Geography of Man and the Environment): secondary geography is thematic, not regional — man and space, man representing his geographic field (remote sensing, projections, maps), man organising it (rural/urban), climate and water, the dynamic Earth, and the great environmental systems.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 ES T2 Workbook): the final-term activities book spans the universe (galaxies, dark matter, the Big Bang), severe weather (tornado and flood modelling) and deep time — ending with building a geological timescale for Jordan itself.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 ES T2): the last term spans galaxies and the origin of the universe, severe weather and how to stay safe in it, and the history of the Earth — with geological tourism in Jordan as the closing enrichment.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 11 Geography): The Advanced level (XI–XII) is full academic geography — map projections, remote sensing and GIS, insolation and pressure belts, climate classification — with Bhutan applied chapters on agriculture, manufacturing and tourism, the last taught on Bhutan's own sustainability principle ("high value, low volume" in the Bhutan Tourism Monitor data the book uses).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 OM Geografía G5) (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher guide teaches the same four units as the programme/student book but adds Cuba-specific complementary materials no other country set carries: the Tarea Vida state climate-change plan as classroom content, project-based-learning orientation, and a long section on mastering the changing world political map through country cases (Congo, Belarus, the Yugoslav successor states). That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Cuba is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 Geografía G5): The first Geography book a Cuban child meets opens with the history of geography itself (cuneiform maps, Hecataeus), builds all map skills before any content, and ends the planet tour with world population, the world political map and world heritage — with Cuba's own places (Llanura del Cauto, Trinidad) woven in as examples throughout.
