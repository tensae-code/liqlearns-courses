# The Solar System — the Sun and the Planets

## Do it first

The solar system is the Sun plus everything that circles it. The Sun is one star among hundreds of billions in the Milky Way — but for us it is the centre of everything.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Sun: the star at the centre

The Sun formed from contracting gas: about 75% hydrogen and 25% helium. It is about the same age as its planets. Its surface temperature is 6,000 °C, and its inside reaches 15,000,000 °C.

How big is it? Its diameter — the line straight through its middle — is 1,390,176 km. Compare that with the Earth: the distance all the way around the Earth is 40,075 km. The Sun is not a solid, and not a liquid: like all stars, it is gas held burning-hot — think of it before you answer: which state of matter is the Sun? Gas.

## Sub lesson 2 — The planets: no light of their own

A planet does not make light. Planets shine only because they reflect sunlight, the way a wall shines when a lamp falls on it.

Each planet follows its own path around the Sun. That path is called its orbit, and no two planets share one.

The first four planets from the Sun — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — are rocky and solid. The outer four — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune — are gas giants, made mostly of compressed hydrogen and helium. Name both sets out loud now, in order.

## Sub lesson 3 — Comparing the planets: reading the table

A data table lets you compare all eight planets at once: each one's mean distance from the Sun, its diameter, its surface temperature, how long it takes to spin once (rotation), how long it takes to go round the Sun once (revolution), and how many moons it has.

Work the table like a tool. Rank the planets by size. Sort them into mooned and moonless. Find each planet's distinguishing feature — the one thing that sets it apart.

Two table puzzles to solve. First: Venus is farther from the Sun than Mercury, yet Venus is hotter — because its dense atmosphere traps heat, like a thick blanket. Second: Pluto is not in the table of planets. In 2006 the world's astronomers' union removed it from the planet list, because it has no orbit of its own clear of other bodies.
