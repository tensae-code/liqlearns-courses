**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Angola, Bahamas, India, Cambodia, Sudan, Syria, Venezuela, Sri Lanka). Haiti is named here as teacher-sequence evidence only, counted nowhere. Jamaica is named here as curriculum-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Jamaica's own way (Grade 4 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Social Studies is one of four subjects fused into a single Exploratory Core guide, organised by four attainment themes; Geography enters through Jamaica itself — Taino settlement maps, cardinal points, Jamaica's physical features, parishes, weather and the Earth in the Solar System — always with hands-on map and model work. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
Jamaica's own way (Grade 5 Exploratory Core, curriculum guide — evidence): Grade 5 Social Studies pairs a heritage year (Europeans and Africans, national heroes, Caribbean culture) with a physical-environment year — erosion, locating Jamaica and the Caribbean by compass and latitude/longitude, types of rainfall, the Solar System, plants and water pollution. That evidence is stocked here as the way Jamaica teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography opens with representing the Earth itself — globe-to-flat projection problems, orientation practised on the ground with compass/GPS/Google Maps, and time-zone puzzles worked on Haiti's own links (midday shared with Québec, 18h in Paris, Port-au-Prince–Paris flights); every séance ends in a croquis, a glossary and field observation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Book error taught corrected: §1.3 gives the Sun's "circumference" as 1,390,176 km — that figure is the Sun's diameter, not its circumference; this lesson teaches it as the diameter, in the text and in Picture 11007. The planet table's moon counts (for example Jupiter 16, Saturn 21, Neptune 2) are the book's period figures from its printing time and are flagged here as dated counts — more moons have been counted since; the lesson teaches the table as a comparison tool, not as today's moon census.

Angola's own way (Grade 5): Geography opens as "geographic space" — the natural space and the human-built space (Luanda port, Benguela railway, Cambambe dam, Huambo housing) — before any physical topic; Angola's own position, borders, population figures (INE) and farm/fishery tables carry the world topics. That way is stocked here as the way Angola teaches this thing.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

India's own way (Grade 6 The Earth Our Habitat): The first Geography year goes planet → globe → motions → maps → Earth's domains → landforms, and only then turns home: the last two chapters are India itself (location, neighbours, physical divisions, monsoon climate, vegetation and wildlife), so world basics are immediately applied to the home country.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Earth Science): Earth Science is Cambodia's physical-geography strand — planet Earth and plate tectonics, water, a full atmosphere-and-climate course (ending with Cambodia's own weather and climate change), and the environment.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies G3): the Grade 3 book widens in rings — Me, Me and You, My Safety, My Society, My Environment, My Homeland — and the Geography lives in the last three rings: road safety, Syrian-made products, ways of living, then a whole environment unit (sky lanterns, locating my position, discovering Syria, the weather bulletin, a water journey).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 2 G2 CS Viva Venezuela): population education (UNESCO population-education guides reworked) woven into story form — Tío Conejo tales, grandparents' memories and the child's own school and street carry population, housing, landscape and weather-sign content.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 8 G8 English): a four-unit arc — Solar System, Earth's uniqueness, then regional geography taught as six South Asian "landscapes" (mountain, valley, coastal, rural, industrial-urban, desert), closing with Sri Lanka's own 1:50,000 topographic-sheet system (92 sheets) that Grade 9 continues.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka G12 Tamil SB Part I): the old A/L course opens with a full astronomy block (universe, solar system, origin theories, telescopes, the space age) before turning to the Earth, then teaches geography as man-and-environment study in the Brunhes / Vidal de la Blache tradition (the bibliography names them), and closes with map interpretation on a Sri Lanka topographic sheet.
