# Small Bodies and the Morning Star

## Do it first

Three smaller kinds of body travel the solar system. Sort them as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Comets, meteors and asteroids

A comet is a solid body with its own orbit, trailing a shining tail of gas. The most famous, Halley's comet, comes back every 76 years.

A meteor is the streak you call a shooting star: a small body burning up as it falls through the air around the Earth. If a piece survives and reaches the ground, its name changes — it is a meteorite.

Asteroids — also called planetoids — are rocky bodies gathered mostly between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. They may be the pieces of a planet that broke apart long ago.

## Sub lesson 2 — Case study: the morning star is not a star

This is a story to listen to, not a list to learn. Kirubel, aged 13, was travelling from Gondar to Addis Ababa with his father, before dawn. In the dark sky he saw one brilliant body shining, and asked his father what it was.

His father answered: the morning star. But a teacher travelling with them corrected him gently: it is not a true star at all. It is a planet — Venus, the planet nearest to the Earth — shining by reflected sunlight, bright enough to be seen even as dawn comes.

So judge the father's answer for yourself: the name "morning star" is what people call it, but the thing itself is a planet. Have you ever seen it, before sunrise? Many people have, without knowing what it was.
