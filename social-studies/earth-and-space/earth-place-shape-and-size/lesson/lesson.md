## Sub lesson 1 — Earth's place

Earth sits in the Milky Way. It is the third planet from the Sun, between Venus on the inside and Mars on the outside. And it does not travel in a circle: its orbit is an ellipse, a stretched circle.

## Sub lesson 2 — Earth's shape, and the proof

Long ago, most people believed the Earth was flat, with an edge you could fall from. Greek thinkers argued it must be a ball. Argument is not proof — the proof came by ship.

In 1519–1522, Magellan's expedition sailed from Spain, kept going in the same general direction, and the surviving crew came back to Spain from the other side. You cannot sail right round a flat disc and arrive home from behind. The Earth is a ball.

Here is a second proof you can test anywhere: climb higher, and you see farther — the visible horizon grows with height. On a flat Earth, height would not widen the edge in that way.

The true shape has one refinement: Earth is an oblate spheroid — a ball slightly flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator. Not a perfect ball, but very nearly one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Earth's size in numbers

Hold these numbers. Total surface: 510.7 million km². Of that surface, 71% is water and 29% is land — you live on the smaller part.

Now the two diameters, and be careful which is which. A diameter is a straight line through the centre. The polar diameter — pole to pole — is 12,714 km. The equatorial diameter — across the equator — is 12,756 km.

The equator is fatter by 42 km. That small difference is exactly the oblate bulge from Sub lesson 2, measured.
