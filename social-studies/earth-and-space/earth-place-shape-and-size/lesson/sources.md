Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 7 geo-1as-m): The student book of the course specified in Book 1 (the teacher guide): the same three units and 13 lessons, each lesson opening with expected outcomes and key concepts and closing with a concepts list per unit. General topics are worked with Mauritanian examples throughout, as the guide prescribes.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 7 Geography (Dari)): the Dari MoE pattern — the world topic is taught first in the abstract (sphere, equator, latitude), then re-taught as Afghanistan's own geography (its position, relief, six climate regions, river basins, lakes, minerals, fuels), then widened to the continent (Asia). Every lesson ends with in-class group activity, questions and an out-of-class task naming the pupil's own locality.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 4 Social Studies (Dari)): primary social studies starts from the child's own world — family, village, city — and only then opens the map: directions, Afghanistan's boundaries and mountains and rivers, the continents and oceans, and finally the Earth's shape, equator, latitude/longitude and movements.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 3 G3 Social Studies): a combined Social Education book that starts from the child's own family, mosque and school, then widens to the neighbourhood/village and finally to phenomena around the child (directions, the Earth, seasons, time) — Geography enters last, through daily life.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 5 LY G5 Social Studies): Half the book is Islamic history; the geography half ("Principles of General Geography") is pure foundations — solar system, Earth's shape/movements, grid, compass/GPS, land-and-water, map scale and symbols — with the symbol lesson taught by reading Libya's own mineral, wind and road distribution maps. Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, Islamic History — the Arabs before Islam (printed pp. 11–12), the Emergence of Islam (pp. 13–20), the Rashidun Caliphs (pp. 21–30), the Umayyad State (pp. 31–35), the Abbasid State (pp. 36–40), the Fatimid State (pp. 41–43), the Ayyubid State (pp. 44–45), the Crusades on the Arab Homeland (pp. 46–47), the Mamluks repelling the Mongols and Crusaders (pp. 48–51).

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 10 LY G10 Principles): Secondary geography opens as a two-part systematic course — physical geography first (astronomy, surface-shaping factors, climate, vegetation and wild animals), then human geography (population and settlement, economic resources) — and closes with a whole book on spatial-data technology: remote sensing, GPS and GIS, then the history of maps from Babylon and Ibn Hawqal.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 5 G5 Geography): first Geography year built as a single Earth-systems arc — planet, envelopes (lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere/biosphere), human society, then a closing "Protecting the Earth" chapter; every paragraph opens with "you will be able to" outcomes and chapters close with evaluation tests, plus two practical applications (map work; a week-long weather calendar).

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 4 Student Book): widening-circles design — Central America — Honduras as 'heart of Central America', then the isthmus: its land, peoples, resources, trade, population and tourism. Every lesson runs the same 'Sembrar y cosechar' activity cycle.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 4 Teacher Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): widening-circles design — Central America — Honduras as 'heart of Central America', then the isthmus: its land, peoples, resources, trade, population and tourism — the teacher guide mirrors the student book lesson-for-lesson (same 38 lessons and blocks) and adds didactics, theory summaries and answer keys; its geography lessons are placed here at the guide's own printed pages. Every lesson runs the same 'Sembrar y cosechar' activity cycle. That evidence is stocked here as the way Honduras teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Honduras is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 6 np-g6): Grade 6 has a full "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth) unit — Earth's introduction, latitude/longitude, outer-layer structure, then Nepal itself: geographical description, climate, rivers/glaciers/lakes, natural vegetation, natural resources and map study, closing with disaster management; a population unit follows (demography, the census). Economic activities are organised by region and resource: agriculture and industry, services and trade, tourism, and economic resources (land and water; forest, minerals and people).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 8 np-g8): this is the old kingdom-era standalone Geography volume — Nepal is taught through its 14 zones (anchal), with full regional accounts of Bagmati, Narayani, Mechi and Mahakali zones (position, environment, resources, development, chief towns, each with maps); Asia is the one continent studied in depth; world economic geography is built on "environment and man" (geographical control). Chapters marked ** in the contents (Earth's surface/interior; the population chapter) were prescribed only for certain school types.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 1): the second cycle opens with the discipline itself — geographic thought, its history from Greek antiquity to the determinist/possibilist schools, then cosmography (celestial sphere, solar system, Earth's shape and revolution) before any regional content.

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 6 laos-g06-geography-teacher): a world-foundations year taught zone by zone — after Earth, maps, movements, atmosphere and waters, the last five lessons walk through how people live in each climate zone (hot, desert, savanna, temperate, cold); closes with a teaching-techniques section (printed p. 66) and a Q&A additional-knowledge appendix (printed p. 75), not placed separately.

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 6 laos-g06-geography-student): the student twin of Book 2 — same 9 parts / 16 lessons with full illustrated text, a reading passage (printed p. 90) and a glossary of terms (printed p. 95), not placed separately.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 7 Social Studies): at intermediate level Social Studies becomes the history of ancient civilisations, and every civilisation is opened through its land — relief, climate, soil and rivers are taught as the stage on which each civilisation arose, starting with Iraq's own four relief regions.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 3 Estudios Sociales G3): The first formal Geography course in the set: Units I–II teach what Geography is, map types, scale, orientation and the Earth, then every later unit works at three nested scales — comunidad, municipio, departamento — ending with Nicaragua's three regions, its volcanic chain and a Köppen climate map of the country. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI municipality origins, customs and citizen participation (printed pp. 72–80, History/Civics, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII first settlers' history and culture (printed pp. 82–93, History); Unit VIII historical celebrations and heroes (printed pp. 94–109, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 5 Estudios Sociales G5): The scale widens from Nicaragua to the whole Central American isthmus: Units I–IV give the planet and map skills, then Central America's position, relief, climate, hydrography, resources, population and economies, with Nicaragua's place in the isthmus as an explicit topic; the second half is Central American History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit V first settlers, Maya and Aztec history (printed pp. 70–90, History, except the two topics placed below); Unit VI European presence (printed pp. 92–111, History); Unit VII independence process (printed pp. 114–140, History); Unit VIII Central America in the 20th century (printed pp. 142–154, History).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 5 Social Studies): Grade 5 spirals from the Earth and lat/long straight into Bhutan itself — its location, three physical zones, neighbours, major rivers and protected areas — so world geography is anchored in the home country's map first.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 6 Social Studies): Grade 6 turns to Bhutan's own climate zones, population and signature environment topics — hydropower, urbanisation and glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF) — a hazard set specific to a Himalayan kingdom.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 9 Geography): The Intermediate level (IX–X) treats each world topic at full textbook depth and keeps the Bhutan case inside it — Soils of Bhutan, agro-ecological zones and farming practices of Bhutan, and common disasters in Bhutan (including GLOF) alongside the world disaster survey.
