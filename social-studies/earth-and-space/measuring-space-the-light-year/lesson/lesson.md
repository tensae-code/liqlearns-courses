# Measuring Space — the Light-Year

## Do it first

Try it first: the nearest star is about 40,000,000,000,000 km away. Say that number out loud. Count its zeros. Earthly units — kilometres — are simply too small for space; the numbers become useless.

## Why kilometres fail, and the staircase up

So astronomers use the light-year. Light is the fastest thing there is: it travels 300,000 km in one second. Now climb the staircase, multiplying step by step:

One second: 300,000 km. Times 60: one minute. Times 60 again: one hour. Times 24: one day. Times 365.25: one year.

The top of the staircase is the light-year: 9,467,280,000,000 km — the distance light travels in a whole year. When you hear "4.3 light-years", you now know the size of the ruler being used.
