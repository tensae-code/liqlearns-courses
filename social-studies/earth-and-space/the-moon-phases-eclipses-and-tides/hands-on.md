# Hands-on — The Moon Phases Eclipses And Tides

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The book's topic
Work through this lesson's topic with your hands: Part Two, Chapter 3: the Moon is Earth's only satellite, orbiting in an elliptical path once in about 27.32 days — the same time it takes to spin — so one face always fronts the Earth and 41% of its surface stays forever hidden, its visible face pitted with circular craters from meteorite strikes; as it circles, its lit shape changes through the phases: new moon, first crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full moon, waning gibbous, last quarter and last crescent; Part Two, Chapter 3: eclipses and tides — a lunar eclipse comes when the Earth stands between the Sun and the Moon, total in Earth's full shadow and partial in the half-shadow; a solar eclipse when the Moon stands between the Sun and the Earth and blocks its light, total in the full shadow, partial in the half-shadow (a partial solar eclipse was seen in Kuwait in March 2006, set as a research task), and annular when a ring of the Sun's light shows around the Moon; and the ebb and flow of the tides are two opposite natural phenomena raised by the differing pull of the Sun's and Moon's gravity and their positions against the Earth. Say each part out loud as you do it, from the lesson.
