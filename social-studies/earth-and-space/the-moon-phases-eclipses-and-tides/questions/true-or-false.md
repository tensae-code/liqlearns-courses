# The Moon Phases Eclipses And Tides — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This lesson's first part teaches: Part Two, Chapter 3: the Moon is Earth's only satellite, orbiting in an elliptical path once in about 27.32 days — the same time it takes to spin — so one face always fronts the Earth and 41% of its surface stays forever hidden, its visible face pitted with circular craters from meteorite strikes; as it circles, its lit shape changes through the phases: new moon, first crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full moon, waning gibbous, last quarter and last crescent; Part Two, Chapter 3: eclipses and tides — a lunar eclipse comes when the Earth stands between the Sun and the Moon, total in Earth's full shadow and partial in the half-shadow; a solar eclipse when the Moon stands between the Sun and the Earth and blocks its light, total in the full shadow, partial in the half-shadow (a partial solar eclipse was seen in Kuwait in March 2006, set as a research task), and annular when a ring of the Sun's light shows around the Moon; and the ebb and flow of the tides are two opposite natural phenomena raised by the differing pull of the Sun's and Moon's gravity and their positions against the Earth.
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** This lesson stands on a teacher guide only, with no student book.
**Answer: False — it stands on Kuwait's student book, named in lesson/sources.md.**
