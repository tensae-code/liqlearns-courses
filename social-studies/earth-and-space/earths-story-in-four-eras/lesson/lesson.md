# The Earth's Story in Four Eras

## Do it first

How old is the ground under your feet, and how do we know its story? The evidence is fossils — the remains of living things locked in rock. Read by fossils, Earth's history divides into four eras. Walk them in order.

## Four eras, one long story

Era 1 — Pre-Cambrian (4.6 billion to 600 million years ago). This one era is about 90% of Earth's whole story. In it, the solar system forms, and the Moon forms.

Era 2 — Palaeozoic (600 to 225 million years ago). The northern continents separate, a great southern land — Gondwanaland — drifts south, and the continents join into one supercontinent: Pangaea.

Era 3 — Mesozoic (225 to 70 million years ago). Pangaea splits fully apart. The Atlantic and Indian oceans open between the pieces.

Era 4 — Cenozoic (70 million years ago to now). The continents drift to today's places: Greenland and North America part, Africa moves north away from Antarctica, and the Americas join at Panama. The map you know is this era's work — and the drift has not stopped.
