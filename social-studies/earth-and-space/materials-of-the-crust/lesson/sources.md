Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 10 mk-g10): the gymnasium course restarts Geography as a systems science — Theme 1 works through the four spheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) lesson by lesson, then natural disasters by sphere; Theme 2 turns to people in geographical space (world urbanisation, energy, farming, industry, transport and trade, tourism, environmental impact); Theme 3 treats the world as one global system (globalisation, transport corridors and sea straits/canals, global networks and navigation systems, international organisations, global challenges and climate-change solutions). 43 numbered lessons.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 9 Geografija 9): Grade 9 turns home — the whole book is Slovenia itself: first the country as a system (position and borders, relief and rocks, climate/vegetation/waters, settlement, population, settlements, economy, transport, environment), then its five natural regions one by one (Alpine, Pre-Alpine with the Ljubljana basin, Dinaric-Karst, Mediterranean, Pannonian). Named, not placed: the EU unit's history of the European Union and Slovenia-in-EU pros/cons (printed p. 7, Civics/History) and the state symbols inside the opening unit (p. 1, Civics).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Địa lí 10): a single Grade 10 course that runs the whole discipline — map craft, the Earth's spheres, then economic geography sector by sector — with Vietnam itself as the standing worked example (its power-plant and rice maps, the Red River's regime at Hanoi, Hanoi's climate chart, Vietnam's GDP structure, its internet and tourism figures) inside world-scale treatment.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 10 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 10 Geography is a population course — population pyramids in depth, world physical geography's effects on settlement, density and economic activity, then PNG-only migration (types, internal migration, routes and trends) and PNG's land-mobilisation questions (undeveloped land, Special Agricultural Business Leases, land grabbing). That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grades 11–12 Geography TG OBE) (teacher guide — evidence): an outcomes-based guide — it prescribes no lesson scripts; for each syllabus unit it lists suggested learning activities and assessment tasks built around the local environment (soil profiles, local hazard sites, a PNG mine and logging case study, a transect along latitude 6°S), with fieldwork and geographical inquiry as the core skills. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 6 Geography 6): world physical Geography taught through Pakistan's own land — its fault lines and 1935/2005 earthquakes, Khewra salt and Thar coal among the rocks, K2 and the Karakoram among the mountains, Pakistan's four climatic regions, its six forest types from mangrove to alpine, and the Indus flood as the country's defining disaster.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 6 LY G6 Social Studies): The geography half is "Principles of Physical Geography" in four units — surface shaping, climate, water, pollution — with Libyan examples inside world content (the Haruj volcanic mountains, Libyan springs such as Ain al-Sharshara). Non-Geography part named, not placed: Topic One, History of Libya — the Ottoman State and Libya as an Ottoman province (printed pp. 12–18), Libya in the Ottoman era 1551–1912 including the Karamanli period (pp. 19–35), modern European colonialism and the Italian occupation of 1911 (pp. 36–43), Libyan resistance to the Italian occupation (pp. 44–54), Libya after the Italian era — liberation, the British/French military administrations, independence and the Libyan Kingdom (pp. 55–64).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 3): a systematic geomorphology course — rocks and Earth's interior first, then isostasy, continental drift and plate tectonics, then internal agents (folds, faults, earthquakes, volcanoes in four lessons), then external agents and the modelled landscapes of hot, temperate, cold and coastal environments, closing with the geological cycle.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G9 Geography T1): From Grade 9 Geography stands alone as its own book: the Earth's rock and water spheres first, then the geography of the Arab homeland — its natural features, its human features and its challenges.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G9 ES T1): Earth Science at Grade 9 is minerals and water, anchored in Jordan — the enrichment case is potash drilling in the Dead Sea.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G10 ES T1): Grade 10 Earth Science is rocks first (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic — with rock wool as the applied enrichment), then the stars.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G10 ES T1 Teacher Guide): the guide walks the teacher through the student book and the activities book together — overviews of both, then lesson-by-lesson teaching notes, answers and enrichment for Rocks and Stars.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 11 Physical Geography): a pure physical-geography course in six chapters — landforms, weather and climate, water, the biosphere, soil, and the environment — with Iraqi anchors kept throughout (flood visits to Iraq's rivers, the hills of northern Iraq, groundwater and the desert caravans, soil salinity in the alluvial plain).

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 7 Geography): From Class VII Geography becomes a standalone subject organised in three strands (Time and Space; Physical Environment; People and Environment), every world topic closed with its Bhutan case — climate of Bhutan, vegetation types of Bhutan, minerals of Bhutan, and Bhutan's Himalayan disaster set (earthquake, flood, windstorm, fire, GLOF).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Geography student): grade-10 Geography is a methods-first course — it opens with the science's history, branches and GIS/GPS/aerospace methods, works through the lithosphere with a full natural-disasters lesson, and closes with resources measured by endowment; Armenia's own rivers fill the hydrology tables and Armenia's place in modern geopolitics closes the political-map theme.
