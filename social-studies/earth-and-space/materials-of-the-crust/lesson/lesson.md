## Sub lesson 1 — Eight elements, and what a mineral is

Over 90 natural elements exist, but just eight of them make over 98% of the crust. In decreasing order: oxygen, silicon, aluminium, iron, calcium, sodium, potassium, magnesium. Learn the first three at least: oxygen, silicon, aluminium lead the list.

Elements join into minerals. A mineral is an inorganic solid with a specific internal structure and a definite chemical composition — the same recipe every time. Minerals are the building blocks of rocks. The major rock-forming mineral groups are six: silicates, oxides, carbonates, sulfides, sulfates and chlorides.

## Sub lesson 2 — Three rock families

Rocks are classed by how they were born, into three families.

Igneous rocks are born from molten rock cooling. If the melt cools inside the Earth, the rock is intrusive (plutonic). If lava cools on the surface, the rock is extrusive (volcanic). Same fire, two addresses.

Sedimentary rocks are born from settling: sediments are transported, deposited and settled in layers, then harden into rock.

Metamorphic rocks are the changed rocks: a rock that already existed, transformed by heat and pressure into a new kind of rock.

Field task from the guide: collect rock samples around your school compound, and report what each one is used for locally. A rock in the hand teaches faster than a rock in a list.
