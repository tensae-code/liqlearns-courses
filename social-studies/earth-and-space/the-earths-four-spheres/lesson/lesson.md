# The Earth's Four Spheres

## Do it first

This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only. It takes the surface features you already know — land, water, living things, air — and frames them as one integrated system of four spheres. Sort first: a rock? A river? A goat? The wind? Each belongs to a sphere.

## One system, four spheres

The lithosphere is the land sphere: rock and soil, the solid surface. The hydrosphere is the water sphere: oceans, lakes, rivers, all Earth's water. The biosphere is the living sphere: every plant, animal and person. The atmosphere is the air sphere: the blanket of gases around the Earth.

The point of the frame is the arrows between the boxes. The spheres are in constant interaction: rain (atmosphere) fills rivers (hydrosphere), rivers wear the land (lithosphere), and living things (biosphere) drink, breathe and build in all three. Change one sphere, and the others feel it in every environment.

## Say it to your companion

Explain the earth's four spheres to your companion in your own words: what it is, the one figure or fact you would never want to forget from it, and one example from your own surroundings. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
