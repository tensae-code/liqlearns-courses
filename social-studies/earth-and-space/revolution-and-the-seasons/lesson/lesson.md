# Revolution and the Seasons

## Do it first

Do it first: in your mind, stand Earth at four places on its orbit — three months apart. The axis leans the same way at all four. At which place does the Sun stand directly overhead at the northern tropic, Cancer? At which at the southern tropic, Capricorn? Work it out before reading on.

## The Sun's journey, the year's seasons

Revolution has three named results. First: the Sun appears to travel between the tropics — overhead at Cancer at one extreme of the year, overhead at the equator twice, overhead at Capricorn at the other extreme. Second: day length varies through the year — longer days in the hemisphere leaning towards the Sun, shorter in the one leaning away. Third: the seasons alternate between the hemispheres: when the north leans in, it has its warm season while the south has its cold one, and half a year later they swap.

Now fill the year in blocks of three months: Sene–Nehasse, Meskerem–Hedar, Tahsas–Yekatit, Megabit–Ginbot. For each block, name each hemisphere's season and compare day and night length. One warning from the books: in the seasons diagram, do not misread the dark half — darkness in the drawing marks night, not a season. Seasons come from the lean, not from the shading.
