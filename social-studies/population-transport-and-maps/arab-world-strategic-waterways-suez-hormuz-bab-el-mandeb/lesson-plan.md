# Lesson plan — Arab World Strategic Waterways Suez Hormuz Bab El Mandeb

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Arab World Strategic Waterways Suez Hormuz Bab El Mandeb
**Place in the curriculum:** Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md) — built in the Geography expansion (Kuwait, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 2 Lesson 4: the Arab world's geographic location as a hidden resource — the strategic waterways of Gibraltar, Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb and the Suez Canal ease world trade and bring income to the states overlooking them; Unit 3 Lesson 2, Political Problems: Ceuta and Melilla are Moroccan cities still held by Spain because of their economic resources and their strategic position near the Strait of Gibraltar; Morocco seeks their return through diplomatic dialogue and international law; Unit 3 Lesson 1: the Islamic world overlooks the Pacific, Atlantic and Indian Oceans and the Mediterranean, Red Sea, Caspian Sea and Arab Gulf, and controls the waterways of Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, Gibraltar and the Suez Canal — as Kuwait's book teaches it (see lesson/sources.md for the book and printed page).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the lesson itself — Unit 2 Lesson 4: the Arab world's geographic location as a hidden resource — the strategic waterways of Gibraltar, Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb and the Suez Canal ease world trade and bring income to the states overlooking them; Unit 3 Lesson 2, Political Problems: Ceuta and Melilla are Moroccan cities still held by Spain because of their economic resources and their strategic position near the Strait of Gibraltar; Morocco seeks their return through diplomatic dialogue and international law; Unit 3 Lesson 1: the Islamic world overlooks the Pacific, Atlantic and Indian Oceans and the Mediterranean, Red Sea, Caspian Sea and Arab Gulf, and controls the waterways of Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, Gibraltar and the Suez Canal.
