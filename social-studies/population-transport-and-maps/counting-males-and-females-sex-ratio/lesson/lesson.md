# Counting males and females — sex ratio

## Do it first

Before you read, count roughly in your own class or street: are there more males or more females? A population's answer to that simple question changes its marriages, its work and its future — the lesson gives it a measure.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ratio itself

The sex ratio compares the sexes in a population. The book's formula frames it as females per males: count the females, count the males, and set one against the other. A ratio near balance means the two counts are close; a tilted ratio tells a story of births, deaths or migration.

## Sub lesson 2 — Africa's sub-regions compared

The book computes the sex structure region by region across Africa's sub-regions and compares them. The point of the comparison is the geographer's habit: a measure means little alone and much beside its neighbours — where the ratio tilts, ask what moved it.
