# GPS field capture; enlarging and reducing maps

## Do it first

Your phone or a handset can fix your exact position from satellites. And a map of your school could be drawn twice as big without changing the ground it shows. Two skills — capturing position, and changing a map's size honestly. This lesson teaches both, from the syllabus that requires them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Representing spatial data using GPS technology (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 syllabus requires learners to **represent spatial data using GPS technology**: going into the field with a GPS receiver, fixing positions as coordinates, and bringing those positions back as data a map or GIS can use. The guide's Unit 8 framing names **GPS and digitisers as the two most important collection tools**, with outputs as numeric values, maps, graphs, charts and diagrams.

In the field the method is simple to state: walk to the feature — a school gate, a well, a boundary corner — let the receiver settle, and record its coordinates with a note of what the point is. Point by point, a boundary or a route becomes a data set. (This part rests on teacher-guide evidence: the syllabus in the guide's back matter.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Enlarging and reducing maps — including with GIS (teacher guide — evidence)

The syllabus also requires **map enlargement and reduction skills** — **enlarge and reduce maps using GIS**, and **make an administration map using GIS**. Enlarging makes a small map bigger so detail can be added; reducing fits a large map onto smaller paper. In both, the ground does not change — the **scale changes**, and the scale bar and scale statement must be redrawn to match the new size, or the map lies.

The honest rule of the skill: a photocopied or GIS-rescaled map keeps its truth only if its scale is recomputed with it. Double the map's size and every distance on paper doubles — the scale bar must double too. (Teacher-guide evidence throughout: syllabus competencies, guide printed pp. 181–182.)

## Say it to your companion

## Do It Yourself

1. List five features around your school you would capture with GPS, in walking order.
2. Draw a small map, then redraw it at twice the size with a correctly doubled scale bar.
3. Write two sentences on why a resized map without a corrected scale bar is a false map.
