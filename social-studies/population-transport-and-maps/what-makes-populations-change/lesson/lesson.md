# What makes populations change

## Do it first

Before you read, think of your own kebele ten years ago and now. What made its numbers move — babies born, people lost, people arriving or leaving? Sort your answer into three flows. The lesson names exactly those three.

## Sub lesson 1 — Births, deaths and migration

Population change is the difference in size over a period of time. Three flows drive it: births add, deaths subtract, and migration does both — in-migration adds, out-migration subtracts. Every population story, from a village to the world, is those three flows in different strengths.

## Sub lesson 2 — How the flows turned — medicine and living standards

The modern turn came from the death flow: medicine and living-standard gains cut infant, child and maternal mortality and raised life expectancy, while births stayed high — so populations grew fast. Change also reshapes composition, not only size: sex ratios and life expectancy are part of the same account.

[GIF 21971 — the tank's level rising as the deaths tap closes while births keep flowing]

## Sub lesson 3 — Shocks in the record

The key-term frame names the historical shocks: colonisation, the slave trade and deadly diseases all cut or moved populations by force. A geographer reading today's composition reads those shocks in it still — change is never only arithmetic.
