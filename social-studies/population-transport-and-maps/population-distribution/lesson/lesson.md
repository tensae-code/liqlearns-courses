# Population — distribution

## Do it first

Picture the world at night from space: bright knots of light, and vast dark lands. People are not spread like butter on bread — they crowd into a few places and leave most land nearly empty. Your job in this lesson: find the pattern, then find its reasons.

## Sub lesson 1 — An uneven world

Here is how uneven it is: **two-thirds of humanity crowds onto one-seventh of the land**. And **90% of people live in the northern hemisphere** — only 10% in the southern.

[Picture 11083 — a dot-density world map, crowded seventh and empty spaces]

Why is the north so heavy? Look at the continents map: most of the world's land itself lies north of the equator — and on that land, some places pull people in and some push them away. The **natural factors** include water, fertile soil, gentle climate and low, workable land. The **human factors** include farming, industry, trade, towns and history.

The teacher guide gives the pattern its formal frame (teacher guide — evidence): distribution is uneven because of **physical/ecological, socio-economic, political and demographic factors**; the densely populated areas are named as the **Asiatic population belt, Europe and the American cluster**; the sparsely populated as **tundra, hot-dry lands, hot-wet lands, high relief and poor-soil areas**. Ethiopia's own concentration in the **highlands** is keyed to altitude, climate and soil — moderate temperatures, rich soil, adequate rainfall.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why people settle where they do

Put the factors to work. A river valley with rich soil and kind rain fills with villages. A hot-dry land with no water stays nearly empty, however wide it is. A cold tundra, a steep high-relief slope, a poor-soil plain — each tells the same story from the other side.

Now read your own area with these eyes: which factors pulled people to your town or village — and which empty places near you stay empty for the mirror-image reasons?

## Sub lesson 3 — Moving and settling (teacher guide — evidence)

People also move (teacher guide — evidence). **Migration** is a **permanent change of residence** — internal (inside one country) or external/international. It is driven by **push factors** that force people out — no work, no land, drought, conflict — and **pull factors** that attract them — jobs, services, safety, family. Mere **movement** is different: any relocation of goods or people over any distance, with no permanent change of home. What migration does at the destination is two-sided: cheap and skilled labour and cultural diversity on one side, pressure and resource degradation on the other.

Settlements themselves come in two kinds. **Rural** settlements: unplanned building, agricultural and primary work. **Urban** settlements: planned building, non-agricultural work — secondary, tertiary and quaternary activities. People prefer cities, the guide keys, for jobs, infrastructure, education, services and transport.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two fractions that show how uneven the world is, one natural and one human factor, and one push and one pull factor from your own area's story.
