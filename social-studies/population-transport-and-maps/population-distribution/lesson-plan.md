# Lesson plan — Population — distribution

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Population — distribution
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** where people live and why — an uneven world, the factors that shape it, and (from the teacher guide) migration, push and pull, and rural and urban settlement
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the growth lessons before it in this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **An uneven world** — two-thirds of humanity on one-seventh of the land; 90% in the northern hemisphere — and the natural and human factors behind the pattern (teacher guide — evidence: the formal factor frame and the named dense and sparse areas).
2. **Why people settle where they do** — the factor story in the learner's own words — land, water, climate, work and history pulling people together or leaving land empty.
3. **Moving and settling (teacher guide — evidence)** — migration as a permanent change of residence, push and pull factors, and rural against urban settlement.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State how uneven world distribution is, in the book's own fractions.
2. Name natural and human factors that shape distribution.
3. Name the guide's dense clusters and sparse land types (teacher guide — evidence).
4. Explain migration with push and pull factors, and tell rural from urban settlement (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.
