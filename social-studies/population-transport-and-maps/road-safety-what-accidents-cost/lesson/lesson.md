# Road safety — what accidents cost

## Do it first

An accident is over in a second. Its costs are not. Before you read the numbers, list everything one bad accident can take from one family. Then compare your list with the book's.

## The world's and Ethiopia's toll

Worldwide, traffic accidents kill about **1,300,000 people a year** and injure **20–50 million**.

[Picture 11089 — the Ethiopian study's age chart, the student bar highlighted]

An Ethiopian university study (from the Gondar and Debre Tabor medical schools) found the victims concentrated at **ages 20–29 (33.01%)** and **30–39 (26.2%)**, mostly **urban (54.4%)** — and **students the largest occupational group (30.1%)**. Why are students so exposed? They walk the most, at the busiest hours, on the most crowded urban roads — exposure is the answer the numbers point to.

## What one accident takes

The harms run far past the crash: **death, disability, psychological crisis, property destruction, family breakup and lost income, heavy medical costs, and poverty** at the end of the chain.

The teacher guide itemises the economic bill the same way (teacher guide — evidence): treatment costs, lost productivity and working time of victims **and relatives**, loss of skilled labour, loss of school hours, increased dependency and poverty, and permanent disability.

So the duties divide four ways — **government** (roads, laws, enforcement), **the public** (care and example), **students** (the five rules, the crossings), and **drivers** (speed and attention). Everyone at the road owns a piece of its safety.
