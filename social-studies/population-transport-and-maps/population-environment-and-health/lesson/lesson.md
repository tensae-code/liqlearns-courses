# Population, environment and health

## Do it first

A town's water makes children sick. Write two responses: one that treats the sick, one that stops the sickness. You have just stated this lesson's two models.

## Sub lesson 1 — The burden, counted

Environmental health covers the aspects of human health and disease determined by the environment. The burden is large: environmental degradation carries an estimated 174–234 million-scale share of the global disease burden, as the book reports it, and unsafe water and poor sanitation drive 58% of diarrhoeal disease in low- and middle-income countries.

Emerging zoonoses — SARS, Marburg and their kin — link ecosystem health to pandemic risk: damage the environment animals live in, and new diseases cross to people. The harms fall hardest on the poor, the young, the elderly and women.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two models of defence

Two models defend health (the book's Figure 4.4). The clinical model treats disease after it appears — the clinic, the medicine, the cure. The public health model controls disease-causing agents and environmental hazards before illness — clean water, sanitation, safe workplaces.

The lesson's arithmetic favours prevention: clean air and water, sanitation, green space and safe workplaces cut mortality and raise productivity together. Treat after, or prevent before — geography argues for before.
