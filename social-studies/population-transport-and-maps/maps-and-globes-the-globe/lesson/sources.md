Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 5): Three geography units climb from the planet to the country: general geography and the American continent's location, then how climate varies across the continents — with climographs the children draw and read themselves — then the Dominican Republic's own climate, activities and population, ending with a “discovering the geography of my country” field activity.

Eritrea's own way (ER G4 Social Studies): the Tigrinya-medium primary book teaches Geography through the child's own surroundings first — our environment, protecting our soil, water, plants and animals, then maps and globes, community life, and the shapes of the land.

India's own way (Grade 6 The Earth Our Habitat): The first Geography year goes planet → globe → motions → maps → Earth's domains → landforms, and only then turns home: the last two chapters are India itself (location, neighbours, physical divisions, monsoon climate, vegetation and wildlife), so world basics are immediately applied to the home country.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 6 Social Studies): the primary Social Studies spiral ends with its first real map work — maps, the globe and the equator/parallels/meridians grid taught inside the “society today” chapter, anchored on a world map with Cambodia marked.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 5 G5 Geography): first Geography year built as a single Earth-systems arc — planet, envelopes (lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere/biosphere), human society, then a closing "Protecting the Earth" chapter; every paragraph opens with "you will be able to" outcomes and chapters close with evaluation tests, plus two practical applications (map work; a week-long weather calendar).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 5 np-g5): Grade 5 "Our Earth" is a first full geography of Nepal — physical features, climate and lifestyle, natural vegetation, soils, map-drawing with symbols, globe and maps, and the wider South Asian neighbours; economic geography is district-based (Sankhuwasabha's occupations mapped place by place, from yak and sheep rearing and herb collecting in the high Himalaya to paddy in the low valleys).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 1): distance-learning self-study module — every lesson follows the same rhythm (introduction, development with worked examples, exercises, answer key, lesson summary); the four Earth spheres are the organising spine of the whole module, each sphere closed by a "use, protection and conservation" lesson; local anchors throughout (Maputo temperature worked example, Zambezi delta, Moma/Namialo weather contrast).

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 6 laos-g06-geography-teacher): a world-foundations year taught zone by zone — after Earth, maps, movements, atmosphere and waters, the last five lessons walk through how people live in each climate zone (hot, desert, savanna, temperate, cold); closes with a teaching-techniques section (printed p. 66) and a Q&A additional-knowledge appendix (printed p. 75), not placed separately.

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 6 laos-g06-geography-student): the student twin of Book 2 — same 9 parts / 16 lessons with full illustrated text, a reading passage (printed p. 90) and a glossary of terms (printed p. 95), not placed separately.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 3 Estudios Sociales G3): The first formal Geography course in the set: Units I–II teach what Geography is, map types, scale, orientation and the Earth, then every later unit works at three nested scales — comunidad, municipio, departamento — ending with Nicaragua's three regions, its volcanic chain and a Köppen climate map of the country. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI municipality origins, customs and citizen participation (printed pp. 72–80, History/Civics, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII first settlers' history and culture (printed pp. 82–93, History); Unit VIII historical celebrations and heroes (printed pp. 94–109, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 Estudios Sociales G7 full-year): A full national Geography in five units — territorial patrimony and cartography, physical geography (relief, climate, hydrography, resources), population ("¿Cuántas mujeres, cuántos hombres?"), production, and a whole unit on natural-risk prevention — followed by six units of national History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I state institutions (printed pp. 14–16, Civics); Unit VI origin societies (printed pp. 128–143, History, except the geographical-setting topic placed below); Unit VII European expansion and the Colony (printed pp. 148–165, History); Unit VIII Independence (printed pp. 170–184, History); Unit IX economic/political evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 190–205, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty and the Somoza period (printed pp. 210–233, History); Unit XI Nicaragua 1979 to the present (printed pp. 244–264, History).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 OM Geografía G5) (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher guide teaches the same four units as the programme/student book but adds Cuba-specific complementary materials no other country set carries: the Tarea Vida state climate-change plan as classroom content, project-based-learning orientation, and a long section on mastering the changing world political map through country cases (Congo, Belarus, the Yugoslav successor states). That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Cuba is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 Geografía G5): The first Geography book a Cuban child meets opens with the history of geography itself (cuneiform maps, Hecataeus), builds all map skills before any content, and ends the planet tour with world population, the world political map and world heritage — with Cuba's own places (Llanura del Cauto, Trinidad) woven in as examples throughout.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).
