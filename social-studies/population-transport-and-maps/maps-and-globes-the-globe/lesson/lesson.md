# Maps and globes — the globe

## Do it first

Hold a ball in your hands — a football will do. The Earth is shaped like it, only flattened a breath at the poles. Every map you will ever use is this ball flattened somehow. Meet the ball first: this is the globe.

## The true-shape model

A **globe** is a model of the Earth that shows the **land and water bodies in their true shapes and places** — nothing stretched, nothing torn, because it keeps the Earth's own shape.

[Picture 11090 — a globe on its tilted stand with routes drawn over it]

Its uses are four. **Seeing day and night, and the seasons** — light one side and the dark side is night. **Telling climate zones** — the bands around it. **Finding air and sea routes** — the shortest way between two places on a ball is not the straight line on flat paper. And **showing rotation**: spin it, and it turns **west to east**, the way the Earth itself turns.

[GIF 11091 — a globe spinning west to east, the day and night line moving]

That spin is not a toy trick. It is the Earth's own movement, in your hands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why a globe's shapes are true, and name its four uses.

## Do It Yourself

1. Spin a globe or ball west to east and say which lands meet the light first.
2. Shine a torch on a ball in a dark room: mark the day side and the night side.
3. Find the air route between two far cities on a globe with a piece of string.
