## Sub lesson 2 — Kuwait's way — the book's own teaching way

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T1): Grade 7 widens the lens from the Gulf (Grade 6) to the whole Arab homeland, and each unit is fronted by a guide character — the Geographer leads Unit 1, the Narrator of the Eras Unit 2, the Historian of Time Unit 3, the Witness of the Age Unit 4. Unit 1 is the Geography unit: the Arab world treated as one region — its definition and shared traits, its relative and astronomical location, its climate and climate regions, its plant and animal life, its terrain — and then Kuwait placed inside the same natural frame. Units 2–4 are a chronological History sweep (ages and civilizations; Arab-Islamic civilization; colonialism to independence), and the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 2, Kuwait and the Arab world through the ages: the rise of the eras (Ice, Stone, Bronze, Iron), printed pp. 40–42; the rise of civilizations and models of the old Arab civilizations (Pharaonic Egypt, Jarma in Libya, Babylon, Saba and its Ma'rib dam), pp. 43–44; the Nabataean civilization and Petra, pp. 45–46; the Kush civilization in Nubia and Meroë, pp. 47–48; civilization stations in Kuwait — Ubaid at Al-Subiya, Dilmun and Hellenistic Ikaros on Failaka Island, pp. 49–50. History — Unit 3, Kuwait and the Arab world in Arab-Islamic civilization: the Arab world before Islam, pp. 58–59; the appearance of Islam and its spread, pp. 60–61; Arab scholars — Maryam al-Asturlabi, Ibn al-Nafis, the navigator Ibn Majid, pp. 62–63; Ibn al-Haytham, al-Kindi, Hunayn al-Abadi, pp. 64–65; pages from Kuwait's history — Kazma in history, culture and war, pp. 66–67. History — Unit 4, between colonialism and independence: the state of the Arab world in the 18th century and European ambitions, pp. 76–77; the colonial movements and the forms and effects of colonialism, pp. 78–79; Arab resistance movements (Palestine, Libya), pp. 80–81; the independence stage in the Arab world, pp. 82–83; Kuwait from protection (1899) to independence (1961), pp. 84–85. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": generosity and giving (Sheikh Jaber "Al-Aish"), pp. 93–94; respecting the teacher, pp. 97–98; congratulations from the heart (Eid greetings), pp. 99–100. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
