# Population policies — encouraging or discouraging babies

## Do it first

Should a government try to change how many babies are born at all? Write yes or no with one reason. This lesson gives you both directions governments actually take.

## Sub lesson 1 — Direct and indirect influence

A population policy is a government's deliberate attempt to influence fertility and growth. It works directly — through access to reproductive health and family planning — or indirectly, through the contexts that shape fertility choices.

Every policy leans one of two ways: toward more births, or toward fewer. The next two parts take each direction in turn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pro-natalist and anti-natalist

Pro-natalist policy seeks to raise fertility: incentives, parental leave, workplace equality measures, and — for ageing, low-fertility societies — facilitated immigration. It raises civil-liberties questions even so; and the evidence says supportive family policy moves fertility more than explicit pro-natalism does.

Anti-natalist policy seeks to lower fertility. Its most famous case is China's one-child policy, standing at the end of a longer history of birth-control campaigns running back to the 1950s.
