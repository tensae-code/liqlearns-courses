# Drawing a watershed boundary

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: every raindrop in a valley answers to one outlet — can you draw the line that decides? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The basin's vocabulary

A drainage basin is defined through its working words: tributary (a feeder stream), confluence (where streams join), source, mouth, and the watershed or divide — the high line that separates one basin from the next.

## Sub lesson 2 — Steps one to three

The delineation method is a doing procedure. One: circle the outlet, the point where the basin drains. Two: highlight the tributary courses and their flow directions. Three: mark the high points and ridges with X's.

## Sub lesson 3 — Steps four and five

Four: draw flow arrows from the high points, showing which way water leaves them. Five: connect the high points, from the outlet all the way around and back, to trace the basin boundary. The divide you draw is the line every raindrop obeys.
