# Counting deaths — death rate and infant mortality rate

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: two numbers tell how a country treats its frailest — what are they? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The crude death rate

The crude death rate counts deaths per 1,000 mid-year population in a year. The book works an example in full, then sets pupils a fresh data set to calculate from.

## Sub lesson 2 — The infant mortality rate

The infant mortality rate counts deaths under one year of age per 1,000 live births. It is the most sensitive of the measures: where babies survive, almost everything else is working too.

## Sub lesson 3 — What decides the numbers

Mortality's determinants are listed: living standards, nutrition, medical services, hygiene and sanitation. The measures are not just counts — they are verdicts on those five things.

## Sub lesson 4 — Infant, maternal and life expectancy — Africa's set

The Grade 10 book completes the mortality set from Africa's side: infant mortality and maternal mortality rates, and life expectancy — how long a newborn can expect to live. Read together, the three tell one story: where infants and mothers survive and elders are many, health systems and living standards are working; the book sets Africa's patterns beside their causes.
