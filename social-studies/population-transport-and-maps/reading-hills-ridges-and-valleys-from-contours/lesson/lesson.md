# Reading hills, ridges and valleys from contours

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: four contour patterns, four landforms — can you read them at sight? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hills and mountains

A hill is concentric closed circles under 1,000 metres high, its summit inside the smallest ring; concave and convex hillside spacing can be distinguished in the rings. Mountains are elongated closed contours rising to the centre.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ridges

A ridge is an elliptical line of high ground. Its U- and V-shaped contours point away from the high ground — downhill, outward, like fingers spread from a spine.

## Sub lesson 3 — Valleys — the mirror

A valley is the reverse: its V contours point upstream, toward high ground, and the pattern runs parallel to its stream before crossing it. Ridge and valley are the same V read in opposite directions — the classic contour-reading test.
