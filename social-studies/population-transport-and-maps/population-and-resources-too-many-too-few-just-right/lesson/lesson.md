# Population and resources — too many, too few, just right

## Do it first

Imagine a boat. Too many people aboard and it sits low, slow, taking water. Too few and there are not enough hands to row or bail. Somewhere between is the loading at which the boat flies. People and a country's resources balance the same way — this lesson names all three states.

## Sub lesson 1 — Too many — over-population

**Over-population** is an **excess of population against available resources** — more people than the land, jobs and services can carry well.

Its marks: **low per-capita income** (the wealth divided among too many), **high unemployment and underemployment**, **malnutrition and famine**, **low living standards**, and **outward migration** — people leaving to find what home cannot give.

## Sub lesson 2 — Too few — under-population

**Under-population** is the mirror state: **too few people for the full use of resources** — land unfarmed, resources untouched, hands missing.

Its marks run opposite: **high per-capita income, low unemployment**, and **inward migration** — people arriving, drawn by the room and the work.

## Sub lesson 3 — Just right — optimum population, and the two policies

Between the two stands **optimum population**: the **balanced population–resource relationship** — enough people to use the resources fully, few enough that each person's share stays good.

[Picture 11101 — a balance scale in three states: people-heavy, balanced, resource-heavy]

Countries steer toward the balance with two policy answers. An **anti-natalist policy** works to **reduce the birth rate and slow growth** — the answer of a country pressing against its resources. A **pro-natalist policy** works to **increase the birth rate and growth** — the answer of a country with room and too few hands. Same balance, approached from opposite sides.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three states with one mark each, and which policy a crowded country and an empty country would each choose.
