# Lesson plan — Population and resources — too many, too few, just right

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Population and resources — too many, too few, just right
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the balance between people and resources — over-population, under-population, optimum population — and the two policy answers, anti-natalist and pro-natalist
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only, beside the population lessons of this title.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Too many — over-population** — an excess of people against resources, and its marks: low income per person, unemployment, malnutrition, outward migration.
2. **Too few — under-population** — too few people to use resources fully, and its opposite marks: high income per person, low unemployment, inward migration.
3. **Just right — optimum population, and the two policies** — the balanced population–resource relationship, and anti-natalist against pro-natalist policy.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Population — measuring change: rates, pyramids and structure. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define over-population, under-population and optimum population.
2. Give the marks of too many and of too few, as opposites.
3. Name the two policy answers and what each tries to do.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Imagine a boat. Too many people aboard and it sits low, slow, taking water. Too few and there are not enough hands to row or bail. Somewhere between is the loading at which the boat flies. People and a country's resources balance the same way — this lesson names all three states. (lesson/lesson.md)
