# Reading saddles, plateaus, spurs and cliffs from contours

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: a spur and a valley are mirror images — what are the other seven patterns? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saddles and depressions

A saddle is the dip between two high areas — the low pass on a ridge. A depression is closed contours with tick marks pointing inward and values falling to the centre; its man-made cousin is the roadway 'cut', ticked toward the roadbed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Plateaus, gorges, spurs

A plateau is broad, roughly rectangular closed contours — a table top in lines. A gorge is contours converging tightly along a river. A spur is U/V contours pointing away from high ground: the mirror of a valley.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cliffs, cuts and fills

A cliff is contours merging into one carrying contour — the lines themselves fall together. A 'fill' is the raised roadbed, drawn like a cut in reverse. The lesson ends as a doing test: identify numbered features on a full contour map and justify each call.
