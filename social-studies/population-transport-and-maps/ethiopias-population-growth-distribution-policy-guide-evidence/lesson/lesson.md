# Ethiopia's population — growth, distribution, policy (guide evidence)

## Do it first

Plot four points on a scrap graph: 1900, 1960, 1984, 2007 — leave the heights blank. Fill them in as you read the guide's series, and watch the curve steepen.

## Sub lesson 1 — The growth series (teacher guide — evidence)

This lesson stands on the 2010–11 teacher's guide alone; every figure is dated and era-labelled. The guide's trend series: 11.8 million in 1900; 23.5 million in 1960; 42.6 million in 1984; 73.8 million in 2007.

On that series the guide frames Ethiopia as, after Nigeria and Egypt, the third most populous country in Africa. (A separate passage in the same guide still carries 'around 63.8 million' — an un-updated layer from an earlier census round; the series, not the stray total, is what this lesson teaches.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Distribution, urbanisation, policy (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide presents urbanisation as a recent phenomenon, beginning in the late 19th century, and its review tasks ask learners to enumerate the three most and least urbanised regions and to discuss the consequences of Addis Ababa's high urbanisation rate.

Its population-policy lesson treats the meaning, importance and types of population policy, then Ethiopia's policy with its goals, objectives and strategies. All of it is 2007-census-era evidence: valuable as the old course's record, labelled with its era wherever it is used.
