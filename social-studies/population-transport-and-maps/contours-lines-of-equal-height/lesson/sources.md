Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. The student book's body numbering for Unit Two drifts from its own contents (regions printed as 2.4, factors as 2.5, local classification as 2.6, where the contents and the teacher guide's contents agree on 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5); this lesson follows the contents numbering and teaches no misprint.

Searchable tags: contours, index contour, contour interval, spot heights, ethiopia, social studies, population transport and maps

Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 9 Social Science): Secondary geography opens with a full map-skills chapter — grid references, contours and cross-sections practised on Fiji maps — before environment use and conservation, including Pacific methods (shifting cultivation, rotational grazing, appropriate fishing methods).

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 11 Geography TG SBC) (teacher guide — evidence): the standards-based senior Geography course keeps the four-unit frame of the Grade 9–10 strand (Geography Skills; People and Places; People and Resources; People and Environment) as a standalone subject — a heavy map-skills unit (grid references, bearings, cross-sections, climate graphs) before climate regions, biomass, oceanography, and the structure of the Earth. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 8 G8 English): a four-unit arc — Solar System, Earth's uniqueness, then regional geography taught as six South Asian "landscapes" (mountain, valley, coastal, rural, industrial-urban, desert), closing with Sri Lanka's own 1:50,000 topographic-sheet system (92 sheets) that Grade 9 continues.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.
