# Contours — lines of equal height

## Sub lesson 1 — What a contour is

Contours are imaginary lines joining points of equal elevation above mean sea level. They are drawn in brown at regular intervals: 20 metres on 1:50,000 sheets, 100 metres on 1:250,000 sheets.

## Sub lesson 2 — Index and intermediate lines

Every fifth contour is an index contour, drawn heavier and carrying the height value. Between the regular lines, intermediate contours can show smaller rises. On a real sheet, the index lines are your first handholds.

## Sub lesson 3 — The four properties

Contours never merge or cross, except at cliffs and waterfalls; they never branch; they are numbered uphill; and their spacing reads the slope — even spacing is a uniform slope, wide spacing gentle, close spacing steep. Contours are generated from spot heights.
