# Living in Sweden — Town and Country

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: living in Sweden: what buildings a home place has (church, school, shops), one-family and many-family houses, and town, suburb and countryside, as Sweden's Grade 1 teacher guide notes the textbook. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a home place has — church, school, shops

Sweden's Grade 1 book looks at the child's own home place: what buildings it has — a church, a school, shops. The teacher guide's notes for the textbook's pages 12–23 start settlement from the buildings a pupil already knows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Town, suburb and countryside — one-family and many-family houses

Homes differ: one-family houses and many-family houses. And places differ: town, suburb and countryside. The lesson sorts the pupil's own living place into those words.

Sweden's own way: Geography starts from the child's own surroundings — Sweden's place on the globe, the home town, Swedish nature and its animals, and sorting the household's own waste — taught with big pictures and sorting games for pupils who read and write only a little.
