# Malthus — mouths multiply, food adds

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 against 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 — who wins? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two kinds of growth

Thomas Malthus, writing in 1798, set two staircases against each other. Population grows geometrically: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Food grows arithmetically: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. On those two rules, mouths must outstrip meals.

## Sub lesson 2 — The checks

When population outruns food, Malthus said it is cut back by 'positive checks' — hunger, disease and war. Against them stand his 'preventive checks' of moral restraint: delayed marriage and fewer children, chosen before the crisis.

## Sub lesson 3 — The neo-Malthusians

Neo-Malthusians keep the imbalance but substitute a modern preventive check: family planning. The next lesson gives the reply — Marx and Boserup read the same staircase and see a different story.
