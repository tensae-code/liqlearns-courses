# The world's population explosion

## Do it first

Before you read, guess: how many people were alive in 1900, and how many in 2000? Write both guesses down. The gap between them is the explosion this lesson measures.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1.6 billion to 6.1 billion

World population entered the twentieth century at 1.6 billion and left it at 6.1 billion. For most of human history growth was slow; the twentieth century bent the curve upward so steeply that geographers call it the population explosion.

## Sub lesson 2 — A billion at a time

The adding sped up: a billion was added in 1960–75 — fifteen years — and another billion in 1975–87, only twelve years. The engine was less-developed countries, whose growth accelerated after the Second World War as death rates fell while births stayed high.

## Sub lesson 3 — The working measures

Population geographers track the explosion with a small toolset: birth and death rates, fertility, mortality, migration — and doubling time, the years a population needs to become twice as large. The next lessons take the measures one by one.
