# Video script — Maps and globes — reading a map's margins

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Someone hands you a map with no words around its edges — no title, no key, no scale. The picture is pretty and nearly useless. The margins are what turn a coloured picture into an instrument. Open any real map now and hunt its edges before you read on.

A map is read at its margins. The seven items:

1. Scale — the shrink rule, worked in the next sub lesson. 2. Title — what the map shows. 3. Key/legend — what the signs and colours mean. 4. Direction — true north against magnetic north; and the rule: a map with no direction mark has north at the top. 5. Date of making — a map ages; roads and borders change. 6. Location lines — the parallels, meridians and grids that give absolute location. 7. Publisher/copyright holder — who made it and answers for it.

Miss one and the map can mislead you: colours without a key are decoration, distances without a scale are guesses.

Scale is a division: map distance ÷ ground distance.

Work the book's example with it. If 1 cm on the map = 5 km on the ground, first put both in the same unit: 5 km = 500,000 cm. So the scale is 1 ÷ 500,000 — written 1:500,000. Every centimetre you measure on that map is half a million centimetres of real Earth.

Now the doing-task: measure your classroom — length and width, in metres — choose a scale that fits your page, and draw the classroom to that scale, margins and all: title, key, north, date, your name as maker.

Tell your companion the seven margin items, the no-mark north rule, and walk them through the 1 cm = 5 km example to 1:500,000.
