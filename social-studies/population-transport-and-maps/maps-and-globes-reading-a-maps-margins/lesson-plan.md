# Lesson plan — Maps and globes — reading a map's margins

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Maps and globes — reading a map's margins
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the seven margin items that make a map readable — scale, title, key, north, date, location lines and publisher — and how to work a scale and draw your classroom to one
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Maps and globes — kinds of maps and scale, next door in this title.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The seven margin items** — scale, title, key/legend, north (true and magnetic), date of making, location lines, publisher — what each one tells the reader.
2. **Working a scale** — the formula — map distance ÷ ground distance — the 1 cm = 5 km worked example giving 1:500,000, and drawing your measured classroom to a chosen scale.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Maps and globes — kinds of maps and scale. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the seven margin items and what each tells you.
2. State the north rule for a map with no direction mark.
3. Work the scale formula and the 1 cm = 5 km example.
4. Measure a classroom and draw it to a chosen scale.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Someone hands you a map with no words around its edges — no title, no key, no scale. The picture is pretty and nearly useless. The margins are what turn a coloured picture into an instrument. Open any real map now and hunt its edges before you read on. (lesson/lesson.md)
