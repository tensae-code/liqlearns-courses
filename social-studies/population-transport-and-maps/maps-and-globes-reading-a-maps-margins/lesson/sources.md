**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ethiopia, Colombia, Kuwait, Cuba).**

Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs and whole student topics (maps, globes, GPS) have no guide counterpart, so no guide coverage is claimed for them. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence.

Searchable tags: marginal information, map scale, formula, 1:500000, legend, key, true north, magnetic north, map title

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado3_01): Grade 3's first cartilla is the orientation cartilla — cardinal points and maps as guides, a different map for each kind of information, then Colombia's political map with its departments and capitals explored department by department.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 OM Geografía G5) (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher guide teaches the same four units as the programme/student book but adds Cuba-specific complementary materials no other country set carries: the Tarea Vida state climate-change plan as classroom content, project-based-learning orientation, and a long section on mastering the changing world political map through country cases (Congo, Belarus, the Yugoslav successor states). That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Cuba is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 5 Geografía G5): The first Geography book a Cuban child meets opens with the history of geography itself (cuneiform maps, Hecataeus), builds all map skills before any content, and ends the planet tour with world population, the world political map and world heritage — with Cuba's own places (Llanura del Cauto, Trinidad) woven in as examples throughout.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 6 Geografía de Cuba G6): The child's first geography book is the home country itself — one chapter of tools (coordinates, orientation, scale), then Cuba whole (situation, relief, coasts, climate, population, economy), then its two great regions landscape by landscape; the political-administrative division taught is the pre-2011 one (Ciudad de La Habana appears as a province), and History episodes woven into the regional chapters (the Santa Clara battle, the Granma landing) are named here but not placed — they belong to the History course.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 6 Cuaderno de actividades G6): The workbook is the practice twin of the G6 student book and the Atlas Escolar — nearly every activity is done ON a map (tracing coordinates, shading the insular shelf, locating features on outline maps) — and it is where the programme's fifth unit, "El paisaje de la localidad", actually lives: the student book has no locality chapter, but the workbook closes with sketching, describing and defending the child's own locality. Section 2 is pure tools: outline maps of the world and of Cuba plus graph paper.
