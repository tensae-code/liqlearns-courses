# The five parts of a GIS

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: a GIS is not just software — what are its other four parts? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hardware and software

Hardware splits into input (computers, GPS, scanners, digitisers) and output (printers, plotters). Software includes the ESRI family — ArcView, ArcInfo, ArcGIS — and free QGIS, with DBMS, the database management system, explained alongside.

## Sub lesson 2 — Data and people

Data is a component in its own right, the subject of the next lesson. People split into users, who ask the questions, and specialists, who build and run the system.

## Sub lesson 3 — Methods — and the warning

Methods are each organisation's designed plan and rules for using its GIS. The book's warning closes the set: the technology has limited value without the people who manage it — four parts can be bought; the fifth must be grown.

## Sub lesson 4 — The five parts in Grade 10's words

The Grade 10 book names the same five components — hardware, software, data, people and methods — and spells out the people: administrators, managers, technicians, application experts, end users and consumers. Its verdict matches the Grade 11 warning exactly: a GIS is of limited value without the people to run and apply it.
