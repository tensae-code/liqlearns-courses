# Counting movers — migration rates

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: how do you count people who arrive against people who leave? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — In and out, per thousand

The immigration rate counts arrivals per 1,000 population; the emigration rate counts departures per 1,000. Each is worked from the same base, so the two can be compared directly.

[Picture 27047 — a border gate with counters clicking in and out, and a balance needle between them]

## Sub lesson 2 — The net rate

The net migration rate is the balance of the two: immigration minus emigration. The book's case example resolves to a net rate of 2.6 per 1,000 — the place gained, on balance, between two and three people per thousand.

## Sub lesson 3 — When places shrink

Set the net rate beside the map of countries where negative natural increase and net migration combine: deaths above births and leavers above arrivers together shrink a population from both sides.
