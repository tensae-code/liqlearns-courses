Fiji's own way (Fiji Years 11–12 Geography): Senior geography splits into Human and Physical volumes; the human volume is organised as four issue-chapters (population/settlement, tourism, agriculture, marine resources), each run the same way — nature, problems, ways of minimising, contemporary issues, future — with Pacific/Fiji theme-country cases.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 10 G10 Geography of Man and the Environment): secondary geography is thematic, not regional — man and space, man representing his geographic field (remote sensing, projections, maps), man organising it (rural/urban), climate and water, the dynamic Earth, and the great environmental systems.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 11 G11 Geography of Yemen and the Arab World): Grade 11 does Yemen in depth first (physical base, population and social development, economic resources), then the Arab world's physical geography, its people and resources, and finally its contemporary problems — water, food, poverty, illiteracy, integration.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 7 Geography 7): a dedicated English-medium Geography book built entirely on Pakistan's own physical and economic geography — the Indus plain and delta, the canal system, Rabi/Kharif crops, Sui gas, Thar coal, Khewra salt and Tarbela power — with world concepts (river courses, settlement hierarchy, greenhouse effect, mining types) taught through Pakistani examples first.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 7 LY G7 Geography of Libya): A whole book on Libya alone, in four units — physical (location, relief, climate, soil, water, vegetation), human (population and urban centres), economic (farming, herding, fishing, minerals and oil, industry, trade), then tourism and transport, with tourism taught through Libya's historical cities (Jarma of the Garamantes) and oases (Awjila).

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 10 LY G10 Principles): Secondary geography opens as a two-part systematic course — physical geography first (astronomy, surface-shaping factors, climate, vegetation and wild animals), then human geography (population and settlement, economic resources) — and closes with a whole book on spatial-data technology: remote sensing, GPS and GIS, then the history of maps from Babylon and Ibn Hawqal.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 5 G5 Geography): first Geography year built as a single Earth-systems arc — planet, envelopes (lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere/biosphere), human society, then a closing "Protecting the Earth" chapter; every paragraph opens with "you will be able to" outcomes and chapters close with evaluation tests, plus two practical applications (map work; a week-long weather calendar).

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 11 G11 Geography): a human geography of the world year — political map and state typology, natural resources by sphere, population and settlements, then the world economy branch by branch (agriculture, each industry, services, transport, tourism), closing with economic-geographic characterisations of 14 states on every continent (Egypt among them) written to one template, plus case studies including labour migration at local/regional/global scale and using Google Maps/Google Earth as geo-information portals.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 4 Social Studies): geography enters inside "Social Studies" through the child's own surroundings — phenomena are sorted from photographs first, then the local environment, then the map of Iraq's governorates, and only then the wider geographical environments (city, countryside); history and homeland/civics chapters follow in the same book.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 10 Geography): the literary track opens geography as a discipline in its own right — its thought from the Sumerians to GIS, how a geographer researches, where geographical data come from and how they are drawn, and only then the fields of the science; Iraq supplies the worked examples throughout (Baghdad research problem, Iraqi topographic maps, Iraq's gas time series, the Baghdad–Basra distance exercise).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Human Geography): a human-geography course in six chapters — population, cities and countryside, agriculture, industry and mining, transport and trade, political geography and tourism — and every chapter closes with an applied study of Iraq itself, so the world pattern is always brought home.
