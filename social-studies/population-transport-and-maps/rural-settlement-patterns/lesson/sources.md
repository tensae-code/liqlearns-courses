**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Palestine, Bangladesh, Indonesia). Haiti is named here as teacher-sequence evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Indonesia's own way (Grade 12): Grade 12 is applied human geography built entirely on Indonesian cases — regional development and spatial planning (tata ruang) measured against a Happiness Index, Industry 4.0 / Society 5.0, and Indonesia's own geopolitics and ASEAN-era cooperation as pillars of national resilience. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Population geography is built entirely on Haiti's own data, handled critically: pupils confront the national statistics institute (IHSI) with international sources, note that the last census is 2003, build density maps and age pyramids themselves, then read the whole country through Port-au-Prince's primacy and the diaspora («les Haïtiens de l'extérieur»). That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Searchable tags: rural settlement, nucleated, dispersed, linear, permanence, farming village, social studies, Haiti, Population urbaine et rurale, teacher sequence evidence

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P2): the settlement story is told as a ladder — farming, animal domestication, the agricultural village, the Palestinian village of today, then the city located on the map — and the year closes with the road itself: what a road is, its parts, and how to cross it safely.

Bangladesh's own way (Grades 9-10): the shared SSC book's big chapter "Living Together Safely" is a genuine geography course inside the combined book — land-use change read off 1988-vs-2017 Khulna maps, world population and density, remote sensing and GIS with its five components, settlement and urbanisation, desertification, and Bangladesh's own squeeze of limited land, dense population and climate risk.
