# Counting births — four fertility measures

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: four different rulers measure births — what does each one actually count? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The crude birth rate

The crude birth rate counts births per 1,000 people in a year. It is simple to work — but blind to age structure: a country of elders and a country of young parents can share a CBR while their futures differ completely.

## Sub lesson 2 — TFR and GRR

The total fertility rate is built from age-specific rates across the seven five-year age groups; the book's worked example resolves to 2 births per woman. The gross reproduction rate counts daughters only — the next generation of mothers — and its replacement meaning sits around 1.0.

## Sub lesson 3 — The general fertility rate

The general fertility rate counts births per 1,000 women aged 15 to 49. Each measure in the set sees what the others miss, which is why demographers carry all four.

## Sub lesson 4 — Africa's fertility — the Grade 10 measures

The Grade 10 book teaches the same measures from Africa's side. The total fertility rate is the children per woman across ages 15–49 at current rates; the crude birth rate is births per 1,000 mid-year population — and it prints Africa's figure: 33 births per 1,000 in mid-2020. Set beside the Grade 11 worked examples, the two books give the measures and the continent's own numbers in them.
