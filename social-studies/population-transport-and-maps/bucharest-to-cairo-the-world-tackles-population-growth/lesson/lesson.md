# Bucharest to Cairo — the world tackles population growth

## Do it first

Two towns both want fewer births. One hands out contraceptives; the other schools its girls and clinics its mothers. Which works better in twenty years? Guess now; Cairo's answer is in the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bucharest 1974 — the organised response begins

The organised world response starts at the 1974 Bucharest World Population Conference: 136 countries agreeing a World Population Plan of Action.

The Plan tied family-planning programmes to resource conservation and living standards — population policy was never only about numbers, even at the start. Behind the conference stood the backdrop the book notes: about 1.5% annual global growth, and lengthening, often unhealthy, retirements in the rich world.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cairo 1994 — the humane turn

Twenty years later, the 1994 Cairo International Conference on Population and Development reframed the issue. Population pressure, Cairo said, comes jointly from rapid growth in poor countries and high consumption in wealthy ones — both sides of the world are in the equation.

And stabilisation should attack roots rather than equate policy with family planning alone: women's education, health care, and economic and political participation. Change the conditions of women's lives, and fertility follows — that is the humane turn.
