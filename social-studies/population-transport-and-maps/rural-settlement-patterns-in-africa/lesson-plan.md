# Lesson plan — Rural settlement patterns in Africa

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Rural settlement patterns in Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Population of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's rural settlement — the pattern of villages and homes across the countryside — is the rural half of the urban–rural pair: how homes cluster or scatter is read against the livelihood base that supports them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Africa urbanising — fastest and slowest: this lesson is the countryside that lesson's towns grow from.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Reading a rural settlement pattern** — Clustered, scattered — the pattern of homes.
2. **The livelihood base beneath the pattern** — Farming life shapes the map of homes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Africa urbanising — fastest and slowest. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define rural settlement pattern.
2. Describe clustered and scattered patterns and why each forms.
3. Explain the livelihood base — farming land and water beneath the homes.
4. Connect rural patterns to the unit's urban shares.

## If the learner is stuck
Two questions unlock every part: what pattern (clustered or scattered?) and what livelihood (what do they live from, and how does that shape the homes?). Ask both, in that order.
