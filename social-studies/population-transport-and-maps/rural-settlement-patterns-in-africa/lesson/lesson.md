# Rural settlement patterns in Africa

## Do it first

Picture the countryside you know best. Do the homes stand together in a village, or scattered one by one across the fields? Why there — water, fields, roads, kin? Your answers are the two questions of this lesson: what pattern, and what livelihood beneath it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading a rural settlement pattern

A **rural settlement pattern** is the way homes and villages are arranged across the countryside. At its simplest the pattern is read two ways: **clustered** — homes gathered together into villages — or **scattered** — homesteads spread out across the land, each on its own ground.

The pattern is never decoration. Homes gather where gathering helps — shared water, shared defence, shared work — and scatter where the livelihood rewards spreading out, each family beside its own fields. To read a pattern, always ask what the people underneath it do for a living.

## Sub lesson 2 — The livelihood base beneath the pattern

Rural settlement rests on a **livelihood base** — above all farming: the fields, grazing and water that feed the settlement. The urban share tables of this unit are read against this rural remainder: in the least urbanised region, Eastern Africa at 26% urban, roughly three of every four people live inside these rural patterns.

That is why the pair of lessons matters together: urbanisation percentages are town numbers, but the majority life of the continent — and the starting point of every rural–urban migration — is written in these village patterns and the farmland beneath them.

## The MLC extension — colonialism and settlement (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 minimum competencies add an expectation neither book states as a section: learners should be able to **discuss the impact of colonialism on the current settlement patterns of Africa** (teacher guide — evidence). When you read Africa's settlement patterns in this lesson, hold that question with them: how much of where people live today was shaped in the colonial period?

## Say it to your companion

Describe a rural settlement pattern to your companion — real or drawn — naming its form and its livelihood base. Your companion asks: clustered or scattered, and what does the pattern tell you about how its people live?
