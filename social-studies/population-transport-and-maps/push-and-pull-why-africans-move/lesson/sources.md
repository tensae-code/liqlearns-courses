Pairing: the teacher guide was first published in 2022 and the student textbook in 2023, with the same writers and the same eight units in the same order; the student book is the learner text of record, and teacher-guide rows above are evidence only.

Pairing warning carried exactly: the teacher guide has NO §7.3 section and folds its coastal material into its §7.2 migration lesson (guide printed pp. 139–140); the migration glossary in Sub lesson 2 rests on that guide passage and is labelled (teacher guide — evidence) in the sources table.

Costa Rica's own way: two-thirds contemporary History/geopolitics, then a closing Geography unit on population dynamics and spatial mobility that always pairs the world pattern with the Costa Rican case (Costa Rica's own population change, internal and international mobility). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Samoa's own way (Year 9, Book 2): Place is taught through Samoan lived places — ordinary, sacred (tapu), natural and political places such as Mulinu'u — and migration is taught through Samoa's own movement story and its New Zealand diaspora, closing with a full mapping-skills unit.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 5): population geography taught as a developed-world/developing-world contrast throughout — distribution factors, growth, Malthus and the transition, then the two evolutions (developed countries; the "Terceiro Mundo" set) with their opposite population policies, closing on structures, demographic problems (incl. HIV cutting life expectancy below 50) and population–economy/environment relations.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 Geography student): grade-10 Geography is a methods-first course — it opens with the science's history, branches and GIS/GPS/aerospace methods, works through the lithosphere with a full natural-disasters lesson, and closes with resources measured by endowment; Armenia's own rivers fill the hydrology tables and Armenia's place in modern geopolitics closes the political-map theme.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 11 S3 Geography SB): A world-geography book (trade routes, megacities, Southeast Asia) anchored back to South Sudan throughout — humanitarian airlift airfields, Thuraya satellite phones, a tourism plan for South Sudan, Juba as the urbanisation example.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 11 S3 Geography TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Mirrors the student's book's 8 units with content maps and expected answers; repeatedly localises world topics (domestic trade with South Sudan examples, what the South Sudan government should improve to sustain tourism). That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — South Sudan is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 3 P3 Social Studies SB): The land unit is anchored on the child's own payam — Mount Kinyeti, the River Nile and the Sudd Swamp are the named examples — and features are taught as double-sided: benefits to us, and problems they can cause, like rivers that fill and flood.

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 3 P3 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide adds the settlement half of migration — factors for migration and settlement together — and a practical question the pupil's book only implies: what can be done to minimise migration? That evidence is stocked here as the way South Sudan teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — South Sudan is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 8 P8 Social Studies SB): The climate unit is two-way and practical — how climate shapes people's activities (poor climate ruins roads) and how people adapt: use scarce water efficiently, build flood defences and raise dykes, grow drought-tolerant crops, and make biogas.
