# Lesson plan — Push and pull — why Africans move

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Push and pull — why Africans move
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Geographic Issues and Public Concerns in Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africans move because home pushes and destinations pull: push factors — poverty, low wages, unemployment, poor governance, corruption, instability and conflict — drive people out, while pull factors — income, security, education and health care — draw them on; behind today's moves stands the forced slave migration of the 16th–19th centuries.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the migration kinds — internal/international, voluntary/forced — whose causes this lesson names.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Push factors — what drives people out** — Poverty, low wages, unemployment, governance, conflict.
2. **Pull factors — what draws people on** — Income, security, education, health care.
3. **Africa's long history of movement** — Forced slave migrations and border-straddling communities.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Where Africans live — density map. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the push factors and sort them into livelihood and safety families.
2. Name the four pull factors: income, security, education, health care.
3. Use the migration glossary terms correctly.
4. State the population effect of the 16th–19th-century slave migrations.
