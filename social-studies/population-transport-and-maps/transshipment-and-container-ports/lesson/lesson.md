# Transshipment and Container Ports

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Transshipment and Container Port: transshipment is unloading cargo from one vessel and loading it onto another to complete its journey; the Freeport container port in Grand Bahama provides many jobs and handles more than 10% of global container traffic; Transportation/Communication Worldwide: imports are inspected daily at the docks and entry ports; Prince George dock in Nassau is a port of entry where containers are offloaded and searched; surface mail, daily flights and instant electronic communication — as the Bahamas' books teach it. ## Sub lesson 1 — Transshipment and Container Ports

Bahamas' Grade 5 student book, the *Bahamas Primary Social Studies — Digital Textbook, Grade 5* (PDF p. 28), teaches: Transshipment and Container Port: transshipment is unloading cargo from one vessel and loading it onto another to complete its journey; the Freeport container port in Grand Bahama provides many jobs and handles more than 10% of global container traffic. Bahamas' Grade 6 student book, the *Bahamas Primary Social Studies — Digital Textbook, Grade 6* (PDF p. 25), teaches: Transportation/Communication Worldwide: imports are inspected daily at the docks and entry ports; Prince George dock in Nassau is a port of entry where containers are offloaded and searched; surface mail, daily flights and instant electronic communication. ## Sub lesson 2 — Bahamas' way — the book's own teaching way

Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism. Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): The Grade 6 book is civics-led (government, elections, courts, constitution, finance ministry) but opens the primary course's widest geography: the world's continents and oceans, political and physical maps, global warming and natural disasters, human resources and the job market, worldwide transport and communication through Nassau's ports of entry, and a closing cultural comparison of The Bahamas with Africa, Australia, Brazil and Japan. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
