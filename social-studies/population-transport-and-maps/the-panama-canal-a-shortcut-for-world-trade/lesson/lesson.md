# The Panama Canal — a Shortcut for World Trade

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: the Panama Canal — the shortcut for world trade across the isthmus, as Guatemala's Grade 4 *Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana* teaches it in Unit 3 on printed p. 138. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The canal that joins two oceans

The Panama Canal cuts across the isthmus of Panama and joins the Atlantic side to the Pacific, so ships trading between the oceans take the shortcut through Central America instead of the long way around South America. Guatemala's book teaches it inside its work on the ports and airports of Central America — the isthmus as a crossing place for the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guatemala's way — the isthmus learned from home

Guatemala learns this inside one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
