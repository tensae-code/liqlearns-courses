# The demographic transition model

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: why does a country's population explode in the middle of its development, then settle? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Stages one and two — balance, then surge

Population geography is framed by fertility, mortality and migration. Stage one: high birth and death rates balance, so numbers hold steady. Stage two: death rates fall — especially deaths of under-fives — while births stay high, so growth surges.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stages three and four — the closing gap

Stage three: births fall too, with urbanisation and contraception, and growth slows. Stage four: births and deaths are both low and near-equal, and numbers settle again — at a far higher level than stage one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Stage five, and two journeys

A possible stage five brings a slight renewed rise. Developed and developing countries travelled the stages in different centuries — the model's shape is the same, its timing is not.
