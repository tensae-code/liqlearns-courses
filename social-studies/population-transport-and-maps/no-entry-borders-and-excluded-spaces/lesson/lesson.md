# No Entry — Borders and Excluded Spaces

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Folha 2: É proibida a entrada! / No entry allowed! — borders, ghettos and closed/private spaces, as Brazil's Grade 10 Geografia (SEED-PR) teaches it in one of its 17 Folhas, each opening with a provocative question. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Borders — lines that say no entry

Folha 2 opens with É proibida a entrada! / No entry allowed! It teaches borders as lines and rules that can forbid entry — a border is not only drawn on a map; it is enforced where people try to cross it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ghettos and closed, private spaces

The same Folha carries the idea inside places as well: ghettos and closed/private spaces are parts of a place where entry is barred to some people. Excluded spaces, at a country's edge or inside a city, are this lesson's one thing.

Brazil's book has no chapters in the old style: it is 17 "Folhas" (sheets) like this one, each opening with a provocative question, grouped under four dimensions of geographical space (political, cultural-demographic, economic, socio-environmental) and taught from songs, news and the student's own place.
