# Colouring Suriname's Map and Flag

## Do it first

Look at an uncoloured outline map of your country. Before you read on, trace its edge with your finger: that outline is the shape of the whole country, small on the page. What else is drawn inside the outline? Look closely before you colour anything.

## Sub lesson 1 — Colouring the outline map of Suriname

In Suriname's Grade 3 workbook, one lesson is called Les 5 Kleuren — Colours. Its first task is a map: the outline map of Suriname, drawn with its rivers inside it, waiting to be coloured.

An outline map shows a country's own shape — the line around the whole country — and this one also shows the rivers that run through it. Colouring it is a first way of learning a map: as your colours follow the outline and the rivers, you learn the shape of your country and where its rivers run. Colour the map as the book shows it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colouring the flag of Suriname

The same workbook lesson gives you a second thing to colour: the flag of Suriname. A flag is a country's own symbol — the sign that stands for the country itself.

Map and flag belong together on the page: the map shows the country's shape and its rivers; the flag is its symbol. Colour the flag as the book shows it, and you hold your whole country on one page — its land in the map, and its symbol in the flag.

## Say it to your companion

Show your companion your coloured page and explain it in your own words: what the outline map shows, and what the flag is. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
