# The digital divide — connected and left out

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: two students, one lesson by video — and one beyond the signal. What divides them? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the divide is

The divide is the gap between persons, households, firms and regions in access to ICTs and the internet — and so to economic and political opportunity. It is measured by access to TVs, computers, phones and connections.

[Picture 27084 — a split scene — a student video-calling a teacher, and an identical student beyond the signal's edge]

## Sub lesson 2 — Drivers and harms

Its drivers: income, connection cost and quality, education and digital literacy, and location. Its harms: lost jobs in old businesses, widened inequality, and new security risks — cybercrime, hate speech, fake news.

## Sub lesson 3 — The gifts, and the bridge-builders

Against the harms stand digitalisation's gifts: telemedicine, e-learning, weather and market information for farmers and fishers, and banking for the unbanked. Governments are cast as the bridge-builders between the connected and the left-out.
