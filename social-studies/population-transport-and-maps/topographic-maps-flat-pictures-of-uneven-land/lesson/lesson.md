# Topographic maps — flat pictures of uneven land

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: how do you draw a mountain on flat paper so a stranger can read its height? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Relief and landforms

Relief is the height difference between the highest and lowest points of a landscape. Landforms — plateaus, escarpments, hills, mountains, volcanoes, saddles, cliffs, valleys, gorges, floodplains and more — are erosion-resistant three-dimensional features standing in that relief.

## Sub lesson 2 — The topographic map

A topographic map is the detailed two-dimensional portrait of an area's natural and man-made features. Flat paper, uneven land: everything in this unit is a device for keeping the third dimension readable in two.

## Sub lesson 3 — The map's language

The portrait is built from colours, symbols, letters and numbers — and scale, the map-to-ground distance ratio that tells how much the portrait shrinks the land.
