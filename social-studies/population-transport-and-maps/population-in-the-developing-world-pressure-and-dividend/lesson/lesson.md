# Population in the developing world — pressure and dividend

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: can fast growth be both a burden and a gift? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Deaths fell first

After the Second World War, medicine, piped water, housing and education cut death rates in less developed countries while births stayed high. Their population nearly doubled in 1980–2010 and is projected from 855 million in 2011 toward 1.67 billion in 2050; African LDCs grew fastest, at 2.8 percent a year.

## Sub lesson 2 — The costs

Fast growth strains cities, services and housing, and builds slums. At least 55 LDCs, meanwhile, are already shrinking — the developing world is not one story.

## Sub lesson 3 — The dividend

As fertility falls, the working-age share swells. That opens a demographic dividend — if governments invest in young people's health and education while the window is open.
