Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 12 geo-6as-cd): A human-geography year in three units — world population (growth, distribution, migration, structure, with an applications lesson on the age pyramid and demographic projections), urbanisation and its problems, and economic activities from traditional farming to modern industry, transport, trade, craft industry and tourism.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 9 G9 Geography of Yemen and the Arab World): Grade 9 returns to Yemen by environments (mountain, coastal-marine, desert), then Yemen's population and resources, then scales up to the Arab world's environment and water problems, population and industrial development.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 11 G11 Geography of Yemen and the Arab World): Grade 11 does Yemen in depth first (physical base, population and social development, economic resources), then the Arab world's physical geography, its people and resources, and finally its contemporary problems — water, food, poverty, illiteracy, integration.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 11 G11 Geography): a human geography of the world year — political map and state typology, natural resources by sphere, population and settlements, then the world economy branch by branch (agriculture, each industry, services, transport, tourism), closing with economic-geographic characterisations of 14 states on every continent (Egypt among them) written to one template, plus case studies including labour migration at local/regional/global scale and using Google Maps/Google Earth as geo-information portals.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 7 np-g7): Grade 7 Earth science is motion-first — Earth's daily and annual motion, surface form and the factors that change it, surface form's relation to human life — then Nepal's climate, the seven provinces of federal Nepal, climate change, disasters and map work; population is taught as structure, change and management.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 7 TG np-g7tg) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide teaches the same Grade 7 course as np-g7 with globe-and-ball demonstrations of rotation/revolution, chart work on Nepal's demographic structure (age, sex, religion, caste, language distributions), and discussion-led lessons; its geography content mirrors the student book lesson for lesson, so placements repeat with guide page references. That evidence is stocked here as the way Nepal teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Nepal is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 10 np-g10): Grade 10 climate is taught by world climate types and the three heat zones (tropical, temperate, cold), then continent studies of North America, South America and Africa, earthquakes, map work and modern map technology (GIS); population closes the book — Nepal's size/growth, structure, distribution, family planning and the population–environment interrelation.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 10 TG np-g10tg) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide teaches the Grade 10 course of np-g10 lesson for lesson, adding map- and chart-based activities (world climate maps, heat-zone maps, continent maps comparing life and economic activity with Nepal) and earthquake-preparedness practice; placements mirror the student book with guide page references. That evidence is stocked here as the way Nepal teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Nepal is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 5): population geography taught as a developed-world/developing-world contrast throughout — distribution factors, growth, Malthus and the transition, then the two evolutions (developed countries; the "Terceiro Mundo" set) with their opposite population policies, closing on structures, demographic problems (incl. HIV cutting life expectancy below 50) and population–economy/environment relations.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G7 SS T2): Term 2's geography is Jordan itself — a journey through three governorates (Karak, Zarqa, Jerash) and a full unit on Jordan's population: growth, distribution, age and sex structure.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 Geography T1): the final-year Geography book is built in three blocks — the forces that shape Earth's surface, political geography (state power, borders, alliances, Jordan's national security), and population geography.

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 10 laos-g10-geography-teacher): a second world-geography pass at a higher level — Earth and map projection first, then life in the climate zones (hot, temperate, cold, mountain/coastal), population characteristics and problems, humans and environment, and a distinctive closing block on how geographic space is organised (agricultural areas, industrial areas, tourism areas, rural and urban geography).

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 10 laos-g10-geography-student): the student twin of Book 6 — same 6 parts / 20 lessons with full illustrated text, opening with a concept map of the whole year (Earth, life zones, population and quality of life, humans and environment, organisation of geographic space, rural and urban geography).

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies): Grade 9 returns to Iraq for the fullest treatment yet — the same national geography as Grade 5 but measured: the plain is 24% and about 600 km long, the desert plateau about 55%, the desert plant belt about 70%, two-thirds of the people live on the central and southern plain; every topic is pinned to numbered maps of Iraq.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 CCSS Módulo Autoformativo G7): The distance-learning parallel of the Grade 7 full-year book (Book 7): the same five Geography units rewritten for rural self-study, so every topic is anchored in the learner's own comunidad — its soils, its production lands, its road links and its risk zones. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I government institutions (printed p. 23, Civics); Unit VI first settlers (printed pp. 113–128, History, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans (printed pp. 131–141, History); Unit VIII independence process (printed pp. 145–154, History); Unit IX evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 157–168, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty (printed pp. 173–202, History); Unit XI triumph of the Revolution to the present (printed pp. 205–231, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 9 Geography): The Intermediate level (IX–X) treats each world topic at full textbook depth and keeps the Bhutan case inside it — Soils of Bhutan, agro-ecological zones and farming practices of Bhutan, and common disasters in Bhutan (including GLOF) alongside the world disaster survey.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 11 Geography): The Advanced level (XI–XII) is full academic geography — map projections, remote sensing and GIS, insolation and pressure belts, climate classification — with Bhutan applied chapters on agriculture, manufacturing and tourism, the last taught on Bhutan's own sustainability principle ("high value, low volume" in the Bhutan Tourism Monitor data the book uses).
