# Video script — Human evolution — fossils, archaeology and the first humans

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Somewhere in Ethiopia, a stone tool and a piece of bone lay in the ground for longer than all of written history. Nobody wrote their story — so how can we know it at all? This lesson is about the science that reads the ground itself.

Archaeology is the science that digs out material objects and fossils of the past — and it serves the study of prehistory, the time before writing. Its evidence is things: tools, bones, remains.

A fossil is the remains of a dead plant, human or animal, preserved from the deep past. Hominids are the family of humans and prehistoric human-like creatures — our wider family, most of it long gone.

Two theories of human origin are set side by side. The creationist theory: humans were created by God. The scientific/evolution theory: humans developed from lower beings through gradual change. The lesson states both fairly; the fossil stages below are the evidence the second theory reads.

The Homo line runs in three stages. Homo habilis — able to make tools. Homo erectus — able to stand upright and walk. Homo sapiens — modern humans, whose distinctive feature is knowledge.

Ethiopia is one of the world's great fossil grounds. The keyed Ethiopian Homo erectus / fossil sites: the Omo basin, Melka Kunture, Konso-Gardula and the Middle Awash. The regional sites complete the map: Olduvai Gorge (Tanzania), Lake Turkana (Kenya) and the Omo valley (Ethiopia).

The doing-skill of this sub lesson is the archaeologist's own: show the sites on a sketch map — the deep past has an address, and much of it is here.

Tell your companion what archaeology digs up, the two theories in one fair sentence each, the three Homo stages with their marks, and two Ethiopian fossil sites.
