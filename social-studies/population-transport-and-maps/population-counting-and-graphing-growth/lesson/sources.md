**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Indonesia, Colombia, Slovenia, Vietnam, Syria, Venezuela, Sri Lanka, Yemen, Pakistan, Nicaragua, Bhutan). Myanmar is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing.

Indonesia's own way (Grade 11 teacher guide — evidence): a Kurikulum Merdeka teacher guide — a general part sets out the Fase F learning outcomes (Capaian Pembelajaran), Pancasila-student-profile targets per chapter, teaching strategies and assessment design; a special part then guides each student-book chapter activity by activity, with prerequisite concepts, pre-learning assessment, competence + character scoring rubrics for every activity, answer keys and reflection. Note: its Bab IV is retitled "Perubahan Iklim dan Kebencanaan" (climate change and disasters), widening the student book's disaster chapter. That way is stocked here as the way Indonesia teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above beyond Indonesia's student-book count.

Myanmar's own way (teacher guide — evidence): the year is split into five parallel strands revisited week by week — physical geography, social geography, environmental geography, neighbouring countries and cartography — each lesson plan built student-centred (Bloom steps, key questions, classroom assessment) around Myanmar and its five neighbours. That evidence is stocked here as the way Myanmar teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs and whole student topics (maps, globes, GPS) have no guide counterpart, so no guide coverage is claimed for them. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado7 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria Grade 7 guide teaches the medieval/colonial world through territory — how societies transformed their natural environment, represented their territory, and used and exploited its natural resources.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Địa lí 10): a single Grade 10 course that runs the whole discipline — map craft, the Earth's spheres, then economic geography sector by sector — with Vietnam itself as the standing worked example (its power-plant and rice maps, the Red River's regime at Hanoi, Hanoi's climate chart, Vietnam's GDP structure, its internet and tourism figures) inside world-scale treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 6 G6 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): the whole Geography block is one argument — "Nuestro espacio geográfico en tiempos del petróleo": how oil remade population (2011 census), cities (metropolisation), industry location, internal migration and landscapes; learners start by learning how to *look* at a piece of oil-era Venezuela.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 11 G11 CS Geografía de Venezuela): a final-year economic geography of Venezuela alone — population past/present/future, human development in numbers, then resource by resource (minerals, land, fisheries, forests, oil, water/hydroelectricity) each read through sovereignty, collective participation and environmental conscience, closing on Latin American union.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 9 G9 English): the student companion to Book 1 — five chapters moving from the whole Asian region down to Sri Lanka's own landscape, its district-by-district development contrasts, environmental balance, and a closing practical chapter reading the 1:50,000 topographic maps.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 5 G5 Geography of Yemen): the first standalone Geography book is entirely about Yemen itself — map skills first, then Yemen's physical geography, then its population, then its economic activity sector by sector.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 3 Estudios Sociales G3): The first formal Geography course in the set: Units I–II teach what Geography is, map types, scale, orientation and the Earth, then every later unit works at three nested scales — comunidad, municipio, departamento — ending with Nicaragua's three regions, its volcanic chain and a Köppen climate map of the country. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI municipality origins, customs and citizen participation (printed pp. 72–80, History/Civics, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII first settlers' history and culture (printed pp. 82–93, History); Unit VIII historical celebrations and heroes (printed pp. 94–109, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 CCSS Geografía Módulo G9): A pure-Geography self-study module (no History/Civics in this book): the Grade 9 world-Geography course taught continent by continent — position and political organisation, physical landscape, human diversity, production and consumption, and a closing planet-care unit on prevention and mitigation — with Nicaragua used as the home reference point throughout.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 6 Social Studies): Grade 6 turns to Bhutan's own climate zones, population and signature environment topics — hydropower, urbanisation and glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF) — a hazard set specific to a Himalayan kingdom.
