# Population — counting and graphing growth

## Do it first

Before any world number: go to your own school's record office (or ask your teacher) and fetch the enrolment — how many pupils in each grade, how many boys and how many girls. Write the numbers down. You have just done what every population counter in the world does: count real people, in real groups, from real records.

## Sub lesson 1 — Counting your own school

Counting people starts close to home. A school keeps a record of every pupil. From that record you can take the enrolment **by grade** — how many in Grade 1, how many in Grade 7 — and **by sex** — how many boys, how many girls.

Write your count as a clear table: one row per grade, one column for boys, one for girls, one for the total. A count you cannot read back is not finished.

## Sub lesson 2 — Graphing growth and judging it

Now take the same school's enrolment for ten years and draw it two ways.

[Picture 11080 — school enrolment as a bar graph and a line graph]

A **bar graph** shows each year as its own bar — good for comparing years. A **line graph** joins the years with a line — good for seeing the climb. Then work out the **yearly percentage growth**: how much bigger this year is than last year, out of every hundred.

Last, judge it. Did the growth fill the classrooms? Did the play space shrink? Did the books and materials stretch thinner? Numbers are only the start — what they did to real places is the lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion your school's total enrolment, its biggest grade, and its ten-year growth. Then say one thing the growth changed at your school.

## Do It Yourself

1. Fetch your school's enrolment by grade and sex and write it as a table.
2. Draw the last five or ten years as a bar graph, then as a line graph.
3. Work out one year's percentage growth and say what the growth did to your classrooms.
