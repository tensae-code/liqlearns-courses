# Lesson plan — Population — counting and graphing growth

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Population — counting and graphing growth
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how to count people where you are — starting with your own school — and show growth in bar and line graphs
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the learner's own school and neighbourhood — counting and simple graphs are the only tools it needs.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Counting your own school** — fetch the school's enrolment by grade and by sex from the school record office — real counting, not guessing.
2. **Graphing growth and judging it** — draw the same ten years as a bar graph and a line graph, work out the yearly percentage growth, and judge what the growth did to classrooms, play space and materials.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing in this title — this is the door into population work. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Fetch a real enrolment count by grade and by sex from a record office.
2. Draw the same numbers as a bar graph and as a line graph.
3. Work out a yearly percentage growth over ten years.
4. Say what growth did to classrooms, play space and materials.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Before any world number: go to your own school's record office (or ask your teacher) and fetch the enrolment — how many pupils in each grade, how many boys and how many girls. Write the numbers down. You have just done what every population counter in the world does: count real people, in real groups, from real records. (lesson/lesson.md)
