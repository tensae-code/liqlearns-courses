# When cities outrun their plans

## Do it first

Before you read, think of the most crowded, unplanned corner of a city you know — no piped water, no road a cart can pass. Ask how it got that way: did the people fail, or did the plan never arrive? Read with that question open.

## Sub lesson 1 — The colonial legacy and urban primacy

Many African cities inherited a colonial street plan built for a small administered centre, not for the millions who came later. Urban primacy compounds it: one city towers over all others in its country, drawing migrants, investment and services to itself while smaller towns starve.

## Sub lesson 2 — Slums and squatter settlements

Where growth outruns planning and housing, people house themselves: slums and squatter settlements rise on land the plan never serviced — Addis Ababa is the book's pictured case. These settlements are not the cause of the problem; they are what people build when the planned city has no place for them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Instability and unrest

The unit's chain ends in strain: social instability, unrest and urban violence grow where work, housing and services fail the crowds the city attracted. Read backwards, the lesson is a warning about sequence — plan, services and housing must grow with the people, not after them.
