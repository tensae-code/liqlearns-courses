# Where Africans live — density map

## Do it first

Spread a handful of stones over a table so most cluster in a few spots and wide areas stay bare. That is a population map in miniature. Now guess for Africa: where are the clusters, and where is the bare table? Then read the map.

## Sub lesson 1 — Distribution and density — two words for the map

**Distribution** describes how population is spread over an area — even or uneven, clustered or scattered. **Density** measures how many people live in each unit of area — the crowding number of a place. Africa's distribution is strikingly **uneven**: the map is a pattern of crowded clusters and almost empty lands, not a smooth spread.

Keep the words apart: distribution is the pattern you see; density is the number you compute for each part of it. The density map joins them — every shade on it is a density, and the pattern of shades is the distribution.

## Sub lesson 2 — Crowded pockets, empty lands — and why

The dense pockets of Africa gather where life is easiest to support: **riverine** lands along the great rivers, **coastal** strips, and the well-watered **highlands**. Against them stand the **near-empty deserts** — above all the Sahara — where water scarcity keeps settlement at almost nothing, with people only at points like oases.

So the density map is really a water-and-land map in disguise: follow the water and the workable land, and you have predicted the dark clusters; find the hyper-arid lands, and you have predicted the pale emptiness between them.

## Say it to your companion

Define distribution and density for your companion, then describe the density map: where the dark clusters are, where the pale emptiness is, and why. Your companion points at a desert on an imaginary map — what density do you predict, and why?

## Do It Yourself

1. Sketch a density map of Africa: dark clusters on a river, a coast and a highland; pale over the Sahara.
2. Write the two definitions in your own words, one line each.
3. Describe your own home area: its density in words, and the water or land reason behind it.
