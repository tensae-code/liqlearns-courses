# Lesson plan — Where Africans live — density map

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Where Africans live — density map
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 4 — Population of Africa, Grade 10 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Africa's population is spread very unevenly: dense pockets along rivers, coasts and highlands stand against the near-empty deserts — population distribution and density are the map of where people actually are.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the population measures of Unit 4 — this lesson puts the people on the map.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Distribution and density — two words for the map** — Where people are, and how crowded.
2. **Crowded pockets, empty lands — and why** — Rivers, coasts, highlands against deserts.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Africa urbanising — fastest and slowest. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define distribution and density and tell them apart.
2. Describe Africa's pattern: dense riverine, coastal and highland pockets against near-empty deserts.
3. Explain the pattern by water and workable land.

## If the learner is stuck
Two words, two jobs: distribution = pattern (seen), density = number (computed). And one cause rules the map: water. Clusters sit on water and workable land; emptiness sits on dry land.
