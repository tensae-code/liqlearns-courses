# Lesson plan — Maps and globes — what a map is

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Maps and globes — what a map is
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a map is — the Earth's surface seen from above, shrunk systematically onto flat paper, showing selected information — and the seven things a map does for us
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Maps and globes — the globe, next door in this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What a map is** — the definition piece by piece — from above, reduced, systematic, flat, selected — and why your classroom cannot be drawn unshrunk.
2. **The seven services of a map** — what maps do: wide area at once, positions, distances, areas, directions, spatial relationships, planning and research, strategy — and your sketch map of the walk home.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Maps and globes — the globe. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a map in the book's own terms.
2. Say why a map can only show selected information.
3. List the map's seven services.
4. Draw a sketch map of the home-to-school walk.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Pace your classroom: count your steps along its length and its width. Now try to draw the room on one page of your exercise book at its true size. You cannot — the page is smaller than the room. That impossibility is where maps begin. (lesson/lesson.md)
