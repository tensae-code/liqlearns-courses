**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Palestine, Egypt, Dominican Republic, Colombia, Eritrea, India, Cambodia, Fiji, Syria, Kuwait, Afghanistan, Yemen, Pakistan). Papua New Guinea is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs and whole student topics (maps, globes, GPS) have no guide counterpart, so no guide coverage is claimed for them. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence.

Palestine's own way (Grade 6, PS G6P1): Grade 6 opens with how we know — the map and its elements as the first source of geographical knowledge — then walks the Arab homeland region by region (peninsula, Levant, Palestine, Iraq, North Africa), each time fixing location first, then surface and climate, then the ancient civilisation the land produced.

Egypt's own way (Grade 4 Term 1): the very first Social Studies book starts from the child's own space — drawing a plan of the home and school, comparing walking routes by length and landmarks — before maps in general, then the child's own city and governorate, and only then Egypt as homeland; a robot mascot teaches alongside named child guides from different governorates.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 3): For third graders geography is the home island first: the planet and its components, then the Antilles and the relief of their own country, then countryside versus city, and finally where their own families came from — migration and a family census the children run themselves.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado2_01): Escuela Nueva self-paced guides (parts A–D: explore, deepen, practise, apply) set in rural Colombia — veredas, resguardos, coffee regions; geography starts from the walk home-to-school, the classroom plan and the home municipality (Aracataca/Ciénaga worked example).

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado08 Secundaria Activa): Grade 8 opens with a full geography unit — cartography first, then the paired physical-and-human treatment of Asia and Africa (spatial relations plus population development) — and only then turns to history.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado7 Postprimaria): the rural Postprimaria Grade 7 guide teaches the medieval/colonial world through territory — how societies transformed their natural environment, represented their territory, and used and exploited its natural resources.

Eritrea's own way (ER G4 Social Studies): the Tigrinya-medium primary book teaches Geography through the child's own surroundings first — our environment, protecting our soil, water, plants and animals, then maps and globes, community life, and the shapes of the land.

Eritrea's own way (ER G5 Social Studies): Grade 5 widens from the village to the world — Eritrea's regions (zobas) and neighbours, the continents, islands, oceans, seas, lakes and rivers of the Earth, then back home to the Red Sea, trade and transport, maps and weather.

India's own way (Grade 6 The Earth Our Habitat): The first Geography year goes planet → globe → motions → maps → Earth's domains → landforms, and only then turns home: the last two chapters are India itself (location, neighbours, physical divisions, monsoon climate, vegetation and wildlife), so world basics are immediately applied to the home country.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 6 Social Studies): the primary Social Studies spiral ends with its first real map work — maps, the globe and the equator/parallels/meridians grid taught inside the “society today” chapter, anchored on a world map with Cambodia marked.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 9 Social Science): Secondary geography opens with a full map-skills chapter — grid references, contours and cross-sections practised on Fiji maps — before environment use and conservation, including Pacific methods (shifting cultivation, rotational grazing, appropriate fishing methods).

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 3 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Social Science is organised in five strands (Community and Organization; Trade; Culture and Society; Environment and Resources; History and Origins) taught through guided lessons that start outdoors in the school grounds — children observe, draw and label the features, resources and maps of their own local community first. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 11 Geography Principles G11 T1): Geography is taught at Grade 11 (literary track) as one half of a combined "Principles" book, in depth and from first principles: the science defined and related to seven other sciences, its information sources treated as a subject in themselves (map, field study, official statistics, previous studies, remote sensing), then the Earth in the universe, then physical geography organised by the four spheres — of which this Term 1 volume carries only the lithosphere chapter; the hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere chapters announced in the introduction and on the Part Three opener belong to the course's later volume. Kuwaiti anchors run through it: the worked time-zone example is a Kuwait–Canary Islands football broadcast, a partial solar eclipse "in Kuwait, March 2006" is set as a research task, and the statistics chapter is built on Kuwait's own census, annual abstract (issued since 1964) and civil-information guide. Dated content to note: the astronomy still counts nine planets — Pluto is listed among the planets (printed p. 53) and "2003-UB313 Xena" is taught as the ninth planet, discovered recently (printed p. 56) — content surviving from the book's 2016 first editions.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 4 Social Studies (Dari)): primary social studies starts from the child's own world — family, village, city — and only then opens the map: directions, Afghanistan's boundaries and mountains and rivers, the continents and oceans, and finally the Earth's shape, equator, latitude/longitude and movements.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 7 G7 Principles of Physical Geography): from Grade 7 the subject splits — this book is pure physical geography organised by the Earth's spheres (atmosphere, lithosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere), opened by map skills and Earth-and-space.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 4 SS4): one combined Social Studies book in Urdu where the Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 61–93) works entirely through Pakistan's own map, landforms, weather and census, with BOLTS as the map-elements mnemonic and "I have learnt!" / "Work for me to do" boxes closing every topic.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 8 Geography 8): the Urdu-medium twin of the English Geography course — deep map-and-graph craft first (dot and choropleth maps of Pakistan itself), then landform agents, oceans, a full Pakistan-anchored disaster chapter, the world's natural regions, Pakistan's own climate, its four neighbouring regions and strategic place, underdevelopment with Pakistan's own causes, and modern techniques down to Pak Sat-1.
