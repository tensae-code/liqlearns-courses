## Sub lesson 1 — Primate city — macrocephaly, Port-au-Prince

Haiti's Grade 8 sequence studies Port-au-Prince as a case of urban macrocephaly: socio-spatial segregation inside the capital, centralisation on the capital, and the imbalance of the whole Haitian urban network — drawn as a sketch (croquis) of that network, with one city outweighing all the others.

## Sub lesson 2 — Haiti's way — one capital outweighing the network

This is a teacher sequence, written for the teacher, not handed to the pupil. In Haiti's Projet Nectar sequence the country's population geography is read through its capital: Port-au-Prince's primacy is not a fact to memorise but a pattern to draw, so the pupil sketches the network and sees the imbalance with their own hand.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Population geography is built entirely on Haiti's own data, handled critically: pupils confront the national statistics institute (IHSI) with international sources, note that the last census is 2003, build density maps and age pyramids themselves, then read the whole country through Port-au-Prince's primacy and the diaspora («les Haïtiens de l'extérieur»). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
