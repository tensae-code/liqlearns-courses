# Lesson plan — Maps and globes — kinds of maps and scale

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Maps and globes — kinds of maps and scale
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sorting maps two ways — by the message they carry (physical, political, special-purpose) and by scale (large, medium, small) — and the trade between coverage and detail

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Kinds of maps by message** — physical maps of landforms, political maps of borders, and special-purpose maps — charts, time-zone maps, statistical maps.
2. **Kinds of maps by scale** — large-scale, medium-scale and small-scale with their number bands — and why coverage and detail trade against each other.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Maps and globes — how parallels and meridians behave. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort maps by content into physical, political and special-purpose.
2. State the three scale bands with their numbers.
3. Explain the coverage–detail trade.
4. Classify real maps (world, kebele, Africa, school) by scale.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Lay out four maps in your mind: the whole world, Africa, your kebele, your school compound. One shows a flea's view, one an eagle's. Which shows the most land? Which shows the most detail? They cannot both win — that trade is this lesson's heart. (lesson/lesson.md)
