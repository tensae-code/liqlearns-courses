Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 8 CCSS Módulo Autoformativo G8): The distance-learning parallel of the Grade 8 full-year book: the same five Geography-of-America units rewritten for self-study, repeatedly narrowing from the continent to Centroamérica, El Caribe and Nicaragua itself (e.g. climate zones and water protection are treated region by region down to Nicaragua). Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI pre-Columbian origins (printed pp. 166–177, History); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans (printed pp. 182–201, History); Unit VIII emancipation of the indigenous peoples (printed pp. 206–220, History); Unit IX transformations after independence (printed pp. 224–246, History); Unit X America and the great changes of the 20th century (printed pp. 252–286, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 9 CCSS Geografía Módulo G9): A pure-Geography self-study module (no History/Civics in this book): the Grade 9 world-Geography course taught continent by continent — position and political organisation, physical landscape, human diversity, production and consumption, and a closing planet-care unit on prevention and mitigation — with Nicaragua used as the home reference point throughout.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 9 Geography): The Intermediate level (IX–X) treats each world topic at full textbook depth and keeps the Bhutan case inside it — Soils of Bhutan, agro-ecological zones and farming practices of Bhutan, and common disasters in Bhutan (including GLOF) alongside the world disaster survey.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 Geography student): opens school geography with the geographic map itself (plan, scale, azimuth, projections) before any Earth science, and runs every theme to a summary lesson plus practical work; world economy is taught branch by branch with Armenia (ՀՀ) used as the worked example in the closing questions.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 8 OM Geografía Económica y Social G8) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide's distinctive Cuban layer is territorial identity — learners characterise their own municipality's resources and the environmental problems of its economic sectors, and the world material (political map, population, resources, production) is always closed by comparison with Cuba. That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Cuba is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 7 Geografía Física G7): Physical geography is taught sphere by sphere (lithosphere → atmosphere → hydrosphere → biosphere) but every sphere chapter closes on rational use and sustainability — wind energy, rational water use, soil protection — and Cuban evidence runs through it (Cuba's forest cover fell from ~95 % at the Spanish arrival to 16 % by 1959 and was recovered by policy).

South Sudan's own way (South Sudan Grade 9 S1 Geography SB): Geography is built around South Sudan itself — its own physical features, resources, floods/drought experience and the Nile — with paper-folding and paper-tearing practical experiments for folding, faulting and sea-floor spreading, and group discussion of local examples in nearly every topic.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 5 Geography Teacher Guide (old ed.)) That evidence is stocked here as the way Greece teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Greece is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 5 Geography Student Book (old ed.)): same 47-chapter "Learning Greece" course as the teacher guide, in 4 units (Maps; The natural environment of Greece; The human-made environment of Greece; Hellenism beyond the borders), each chapter built from story/dialogue, photos and maps, with activity symbols (individual work, discussion, geographical glossary, "add to your knowledge") and a per-chapter glossary.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 5 Geography Workbook (old ed.)): one activity worksheet per student-book chapter (draw your own park map, true/false, acrostics, map-reading tasks) plus 11 interspersed assessment sheets working whole maps of Greece — thematic, geomorphological (repeated at rising difficulty), national parks and political.

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 7 Geology-Geography Student Book): Gymnasium Geography is organised by Earth's spheres — maps first, then the natural environment sphere by sphere (atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere, biosphere), then the human environment (population, cities, resources), closing with one "snapshot" chapter per continent, Antarctica included. Lessons open with art and literature (a Little Prince extract introduces coordinates) and state their learning goals up front ("In this chapter you will learn…").

Greece's own way (Greece Grade 7 Geology-Geography Teacher Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): the guide opens with the philosophy of the whole package — against memorising lists of countries and numbers: geography as systems, change over time and cross-curricular links (the ΔΕΠΠΣ framework), with "mental maps" as the goal.
