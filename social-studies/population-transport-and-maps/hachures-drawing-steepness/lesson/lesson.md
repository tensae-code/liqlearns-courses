# Hachures — drawing steepness

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: before contours existed, how did mapmakers draw a steep slope? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The older way

Hachures are short disconnected lines drawn down the direction of slope or water flow. They are the older way of showing relief, used for mountain ranges, depressions and small escarpments.

[Picture 27086 — a hachured landform drawing, with steep and gentle patches contrasted]

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading them

The rule is tactile: thick, short and close together means steep slopes; thin, long and wide apart means gentle ones. A hachured hillside you can almost feel with your eye.

## Sub lesson 3 — Their limits

Hachures give no heights and no exact gradients; they are laborious to draw and hard to read precisely. So today they are used only alongside contours — the subject of the next lesson.
