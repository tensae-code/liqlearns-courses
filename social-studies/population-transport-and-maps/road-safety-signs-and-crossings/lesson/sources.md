**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Ecuador, Palestine, Syria, Venezuela). Zimbabwe is named here as teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere.**

Turkey's own way: Hayat Bilgisi (Life Studies) carries almost no Geography in this semester book — self/school, health/safety and family/society — except road signs and the two end-of-book maps; the Geography parts only are placed. That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs and whole student topics (maps, globes, GPS) have no guide counterpart, so no guide coverage is claimed for them. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 3 book): the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Searchable tags: traffic light, zebra crossing, pedestrian light, red standing figure, green walking figure, Ecuador

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P2): the settlement story is told as a ladder — farming, animal domestication, the agricultural village, the Palestinian village of today, then the city located on the map — and the year closes with the road itself: what a road is, its parts, and how to cross it safely.

Zimbabwe's own way (ECD B) (teacher guide — evidence): Same HBC topic spiral as ECD A, one step deeper — transport starts from traditional/indigenous means (walking, animal-drawn) before modern vehicles; heritage sorted hands-on into animals / plants (baobab, maize) / artefacts (ngoma, hosho, mbira). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 1) (teacher guide — evidence): From Grade 1 the guide is unit-numbered (41 units across the same 14 HBC topics) with suggested answers to the Learner's Book; geography grows outward from home/school — my country and its ethnic groups and neighbours (South Africa, Zambia, Botswana), then transport, shelter, and Zimbabwe-specific hazards (veldfires, water disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 2) (teacher guide — evidence): The spiral widens from family to community — ethnic groups and languages of Zimbabwe (Kalanga, Sotho and others), production in the community, shelter types in the neighbourhood (granary, silos, community hall, clinic), and global issues introduced as floods and deforestation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 6) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 6 shelter becomes settlement geography — development of settlements in Zimbabwe, siting of shelter in rural and urban areas, and disaster and risk management; transport adds road signs/signals/markings and Zimbabwe's transport and communication links with other countries, plus ICT development. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 3 Social Studies G3): the Grade 3 book widens in rings — Me, Me and You, My Safety, My Society, My Environment, My Homeland — and the Geography lives in the last three rings: road safety, Syrian-made products, ways of living, then a whole environment unit (sky lanterns, locating my position, discovering Syria, the weather bulletin, a water journey).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 1 G1 CS Viva Venezuela): critical, question-first pedagogy ("pedagogía crítica") — spatial notions (up/down, north/south/east/west, near/far, yesterday/today) are built from the child's own home, classroom and community before any map appears.
