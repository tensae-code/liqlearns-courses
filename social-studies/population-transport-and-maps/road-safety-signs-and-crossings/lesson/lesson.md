# Road safety — signs and crossings

## Do it first

You are standing at a crossing. The light facing the cars is one colour; the little figure facing you is another. Which one is talking to YOU? Sort that out in your head before you read on — crossing on the wrong signal is the mistake this lesson exists to prevent.

## The crossing's language

A crossing speaks in signs. The **traffic light** commands the vehicles — and tells you when the road is being held for you. The **white zebra stripes** painted across the road mark the walker's path: cross on the stripes, where drivers expect you.

[Picture 11087 — a zebra crossing with the red standing and green walking figures]

Above all, the **pedestrian lights** speak directly to the walker, in figures, not words. A **red standing figure** means **do not cross** — the figure stands still, and so do you. A **green walking figure** means **cross — quickly**: the figure walks, and so do you, without lingering.

[GIF 11088 — the pedestrian light changing from red to green and figures crossing]

Watch the change once more: red — stand. Green — walk, quickly. That is the whole language, and it can save your life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what the red standing figure and the green walking figure each command, and what the zebra stripes are for.

## Do It Yourself

1. At a real crossing (with an adult if you are young), wait for the green walking figure before you cross — once, deliberately.
2. Draw the two pedestrian figures from memory and write each one's command under it.
3. Count the zebra crossings on your school route and note which have pedestrian lights.
