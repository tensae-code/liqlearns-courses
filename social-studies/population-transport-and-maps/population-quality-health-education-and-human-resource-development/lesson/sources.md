**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Indonesia, Nepal, India).**

India's own way (India Grade 8 Resources and Development): Economic geography is taught through Indian anchors — a 1.5-hectare Uttar Pradesh farm set against a 250-hectare Iowa farm, Jamshedpur steel, Ahmedabad as the "Manchester of India", and cooperative dairies (Amul/AMUL pattern, Sudha) as an ownership form.

India's own way (India Grade 9 Economics): The whole first Economics course is built on Indian cases — a fictional UP village for production, two boys (Sakal who studies, Vilas who cannot) for human capital, Tendulkar poverty-line numbers, and the FCI/PDS machinery for food security.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 6 Social Studies & Human Value Education G6): the unit opens with two entrepreneur stories — a Chepang beekeeper of Makwanpur who began with Rs. 1,000 in 2064 BS and now sells 15–20 tons of honey a year, and Magar homestays whose community fund keeps 10% of income — then defines the concept from them: economic activities are the means and sources of income.

Indonesia's own way (Grade 11): the whole book is organised around Indonesia itself — its strategic position first, then its own biodiversity (Wallace/Wallacea), its own environment-and-population problems, and its own disaster map; every concept is taught through Indonesian examples, maps and infographics, with a chapter-end knowledge assessment (Asesmen) per chapter.
