# Making maps with GIS

## Do it first

With data gathered and generalised, the GIS earns its name: it answers location questions no single layer could answer alone.

## Sub lesson 1 — Analysis works the layers

GIS analysis works the layers — querying, combining and analysing spatial data to answer location questions. Which villages lie inside the flood zone and within reach of the clinic? No paper map of rivers, roads or clinics alone can say; the layers, questioned together, can. Analysis is where stored geography becomes decisions.

## Sub lesson 2 — The finished products

The finished products: statistical diagrams built from generated data, and maps made and interpreted of the three working kinds — administrative maps (boundaries and seats of government), topographic maps (the land's shape and features) and watershed maps (where the water gathers and drains). A map made with GIS is not the end of the work; it is the argument the work makes, drawn.
