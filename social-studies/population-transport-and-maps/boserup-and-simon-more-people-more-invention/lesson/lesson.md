# Boserup and Simon — more people, more invention?

## Do it first

A village doubles in size on the same land. Malthus predicts disaster. Write one way the village could instead end up feeding itself better than before. Boserup's whole theory lives in your answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Boserup — pressure as inventor

Ester Boserup, a Danish economist, inverted Malthus from field evidence in 'The Conditions of Agricultural Growth' (1965): population growth is the driver that pushes farming to intensify and innovate. Growth leads to development rather than blocking it — more mouths force better methods.

Her critics answer in kind: her economics are thin; food shortage more often triggers out-migration than innovation; diet quality is ignored; and adaptation can lag or turn to damaging practices. Boserup herself conceded that overpopulation can drive unsuitable farming.

## Sub lesson 2 — Simon — the ultimate resource

Julian Simon completes the optimist bench. Resources are not fixedly finite, he argues: human ingenuity expands supply — finding, substituting, recycling, inventing.

On that view, world food prospects defy the doom predictions. Set the three in a row and the unit's debate is complete: Malthus — people outrun food; Boserup — people invent their way out; Simon — invention expands the very idea of a resource.
