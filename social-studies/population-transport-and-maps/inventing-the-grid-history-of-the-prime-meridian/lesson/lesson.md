## Sub lesson 1 — Inventing the grid — the history of the prime meridian

Haiti's Grade 7 sequence teaches how the grid was invented, not just used: the international conventions that fixed an origin meridian, the older île de Fer origin that came before it, and where the very words latitude and longitude come from.

## Sub lesson 2 — Haiti's way — conventions agreed, not found

This is a teacher sequence, written for the teacher, not handed to the pupil. In Haiti's Projet Nectar sequence the grid's history sits inside learning to orient yourself on the Earth — after parallels, meridians and coordinates, the class stops to ask who decided where counting begins, and why everyone had to agree.

Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography opens with representing the Earth itself — globe-to-flat projection problems, orientation practised on the ground with compass/GPS/Google Maps, and time-zone puzzles worked on Haiti's own links (midday shared with Québec, 18h in Paris, Port-au-Prince–Paris flights); every séance ends in a croquis, a glossary and field observation. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
