Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 8 Social Science): Year 8 turns fully outward — "Exploring the World": world climate zones read from Fiji's tropical position, then global warming, resource management (including money and time) and globalisation from a Fiji/Pacific viewpoint.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 8 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): the local-to-global ladder completes — after province (G5), nation (G7), Grade 8's Environment and Resources strand is global: global climate, global settlement and population, global farming, urbanization and logging impacts, global hazards and disaster resilience. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 8 UG S1 Geography Student): Competency-based lower-secondary course taught entirely through East Africa: every skill (maps, fieldwork, photographs) is practised on Ugandan/East African places (Tororo topographic map, Uganda lat/long map), and the physical half is East Africa's relief, landform formation, climate and vegetation rather than world coverage.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 6 SD Geography 6): A whole-Sudan book in three units — its natural environments (desert to rich savanna, plus the Red Sea coast), its productive resources taught environment by environment (farming, herding with the transhumance year of the Kabbabish and Kawahla, industry, mining), then its human geography: population, public services and its important cities one by one.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 6 Social Studies G6): Grade 6 Social Studies is organised as a regional tour of the Arab world in Africa (Nile Valley, Horn of Africa, Libya, Maghreb); every region gets the same pair of Geography lessons — relief and environments first, then natural and human resources with distribution maps — before its History lessons, and every lesson opens with a map-reading task ("read the map, then try to…").

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 7 G7 Geography Humanidad): a Geohistoria (Ramón Tovar) approach — geographic space is always explained through the historical conditions that built it; every chapter opens with a literary epigraph, works through "Entérate / Entretejiendo saberes / Indagando" boxes, and keeps returning to the learner's own community and to Caracas as the worked example.

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 9 G9 Geography Venezuela): a full national geography written as Geohistoria — every physical topic (position, climate, relief, hydrography) is paired with the historical stages that organised the same space (agrarian Venezuela, oil Venezuela, 21st-century Venezuela), and the Amazon rainforest closes the book as a defence-of-life argument.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 8 G8 English): a four-unit arc — Solar System, Earth's uniqueness, then regional geography taught as six South Asian "landscapes" (mountain, valley, coastal, rural, industrial-urban, desert), closing with Sri Lanka's own 1:50,000 topographic-sheet system (92 sheets) that Grade 9 continues.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 6 G6 Tamil 2007): dialogue-driven lessons (teacher–pupil conversations) that open with Sri Lanka's location and a comparison with the world's other islands, then work through the home and its surroundings, caring for that environment, and finish with map-making and scale.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka G12 Tamil SB Part I): the old A/L course opens with a full astronomy block (universe, solar system, origin theories, telescopes, the space age) before turning to the Earth, then teaches geography as man-and-environment study in the Brunhes / Vidal de la Blache tradition (the bibliography names them), and closes with map interpretation on a Sri Lanka topographic sheet.
