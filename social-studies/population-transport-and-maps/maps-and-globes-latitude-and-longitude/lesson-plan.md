# Lesson plan — Maps and globes — latitude and longitude

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Maps and globes — latitude and longitude
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the line grid that gives every place its absolute location — parallels and meridians, the equator and the prime meridian, and the 23½° lines
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the globe and map lessons before it in this title.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Parallels and meridians** — the two families of lines — side-to-side and top-to-bottom — and what together they give: absolute location.
2. **The named lines and the limits** — the equator, the prime meridian, latitude to 90° and longitude to 180°, the tropics at 23½° — and meridians serving time.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Maps and globes — globe or map? Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell parallels from meridians by their run.
2. Say what the two families give together: absolute location.
3. Name the equator and the prime meridian and the degree limits.
4. Name the 23½° lines, Cancer and Capricorn.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.
