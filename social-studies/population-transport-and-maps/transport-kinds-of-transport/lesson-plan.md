# Lesson plan — Transport — kinds of transport

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Transport — kinds of transport
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the ways people and goods move — from walking and pack animals to land, water and air — and what each mode carries best
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the population lessons before it in this title — people counted and placed, now on the move.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **How transport began (teacher guide — evidence)** — walking first, then animals, then the wooden cart — the development arc the guide opens with.
2. **Carriage in Ethiopia — the traditional ways** — donkey, mule, horse and camel carrying goods and people between countryside and town.
3. **The three modern modes** — land (vehicles, rail), water (ships and boats) and air — and the loads each one is fit for.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Population — distribution. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell transport's beginning: walking, animals, the cart (teacher guide — evidence).
2. Name Ethiopia's traditional carriers.
3. Name the three modern modes and the load each fits.
4. Say why water is cheapest for heavy goods.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.
