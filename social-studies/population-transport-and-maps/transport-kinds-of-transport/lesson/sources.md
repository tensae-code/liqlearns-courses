Pairing warning (honesty): Book 2 is an Addis Ababa bureau guide in English; Book 1 is an Amhara bureau student book in Amharic. They are NOT edition-matched companions — the guide's unit order differs and whole student topics (maps, globes, GPS) have no guide counterpart, so no guide coverage is claimed for them. Guide material is used only as labelled teacher evidence.

Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Ecuador's own way (Grade 3 book): the older SM edition is a classic skills-first book — a whole opening unit on orientation, cardinal points, the compass rose and plans before any content; every unit runs the same skeleton (content lessons, “Ser Estudiante” test, Cultura del Buen Vivir, scientific work, digital skills, unit evaluation), and the country is learned parish → canton → province → nation. That way is stocked here as the way Ecuador teaches this thing.

Searchable tags: transport, donkey, mule, camel, land transport, water transport, air transport, crude oil, perishable goods, Guatemala, Ecuador

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7 Term 2): Term 2 turns from Africa's nature to its people and economy — population growth worked from real birth/death-rate charts (1970–2023) with illegal migration treated through Egypt's own 2016 anti-smuggling law, then Africa's economic activities and the practical machinery of integration (trans-African highways, the AfCFTA, chambers of commerce between Egypt and African states).

Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Zimbabwe's own way (ECD A) (teacher guide — evidence): Heritage-Based Curriculum organised by cross-cutting topics repeated at every level (Identity, National history, Heritage, Work and leisure, Transport and communication, Shelter, Global issues…); teaching is play-, song- and role-play-based with workbook colouring/cutting tasks; local examples throughout (totems, pada hopscotch, veld fires, Zimbabwe outline map). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (ECD B) (teacher guide — evidence): Same HBC topic spiral as ECD A, one step deeper — transport starts from traditional/indigenous means (walking, animal-drawn) before modern vehicles; heritage sorted hands-on into animals / plants (baobab, maize) / artefacts (ngoma, hosho, mbira). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 1) (teacher guide — evidence): From Grade 1 the guide is unit-numbered (41 units across the same 14 HBC topics) with suggested answers to the Learner's Book; geography grows outward from home/school — my country and its ethnic groups and neighbours (South Africa, Zambia, Botswana), then transport, shelter, and Zimbabwe-specific hazards (veldfires, water disasters). That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 4) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 4 heritage turns practical — Indigenous Knowledge Systems (aloe, mubvamaropa, moringa) and conservation of natural resources (gully filling, afforestation); transport names Zimbabwe's major roads and railways and their economic importance; shelter compares indigenous types (tsapi granary) with shelters outside Zimbabwe (igloo, pyramid) and the materials and climate factors behind them. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
