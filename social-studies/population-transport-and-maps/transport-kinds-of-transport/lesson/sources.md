Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies SB): the P6 pupil's book is the fullest Geography book of the set — a whole unit on direction and location works Rwanda onto the East African and African maps by latitude and longitude and time zones, then physical features and climate, and a climate-change sequence (types, change, effects, overcoming) sits inside it; regional co-operation is taught bloc by bloc (COMESA, CEPGL, ECCAS, AU, NEPAD).

Uzbekistan's own way (Uzbekistan Grade 9 Economic and Social Geography of the World): Grade 9 turns from physical to economic and social geography of the whole world: Part I works through the political map, world natural resources, world population, the scientific-technical revolution and the branches of the world economy; Part II then reviews every region in a fixed country-profile pattern (territory and position, natural resources, population, economy, transport, foreign ties), with Central Asia and Uzbekistan's neighbours given the fullest treatment, and closes with the global problems of humanity.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 9 Geografija 9): Grade 9 turns home — the whole book is Slovenia itself: first the country as a system (position and borders, relief and rocks, climate/vegetation/waters, settlement, population, settlements, economy, transport, environment), then its five natural regions one by one (Alpine, Pre-Alpine with the Ljubljana basin, Dinaric-Karst, Mediterranean, Pannonian). Named, not placed: the EU unit's history of the European Union and Slovenia-in-EU pros/cons (printed p. 7, Civics/History) and the state symbols inside the opening unit (p. 1, Civics).

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 10 Geografija 1 (gimnazija)): the gimnazija first year is systematic physical geography first (Earth's interior, rocks, landforms, weather and climate, soils, vegetation, waters), then human geography (population, settlements, farming, industry and energy, transport, tourism), closing on sustainable development and spatial planning — with Slovenian cases (earthquake risk in Slovenia, the Port of Koper) set inside the world topics.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 3 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): Social Science is organised in five strands (Community and Organization; Trade; Culture and Society; Environment and Resources; History and Origins) taught through guided lessons that start outdoors in the school grounds — children observe, draw and label the features, resources and maps of their own local community first. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 10 UG S3 Geography Home-Study): COVID-era self-study booklet: first half is East Africa's human geography (towns/ports, transport, trade) plus heavy practical map-work and photograph interpretation on Ugandan survey-map extracts (Tororo, Kakuto, Samia); second half is a "studies in development" comparison — the Rhinelands and landlocked Switzerland held up as models against East Africa.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 4 UG P4 SST Home-Study): Social Studies organised entirely around "our district": the child's own district's physical features, vegetation, settlement and work are the geography content. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Term Two social activities and ceremonies in the district (printed pp. 17–19); Term Three — Our Leaders in the District, Children's Rights, Responsibilities of People in Our District, Social Services in Our District and caring for social service centres (Civics, printed pp. 20–35).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG P6 SST Home-Study): East Africa is the unit of study throughout: population, resources, crops, mining, lakes and transport are all taught at East African Community scale with sketch maps learners draw themselves. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: The Cradle of the Human Race in East Africa and sources of history (History, printed pp. 1–6); ethnic groups and migrations — Bantu, Bachwezi (History, pp. 7–16); early kingdoms, chiefdoms and clan systems (History, pp. 11–23); culture and religions in East Africa (pp. 24–28); The Road to Independence and explorers/missionaries/colonialists (History, pp. 48–52); electoral process and democracy (Civics, pp. 53–54); modern communication gadgets (pp. 44–46, named here).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 12 SD Geography 12): The final-year book is organised as applied geography: it opens with a full revision exercise on a Port Sudan contour map (contours, scale, cross-sections, watershed lines), then remote sensing and photo interpretation, then world economic geography, and closes on human–environment global problems, repeatedly pulling Sudanese examples (Gezira scheme, gum arabic, Sudanese oil, farming systems of Sudan) into the world treatment.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 7 SD Geography 7): A continents book: after a world opener (continents, oceans, relief, world environments), each continent — Africa, Asia, Europe — gets the same treatment in the same order (relief, drainage, climate, vegetation, population, economic activity, environmental problems), closing with a British Isles case study; Sudan and Africa anchor the examples throughout.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 12 Geography G12-Literary): the final-year Literary-track book is a three-scale spiral — the Arab homeland as one integrated natural unit, then Syria itself at the same two levels (nature/resources, then human/economic life), then the world split into developing and developed with Japan, India and Senegal as the three case studies; every unit works from maps, statistical tables and graphs, and the water pair of lessons (scarcity, then "a looming crisis and future conflict") is the book's signature treatment.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 9 Geography G9): every unit is taught twice at two scales — the Arab homeland first, then Syria — with parallel lesson pairs (relief, climate, water, soil, agriculture, industry, transport, tourism); Unit 3's framing is pointed: "huge human resources… and weak investment", and the subsoil lessons are titled "hidden treasures".

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 4 Social Studies G4): the whole primary book is one-country geography of Syria — nature, then life (Badia, countryside, city), then work (agriculture, industry, trade, tourism) — with map tools (map symbols, four directions) and a tree-cutting/environment strand built into the opening unit's goals.
