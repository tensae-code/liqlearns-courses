# Transport — kinds of transport

## Do it first

Watch the road outside for one minute and list everything that carries something — feet, hooves, wheels, wings. This lesson sorts that living list into kinds, from the oldest carrier of all — your own feet — to the aeroplane.

## Sub lesson 1 — How transport began (teacher guide — evidence)

Transport has a history (teacher guide — evidence). At first there was **no means of transport other than walking**. Gradually people used **animals** to travel and to carry loads on land — and invented the **wooden cart**, wheels under the load.

[Picture 11102 — transport history: walker, pack animal, wooden cart, modern vehicles]

Every mode you will meet in this lesson stands on that arc: feet, animals, wheels — then engines.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carriage in Ethiopia — the traditional ways

Transport means moving **goods and people**, and in Ethiopia you see it daily between countryside and town — **rural ↔ urban**. The traditional carriers are still at work: **donkey, mule, horse and camel**, each fitted to its terrain — the camel to the dry lowlands, the mule to the steep highland paths.

## Sub lesson 3 — The three modern modes

The three modern modes:

[Picture 11084 — five ways to carry: donkey, truck, train, ship, aeroplane]

**Land** — vehicles on roads and trains on rail. **Water** — ships and boats: the **cheapest way to move heavy goods**, like crude oil — a ship floats its load instead of lifting it, and water offers little resistance, so one ship carries what hundreds of trucks carry. **Air** — aeroplanes: **fast**, and therefore fit for **costly and perishable goods** — gold, diamonds, vegetables and fruit that would spoil on a slow journey.

The guide adds transport's wider gift (teacher guide — evidence): good transport spreads the benefits of **health care and education**, not only trade.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the transport arc — walking, animals, cart — then name the three modern modes and one load each is fit for.

## Do It Yourself

1. Count the modes passing your home or school in ten minutes; sort them traditional / modern.
2. Pick the best mode for: crude oil, fresh vegetables for export, grain to the next town. Say why each time.
3. Walk a load the old way — carry something home on foot — and note what the cart or vehicle saves.
