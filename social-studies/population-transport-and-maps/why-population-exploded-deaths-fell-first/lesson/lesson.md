# Why population exploded — deaths fell first

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: the explosion was not more births — so what changed? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Farms, then revolutions

Agriculture's surpluses let communities settle and specialise. The industrial and agricultural revolutions then raised food output and nutrition, and population climbed.

[Picture 27043 — a three-step staircase — farms, factories, clinics — with the population curve steepening at each step]

## Sub lesson 2 — The great fall in deaths

Above all, death rates fell on medical knowledge and public health. People did not suddenly have more babies — they stopped dying so young, and the numbers compounded.

## Sub lesson 3 — What holds fertility up or down

Against control stand illiteracy and rural resistance to family planning. Fertility itself answers to marriage age, to contraception — the 'reproductive revolution' of modern methods — and to breastfeeding's natural spacing of births.
