# GIS — maps that think

## Do it first

A paper map shows. A GIS answers: it holds the world's data in layers a computer can question. Start with what it does.

## Sub lesson 1 — A system, not just a map

A Geographical Information System captures, stores, manipulates, analyses, manages and presents geographic (spatial) data on a computer. It stands on five components — data, software (the GIS application), hardware, people and methods. Remove the people or the methods and the rest is an expensive drawing; the system is the five together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Data — gathered or borrowed, always abstracted

Primary data are gathered directly — field survey and remote-sensing imagery. Secondary data are made by others — paper maps and existing digital datasets. Handling them starts with abstraction: choosing levels of measurement — nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio — to symbolise the world in the machine. A town becomes a point with a name (nominal) and a population (ratio); the choices made here decide everything the GIS can later say.
