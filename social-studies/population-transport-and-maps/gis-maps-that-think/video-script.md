# Video script — GIS — maps that think

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A Geographical Information System captures, stores, manipulates, analyses, manages and presents geographic (spatial) data on a computer. It stands on five components — data, software (the GIS application), hardware, people and methods. Remove the people or the methods and the rest is an expensive drawing; the system is the five together. Primary data are gathered directly — field survey and remote-sensing imagery. Secondary data are made by others — paper maps and existing digital datasets. Handling them starts with abstraction: choosing levels of measurement — nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio — to symbolise the world in the machine. A town becomes a point with a name (nominal) and a population (ratio); the choices made here decide everything the GIS can later say.
