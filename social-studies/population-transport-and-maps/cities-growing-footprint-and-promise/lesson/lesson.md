# Cities growing — footprint and promise

## Do it first

Before you read, picture the edge of a growing town you know: what stood there ten years ago — fields, trees, a marsh? And what stands there now? Hold both pictures. This lesson reads that change.

## Sub lesson 1 — What urbanisation is

Urbanisation is the shift of population from rural to urban life, and society's adaptation to that shift. It is not only more city people: it is a whole way of living moving — work, housing, food and travel all change their shape.

[Picture 21961 — a city edge expanding into farmland, with a well-managed city inset]

## Sub lesson 2 — The footprint — land eaten, air and water burdened

Growing cities expand outward into forests, wetlands and farmland. Habitat is cleared and fragmented at the edge, while consumptive urban lifestyles raise air, water and soil pollution inside. That double mark — land consumed at the edge, pollution raised at the centre — is the city's footprint.

[GIF 21977 — a city's outline expanding year by year over farmland]

## Sub lesson 3 — The promise — economies of scale

The same concentration that burdens also serves: well-managed cities bring economies of scale — services, water, power, schools and hospitals reaching many people cheaply because they live close — and development benefits that scattered settlement cannot match. The lesson's balance is deliberate: footprint and promise are both true.
