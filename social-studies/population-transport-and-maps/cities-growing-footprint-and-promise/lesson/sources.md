**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Bangladesh, Turkmenistan, North Macedonia, Cambodia, Fiji).**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. PAIRING WARNING carried from the placements: the Grade 10 teacher guide has no separate section for the student book's §7.3 and allots Unit 7 only five periods overall — the tightest in the book; the student book's fuller treatment governs here and the guide is cited as evidence only.

Searchable tags: urbanisation, footprint, habitat fragmentation, economies of scale, ethiopia, social studies, population transport and maps

Bangladesh's own way (Grades 9-10): the shared SSC book's big chapter "Living Together Safely" is a genuine geography course inside the combined book — land-use change read off 1988-vs-2017 Khulna maps, world population and density, remote sensing and GIS with its five components, settlement and urbanisation, desertification, and Bangladesh's own squeeze of limited land, dense population and climate risk.

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.

North Macedonia's own way (Grade 10 mk-g10): the gymnasium course restarts Geography as a systems science — Theme 1 works through the four spheres (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) lesson by lesson, then natural disasters by sphere; Theme 2 turns to people in geographical space (world urbanisation, energy, farming, industry, transport and trade, tourism, environmental impact); Theme 3 treats the world as one global system (globalisation, transport corridors and sea straits/canals, global networks and navigation systems, international organisations, global challenges and climate-change solutions). 43 numbered lessons.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 7 Social Studies): from Grade 7 the combined Social Studies book splits into four named subject sections; its Geography section teaches people-in-environments — hot zones, deserts and oases, polar peoples, climate zones — through world case studies (Kenya farmers, Amazon, Inuit, Sahara oil) before turning to world population.

Cambodia's own way (Cambodia Grade 11 Geography): the standalone upper-secondary Geography book is a human-impact course — people reshaping the earth, people destroying natural resources, the sea, pollution of air/water/noise/waste, economic globalisation, city and countryside, and a world of uneven development.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 13 Geography): The final year integrates everything at advanced level (plate tectonics to urban models, largely from world texts such as Waugh) and then lands it in Chapter 8, South Pacific Islands Geography — Oceania's geology, island types, development prospects and regionalism.
