# What landlocked costs — Ethiopia’s road to the sea

## Do it first

Every coffee sack Ethiopia exports and every fuel tanker it imports must cross someone else’s country. Read what being landlocked costs, and the road that pays it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The landlocked fact

Ethiopia is landlocked — it has no seacoast of its own. It is bordered by Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, Kenya, South Sudan and Sudan, and lies near the Red Sea without touching it. For trade, that position has consequences: imports and exports must travel through a neighbour’s territory to a foreign port, paying the time and cost that crossing adds.

## Sub lesson 2 — The road and rail to Djibouti

Ethiopia’s answer is the corridor to Djibouti: the Addis–Djibouti road and the Ethio-Djibouti railway carry the bulk of the country’s foreign trade to the port. The railway — the Addis–Djibouti line — is the modern steel version of the same road to the sea.
