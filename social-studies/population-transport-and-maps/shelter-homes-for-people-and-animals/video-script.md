Zimbabwe's own way (ECD A): Heritage-Based Curriculum organised by cross-cutting topics repeated at every level (Identity, National history, Heritage, Work and leisure, Transport and communication, Shelter, Global issues…); teaching is play-, song- and role-play-based with workbook colouring/cutting tasks; local examples throughout (totems, pada hopscotch, veld fires, Zimbabwe outline map). Zimbabwe's own way (ECD B): Same HBC topic spiral as ECD A, one step deeper — transport starts from traditional/indigenous means (walking, animal-drawn) before modern vehicles; heritage sorted hands-on into animals / plants (baobab, maize) / artefacts (ngoma, hosho, mbira). Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 1): From Grade 1 the guide is unit-numbered (41 units across the same 14 HBC topics) with suggested answers to the Learner's Book; geography grows outward from home/school — my country and its ethnic groups and neighbours (South Africa, Zambia, Botswana), then transport, shelter, and Zimbabwe-specific hazards (veldfires, water disasters). Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 2): The spiral widens from family to community — ethnic groups and languages of Zimbabwe (Kalanga, Sotho and others), production in the community, shelter types in the neighbourhood (granary, silos, community hall, clinic), and global issues introduced as floods and deforestation. Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 5): Grade 5 transport is taught as a landlocked country's system — goods by rail/road/air/sea, animal transport (riding, leading, herding), and the roadports, seaports and airports Zimbabwe depends on; shelter links heritage sites (Matobo Hills) and map-drawing of Zimbabwe to building safely in disaster-prone areas. Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 7): Grade 7 turns analytical — factors behind transport-mode choice, up-to-date maps/charts (routes, contours, scale-and-distance work) for domestic, regional and international travel, transport hazards from floods/fog/earthquakes, shelter shortages and siting factors, and desertification with land rehabilitation (afforestation, gully filling). Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 3): Grade 3 transport opens with the relationship between population and transport systems, then road accidents, pedestrian and passenger safety; global issues are drought, afforestation and reforestation. Contents numbering jumps from Unit 23 to Unit 25 (no Unit 24 listed). Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 4): Grade 4 heritage turns practical — Indigenous Knowledge Systems (aloe, mubvamaropa, moringa) and conservation of natural resources (gully filling, afforestation); transport names Zimbabwe's major roads and railways and their economic importance; shelter compares indigenous types (tsapi granary) with shelters outside Zimbabwe (igloo, pyramid) and the materials and climate factors behind them.
