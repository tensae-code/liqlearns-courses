Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Geography opens with representing the Earth itself — globe-to-flat projection problems, orientation practised on the ground with compass/GPS/Google Maps, and time-zone puzzles worked on Haiti's own links (midday shared with Québec, 18h in Paris, Port-au-Prince–Paris flights); every séance ends in a croquis, a glossary and field observation. That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. The student book's body numbering for Unit Two drifts from its own contents (regions printed as 2.4, factors as 2.5, local classification as 2.6, where the contents and the teacher guide's contents agree on 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5); this lesson follows the contents numbering and teaches no misprint.

Bangladesh's own way (Grades 9-10): the shared SSC book's big chapter "Living Together Safely" is a genuine geography course inside the combined book — land-use change read off 1988-vs-2017 Khulna maps, world population and density, remote sensing and GIS with its five components, settlement and urbanisation, desertification, and Bangladesh's own squeeze of limited land, dense population and climate risk.

Egypt's own way (Grade 11): a whole secondary book on development geography alone, built as concept → resources → economy → people: the idea of development traced as it widened (economic, then human, then sustainable development, with its principles of comprehensiveness, integration and sustainability), resources always paired with how to manage them, and the human-development unit closing with full worked country models (Malaysia profiled in depth — position, poverty fall, education, health, electronics and car industries — and Brazil) before the future of development in the Arab homeland.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 10 SD Geography 10): A second-edition foundations book (the preface says necessity forced this 2nd edition): physical geography first (universe to rivers), then climate and vegetation, then a long practical cartography unit, closing with GIS — geography taught as a skills subject, with an activity (نشاط) box on most spreads.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 7 Geography G7 عالم الجغرافية): a pure Geography book built entirely around activity lessons with story-style titles ("Mother of sciences", "Stones from the sky", "The origami art" for folding, "A tourist in my homeland"); technology first — aerial photos, GPS, remote sensing and GIS open the book before any physical geography — and each semester closes with student/teacher-designed lessons and an end-of-semester project.

Afghanistan's own way (Afghanistan Grade 10 Geography (Dari)): the upper-secondary pattern changes from continent tours to themes studied in depth through Afghanistan itself: a full map-skills chapter (map types, scale, colour, projection, satellite photos), Afghanistan's climate and each of its river basins one by one (Amu, Kabul, Helmand–Sistan, Harirud, the closed basins), an environment-protection chapter aimed at Kabul's air and streets, a natural-hazards chapter, then earth-and-space and atmosphere chapters.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 6 Geography 6): world physical Geography taught through Pakistan's own land — its fault lines and 1935/2005 earthquakes, Khewra salt and Thar coal among the rocks, K2 and the Karakoram among the mountains, Pakistan's four climatic regions, its six forest types from mangrove to alpine, and the Indus flood as the country's defining disaster.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 10 LY G10 Principles): Secondary geography opens as a two-part systematic course — physical geography first (astronomy, surface-shaping factors, climate, vegetation and wild animals), then human geography (population and settlement, economic resources) — and closes with a whole book on spatial-data technology: remote sensing, GPS and GIS, then the history of maps from Babylon and Ibn Hawqal.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 11 G11 Geography): a human geography of the world year — political map and state typology, natural resources by sphere, population and settlements, then the world economy branch by branch (agriculture, each industry, services, transport, tourism), closing with economic-geographic characterisations of 14 states on every continent (Egypt among them) written to one template, plus case studies including labour migration at local/regional/global scale and using Google Maps/Google Earth as geo-information portals.
