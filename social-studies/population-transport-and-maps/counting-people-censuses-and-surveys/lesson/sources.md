Bolivia's own way (Grade 8, Secundaria 2): Secundaria 2 is the colonial-history year, so geography enters through the land itself: the Tawantinsuyo's territorial division into suyos, the Amazonian hydraulic cultures who farmed a flooding landscape with raised beds and purified water, the ayllu-versus-hacienda struggle over communal land, and where Afro-Bolivian communities settled in the Yungas.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 10, Secundaria 4): Secundaria 4 is the nineteenth-century history year, and its geography is the geography of what Bolivia lost and what it lives from: the Atacama Litoral and the sea it no longer has, Potosí's silver and the tin north, the Amazon rubber boom, and a closing resources unit that runs from natural-resource classification to Vivir Bien and sustainable development.

Bolivia's own way (Grade 12, Sexto Año): The final year opens with the census itself — the censista's work as civic-geographic practice — and later teaches geopolitics critically: Haushofer and geopolitical thought, geographic scales, and natural resources as a product of human knowledge (Sunkel), alongside Bolivia's own resource politics: the Gas War, hydrocarbon nationalisation and the urea plant that industrialises gas.

Bolivia's own way (teacher guide — evidence): The teacher compendium teaches the same four-moment lesson shape (Práctica, Teoría, Valoración, Producción) with QR-linked videos and local openings — a La Paz landslide video opens the geography unit — and it adds two geography units the sampled student books do not carry at those grades: the scientific development of geographic science with cartography and coordinates (year 3), and water as a human right with Andean water management (year 4). That evidence is stocked here as the way Bolivia teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 3): For third graders geography is the home island first: the planet and its components, then the Antilles and the relief of their own country, then countryside versus city, and finally where their own families came from — migration and a family census the children run themselves.

Samoa's own way (Year 10, Book 1): The Geography value of this civics book is its skills spine — graph work and a Samoa census inquiry that teach learners to count and read their own population.

India's own way (Grade 9 Contemporary India-I): From Grade 9 the Geography book is India alone, in depth — Contemporary India-I is the physical book: size and location, physiography built from plate tectonics, drainage, the monsoon system, vegetation and wildlife, and population, each chapter working from India's own maps and census data.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 3 Amasomo Mbonezamubano SB): P3 moves the home ground up one rung of Rwanda's administrative ladder, from the cell to the SECTOR (Umurenge) — and it is here, at age 8–9, that formal map work begins: the sector map of Gikomero, the district map of Gasabo, symbols, scale, and the sectors that share a border with ours.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 5 Social Studies SB): the pupil's book teaches the province through named Rwandan things — the five provinces with Kigali City as the case study, Rwanda's four seasons by their Kinyarwanda names (Umuhindo, Urugaryi, Itumba, Icyi), and the country's two international airports (Kigali and Kamembe) in the transport-places lesson.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 13 Geography): The final year integrates everything at advanced level (plate tectonics to urban models, largely from world texts such as Waugh) and then lands it in Chapter 8, South Pacific Islands Geography — Oceania's geology, island types, development prospects and regionalism.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 11 Geography TG SBC) (teacher guide — evidence): the standards-based senior Geography course keeps the four-unit frame of the Grade 9–10 strand (Geography Skills; People and Places; People and Resources; People and Environment) as a standalone subject — a heavy map-skills unit (grid references, bearings, cross-sections, climate graphs) before climate regions, biomass, oceanography, and the structure of the Earth. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG P5 Merged Home-Study SST): The whole SST course is Uganda itself: locating Uganda and its districts/neighbours, Uganda's physical features, climate and vegetation, then Uganda's population. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Mathematics section (PDF pp. 9–220); English section (PDF pp. 221–274); SST Term Two — The People of the Pre-Colonial Period, ethnic groups, explorers, colonial rule, Legislative Council and the independence struggle (History, SST printed pp. 36–54); SST Term Three — national symbols, democracy, The Government of Uganda, duties of citizens (Civics, SST printed pp. 56–70).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 5 UG Excel P5 SST): A full-year commercial companion to the NCDC syllabus, far more detailed than the home-study booklet: Uganda's districts/regions are listed sub-region by sub-region, and each Uganda topic (physical features, climate, vegetation, resources, population) gets its own full treatment. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 6 The People of Pre-colonial Uganda — origins, ethnic groups, political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 89–117, except its migration and settlement sections, placed below); Topic 7 Foreign Influence — traders, explorers, missionaries, colonial administrators (History, pp. 118–142); Topic 8 How Uganda Became a Nation (History, pp. 143–148); Topic 9 The Road to Independence (History, pp. 149–158); Topic 10 Uganda as an Independent Nation — presidents, national symbols, democracy, elections (Civics, pp. 159–173); Topic 11 The Government of Uganda — levels, organs, constitution, revenue, budgeting, citizens' rights (Civics, pp. 174–193).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 11 SD Geography 11): The human-geography year: population first (races, religions, census, density, dynamics, structure), then a full practical unit on distribution maps and graphs, then regional geography — the world studied continent by continent as regions — and a closing full unit on Sudan itself, natural basis to environmental problems.

Sri Lanka's own way (Sri Lanka Grade 9 G9 English): the student companion to Book 1 — five chapters moving from the whole Asian region down to Sri Lanka's own landscape, its district-by-district development contrasts, environmental balance, and a closing practical chapter reading the 1:50,000 topographic maps.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.
