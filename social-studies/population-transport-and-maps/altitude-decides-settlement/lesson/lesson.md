# Altitude decides settlement

## Do it first

If altitude were the only map, you could still find the people. Read how the height bands divide land and population.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bands of height

A table relates altitude bands to two shares: the share of Ethiopia’s area in each band, and the share of its population. The bands do not match. The middle-high belt — about 1,800 to 2,600 metres — holds a share of people far larger than its share of the land.

[Picture 21037 — altitude bands beside matching population bars]

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the middle heights win

The middle heights win because of the altitude bargain: high enough to escape the worst lowland heat and disease, low enough for reliable rain and farming. Below, heat and disease thin settlement; above, cold and steepness thin it. Altitude, in Ethiopia, decides settlement.
