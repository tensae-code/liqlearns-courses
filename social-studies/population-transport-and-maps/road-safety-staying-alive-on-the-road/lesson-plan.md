# Lesson plan — Road safety — staying alive on the road

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Road safety — staying alive on the road
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what road safety means, why a struck person's chance of living falls once a vehicle passes 30 km/h, and the five pedestrian rules from the teacher guide
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the transport lessons before it in this title — the learner knows the modes; now the danger they carry.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What road safety means — and why speed kills** — the definition, the street photograph judged safe/unsafe, the sample signs, and the 30 km/h line.
2. **Five rules for walkers (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide's keyed pedestrian rules, and who the danger falls on hardest.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Transport — choosing a mode. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define road safety in the book's own words.
2. State the 30 km/h survival point.
3. Judge a street scene safe or unsafe and name the sample signs.
4. Carry the guide's five pedestrian rules (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.
