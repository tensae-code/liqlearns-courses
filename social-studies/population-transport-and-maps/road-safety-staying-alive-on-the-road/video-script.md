# Video script — Road safety — staying alive on the road

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Stand at a safe place by a road and watch. Which walkers are safe, and which are one step from danger? Judge the scene in front of you before the lesson gives you the words for it.

Road safety is the discipline by which people on roads protect themselves from vehicles.

Learn to judge a street the way the book judges its photograph: who is on the road, who is off it, who can be seen, who is crossing with care — and learn the sample road signs the book shows, the road's own language.

And hold this plain fact: once a vehicle passes 30 km/h, a struck person's chance of living falls as speed rises. Speed is not a small difference. Past that line, every extra kilometre per hour takes life away.

The teacher guide adds who the danger falls on and the walker's rules (teacher guide — evidence): more than half of all road traffic deaths and injuries involve vulnerable road users — walkers first among them.

The five keyed pedestrian rules: know your safety signals; take extra care crossing at curves; be careful while crossing; always use sidewalks; never jump over divider railings. Five small habits — together they are the walker's armour.

Tell your companion the definition of road safety, the 30 km/h point, and the five pedestrian rules without looking.
