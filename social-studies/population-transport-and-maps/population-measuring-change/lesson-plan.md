# Lesson plan — Population — measuring change: rates, pyramids and structure

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Population, Transport and Maps · **Lesson:** Population — measuring change: rates, pyramids and structure
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Population, Transport and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the demographer's toolkit — the three components of population change, the rates, population pyramids, the three age groups and the sex ratio
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only, supporting the counting and growth lessons of this title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The three components and the rates** — fertility, mortality and migration — and calculating fertility, mortality, migration and growth rates.
2. **Structure and the pyramid** — population structure, the pyramid shape of developing and developed countries, and how to read one.
3. **Age groups, dependency and the sex ratio** — young 0–14, working 15–64, old 65+; dependent and active shares; the sex ratio — and what shapes fertility.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Population — counting and graphing growth. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three components of population change and which are biological.
2. Say what each rate measures and that all four can be calculated.
3. Read a population pyramid and name the developing-country shape.
4. Give the three age groups and say what the sex ratio counts.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A globe or ball helps where the lesson names one. Nothing else.

1. **Do it first** — A town's population changed by a thousand people this year. A thousand whats — babies born? elders died? families arriving, or leaving? Until you split the change into its parts, the number tells you almost nothing. This lesson is the splitting. (lesson/lesson.md)
