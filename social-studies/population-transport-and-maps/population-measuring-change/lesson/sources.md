Palestine's own way (Grade 9, PS G9P2): the course closes at world scale — the diversity of natural environments, peoples and economies, then the age's challenges (population explosion, famines, poverty) framed as matters for international cooperation.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 12): The final secondary year turns from national history to the country's present-day human geography, studied region by region: 150 years of population growth, accelerated urbanisation, the economy's branches and foreign trade, and the spatial distribution of the population across Cibao, Este and Suroeste, closing on migration and Santo Domingo as a fragmented metropolis.

Colombia's own way (CS_Grado07 Secundaria Activa): Secundaria Activa organises Grade 7 as climate first (with climate change and hydrometeorological threats), then a paired physical-and-human geography of Europe and America — continents taught as physical environments plus population — before the history units.

Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

Eritrea's own way (ER G7 Social Studies): Grade 7 turns fully to skills and Eritrea's land — map scale, area and population arithmetic first, then Eritrea's natural heritage (Red Sea coast, wildlife) and Eritrea's own disaster set (drought, locusts, earthquakes, volcanism) as the environmental core.

Vietnam's own way (Vietnam Grade 10 Địa lí 10): a single Grade 10 course that runs the whole discipline — map craft, the Earth's spheres, then economic geography sector by sector — with Vietnam itself as the standing worked example (its power-plant and rice maps, the Red River's regime at Hanoi, Hanoi's climate chart, Vietnam's GDP structure, its internet and tourism figures) inside world-scale treatment.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 9 SD Geography 9): A single-country book: the whole of Sudan taught in three units — its physical geography, its human geography, then its environmental problems — with map-work activities on Sudan's own maps (states, roads, the Nile) and an infographic "resources of Sudan" spread; the preface warns its statistics change and are only indicators.

Sudan's own way (Sudan Grade 11 SD Geography 11): The human-geography year: population first (races, religions, census, density, dynamics, structure), then a full practical unit on distribution maps and graphs, then regional geography — the world studied continent by continent as regions — and a closing full unit on Sudan itself, natural basis to environmental problems.

Pakistan's own way (Pakistan Grade 5 SS5): the Class 5 Geography chapter (Ch.5, printed pp. 51–85) deepens the Class 4 spiral — same BOLTS, landforms, weather and population topics, but now with scale types, latitude/longitude and time-zone arithmetic, worked population-density calculations, and graph-drawing exercises from real Pakistani city data.

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 11 G11 Geography): a human geography of the world year — political map and state typology, natural resources by sphere, population and settlements, then the world economy branch by branch (agriculture, each industry, services, transport, tourism), closing with economic-geographic characterisations of 14 states on every continent (Egypt among them) written to one template, plus case studies including labour migration at local/regional/global scale and using Google Maps/Google Earth as geo-information portals.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 8 Student Book): widening-circles design — the Americas in depth at secondary level — maps and location first, then the continent's regions, resources, population, migration and demography. Every lesson runs the same 'Sembrar y cosechar' activity cycle.

Honduras's own way (Honduras Grade 8 Teacher Guide) (teacher guide — evidence): widening-circles design — the Americas in depth at secondary level — maps and location first, then the continent's regions, resources, population, migration and demography — the teacher guide mirrors the student book lesson-for-lesson (same 38 lessons and blocks) and adds didactics, theory summaries and answer keys; its geography lessons are placed here at the guide's own printed pages. Every lesson runs the same 'Sembrar y cosechar' activity cycle. That evidence is stocked here as the way Honduras teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Honduras is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 6 np-g6): Grade 6 has a full "Hamro Prithvi" (Our Earth) unit — Earth's introduction, latitude/longitude, outer-layer structure, then Nepal itself: geographical description, climate, rivers/glaciers/lakes, natural vegetation, natural resources and map study, closing with disaster management; a population unit follows (demography, the census). Economic activities are organised by region and resource: agriculture and industry, services and trade, tourism, and economic resources (land and water; forest, minerals and people).

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 9 np-g9): Grade 9 "Hamro Prithvi" widens from Nepal (latitude/longitude, surface form, climate, vegetation, minerals and water resources, map work) to whole continents — Asia, Europe and Australia, each in two lessons (geographical/natural environment, then economic activities and social life); the preface notes this is a model textbook tested in schools under the 2076 secondary curriculum.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 10 np-g10): Grade 10 climate is taught by world climate types and the three heat zones (tropical, temperate, cold), then continent studies of North America, South America and Africa, earthquakes, map work and modern map technology (GIS); population closes the book — Nepal's size/growth, structure, distribution, family planning and the population–environment interrelation.

Nepal's own way (Nepal Grade 10 TG np-g10tg) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide teaches the Grade 10 course of np-g10 lesson for lesson, adding map- and chart-based activities (world climate maps, heat-zone maps, continent maps comparing life and economic activity with Nepal) and earthquake-preparedness practice; placements mirror the student book with guide page references. That evidence is stocked here as the way Nepal teaches this thing; it is cited as evidence only — Nepal is counted in this lesson through its student book(s).

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 10 PESD II Mod 5): population geography taught as a developed-world/developing-world contrast throughout — distribution factors, growth, Malthus and the transition, then the two evolutions (developed countries; the "Terceiro Mundo" set) with their opposite population policies, closing on structures, demographic problems (incl. HIV cutting life expectancy below 50) and population–economy/environment relations.

Jordan's own way (Jordan G12 Geography T1): the final-year Geography book is built in three blocks — the forces that shape Earth's surface, political geography (state power, borders, alliances, Jordan's national security), and population geography.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 6 Social Studies): Grade 6 turns to Bhutan's own climate zones, population and signature environment topics — hydropower, urbanisation and glacial lake outburst floods (GLOF) — a hazard set specific to a Himalayan kingdom.

Bhutan's own way (Bhutan Grade 8 Geography): Class VIII deepens the same three strands with real map-work (scale conversion, grid references, contours and profiles) and Bhutan-specific hazard and resource content, including the Rubesa wind turbine and hydroelectric dams as national resource examples.
