Guatemala's own way: one combined Social Studies and Citizenship book per grade, organised in four units that alternate History of Guatemala/Central America with the physical geography of the isthmus — every map carries the note about the unresolved Belize territorial differendum, and Geography is always worked on maps of the seven Central American countries with Guatemala's own Sierra de las Minas, Motagua river and Maya Biosphere as the home examples. That way is stocked here as the way Guatemala teaches this thing.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 4): From Grade 4 the format changes: instead of the integrated Social Science reader, the child gets a one-topic-per-page infographic textbook keyed spread-by-spread to the Primary Social Studies Curriculum 2022–2027 — Bahamian culture first, then the peoples who settled the islands, then the islands' own geography (southeastern islands, the Bahama Banks, island resources, tourism, transport), closing with leaders, laws, festivals and the bush plants of The Bahamas.

Zimbabwe's own way (Grade 5) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 5 transport is taught as a landlocked country's system — goods by rail/road/air/sea, animal transport (riding, leading, herding), and the roadports, seaports and airports Zimbabwe depends on; shelter links heritage sites (Matobo Hills) and map-drawing of Zimbabwe to building safely in disaster-prone areas. That evidence is stocked here as the way Zimbabwe teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Malaysia's own way (MY T2 Geografi Tingkatan 2): the year pairs map skills (scale, topographic maps) with weather and climate, then transport and telecommunications as the human-geography core, an Asia regional study, and global warming with green technology as the environmental close.

India's own way (Grade 10 Contemporary India-II): Contemporary India-II is the human/economic book, matching Part I's physical book: India's resources, forests, water, agriculture, minerals and energy, manufacturing and the "lifelines" (transport, communication, trade) — taught throughout as development-versus-environment argument, with movements and case studies (Chipko, Narmada Bachao, rooftop rainwater harvesting in the Thar) carrying the criticism.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 8 Geografija 8): Grade 8 is pure world regional geography, continent by continent (Africa, Australia/Oceania, the Americas and polar regions), each region worked through the same grid — position/relief/waters, climate/vegetation, population, economy — and the book closes with two world-problems units (rainforest loss, desert spread, water conflicts, the Aral Sea).

Uganda's own way (Uganda Grade 6 UG Excel P6 SST): The P6 course widens from Uganda to the whole East African Community: location by latitude/longitude, resources, transport and communication are all taught at EAC scale. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Topic 1 — historical background, formation, revival, organs and symbols of the EAC (Civics); The People of East Africa — origin of human beings, sources of history, archaeological sites, Stone Age, ethnic groups and their political/social/economic organisation (History, printed pp. 25–54); Early visitors — Arab traders, long-distance and slave trade, European traders, Christian missionaries (History, pp. 55–64); Topic 4 The Road to Independence in East Africa — explorers, scramble and partition, colonial rule and reactions, independence struggle, post-independence leaders and democratic rule (History/Civics, pp. 140–188).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 3 G3 CS Viva Venezuela): the second half is a state-by-state tour of Venezuela (territorial organisation, Nueva Esparta, Sucre, the Capital, Vargas, Zulia, the Andean states, the Llanos, Guayana) taught through local stories and children of each region — regional geography as a journey around the child's own country.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 6 Social Studies G6 T2): Term 2 turns from the region's nature (Term 1) to its people and its wealth. Unit 1 is History-led — colonialism to independence — with a single Geography lesson on the economic importance of the Gulf's location; Unit 2 (Geography/Civics) covers population growth, the population-structure imbalance and cultural similarity; Unit 3 (Geography) covers the economic resources — energy, agriculture, animal and fish wealth, industry and trade — and the challenges of development; Unit 4 (Civics) covers GCC cooperation. The same architecture as Term 1: a standards page per unit, lessons, a unit project and a full assessment, a values unit ("Stations with Good Kuwait") and a maps appendix. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History — Unit 1 Lesson 2, European colonial rivalry over the Gulf (Portuguese, Dutch, British), printed pp. 21–23; Lesson 3, the 1899 protection agreement between Kuwait and Britain, pp. 24–25; Lesson 4, the independence of Kuwait (19 June 1961) and the Gulf States, pp. 26–28. Civics — Unit 1 Lesson 5, the development of political life in Kuwait: the 1962 constitution, sovereignty, democracy, human rights, women's political rights, pp. 29–31. ICT/digital skills (outside this wave's scope) — Unit 2 Lesson 4, the digital culture of the Gulf person: artificial intelligence and its uses, cybersecurity and Kuwait's national cybersecurity centre, pp. 49–51. Civics/media — Unit 2 Lesson 5, cultural awareness: cultural invasion, social media's benefits and harms, the cybercrime law, pp. 52–53. Civics — Unit 4 Lesson 1, the Gulf Cooperation Council: six member states, founded 25 May 1981 in Abu Dhabi, headquarters Riyadh, pp. 82–83; Lesson 2, joint security and defence cooperation (Peninsula Shield), pp. 84–85; Lesson 4, joint cultural and educational cooperation (Arab Bureau of Education, Riyadh; Joint Programme Production Institution, Kuwait), pp. 89–91; Lesson 5, future aspirations of the GCC states and Kuwait Vision 2035, pp. 92–94. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": Bu Tbeila, the Ramadan drummer of old Kuwait, pp. 100–101; etiquette of sitting in the diwaniya, pp. 102–103; a helping hand and a reassured heart, pp. 104–105; a kind word and a gentle reply, pp. 106–107. The maps appendix (p. 108) is present only as instructions for fitting a transparent sleeve over the appendix maps.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 12 Geography (3)): 'applied' geography organised as three problems of Iranian life — settlements, transport, and natural hazards — each taken in two lessons (what it is, then how to manage it) with a geographical-skills section (GIS, time zones, remote sensing) closing each chapter.

Yemen's own way (Yemen Grade 12 G12 Geography of the Contemporary World): the final year is political and economic geography of the whole contemporary world — how it is classified and developed, its blocs in the developed and developing worlds, transport and communications, world trade and tourism, and its shared problems (energy, pollution, unemployment).

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 7 LY G7 Geography of Libya): A whole book on Libya alone, in four units — physical (location, relief, climate, soil, water, vegetation), human (population and urban centres), economic (farming, herding, fishing, minerals and oil, industry, trade), then tourism and transport, with tourism taught through Libya's historical cities (Jarma of the Garamantes) and oases (Awjila).

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 8 LY G8 Arab World): The scale widens from Libya (Grade 7) to the whole Arab homeland: one regional geography — location and strategic importance, relief, climate, vegetation, population, economic resources and transport — closed by a full unit on the Arab food problem and food security.

Libya's own way (Libya Grade 11 LY G11 Arab World): The Grade 8 Arab World survey returns at secondary depth in two parts — physical (relief treated separately for the African and Asian sides, climate factors and regions, soils, vegetation, water with its problems) and human (population structure and problems, agriculture and its difficulties, crop-by-crop chapters, livestock and sea wealth, minerals metal by metal, oil and gas, alternative energy, industry, transport).

Moldova's own way (Moldova Grade 11 G11 Geography): a human geography of the world year — political map and state typology, natural resources by sphere, population and settlements, then the world economy branch by branch (agriculture, each industry, services, transport, tourism), closing with economic-geographic characterisations of 14 states on every continent (Egypt among them) written to one template, plus case studies including labour migration at local/regional/global scale and using Google Maps/Google Earth as geo-information portals.

Mozambique's own way (Mozambique Grade 7 PESD I Mod 2): human/economic geography organised sector by sector — population first, then each economic activity taught world-then-Mozambique (agriculture, livestock, fishing, forestry, industry, commerce, tourism, transport), with Mozambique's own distribution tables, ports and exports as the local evidence.

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 11 laos-g11-geography-teacher): a world-population and world-countries year — Part I works through world population growth, changing characteristics, distribution, migration, the developed/developing contrast and urban growth; Part II then studies nine countries one by one (USA, Russia, France, Germany, UK, China, India, Japan, South Africa) plus development in Africa, with map plates (the book ends on a South Africa agriculture map, printed p. 140).

Laos's own way (Laos Grade 11 laos-g11-geography-student): the student twin of Book 5 — same 2 parts / 21 lessons (world population, then nine country studies plus development in Africa) with full illustrated text.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies): Grade 9 returns to Iraq for the fullest treatment yet — the same national geography as Grade 5 but measured: the plain is 24% and about 600 km long, the desert plateau about 55%, the desert plant belt about 70%, two-thirds of the people live on the central and southern plain; every topic is pinned to numbered maps of Iraq.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Human Geography): a human-geography course in six chapters — population, cities and countryside, agriculture, industry and mining, transport and trade, political geography and tourism — and every chapter closes with an applied study of Iraq itself, so the world pattern is always brought home.

Cuba's own way (Cuba Grade 8 OM Geografía Económica y Social G8) (teacher guide — evidence): The guide's distinctive Cuban layer is territorial identity — learners characterise their own municipality's resources and the environmental problems of its economic sectors, and the world material (political map, population, resources, production) is always closed by comparison with Cuba. That evidence is stocked here as the way Cuba teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.
