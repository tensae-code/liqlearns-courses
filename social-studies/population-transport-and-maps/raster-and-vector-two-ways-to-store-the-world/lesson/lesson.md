## Sub lesson 1 — Raster — the world as a grid

Raster data is grids of cells, each cell holding a single value. It suits imagery and continuous surfaces: elevation, rainfall and soils, where every place has a reading.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vector — the world as geometry

Vector data is geometry: points for wells, schools and airports; lines and polylines for rivers and roads; polygons for lakes, parks and boundaries. Vector data is stored as shapefiles and feature datasets, and acquired by digitising, scanning, GPS survey and remote sensing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Spatial and attribute

Every feature carries two kinds of data. Spatial: where it is. Attribute: what it is like — a lake's depth, quality and name are the book's example — the two linked at input.
