Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 5 Social Studies): economics first appears inside the geography of Iraq itself, as "human phenomena and economic activity" — farming, industry and transport read off Iraq's own land and rivers.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 6 Social Studies): the whole country is the textbook — every governorate is read the same way (land, water, then its economic activity: farming, industry and mineral wealth, transport, tourism), so a child learns economics as the sum of what each part of Iraq makes and moves.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 9 Social Studies): Grade 9 turns the governorate survey into one national economy — farming, animals, oil and minerals, factories, transport, trade and tourism of Iraq as a single system, with livestock and oil both named as sources of the national income.

Iraq was already counted in this lesson from the earlier waves; the Economics-expansion rows above are stocked as depth for the same country, so the count does not rise.

Iraq's own way (Iraq Grade 12 Human Geography): a human-geography course in six chapters — population, cities and countryside, agriculture, industry and mining, transport and trade, political geography and tourism — and every chapter closes with an applied study of Iraq itself, so the world pattern is always brought home.
