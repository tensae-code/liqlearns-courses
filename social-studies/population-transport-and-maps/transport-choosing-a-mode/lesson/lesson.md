# Transport — choosing a mode

## Do it first

A farmer has grain, a trader has oil, a patient needs a hospital in the city. Each journey wants a different carrier. This lesson is the judgement table that chooses well — fill it in as you read.

## The judgement table

Judge every mode on four counts: **carrying capacity** (how much it holds), **speed**, **cost**, and **fitness for Ethiopia's terrain** — steep highlands, dry lowlands, rivers and plains.

[Picture 11085 — the mode comparison table as a clean grid]

The pack animals hold little but go where no road runs. The car and truck are flexible and quick on roads. The train holds much and runs cheaply per load where rails exist. The ship holds the most, slowest, cheapest per heavy load. The aeroplane is fastest and dearest, holding least per cost.

The teacher guide makes the same judgement in advantages and disadvantages (teacher guide — evidence): **land** — door-to-door service, but high construction cost; **water** — low maintenance cost, but low speed; **air** — fast and suitable, but high cost.

## What transport does for a country

Transport is the thread through **farming** (crops to market, fertiliser to farms), **industry** (raw materials in, goods out) and **trade** (town to town, country to country). Cut the thread and each strangles separately. Finish by naming the **dominant modes in your own area** — and the terrain reason behind them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion which mode wins on capacity, which on speed, which on cost for heavy goods — and which modes dominate your own area, and why.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the comparison table for donkey, car, train, ship and aeroplane from memory.
2. Choose a mode for three loads from your area and defend each choice on the four counts.
3. Name your area's dominant mode and the terrain reason for it.
