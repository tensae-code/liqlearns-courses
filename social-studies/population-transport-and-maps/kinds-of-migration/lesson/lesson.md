# Kinds of migration

## Do it first

Before you read, name every move you know of in your own family — who moved, how far, and did they choose it or were they driven? Keep the list; the lesson sorts every move on it into four boxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Internal and international

Migration is a permanent change of residence. By distance it sorts into internal migration — a move within one country — and international migration — a move across a border into another country. The sorter is the border: crossed, or not.

[Picture 21963 — a two-by-two grid of migration kinds — internal and international against voluntary and forced]

## Sub lesson 2 — Voluntary and forced

By choice it sorts again. Voluntary migration is a move made for better opportunities and a better life. Forced migration is expulsion from home — driven by war, drought, famine, disaster or conflict. Together the two sortings make four kinds, and every real move sits in one of the four.

## Say it to your companion

Explain kinds of migration to your companion in your own words: what it is, the one fact you would never want to forget from it, and one example from your own surroundings or from Ethiopia. Your companion asks you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
