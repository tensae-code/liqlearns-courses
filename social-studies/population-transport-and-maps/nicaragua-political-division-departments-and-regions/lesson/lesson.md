## Sub lesson 2 — Nicaragua's way — the book's own teaching way

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 CCSS Geografía 10): A distance-learning self-study module ("Geografía Dinámica") built entirely around Nicaragua itself — every physical topic (relief, climate, watersheds, coasts) is taught through Nicaraguan regions, and risk/vulnerability (hurricanes, Casita landslide, SINAPRED) closes the book. Non-Geography parts: Unit IV population-policy content (Hambre Cero, Usura Cero, housing projects) is Civics/social policy — named here, not placed beyond its population-geography topics.

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 4 Estudios Sociales G4): Units I–V are a full national Geography of Nicaragua — coordinates, time zones and seasons first, then the country's position, borders, political division and hydrography, and a closing landscape unit on climate, natural resources, relief by region, earthquakes and hurricanes; the second half of the book is national History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit VI first settlers' history and culture (printed pp. 120–137, History, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII European presence and colonisation (printed pp. 140–164, History); Unit VIII Independence and the National War (printed pp. 168–183, History); Unit IX Nicaragua 1857–1909 (printed pp. 186–197, History); Unit X Nicaragua in the 20th century (printed pp. 200–221, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 Estudios Sociales G7 full-year): A full national Geography in five units — territorial patrimony and cartography, physical geography (relief, climate, hydrography, resources), population ("¿Cuántas mujeres, cuántos hombres?"), production, and a whole unit on natural-risk prevention — followed by six units of national History. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I state institutions (printed pp. 14–16, Civics); Unit VI origin societies (printed pp. 128–143, History, except the geographical-setting topic placed below); Unit VII European expansion and the Colony (printed pp. 148–165, History); Unit VIII Independence (printed pp. 170–184, History); Unit IX economic/political evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 190–205, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty and the Somoza period (printed pp. 210–233, History); Unit XI Nicaragua 1979 to the present (printed pp. 244–264, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 7 CCSS Módulo Autoformativo G7): The distance-learning parallel of the Grade 7 full-year book (Book 7): the same five Geography units rewritten for rural self-study, so every topic is anchored in the learner's own comunidad — its soils, its production lands, its road links and its risk zones. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit I government institutions (printed p. 23, Civics); Unit VI first settlers (printed pp. 113–128, History, except the geography-influence topic placed below); Unit VII arrival of the Europeans (printed pp. 131–141, History); Unit VIII independence process (printed pp. 145–154, History); Unit IX evolution 1857–1909 (printed pp. 157–168, History); Unit X defence of sovereignty (printed pp. 173–202, History); Unit XI triumph of the Revolution to the present (printed pp. 205–231, History).

Nicaragua's own way (Nicaragua Grade 10 Ciencias Sociales G10 full-year): The full-year companion to the Grade 10 Geography module (Book 1): Units I–VI repeat and deepen the same national Geography (cartography, relief and geological provinces, climate and watersheds, population, economic sectors, territorial vulnerability and SINAPRED) in classroom form, and the book's second half is a full Economics course. Non-Geography parts named, not placed: Unit IV government anti-poverty programmes (printed p. 106, Civics/social policy); Units VII–XII Economics (printed pp. 156–285): utility of economics and socio-economic formations, what the country produces, price as market regulator, commercial exchange and the banking system, state economic policy, and integration in the face of globalisation. This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
