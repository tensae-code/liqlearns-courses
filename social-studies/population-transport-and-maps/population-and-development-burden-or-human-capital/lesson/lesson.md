# Population and development — burden or human capital?

## Do it first

Is a growing population a burden or a resource? Write your answer with one reason. This lesson's answer is 'both — it depends on one investment'. Find it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Development unpacked

Development is unpacked through its elements — income, employment, education, health and living standards (the book's Figure 4.2) — and measured per capita, person by person. Tables of the richest nations and the least developed countries frame the contrast.

Population growth presses on every component at once: jobs and employment, housing, sanitation and services. In the least developed group, high infant and maternal mortality and inadequate infrastructure mark how hard the pressure falls.

## Sub lesson 2 — Human capital — the counterweight

Against the pressure stands one counterweight: human capital. Investment in education and health builds people's capacity to work, earn and build — turning population from a count of mouths into a stock of skill.

That is the lesson's verdict in its title's question: population is a burden where services and investment fail to keep pace, and an asset where human capital is built. The number alone decides nothing; the investment decides.
