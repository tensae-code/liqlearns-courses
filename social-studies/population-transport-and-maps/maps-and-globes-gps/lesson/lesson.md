# Maps and globes — GPS

## Do it first

The ship in the ocean from an earlier lesson phones again: 'Come and get us!' This time their phone knows where it is — to within a few steps — though no human looked at a chart. How? Three things working together, two of them in space. Read on.

## Position from satellites

The **Global Positioning System (GPS)** is an information-relay system of **satellites, receivers and computer software** that shows **exactly where anything on Earth is**. The satellites circle overhead; the receiver listens to them; the software turns their signals into a position — the latitude-and-longitude address you learned, delivered live.

[Picture 11098 — satellites beaming to a plane, a ship and a hand receiver]

Its services to **flight** are listed: **continuous exact position and weather information; flight safety; fewer delays and less fuel through direct routes; and landing in fog**, when eyes cannot find the runway but signals can.

And ships? Infer it the book's way: everything GPS does for a plane over empty land it does for a ship over empty water — exact position where no landmark exists, safest direct routes, and safe arrival in fog. The ocean ship from our opening story is rescued by exactly this.
