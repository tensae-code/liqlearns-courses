# Migration — moving for work, safety and family

## Do it first

Before you read, think about this: migration does not grow the world's population — so what does it do? Say what you think first, then read on and check yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Redistribution, not growth

Migration moves the balance B−D+I−E between places: one country's loss is another's gain, and the world's total does not change. Its history runs from early hunting-and-gathering moves to the great free migrations out of Europe.

## Sub lesson 2 — Forced and working moves

Today's moves include forced migration from war and disaster, and trafficking. Above all stands temporary labour migration, from poorer to richer economies, driven by push factors: weak jobs, low pay, large populations at home.

## Sub lesson 3 — Senders and receivers

Labour-exporting senders include Bangladesh, Nepal and the Philippines; conflict-driven senders include Syria, Venezuela and Myanmar. Receiver regions take the other end of the same arrows — the map of senders and receivers by decade is the lesson's picture.
