Haiti's own way (teacher sequence — evidence): Population geography is built entirely on Haiti's own data, handled critically: pupils confront the national statistics institute (IHSI) with international sources, note that the last census is 2003, build density maps and age pyramids themselves, then read the whole country through Port-au-Prince's primacy and the diaspora («les Haïtiens de l'extérieur»). That evidence is stocked here as the way Haiti teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

No book error is flagged for this lesson's pages in the placements. The student book's body numbering for Unit Two drifts from its own contents (regions printed as 2.4, factors as 2.5, local classification as 2.6, where the contents and the teacher guide's contents agree on 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5); this lesson follows the contents numbering and teaches no misprint.

Costa Rica's own way: two-thirds contemporary History/geopolitics, then a closing Geography unit on population dynamics and spatial mobility that always pairs the world pattern with the Costa Rican case (Costa Rica's own population change, internal and international mobility). That way is stocked here as the way Costa Rica teaches this thing.

Turkmenistan's own way (Grade 10): A classic post-Soviet economic-and-social geography of the whole world in one book: Part I general world (political map, resources, population, world economy by sector), Part II the same template region by region and country by country, Part III global problems — with Turkmenistan's own memberships and ecological policy woven into the questions.

Dominican Republic's own way (Grade 3): For third graders geography is the home island first: the planet and its components, then the Antilles and the relief of their own country, then countryside versus city, and finally where their own families came from — migration and a family census the children run themselves.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 infographic textbook is history-led — settlers, pirates, wrecking, blockade-running and bootlegging — and its geography is the geography of a small island state among bigger neighbours: where The Bahamas sits and where its people live, the sea's resources (including aragonite mined off Bimini), straw and sisal manufacturing, offshore banking, the Freeport transshipment container port, migration into The Bahamas, and Junkanoo as cultural tourism.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 6): The Grade 6 book is civics-led (government, elections, courts, constitution, finance ministry) but opens the primary course's widest geography: the world's continents and oceans, political and physical maps, global warming and natural disasters, human resources and the job market, worldwide transport and communication through Nassau's ports of entry, and a closing cultural comparison of The Bahamas with Africa, Australia, Brazil and Japan.

Malaysia's own way (MY T4 Geografi Tingkatan 4): upper-secondary Geography is a full topographic-map apprenticeship (bearings, area, cross-sections, landscape reading) followed by process geography — plates, rocks, weathering, mass movement, rivers and coasts — and a world-scale human geography of population, migration, settlements and urbanisation.

Eritrea's own way (ER G6 Social Studies): one integrated Social Studies book per grade weaving History, Geography, Civics and Culture strands; Geography is taught through Eritrea itself first (Eritrea's location and size, Eritrean settlement, migration and disasters) with very practical, activity-led lessons.

United Kingdom's own way: A single exam-board (AQA) GCSE textbook organised as thematic units with numbered two-page spreads, every big topic anchored to a named case study (Typhoon Haiyan, Somerset Levels, Rio de Janeiro, Bristol, Nigeria, the Indus Basin, Lesotho Highlands) and a final unit on issue evaluation, fieldwork enquiries and cartographic/graphical/statistical skills.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies TG) (teacher guide — evidence): P6 widens the frame from the province to the country, then to East Africa and Africa — Rwanda is located by latitude and longitude (1°S–3°S, 28°E–31°E), its landlocked position is taught as the reason for regional co-operation, and every unit is built around a problem statement with the pupil's book activities answered in the guide. That book is a teacher guide, cited as evidence only; Rwanda is counted in this lesson for its student book(s) named here, not for this guide.

Rwanda's own way (Rwanda Grade 6 Social Studies SB): the P6 pupil's book is the fullest Geography book of the set — a whole unit on direction and location works Rwanda onto the East African and African maps by latitude and longitude and time zones, then physical features and climate, and a climate-change sequence (types, change, effects, overcoming) sits inside it; regional co-operation is taught bloc by bloc (COMESA, CEPGL, ECCAS, AU, NEPAD).

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 7 Social Science): Year 7 widens the lens to the whole South Pacific — island types, "islandness" and vulnerability, Pacific hazards, and resources owned by tribes and clans; Fiji is taught as the hub of the South West Pacific.

Fiji's own way (Fiji Year 10 Social Science): Year 10 is the foundation for senior Geography and History — Fiji's own physical geography (tectonics, relief, rivers, climate, soils) taught in full, then its industries, population and urbanisation, all with Fiji data.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 9 Geografija 9): Grade 9 turns home — the whole book is Slovenia itself: first the country as a system (position and borders, relief and rocks, climate/vegetation/waters, settlement, population, settlements, economy, transport, environment), then its five natural regions one by one (Alpine, Pre-Alpine with the Ljubljana basin, Dinaric-Karst, Mediterranean, Pannonian). Named, not placed: the EU unit's history of the European Union and Slovenia-in-EU pros/cons (printed p. 7, Civics/History) and the state symbols inside the opening unit (p. 1, Civics).

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 9 Social Science TG) (teacher guide — evidence): at secondary level Social Science splits into five strands — Geography, History, Political Science, Economics, Environment. The Geography strand is a weather-and-climate skills course (instruments, data, graphs — with Port Moresby and Kokopo data) moving to world climate/vegetation, migration, and land use; the Environment strand carries Earth systems, resources and sustainability. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Papua New Guinea Grade 12 Geography TG SBC) (teacher guide — evidence): Grade 12 Geography closes with global population and mega-cities, a full migration unit, and a seven-topic climate-change unit that ends at home — the impact of climate change in PNG and the regions affected. That evidence is stocked here as the way Papua New Guinea teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 8 Geography G8): Grade 8 leaves the Arab world and tours the "new" continents in a fixed six-lesson pattern (location and surface, natural environments, settlement/migration, population and cities, resources and economy, one big country study — USA, Brazil, Australia), gives Antarctica a whole unit as a land of ice and science, and closes with a unit of contemporary global challenges (water, food, pollution, warming, population, desertification).

Venezuela's own way (Venezuela Grade 6 G6 CS Venezuela y Su Gente): the whole Geography block is one argument — "Nuestro espacio geográfico en tiempos del petróleo": how oil remade population (2011 census), cities (metropolisation), industry location, internal migration and landscapes; learners start by learning how to *look* at a piece of oil-era Venezuela.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 12 geo-6as-cd): A human-geography year in three units — world population (growth, distribution, migration, structure, with an applications lesson on the age pyramid and demographic projections), urbanisation and its problems, and economic activities from traditional farming to modern industry, transport, trade, craft industry and tourism.
