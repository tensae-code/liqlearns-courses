# Turkey's Administrative Units

## Do it first

Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think this is about: Turkey's administrative units — Turkey's administrative divisions and its land and sea neighbours on the Grade 2 end map, and administrative units in Turkey in the Grade 9 book, as Turkey's Grade 2 Hayat Bilgisi end map and Grade 9 Coğrafya teach it. Hold your idea; the lesson will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The end map — Turkey's administrative divisions and its neighbours

Turkey's Grade 2 Hayat Bilgisi book closes with a map: Türkiye'nin Mülki İdare Bölümleri ile Kara ve Deniz Komşuları Haritası — the map of Turkey's administrative divisions together with its land and sea neighbours. A Grade 2 child meets the whole country at the end of the book: Turkey divided into its administrative units, and the neighbours, on land and at sea, that surround it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Administrative units in Turkey — the Grade 9 treatment

Turkey's Grade 9 Coğrafya returns to the same thing at secondary depth: II-Ç Ülkemizde İdari Birimler — administrative units in Turkey — closing the Human Systems unit on settlements. What the Grade 2 child saw as one end-of-book map, the Grade 9 student studies as a topic in its own right.

Turkey's own way: Hayat Bilgisi (Life Studies) carries almost no Geography in this semester book — self/school, health/safety and family/society — except road signs and the two end-of-book maps; the Geography parts only are placed. Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). This lesson teaches the placements' thing in the course's own words — the book's text itself is never transcribed.
