# Malthus — population against food

## Do it first

A household's grain store is fixed but the family keeps growing. Write what must happen eventually if nothing changes. That 'must happen' is Malthus's whole argument — now meet it properly.

## Sub lesson 1 — The argument of 1798

Thomas Malthus (1776–1834) — clergyman, demographer and economist — argued in his 1798 'Essay on the Principle of Population' that population's constant tendency is to outgrow its food supply.

If people multiply faster than food, crisis follows: the Malthusian crisis, in which misery is not an accident but the arithmetic working itself out. Population, unchecked, presses always against the means of feeding it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The checks

Population is held in check two ways. Preventive checks act before the crisis — above all 'moral restraint', people choosing to delay or limit family size.

Positive checks act after: every cause that raises mortality and cuts life short — famine, disease and war are Malthus's grim list. Between restraint beforehand and suffering after, Malthus saw no third road. The rest of this unit argues with him.
