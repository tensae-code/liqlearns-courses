Malthus argued in 1798 that population constantly tends to outgrow its food supply — so crisis checks it, by moral restraint beforehand or by famine, disease and war after. First, the argument of 1798: Thomas Malthus (1776–1834) — clergyman, demographer and economist — argued in his 1798 'Essay on the Principle of Population' that population's constant tendency is to outgrow its food supply. Then, the checks: Population is held in check two ways. Hold those two parts together and you hold the lesson.
