**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Turkey, Palestine, Bangladesh, Egypt, Bahamas, Malaysia, Eritrea, India, Slovenia, Syria, Mauritania, Kuwait, Iran).**

Turkey's own way: a systems course — Natural Systems first (nature–human interaction, coordinates, maps, Earth's motions, climate), then Human Systems (settlements), then Regions and Countries, then Environment and Society — with a Turkey case closing most chapters (Turkey's location, Turkey's climate, settlements in Turkey). That way is stocked here as the way Turkey teaches this thing; it does not change the acceptance count above.

Searchable tags: temporary settlement, pastoral camp, urban definition, urbanisation, social studies

Palestine's own way (Grade 5, PS G5P2): the settlement story is told as a ladder — farming, animal domestication, the agricultural village, the Palestinian village of today, then the city located on the map — and the year closes with the road itself: what a road is, its parts, and how to cross it safely.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7): Geography thins into economic history and a long Sustainable Development inquiry: pupils build a delta in a sand tray, trace the rivers that enter Bangladesh from other countries, map the country's new maritime boundary and its minerals, and audit groundwater and fossil fuels — the delta and the sea are the classroom.

Egypt's own way (Grade 4 Term 1): the very first Social Studies book starts from the child's own space — drawing a plan of the home and school, comparing walking routes by length and landmarks — before maps in general, then the child's own city and governorate, and only then Egypt as homeland; a robot mascot teaches alongside named child guides from different governorates.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 1): One integrated "Social Science" book fusing Religious Studies, Science/Health and Social Studies in a single picture-led volume; Geography enters through the home island itself — The Bahamas as a group of islands, its national symbols, mailboat/jitney transport between islands, conch-and-crawfish fishing, national parks and bush-medicine plants.

Bahamas' own way (Grade 3): The integrated Social Science pattern continues, but Grade 3 adds a distinct Bahamas-geography block in the middle of the book: a full map study of the archipelago (700 islands and cays, Family Islands, three sections), inter-island travel by Bahamasair, fast ferry and mailboat, and an island-by-island inventory of natural resources (Andros crabs, Abaco farming, Grand Bahama citrus and sponge), closing with Hurricane Dorian (2019) as the national extreme-weather case.

Malaysia's own way (MY T1 Geografi Tingkatan 1): a pure Geography textbook organised as skills first (direction, position, sketch maps), then physical geography, human geography, a regional study of Southeast Asia, environmental issues and a full fieldwork chapter — every chapter anchored to Malaysian places first, then the region.

Eritrea's own way (ER G6 Social Studies): one integrated Social Studies book per grade weaving History, Geography, Civics and Culture strands; Geography is taught through Eritrea itself first (Eritrea's location and size, Eritrean settlement, migration and disasters) with very practical, activity-led lessons.

India's own way (Grade 7 Our Environment): Grade 7 is organised around "environment" in widening rings — the concept itself, then inside/changing Earth, air and water, world vegetation belts, human settlement and transport, and finally three lived-in region studies (Amazon basin, Ganga-Brahmaputra basin, Sahara and Ladakh deserts) where all the strands meet in real places.

Slovenia's own way (Slovenia Grade 9 Geografija 9): Grade 9 turns home — the whole book is Slovenia itself: first the country as a system (position and borders, relief and rocks, climate/vegetation/waters, settlement, population, settlements, economy, transport, environment), then its five natural regions one by one (Alpine, Pre-Alpine with the Ljubljana basin, Dinaric-Karst, Mediterranean, Pannonian). Named, not placed: the EU unit's history of the European Union and Slovenia-in-EU pros/cons (printed p. 7, Civics/History) and the state symbols inside the opening unit (p. 1, Civics).

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 4 Social Studies G4): the whole primary book is one-country geography of Syria — nature, then life (Badia, countryside, city), then work (agriculture, industry, trade, tourism) — with map tools (map symbols, four directions) and a tree-cutting/environment strand built into the opening unit's goals.

Syria's own way (Syria Grade 2 Social Studies G2): the same widening-rings design as Grade 3 (Me → Me and You → My Safety → My Society → My Environment → My Homeland), with the Geography carried by the society/environment/homeland rings: Earth's inhabitants, building in the homeland, heritage, and an environment unit built on the land's beauty and riches, diverse nature, water drops and a windy day.

Mauritania's own way (Mauritania Grade 4 geo-4af-m): A one-topic-per-spread observation book that moves from the child's own wilaya outward: the wilaya map first, then the landforms of home (watercourses, mountains, plains, plateaus, sand dunes), the weather elements and the two measuring instruments (thermometer, rain gauge), then the lived place — public places, archaeological sites, street names, my village, the capital of my wilaya — closing with integration exercises on the relief and climate of the wilaya capital.

Kuwait's own way (Kuwait Grade 7 Social Studies G7 T2): Term 2 turns from the Arab homeland's nature (Term 1) to its people, its wealth, its problems and its futures, each unit fronted by a guide character — the Architect/Engineer leads Unit 2, the Crisis Observer Unit 3, the Future Pioneer Unit 4. Unit 1 (Geography) covers population in Kuwait and the Arab world; Unit 2 (Geography) resources and wealth; Unit 3 contemporary challenges — natural, political, social and economic problems, and Kuwait's own; Unit 4 visions and ambitions — national visions, Kuwait Vision 2035, future projects, the digital future and Arab integration; the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait" closes the book. Non-Geography parts (named, not placed): History/Civics — Unit 3 Lesson 2's treatment of the Palestine question (from 1948) and of nuclear risks (weapons, radiation leaks, nuclear waste), printed pp. 54–56 — the geographic parts (Ceuta and Melilla's strategic position; the Sudan split) are placed below. Health/values — Unit 3 Lesson 3, social problems: addiction (drugs, smoking, digital) and unaware digital interaction, rumours and blind imitation, pp. 57–59. Values — Unit 3 Lesson 5's bullying section, p. 62. Civics/planning — Unit 4 Lesson 1, the concept of a national vision and the traits of a successful one, pp. 72–73; Lesson 2, Kuwait Vision 2035 (turning Kuwait into a financial and trade hub; its seven pillars) and its digital projects, pp. 74–76 — the development projects' Geography is placed below. ICT — Unit 4 Lesson 4, the digital future: digital transformation, smart government services and artificial intelligence in the Arab world (Saudi SDAIA, Jordan's Smart Desert), pp. 79–80. Civics/institutions — Unit 4 Lesson 5, Arab integration: its definition, importance and fields, and its organisations — the Arab League (Cairo), the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development (Kuwait), ALECSO (Tunis), the Arab Organization for Agricultural Development (Khartoum) — and the joint Kuwait–Oman Duqm refinery, pp. 81–82. Values — the values unit "Stations with Good Kuwait": a meeting of old friends in Souq Al-Mubarakiya, pp. 88–89; Nabati poetry as Kuwaiti cultural heritage (the Diwaniya of Nabati Poets), p. 90; the Kuwaiti grandmother, the beating heart of the family, pp. 92–93; the neighbour's duty — a story set in the pearl-diving season, pp. 94–96 (the heritage part is placed below).

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 10 Geography of Iran): the whole of Grade 10 geography is one national book — Iran's own natural and human geography for every branch; nearly every lesson closes with a "know your own living place" activity tied to the separate provincial-studies book, so national content is always re-applied to the learner's own province.

Iran's own way (Iran Grade 12 Geography (3)): 'applied' geography organised as three problems of Iranian life — settlements, transport, and natural hazards — each taken in two lessons (what it is, then how to manage it) with a geographical-skills section (GIS, time zones, remote sensing) closing each chapter.
