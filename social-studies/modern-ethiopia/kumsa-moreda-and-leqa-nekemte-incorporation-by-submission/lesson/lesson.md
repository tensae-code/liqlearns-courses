# Kumsa Moreda and Leqa Nekemte — incorporation by submission

## Do it first

Before any explanation, tell this story to yourself in one breath, from the title alone: a state joins a growing empire — and nobody fights. How could that happen? What would each side gain? Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Leqa Nekemte — a hereditary Oromo state

In the west of the country, in the land we now call Wollega, there stood a state of its own: Leqa Nekemte, an Oromo state ruled in line by its own hereditary rulers — power passing within the ruling house, generation to generation. Its ruler was Kumsa Moreda. Hold the three names together, because the whole story hangs on them: the man, Kumsa Moreda; the state, Leqa Nekemte; the land, present-day Wollega.

[Picture 23700 — a schematic map of western Ethiopia with Wollega and Nekemte marked]

Look at the map. Find Wollega in the west, and Nekemte inside it. Every state on that map faced the same question in those years — because from the centre, a greater power was on the move.

## Sub lesson 2 — The submission — joining without a war

Emperor Menelik's forces were drawing state after state into one Ethiopia. The usual road in was war: an army marched, a battle decided, a ruler fell. Leqa Nekemte took the other road. Kumsa Moreda's state submitted peacefully to Menelik's forces. No battle is the headline of this story — the state laid down its separate independence by agreement, and was incorporated: taken inside the empire whole, as a going concern, with its own rulers still standing. That is why this case has its own name: incorporation by submission.

[GIF 23703 — an animated map: the peaceful coming to Nekemte]

Watch the map. The marker travels, the sides meet — and what rises over Nekemte is a banner of agreement, not the smoke of a fight. When you retell this lesson, that absence is the point: say clearly that here, uniquely in this telling, there was no war.

## Sub lesson 3 — Baptism and the title kept — Dejazmach

Submission could have meant the end of the old ruling house. Here it did not. The rulers of Leqa Nekemte kept their power after their baptism — received into the Christian imperial order, and ruling on inside it. And they held it under traditional titles, such as Dejazmach — the old honours of rank, worn now within the empire they had joined. So the bargain reads whole: the state gave up standing alone; its rulers kept their place, their power and their titles; and Ethiopia gained Wollega without spending a battle on it.
