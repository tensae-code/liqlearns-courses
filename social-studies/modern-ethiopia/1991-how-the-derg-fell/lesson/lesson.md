# 1991 — how the Derg fell

## Do it first

Seventeen years after soldiers took power in the name of a committee, other soldiers — organised in fronts that had grown in the hills for a generation — march on the same capital. This lesson is the fall: what held the Derg up, and what was taken away.

## Sub lesson 1 — The fronts close in; the patron departs

The Derg faced armed opposition on many fronts at once: the EPLF in Eritrea, the TPLF in Tigray, the OLF in Oromia and others besides — movements born, each of them, from the grievances this unit has traced: the undone federation, the land and nationality questions, the state's own violence. The civil war impoverished the country year by year, consuming the resources the socialist build was meant to create.

Then the Derg's external pillar gave way. When change came in the Soviet Union — the Gorbachev reforms of the earlier lesson — Ethiopia lost its vital eastern-bloc military support and aid. A regime armed and financed from Moscow stood, at the end, with its arsenal ageing, its treasury empty and its fronts advancing.

## Sub lesson 2 — 28 May 1991 — the EPRDF enters Addis Ababa

The fronts had learned to combine. The EPRDF — the Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front, a coalition whose named components were the TPLF, the EPDM, the OPDO and the EDORM — fought its way from the northern hills to the capital. On 28 May 1991 it controlled Addis Ababa.

The Derg regime was ended — seventeen years after the committee of soldiers had ended the monarchy, a coalition of movements ended the committee's state. What followed — a transitional government, a charter, and in time a federal republic — is the next unit's story, and it begins, as this unit began, with a capital changing hands and a country asking what will be built on the cleared ground.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the fall in three moves: the fronts, the departed patron, 28 May 1991. Name the EPRDF's four components to finish.
