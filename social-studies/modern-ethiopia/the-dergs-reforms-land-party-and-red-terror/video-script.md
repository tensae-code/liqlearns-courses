# Video script — The Derg's reforms — land, party and Red Terror

The story voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Told as story, in the animated-map spirit: the map and the timeline move as the story moves. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

In 1975 the new regime made the revolution's greatest promise real: land was taken from the landlords and declared public property, to be worked by those who tilled it. The demand the students had marched for — land to the tiller — was now law. For millions of peasants this was the revolution's true day one, and the old land order that had held for centuries was swept away in a single proclamation.

The state reached further: banks, factories and large businesses were nationalised, and the economy was placed under government command. In the countryside the regime later drove two vast schemes from above — villagisation, gathering scattered farms into planned villages, and resettlement, moving people from drought-stricken areas to new land. Both moved hundreds of thousands of lives by order, not by choice, and both are taught with their costs.

Politically, the soldiers built themselves a party — the Workers' Party of Ethiopia — and in 1987 a new constitution renamed the country the People's Democratic Republic of Ethiopia, with the party at its centre. But the regime's rule also ran through fear: in the years of struggle against its opponents it unleashed the Red Terror, a campaign of arrests and killings in the cities that the book names directly, and that families across the country still remember by name and by face. A reforming regime and a terrorising regime were the same regime; the lesson holds both truths at once.
