# The war of 1935–36 — the southern front and the exile

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: In the south Graziani broke through at Qorahe, Ras Desta's army died of thirst and epidemic on the Dolo road — and the emperor went into exile to plead at the League. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Qorahe falls; the road to Harar opens

The south repeated the north: **Graziani's** army, militarily superior, entered through the **Ogaden**; Ethiopia's commanders were **Dejazmach Nasibu, Ras Desta Damtew, Dejazmach Balcha Safo, Dejazmach Endalkachew Makonnen** and other young, battle-experienced generals, with **Grazmach Afework** mounting a remarkably strong defence at **Qorahe**, Graziani's first strategic target on the road to Jijiga and Harar — Qorahe still fell, opening **Jijiga and Harar**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Genale Doria, and the exile

**Ras Desta's** army, crossing the desert to **Dolo Ado** without adequate provisions, lost many to **epidemic**; the weakened remainder fought **three days at Genale Doria** and was defeated, and Desta retreated inland to begin the **first guerrilla resistance**. With both fronts collapsed, **Haile Selassie** returned to Addis Ababa, consulted his great officers, and left **through Djibouti into exile in Britain** — to present Italy's invasion in person to the **League of Nations**, where he made his **celebrated speech**; the Europeans, having consented to the invasion, gave **no satisfactory answer**.
