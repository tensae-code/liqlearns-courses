# Three kinds of heritage — nature, practice, thing

## Do it first

One mountain, one festival, one stone tower. All three are Ethiopian heritage — but a mountain cannot be carried, a festival cannot be locked in a museum, and a tower cannot be sung. This lesson sorts heritage the way the world's heritage system does: by what kind of thing it is, because each kind is kept in a different way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Natural and tangible — place and thing

Natural heritage, in UNESCO's frame, is the world's own work: natural features, geological and physiographical formations, habitats of threatened species, and natural sites of outstanding scientific, conservation or natural beauty value. Ethiopia's Simien peaks are the lesson's picture of the kind: heritage no human built, which humans must nevertheless choose to keep — by protection, since a habitat cannot be stored.
[Picture 23110 — three kinds of heritage]

Tangible heritage is the built and made family: physical artefacts produced, maintained and transmitted across generations — artistic creations, buildings, historic places, monuments. The Aksum stela of the picture is its type: a thing with weight and place, kept by preservation. Nature and artefact — the heritage that was given, and the heritage that was made.

## Sub lesson 2 — Intangible — the heritage that lives in people

Intangible heritage is the practices family given its formal name: the practices, representations, expressions, knowledge and skills — together with their associated instruments and artefacts — that communities recognise as their heritage. The definition runs in five domains: oral traditions and expressions, including language; performing arts; social practices, rituals and festive events; knowledge and practices concerning nature and the universe; and traditional craftsmanship.

The Meskel celebration in the picture is the kind's Ethiopian face. Intangible heritage has one keeping-rule the other kinds escape: it survives only by being done. A festival uncelebrated for a generation is not stored heritage but lost heritage — the museum of this kind is the living community itself, which is why this unit taught indigenous knowledge first.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three kinds with their keeping-verbs — protect, practise, preserve — and the five domains of the intangible.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three kinds with one Ethiopian example each.
2. Write the five intangible domains as a list.
3. Sort these into kinds: a waterfall habitat, a wedding song, a carved door, a farming calendar. Defend each sort.
