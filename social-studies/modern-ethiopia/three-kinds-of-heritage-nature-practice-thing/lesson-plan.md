# Lesson plan — Three kinds of heritage — nature, practice, thing

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Three kinds of heritage — nature, practice, thing
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the heritage thread; Grade 10 Unit 9, in UNESCO's frame (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** UNESCO's three kinds: natural heritage (features, habitats and sites of scientific, conservation or beauty value), intangible heritage (practices, expressions, knowledge and skills in five domains), and tangible heritage (physical artefacts, buildings, historic places and monuments).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Heritage — things and practices we choose to keep; this lesson sorts that insight into three kinds. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Natural and tangible — place and thing** — natural heritage defined by features, formations, threatened habitats and sites of outstanding value; tangible heritage as physical artefacts produced, maintained and transmitted across generations.
2. **Intangible — the heritage that lives in people** — practices, representations, expressions, knowledge and skills, with their instruments and artefacts — in five domains from oral traditions to traditional craftsmanship.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Heritage — things and practices we choose to keep. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the three kinds, each in one line.
2. List intangible heritage's five domains.
3. Sort any example given into its kind and defend the sort.

## If the learner is stuck
Three kinds, three verbs of keeping: natural — PROTECT the place; intangible — PRACTISE and transmit; tangible — PRESERVE the object. The five intangible domains are the long peg: oral traditions, performing arts, social practices/rituals/festivals, knowledge of nature and the universe, traditional craftsmanship.
