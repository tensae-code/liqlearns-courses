# Lesson plan — Heritage hunt: your own town

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Heritage hunt: your own town
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 11 — Modern Ethiopia (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Hunt your own town, zone or region for its heritage — find its tangible heritage (things you can touch) and its intangible heritage (practices you can only watch, hear and join), list them, and argue for them together.
**This lesson's own level:** the Grade 12 depth of this title — it stands on the title's heritage lessons and needs nothing else; a learner may jump straight in, as every term is explained where it first appears. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. History is taught as story: the video script is a story script in the animated-map spirit.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Two kinds to hunt — the touchable and the living** — Sort heritage into tangible (sites and things) and intangible (practices and know-how) before hunting for either.
2. **Walking your own ground — town, zone, region** — Search your own town, zone or region, in widening circles, and write every find down with where it is and who keeps it.
3. **Naming it together — the group list** — Bring the finds to the group, with the teacher helping the identification, agree the town's list, and say why each entry deserves keeping.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: "Heritage — what it is and why it matters" in this title — the definitions this hunt puts to work. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort any heritage find into tangible or intangible, and defend the sorting.
2. Run a heritage hunt over their own town, zone or region and record finds properly.
3. Present a group list, with the teacher's help on hard identifications, and say why each entry matters.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and your own streets. A camera or phone helps if you have one. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Learner lists only old buildings → half of every town's heritage is intangible: ask what the town DOES — its festivals, crafts, foods, ceremonies and skills — not only what it has.
- A find will not sort → ask the touch test: could you photograph the thing itself, or only people doing it? The first is tangible, the second intangible.
