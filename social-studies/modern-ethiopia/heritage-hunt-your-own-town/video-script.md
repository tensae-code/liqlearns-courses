# Video script — Heritage hunt: your own town

The story voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Told as story, in the animated-map spirit: the map and the timeline move as the story moves. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

[Animated map: one ordinary town, seen from above. Nothing is marked. The streets look empty of history.]

Every town thinks its history lives somewhere else — in the capital, in the famous sites, in the books. This lesson says: look down. You are standing on it.

[A walker sets out. First star: an old bridge. Second: a carved church door. The tangible finds light up, one by one.]

Some heritage you can touch — a bridge your great-grandparents crossed, a door a carver cut, a tree the elders meet under. That is the tangible heritage, and your town has more of it than you think.

[The walker stops. Music drifts from a doorway. Third star: a festival practice. Fourth: a craft, hands at work. These stars pulse — they are alive.]

And some heritage you cannot touch at all, because it lives in doing — a festival, a craft in someone's hands, a ceremony done the old way. That is the intangible heritage. Miss it, and you have kept the town's body and lost its voice.

[The map pulls back: town, then zone, then region, stars multiplying. A circle of learners gathers round the map, and a teacher's hand steadies the list.]

So hunt in widening circles — your town, your zone, your region. Write every find down. Then name them together, and let your teacher help you settle the hard ones.

[Final frame: the same town, now starred all over.]

The history course ends where it should: not in a book, but in your own streets — with a list that is yours, of things that must not be lost.
