## Sub lesson 1 — Kassa of Quara takes the throne

The making of modern Ethiopia ran on **twin processes**: the **unification** of the northern and north-central regions and principalities, and **territorial expansion** into the southern half. Its first driver was a man from the margins. **Kassa Hailu of Quara** rose serving his half-brother **Dejjazmach Kenfu of Dembya** — soldier, then commander, then the strongest arm in a land of warlords. In **1855** he took the throne as **Tewodros II**, and with his crowning the **Zemene-Mesafint** — the Era of the Princes, when emperors reigned and regional lords ruled — came to its end. He is remembered as the **first emperor of modern Ethiopia**, because what he attempted next had no precedent: to **unify and modernise** the whole country.

## Sub lesson 2 — The reformer — a salaried army and the Sebastopol mortar (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide's activity feedback gives the reform programme in working detail — learn it as the machinery behind the story. Tewodros reformed **administration, religion and the military**, and the military reform was the **most important and least ambivalent**. In organisation he introduced **decimal command titles — Yasr Aleqa, commander of ten; Yamsa Aleqa, commander of the hundred; Yeshi Aleqa, commander of the thousand**. In recruitment he tried to **replace the regional armies of the Zemene-Mesafint with a salaried national army** — soldiers paid by the state, loyal to the state. In armament he **manufactured firearms at Gafat**, near Debre Tabor, with the help of European missionaries and artisans — **about 35 cannons, including his famous mortar 'Sebastopol'**. And in religion he tried to **separate church and state, reducing church land and the number of priests**. Every item on that list is modernisation with a address: one army, one paymaster, one state.
