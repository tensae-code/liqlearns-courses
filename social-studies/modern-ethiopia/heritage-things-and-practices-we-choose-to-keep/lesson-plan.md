# Lesson plan — Heritage — things and practices we choose to keep

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Heritage — things and practices we choose to keep
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the heritage thread's opening lesson; Grade 10 Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** heritage is what can be passed from generation to generation with historic or cultural value — a physical object or place, and just as truly a practice: language, song, literature and dress — the social glue binding communities and expressing identity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the indigenous knowledge lessons — heritage is knowledge's twin: what a community keeps, in things and in doings. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What heritage is — kept, valued, passed on** — heritage defined: what can be passed from generation to generation, conserved or inherited, with historic or cultural value; physical objects, property, buildings and places.
2. **Practices are heritage too — the social glue** — language, culture, popular song, literature and dress as heritage equal to artefacts; heritage as social glue binding communities and expressing identity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define heritage and heritage values.
2. Give the two great families: material things and practices.
3. Say heritage's social function in the summary's terms.

## If the learner is stuck
Two families, never let them merge: heritage as THING (object, building, place) and heritage as PRACTICE (language, song, literature, dress). Heritage values = the meanings groups bestow, not the market price.
