# Heritage — things and practices we choose to keep

## Do it first

Your grandmother's coffee pot is worth almost nothing at market. Refuse to sell it at any price, though, and you have understood this lesson. Some things are valuable because of what they carry — and a whole country has such things. This lesson learns to name them.

## Sub lesson 1 — What heritage is — kept, valued, passed on

Heritage is what can be passed from generation to generation, conserved or inherited, with historic or cultural value. In its most visible form it is physical: an object, a piece of property, a building or a place that is owned and passed on — the stela in the field, the church on the hill, the manuscript in the chest. Such things survive because each generation chooses to keep them: heritage is never automatic; it is a decision renewed.

The lesson adds the term that does the valuing: "heritage values" are the meanings and values individuals and groups bestow on heritage. The pot is clay; the value is bestowed. Understanding heritage means watching the bestowing — who values this, why, and what they will spend to keep it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Practices are heritage too — the social glue

Just as truly, heritage is practice: language, culture, popular song, literature and dress help us comprehend who we are as much as artefacts and buildings do. A song taught at a grandmother's knee is heritage in the full sense — passed down, valued, identity-carrying — though nothing physical changes hands but breath. The unit's summary gathers both families into one function: heritage is part of the social glue binding communities and expressing identity — everything people want to save, material culture and nature together.

Hold the two families ready: the next lesson sorts heritage into UNESCO's three kinds, and the tour that follows walks Ethiopia's own list. The sorting begins from this lesson's insight — that a practice can be heritage exactly as a palace can.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition, the two families with two examples each, and the phrase for heritage's social work — social glue.

1. Write the definition of heritage in your own words.
2. Write two thing-examples and two practice-examples from your own community.
