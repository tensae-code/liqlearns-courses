# Repulsing three aggressions — Britain, Egypt, the Mahdists

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Napier — the expedition Tewodros's modernisation provoked

The first test came from the moderniser's own contradictions. **Tewodros's modernisation drive** reached out to Europe for skills and arms — and when his demands met delay, he **detained envoys and officials**. Britain answered with a military expedition under **Sir Robert Napier**: an army that marched the mountain roads to Maqdala with engineering precision. It ended with **Tewodros's death** in 1868 and the expedition's withdrawal — Britain had come to free captives, not to colonise, and it left. But the lesson stands: Ethiopia's emperors now dealt with industrial powers whose reach no African state could ignore.

## Sub lesson 2 — Egypt at Gundat and Gura — and the Hewett Treaty

The second test came from the north-east. **Egypt** reached for dominion over the **Nile basin** — and over Ethiopia itself. The answer was given in two battles whose names every Ethiopian schoolchild keeps: **Gundat in 1875 and Gura in 1876**, where Egyptian invasions were **repulsed decisively** by Yohannes IV's forces. After the fighting came paper: the **Hewett Treaty** and its results settled the northern questions between Ethiopia, Egypt and Britain for the moment. Mark the sequence — invasion, decisive repulse, treaty: it is the template Ethiopian diplomacy would use again at Adwa.

## Sub lesson 3 — Italy creeps north — and the Mahdists take an emperor

The third test came from two directions at once. In the north, **Italian encroachment** pressed into the highlands — and was **repulsed through the 1880s**, in the fighting this title's earlier lessons follow pass by pass. From the west came the graver blow: the **Mahdist invasion** from the Sudan, which **consumed Emperor Yohannes IV's life in 1889** — the emperor died defending the country at the frontier. Three aggressions, three decades, one theme, and the book states it as the lesson's own: **unity in defence of sovereignty**. Ethiopia survived the age of partition not by luck but because, invasion after invasion, it fought as one.
