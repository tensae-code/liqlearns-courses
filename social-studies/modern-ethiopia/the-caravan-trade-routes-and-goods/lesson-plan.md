# Lesson plan — The caravan trade — routes and goods

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** The caravan trade — routes and goods
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 11 — Modern Ethiopia (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The 19th century's lifeline was the **caravan trade**: goods by barter, **amole salt bars as money**, caravans moving under a **negadras** from hubs like **Bonga**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has followed modern Ethiopia's story in this title's order from 1855. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Barter, salt money, and the negadras** — Say caravans traded by barter with amole salt bars as money, led by a negadras who coordinated, chose rest places and organised defence.
2. **The routes from Bonga** — Trace both route families from Bonga: north-west to Gondar and Metema (and to Mitsiwa via Adwa), and east to Harar, Zeila and Berbera.
3. **The Grade 10 depth: two routes from Bonga, the traders, and trade as the great connector** — the Grade 10 book's wider telling: two major routes from Bonga, capital of Kafa; the Muslim traders Afkala and Jabarti (Jeberti) in the pivotal role; amole and Maria Theresa thalers as the medium of exchange; and trade as the most important catalyst in relations between peoples and polities.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how caravan trade was paid for (barter; amole salt bars as money).
2. Name the negadras's three jobs.
3. Trace the Bonga routes to Gondar/Metema, Mitsiwa, and Harar/Zeila/Berbera.
4. Name the Afkala and Jabarti (Jeberti) traders, the two moneys (amole, Maria Theresa thalers), and trade's role as the most important catalyst between peoples and polities.
