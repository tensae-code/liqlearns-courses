# The caravan trade — routes and goods

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: The 19th century's lifeline was the caravan trade: goods by barter, amole salt bars as money, caravans moving under a negadras from hubs like Bonga. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Barter, salt money, and the negadras

Trade was the 19th century's vital economic activity — local trade and the long-distance **caravan trade**, conducted mainly by **barter**, with **amole salt bars** and the like serving as money. For the peoples of Ethiopia and the Horn, trade was their **main meeting ground and economic base**, and the caravan trade tied Ethiopia to the outside world. Caravans travelled in groups under a **negadras**, who coordinated the journey, chose the resting places and organised defence.

## Sub lesson 2 — The routes from Bonga

The book maps the routes from **Bonga**: through Jiren and Seqa, one line runs north-west through **Asendabo–Baso–Darita–Yifag to Gondar** and on to **Metema**; another runs through **Adwa to Mitsiwa**; and from Seqa a line runs through **Sodo–Aliyu Amba–Abduresul to Harar, Zeila and Berbera** (map after Bahru Zewde, 2002).

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 10 depth: two routes from Bonga, the traders, and trade as the great connector

The older book tells the same caravan world with a wider lens. The peoples and polities of southern, northern and eastern Ethiopia and the Horn were connected by **two major trade routes starting from Bonga, capital of Kafa** — the hub you traced in Sub lesson 2, now named as the capital from which the whole system radiated. The men who worked those routes were Muslim traders known as **Afkala** and **Jabarti (Jeberti)**, and they played the **pivotal role** in the trade — the system's indispensable people. Its money you already know, now stated in full: **salt bars (amole) and Maria Theresa thalers** served as the medium of exchange. And the older book gives the trade its largest meaning: **trade itself was the most important catalyst in relations between peoples and polities** — the caravans did not only move goods; they moved acquaintance, alliance and news between peoples who would otherwise never have met. Routes, major market centres, trade items and people's interaction: those are this depth's four contents, and they complete the caravan picture.
