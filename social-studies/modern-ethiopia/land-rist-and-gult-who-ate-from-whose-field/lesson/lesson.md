# Land, rist and gult — who ate from whose field

## Do it first

Ask of any old empire the simplest question: who eats from whose field? In Ethiopia the answer ran through two old words — rist and gult — and the answer decided everything: who was powerful, who was hungry, and eventually, who revolted. This lesson learns the two words.

## Sub lesson 1 — The backbone — farming, coffee, herds

Agriculture was the imperial economy's backbone, as it had always been. For most rural society it meant subsistence farming — households growing what they ate, on the land their ancestors had farmed. What Ethiopia sold to the world came from the same soil: coffee and oilseeds made up the bulk of exports. And on the hoof, Ethiopia held the largest livestock population in Africa — wealth that walked.

In such an economy, land is not one asset among many. Land is the basis of power itself: to command land is to command the food, the labour and the loyalty of everyone who must eat from it. The systems that divided land's fruits divided the empire's power with them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rist and gult — two claims on one field

The peasant's own claim was rist: communal land rights held through descent — the field your lineage holds because it has always held it, worked by the family, inherited within the kin group. Over that claim the emperor laid another: gult, the right — granted by the emperor to ranks of the warrior class and to local rulers — to collect tribute, gibir, from the peasants on the land. The gult-holder did not own the soil as a modern landlord owns an estate; he owned a right to its fruits, granted from above, held at the throne's pleasure.

Where the two claims met, tenancy grew: peasants working land under obligations to those who held rights over them, delivering tribute in grain, labour and kind. These tenancy relations are the seedbed of the opposition that follows in this unit — the peasant revolts, and eventually the land question that the students of the 1960s would shout in the streets. Who ate from whose field was never only an economic question.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two definitions — rist as the peasant's claim, gult as the granted right over it — and finish with the word the system built: tenancy.
