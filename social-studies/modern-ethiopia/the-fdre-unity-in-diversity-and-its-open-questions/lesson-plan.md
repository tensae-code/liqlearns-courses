# Lesson plan — The FDRE — unity in diversity, and its open questions

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** The FDRE — unity in diversity, and its open questions
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the post-1991 unit; Grade 10 Unit 8, with the Grade 10 guide's nine states stocked as labelled evidence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the 1995 constitution founded the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia — nine regional states in an ethnic federalism whose stated objective is "unity in diversity", with the challenges the book itself lists: secession's threat, imbalance, state-building in underserved regions, power-sharing, the capital and the working-language question.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on 1991 — the Charter and the transitional government; the FDRE is the permanent order the Charter pointed to. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1995 — a federation of peoples** — the FDRE established: nine regional states and two city administrations, ethnic federalism, 106 articles in eleven chapters, sovereignty of the peoples.
2. **The nine states named (teacher guide — evidence)** — the Grade 10 guide's activity answers name the nine regional states the student book leaves as a count.
3. **Unity in diversity — and the open questions** — the constitution's stated objective, and the challenges the book lists: secession, imbalance, underserved state-building, power-sharing, the capital, the working language.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: 1991 — the Charter and the transitional government. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the constitution's year, the state's name, and the states' number.
2. Name the constitution's scale and its organising principles.
3. State the objective — unity in diversity — and three of the book's listed challenges.
