# Lesson plan — Who owns the Nile? — Abay hydro-politics

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Who owns the Nile? — Abay hydro-politics
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the post-1991 unit; Grade 10 Unit 8 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia contributes about 86% of the Nile's waters through the Abay and Tekkeze, yet the 1929 and 1959 agreements divided all the water between Egypt and Sudan alone — the upper riparians now assert equitable rights and harmonious use.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the post-1991 lessons — development is the frame in which Ethiopia presses its claim. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The giver of the river — Abay, Tekkeze, 86%** — Ethiopia sources two of the Nile's three major tributaries and about 86% of its waters — while the most extensive use has been Egypt's.
2. **1929 and 1959 — divided without the givers; the new claim** — the agreements that shared all the water between Egypt and Sudan, ignoring the other riparians; the upper riparians' assertion of equitable rights and harmonious use.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The FDRE — unity in diversity, and its open questions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Ethiopia's two Nile tributaries and its 86% share.
2. Tell the 1929 and 1959 agreements: who shared, who was ignored.
3. State the upper riparians' new claim in the book's terms.
