# Lesson plan — Liberated but influenced — the British era and the American turn

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Liberated but influenced — the British era and the American turn
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the post-liberation unit's opening lesson; Grade 10 Unit 6 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Haile Selassie returned to Addis Ababa on 5 May 1941 — yet liberated Ethiopia remained under British influence through the treaties of 1942 and 1944, before American influence succeeded the British as the restored monarchy's main external anchor.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the liberation lessons of 1941 — the return this lesson begins with. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **5 May 1941 — the return, and the shadow** — the Emperor's return to Addis Ababa, official recognition of sovereignty — and continued British influence in fact.
2. **The treaties of 1942 and 1944 — then the American turn** — the Anglo-Ethiopian treaties pupils must evaluate, and the succession of American for British influence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the return's date — 5 May 1941.
2. Name the two British treaties and what pupils must evaluate about them.
3. Say who succeeded Britain as the main external influence.
