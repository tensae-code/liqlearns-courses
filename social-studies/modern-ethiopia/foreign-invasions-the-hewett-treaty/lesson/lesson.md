# Foreign invasions — the Hewett Treaty

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: The Hewett (Adwa) Treaty of 1884 promised Ethiopia Mitsiwa's free use and the occupied territories back — and made Ethiopia the Mahdists' enemy. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why Egypt came to terms

Two changes moved Egypt to seek accommodation: **Britain held Egypt from 1882**, and the **Mahdist rising in Sudan** shattered Egypt's army and besieged its garrisons on the Ethio-Sudan border. Britain and Egypt, unable to lift the sieges, sought Ethiopia's help: **Rear-Admiral Sir William Hewett** signed the **Hewett (Adwa) Treaty** with Yohannes in **1884**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Three articles — and the hidden cost

The treaty's articles: **Article 1**, Ethiopia's **free use of Mitsiwa port**; **Article 2**, **return of the occupied territories**; **Article 3**, Ethiopia's promise to **evacuate the besieged Egyptian troops** through her territory to Mitsiwa and on to Egypt. It promised a sea outlet and the territories back — but it made Ethiopia the Mahdists' enemy: **Ras Alula** fulfilled the order, fighting the Mahdists at **Kufit in 1885**, and Mahdist attacks on the north-west followed. Above all, the treaty **opened the door for Italy to take Mitsiwa**.
