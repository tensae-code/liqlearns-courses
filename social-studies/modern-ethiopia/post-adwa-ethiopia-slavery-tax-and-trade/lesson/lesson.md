# Post-Adwa Ethiopia — slavery, tax and trade

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: After Adwa: slavery fought decisively (slaves freed a year after Ethiopia joined the League in 1923), tax payable in money, and trade revived. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ending slavery and forced labour

The first three decades of the 20th century brought better social and economic change, tied to centralisation and the defence campaigns. **Slavery and the slave trade were fought decisively**: the trade's chief victims were the **youth**; it destroyed security of life and property where slaves were raided, killed productive initiative, and invited foreign interference. A year after joining the **League of Nations in 1923** Ethiopia proclaimed **slaves freed and slave buying and selling illegal**, and a law banning **forced labour** followed in **1935**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tax in cash; trade revives

**Tax was made payable in money — 30 birr per gasha** as printed. The peace after centralisation and Adwa **revived trade**, and Ethiopia's wide diplomacy gave her **better trade links than before Adwa**.
