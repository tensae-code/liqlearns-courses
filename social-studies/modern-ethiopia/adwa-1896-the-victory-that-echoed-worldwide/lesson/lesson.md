# Adwa 1896 — the victory that echoed worldwide

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Wuchale — one treaty, two texts

The road to Adwa begins with a document — and a deception inside it. By the **Treaty of Wuchale**, Ethiopia and Italy settled their relations; but the treaty's **Article XVII differed between its Amharic and Italian versions**. In the Italian text, the clause bound Ethiopia to conduct its foreign affairs through Italy — the language of a **protectorate**, a colony in all but name. In the Amharic text, no such obligation stood. The book sets you the exact task: **elaborate the difference between the two versions**. Do it carefully — an empire's freedom turned on the gap between two sentences. When Menilek saw the claim Italy built on that gap, he denounced the treaty. Italy marched to enforce it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Adwa — the morning that changed the story

The claim was settled at the **Battle of Adwa in 1896**, in the northern hills — Ethiopian forces converging from the highlands in the picture, a whole nation in arms under Menilek. The result rewrote the age's script: Adwa was the **first decisive victory of Black people over a powerful white European power**. In Italy, the shock **brought down Premier Francesco Crispi's government**. Across the colonised world, the news ran like fire: Adwa became **a source of pride and motivation for freedom fighters worldwide** — proof, in an age that preached African inferiority, that a united African state could meet a European army in the field and destroy it. Ethiopia kept its sovereignty — the one great exception on the Scramble's map — and Adwa is why.
