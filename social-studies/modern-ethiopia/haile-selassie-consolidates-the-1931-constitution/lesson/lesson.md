# Haile Selassie consolidates — the 1931 constitution

## Do it first

After a generation of shared and contested power, one man gathers all the strings. He is crowned in 1930 before the world's envoys — and within a year he gives his empire something it has never had: a written constitution. This lesson is about what that constitution really did.

## Sub lesson 1 — From diarchy to one throne — coronation, 1930

The settlement after Lij Iyasu's fall shared power: an empress on the throne and a regent who held the real direction of the state — a diarchy, two centres of authority in one palace. Such arrangements live only as long as both halves do. When the settlement's other half died, the regent stood alone, and in 1930 he was crowned emperor as Haile Selassie.

The coronation was staged for the world — envoys and journalists from across the globe in Addis Ababa — announcing a modernising monarch. The modernising was real, and so was the gathering: the new emperor set out to convert a throne shared and contested into an autocracy held by one hand.

## Sub lesson 2 — Autocracy on paper — the constitution of 1931

Through the 1930s the regional rulers lost their autonomy: the great provincial houses that had governed their lands as near-kings were drawn down, their powers absorbed by the centre. Ethiopia's age-old pattern — a strong throne balanced by strong regions — tilted decisively to the throne.

The legal frame of the new order was the constitution of 1931, the empire's first written constitution, promulgated the year after the coronation. Pupils must be able to state its features: it framed a centralised monarchy in law. Hold its shape in mind — the post-liberation unit will set the 1955 constitution beside it and ask what changed, and the honest answer begins here: 1931 wrote the emperor's autocracy onto paper.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the inward flow: diarchy, sole rule, coronation 1930, constitution 1931, regions drawn down. End with the contrast the course will later draw — 1955.
