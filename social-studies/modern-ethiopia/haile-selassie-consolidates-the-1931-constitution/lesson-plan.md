# Lesson plan — Haile Selassie consolidates — the 1931 constitution

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Haile Selassie consolidates — the 1931 constitution
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the succession unit's resolution; the Grade 10 book's second Unit 3 closing lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the succession struggle ends in one man's hands: Haile Selassie, crowned emperor in 1930, strips the regional rulers of their autonomy through the 1930s and gives the centralised monarchy its legal frame — the constitution of 1931.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on After Menilek — Lij Iyasu: the struggle that lesson opened is the one resolved here. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **From diarchy to one throne — coronation, 1930** — the shared-rule settlement after Iyasu gives way to Haile Selassie's consolidation and coronation.
2. **Autocracy on paper — the constitution of 1931** — regional rulers lose their autonomy through the 1930s; the 1931 constitution gives the centralised monarchy its legal frame, to be contrasted with 1955's.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: After Menilek — Lij Iyasu and the struggle for the throne. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the resolution's order: diarchy, then consolidation, coronation 1930.
2. State what happened to regional rulers' autonomy in the 1930s.
3. Say what the 1931 constitution framed — and what Unit 6 will contrast it with.
