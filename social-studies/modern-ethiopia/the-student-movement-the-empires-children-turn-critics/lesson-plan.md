# Lesson plan — The student movement — the empire's children turn critics

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** The student movement — the empire's children turn critics
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the opposition unit; Grade 10 Unit 6 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the universities' students became the regime's most articulate opposition: educated in the empire's own schools, they named the land question and carried the vocabulary of revolution into the streets — the lesson built around Tilahun Gizaw's speech.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the imperial ledger — education was the sector that produced these critics. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The universities turn** — students become the most articulate opposition: the land question named, the revolution's vocabulary carried into the streets.
2. **Tilahun Gizaw — the lesson's voice** — the book builds this lesson around a video of Tilahun Gizaw's speech; pupils watch it and describe the movement's nature from it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The imperial ledger — trade, schools, roads, clinics. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say where the student movement came from — the regime's own universities.
2. Name Tilahun Gizaw and the lesson's use of his speech.
3. State the question the students named above all: land.
