# Autocracy and the rising opposition

## Do it first

Before any explanation, tell this story to yourself in one breath, from the title alone: Autocracy and the rising opposition. What do you already think happened, and what would you need to check? Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Power gathers in one pair of hands

After the restoration, power gathered steadily into the emperor's own hands. Ministers, commanders and governors held office at his pleasure, and the great decisions of the empire — war, land, appointments — rose to one desk in Addis Ababa. An empire run from a single centre can look strong, and for a time it did. But a state where everything waits on one man has no second storey to stand on when the first one cracks.

## Sub lesson 2 — The December 1960 coup attempt

In December 1960, while the emperor was abroad, a group of officers and officials — the coup attempt the book places at the head of the opposition story — seized key points in the capital and proclaimed a new order. For a few days Addis Ababa held its breath. Then loyal forces rallied, the coup collapsed, and its leaders were destroyed. The attempt failed, but it had shown something that could not be un-shown: the throne could be challenged from inside its own guard.

## Sub lesson 3 — Students, soldiers' grievances, and revolts in the provinces

Through the 1960s the challenge widened. University students marched under the cry of land to the tiller, naming the land order as the empire's deepest injustice. In Eritrea, annexed in 1962, an armed insurgency began that would burn for thirty years. In the provinces, peasants rose in revolt — in Bale and in Gojjam — against taxes and landlords. Each strand pulled at a different thread of the same cloth.

## Sub lesson 4 — The famine that discredited the throne

Then came drought and famine in Wollo and Tigray. The suffering was vast, and for a long time it was hidden from the world — and when it could no longer be hidden, the picture of an ageing emperor presiding while his people starved broke the throne's remaining claim to care for the country. By early 1974, the empire's authority was hollow. The next lesson tells what filled the hollow.
