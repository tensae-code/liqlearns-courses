# 1974 — the Ethiopian revolution

## Do it first

Before any explanation, tell this story to yourself in one breath, from the title alone: 1974 — the Ethiopian revolution. What do you already think happened, and what would you need to check? Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — A country already at breaking point

The revolution did not begin with a plan. It began with pressure from every side at once: the famine's shame still fresh, world oil prices climbing and pushing up the cost of living, soldiers angry about pay and conditions, taxi drivers, teachers and workers ready to strike. The old order had run out of answers, and everyone could feel it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mutinies, strikes, and a committee of soldiers

In the first months of 1974 the cascade came: military units mutinied, strikes spread through the capital, and demonstration followed demonstration. Out of the armed forces stepped a coordinating committee of soldiers — the body the country would know as the Derg. It arrested ministers, took the radio and the ministries, and gathered power piece by piece through the summer, while the emperor still sat, powerless, in his palace.

## Sub lesson 3 — September 1974 — the emperor deposed, the monarchy abolished

In September 1974 the committee finished the work: the emperor was deposed and driven away, and the monarchy itself was abolished. An institution that had claimed descent from Solomon ended not with a great battle but with an old man in a small car, leaving the palace while soldiers watched. Ethiopia had crossed into a wholly new age — and the men who now held it had not yet said what they meant to build.
