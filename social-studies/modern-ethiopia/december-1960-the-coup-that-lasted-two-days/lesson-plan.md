# Lesson plan — December 1960 — the coup that lasted two days

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** December 1960 — the coup that lasted two days
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the opposition unit; Grade 10 Unit 6 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on 14 December 1960 the brothers Mengistu and Germame Neway proclaimed a new government — the Crown Prince as constitutional monarch, Ras Imru as prime minister — and in barely two days of fighting in Addis Ababa it fell, for the book's stated main cause: lack of support.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the imperial ledger and peasant revolts — palace opposition now joins the countryside's. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **14 December 1960 — a new government proclaimed** — the coup makers, their proclaimed government, and the programme implied by salaried constitutional monarchy.
2. **Two days of fighting — and the verdict** — loyalist and rebel forces fight in Addis Ababa; the Emperor returns; the leaders' fates; the main cause of failure: lack of support.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Peasants against the empire — Woyane, Bale, Gojjam. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the date and the two brothers at the coup's head.
2. Name the proclaimed government's three posts and holders.
3. Tell the two days' end: who died, who fled to suicide, who was sentenced.
4. State the book's main cause of failure.
