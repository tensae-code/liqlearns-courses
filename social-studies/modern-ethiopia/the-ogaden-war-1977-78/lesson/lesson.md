# The Ogaden war 1977–78

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: Somalia's Greater Somalia invasion drove 700 km into Ethiopia in 1977 — and was smashed at Karamara in March 1978 by militia, air force and new allies. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Greater Somalia and the invasion

The war's roots reach to the **1940s**: after the Italian invasion the Ogaden was under **British administration**; after long diplomacy Britain handed **most of it to Ethiopia in 1948 and the whole in 1954** — to Somalia's displeasure. The Somali Republic made gathering **all Somali-inhabited lands** — British Somaliland, French Somaliland, Italian Somaliland, the Ogaden and northern Kenya — under one flag its main aim; after Italian and British Somaliland united into a state in **1960** it opened war for the Ogaden, and the two governments fought first in **1964, Ethiopia winning**. The question flared again in **1977**: President **Siad Barre**, claiming the land 'as far as the Awash is ours' under a **Greater Somalia** policy, invaded while the Derg faced heavy internal trials; in 1977 the regular Somali army advanced **700 km** into eastern Ethiopia.

## Sub lesson 2 — The motherland call and Karamara

Ethiopia answered: **300,000 militia** trained in a short time under the **motherland call** to support the regular army; the Derg, having proclaimed Marxism-Leninism, won **Soviet arms** after the Soviets broke with Somalia, and **Cuban and South Yemeni soldiers** fought beside Ethiopia's. With that help Ethiopia counter-attacked — the **air force and the militia** playing a great role — and after the Somali army was **smashed at Karamara in March 1978** it left Ethiopian territory altogether.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 10 depth — the five-pointed star, 1964, and who began 1977 (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 10 book's framing of the war is stocked here. The Ogaden had been contested since Somalia's independence in 1960: Somalia was committed to uniting all five Somali-speaking territories under its flag's five-pointed star — South and North Somalia, French Somaliland (Djibouti), the Ogaden, and Kenya's Northern Frontier District. A border war in January 1964 ended, after OAU peacemaking, with no change of boundaries — and in 1977 the two countries entered serious war again, with foreign powers' influence on both sides.

[Picture 23107 — the five-pointed star over the Horn]

The Grade 10 teacher guide's war overview assigns the initiative plainly, and it is stocked as evidence beside the student frame: the 1977 war was initiated by President Siad Barre of Somalia, pursuing his irredentist policy of creating a "Greater Somalia" (guide, printed p. 111). Read with this lesson's account of the war itself, the Grade 10 depth completes the picture: the star's five points are the war's map and its motive in one emblem.
