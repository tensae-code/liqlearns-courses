# Modern Ethiopia — the founding struggle

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: From 1769 to 1855 Ethiopia's centre was weak — the Zemene Mesafint — and the era ended when Tewodros rose to rebuild a central government. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — A centre that came apart, 1769–1855

For almost ninety years Ethiopia had emperors in name, but real power sat with rival princes and lords who fought each other — the **Zemene Mesafint, the Era of the Princes (1769–1855)**. The central government was weakened by internal war, and no single hand could hold the country together or defend it as one land.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tewodros rises — and what this chapter follows

The era ended with **Tewodros's rise**: a strong king set out to **rebuild a central government** and bring modern change. This part of the story then follows the founding struggles, the **caravan trade**, and the long defence of Ethiopia's unity and freedom against **Egypt, the Mahdists, Italy and Somalia** — then the changes after Adwa, and the Fascist occupation and liberation.
