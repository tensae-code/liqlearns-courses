# Lesson plan — After Menilek — Lij Iyasu and the struggle for the throne

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** After Menilek — Lij Iyasu and the struggle for the throne
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the succession unit opening the 1906–1935 story; the Grade 10 book's first Unit 3 closing lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** from 1906, as Menilek's health failed, Ethiopia's ruling elites struggled for the succession he had tried to arrange: his designated heir, the young Lij Iyasu, reigned and attempted reforms — and the struggle for the throne consumed them both.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Menilek II — the present shape of Ethiopia: the state Menilek built is the prize of this struggle. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1906 — the old emperor fades, the elites manoeuvre** — Menilek's precautions against a succession struggle among his heirs and the elites who would contest it.
2. **Lij Iyasu — the designated heir reigns and reforms** — the young heir's reign and reforms, and the opposition they gathered — the first act of the 1906–1935 succession drama.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Menilek II — the present shape of Ethiopia. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Date the struggle's opening — 1906 — and say why it opened then.
2. Name Menilek's precautions and his designated heir.
3. Tell who Lij Iyasu was and what his reign attempted.
4. Say what this act sets up: the diarchy and consolidation of the next lesson.
