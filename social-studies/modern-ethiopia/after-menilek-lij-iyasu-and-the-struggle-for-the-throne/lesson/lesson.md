# After Menilek — Lij Iyasu and the struggle for the throne

## Do it first

An old emperor lies ill in his palace, and every great lord in the empire begins quietly counting soldiers and marriages. The succession of an empire is the most dangerous moment in its life. This lesson is Ethiopia's — from 1906, the year the counting began.

## Sub lesson 1 — 1906 — the old emperor fades, the elites manoeuvre

Menilek II had built the present shape of Ethiopia — and by 1906 the builder was failing. From that year the ruling elites' power struggle opens: the emperor's illness made the question every courtier had whispered unavoidable — who rules next? A succession unsettled is an invitation to every ambitious house in the land, and Menilek knew it.

So the old emperor took measures to avert a struggle among his successors: arranging the line of inheritance in advance, binding the great men to his design while his authority still stood. The design named his heir — his young grandson. Whether paper arrangements can hold when the arranger is gone is the question the next years answered.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lij Iyasu — the designated heir reigns and reforms

The designated heir was Lij Iyasu, Menilek's grandson, raised to the succession while still very young. His reign — first under the shadow of the old emperor's court, then in his own name — attempted reforms of the order he inherited, reaching toward constituencies the highland court had kept at its margins and governing with a freedom that alarmed the established elites.

Alarm became opposition. The great men of the empire, the church establishment and foreign powers watching Ethiopia's direction combined against the young ruler, and the succession struggle Menilek had tried to pre-empt arrived in full. Iyasu's fall closes this first act; the settlement that followed — a diarchy, then one man's consolidation — is the next lesson's story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: 1906 and why; the precautions and the heir's name; the reforms and the alarm. End by naming what the struggle sets up.
