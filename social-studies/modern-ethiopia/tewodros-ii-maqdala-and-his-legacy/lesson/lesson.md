# Tewodros II — Maqdala and his legacy

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: At Maqdala in April 1868 Tewodros fell to Napier's expedition — but his dream of a united Ethiopia was inherited, not buried. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Enemies multiply; Napier comes

Tewodros's measures multiplied his enemies at home and abroad, and repeated campaigns shrank his army — by the **1867 decrees** it was much reduced. Britain, whose envoys' demand for the prisoners' release he refused, sent **General Robert Napier's expedition in 1868**. Tigray's **Dejazmach Kassa Mircha** guided and aided the British — rewarded with many arms and experts, among them the adviser **Kirkham** — and so helped bring the government down.

## Sub lesson 2 — Arogi, the refusal, and the dream that outlived him

The one great battle was fought on the **Arogi plain below Maqdala on April 10, 1868**: **Fitawrari Gebriye's** force was badly mauled and its leader killed. Refusing unconditional surrender, **Tewodros shot himself on April 13, 1868**. The British looted heavily, including the king's crown. The succession struggle that followed — **Wagshum Gobezé, Kassa Mircha and Shewa's Menilek** competing — was a struggle for the King of Kings' title, **not a return to the Zemene Mesafint**: Tewodros's dream of a united Ethiopia was inherited, and later fulfilled by **Menilek II**.
