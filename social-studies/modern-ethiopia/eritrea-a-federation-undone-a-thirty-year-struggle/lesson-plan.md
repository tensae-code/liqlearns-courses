# Lesson plan — Eritrea — a federation undone, a thirty-year struggle

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Eritrea — a federation undone, a thirty-year struggle
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the Derg-era unit; Grade 10 Unit 6 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Eritrea, under British control from 1941, was federated with Ethiopia by the UN in 1952 — Eritrea governing its domestic affairs, Ethiopia holding defence, foreign affairs, currency and communications; measures contrary to the Federal Act then undid the autonomy, and from the discontent grew an armed struggle carried up to referendum.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the post-liberation lessons — the federation was the restored empire's first great external question. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **1941–1952 — an international question, a federal answer** — British control from 1941; the UN General Assembly's 1950 recommendation; federation in 1952 with its division of powers.
2. **The federation undone — discontent and armed struggle** — measures contrary to the Federal Act undermine Eritrean autonomy; from the discontent the armed struggle grows, carried to referendum.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Liberated but influenced — the British era and the American turn. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell Eritrea's path: British control 1941, international question, UN decision 1950.
2. State the federation's division of powers, both sides.
3. Tell how autonomy was undone and what grew from the discontent.

## If the learner is stuck
The division of powers is the peg: Eritrea's side — domestic affairs through its own legislative, executive, judicial powers; the federal side — defence, foreign affairs, currency and finance, interstate commerce, communications. Undo the division and you undo the federation.
