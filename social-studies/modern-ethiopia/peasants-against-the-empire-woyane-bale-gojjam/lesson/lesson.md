# Peasants against the empire — Woyane, Bale, Gojjam

## Do it first

Long before students marched in the capital, farmers were already saying no — with petitions first, and then with the tax assessor turned away at the village boundary. This lesson follows the countryside's three great refusals.

## Sub lesson 1 — Woyane 1943 and Bale — the first refusals

The earliest opposition was rural, and it began almost with the restoration itself. In Tigray, the Woyane revolt of 1943 rose against the returned regime: semi-pastoralists, highland peasants and some local feudal lords formed a united front — a coalition across the countryside's own ranks — under Bilata Haile Mariam Reda. The revolt's breadth is its lesson: discontent with the restored order reached from herders to lords within two years of liberation.

In the south, the Bale revolt wrote the same refusal in another region's hand. Its thread runs forward in the book's telling: General Jagema Kello, a figure of the Bale resistance world, was later assigned as Bale's governor — the state answering revolt, in part, by absorbing its opponents into office. Absorption worked sometimes. In Gojjam it would not be tried soon enough.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gojjam 1968 — the tax refused

The Gojjam revolts built over years and were triggered finally in 1968 by the agricultural income tax of 1967 — the state's attempt to tax the peasant's income in a countryside that already delivered tribute, dues and labour. The refusal followed a disciplined sequence: peasants refused the tax assessors entry, then petitioned, then defied the state outright.

The government's answer was force: militia and armed police sent into the resisting districts — Mota, Qolla Dega Damot and Bichena are the names the book records. Gojjam's lesson for the unit is exact: the countryside's grievance was land and tax, its method was refusal escalating step by step, and the empire — twenty-five years after Woyane — was still answering its farmers with soldiers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three revolts with their pegs — Woyane 1943 and its front; Bale and Jagema Kello; Gojjam 1968 and the refused tax.
