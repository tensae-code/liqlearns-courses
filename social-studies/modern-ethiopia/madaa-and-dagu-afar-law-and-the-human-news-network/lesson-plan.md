# Lesson plan — Mada'a and Dagu — Afar law and the human news network

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Mada'a and Dagu — Afar law and the human news network
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the indigenous knowledge unit; Grade 10 Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Afar keep two systems: Mada'a, an unwritten law administered by elders chosen from outside the disputing clans, deciding by forgiveness, respect and compensation — and Dagu, the age-old travellers' news network that carries what people have seen and heard to everyone, regardless of status.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the two indigenous-knowledge opening lessons. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Mada'a — law without writing** — the Afar's unwritten indigenous law: orally transmitted, administered by elders and clan leaders chosen from different clans who do not represent the disputants; decisions resting on forgiveness, respect for elders and compensation.
2. **Dagu — the human news network** — the age-old information-exchange network: news of what travellers have seen or heard passes person to person, serving all members of society virtually equally regardless of status.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: How indigenous knowledge behaves — oral, local, inherited. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe Mada'a: unwritten, elder-administered, its three resting values.
2. Explain who may judge under Mada'a — and who may not.
3. Tell how Dagu moves news and whom it serves.
