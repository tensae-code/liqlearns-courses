# Mada'a and Dagu — Afar law and the human news network

## Do it first

In the Afar lowlands the temperature can kill, distances are enormous, and for centuries there were no courts, no newspapers, no wires. Yet disputes were settled and news travelled faster than caravans. Two systems did it: a law carried in memory, and a news network carried by travellers. This lesson walks both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mada'a — law without writing

The Afar, a predominantly pastoral people of Ethiopia's north-east, resolve conflicts by Mada'a: an unwritten indigenous law, transmitted orally across the generations like all the knowledge of the last lessons. Its court is a council: elders and clan leaders administer it — chosen, and this is the system's genius, from different clans who do not represent the disputing members. The judges owe nothing to either side; their authority is the law's memory and the community's respect.
[GIF 23121 — news crossing Afar country by Dagu]

Mada'a's decisions rest on three values, named in the book: forgiveness, respect for elders, and compensation by transfer of resources. The aim is not a winner and a loser but a settlement the community can live inside afterward — the offender's side compensates, the offended side forgives, and the elders' word closes the wound the dispute opened. A pastoral people's law, engineered for a country where your opponent is also your neighbour at the next well.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dagu — the human news network

The Afar's second system moves information the way Mada'a moves justice: person to person. Dagu is an age-old information-exchange network — an indigenous communication system in which travellers meeting on the road or at wells exchange the news of what they have seen or heard, and carry it onward. In a land without wires, the traveller is the wire.

The book's figure shows Dagu in action — two Afar travellers exchanging news in the desert — and its social point is equality: the network serves all members of society virtually equally regardless of status. The chief and the herdboy drink from the same stream of news. Dagu is indigenous knowledge as infrastructure: no office, no fee, no editor — a protocol of greeting, asking and telling that a whole people runs by custom.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion Mada'a's judge-rule and three values, then Dagu's mechanism and its one social miracle — equality of access.
