# Foreign invasions — Egypt's expansion

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: Egypt under Muhammad Ali and Khedive Ismail drove at the Horn for the Abay's source — taking Sudan (1821), then Red Sea ports and Bogos (1872). Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Old ties, new ambition

Ethiopia and Egypt had **long ties of culture, trade and religion** — but 19th-century Egypt's great territorial drive aimed at the Horn and at Ethiopia, the **Abay's source**. **Muhammad Ali's** government took **Sudan in 1821** and struck Ethiopia's north-west — **Simien, Dembiya, the Asosa sheikhdoms** — taxing the border people heavily, enslaving, burning houses and crops. In **1872** Egyptian forces took other Red Sea ports, **Mitsiwa** and its dependencies and the **Bogos** country.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ismail's greater Egypt

**Khedive Ismail Pasha (1863–79)** planned to **control the Abay's source** and build a **greater Egypt in North Africa**, seeking European support under the cover of 'spreading Western civilisation and stopping the slave trade'. The river was the prize: whoever held its source held a hand on Egypt's life.
