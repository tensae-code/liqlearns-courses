# Lesson plan — The 1955 constitution — an empire re-founded on paper

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** The 1955 constitution — an empire re-founded on paper
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the post-liberation unit; Grade 10 Unit 6 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the restored monarchy consolidated its autocracy through administrative reform and a new constitution in 1955 — the legal frame of the post-liberation empire, to be read against the 1931 constitution it replaced.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Haile Selassie consolidates — the 1931 constitution is the document this one is compared with. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Administrative reform — the empire organised** — the restored monarchy's administrative consolidation, its units sketched by pupils as the lesson's map-work.
2. **1955 against 1931 — what changed on paper** — the new constitution of 1955 and the differences from 1931 that pupils must summarise.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how the restored monarchy consolidated: administrative reform.
2. Contrast 1955 with 1931 as the book sets pupils to do.
3. Say what the imperial administrative map shows.
