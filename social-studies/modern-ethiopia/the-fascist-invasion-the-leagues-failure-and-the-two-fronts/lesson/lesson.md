# The Fascist invasion — the League's failure and the two fronts

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: The League sacrificed Ethiopia — an arms embargo on both sides that hurt only Ethiopia — and in October 1935 Mussolini invaded from north and south. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — The League fails Ethiopia

**Britain and France**, who ran the League, delayed the Walwal agenda repeatedly — on the eve of a second world war they hoped to keep Italy from joining Germany and secretly chose to **sacrifice Ethiopia**. The League finally imposed an **arms embargo on both countries**, which did not hurt Italy, who kept importing fuel and arms from Germany and neutral America. A year after Walwal, Mussolini invaded.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two fronts, poison gas

The attack came from the **north (Eritrea)** and the **south (Italian Somaliland)**: in the north **Marshal Emilio De Bono** commanded first, replaced by **Pietro Badoglio**, under whom the war's cruel phase ran; the armies crossed the Mereb fast and advanced into Tigray using **internationally banned poison gas**; the northern invasion began **October 3, 1935**. In the south **General Rodolfo Graziani** commanded, advancing later, under air bombardment, into the interior.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 10 depth — the invasion as the Grade 10 book frames it

The Grade 10 book frames the invasion with an insistence: despite Adwa, Italy never gave up its desire for Ethiopia. Under Mussolini, Fascist Italy invaded in the Second Italo-Ethiopian War of 1935–36 — and the book maps the Italian advances on two fronts, against an Ethiopia whose emperor's plea the League of Nations met with deaf ears: the lesson's own brainstorming question asks pupils to sit inside that deafness. The Grade 10 telling binds the invasion and the League's failure into one event, which is how this lesson teaches it too.
