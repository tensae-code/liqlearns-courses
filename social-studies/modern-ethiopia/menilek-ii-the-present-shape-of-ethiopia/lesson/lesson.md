# Menilek II — the present shape of Ethiopia

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Expansion by three methods

With **Menilek II**, the map itself changes. He is credited with **unprecedented territorial expansion** and nothing less than **the creation of the present shape of Ethiopia**. The expansion worked by **three methods combined** — learn them as a set, because the book insists on all three: **forceful subjugation**, where armies imposed rule; **peaceful submission**, where rulers accepted Menilek's overlordship without war; and **boundary-delimitation treaties**, where diplomats fixed the new state's edges against the colonial powers crowding around it. Watch the GIF: treaty by treaty, campaign by campaign, the boundary line crawls around the highlands and lowlands until the shape you know from today's map stands complete.

## Sub lesson 2 — Centralisation and modernisation — the founding order

Expansion alone makes a big state, not a governed one. Menilek paired the sword and the treaty with **centralisation and modernisation attempts** — **administrative and religious reforms** that drew the enlarged territory under one government's order. Together they form **the founding order of the modern state**: the Ethiopia of the map, ruled from one centre, that the rest of this course — Adwa, the succession struggle, the empire of Haile Selassie — will inherit. With Menilek, the making-of-the-state lesson that began with Kassa of Quara in 1855 closes: one country, present shape, one government.
