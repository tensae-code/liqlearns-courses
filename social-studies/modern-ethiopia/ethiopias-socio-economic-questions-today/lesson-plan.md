# Lesson plan — Ethiopia's socio-economic questions today

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Ethiopia's socio-economic questions today
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 11 — Modern Ethiopia (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** State the growth story and its other half.
**This lesson's own level:** the Grade 12 depth of this title — it stands on the title's earlier lessons (the empire to 1941, the restoration) and needs nothing else; a learner may jump straight in, as every name is explained where it first appears.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. History is taught as story: the video script is a story script in the animated-map spirit.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Growth — and its discontents** — Say that post-1991 Ethiopia records real growth, and that the book teaches it together with its discontents.
2. **Poverty, land, unemployment, inflation** — Name the book's open questions: poverty, land, unemployment and inflation.
3. **Development policy as an open question — ADLI and after** — Say that development policy — the agriculture-led strategy (ADLI) and what follows it — is taught as an open question for the learner's generation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the growth story and its other half.
2. Name the four open questions: poverty, land, unemployment, inflation.
3. Say what ADLI stood for and why policy remains an open question.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A map of Ethiopia helps for the map lessons. Nothing else.
