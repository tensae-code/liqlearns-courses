# Video script — Ethiopia's socio-economic questions today

The story voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Told as story, in the animated-map spirit: the map and the timeline move as the story moves. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The post-1991 decades brought roads, dams, schools, universities and years of fast economic growth — the development story the country tells about itself. The book teaches that story whole, and then teaches its other half in the same breath: growth that many citizens still experience from the outside, as prices, queues and joblessness.

The book names the open questions plainly. Poverty still binds millions. Land — who holds it, who may use it, what a young farmer can hope for — remains the countryside's central question, as it was in 1974. Unemployment presses the towns' youth. Inflation eats wages and savings. These are not presented as solved; they are handed to the pupils' own generation as its work.

How should a poor, farming country grow? The post-1991 answer began with ADLI — Agricultural Development Led Industrialisation: raise farm incomes first, and let industry grow on agriculture's back. Later strategies widened the aim toward industry and exports. The book does not close this argument; it presents development policy as the open question of the pupils' generation — which is why this lesson ends with questions, not with a verdict.
