# Lesson plan — How indigenous knowledge behaves — oral, local, inherited

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** How indigenous knowledge behaves — oral, local, inherited
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the indigenous knowledge unit; Grade 10 Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous knowledge has three marks the review tests: it is transmitted orally from generation to generation, it provides problem-solving strategies for local communities, and it is generated within a community in its own location and culture — local, practised and inherited, not universal or written-first.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Indigenous knowledge — the knowledge that keeps a community alive. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

1. Recite the three marks: oral transmission, local problem-solving, community generation.
