# Jaarsummaa, Sinqe, Gumaa — Oromo ways of making peace

## Do it first

Three words, three ways a society refuses to let a wrong stand forever. Elders who mediate. Women who walk out with a sacred stick that no one may cross. A payment that buys back peace after the worst wrong of all — a killing. This lesson learns the three Oromo instruments of peace.

## Sub lesson 1 — Jaarsummaa — the elders reconcile

Jaarsummaa is the Oromo reconciliation process mediated by elders — the community's recognised peace-makers. When a dispute has torn the social fabric, the jaarsa (elders) convene the parties, hear the wrong in full, weigh the customary law, and join what was torn: a settlement spoken before witnesses, resting on the elders' moral authority rather than on force. The book figures the Jaarsuummaa process, and pupils study it as a procedure with steps, not a vague goodwill.

Set it beside Mada'a: two peoples, one architecture — elders judging, compensation repairing, reconciliation as the goal. The unit's comparisons are building a general claim about Ethiopian peace-making, and the Oromo systems are its richest case.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sinqe — the women's voice; Gumaa — closing the feud

Sinqe is the institution centred on the ritual stick of an Oromo woman — the book figures the Sinqee stick — through which women's collective voice intervenes against injustice. When women take up the siinqee and move together, their intervention carries a sacred protection: to violate it is to wrong the community's moral order itself. Sinqe gives the society's least formally powerful members an instrument no elder or official may lawfully brush aside — a designed check, inside custom, on the abuse of women and on injustice itself.

Gumaa answers the hardest case: a killing. Unsettled, a killing starts a feud — blood answering blood across generations. Gumaa is the blood-compensation settlement that closes it: the killer's side transfers compensation under the elders' ruling, the victim's side accepts settlement in place of vengeance, and the feud's chain is cut at its first link. Peace after the worst wrong is the system's whole point.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three instruments in order — elders reconcile; women intervene with the stick; compensation closes the feud — with one sentence each on how it works.
