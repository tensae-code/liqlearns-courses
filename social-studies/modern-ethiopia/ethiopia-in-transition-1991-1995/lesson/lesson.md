# Ethiopia in transition, 1991–1995

## Do it first

Before any explanation, tell this story to yourself in one breath, from the title alone: Ethiopia in transition, 1991–1995. What do you already think happened, and what would you need to check? Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Transitional Government and its Charter

After May 1991 the victors had to invent a state before they could run one. An EPRDF-led Transitional Government took power, and a transitional charter served as the country's founding law for the in-between years — the Charter period. Its task was enormous: keep the peace, feed the cities, and decide what Ethiopia now was.

## Sub lesson 2 — Eritrea's independence, 1993

The charter's order carried one enormous settlement inside it: Eritrea, whose insurgency had helped bring the Derg down, went its own way, and in 1993 it stood as an independent state. The federation of 1952, the annexation of 1962, and thirty years of war closed with a separation both sides' fighters had, in their different ways, fought toward. Ethiopia became a landlocked country — a fact its later history keeps meeting.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reordering the state on ethnic-federal lines

At home, the transition redrew the map. The old provinces gave way to regional states organised on ethnic-federal lines — the principle that Ethiopia's nations and nationalities would govern themselves inside one federal country. That reordering, begun under the charter, is what the 1995 constitution would write into permanent law, and it is the subject of the next lesson.
