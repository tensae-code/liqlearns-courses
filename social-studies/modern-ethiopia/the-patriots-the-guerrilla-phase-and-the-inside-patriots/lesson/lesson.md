# The Patriots — the guerrilla phase and the inside patriots

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: After the Yekatit 1937 massacre — 30,000 killed in Addis Ababa as printed — the Patriots turned guerrilla, fed by farmers and by inside patriots like Shewareged Gedle. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Graziani massacre

The second phase began after the **Yekatit 1937 Graziani massacre**: two youths, **Abriham Deboch and Moges Asgedom**, threw a bomb to kill the Viceroy **Marshal Graziani**, wounding him and killing some Italian officials; the Fascists answered with **three days of mass killing** in Addis Ababa and its surroundings, aimed chiefly at the **educated Ethiopians** suspected of planning it — **30,000 Ethiopians killed in Addis Ababa alone, as printed**. The massacre showed the survivors Fascism's true face and **strengthened the second phase**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guerrillas, farmers, inside patriots

The second phase was a **countryside guerrilla war**: sudden attacks on enemy forces, **cutting communication lines**, striking when conditions favoured. The Patriots solved supply by **sheltering in farmers' houses** and **capturing provisions and arms from the enemy**; medicine and intelligence came from the **'inside patriots'** living and working with the Italians — the most famous named is **Woizero Shewareged Gedle** — and many **women patriots** fought, among them **Kebedech Seyoum, Liqieylesh Beyan and Mulunesh Firde**. The struggle wore Italy down until her movement was confined to town administration; the named leaders fill a portrait gallery: **Ras Abebe Aregay, Dejazmach Hailemariam Mamo, Dejazmach Beqele Weya, Dejazmach Geressu Duki, Dejazmach Belay Zeleke, Bitweded Mangasha Jembere, Bitweded Ayalew Makonnen, Ras Amoraw Wubneh, Bitweded Adane Makonnen, Blata Takele Welde Hawariat, Ras Desta Damtew, Lt-General Haylu Kebede and Jagama Kelo**, among many others.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Grade 10 synthesis — two phases, the slogan, the women of the resistance

The Grade 10 book's synthesis of the whole resistance is stocked here whole. Resistance began almost immediately after the Battle of Maychew and never let the occupation become effective, especially in rural northern, eastern and central Ethiopia — under the slogan printed in Amharic in the book: "Stand! Rise up; you cannot sit idle while the nation is invaded." It fell into two phases divided by the reprisals after the failed assassination attempt on Graziani in February 1937: first, traditional battles led largely by upper-class nobles; then, guerrilla warfare — the phase this lesson teaches.

Inside the towns worked the Yewust Arbegnoch, the "inner patriots" — passing intelligence and secretly delivering weapons, medicine and food. Ethiopian women stand at the centre of the Grade 10 account: gathering information from the Italians, some arranging killings of Italian officials, others leading troops in battle or rallying soldiers after their husbands fell — Shewareged Gedle among the named examples — until liberation in 1941. The Grade 10 book makes the resistance's women a headline, and this lesson's depth follows it.
