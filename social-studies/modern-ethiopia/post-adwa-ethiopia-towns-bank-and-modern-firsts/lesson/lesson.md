# Post-Adwa Ethiopia — towns, bank and modern firsts

## Do it first

Before any dates, walk the story once in your head: The railway pulled trade and towns east–west: Bank of Abyssinia 1905, Taytu's first hotel 1907, and the dated firsts — car 1904 to aeroplane 1929. Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Trade turns; bank, hotel, towns

At the century's turn trade flow shifted **east and west**: Shewa's victory at **Embabo (1882)** and Shewa becoming the centre turned northern trade southward; the **Djibouti–Addis Ababa railway** and the British trade station at **Gambela (1904)**, above all for the coffee export trade, pulled likewise — and until mid-century Ethiopia's foreign trade ran through **Djibouti**. After Adwa foreign merchants mainly controlled trade, with some of the ruling class joining: **Empress Taytu opened Addis Ababa's first hotel in 1907** in her own name; the emperor and empress with great lords founded the **first bank, the Bank of Abyssinia, in 1905**; Gojjam's **Ras Hailu Tekle Haymanot** started a cinema and taxi service. Towns grew — **Gore and Goba** from Menilek's army centres, and **Dire Dawa, Nazret and Mojo** from the railway reaching Addis Ababa in **1917**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The modern firsts, dated

The modern firsts are dated: **motor car 1904, school 1908, hospital 1910, printing press 1912, aeroplane 1929** — with telegram and postal services, and sawmills, an ammunition factory and food-processing plants opened.
