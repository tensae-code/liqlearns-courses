# The fall of the Derg, 1991

## Do it first

Before any explanation, tell this story to yourself in one breath, from the title alone: The fall of the Derg, 1991. What do you already think happened, and what would you need to check? Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — Wars that would not end

The regime that had seized a united empire spent its life fighting to hold it. In Eritrea the insurgency that began in the 1960s grew into a full war; in Tigray another insurgency rose and hardened. Year after year the wars ate the budget, the harvests of young men sent to the fronts, and the regime's claim to be building anything at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — An economy failing, a patron falling

At home the economy failed under command, war and drought. Abroad, the regime's great patron — the Soviet Union and its bloc — was itself collapsing by the end of the 1980s; the aid and arms that had propped the Derg up thinned and stopped. A regime can survive unpopularity, and it can survive poverty, but it cannot easily survive both while its wars are being lost and its patron is disappearing.

## Sub lesson 3 — May 1991 — Mengistu flees, the EPRDF advances

In May 1991 the end came quickly. Mengistu Haile Mariam, the Derg's leader, fled the country. The EPRDF — the coalition whose forces had fought their way down from the north — advanced on the capital and entered Addis Ababa. Seventeen years of military rule were over. What would replace them is the next part of the course: the transition, the charter, and a constitution that remade the Ethiopian state.
