# Yohannes IV — unity by decentralisation

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Three contenders, in turn

Tewodros's fall reopened the question he had closed: who holds Ethiopia together? Three contenders came to the throne **in turn**. First, **Wagshum Gobeze of Lasta** — whose reign was **too short and uneventful** to settle anything. Then **Kassa Mircha of Tigray**, who would reign as **Yohannes IV**. And waiting in the south, **Menilek of Shewa** — whose turn, and whose very different empire, fills the next lesson. The succession itself teaches the era's truth: unity was not inherited; it had to be re-made by each emperor, in his own way.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rule by accommodating — and a church settled

Yohannes IV is renowned for attempting the opposite method to Tewodros's. Where Tewodros crushed regional power, Yohannes built a **decentralised system of administration** — a unifier who **ruled by accommodating regional power rather than crushing it**: regional rulers kept their place inside one empire, bound to its centre. And he settled the empire's other fracture: he **terminated the religious controversy within the Ethiopian Orthodox Church**, ending the doctrinal quarrels that had divided the church — and with it, the country — for generations. Decentralisation in government, settlement in religion: that is Yohannes's design for unity, and the picture's web — regions holding their circles, all lines running to one centre — is exactly it.
