# Video script — Heritage — what it is and why it matters

The story voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Told as story, in the animated-map spirit: the map and the timeline move as the story moves. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Heritage is what a people inherits and hands on. Some of it you can touch — a castle, a stele, a terraced hillside: the tangible heritage. Some of it lives only in doing — a festival, a chant, a system of governance, a coffee ceremony done the old way: the intangible heritage. Lose the stones and you lose the proof; lose the doing and you lose the meaning. A country must keep both.

Why keep it? The book gives heritage three kinds of value. Historical: it is evidence — the past you can stand in front of. National-symbolic: it is identity — the things a whole country points to and says, that is us. And socio-political: it binds communities, draws the world's respect and its travellers, and gives the present something shared to defend together.

In 1972 the world agreed a convention to protect heritage of outstanding value to all humankind. Its register sorts sites into two great types: cultural heritage — what people built and made — and natural heritage — what the earth made and people must not spoil. The next lesson walks Ethiopia's own entries on that register, site by site.
