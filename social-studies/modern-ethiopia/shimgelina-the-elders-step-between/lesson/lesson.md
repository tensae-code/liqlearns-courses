# Shimgelina — the elders step between

## Do it first

In the Amhara country the book pairs with this system, when a quarrel is ripening toward harm, the community does not wait for a court date. The old men come — unarmed, respected, and very hard to refuse — and they step between. This lesson studies the stepping-between itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The elders step between

Shimgelina — the book also spells it Shimegilena, and figures the peace-building process — is the cultural practice of elder mediation associated in the pair work with the Amhara people. When conflict opens between persons, families or villages, respected elders step between the parties: they hear grievances in full, invoke the shared norms both sides honour, and work the dispute toward reconciliation — a settlement both parties accept before the community, which is the only enforcement the system needs or wants.

Its authority is the same currency the whole unit trades in: age, reputation and the community's memory of the mediator's fairness. No office confers it and no salary pays for it; it is earned across a lifetime and spent, in old age, on peace.

## Sub lesson 2 — Beside the other systems — and beside your own

The book sets Shimgelina beside the Oromo systems for comparison, and the likenesses are structural: elders as mediators; reconciliation, not victory, as the goal; the community's acceptance as the settlement's force. The differences are of flavour and detail — each people's own names, rituals and emphases — laid over one Ethiopian architecture of peace.

Then the book turns the comparison home: pupils must compare their own locality's method of settling conflict with Shimgelina. Every community in Ethiopia runs some version of this machinery — named or unnamed, formal or kitchen-table. Finding yours, describing it honestly, and setting it beside the book's systems is the unit's standing exercise, and this lesson's homework in the deepest sense.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion Shimgelina's mechanism, its two likenesses with Jaarsummaa, and then your own locality's method — as the book asks.
