# Lesson plan — Shimgelina — the elders step between

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Shimgelina — the elders step between
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the indigenous knowledge unit; Grade 10 Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Shimgelina is the cultural peace-building process of elder mediation — respected elders stepping between conflicting parties toward reconciliation — set beside the Oromo systems for comparison, with pupils comparing their own locality's method.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Afar and Oromo systems — Shimgelina completes the unit's elder-mediation family. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The elders step between** — Shimgelina (Shimegilena), the figured cultural peace-building process: respected elders mediating conflict toward reconciliation.
2. **Beside the other systems — and beside your own** — the comparison the book sets: Shimgelina beside the Oromo systems, and pupils comparing their own locality's conflict method with it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Jaarsummaa, Sinqe, Gumaa — Oromo ways of making peace. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe Shimgelina's mechanism: who steps in, and toward what end.
2. Compare it with Jaarsummaa in two points.
3. Perform the book's task: compare your own locality's method with it.
