# Lesson plan — Indigenous knowledge — the knowledge that keeps a community alive

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Indigenous knowledge — the knowledge that keeps a community alive
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the indigenous knowledge unit's opening lesson; Grade 10 Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous knowledge is the knowledge local people use to make a living in a particular environment — passed down through oral tradition and practice — which helps a society judge what fits its way of life and guides its survival.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the learner's own community: everyone alive is carried by such knowledge. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What it is — local knowledge for living** — the definition: knowledge that helps a society judge which activities are compatible with its way of life; the knowledge local people use to make a living in a particular environment.
2. **Its forms — laws, rituals, decisions, peace** — indigenous laws, rituals, local classifications, management practices, decision-making processes, conflict-management practices and rights — and its role in overall and sustainable development.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the post-1991 lessons — the unit turns from regimes to what communities know and keep. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define indigenous knowledge in the book's terms.
2. Name its carriers: oral tradition and practice.
3. List its forms: laws, rituals, classifications, management, decisions, conflict practices, rights.
