# Lesson plan — Ethiopia's UNESCO heritages

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** Ethiopia's UNESCO heritages
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 11 — Modern Ethiopia (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Name the six stone-register places the book walks.
**This lesson's own level:** the Grade 12 depth of this title — it stands on the title's earlier lessons (the empire to 1941, the restoration) and needs nothing else; a learner may jump straight in, as every name is explained where it first appears.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. History is taught as story: the video script is a story script in the animated-map spirit.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The stone register: Aksum, Tiya, Harar, Lalibela, Konso, Gondar** — Walk the registered sites the book names: Aksum, Tiya, Harar Jegol, Lalibela, Konso, Gondar.
2. **The intangible register: Timket, Meskel, Gada, Fiche Chembelela, Irreecha** — Name the intangible inscriptions the book and its review name: Timket, Meskel, the Gada system, Fiche Chembelela, and Irreecha.
3. **Preservation duties — and the unregistered wealth** — Say that preservation is a duty the book lays on the learner's generation, and that much of Ethiopia's heritage is still unregistered.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six stone-register places the book walks.
2. Name the five intangible inscriptions.
3. State the preservation duty and the point about unregistered wealth.
