# The imperial ledger — trade, schools, roads, clinics

## Do it first

Every government keeps two books: the book of promises, read aloud at ceremonies, and the book of what actually got built. This lesson opens the second book for the restored empire — six sectors, one by one — because the opposition of the next lessons was doing exactly the same accounting.

## Sub lesson 1 — The commercial sectors — trade, industry, finance

The survey opens with the sectors that move goods and money. Trade carried the agricultural surplus — coffee and oilseeds above all — to the world, and brought the world's manufactures in; on its flows the state's revenue largely floated. Industry remained young and small beside the agrarian ocean: processing and light manufacture clustered in the towns, growing, but nowhere near transforming the economy's backbone.

The financial sector — banks and the machinery of credit and currency — served the modernising state and its commerce. Together the three sectors describe an economy monetising and connecting at the top, while its base, the subsistence village of the last lesson, changed far more slowly. That gap between the ledger's top and its base is the unit's recurring tension.

## Sub lesson 2 — The social sectors — education, transport, health

Education expanded from the tiny base the pre-war empire had built: schools and a university in the capital trained the administrators, teachers and officers the modern state required — and, in the event, its critics, for the student movement of the next lessons was educated in exactly these classrooms. Transport and communication — roads, the railway, posts and telephones — stitched the administered empire together, unevenly: the map of good roads and the map of imperial control are close cousins.

Health services reached furthest where the state was strongest and thinnest where most Ethiopians lived. Sector by sector, the ledger's pattern repeats: real building, real growth — and a reach that stopped far short of the promises, in a country where most people still met the state mainly as tax and tribute. The opposition that fills the rest of this unit — peasants, coup-makers, students — each read this ledger and drew their conclusions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the six sectors in order, then the ledger's pattern: real building, limited reach. Name the irony the lesson plants — the schools educated the critics.
