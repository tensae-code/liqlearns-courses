# Lesson plan — The imperial ledger — trade, schools, roads, clinics

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern Ethiopia · **Lesson:** The imperial ledger — trade, schools, roads, clinics
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern Ethiopia — the post-liberation unit; Grade 10 Unit 6 §6.1.5 survey (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the development ledger of the post-liberation decades, sector by sector — trade, industry, finance, education, transport and communication, health — the record against which the regime's promises, and its opposition, are measured.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Land, rist and gult — the agrarian base this ledger builds on. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The commercial sectors — trade, industry, finance** — the empire's trade, its young industry and its financial sector in the post-liberation decades.
2. **The social sectors — education, transport, health** — schools, roads and communications, and clinics: the services whose reach — and limits — the opposition measured.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Land, rist and gult — who ate from whose field. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six sectors the survey covers.
2. Say what the ledger is for: measuring promises against record.
3. Give one honest summary line for the imperial development record.
