# The 1995 Constitution and federal Ethiopia

## Do it first

Before any explanation, tell this story to yourself in one breath, from the title alone: The 1995 Constitution and federal Ethiopia. What do you already think happened, and what would you need to check? Now read it slowly, part by part, and let the story carry the facts.

## Sub lesson 1 — A federal republic

In 1995 the transition closed into a constitution. Ethiopia became the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia — a federation: power divided between a federal government and regional states, each with its own government, inside one country. After an empire run from one desk and a military regime run from one committee, the constitution's answer was to divide power on paper and in structure.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nations, nationalities, and Article 39

The constitution vests sovereignty in Ethiopia's nations, nationalities and peoples, and guarantees their rights — to their language, culture, and self-government. Its most debated clause is Article 39, which gives every nation and nationality the right even to secession — to leave the federation — under the constitution's own procedures. The book presents the clause plainly; the lesson does the same, and leaves the arguing to the learner's own generation, where the book leaves it too.

## Sub lesson 3 — Elections and the party landscape since 1995

Since 1995 the country has been governed through elections under this constitution, with a party landscape that has changed across the decades — ruling coalitions, opposition parties, and contests the book surveys as the working life of the federal order. The constitution is the frame; the politics inside the frame is the country's continuing story.
