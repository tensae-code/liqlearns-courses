# Fiji — indentured Indians and blackbirding — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Governor Arthur Gordon, needing plantation labour iTaukei would not supply, has Solomon Islanders blackbirded and, from the Leonidas in 1879 to 1916, Indian girmit labourers brought from Calcutta — most stay after indenture ends and become farmers and businessmen.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Continuity and Change: arrival of other ethnic groups

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Year 5 Social Studies (title from contents/front matter; PDF opens at the Acknowledgement page)* is about United Nations agencies and their founders.
**Answer: False** — it is about Continuity and Change: arrival of other ethnic groups

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Girmit life is marked by a broken 40-women-per-100-men quota, jealousy and fighting, and no schooling for children, until C.F. Andrews and W.W. Pearson investigate in 1915 and their report brings the system down.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The Early Sugar Industry: indentured labourers in Fiji

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Social Science Year 10 — Global Perspectives, Local Interaction* is about United Nations agencies and their founders.
**Answer: False** — it is about The Early Sugar Industry: indentured labourers in Fiji

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** In 1879 Fiji changes again as Indians arrive, most enticed by honeyed promises and deceptions to sign an indenture contract — the girmitiya, not slaves, work the plantations under a pass system, and many make Fiji home.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Indians in Fiji

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *History Year 11 — Cultural Interaction and Integration, Unit 1: Origin of Fiji's People (Kilakila Na kena Qele), prepared by Morgan Tuimaleali'ifano* is about United Nations agencies and their founders.
**Answer: False** — it is about Indians in Fiji
