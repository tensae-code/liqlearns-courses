## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Continuity and Change: arrival of other ethnic groups** — Governor Arthur Gordon, needing plantation labour iTaukei would not supply, has Solomon Islanders blackbirded and, from the Leonidas in 1879 to 1916, Indian girmit labourers brought from Calcutta — most stay after indenture ends and become farmers and businessmen
2. **The Early Sugar Industry: indentured labourers in Fiji** — girmit life is marked by a broken 40-women-per-100-men quota, jealousy and fighting, and no schooling for children, until C.F. Andrews and W.W. Pearson investigate in 1915 and their report brings the system down
3. **Indians in Fiji** — in 1879 Fiji changes again as Indians arrive, most enticed by honeyed promises and deceptions to sign an indenture contract — the girmitiya, not slaves, work the plantations under a pass system, and many make Fiji home
