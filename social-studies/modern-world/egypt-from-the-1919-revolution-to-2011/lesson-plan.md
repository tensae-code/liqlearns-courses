# Lesson plan — Egypt — from the 1919 revolution to 2011

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Egypt — from the 1919 revolution to 2011
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From the protectorate of 1914 and the revolution of 1919 through Nasser, Sadat and Camp David, Egypt's story in Syria's book ends with the popular revolution of 2011.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Egypt — from the 1919 revolution to 2011** — The protectorate of 1914 and Saad Zaghloul's exile fired the revolution of 1919; Britain's declaration of 28 February 1922 ended the protectorate with reservations, and the 1936 treaty followed; the coup of 23 July 1952 made Egypt a republic under Naguib then Nasser — evacuation, nationalisations, the High Dam, the Tripartite Aggression of 1956, union with Syria 1958–1961, the June 1967 loss of Sinai, Sadat and the October 1973 war and Camp David 1979 — until the popular revolution ended Mubarak's rule in 2011.
