# Lesson plan — The Kalakani interlude and Nadir Shah

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Kalakani interlude and Nadir Shah
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Afghanistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** revolt against Amanullah's reforms lifts the Tajik bandit-king Kalakani to Kabul's throne for nine months
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lesson 25: How did Habibullah Kalakani come to power?** — revolt against Amanullah's reforms lifts the Tajik bandit-king Kalakani to Kabul's throne for nine months
2. **Lesson 26: Do you know how Muhammad Nadir Shah reached the throne?** — Nadir Khan, the independence war's general, marches from the south, ends the Kalakani interlude and founds a new royal line
3. **Lesson 21: The reaction against the reforms** — revolts gather against the pace of change and the reforming king's throne shakes
4. **Lesson 22: The end of the crisis and the consolidation of power, 1929 (1308 SH)** — Nadir Khan ends the Kalakani crisis and consolidates a new royal order
5. **Lesson 23: The drafting and compilation of the Usul-nama (constitution)** — the new order writes its fundamental principles into a constitution

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
