# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan Inside The Russian Empire

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan Inside The Russian Empire
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the 1867–68 statute remakes steppe administration
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§§33–34 Tsarism's reforms in Kazakhstan in the second half of the 19th century** — the 1867–68 statute remakes steppe administration
2. **§§35–36 The liberation struggle of 1860–1870** — Ural and Turgai Kazakhs resist the new order by arms
3. **§37 Colonial policy's influence on the traditional Kazakh economy** — pasture loss forces herders toward farming and wage work
4. **§38 The Russian Empire's resettlement policy** — peasant settlers take the best-watered Kazakh lands
5. **§39 Changes in traditional Kazakh society** — aul, clan and biy courts adapt under colonial rule
6. **§§40–41 Socio-economic development in the second half of the 19th century; capitalist relations penetrate** — fairs, money and hired labour enter the steppe economy
7. **§42 Administrative-territorial reforms of the 1880s–90s** — the Steppe Statute of 1891 completes the colonial framework
8. **§43 Trade relations develop in Kazakhstan** — caravan fairs link the steppe to Russia and Central Asia
9. **§§31–32 Administrative-territorial reforms (second half of the 19th century)** — the Provisional Statute divides the steppe into oblasts and uezds
10. **§§33–34 The liberation struggle in Kazakhstan in the 1860s–70s** — armed risings answer the new colonial administration
11. **§§35–36 Tsarism's mass-colonisation resettlement policy in Kazakhstan** — settler villages take the fertile northern pastures
12. **§§37–38 Capitalist relations develop in Kazakhstan** — money, fairs and wage labour reshape the aul
13. **§§39–40 Fair trade develops** — the great steppe fairs link herders to world markets
14. **§§41–42 Kazakh society in the second half of the 19th century** — clan society stratifies under colonial pressure
