# Lesson plan — Malay rulers answer the West — Bangkok 1909 and Johor

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Malay rulers answer the West — Bangkok 1909 and Johor
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Malaysia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Bangkok Treaty of 1909 splits the northern Malay states between Siam and Britain; Terengganu rejects it outright, and Johor's Sultan Abu Bakar modernises precisely to leave Britain no opening to intervene
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The rulers' wisdom** — the Bangkok Treaty of 1909 splits the northern Malay states between Siam and Britain; Terengganu's sultan, ulama and chiefs reject it outright, and Kelantan's Sultan Muhammad IV hears the news from the British officer J.S. Mason; Johor answers differently — Sultan Abu Bakar, with a capable and loyal Majlis Jemaah Menteri, continues Temenggong Daeng Ibrahim's modernisation of administration, finance and law precisely to leave Britain no opening to intervene (Bab 8: the rulers' wisdom)
