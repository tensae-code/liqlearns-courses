# Romanticism — art of feeling and nation

## Do it first

Before you read on, picture the scene this lesson is about, from its title alone: Romanticism — art of feeling and nation. What do you expect to happen, and what do you expect to change? Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — A revolt of feeling

Reason's age provokes a revolt of feeling.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 2 — Goya and the French romantics

Goya paints war's madness and monsters of sleep, and French romantics fill canvases with shipwrecks, revolutions and oriental dreams.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 3 — The voice of nations

Everywhere art becomes the voice of nations longing to be free.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: Reason's age provokes a revolt of feeling. Then: Goya paints war's madness and monsters of sleep, and French romantics fill canvases with shipwrecks, revolutions and oriental dreams. And finally: Everywhere art becomes the voice of nations longing to be free. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
