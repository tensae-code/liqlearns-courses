# Lesson plan — Central America Dictatorship Civil War And Peace

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Central America Dictatorship Civil War And Peace
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Honduras, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the 1929 crash breaks the banana-and-coffee economies and dictators rise — Ubico (1931–1944) in Guatemala, Hernández Martínez in El Salvador, whose answer to a rising is La Matanza of 10,000–30,000 including Farabundo Martí, Carías in Honduras, Somoza in Nicaragua — all keeping power by armies, rewritten laws and US companies' support
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lección 27 Democratización y autoritarismo: las dictaduras** — the 1929 crash breaks the banana-and-coffee economies and dictators rise — Ubico (1931–1944) in Guatemala, Hernández Martínez in El Salvador, whose answer to a rising is La Matanza of 10,000–30,000 including Farabundo Martí, Carías in Honduras, Somoza in Nicaragua — all keeping power by armies, rewritten laws and US companies' support
2. **Lección 27: los acuerdos de paz** — Costa Rica, army-free since 1949, leads the way out — Esquipulas (1987), Chapultepec for El Salvador (1992), Guatemala's final peace (1996); guerrillas (FSLN, FMLN, URNG) disarm and become legal parties that later win power
