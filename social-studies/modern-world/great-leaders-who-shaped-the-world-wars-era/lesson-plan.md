# Lesson plan — Great leaders who shaped the world-wars era

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Great leaders who shaped the world-wars era
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Papua New Guinea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** One generation of giants — Hitler hunting lebensraum through Austria, Czechoslovakia and Poland, Stalin, Mussolini, Churchill, Wilson, Roosevelt, Truman and Martin Luther King — each studied in depth for how a single person bends politics, society and belief
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Influence of great leaders (just before WWI – WWII and aftermath)** — one generation of giants — Hitler hunting lebensraum through Austria, Czechoslovakia and Poland, Stalin, Mussolini, Churchill, Wilson, Roosevelt, Truman and Martin Luther King — each studied in depth for how a single person bends politics, society and belief
2. **Folio** — ten politically significant lives from WWII to the present, at least one Papua New Guinean, profiled and judged — early life, beliefs, rise, achievements good and bad, and the legacy each left
3. **Influence of individuals on systems of power, authority and governance** — Weber's charisma — authority legitimised by a leader's exceptional qualities, a relationship between leader and followers that challenges every settled order and is "often seen as revolutionary"
4. **Impact of powerful individuals and groups on society** — 1 September 1969 — young officers led by Gaddafi overthrow King Idris in a bloodless coup; the Revolutionary Command Council abolishes the monarchy, makes education and medical care free, and per-capita income climbs — students explain why such leaders and governments are removed from power

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
