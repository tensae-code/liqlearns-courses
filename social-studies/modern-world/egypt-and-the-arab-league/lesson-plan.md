# Lesson plan — Egypt and the road to the Arab League

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Egypt and the road to the Arab League
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Shared language, history and a common struggle pull the Arabs together despite colonialism's wedges — Nahhas Pasha's call of 1943 opens the Cairo consultations, the Alexandria Protocol of 1944 fixes the rules, and the Arab League is born.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chapter 7: Egypt and the issues of the contemporary Arab world — the road to the Arab League** — Colonialism drives its wedges — Zaydi against Sunni, Maronite against Muslim, republic against throne, French and Italian forced on Arab schoolchildren — yet language, history and a shared war pull the Arabs together: Taif in 1934, the Iraq–Saudi treaty of 1936, Egypt's own treaty with Saudi Arabia the same year; Nahhas Pasha's call of March 1943 opens the Cairo consultations, the Alexandria Protocol of 1944 fixes the League's rules — equality of members, no force between them, respect for existing borders — and the Arab League is born
