# Guyana: our nation — independence and symbols

## Do it first

Think of your own birthday: one day in the year that is yours, when everyone remembers the day you began. A country has days like that too. Before you read on, say in your mind: what day do you think Guyana's birthday is — and how would a country show everyone that it is a real country, standing on its own? Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guyana is an independent state

In Guyana's first social-studies book, two children — Ron and Sue — carry the learner through the year. When the book reaches the chapter called Our Nation, Ron and Sue's lessons stop being about home and school and become about the whole country.

They learn that Guyana is an independent state. Independent means the country stands on its own and makes its own decisions. Guyana became independent on 26 May 1966. And the new nation is led by a President — the head of the country its own people choose to lead them.

That is the first way Guyana's book gives a six-year-old the nation's story: not a long list of rulers, but one date to hold — the day the country began to stand on its own.

## Sub lesson 2 — The symbols of the nation

A new country needs a face — signs that say "this is us" wherever they are seen or heard. Ron and Sue learn some of Guyana's national symbols: the flag, the coat of arms, and the national bird.

Two of the symbols are not drawn but performed. The children learn the National Anthem — the nation's own song, sung standing together — and the National Pledge — the nation's promise words, said together. A flag can be drawn in a copybook; an anthem and a pledge live in the children's own voices. Together, the symbols give the new nation a face its children can draw and sing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Celebrations that remember

A country's year is marked by celebrations, and Guyana's book teaches two that carry the nation's story.

On Remembrance Day, people wear red poppies. The poppies honour those who went to war — the country stops, remembers them, and says their going is not forgotten.

And then comes Mashramani — Guyana's great festival of music and colour in the streets. Mashramani celebrates the day Guyana became a Co-operative Republic: 23 February 1970. Independence in 1966 made Guyana its own country; the republic day in 1970 is the second date in the story, and the festival is how the country sings about it every year.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: a country becomes independent on 26 May 1966, led by a President; its symbols — flag, coat of arms, national bird, anthem and pledge — give it a face; and its celebrations remember — poppies for those who went to war, Mashramani for the Co-operative Republic of 23 February 1970. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
