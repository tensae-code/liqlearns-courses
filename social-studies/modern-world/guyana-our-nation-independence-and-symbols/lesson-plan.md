# Lesson plan — Guyana: our nation — independence and symbols

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guyana: our nation — independence and symbols
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Guyana, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Guyana became an independent state on 26 May 1966, led by a President, with national symbols — flag, coat of arms, national bird, anthem and pledge — that give the new nation a face its children can draw and sing; and its celebrations remember that story, from Remembrance Day poppies to Mashramani, which marks Guyana becoming a Co-operative Republic on 23 February 1970.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the learner's own country feeling: a birthday for a country, and the signs that stand for it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Guyana is an independent state** — Ron and Sue learn their country became independent on 26 May 1966 and is led by a President.
2. **The symbols of the nation** — flag, coat of arms, national bird, national anthem and national pledge give the new nation a face its children can draw and sing.
3. **Celebrations that remember** — Remembrance Day poppies honour those who went to war, and Mashramani celebrates Guyana becoming a Co-operative Republic on 23 February 1970.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nationalism — the feeling that made nation states. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say when Guyana became independent — 26 May 1966 — and who leads the country.
2. Name Guyana's national symbols as the book teaches them: flag, coat of arms, national bird, anthem and pledge.
3. Say what Remembrance Day poppies honour.
4. Say what Mashramani celebrates, and the date Guyana became a Co-operative Republic — 23 February 1970.
