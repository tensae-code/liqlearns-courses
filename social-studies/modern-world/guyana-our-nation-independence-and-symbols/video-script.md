# Video script — Guyana: our nation — independence and symbols

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: two children, Ron and Sue — the two children Guyana's book follows — learn their country's story at school. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Ron and Sue come to school. Today the lesson is not about their street or their family. Today it is about their whole country.

Their teacher writes a date on the board: 26 May 1966. On that day, Guyana became independent — a country standing on its own, led by its own President.

Then the symbols rise, one by one: the flag, bright and new; the coat of arms; the national bird. The children stand for the anthem, and say the pledge — the promise words of the new nation.

Last, the year turns to its celebrations. Red poppies for Remembrance Day, for those who went to war and are remembered. And then music and colour in the streets: Mashramani — the festival for 23 February 1970, the day Guyana became a Co-operative Republic.

Two dates, a flag, a song and a promise. That is how a six-year-old first holds a whole nation.
