## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 1 Lesson 1: partition inside Palestine** — the Zionist movement plants its first settlement, Montefiore, south-west of Jerusalem's wall in 1856, and the settlements double as training camps and arms stores; after the 1948 Nakba the occupation holds over 78% of Palestine.
2. **Unit 6 Lesson 2: racial discrimination — the 1948 catastrophe** — the lesson names 1948 the great racist catastrophe: armed Zionist gangs occupy 77% of Palestine, commit massacres, destroy 531 Palestinian villages and drive about a million Palestinians into refuge in what remains of Palestine and in Jordan, Syria, Lebanon and the wider diaspora; the rest of Palestine is occupied in June 1967.
3. **Unit 2 Lesson 3: colonialism and Zionism** — the Zionist idea crystallises in Europe (Shaftesbury's "a land without a people" slogan dissected and refuted in class), the colonial powers back it for their own ends, Herzl organises the movement and the Basel Congress of 1897 resolves on a Jewish national home in Palestine — while Sultan Abdul Hamid II refuses to sell "even one foot" of an empire bought with his people's blood.
4. **Unit 2 Lesson 4: Zionist immigration and settlement colonisation in the late Ottoman era** — the machinery is built — Jewish Colonial Trust (1899), Histadrut, the kibbutz — and the first settlements rise: a Jewish quarter outside Jerusalem's walls (1857), Petah Tikva on the lands of Mlabbis (1882), Zikhron Ya'akov on Zamarin; land is got by purchase and forged deeds through corrupt officials and consulates, the empire answers with the 1883 waqf law and the 1887 "red passport" three-month rule, and Palestinians answer in print — Ruhi al-Khalidi's History of Zionism and the papers al-Karmil and al-Manar.
5. **Unit 2 Lesson 5: the international conspiracies of the First World War** — Britain promises Sharif Hussein (first letter 14 July 1915, on the Damascus Protocol's terms) a great Arab state, while secretly signing Sykes–Picot (1916) with France and Russia to divide the same lands, then issues the Balfour Declaration (2 November 1917) promising the Zionists a national home; Allenby enters Jerusalem on 9 December 1917 and Arab anger is met with fresh British reassurances.
6. **Unit 3 Lesson 2: mandate immigration and land takeover** — immigration waves climb steeply across the mandate years (one period alone bringing some 215,000), the mandate's own text lets the administration work public projects with the Jewish Agency, and land passes steadily to the settlers.
7. **Unit 4 Lesson 1: the 1948 War (the Nakba)** — towns fall before any Arab army enters — Britain bars Arab intervention before 15 May 1948 and Abd al-Qadir al-Husseini is martyred at al-Qastal; some 15,000 Arab fighters (Egypt, Jordan, Iraq, Lebanon, Syria) then enter, the first truce (11 June 1948) is used by the Zionists to rearm with American and British help, and the war ends with about a million Palestinians displaced, over 60 massacres and 531 villages destroyed (al-Mansura, Saffuriyya, Deir Yassin, al-Faluja among them), Israel recognised and seated at the UN, and Resolution 194 affirming the refugees' right of return.
