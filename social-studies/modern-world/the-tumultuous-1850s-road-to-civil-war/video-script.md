# Video script — The tumultuous 1850s — the road to civil war

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told straight, in the order it happened. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A compromise that only paused the quarrel. A bleeding territory, a court ruling that denied a people's rights, a raid, an election — and the Union split.

The Compromise of 1850 only paused the quarrel over slavery's spread. The Kansas-Nebraska Act then tore the pause open: Kansas bled as pro- and anti-slavery settlers fought — and the fighting birthed the Republican Party, built to stop slavery's expansion.

The Supreme Court's Dred Scott decision told Black Americans they had no rights a court must respect — a ruling meant to settle the question that instead inflamed it. Then John Brown raided Harpers Ferry, trying to arm a slave rising; the South saw its nightmare armed, the North argued over a martyr.

Lincoln's election in 1860 — a Republican president pledged against slavery's spread — split the Union. Southern states began to leave. The tumultuous decade ended where the next lesson begins: secession and war.
