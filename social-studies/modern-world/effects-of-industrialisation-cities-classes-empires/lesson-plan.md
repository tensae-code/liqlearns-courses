# Lesson plan — Effects of industrialisation — cities, classes, empires

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Effects of industrialisation — cities, classes, empires
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — the age of revolutions unit; the Grade 11 book's second industrial lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the factory system remade the whole of society — crowds moved into industrial cities, a middle class and a working class grew up facing each other, workers organised and socialism was born, and the hunt for markets and raw materials pushed Europe's states out into empire.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The Industrial Revolution lesson — what changed in how things were made. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Cities fill, people multiply** — urbanisation and population growth as the mills pull people off the land.
2. **Two new classes face each other** — a property-owning middle class and a wage-working class, and the unions and socialism their clash produced.
3. **The push outward — markets, materials, empire** — industry's hunger for raw materials and buyers drives the new imperialism.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Industrial Revolution — what the factory system is. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell how industrialisation filled new cities and grew the population.
2. Name the two new classes the factories made, and say what each one owned and did.
3. Say why workers formed trade unions, and where socialism came from.
4. Explain why industrial states went looking for colonies — markets to sell to and raw materials to feed the mills.

## If the learner is stuck
If the learner mixes the two classes up, anchor them at the factory gate: the middle class owns the mill and counts the money; the working class stands at the machines and is paid by the week. Everything else in the lesson hangs on that one picture.
