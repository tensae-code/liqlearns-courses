# Lesson plan — Yemeni resistance to Ottoman rule

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Yemeni resistance to Ottoman rule
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Yemen, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Yemen answers occupation with its mountains — fortresses like Thula, Kawkaban and Shahara, cave-and-pass warfare, and Imams who will side with a just Imam against a tyrant wali or the reverse
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Yemeni resistance to Ottoman rule** — yemen answers occupation with its mountains — fortresses like Thula, Kawkaban and Shahara, cave-and-pass warfare, and Imams who will side with a just Imam against a tyrant wali or the reverse, caring only for justice; Zabid and Aden resist from the first day, the southern plains tribes raid, Taiz and Ibb bleed the columns in Wadi Dhubab, Imam Sharaf al-Din's sons maul the army marching on Sanaa, and al-Qasim's revolt rolls from Aden to Saada until al-Mu'ayyad and Muhammad bin al-Qasim drive the Ottomans out entirely in 1635; in the second rule the pattern repeats — Sanaa besieged in 1892, Shahara's slaughter earning Yemen the Ottoman name "graveyard of Anatolia", uprisings everywhere forcing the moderate Husayn Hilmi Pasha in 1908, revolt and siege again in 1910, the Da'an peace of 1911, and Imam Yahya entering Sanaa in 1918
