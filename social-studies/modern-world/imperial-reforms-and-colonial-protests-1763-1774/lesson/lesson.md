# Imperial reforms and colonial protests, 1763–1774

## Do it first

Britain won its war and sent the colonies the bill. Eleven years of taxes, boycotts and tea in a harbour turned loyal colonists into a Congress. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — War debt brings the Stamp Act

Britain's war debt brought new imperial reforms — above all the Stamp Act, a tax laid directly on the colonies. Colonists who had cheered the victory now faced paying for it, without a voice in the Parliament that taxed them.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Boycotts, the Townshend Acts and the Boston Tea Party

The Sons and Daughters of Liberty answered with boycotts — refusing British goods until the taxes bent. The Townshend Acts taxed anew; protest sharpened; and in Boston, tea went into the harbour in the Boston Tea Party.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Coercive Acts and the First Continental Congress

Britain answered with the Coercive Acts to punish Massachusetts. The colonies answered together: the First Continental Congress met — and in meeting, discovered an American identity. Protest had made a people.
