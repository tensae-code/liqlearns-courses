# Lesson plan — Central America Independence And Federation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Central America Independence And Federation
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Nicaragua, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** internal grievances and external revolutions converge; independence is the end of a long process, not a single day
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Independencia de Centroamérica: causas internas y externas** — internal grievances and external revolutions converge; independence is the end of a long process, not a single day
2. **Movimientos populares de 1811 en Centroamérica** — in 1811 popular movements rise in San Salvador, León and Granada a decade before independence is won
3. **Proclamación de la Independencia de Centroamérica** — on 15 September 1821 the Act is signed in Guatemala and the isthmus leaves Spain
4. **Próceres de la Independencia de Centroamérica** — the próceres earn the title by ending colonial rule — among them priest Tomás Ruiz of Chinandega, tortured and held nearly seven years in irons for the Belén conspiracy
5. **Centroamérica ante la anexión a México** — the new provinces are annexed to Mexico and must fight again, this time to be free of an American empire
6. **La Federación Centroamericana** — the five provinces try to stay one country in a federation, the isthmus's great failed union
7. **Independencia de México (Nueva España) y Centroamérica** — Hidalgo's Grito is crushed, but when the Cádiz Constitution threatens criollo interests, Iturbide and Guerrero join in the Plan de Iguala of 24 February 1821
