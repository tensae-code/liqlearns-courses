# Lesson plan — Vietnam Fifty Four Ethnic Groups

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Fifty Four Ethnic Groups
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the word "dân tộc" carries two senses — the nation, the whole people of a country (the Vietnamese nation), and the ethnic group, a community sharing a language, a culture and an ethnic self-consciousness (the Kinh, Mường and Thái peoples); by population the country's groups divide into the majority group and the minority groups, the 2011 government decree quoted defining a minority ethnic group as one whose population is smaller than the majority's on the territory of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam, the majority being the group over 50% of the national population in the census; the 2019 census table (General Statistics Office) then names all 54 groups with their numbers — the Kinh 82,085,826 people, 85.32% of the country, then the Tày (1,845,492), Thái (1,820,950), Mường (1,452,095), Mông (1,393,547), Khơ-me (1,319,652), Nùng, Dao, Hoa, Gia Rai, Ê Đê, Ba Na, Xơ Đăng, Sán Chay, Cơ Ho, Sán Dìu, Chăm (178,948), Hrê and on down through the Raglai, Mnông, Xtiêng, Bru–Vân Kiều, Thổ, Khơ Mú, Cơ Tu, Giáy, Gié–Triêng, Tà Ôi, Mạ, Co, Chơ Ro, Xinh Mun, Hà Nhì, Chu Ru, Lào and Kháng to the smallest communities — the La Chí, Phù Lá, La Hủ, La Ha, Pà Thẻn, Chứt, Lự, Lô Lô, Mảng, Cờ Lao, Bố Y, Cống, Ngái (1,649), Si La (909), Pu Péo (903), Rơ Măm (639), Brâu (525) and Ơ Đu (428); and by language the 54 groups fall into five language families with eight language groups, the diagram sorting them: Austroasiatic (Việt–Mường: Kinh, Mường, Thổ, Chứt; Môn–Khơ-me: Khơ Mú, Xinh Mun, Mảng, Kháng, Ơ Đu, Bru–Vân Kiều, Tà Ôi, Cơ Tu, Gié–Triêng, Co, Hrê, Brâu, Rơ Măm, Ba Na, Xơ Đăng, Mnông, Cơ Ho, Mạ, Xtiêng, Chơ Ro, Khơ-me), Tai–Kadai (Tày–Thái: Tày, Thái, Nùng, Giáy, Lào, Lự, Sán Chay, Bố Y; Ka-đai: La Chí, La Ha, Cờ Lao, Pu Péo), Hmong–Dao (Mông, Dao, Pà Thẻn), Austronesian (Malayo-Polynesian: Chăm, Gia Rai, Ê Đê, Chu Ru, Raglai) and Sino-Tibetan (Hoa: Hoa, Sán Dìu, Ngái; Tạng–Miến: Hà Nhì, Phù Lá, La Hủ, Lô Lô, Cống, Si La) — the application sends pupils to find which groups live in their own commune or province and to which families they belong
