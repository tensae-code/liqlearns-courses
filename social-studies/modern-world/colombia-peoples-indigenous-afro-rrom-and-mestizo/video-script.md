# Video script — Colombia's peoples — Indigenous, Afro-Colombian, Rrom and mestizo

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Colombia's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Colombia is Indigenous, Afro-Colombian, Raizal, Palenquero and Rrom at once — a mestizo people born of their meeting, recognised in the 1991 Constitution.

Indigenous peoples, Black/Afro-Colombian, Raizal and Palenquero communities, and the Rrom (Gitano) people are presented one by one, and Colombia is then named a mestizo people born of their meeting.

Colombia is mestizo with African roots that persist and change; Afro-Colombian children narrate their own lives — Antonio Mow, raizal of San Andrés, and Juana Pabla Matamba, palenquera — the 1991 Constitution recognises Afro-Colombian rights, and Manuel Zapata Olivella heads the roll of the distinguished.

94 Indigenous peoples live in Colombia, many bilingual or trilingual, educated first by elders' counsel, protected in resguardos; Páez leader and senator Jesús Enrique Piñacué and the Wayúu of La Guajira carry the telling.

Diversity predates the Europeans; Colombia's surviving Indigenous groups are listed by territory — Emberá, the peoples of Vaupés and more — and the Guambianos are reconstructing their own history, memory and legacies, long forgotten and ignored.

Cirilo cannot believe a man will tell him everything he always wanted to know about his ancestors; in the Baudó the newborn's ombligo ritual binds the child to its land.
