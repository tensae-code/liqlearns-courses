# Foreign friends of the Liberation War

## Do it first

In 1971 a young reporter hid in a Dhaka hotel while every other foreign journalist was expelled — because the world had to know. Before you read on, list who else, far away, might help a people's war. Hold your list; the foreign friends are coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — The journalists — Simon Dring and the world's wires

Bangladesh's Grade 7 book honours the foreigners who helped in 1971 — and starts with a reporter. On 25 March 1971 foreign reporters are expelled from Dhaka; 26-year-old Simon Dring hides in the Hotel Intercontinental instead, and cables the massacre to The Daily Telegraph. After him, the BBC and Akashvani carry the war to the world. The book's point: a war the world cannot see can be denied; the journalists made denial impossible.

[Picture slot — a picture of a 1971 refugee camp on India's border — long lines of families with bundles, aid workers and tents stretching to the horizon — the student points to the scale of the camp and says how many refugees the book counts.]

## Sub lesson 2 — Refugees, diplomacy and song

The second half of the Grade 7 chapter widens the circle of friends. About one crore — ten million — refugees flood into India, which shelters, feeds and speaks for them under Indira Gandhi. The Soviet Union backs Bangladesh diplomatically. US Senator Edward Kennedy visits the camps and carries their story home. And George Harrison sings for Bangladesh, turning a concert into aid and attention. Shelter, diplomacy, witness and song: the book counts all four as friendship in wartime.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 2 beats: The journalists — Simon Dring and the world's wires; Refugees, diplomacy and song. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory: name the reporter who hid and cabled the massacre: Simon Dring, to The Daily Telegraph.
2. Write, from memory: say when foreign reporters were expelled: 25 March 1971.
3. Write, from memory: name the broadcasters who carried the war to the world: the BBC and Akashvani.
4. Write, from memory: say how many refugees fled to India: about one crore (ten million).
5. Write, from memory: name India's leader who sheltered and spoke for them: Indira Gandhi.
6. Write, from memory: name the other friends and their kinds of help: the Soviet Union (diplomacy), Edward Kennedy (witness), George Harrison (song).
