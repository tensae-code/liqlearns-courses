# Lesson plan — The United States and Latin America — Monroe to the Maine

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The United States and Latin America — Monroe to the Maine
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Colombia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Monroe warns Europe off, the United States takes Mexico's north in 1846, the Platt Amendment binds Cuba, and the Maine's explosion gives the excuse for war on Spain
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Tema 18: Relations of the United States with Latin America** — Monroe's discourse warns Europe off; on 13 May 1846 Congress declares war on Mexico and takes Arizona, California, Nevada and New Mexico; and the Maine's explosion gives the excuse for war on Spain
2. **Tema 9: The United States and its interventionist role in Latin America and the Caribbean** — the Platt Amendment's articles let Washington bind Cuba's foreign agreements, the template of early-century intervention

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
