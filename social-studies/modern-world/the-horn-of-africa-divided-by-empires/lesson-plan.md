# Lesson plan — The Horn of Africa — divided by empires

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Horn of Africa — divided by empires
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The empires carve up the Somali lands, ban Arabic and plunder — until Somalia unites in 1960, Djibouti and the Comoros follow, and Eritrea wins independence in 1993.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Horn of Africa — divided by empires** — The empires carved up the Somali lands — Djibouti to France, the north to Britain, the south and Eritrea to Italy — and banned Arabic and plundered the resources; Sayyid Muhammad ibn Abd Allah Hassan's revolt held out against Britain from 1899 to 1920, the Somali Youth League carried the struggle from 1943, and north and south united as the Somali Republic in 1960; Eritrea's armed struggle from 1961 won independence in 1993, Djibouti in 1977, and the Comoros — French since 1892 — declared independence in 1975.
