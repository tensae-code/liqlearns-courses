# Lesson plan — Gallipoli and the Ottoman fronts, 1914–1918

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Gallipoli and the Ottoman fronts, 1914–1918
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Turkey, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Unit 1, Section C (fronts part): the offensive fronts (Caucasus, the Canal), the defensive fronts (Çanakkale/Gallipoli, Iraq, Syria-Palestine, Hijaz-Yemen) and the aid fronts (Galicia, Romania, Macedonia).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: the empire fights on more fronts than any ally, and its one immortal victory is won by the colonel who will found the republic. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort the Ottoman fronts into the book's three kinds: offensive, defensive and aid.
2. Name the defensive fronts: Gallipoli (Çanakkale), Iraq, Syria-Palestine and Hijaz-Yemen.
3. Name the aid fronts: Galicia, Romania and Macedonia.
4. Say why Gallipoli stands above the rest in the book: the empire's immortal victory, won by the colonel who will found the republic.
