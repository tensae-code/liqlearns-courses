# Gallipoli and the Ottoman fronts, 1914–1918

## Do it first

One empire fights the First World War on more fronts than any of its allies — attacking in the Caucasus and toward the Canal, defending at Gallipoli, in Iraq, in Syria-Palestine and the Hijaz, and sending soldiers as far as Galicia and Macedonia. Before you read on, guess which front becomes immortal, and whose name it makes. Hold your guess; Çanakkale is waiting.

## Sub lesson 1 — The offensive, defensive and aid fronts

Turkey's Grade 12 book sorts the Ottoman war into three kinds of front. The offensive fronts reach outward: the Caucasus and the advance toward the Canal. The defensive fronts guard the empire's own body: Çanakkale — Gallipoli — at the straits, Iraq, Syria-Palestine and Hijaz-Yemen. And the aid fronts send Ottoman soldiers to fight beside allies far from home: Galicia, Romania and Macedonia.

Above them all stands Gallipoli, the empire's one immortal victory of the war — won under the colonel who will found the republic, Mustafa Kemal. The book's beat joins the map to the man: the empire fights on more fronts than any ally, and its one immortal victory is won by the colonel who will found the republic.

In your mind's eye: the Gallipoli peninsula — trenches on the ridges above the straits, warships held off in the water below. The student points to the straits and says why holding them mattered to the whole empire.
