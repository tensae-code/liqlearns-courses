# Jordan's social development — tribes, refugees and cities — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lessons 1–2: social life 1921–1999
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Emirate's society is tribal to its roots — landowners, merchants, officials, clergy and craftsmen in the qasabas, Circassians and Chechens among them since the 1800s; a mobile medical unit (one doctor, two nurses) tours the Badia from 1937 while mukhtars report malaria, typhoid, smallpox and measles, until the first Health Ministry in 1950; women's public life begins in charity — the first women's association, the Women's Social Solidarity Association, in 1926; after 1950 the society counts four peoples — city, countryside, badia and the Palestinian refugee camps — Jordan grants the refugees citizenship, UNRWA serves the camps, the nuclear family spreads with the move to the cities, and the Royal Medical Services crown the age with King Hussein Medical City (1973) and the Youth Welfare Organisation (1966)

## Sub lesson 2 — Lesson 3: social life since 1999
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the book counts 9.5 million Jordanians in 1999 and some 11.5 million in 2023, most of them townspeople; the National Social Protection Strategy (2019–2025) organises welfare; youth are gathered by the Supreme Council for Youth (2001), the King's Fitness Award (2005) and the Kulna al-Urdun commission (2006); disability centres multiply — and women's public share, begun in the charities of 1926, reaches parliament through the 2003 quota
