# Vietnam Hoi An Trading Port — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** 17th–19th-century merchant houses and Chinese assembly halls line the port; Japanese traders roof a bridge like a sword across the back of a water monster to keep the town at peace.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Bài 20, the old town of Hội An and the legend of Chùa Cầu

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Lịch sử và Địa lí 4 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Bài 20, the old town of Hội An and the legend of Chùa Cầu

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** market networks spread across deltas and coasts; in the North, Thăng Long — Kẻ Chợ with its 36 guild streets — and Phố Hiến, with its twenty wards and its many resident foreigners, become great centres; in the South, Thanh Hà, Gia Định and above all Hội An, "the largest port town of Đàng Trong", where Japanese, Chinese, Portuguese, Dutch, English and French merchants sell weapons, fine crafts, zinc and silver and buy silk, forest treasures, bird's nests and farm produce past the Japanese-built Covered Bridge — until, in the later 18th century, both governments restrict foreign trade and the towns fade.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Bài 9, commerce and the great trading towns
