**Answer: 17th–19th-century merchant houses and Chinese assembly halls line the port; Japanese traders roof a bridge like a sword across the back of a water monster to keep the town at peace (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 9, commerce and the great trading towns.
**Answer: market networks spread across deltas and coasts; in the North, Thăng Long — Kẻ Chợ with its 36 guild streets — and Phố Hiến, with its twenty wards and its many resident foreigners, become great centres; in the South, Thanh Hà, Gia Định and above all Hội An, "the largest port town of Đàng Trong", where Japanese, Chinese, Portuguese, Dutch, English and French merchants sell weapons, fine crafts, zinc and silver and buy silk, forest treasures, bird's nests and farm produce past the Japanese-built Covered Bridge — until, in the later 18th century, both governments restrict foreign trade and the towns fade (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Vietnam's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Lịch sử và Địa lí 8 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* (Vietnam), Bài 9, commerce and the great trading towns, printed p. 43.**
