# Nationalism — the feeling that made nation states

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Three movements prepare the feeling

Nationalism did not appear from nowhere. Three earlier movements prepared it, each in its own way. The **Renaissance** raised national languages and literatures — and with them, national self-consciousness: a people who read and write in their own tongue begin to feel they are one people. The **Protestant Reformation** broke Europe's religious unity and the supremacy of the pope — after it, peoples no longer looked to one universal church above their own lands. And the **expansion of commerce and colonies** piled up wealth — and wealth built strong states that could carry a nation's ambitions.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vienna 1815 — reaction against the feeling

Then Napoleon fell, and the victors tried to turn the clock back. At **Vienna in 1815**, Britain, Russia, Prussia and Austria drew a settlement meant to **reverse the Napoleonic changes** — the age of 'reaction'. But the feeling they faced would not be reversed. **Nationalism** — the bond of shared **language, culture, history and religion** — pressed peoples who shared those bonds to demand states of their own: **nation states**. The next two lessons watch that pressure succeed, in Italy and in Germany.
