# Lesson plan — Nationalism — the feeling that made nation states

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Nationalism — the feeling that made nation states
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Development of Capitalism and Nationalism 1815–1914 (Grade 10 History).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Nationalism — the feeling of belonging to one people, sharing **language, culture, history and religion** — was prepared by the Renaissance, the Reformation and expanding commerce, and after 1815 it pressed against the Vienna settlement until nation states were made.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands beside the capitalism lesson in Unit 1 — the learner has seen the economic system change; now the political feeling that redrew the map. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Three movements prepare the feeling** — Name the three preparing movements — Renaissance, Protestant Reformation, expansion of commerce and colonies — and say what each contributed.
2. **Vienna 1815 — reaction against the feeling** — Explain the Vienna settlement of 1815 as 'reaction' meant to reverse the Napoleonic changes, and nationalism — shared language, culture, history and religion — pressing for nation states against it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three movements that prepared nationalism and what each contributed.
2. Say what nationalism is: the bond of shared language, culture, history and religion.
3. Explain what the Vienna settlement of 1815 tried to do, and why it could not hold.
