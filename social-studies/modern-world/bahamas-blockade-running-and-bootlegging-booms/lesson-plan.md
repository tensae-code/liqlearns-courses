# Lesson plan — Bahamas — blockade-running and bootlegging booms

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas — blockade-running and bootlegging booms
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Bahamas, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Twice, American wars and bans make Nassau rich: blockade-running to the Confederacy in 1861–1865, and bootlegging liquor into Prohibition America from 1920 to 1933 — booms that build infrastructure while ordinary communities benefit far less
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Blockade 1861–1865: The Bahamas runs the blockade** — Nassau becomes the Confederacy's back door to Europe; businesses flourish and infrastructure improves on blockade money — and crime grows with the population (Blockade 1861–1865: The Bahamas runs the blockade)
2. **Bootlegging 1920: rum-running through Prohibition** — America's 1919 liquor ban makes Bahamian bootleggers rich from 1920 to 1933 smuggling drink into the United States; the money improves rich homes and tourist infrastructure, but the Over-the-Hill communities benefit far less (Bootlegging 1920: rum-running through Prohibition)
3. **Early Connections: bootlegging benefits the colony** — when American prohibition laws choke the liquor flow, rum-running becomes an economic benefit to the growing colony (Early Connections: bootlegging benefits the colony)
