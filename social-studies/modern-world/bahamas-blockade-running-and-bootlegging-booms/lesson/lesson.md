# Bahamas — blockade-running and bootlegging booms

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Twice, American wars and bans make Nassau rich: blockade-running to the Confederacy in 1861–1865, and bootlegging liquor into Prohibition America from 1920 to 1933 — booms that build infrastructure while ordinary communities benefit far less. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Blockade 1861–1865: The Bahamas runs the blockade

Nassau becomes the Confederacy's back door to Europe; businesses flourish and infrastructure improves on blockade money — and crime grows with the population (Blockade 1861–1865: The Bahamas runs the blockade)
