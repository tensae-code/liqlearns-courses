# Video script — Bahamas — blockade-running and bootlegging booms

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bahamas books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Nassau becomes the Confederacy's back door to Europe; businesses flourish and infrastructure improves on blockade money — and crime grows with the population; America's 1919 liquor ban makes Bahamian bootleggers rich from 1920 to 1933 smuggling drink into the United States; the money improves rich homes and tourist infrastructure, but the Over-the-Hill communities benefit far less; when American prohibition laws choke the liquor flow, rum-running becomes an economic benefit to the growing colony

Hold that story: Twice, American wars and bans make Nassau rich: blockade-running to the Confederacy in 1861–1865, and bootlegging liquor into Prohibition America from 1920 to 1933 — booms that build infrastructure while ordinary communities benefit far less
