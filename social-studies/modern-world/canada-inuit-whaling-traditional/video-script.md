# Video script — Inuit whaling — the traditional hunt

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the old Arctic hunt, told as the Nunavut reader tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Far in the north, where the sea freezes and thaws, Inuit hunters have taken whales since long ago. Why? Because one whale is enormous — one whale can feed and supply a whole community.

Nothing of the whale is wasted. Its bones become tools and sleds. Its oil burns in the lamps and gives light. Its skin and blubber — maktaq — are food.

But a whale is not caught by one person. The Elders carry the knowledge: where the bowhead travels, how the ice and the water move. A crew rows out in an open boat, together, with hand harpoons. When the bowhead is brought in, the whole camp shares the catch.

One whale. Everyone's knowledge, everyone's hands — and everyone's share.
