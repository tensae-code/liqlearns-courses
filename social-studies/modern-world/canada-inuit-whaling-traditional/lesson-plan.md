# Lesson plan — Inuit whaling — the traditional hunt

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Inuit whaling — the traditional hunt
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Canada, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Inuit hunters have taken whales since long ago because one whale fed and supplied a whole community — bones for tools and sleds, oil for lamps, skin and blubber (maktaq) for food — and the old way of hunting rested on Elders' knowledge and crew teamwork in open boats with hand harpoons, with the whole camp sharing the catch.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the learner's own sense of a community sharing one big gift: one whale, and everything it gave. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why hunt whales? What the whale gave** — one whale fed and supplied a whole community: bones for tools and sleds, oil for lamps, skin and blubber (maktaq) for food.
2. **Whaling the old way** — Elders' knowledge and crew teamwork in open boats with hand harpoons bring the bowhead in, and the whole camp shares the catch.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why Inuit hunters have taken whales since long ago.
2. Name what the whale gave: bones for tools and sleds, oil for lamps, skin and blubber (maktaq) for food.
3. Tell how the old-way hunt worked: Elders' knowledge, a crew in an open boat, hand harpoons.
4. Say who shared the catch — the whole camp.
