# Lesson plan — Vietnam Hong Duc And Gia Long Law Codes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Hong Duc And Gia Long Law Codes
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Vietnam's first written code is issued under the Lý in 1042; the Quốc triều hình luật — the Hồng Đức code — is begun under Lê Thái Tổ and completed under Lê Thánh Tông: 13 chapters and 722 articles in 6 volumes covering criminal, civil, marriage-family, land and property law; it guards the king's, royal family's and officials' privileges with the harshest penalties, yet carries progressive content the book stresses — Phan Huy Lê is cited for its protection of women's rights, found in no contemporary Eastern code: daughters inherit on a par with sons (Article 388) and a widow's share of the couple's property is fixed (Articles 374–375); the Hoàng Việt luật lệ — the Gia Long code of 1815 — has 398 articles in 22 volumes arranged in six categories matching the Six Ministries, concentrates on protecting the monarchy and ruling class (Article 224 makes even listening to a plot against the throne a capital offence for the whole family), yet also protects the elderly, children and women — its Book 19, Article 10 forbids torturing offenders over 70, under 15 or disabled; both codes share an advanced legislative technique, every norm built in three parts: the case supposed, the conduct required, and the sanction

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chuyên đề 3, I.2, the two great law codes before 1858** — Vietnam's first written code is issued under the Lý in 1042; the Quốc triều hình luật — the Hồng Đức code — is begun under Lê Thái Tổ and completed under Lê Thánh Tông: 13 chapters and 722 articles in 6 volumes covering criminal, civil, marriage-family, land and property law; it guards the king's, royal family's and officials' privileges with the harshest penalties, yet carries progressive content the book stresses — Phan Huy Lê is cited for its protection of women's rights, found in no contemporary Eastern code: daughters inherit on a par with sons (Article 388) and a widow's share of the couple's property is fixed (Articles 374–375); the Hoàng Việt luật lệ — the Gia Long code of 1815 — has 398 articles in 22 volumes arranged in six categories matching the Six Ministries, concentrates on protecting the monarchy and ruling class (Article 224 makes even listening to a plot against the throne a capital offence for the whole family), yet also protects the elderly, children and women — its Book 19, Article 10 forbids torturing offenders over 70, under 15 or disabled; both codes share an advanced legislative technique, every norm built in three parts: the case supposed, the conduct required, and the sanction
2. **Bài 10.3a, Đại Việt's political institutions, reforms and law** — the Đinh–Tiền Lê dynasties build the centralised monarchy, the institution is perfected through the Lý–Trần and reaches its peak under the early Lê, whose state organisation the lesson details — the emperor at the head deciding all work; the central organs organised in three parts, civil administration, military and supervision, the court comprising the Six Ministries and Six Courts with the Six Offices and the Censorate supervising them; and local government in four levels, from the đạo/thừa tuyên with its three offices (Thừa ty for administration, Đô ty for the military, Hiến ty for justice) down through the phủ and the huyện/châu to the xã headed by the xã trưởng — though each dynasty keeps its own character, the Lý–Trần institutions still close to the people (thân dân), those from the early Lê onward bureaucratic and autocratic; the monarchies also carry out reforms, typically Hồ Quý Ly's and the Hồ dynasty's (late 14th–early 15th century), Lê Thánh Tông's (later 15th century) and Minh Mạng's (early 19th century); and the dynasties attend to building law, issuing the codes Hình thư (Lý), Hình luật (Trần), Quốc triều hình luật (early Lê) and Hoàng Việt luật lệ (Nguyễn) — the box repeats the Hồng Đức code's distinction: its 722 articles are the most complete code applied from the early Lê to the Lê restoration, containing progressive provisions rarely seen in the codes of contemporary Eastern countries, especially protecting women's rights — daughters receive a share of property like sons (Article 388), a daughter inherits if there is no son (Article 391), and property is divided between wife and husband (Articles 374 and 375)
