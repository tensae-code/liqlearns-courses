# Lesson plan — Nicaragua Sandinista Revolution And Its Heroes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Nicaragua Sandinista Revolution And Its Heroes
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Nicaragua, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** pupils explain the Revolution's importance and name its heroes and martyrs
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Efemérides: anniversary of the Revolución Popular Sandinista (19 July)** — pupils explain the Revolution's importance and name its heroes and martyrs
2. **Efemérides: birth of Comandante Carlos Fonseca Amador** — Fonseca, a teacher born 23 June, becomes founder and commander-in-chief of the Sandinista Revolution
3. **Héroes de la Patria: Carlos Fonseca Amador** — Fonseca founds the Frente Sandinista and is named Father of the Sandinista Popular Revolution, a life shown as proof ordinary people can change the country
4. **Héroes de la Patria: Julio Buitrago Urroz** — Buitrago, a hardworking boy who loved work from childhood, becomes Father of the Urban Resistance, with a house-museum keeping his story
5. **Héroes de la Patria: Arlen Siu** — four years before the Revolution triumphs, young Arlen Siu gives her life; a song, "El cenzontle pregunta por Arlen Siu", asks after the girl who never came back
6. **Héroes de la Patria: Bertha Calderón** — Calderón, a reader inspired by Fidel's books, is killed a month before the Revolution's triumph and is remembered as a heroine of that final push
7. **Efemérides: anniversary of Comandante Carlos Fonseca Amador's passing** — the year closes by returning to Fonseca's mausoleum and death anniversary, the founder remembered at the moment he fell
8. **Aniversario de la Revolución Popular Sandinista** — the Revolution's anniversary is celebrated as the department's living historical feast, linking 1856's defence of sovereignty to 1979
9. **La acción heroica de Rigoberto López Pérez** — poet Rigoberto López Pérez decides to end the Somoza dictatorship at its root and shoots the dictator, an act the book calls heroic sacrifice
10. **Legado histórico de Carlos Fonseca Amador** — Fonseca's legacy is founding the FSLN and giving the later Revolution its ideas and organisation
11. **Personajes históricos: Arlen Siu** — Arlen Siu, "la chinita de Jinotepe", joins the guerrilla and dies young; her story closes the department's hero gallery
12. **Surgimiento y desarrollo del FSLN; participación de las mujeres** — Fonseca and others found the FSLN; its combats grow over two decades and women fight throughout, not at the margins
13. **Insurrección popular de 1978-1979 y ofensiva final** — whole towns rise in 1978-79; the people's participation in the final offensive brings the dynasty down on 19 July 1979
14. **La Revolución Popular Sandinista: gobierno, logros y dificultades; la mujer en la Revolución** — the Junta and Council of State replace Somoza's institutions, the Guardia becomes the Ejército Popular Sandinista, gains come in health, education and land — alongside real difficulties and women's central role
15. **Triunfo de la Revolución Popular Sandinista** — on 19 July 1979 the Revolution overthrows the Somoza dictatorship after an insurrection that costs some 50,000 lives, most of them young
16. **El gobierno sandinista: características** — a mixed economy, single health system, free compulsory education and labour protections define the revolutionary government's programme
17. **Logros de la Revolución: reforma agraria y proyectos** — agrarian reform delivers 4 million manzanas to 120,000 families and organises 3,151 cooperatives, while African palm, textile and cardboard projects rebuild production
18. **La acción de Rigoberto López Pérez, 1956** — López Pérez shoots Somoza García at León's Casa del Obrero on 21 September 1956, proving the dictatorship is not invincible; guerrilla movements, including old Sandinista Ramón Raudales, rise after him
19. **Fundación del Frente Sandinista de Liberación Nacional** — the FSLN is built to give the popular struggle the political-military organisation it lacked; early guerrillas fall at Raití and Bocay in 1963, the 22 January 1967 massacre breaks conservative hegemony, and Pancasán relaunches the armed struggle
20. **La ofensiva sandinista de 1974-1978** — after the 1972 Managua earthquake exposes the regime's theft of aid, the Juan José Quezada commando seizes the Chema Castillo house on 27 December 1974 and the dictatorship answers with savage rural repression
21. **Carlos Fonseca, Padre de la Revolución Popular Sandinista** — Fonseca's life is told whole — Moscow and Kiev congresses, arrests and deportations, founding the Movimiento Nueva Nicaragua in 1961 and publishing Sandino's Ideario — the founder as the Revolution's father
22. **Derrocamiento del régimen somocista: la insurrección de 1978** — Monimbó and Subtiava rise, and on 22 August 1978 a Sandinista commando seizes the National Palace with the Congress hostage, forcing Somoza to free political prisoners on live television
23. **Triunfo de la Revolución Popular Sandinista** — the tactical retreat to Masaya, the Junta's first proclamation on 18 June and the final siege close in; Somoza Debayle flees on 17 July 1979 and revolutionary forces enter Managua
24. **Nicaragua durante la Revolución: forma de gobierno** — the five-member Junta de Gobierno de Reconstrucción Nacional replaces the Somocista state and proclaims a Fundamental Statute of rights on the road to a new constitution
25. **Transformaciones socio-económicas de la Revolución** — Somoza-family property is expropriated, banking and foreign trade nationalised, and an agrarian reform creates People's Property Areas and cooperatives inside a mixed economy
26. **La lucha en Nicaragua: del somocismo al triunfo de 1979** — the FSLN learns from its Bocay-Raití defeat, builds among peasants and city masses, unites its three tendencies and launches the final offensive; Washington itself asks Somoza to resign, he flees on 17 July and the FSLN enters Managua on 19 July 1979
