# Lesson plan — Vietnam Fatherland Protection Wars Overview

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Fatherland Protection Wars Overview
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one river wins three wars across 350 years, and a diplomat wins a fourth with a sentence
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 7 Vietnam's wars of defence in one view: geography makes the country a bridge everyone must cross, so its history is a chain of defensive wars; the table of victories runs Bạch Đằng 938, 981 and 1288, Như Nguyệt 1075–1077, Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút 1785, Ngọc Hồi–Đống Đa 1789 — and in 1637 envoy Giang Văn Minh answers the Ming king's mocking couplet with "the Bạch Đằng's blood is still red"** — one river wins three wars across 350 years, and a diplomat wins a fourth with a sentence
2. **Bài 9 Lessons of the Fatherland-protection wars, 1945 to today: keep patriotism alight in every generation (Hồ Chí Minh's image of patriotism as a treasure sometimes displayed, sometimes hidden in a chest, which leaders must bring out into action); consolidate the great national unity bloc; combine national strength with the strength of the age while fusing the two strategic tasks, building and defending; and keep developing people's-war art into an all-people national defence that protects the country "early, from afar"** — eighty years of wars are distilled into four transferable lessons for a peacetime generation
