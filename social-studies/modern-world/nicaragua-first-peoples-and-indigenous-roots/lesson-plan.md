# Lesson plan — Nicaragua First Peoples And Indigenous Roots

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Nicaragua First Peoples And Indigenous Roots
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Nicaragua, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** many town names are Nahuatl left by the first settlers; Managua, "surrounded by water", begins as fishing settlements on Lake Xolotlán
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Origen del nombre y de los primeros pobladores de mi municipio** — many town names are Nahuatl left by the first settlers; Managua, "surrounded by water", begins as fishing settlements on Lake Xolotlán
2. **Influencia del medio geográfico en la forma de vida de los primeros pobladores** — lakes, rivers and land shape where the first peoples settle and how they live, a pattern each child traces in their own municipality
3. **Manifestaciones culturales de los primeros pobladores del municipio** — the first settlers leave cultural expressions that still mark the municipality's identity today
4. **Primeros pobladores de mi departamento** — department names themselves — Matagalpa, Estelí, Masaya, Chontales, Chinandega — and river, hill, food and plant names prove an indigenous past under modern Nicaragua
5. **Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento del departamento** — geography decides where department settlements grow, the same land-first logic scaled up from municipality to department
6. **Actividades económicas de los primeros pobladores de mi departamento** — the first peoples farm, fish and trade to live; their work is the department's first economy
7. **Organización política y social de los primeros pobladores** — cacique-led indigenous societies organise power and community life before any Spanish town exists
8. **Origen y forma de vida de los primeros pobladores de Nicaragua** — the first peoples arrive and make lives across the territory that becomes Nicaragua, the root story every later chapter grows from
9. **Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento; otras tribus que se establecieron** — tribe after tribe settles where water and land allow, so Nicaragua's map is layered with different peoples before Europeans come
10. **Actividades económicas de los primeros pobladores** — farming, fishing and exchange feed the first communities; work organises their world
11. **Organización política y social de los primeros pobladores** — caciques and social ranks order indigenous life; the book separates how they governed from how families and classes lived
12. **Manifestaciones culturales: religión, costumbres, tradiciones, creencias y ritos** — religion and ritual are taught as the first peoples' way of explaining and holding their world together
13. **Sociedad precolombina de Nicaragua** — before any European arrives, ranked indigenous societies with caciques, councils and markets already order life in the territory
14. **Influencia del medio geográfico en el poblamiento de Nicaragua** — two fertile coastal plains, two great lakes and river-crossed mountains decide where Nicaragua's first peoples settle
15. **Organización política, tenencia de la tierra y economía precolombina** — Nicarao caciques rule with the Monéxico council of elders; land is held collectively by families, cacao beans serve as money, and the council-run market anchors each village
16. **Desarrollo y legado cultural de los grupos aborígenes** — migrant peoples from Mexico build temple-plaza towns with stepped sacrifice mounds, and their legacy still shapes Nicaraguan identity
17. **Pobladores del Centro y Caribe: origen Macro-Chibcha y afrodescendientes** — Matagalpa, Mayangna, Miskitu and Rama peoples of Chibcha origin share the centre and Caribbean with Afro-descendant Zambos, Creoles and Garífuna; war chiefs are elected for skill and dismissed for misconduct
18. **Primeros pobladores de origen mesoamericano** — war in central Mexico in the 7th-8th centuries pushes Chorotegas, Maribios and Nicaraos south; by about 800 CE they hold the Pacific strip from León to Rivas
19. **Persistencia de los grupos aborígenes en el territorio actual** — today's towns are mapped to their ancestry — Chorotega Jinotega, Mozonte and Totogalpa, Nahoa Ostional and Salinas — the first peoples never vanished
20. **El medio geográfico en Nicaragua** — Nicaragua's lakes, plains and coasts decide where the first peoples can settle and how their cultures take shape
21. **Sociedades precoloniales del Pacífico: la migración mesoamericana** — the fall of Teotihuacán and Tula pushes Chorotegas, Maribios and Nicaraos south from Mexico between the 8th and 15th centuries; the Chorotegas reach Nicaragua about 800 CE and displace earlier peoples toward the centre
22. **Organización política y social indígena** — the Monéxico council of elders is elected by vote and in turn elects — and can remove — each town's cacique, so Pacific power is shared, not absolute
23. **Organización económica: tierra, agricultura y subsistencia** — land is held by the community, never individually; farming feeds the village and cacao beans serve as money
24. **La artesanía indígena** — deer-bone combs and hand-worked objects from farm, plant and hunt remains show a craft economy inside every village
25. **El comercio y las relaciones de trabajo: los tiangues** — women traders fill the plaza markets where goods move by cacao money and barter, and only same-language traders are admitted
26. **La cultura indígena: religión y sacrificio** — religion binds the whole culture together; offerings, including human sacrifice, are made at sacred places such as the Masaya volcano, the indigenous Popocatepetl
27. **Grupos indígenas del Centro y Atlántico** — the centre belongs to the Chontal-Matagalpa family of uncertain origin, the Caribbean rivers to Misumalpan and Chibcha-descended peoples such as the Rama, known mostly through language because documents are few
