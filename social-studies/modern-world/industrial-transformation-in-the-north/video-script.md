# Video script — Industrial transformation in the North, 1800–1850

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told straight, in the order it happened. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Mills rose, banks multiplied, canals and rails stitched a continent — and work itself changed: wages, clocks and classes.

Mills rose in the Northeast — factories gathering workers under one roof to make cloth and goods by machine and water power. Making things stopped being only a household craft.

A vibrant capitalist republic of banks and markets followed the mills: credit, buying and selling, and fortunes made in trade and manufacture. Money organised the new North.

Canals and railroads stitched the new economy together in a transportation revolution, moving goods and people faster than ever. And wage labour sorted the North into new classes — owners, managers and the wage workers whose time was now sold by the hour.
