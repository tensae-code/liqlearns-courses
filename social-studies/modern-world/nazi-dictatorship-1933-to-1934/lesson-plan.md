## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **7.1 The importance of the Reichstag fire** — The Reichstag burns on 27 February 1933; the Nazis blame communist van der Lubbe, win an emergency decree suspending civil rights and arrest thousands before the March election
2. **7.2 The importance of the Enabling Act** — With communist deputies arrested and the SA lining the chamber, the Reichstag votes itself out of power in March 1933; Hitler can now legislate without parliament or president
3. **7.3 The removal of opposition** — Gleichschaltung brings states, unions and parties into line through 1933 — unions smashed on 2 May, parties banned by July — until the Nazi Party is the only organisation left standing
4. **7.4 The importance of the Night of the Long Knives** — On 30 June 1934 Hitler purges Röhm and the SA leadership who wanted a 'second revolution', murdering old comrades to buy the army's loyalty
5. **7.5 The support of the army** — When Hindenburg dies in August 1934 the army swears its oath to Hitler personally, and he merges Chancellor and President as Führer — the dictatorship is complete
