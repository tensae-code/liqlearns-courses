# Nazi dictatorship, 1933 to 1934

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: In 1933–34 Hitler destroys opposition step by step until the dictatorship is complete. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — 7.1 The importance of the Reichstag fire

The Reichstag burns on 27 February 1933; the Nazis blame communist van der Lubbe, win an emergency decree suspending civil rights and arrest thousands before the March election.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — 7.2 The importance of the Enabling Act

With communist deputies arrested and the SA lining the chamber, the Reichstag votes itself out of power in March 1933; Hitler can now legislate without parliament or president.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — 7.3 The removal of opposition

Gleichschaltung brings states, unions and parties into line through 1933 — unions smashed on 2 May, parties banned by July — until the Nazi Party is the only organisation left standing.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — 7.4 The importance of the Night of the Long Knives

On 30 June 1934 Hitler purges Röhm and the SA leadership who wanted a 'second revolution', murdering old comrades to buy the army's loyalty.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 5 — 7.5 The support of the army

When Hindenburg dies in August 1934 the army swears its oath to Hitler personally, and he merges Chancellor and President as Führer — the dictatorship is complete.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: 7.1 The importance of the Reichstag fire; 7.2 The importance of the Enabling Act; 7.3 The removal of opposition; 7.4 The importance of the Night of the Long Knives; 7.5 The support of the army. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
