# Lesson plan — Domination and resistance in the modern West

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Domination and resistance in the modern West
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Brazil, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colonised peoples, enslaved Africans and the new working classes resist the modern West's domination — the story of domination told from underneath.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Domination — the modern West rises** — The modern West rose by dominating: lands colonised, peoples ruled, Africans enslaved and carried across the ocean, and at home a new working class held under factory discipline. Domination is the first half of the book's story.
2. **Resistance — the answer from underneath** — The second half is resistance. Colonised peoples fought their colonisers. Enslaved Africans resisted slavery. The new working classes organised against the domination of the factory and the wage. Wherever domination stood, the book finds people pushing back.
3. **The story told from underneath** — The book's way is the lesson's point: this chapter tells the earlier chapters' story from underneath — from the side of the colonised, the enslaved and the workers, for whom domination was never the whole story because resistance was always in it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Brazil — from slavery to free labour; The Industrial Revolution — what changed and why England first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name three groups the book shows under the modern West's domination.
2. Say what each of those groups did in answer — the forms resistance took.
3. Say what 'told from underneath' means for how this chapter teaches the story.
