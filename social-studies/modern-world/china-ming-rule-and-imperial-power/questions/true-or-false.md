# China Ming Rule And Imperial Power — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Zhu Yuanzhang, blaming Yuan's fall on weak centre, abolishes the chancellorship forever, splits provinces into three offices that cannot command each other, and makes the six ministries answer to the emperor alone.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Ming rule

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *中国历史 七年级 下册 (Chinese History, Grade 7, second volume)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Ming rule

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Zhu Yuanzhang abolishes the chancellorship, creates the palace guard and secret-police factories and moves the capital to Beijing under Zhu Di, while Zheng He's voyages open the sea road to Asia and Africa.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in The great unified state rises

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *历史与社会 八年级 下册 (History and Society, Grade 8, second volume)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about The great unified state rises

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Zhu Yuanzhang's terror and centralisation, Zhu Di's capital and fleets, then a Ming too rigid to reform, falling to Li Zicheng and the Manchu banners in 1644.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in From Ming's founding to the Qing entry
