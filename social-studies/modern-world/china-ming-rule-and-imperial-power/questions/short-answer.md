# China Ming Rule And Imperial Power — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Ming rule.
**Answer: Zhu Yuanzhang, blaming Yuan's fall on weak centre, abolishes the chancellorship forever, splits provinces into three offices that cannot command each other, and makes the six ministries answer to the emperor alone (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In China's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *中国历史 七年级 下册 (Chinese History, Grade 7, second volume)* (China), Lesson 14: Ming rule, printed p. 66.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: The great unified state rises.
**Answer: Zhu Yuanzhang abolishes the chancellorship, creates the palace guard and secret-police factories and moves the capital to Beijing under Zhu Di, while Zheng He's voyages open the sea road to Asia and Africa (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In China's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *历史与社会 八年级 下册 (History and Society, Grade 8, second volume)* (China), Unit 5 Lesson 1: The great unified state rises, printed p. 2.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: From Ming's founding to the Qing entry.
**Answer: Zhu Yuanzhang's terror and centralisation, Zhu Di's capital and fleets, then a Ming too rigid to reform, falling to Li Zicheng and the Manchu banners in 1644 (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** In China's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *普通高中教科书 历史 必修 中外历史纲要（上） (Senior High History, Compulsory: Outline of Chinese and World History, upper volume — Chinese history)* (China), Lesson 13: From Ming's founding to the Qing entry, printed p. 77.**
