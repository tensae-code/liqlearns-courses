# The Turkish Republic is recognised — Mudanya and Lausanne

## Do it first

Wars are ended twice: once by soldiers, once by signatures. The guns fall silent at an armistice in a small Marmara town — but the peace, and the world's recognition of a state the powers had tried to partition, must be won again at a table in Switzerland. Before you read on, decide which victory is harder. Hold your answer; Lausanne is the book's verdict.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Mudanya Armistice and the Lausanne Peace Treaty

Turkey's Grade 12 book closes the National Struggle with two signatures. The Mudanya Armistice, 11 October 1922, silences the guns: the fighting ends, and the road to a peace conference opens. Then, at Lausanne, the new state's delegation faces the powers that had signed Sèvres — and this time the terms are Turkey's.

The Treaty of Lausanne, 24 July 1923, wins the peace and the world's recognition: the partition is buried, and the state born in Anatolia takes its seat among nations as an equal. The book's beat is the pairing it wants remembered: what the sword won at Sakarya, the pen keeps at Lausanne.

In your mind's eye: the Lausanne conference table in 1923 — the Turkish delegation facing the powers across the long table, the treaty document between them. The student points to the document and says what its signatures recognise.
