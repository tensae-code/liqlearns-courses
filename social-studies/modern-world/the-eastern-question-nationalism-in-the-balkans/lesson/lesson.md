# The Eastern Question — nationalism in the Balkans, empires in collision

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Three forces make one question

End the unit in Europe's south-east, where three forces met and made a problem diplomats named the **Eastern Question**. First: **rising nationalist feeling among the Balkan peoples**, who wanted states of their own — the same feeling that made Italy and Germany. Second: the **declining Ottoman (Turkish) Empire**, the old power that ruled them, growing too weak to hold its lands. Third: the **divergent interests of the Great Powers** in the Near East — Russia, Austria, Britain and France each watching the Balkans and each wanting the decline to profit themselves, not a rival.

## Sub lesson 2 — Crises without an answer

Put the three forces together and the result was not one crisis but a **succession of Balkan crises**. Economic and social change stirred the peoples; international rivalry armed the watching powers; and the problems themselves stayed **unsolved**, because every solution that pleased one power alarmed another. The consequences reached far beyond the Balkans — they were **serious for all Europe**. Remember this corner of the map: Unit 4's story of 1914 begins on these same streets.
