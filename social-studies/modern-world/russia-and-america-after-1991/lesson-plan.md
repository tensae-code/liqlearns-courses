# Lesson plan — Russia And America After 1991

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Russia And America After 1991
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Russia passes from 1990s instability over its political system to the presidential republic of the December 1993 Constitution, balances Europe and Asia in foreign policy, and climbs from crisis (1991–99: GDP growth −4.8%, inflation 22%, budget deficit) to recovery (2000–21: growth around 3.5% a year, budget surplus, a top-ten world economy with 2021 GDP near $1.5 trillion), joining the WTO in 2012 with defence, energy, mining and aerospace still its spearheads; America keeps its two-party democracy and its aim of leading the world — Clinton's 1990s "engagement and enlargement" strategy of strong military, stronger alliances and the "democracy" slogan — and remains the largest economy (over 30% of world GDP in 2000, 24% in 2021, per-capita GDP above $70,000 in 2021, leading the WTO, World Bank and IMF) despite short recessions in 1997–98, 2008–09 and 2014–15
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.
