# Vietnam Dang Ngoai Peasant Uprisings — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 7, why Đàng Ngoài exploded: the crisis of the Lê–Trịnh regime
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the chronicles quoted here do the accusing — in 1730 tens of thousands of Hải Dương people are conscripted to dig rivers and haul bricks for Lord Trịnh Giang's pagodas; the lord's palace keeps four or five hundred arrogant eunuchs "hated and feared by the whole country"; a tax is laid on every trade, so that for the silk tax people cut down their mulberry trees and for the fish tax hide their nets; famine follows, and "a hundred đồng cannot buy one full meal"

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 7, the great risings of the 18th-century North
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Hoàng Công Chất (1739–1769) gathers the poor, builds his base at Mường Thanh in the far north-west, protects the border and settles people's lives so the Tây Bắc peoples support him to the end, and his son fights on after him; Nguyễn Danh Phương (1740–1751) holds the Tam Đảo mountains until he is captured; Nguyễn Hữu Cầu (1741–1751), the "Quận He" who swims like the great he fish that never harms a soul at sea, strikes from Đồ Sơn, threatens Thăng Long itself and ranges into Thanh Hoá and Nghệ An — all fail, but they shake the Lê–Trịnh regime to its foundations and clear the ground for Tây Sơn
