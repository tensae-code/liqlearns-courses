# Lesson plan — Finland's wartime rationing and shortage

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Finland's wartime rationing and shortage
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Finland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Foreign trade stops, men and fields are lost to the war, so a new ministry rations sugar and coffee first, then nearly all food and clothes by work-heavy card classes
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **introduction: the Ministry of Public Provision and the ration-card system** — foreign trade stops, men and fields are lost to the war, so a new ministry rations sugar and coffee first, then nearly all food and clothes by work-heavy card classes
2. **Task 1, Rantasalmi letter 1942** — a pregnant evacuee mother of three, husband at the front, begs bigger B/G2/M2 cards and is told where to appeal
3. **Task 2, Ivalo letter 1942** — a Lapland writer exposes under-the-counter marmalade sales for favoured customers and asks if Lapland lives under a different law
4. **Task 3, Tohmajärvi letter 1943** — a poor potato year breaks the delivery quota, and eight people share one pair of new shoes all war — the family asks how to get footwear permits
5. **Task 4, Pietarsaari letter 1943** — workers miss the egg distribution twice because the eggs run out before their shift ends; equality of coupons becomes the argument
6. **Task 5, Helsinki letter 1942** — a self-styled 'home and finance minister' mother of five jokes through vinegar-and-saccharin dinners and black-market tobacco prices — humour as survival
7. **Task 6, Kauhava letter 1942** — the state requisitions 320 kg of hay at 1.3 mk/kg while free hay costs 3.5; a smallholder wife faces destroying her herd
