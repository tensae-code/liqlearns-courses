# Bahamas — English settlers and enslaved Africans — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bahamian History: Eleuthera settlers, enslaved Africans and the freed Africans who chose The Bahamas
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: English settlers reach Eleuthera in 1648 and bring enslaved Africans to work plantations; much later Africans freed on the way to Cuba choose to settle in The Bahamas instead, carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer Bookie and Rabbie stories with them

## Sub lesson 2 — Lucayans and Eleutheran Adventurers: the Adventurers settle Eleuthera and New Providence
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the Eleutheran Adventurers name Eleuthera, struggle to farm, trade wood and salvage wrecks; Captain Sayle moves on to the island that becomes New Providence, and settlements grow on both islands

## Sub lesson 3 — Slavery: how enslaved and free Africans reached The Bahamas
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Bahamian slaves arrive by different routes — direct from Africa, with the Loyalists from America, freed in America and settling, or freed by shipwreck — and bring new peoples and traditions with them

## Sub lesson 4 — Early Settlers: Adventurers, Loyalists and the enslaved
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the Eleutheran Adventurers settle Eleuthera and Captain Sayle moves to New Providence; 1700s Loyalists plant cotton on the Family Islands with enslaved Africans, who win freedom in 1834 — emancipation
