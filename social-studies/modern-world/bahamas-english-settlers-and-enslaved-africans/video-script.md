# Video script — Bahamas — English settlers and enslaved Africans

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bahamas books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

English settlers reach Eleuthera in 1648 and bring enslaved Africans to work plantations; much later Africans freed on the way to Cuba choose to settle in The Bahamas instead, carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer Bookie and Rabbie stories with them; the Eleutheran Adventurers name Eleuthera, struggle to farm, trade wood and salvage wrecks; Captain Sayle moves on to the island that becomes New Providence, and settlements grow on both islands; Bahamian slaves arrive by different routes — direct from Africa, with the Loyalists from America, freed in America and settling, or freed by shipwreck — and bring new peoples and traditions with them; the Eleutheran Adventurers settle Eleuthera and Captain Sayle moves to New Providence; 1700s Loyalists plant cotton on the Family Islands with enslaved Africans, who win freedom in 1834 — emancipation

Hold that story: English settlers reach Eleuthera in 1648 and New Providence after, and enslaved Africans are brought to work the islands — arriving direct from Africa, with the Loyalists, by freedom and by shipwreck — carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer tales that become Bahamian culture
