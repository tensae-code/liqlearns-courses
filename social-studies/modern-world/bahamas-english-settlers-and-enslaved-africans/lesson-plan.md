# Lesson plan — Bahamas — English settlers and enslaved Africans

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Bahamas — English settlers and enslaved Africans
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Bahamas, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** English settlers reach Eleuthera in 1648 and New Providence after, and enslaved Africans are brought to work the islands — arriving direct from Africa, with the Loyalists, by freedom and by shipwreck — carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer tales that become Bahamian culture
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bahamian History: Eleuthera settlers, enslaved Africans and the freed Africans who chose The Bahamas** — English settlers reach Eleuthera in 1648 and bring enslaved Africans to work plantations; much later Africans freed on the way to Cuba choose to settle in The Bahamas instead, carrying Junkanoo, asue, cooking ways and the Anansi and Brer Bookie and Rabbie stories with them (Bahamian History: Eleuthera settlers, enslaved Africans and the freed Africans who chose The Bahamas)
2. **Lucayans and Eleutheran Adventurers: the Adventurers settle Eleuthera and New Providence** — the Eleutheran Adventurers name Eleuthera, struggle to farm, trade wood and salvage wrecks; Captain Sayle moves on to the island that becomes New Providence, and settlements grow on both islands (Lucayans and Eleutheran Adventurers: the Adventurers settle Eleuthera and New Providence)
3. **Slavery: how enslaved and free Africans reached The Bahamas** — Bahamian slaves arrive by different routes — direct from Africa, with the Loyalists from America, freed in America and settling, or freed by shipwreck — and bring new peoples and traditions with them (Slavery: how enslaved and free Africans reached The Bahamas)
4. **Early Settlers: Adventurers, Loyalists and the enslaved** — the Eleutheran Adventurers settle Eleuthera and Captain Sayle moves to New Providence; 1700s Loyalists plant cotton on the Family Islands with enslaved Africans, who win freedom in 1834 — emancipation (Early Settlers: Adventurers, Loyalists and the enslaved)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
