# Lesson plan — Hitler's rise to power, 1919 to 1933

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Hitler's rise to power, 1919 to 1933
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (United Kingdom, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Hitler turns a fringe party into a national movement and is appointed Chancellor on 30 January 1933.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **5.1 Hitler's early career** — A failed Austrian art student is radicalised in Vienna and Munich, finds his voice as an army informant after the war and is sent to spy on a tiny workers' party he ends up leading
2. **5.2 The early growth of the Nazi Party** — Drexler's DAP becomes the NSDAP with a 25-point programme; Hitler's speeches, the SA and the party newspaper turn a Munich fringe group into a movement
3. **5.3 The Munich Putsch** — In November 1923 Hitler bursts into a beer hall to seize Bavaria and march on Berlin; police fire stops it, but the trial gives him a national platform and prison time to write Mein Kampf
4. **5.4 The lean years, 1924–28** — Banned then refounded, the party reorganises into a national machine with Goebbels' propaganda, yet recovery-era voters ignore it — 12 seats in 1928
5. **6.2 Growth in support for the Nazis** — Fear of communism, Goebbels' posters and rallies, SA spectacle and Hitler's flights across Germany turn the Nazis into the party of the desperate and the frightened alike
6. **6.3 Political developments in 1932** — Brüning falls, von Papen and Schleicher intrigue, the Nazis become the largest party in July 1932, and Hindenburg — who detests Hitler — is talked into appointing him Chancellor on 30 January 1933
