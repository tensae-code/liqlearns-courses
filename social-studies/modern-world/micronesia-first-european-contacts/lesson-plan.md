# Lesson plan — Micronesia — first European contacts

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Micronesia — first European contacts
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Micronesia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The first outsiders reached Micronesia one landing at a time — storm-blown Portuguese in Ulithi, Spaniards in Yap, welcomes that turned hostile in Chuuk Lagoon and Pulap — and the small atolls kept their own registers of every arrival, from the man Tongohiti to canoes and ships logged by name and date.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told in order, part by part, as Micronesia's books tell it. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Portuguese in Ulithi, Spaniards in Yap, musket fire in the Marshalls** — say that storm-blown Portuguese under Dioga da Rocha met islanders face to face, Spanish visits puzzled both sides, and welcomes turned hostile in Chuuk Lagoon and Pulap as ships demanded provisions.
2. **Kapingamarangi: the first outsiders, one by one** — say that Kapingamarangi remembers its first outsiders one by one — Tongohiti, the Wareai traders of the 1800s, castaways from Kiribati, and the Moduroo people in the 1870s.
3. **Nukuoro: an atoll keeping its own contact register** — say that Nukuoro logged arrivals one by one — canoes and money from Fiji, Kiribati, the Marshalls in 1870, Onotoa, Yap, Sonsorol and Namoluk, and European sightings in 1806 and 1840.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
