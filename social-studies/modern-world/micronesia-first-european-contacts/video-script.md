# Video script — Micronesia — first European contacts

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as Micronesia's books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

In *Over Seas and Time*, first contact is not one event but many small ones. Storm-blown Portuguese under Dioga da Rocha meet islanders face to face in Ulithi; Spanish visits to Yap puzzle both sides, each trying to read the other.

And welcomes could turn hostile: in Chuuk Lagoon and Pulap, meetings soured as ships demanded provisions, and musket fire enters the islands' story. First contact, the book teaches, was face to face, island by island — wonder, trade and violence close together.

The Kapingamarangi reader keeps the atoll's own record of the time before the foreigners settled in. The first outsiders arrive one by one: the man Tongohiti, the Wareai traders of the 1800s, castaways from Kiribati, and the Moduroo people in the 1870s.

Each arrival is remembered in the atoll's own record, in its own language — the island, not the ship, keeps the list.

Nukuoro's reader does the same work as a register. Arrivals are logged one by one: money and canoes from Fiji, Kiribati, the Marshalls (1870), Onotoa, Yap, Sonsorol and Namoluk — and a European sighting in 1806, and another in 1840.

Long before any government archive, the atoll itself was keeping dated, named records of who came. That register is the island's first written history of contact.
