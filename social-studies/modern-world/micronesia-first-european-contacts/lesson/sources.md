Micronesia's own way (Over Seas and Time): one national story told island by island in strict time order — settlement, contact, visitors, then four foreign rulers in turn (Spain, Germany, Japan, America) — with every chapter opening in a lived scene (a boy watching a strange ship, a Chuukese man weeping at a flag) and closing with vocabulary, understanding, critical-thinking and community-research tasks. Micronesia's own way (Kapingamarangi History Reader): the atoll writes its own history in its own language — the science of settlement first, then the founding story of Utamatua, the chiefs (ariki) and their land system, and finally the foreign eras, Spanish, German, Japanese and American, each given its own dated section. That way is stocked here as the way Micronesia teaches this thing. The reader's stories are filed here as plot summaries in the course's own words only — never transcribed.

Micronesia's own way (Nukuoro History Reader): Nukuoro remembers itself as a meeting place — the settlement science, then the social order of its chiefs, then a long roll of arrivals: Vave the culture-bringer, and canoes and ships from Fiji, Kiribati, the Marshalls, Onotoa, Yap, Sonsorol and Namoluk, each landing dated and named.
