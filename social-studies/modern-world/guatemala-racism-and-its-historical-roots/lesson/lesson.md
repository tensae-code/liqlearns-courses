# Guatemala — racism and its historical roots

## Do it first

Before you read on, think about this lesson's one thing: racism is taught as a relation of domination built across history to strip peoples of their goods, lived daily by the Maya peoples — told through the 2002 restaurant exclusion of Dr Irma Alicia Velásquez Nimatuj (teacher guide — evidence only). Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — 2002 — the restaurant door (teacher guide — evidence)

The Grade 8 teacher guide teaches racism through one case. In 2002, Dr Irma Alicia Velásquez Nimatuj, a Maya K'iche' anthropologist in gala traditional dress, is turned away from a Guatemala City restaurant while her ladina companions are let in. Her case becomes a landmark in the national fight against racism. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

In your mind's eye: a Guatemala City restaurant door in 2002 turned against a Maya K'iche' scholar in traditional dress, and a classroom chart naming everyday racist phrases. Name Dr Velásquez Nimatuj and the case.

## Sub lesson 2 — El racismo escolar — racism defined (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's content sheet 'El racismo escolar' defines its subject: racism is a relation of domination built on telling peoples apart to strip their goods, its prejudices formed across history. In Guatemala, it says, the Maya peoples meet it daily — in schools, hospitals, buses and government — in everyday phrases the guide lists and names as racist. Racism here is history still being lived. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence only.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 2 beat(s): 2002 — the restaurant door (teacher guide — evidence); El racismo escolar — racism defined (teacher guide — evidence). Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
