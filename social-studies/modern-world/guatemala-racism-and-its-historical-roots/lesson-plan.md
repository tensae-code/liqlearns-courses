# Lesson plan — Guatemala — racism and its historical roots

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala — racism and its historical roots
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Guatemala, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** racism is taught as a relation of domination built across history to strip peoples of their goods, lived daily by the Maya peoples — told through the 2002 restaurant exclusion of Dr Irma Alicia Velásquez Nimatuj (teacher guide — evidence only).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **2002 — the restaurant door (teacher guide — evidence)** — a Maya K'iche' anthropologist in gala traditional dress is turned away from a Guatemala City restaurant while ladina companions are let in; her case becomes a landmark in the national fight against racism
2. **El racismo escolar — racism defined (teacher guide — evidence)** — racism is defined as a relation of domination built on telling peoples apart to strip their goods, its prejudices formed across history; in Guatemala the Maya peoples meet it daily — in schools, hospitals, buses and government — in everyday phrases the guide lists and names as racist

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the scholar turned away in 2002: Dr Irma Alicia Velásquez Nimatuj.
2. Say who she is: a Maya K'iche' anthropologist.
3. Say what her case became: a landmark in the fight against racism.
4. Give the guide's definition: racism as a relation of domination built to strip peoples of their goods.
5. Name where Maya peoples meet it daily: schools, hospitals, buses and government.
6. Say how its prejudices formed: across history.
