# Lesson plan — Guatemala — independence: causes and proclamation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala — independence: causes and proclamation
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Guatemala, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** independence is shaken loose from outside by US independence, the French Revolution and Napoleon's seizure of Spain, and rotted from inside by criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes — until Central America's Act is signed on 15 September 1821, drafted by José Cecilio del Valle.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Causes — shaken outside, rotted inside** — US independence, the French Revolution's liberty-equality-fraternity and Napoleon's seizure of Spain shake the empire from outside; criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes rot it from inside
2. **15 September 1821 — Central America declares** — Delgado raises San Salvador in 1811, the Belén conspiracy is uncovered in 1813, Atanasio Tzul rebels against tribute in 1820; liberals and conservatives argue in rival newspapers until José Cecilio del Valle drafts the Act signed on 15 September 1821

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other History lessons of this title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name an outside cause: US independence, the French Revolution, or Napoleon's seizure of Spain.
2. Name an inside cause: criollo exclusion, trade monopoly, or war taxes.
3. Name the French Revolution's three words: liberty, equality, fraternity.
4. Name the date of the Act: 15 September 1821.
5. Name its drafter: José Cecilio del Valle.
6. Name the 1820 rebel against tribute: Atanasio Tzul.
