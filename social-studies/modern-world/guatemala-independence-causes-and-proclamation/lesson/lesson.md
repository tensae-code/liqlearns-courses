# Guatemala — independence: causes and proclamation

## Do it first

Before you read on, think about this lesson's one thing: independence is shaken loose from outside by US independence, the French Revolution and Napoleon's seizure of Spain, and rotted from inside by criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes — until Central America's Act is signed on 15 September 1821, drafted by José Cecilio del Valle. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Causes — shaken outside, rotted inside

Guatemala's Grade 4 book divides independence's causes in two. From outside: US independence, the French Revolution's liberty-equality-fraternity, and Napoleon's seizure of Spain shake the empire. From inside: criollo exclusion, trade monopoly and war taxes rot it. An empire weakened on both sides cannot hold.

In your mind's eye: the signing on 15 September 1821, with newspapers of liberals and conservatives on the table and José Cecilio del Valle drafting the Act. Name the date and the drafter.

## Sub lesson 2 — 15 September 1821 — Central America declares

The road to the Act runs through revolt: Delgado raises San Salvador in 1811, the Belén conspiracy is uncovered in 1813, Atanasio Tzul rebels against tribute in 1820. Liberals and conservatives argue in rival newspapers until José Cecilio del Valle drafts the Act signed on 15 September 1821 — Central America's independence proclamation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 2 beat(s): Causes — shaken outside, rotted inside; 15 September 1821 — Central America declares. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
