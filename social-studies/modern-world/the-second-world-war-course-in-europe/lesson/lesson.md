# The Second World War — course in Europe

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — poland, the fall of france, pearl harbor is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — Poland, the fall of France, Pearl Harbor

**Germany's invasion of Poland opened the war.** Turning west, she invaded **Norway, Denmark and Luxembourg**, used Norway to enter **France** and took its **north and west**, while a government of her own was set up at **Vichy** in the south — and the escaped French under **General de Gaulle** continued the fight as **Free France**. Across the Pacific, **America opposed Japan's Far East expansion with a trade embargo**; Japan answered by **bombing the Pearl Harbor naval base in Hawaii on December 7, 1941**. **Germany and Italy declared war on America, and America entered the war.** The war was now truly a world war, on every ocean.

## Sub lesson 2 — The invasion of Russia and Stalingrad

On **June 22, 1941, Germany invaded Russia on three prongs** — **north toward Stalingrad** (as printed in the book), **centre to Moscow**, **south to Ukraine**. The invasion reached deep — and then broke. In **1943 the Soviet Red Army first defeated the Germans at Stalingrad**, and **from that battle the German retreat and the Red Army's advance began**, clearing **Romania, Bulgaria, Yugoslavia, Hungary and Poland** as it drove on Germany. Stalingrad is this war's hinge in Europe: before it, Germany advanced; after it, Germany retreated, all the way home.

## Sub lesson 3 — El Alamein, Normandy, the Elbe

In **North Africa**, the combined **American and British force defeated the German and Italian armies at El Alamein in Egypt**, then invaded **Italy, which accepted defeat unconditionally**. On **June 6, 1944**, the combined force **landed in Normandy** to free France, drove the Germans out, and on **April 25, 1945, met the Red Army coming from the east at the Elbe river** for the final victory — the two pincers, west and east, closing on the same handshake inside Germany.
