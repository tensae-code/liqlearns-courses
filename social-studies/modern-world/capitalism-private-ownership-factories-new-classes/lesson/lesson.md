# Capitalism — private ownership, factories, new classes

## Sub lesson 1 — Private ownership — the system's foundation

Begin with a simple picture. In the world this lesson opens on, the things that make other things — land, tools, workshops, mines, machines — are owned not by kings or by whole villages, but by private people. That is capitalism's foundation stone: **private ownership of most of the means of production**. It became the dominant system in the West as the old feudal order collapsed.

Hold that one idea and the rest of the lesson hangs on it. Whoever owns the means of production decides what is made, and keeps the profit. Everyone else must sell the one thing they own — their work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Steam, factories, new classes

Then comes the great change. The **Industrial Revolution** began in 18th-century England and spread to Belgium, France and the German states. **Coal and steam** took the place of wind and water as the powers that drove machines. Work left the home: the **factory system** replaced the **domestic system** of home workshops, gathering workers under one roof, at machines they did not own.

And society itself was re-sorted. Out of this tremendous transformation of Western Europe and the USA in the 19th century came new socio-economic classes — above all the **industrial capitalists**, who owned the factories, and the **factory workers**, who worked in them for wages. ## Sub lesson 3 — The definition in class terms (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher's guide states the same system in sharper, class terms — learn it as the definition behind the story. Capitalism is a socio-economic system in which resources are owned by **a few rich private people** and production is **for profit**. The factors of production are owned by the capitalists — the **bourgeoisie** — while the **proletariat** sell their labour for **wages**. Above it all stands one rule of the system as the guide gives it: **complete free trade and competition, with no restriction or interference by government** — everything is left to private enterprise.
