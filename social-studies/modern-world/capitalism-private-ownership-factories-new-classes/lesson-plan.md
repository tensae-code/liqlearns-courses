# Lesson plan — Capitalism — private ownership, factories, new classes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Capitalism — private ownership, factories, new classes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Development of Capitalism and Nationalism 1815–1914 (Grade 10 History).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Capitalism rests on **private ownership** of the means of production — and the Industrial Revolution remade it: steam and factories replaced home workshops, and two new classes, industrial capitalists and factory workers, stepped onto the stage.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier Modern World lessons of this title — the learner has met the early-modern world of merchants and towns. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Private ownership — the system's foundation** — Say that capitalism rests on private ownership of most means of production, and that it became dominant in the West as feudalism collapsed.
2. **Steam, factories, new classes** — Explain how the Industrial Revolution transformed capitalism: coal and steam replaced wind and water, the factory system replaced the domestic system, and industrial capitalists and factory workers emerged as new classes.
3. **The definition in class terms (teacher guide — evidence)** — State the guide's sharper definition: resources owned by a few rich private people, production for profit, the bourgeoisie owning the factors of production, the proletariat selling labour for wages, and no government interference.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what capitalism rests on: private ownership of most means of production.
2. Explain what the Industrial Revolution changed: steam for wind and water, factories for home workshops.
3. Name the two new classes the factory age created.
4. State the teacher guide's definition in class terms: bourgeoisie, proletariat, wages, no government interference.
