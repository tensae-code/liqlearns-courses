# Lesson plan — Old Kuwait's economy — herding, crafts and the sea

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Old Kuwait's economy — herding, crafts and the sea
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Kuwait, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Before oil, Kuwait lived by herds, hands and hulls — herding and palm farming, household crafts, and shipbuilding and fishing on some of the oldest grounds in the region.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Old economic life — herding and farming** — herders water the flocks while women milk them and churn the milk in the leather samil, and palm and field farming feed the settlements (Unit 4 Lesson 1: Old economic life — herding and farming)
2. **Old handicrafts in the Gulf** — wool is clipped, women gather to work it, and the old crafts turn local wool, clay and fibre into the goods every household needs (Unit 4 Lesson 2: Old handicrafts in the Gulf)
3. **Shipbuilding and fishing** — builders raise the Gulf's named sailing ships and fishermen work some of the oldest grounds in the region, the twin crafts that fed and carried the coast (Unit 4 Lesson 4: Shipbuilding and fishing)
