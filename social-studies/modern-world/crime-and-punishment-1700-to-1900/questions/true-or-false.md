# Crime and punishment, 1700 to 1900 — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Highway robbery, poaching and organised smuggling gangs like the Hawkhurst Gang grow with trade and taxes, while the Tolpuddle Martyrs are punished for forming a union.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 3.2 Changing attitudes to punishment, c1700–c1900.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The Bloody Code swells to 222 capital crimes by 1810, then opinion turns — transportation to Australia rises and falls, public execution ends and prison takes over.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.2 Changing attitudes to punishment, c1700–c1900

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.2 Changing attitudes to punishment, c1700–c1900

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The Fielding brothers' Bow Street Runners (1748) start the shift from catching thieves after the fact to preventing crime, leading to organised police forces.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.3 Law enforcement, c1700–c1900

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.3 Law enforcement, c1700–c1900

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** As reported crime quadruples, Victorians build Pentonville (opened 1842) on silence and isolation to reform prisoners; the regime is later softened as its harms show.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.4 Case study: the separate system at Pentonville Prison

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.4 Case study: the separate system at Pentonville Prison

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** Home Secretary Robert Peel cuts the capital statutes, reforms prisons and in 1829 founds the Metropolitan Police, the model copied across the country.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.5 Case study: the reforms of Robert Peel

**10. (Sub lesson 5)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Crime and punishment through time, c1000–present* is about 3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.5 Case study: the reforms of Robert Peel
