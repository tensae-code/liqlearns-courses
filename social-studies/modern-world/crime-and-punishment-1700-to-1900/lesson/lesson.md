# Crime and punishment, 1700 to 1900

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Between 1700 and 1900, crime changes with trade and towns, the Bloody Code falls, and police and prisons take over. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — 3.1 Changing definitions of crime, c1700–c1900

Highway robbery, poaching and organised smuggling gangs like the Hawkhurst Gang grow with trade and taxes, while the Tolpuddle Martyrs are punished for forming a union.

## Sub lesson 2 — 3.2 Changing attitudes to punishment, c1700–c1900

The Bloody Code swells to 222 capital crimes by 1810, then opinion turns — transportation to Australia rises and falls, public execution ends and prison takes over.

## Sub lesson 3 — 3.3 Law enforcement, c1700–c1900

The Fielding brothers' Bow Street Runners (1748) start the shift from catching thieves after the fact to preventing crime, leading to organised police forces.

## Sub lesson 4 — 3.4 Case study: the separate system at Pentonville Prison

As reported crime quadruples, Victorians build Pentonville (opened 1842) on silence and isolation to reform prisoners; the regime is later softened as its harms show.

## Sub lesson 5 — 3.5 Case study: the reforms of Robert Peel

Home Secretary Robert Peel cuts the capital statutes, reforms prisons and in 1829 founds the Metropolitan Police, the model copied across the country.
