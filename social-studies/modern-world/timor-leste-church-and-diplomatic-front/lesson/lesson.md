# Timor-Leste — the Church and the diplomatic front

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of a country whose whole modern story fits inside one person's lifetime — grandparents who were there at the proclamation, children who learn it from their own family's memory. Hold that thought: this lesson is one chapter of exactly such a story, as Timor-Leste's own Grade 6 book teaches it. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Church shelters the people

Under occupation, the Catholic Church becomes a shelter for the people — a place of protection when little else can protect them. Its power shows in one great scene the book teaches: the Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu, where a religious gathering becomes, in the same moment, a demonstration — a whole people visible to the world.

In your mind's eye: the key scene of this lesson as the book teaches it. Name the people and the place in the picture and say what happened there.

## Sub lesson 2 — Diplomats carry the cause to the world

The second weapon in this lesson carries no gun at all: diplomacy. A handful of diplomats carry the cause of a small occupied country to the United Nations and to the world, keeping its claim alive abroad through all the years when it cannot be won at home.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of this lesson in your own words, keeping its story beat: The role of the Catholic Church and the diplomatic service: the Church shelters the people, the Pope's mass at Tasi-Tolu becomes a demonstration, and diplomats carry the cause to the UN and the world — when guns cannot win, a bishop's church and a handful of diplomats keep a nation's claim alive abroad. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

1. Write, from memory, the lesson's key names and dates in the order they happened.
2. Write the story beat of this lesson in two or three sentences, in your own words — summary only, never copied words.
3. Say what Timor-Leste's own way of teaching this chapter is: lived memory, taught through songs, drama, photographs, poems and interviews.
