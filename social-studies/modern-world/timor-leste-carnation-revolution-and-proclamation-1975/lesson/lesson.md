# Timor-Leste — the Carnation Revolution and the proclamation of 1975

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of a country whose whole modern story fits inside one person's lifetime — grandparents who were there at the proclamation, children who learn it from their own family's memory. Hold that thought: this lesson is one chapter of exactly such a story, as Timor-Leste's own Grade 6 book teaches it. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A song topples a dictatorship in Lisbon

In Portugal, a dictatorship falls in the Carnation Revolution. The book teaches that moment through a song: Zeca Afonso's Grândola Vila Morena, the song-signal of the revolution. For a small colony far away, that fall in Lisbon is the door opening — decolonisation, long denied, suddenly becomes possible.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nine days of freedom in Dili

In Dili, Fretilin proclaims the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste on 28 November 1975. Francisco Xavier do Amaral becomes president, and the anthem Pátria is sung for the first time. The book gives this freedom its true measure: nine days. Nine days of an independent republic before the invasion that the next lesson tells.
