# Timor-Leste — the Indonesian invasion and the war, 1975–1979

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of a country whose whole modern story fits inside one person's lifetime — grandparents who were there at the proclamation, children who learn it from their own family's memory. Hold that thought: this lesson is one chapter of exactly such a story, as Timor-Leste's own Grade 6 book teaches it. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The giant neighbour invades

In December 1975, Indonesia — the giant neighbour — invades the newly proclaimed republic. Fretilin organises the war against it, at first as positional war: holding ground and defending positions, as a regular army would.

In your mind's eye: the key scene of this lesson as the book teaches it. Name the people and the place in the picture and say what happened there.

## Sub lesson 2 — Encirclement and annihilation

The invasion becomes campaigns of encirclement and annihilation, and the population is devastated. The book does not teach this only as operations on a map: children read photographs, and they read a poem — Esperanças Rasgadas, Torn Hopes, by Xanana Gusmão — so the war is felt in a person's words as well as counted in campaigns. The people's army retreats to the mountains as the bombing campaigns begin.
