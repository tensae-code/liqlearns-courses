# Republic and Reign of Terror

## Do it first

A revolution that began with the rights of man ends up, four years later, cutting off heads by the thousand in the name of saving those rights. How does a promise turn into a blade? This lesson follows the turn — honestly, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — The monarchy falls; the republic fights for its life

The revolution's first constitution satisfied almost no one for long. France slid into war with the monarchies of Europe, which feared the revolution's ideas crossing their own borders — and the war went badly. In Paris the crowd rose, the monarchy was overthrown, and a republic was proclaimed. The king, tried by the new National Convention, was executed — an act that told Europe's kings this revolution would show no mercy to crowns.

The republic now fought on two fronts at once: foreign armies on its borders, and armed revolt inside its own provinces, where many rose against conscription and the revolution's attacks on the Church. A government that believes it is surrounded by enemies, inside and out, reaches for emergency powers. France's emergency took a committee's name — and then a terror's.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Terror, 1793–94 — and Thermidor

Power gathered into the Committee of Public Safety, dominated by Maximilien Robespierre. Its answer to the republic's enemies was the Reign of Terror of 1793–94: revolutionary tribunals, suspicion as evidence, and the guillotine working in the squares of Paris and the provinces. Thousands were executed — nobles and priests, but also ordinary people and, increasingly, revolutionaries themselves, devoured by the machine they had built.

In the month of Thermidor, 1794, the Convention turned at last on Robespierre himself; he went to the guillotine he had served, and the Terror collapsed with him. Reaction followed: the tribunals were reined in, the emergency relaxed, and France swung away from the revolution's most radical phase — exhausted, governed by weaker hands, and open to the soldier who ends this story in the next lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the turn in four steps: the monarchy falls; the republic fights outside and inside; the Committee answers with the Terror; Thermidor ends it. Finish by saying what the Terror cost the revolution's own promise.
