# Lesson plan — The Cold War — what it was and what it did to Africa

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Cold War — what it was and what it did to Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 13 — The Modern World (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From 1945 to 1991 America and the Soviet Union fought an ideological war — capitalism against communism — without meeting in direct battle; its weight fell on the developing world, and on Africa above all.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has followed the early-modern and modern world in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **An ideology war between two powers** — Capitalism against communism, America against Russia — a war of words and propaganda.
2. **What it did to Africa** — Aid and support used to trigger coups and revolutions; new ties that continued the old rule by an indirect hand.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the Cold War: years, the two ideologies, the two leaders, and the glossary's 'war of words and propaganda'.
2. Explain how it fell on Africa: aid and support triggering coups and revolutions; close ties with six named powers.
3. State the verdict: Cold War and colonialism together kept Africa from fast development and spread neo-colonialism, the indirect-hand rule.
