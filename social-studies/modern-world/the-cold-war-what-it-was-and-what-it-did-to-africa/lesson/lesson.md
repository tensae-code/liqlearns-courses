# The Cold War — what it was and what it did to Africa

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — an ideology war between two powers is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — An ideology war between two powers

The **Cold War (1945–1991)** was an **ideological war between the proponents of capitalism and communism**, led by **America** and **Russia (the Soviet Union)** — the two great powers the last lesson left standing. The chapter glossary fixes its flavour: the Cold War was '**a war of words and propaganda dividing the world into two ideologies**'. Cold, because the two leaders did not fight each other directly; a war, because each sought by every other means — allies, aid, arms, ideas — to win the world to its side. Every country was pressed to choose a camp, or to be fought over by both.

## Sub lesson 2 — What it did to Africa

Though a world rivalry, the Cold War **heavily affected the developing countries, and Africa in particular**. In Africa, **aid and support were used to trigger coups and revolutions** — outside money and weapons deciding who ruled. Africa in this age drew **close political ties with America, Russia, Britain, France, China and Cuba** — so that **her tie to the colonial powers continued**. The book's verdict is blunt: **the Cold War and colonialism together kept Africa from fast development**, and both **spread neo-colonialism — the 'indirect-hand' colonial rule — in Africa**: rule no longer by direct governors, but by influence from outside, through aid, arms and friendly rulers.
