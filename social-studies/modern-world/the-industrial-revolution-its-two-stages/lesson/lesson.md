# The Industrial Revolution — its two stages

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — the first stage, 1750–1870 is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first stage, 1750–1870

The **first Industrial Revolution (1750–1870)**: the **first complex machines spread**, the **early factories were built**, **steam became the power source** for production and transport, **coal, iron and steel were produced in bulk**, and **new transport kinds multiplied** — the railway age of the lesson before this one. Hold its signature in one line: muscle and water gave way to **steam and coal**, and making things moved into factories for good.

## Sub lesson 2 — The second stage, after 1870 — and the verdict

The **second stage (after 1870)** changed the power and the products. **Electricity, petroleum and atomic energy** became power sources. **Science was put to building industry**, and **synthetics** were produced in bulk — **textiles, metals, chemicals, transport goods, plastic, nylon**. **Aeroplanes, radio, television and electronic messages** were invented and put to use, and the age runs on to **artificial satellites circling the Earth and craft sent to other planets**. Then the book gives its verdict, and this lesson keeps it whole: the revolution **changed the face of the world**; **despite negative results, its benefits dominated**. The next lessons show the shadow side too — arms races and world wars were also built in factories.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the whole lesson as a story, without looking: Industrialisation came in two stages: 1750–1870, the age of steam, coal and iron; after 1870, the age of electricity, petroleum, synthetics and satellites — and the book's verdict is that its benefits dominated.
