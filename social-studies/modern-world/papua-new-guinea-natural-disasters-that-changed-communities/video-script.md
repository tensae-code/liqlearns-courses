# Video script — Papua New Guinea — natural disasters that changed communities

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Papua New Guinea's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The sea that carried the first migrants can also erase a coastline in minutes — causes and effects of tidal waves and tsunamis, with PNG's disaster history (the 1998 tsunami in the guide's sources) as the warning.

Every few years the warm pool slides east — drought where rain belongs in Indonesia, Australia and PNG, floods on South America's dry coast, a weakened Indian monsoon — a climate event with a human history.

Waves that travel at jet speed in deep water and rear up in the shallows — destruction, death, disease, environmental ruin, cost and lifelong psychological wounds counted effect by effect.

Three earthquakes side by side — Christchurch 2011 (185 dead), Tohoku 2011 (magnitude 9, 15,897 dead, US$360 billion), Haiti 2010 (230,000 dead) — devastation compared country by country.

Blanche Bay itself was blown out 1,400 years ago; on 19 September 1994, after 27 hours of shaking, Tavurvur and Vulcan erupt together at dawn and destroy the picturesque, historical town of Rabaul — at least seven die and a town's history is buried in ash.

One storm, three names — hurricane in the Atlantic, typhoon in the Northwest Pacific, cyclone in the South Pacific and Indian Ocean — and what each has done to the coasts it struck.
