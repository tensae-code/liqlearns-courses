# Video script — Micronesia — Protestant missions and mission schools

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as Micronesia's books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

*Over Seas and Time* calls this chapter The Foundations of Christianity. In the 1850s, American Protestant missionaries landed in Kosrae and Pohnpei. The faith was not simply imposed: chiefs weighed it — the Nahnken of Kitti among them — judging what the new teaching would do to their people.

And islanders became the carriers: islander missionaries took the faith on to the Marshalls, the Mortlocks and Chuuk. Christianity spread across Micronesia largely in Micronesian hands.

The missions built schools. Mission schools trained islander teachers and pastors, and literacy spread with the faith: English church-and-school words entered the island languages, words still heard today.

Reading changed the balance of power: literacy made native Christians too strong for the last stands against them. The school, as much as the church, is this chapter's lasting building.

The Grade 11 government book tells Kosrae's part of the story. American missionaries reached a Kosrae whose population had fallen ninety percent in fifty years, and whose old religion seemed to have failed its people.

There the church did double work: it rebuilt Kosraean leadership and faith together. On Kosrae, the mission is remembered not only as a new religion but as the frame in which a shattered society was put back together.
