# Lesson plan — Guatemala's four peoples — Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala's four peoples — Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Guatemala, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** four peoples share one country — Maya rooted before the conquest, Garífuna descended from Africans brought as slaves, Xinka traders of the south coast, Ladino born of colonial mixing — each with its own way of learning, and all recognised by the Peace Accords.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Los cuatro pueblos — four peoples, one country** — four peoples share one country — Maya rooted before the conquest, Garífuna descended from Africans brought as slaves, Xinka traders of the south coast, Ladino born of colonial mixing — and the Peace Accords recognise them all
2. **Four ways of knowing — the peoples' learning (teacher guide — evidence)** — four peoples, four ways of knowing — Xinka learning centred on the Moon and community, Maya gratitude and the value of work (mitijul) handed down from the first four couples, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors, Ladino identity still being built
3. **A classroom of many peoples (teacher guide — evidence)** — a lottery game and a family tree show one classroom holding many peoples and 20+ languages, all descended from shared roots
4. **The four peoples in primero básico (teacher guide — evidence)** — Xinka learning is centred on the Moon and the community, with women forming the youngest children; Maya gratitude is traced to the first four couples of creation, and work done with diligence (mitijul) is what keeps violence away; Garífuna learning flows from the sea, the ancestors and the Hiyuruwa
5. **The four peoples in segundo básico (teacher guide — evidence)** — the same ancestral foundations are restated for older pupils — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors
6. **The four peoples in tercero básico (teacher guide — evidence)** — the same ancestral foundations are restated for the oldest básico pupils — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Guatemala's four peoples: Maya, Garífuna, Xinka and Ladino.
2. Say where the Garífuna descend from: Africans brought as slaves.
3. Say what recognises all four peoples: the Peace Accords.
4. Name the centre of Xinka learning: the Moon and community.
5. Name the Maya value of work: mitijul, with gratitude.
6. Say where Garífuna spirituality flows from: the sea and the ancestors.
7. Name the game that teaches the languages: the language lottery.
8. Name three languages on its cards: Cho'rti', Mopan and Tz'utujil.
9. Say what the family tree shows: many peoples descended from shared roots.
10. Say who forms the youngest Xinka children: women of the community.
11. Say what mitijul keeps away, in the guide's telling: violence.
12. Name the Garífuna source named here: the Hiyuruwa, with sea and ancestors.
13. Say how the series treats these foundations: it restates them for older pupils.
14. Name the three peoples named in this version: Xinka, Maya and Garífuna.
15. Say for whom this version is restated: the oldest básico pupils.
16. Name the pattern across the series: the four peoples open every guide.
