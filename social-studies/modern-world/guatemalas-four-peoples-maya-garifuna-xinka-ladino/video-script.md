# Video script — Guatemala's four peoples — Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Guatemala book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Guatemala's Grade 4 book frames the whole country as four peoples. The Maya are rooted before the conquest. The Garífuna descend from Africans brought as slaves. The Xinka are traders of the south coast. The Ladino people were born of colonial mixing. The Peace Accords recognise them all — four peoples, one shared country, many languages and cosmovisions.

The second-cycle primary teacher guide gives each people its own way of learning and teaching. Xinka learning is centred on the Moon and community. Maya learning teaches gratitude and the value of work — mitijul — handed down from the first four couples. Garífuna learning flows from spirituality of sea and ancestors. Ladino identity, the guide says, is still being built. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence.

The primary guide makes the four peoples visible in class: activities called 'Guatemala una sociedad de muchos pueblos', a language lottery with word cards in Cho'rti', Mopan, Tz'utujil and other Mayan languages, a family tree, and 'Guatemala una sociedad diversa por sus pueblos' for Grade 5. A lottery game and a family tree show one classroom holding many peoples and more than twenty languages, all descended from shared roots. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence.

The Grade 7 guide restates the same foundations for older pupils: Xinka learning centred on the Moon and the community, with women forming the youngest children; Maya gratitude traced to the first four couples of creation, and work done with diligence — mitijul — as what keeps violence away; Garífuna learning flowing from the sea, the ancestors and the Hiyuruwa. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence.

The Grade 8 guide restates the ancestral foundations again for segundo básico — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors. The repetition itself is the point: every year of the series opens on the same four-peoples ground. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence.

The Grade 9 guide keeps the same opening for the oldest básico pupils — Xinka community learning under the Moon, Maya gratitude and the value of work, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors. By the end of básico, a Guatemalan pupil has met the four peoples' ways of knowing at every level. This part stands on teacher-guide evidence.
