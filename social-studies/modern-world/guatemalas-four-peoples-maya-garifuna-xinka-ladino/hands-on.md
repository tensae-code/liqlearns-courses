# Hands-on — Guatemala's four peoples — Maya, Garífuna, Xinka, Ladino

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Part 1 — Los cuatro pueblos — four peoples, one country

Draw or map this part from memory, then write its story beat in your own words: four peoples share one country — Maya rooted before the conquest, Garífuna descended from Africans brought as slaves, Xinka traders of the south coast, Ladino born of colonial mixing — and the Peace Accords recognise them all.

## Part 2 — Four ways of knowing — the peoples' learning (teacher guide — evidence)

Draw or map this part from memory, then write its story beat in your own words: four peoples, four ways of knowing — Xinka learning centred on the Moon and community, Maya gratitude and the value of work (mitijul) handed down from the first four couples, Garífuna spirituality of sea and ancestors, Ladino identity still being built.

## Part 3 — A classroom of many peoples (teacher guide — evidence)

Draw or map this part from memory, then write its story beat in your own words: a lottery game and a family tree show one classroom holding many peoples and 20+ languages, all descended from shared roots.

## Part 4 — The four peoples in primero básico (teacher guide — evidence)

Draw or map this part from memory, then write its story beat in your own words: Xinka learning is centred on the Moon and the community, with women forming the youngest children; Maya gratitude is traced to the first four couples of creation, and work done with diligence (mitijul) is what keeps violence away; Garífuna learning flows from the sea, the ancestors and the Hiyuruwa.
