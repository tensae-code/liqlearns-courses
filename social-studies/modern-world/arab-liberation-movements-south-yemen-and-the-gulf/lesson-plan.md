# Lesson plan — Arab liberation movements — South Yemen and the Gulf

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Arab liberation movements — South Yemen and the Gulf
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Sinan Pasha takes Aden for the Ottomans in 1538 but the interior stays its own; the National Front forms in 1963, a bomb at Aden airport wounds the British High Commissioner, and Britain cannot hold the South; in the Gulf, Britain brands the Trucial Coast the "Pirate Coast", binds the sheikhdoms with perpetual protection treaties and forbids oil concessions without her consent — then, days before the Emirates unite in 1971, Iran seizes the Greater and Lesser Tunbs and lands on Abu Musa, as Prime Minister Hoveyda announces it to his parliament; Qatar and Bahrain nurse their own national movements around the British Political Resident's base, and Oman — Ya'ariba imams, Sultan bin Ahmad's treaty of 1798, the Dhofar revolution — fights its long road out of the Portuguese and British shadow
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
