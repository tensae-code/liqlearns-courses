# Lesson plan — Fascism and Nazism — two dictatorships compared

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Fascism and Nazism — two dictatorships compared
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — the interwar unit; the Grade 10 book's Fascism and Nazism lesson, taught after the Depression lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two ideologically similar movements rose from the First World War's ruins and the Depression's misery — Mussolini's Fascism in Italy (party 1919, state from 1922) and Hitler's Nazism in Germany (power in 1933) — and became the main causes of the Second World War.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Great Depression lesson and the First World War results — resentment and misery are the soil of both movements. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Italy — Fascism first** — Mussolini forms the Fascist party in 1919; a Fascist state stands from 1922, built on disappointed wartime hopes.
2. **Germany — Nazism, and the comparison** — Hitler's National Socialist party takes power in 1933 on resentment of Versailles and Depression misery; the two movements compared.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Great Depression — 1929 and the world it broke. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give Mussolini's dates: party 1919, Fascist state from 1922.
2. Say whose disappointed hopes Fascism fed on in Italy.
3. Give Hitler's rise: party, resentment of Versailles, power in 1933.
4. Compare the two: what they shared, and why together they caused WWII.
