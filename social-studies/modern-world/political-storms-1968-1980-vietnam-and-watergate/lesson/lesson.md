# Political storms, 1968–1980 — Vietnam and Watergate

## Do it first

A war that spiralled and divided a nation. A break-in that brought down a president. By 1980, trust in government lay in ruins — this lesson tells how. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Identity politics fractures the old coalitions

Identity politics fractured the old coalitions — the great voting blocs that had held since the New Deal cracked along lines of race, region, gender and generation. Politics rearranged itself around the fractures.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Vietnam spirals down and divides the nation

Vietnam spiralled down: more troops, more deaths, no visible victory — and the nation divided over the war in its streets and living rooms. The war abroad became the storm at home.

## Sub lesson 3 — Watergate brings down a president

Watergate brought down a president: a break-in, a cover-up, and a resignation. The seventies ended with trust in government in ruins — the storm's wreckage was measured in what Americans no longer believed.
