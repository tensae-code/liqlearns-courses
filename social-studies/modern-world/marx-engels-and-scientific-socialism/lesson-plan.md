# Lesson plan — Marx Engels And Scientific Socialism

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Marx Engels And Scientific Socialism
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the industrial revolution creates the working class — landless peasants crowding into factories and mines, demonstrating with the English Chartists by 1848; Karl Marx (1818–1883), a Trier doctor of philosophy at 23, is expelled from Germany for his revolutionary views and continues in Paris; Friedrich Engels (1820–1895), a Barmen factory-owner's son, sees his own class's greed at first hand and writes The Condition of the Working Class in England; the two meet in 1844, found the League of Communists — the international proletariat's first independent party — and in early 1848 publish the Communist Manifesto, whose appearance the book takes as the birth of scientific socialism
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio.
