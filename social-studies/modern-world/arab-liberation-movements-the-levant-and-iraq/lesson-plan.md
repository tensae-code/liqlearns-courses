# Lesson plan — Arab liberation movements — the Levant and Iraq

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Arab liberation movements — the Levant and Iraq
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Syria answers partition with revolts at Deir ez-Zor (1921), Jabal Druze (1922) and the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925; Jordanians reject the 1928 treaty with Britain as domination and hold five national congresses (1928–1933) under the Jordanian Arab Party of Muhammad Subhi Abu Ghanima; Iraq explodes in the 1920 Revolution against British exploitation, and King Ghazi (1933–1939) defies Britain with his own radio station defending Palestine before dying in a car crash; after 1941 Lebanon wins independence in 1943 and the last French soldier leaves Syria in 1946
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
