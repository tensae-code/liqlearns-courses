# Lesson plan — The English empire in America, 1660–1763

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The English empire in America, 1660–1763
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (United States, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In one century the English colonies multiplied, bought British goods, heard revival preachers and fought Britain's wars — and slowly began to think of themselves as one people.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Restoration colonies multiply** — After the Restoration the colonies multiplied, and the Glorious Revolution reached across the ocean to America too.
2. **Slavery and consumer goods bind the colonies to Britain** — Two bindings tightened.
3. **Awakening, Enlightenment and the wars for empire** — The Great Awakening's revival preaching and the Enlightenment's new ideas gave colonists shared experiences and shared language across colony lines.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
