# The English empire in America, 1660–1763

## Do it first

In one century the English colonies multiplied, bought British goods, heard revival preachers and fought Britain's wars — and slowly began to think of themselves as one people. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Restoration colonies multiply

After the Restoration the colonies multiplied, and the Glorious Revolution reached across the ocean to America too. Britain's American empire grew wider — and its grip and its quarrels grew with it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Slavery and consumer goods bind the colonies to Britain

Two bindings tightened. Slavery bound colonial labour and wealth ever tighter to Britain's Atlantic system, and consumer goods bound colonial households to British shops and tastes. The colonies were more British than ever — and more dependent.

## Sub lesson 3 — Awakening, Enlightenment and the wars for empire

The Great Awakening's revival preaching and the Enlightenment's new ideas gave colonists shared experiences and shared language across colony lines. The wars for empire did the same. Together they taught colonists to think of themselves as one people — the seed of everything that follows.
