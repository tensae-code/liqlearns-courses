# Video script — Antebellum reform and abolition, 1820–1860

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told straight, in the order it happened. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A revival fired a generation. Out of its fire came communes, temperance pledges, the abolitionist assault on slavery — and, at Seneca Falls in 1848, the first organised demand for women's rights.

The Second Great Awakening's revival preaching fired a generation of reformers. If souls could be perfected, why not society? Reform became a religious duty carried into every corner of American life.

The fire spread widely: utopian communes trying to live a perfect society now, temperance campaigns against drink, and health reform. Each movement, in its own way, refused to accept the world as it was.

The sharpest reform was the abolitionist assault on slavery itself. And at Seneca Falls in 1848 the fire reached women's place in the republic: the first organised demand for women's rights. Reform had named the two great exclusions — slavery and women's inequality.
