# Hands-on — Guatemala — the colonial era

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Part 1 — Four foundings — a capital that keeps moving

Draw or map this part from memory, then write its story beat in your own words: founded at Iximche' in 1524, the capital flees Kaqchikel revolt to Almolonga, is drowned by Volcán de Agua mud in 1541, rebuilt at Panchoy for 230 years, then the Santa Marta earthquakes of 1773 drive it to the Ermita valley as Nueva Guatemala.

## Part 2 — Colonial society — slavery, encomienda, repartimiento

Draw or map this part from memory, then write its story beat in your own words: captured indigenous people are enslaved and wash gold; the 1542 New Laws abolish slavery but tribute and the weekly repartimiento labour draft take its place under peninsulares, criollos, castas, indigenous and Black slaves.

## Part 3 — Colonial economy, money, art and schools

Draw or map this part from memory, then write its story beat in your own words: cacao, cotton and indigo pay the colony's way, cacao beans still pass as coins until the 1733 mint; friars teach reading to teach the faith, the first press arrives in 1660 and San Carlos University in 1676 — women may only attend primary school.
