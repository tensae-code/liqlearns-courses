# Guatemala — the colonial era

## Do it first

Before you read on, think about this lesson's one thing: Santiago de Guatemala is founded and moved four times — Iximche', Almolonga, Panchoy, then the Ermita valley after the 1773 earthquakes — while colonial society is ordered by slavery, encomienda and repartimiento, and cacao, cotton and indigo pay the colony's way. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Four foundings — a capital that keeps moving

The colonial capital would not stay still. Founded at Iximche' in 1524, Santiago flees Kaqchikel revolt to Almolonga; in 1541 Volcán de Agua mud drowns it; rebuilt at Panchoy, it stands 230 years — until the Santa Marta earthquakes of 1773 drive it to the Ermita valley as Nueva Guatemala. Guatemala's Grade 4 book teaches the colony through its moving capital.

In your mind's eye: santiago's four homes on a map — Iximche', Almolonga under Volcán de Agua, Panchoy, and the Ermita valley — with cacao beans used as coins beside a 1733 mint coin. Name two of the capitals.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colonial society — slavery, encomienda, repartimiento

Colonial society, in the Grade 4 book, is a pyramid built on forced labour. Captured indigenous people are enslaved and wash gold. The 1542 New Laws abolish slavery — but tribute encomienda and the weekly repartimiento labour draft take its place, under peninsulares, criollos, castas, indigenous people and Black slaves. Abolition on paper, the book shows, did not end forced work.

## Sub lesson 3 — Colonial economy, money, art and schools

Cacao, cotton and indigo pay the colony's way — and cacao beans still pass as coins until the mint of 1733. Friars teach reading to teach the faith; the first press arrives in 1660 and San Carlos University in 1676. Women, the book notes plainly, may only attend primary school. Economy, money and schooling all serve the colonial order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 3 beat(s): Four foundings — a capital that keeps moving; Colonial society — slavery, encomienda, repartimiento; Colonial economy, money, art and schools. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory: name Santiago's first home: Iximche', founded 1524.
2. Write, from memory: name the disaster of 1541: Volcán de Agua mud at Almolonga.
3. Write, from memory: name the earthquakes that moved it in 1773: the Santa Marta earthquakes.
4. Write, from memory: name the laws of 1542: the New Laws, abolishing slavery.
5. Write, from memory: name the weekly labour draft: the repartimiento.
6. Write, from memory: name the top of the pyramid: the peninsulares.
7. Write, from memory: name the colony's paying crops: cacao, cotton and indigo.
8. Write, from memory: say what served as coins until 1733: cacao beans.
9. Write, from memory: name the university founded in 1676: San Carlos University.
