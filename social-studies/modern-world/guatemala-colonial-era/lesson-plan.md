# Lesson plan — Guatemala — the colonial era

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Guatemala — the colonial era
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Guatemala, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Santiago de Guatemala is founded and moved four times — Iximche', Almolonga, Panchoy, then the Ermita valley after the 1773 earthquakes — while colonial society is ordered by slavery, encomienda and repartimiento, and cacao, cotton and indigo pay the colony's way.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Four foundings — a capital that keeps moving** — founded at Iximche' in 1524, the capital flees Kaqchikel revolt to Almolonga, is drowned by Volcán de Agua mud in 1541, rebuilt at Panchoy for 230 years, then the Santa Marta earthquakes of 1773 drive it to the Ermita valley as Nueva Guatemala
2. **Colonial society — slavery, encomienda, repartimiento** — captured indigenous people are enslaved and wash gold; the 1542 New Laws abolish slavery but tribute and the weekly repartimiento labour draft take its place under peninsulares, criollos, castas, indigenous and Black slaves
3. **Colonial economy, money, art and schools** — cacao, cotton and indigo pay the colony's way, cacao beans still pass as coins until the 1733 mint; friars teach reading to teach the faith, the first press arrives in 1660 and San Carlos University in 1676 — women may only attend primary school

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Santiago's first home: Iximche', founded 1524.
2. Name the disaster of 1541: Volcán de Agua mud at Almolonga.
3. Name the earthquakes that moved it in 1773: the Santa Marta earthquakes.
4. Name the laws of 1542: the New Laws, abolishing slavery.
5. Name the weekly labour draft: the repartimiento.
6. Name the top of the pyramid: the peninsulares.
7. Name the colony's paying crops: cacao, cotton and indigo.
8. Say what served as coins until 1733: cacao beans.
9. Name the university founded in 1676: San Carlos University.
