# Video script — Guatemala — the colonial era

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Guatemala book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The colonial capital would not stay still. Founded at Iximche' in 1524, Santiago flees Kaqchikel revolt to Almolonga; in 1541 Volcán de Agua mud drowns it; rebuilt at Panchoy, it stands 230 years — until the Santa Marta earthquakes of 1773 drive it to the Ermita valley as Nueva Guatemala. Guatemala's Grade 4 book teaches the colony through its moving capital.

Colonial society, in the Grade 4 book, is a pyramid built on forced labour. Captured indigenous people are enslaved and wash gold. The 1542 New Laws abolish slavery — but tribute encomienda and the weekly repartimiento labour draft take its place, under peninsulares, criollos, castas, indigenous people and Black slaves. Abolition on paper, the book shows, did not end forced work.

Cacao, cotton and indigo pay the colony's way — and cacao beans still pass as coins until the mint of 1733. Friars teach reading to teach the faith; the first press arrives in 1660 and San Carlos University in 1676. Women, the book notes plainly, may only attend primary school. Economy, money and schooling all serve the colonial order.
