# Lesson plan — Jordan — from emirate to kingdom

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Jordan — from emirate to kingdom
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From Abdullah's emirate of 1921 under British supervision to the kingdom of 1946, the wars of 1948 and 1967, and the Wadi Araba treaty of 1994.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Jordan — from emirate to kingdom** — Abdullah ibn Hussein reached Ma'an in 1920 and agreed with Churchill in 1921 on an emirate east of the Jordan under British supervision; the national movement rejected the 1928 treaty and Balfour's promise; the 1946 treaty ended the mandate and Abdullah became king; Jordan fought in 1948 and joined the West Bank and Jerusalem to the kingdom, lost them in June 1967, and signed the Wadi Araba treaty in 1994.
