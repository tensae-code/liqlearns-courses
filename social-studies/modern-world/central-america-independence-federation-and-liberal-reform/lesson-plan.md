## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lección 26 Independencia de Centroamérica** — Napoleon in Spain, Cádiz 1812, the French Revolution and US independence meet criollo–peninsular rivalry and pre-independence movements; independence (15 September 1821) is signed at Guatemala by Gainza, Delgado, Cañas, Aycinena and others as an elite settlement, then comes annexation to Iturbide's Mexico and absolute independence on 1 July 1823 (Chiapas stays with Mexico)
2. **Lección 26: la Federación y la Reforma Liberal centroamericana** — the Federation's 1824 constitution abolishes slavery; Arce presides, Valle dies travelling to take the presidency he won, and Morazán (1835–1839) separates church and State and frees education; a generation later Barrios in Guatemala and Soto and Rosa in Honduras carry the Liberal Reform — codes, the 1880 constitution, secularised cemeteries and church goods, the southern road
3. **annexation, elections and the federation's advances** — the criollo elite annexed Central America to Iturbide's Mexico (5 January 1822) over the fierce resistance of Molina, Barrundia and Córdova; freed 1 July 1823, the federation held 1824 elections (liberal Arce vs the conservatives' Valle), planned an interoceanic canal, abolished slavery, consecrated asylum and limited the death penalty — before dissolving in 1839; Morazán's 1842 testament is printed at length
