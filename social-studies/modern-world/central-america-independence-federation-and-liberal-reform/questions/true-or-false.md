# Central America Independence Federation And Liberal Reform — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Napoleon in Spain, Cádiz 1812, the French Revolution and US independence meet criollo–peninsular rivalry and pre-independence movements; independence (15 September 1821) is signed at Guatemala by Gainza, Delgado, Cañas, Aycinena and others as an elite settlement, then comes annexation to Iturbide's Mexico and absolute independence on 1 July 1823 (Chiapas stays with Mexico).
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lección 26 Independencia de Centroamérica

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Libro para Estudiantes, Ciencias Sociales, Cuarto grado* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lección 26 Independencia de Centroamérica

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The Federation's 1824 constitution abolishes slavery; Arce presides, Valle dies travelling to take the presidency he won, and Morazán (1835–1839) separates church and State and frees education; a generation later Barrios in Guatemala and Soto and Rosa in Honduras carry the Liberal Reform — codes, the 1880 constitution, secularised cemeteries and church goods, the southern road.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Lección 26: la Federación y la Reforma Liberal centroamericana

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Libro para Estudiantes, Ciencias Sociales, Cuarto grado* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Lección 26: la Federación y la Reforma Liberal centroamericana

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The criollo elite annexed Central America to Iturbide's Mexico (5 January 1822) over the fierce resistance of Molina, Barrundia and Córdova; freed 1 July 1823, the federation held 1824 elections (liberal Arce vs the conservatives' Valle), planned an interoceanic canal, abolished slavery, consecrated asylum and limited the death penalty — before dissolving in 1839; Morazán's 1842 testament is printed at length.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in annexation, elections and the federation's advances

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Libro para Estudiantes, Ciencias Sociales — Octavo Grado de Educación Básica* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
