# Imperialism and its crisis

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Capitalist states carve up the world into empires, and rivalry between them brings the imperial order to crisis and world war. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Capitalist states reach outward

The book teaches imperialism as capitalism reaching outward: the capitalist states, hungry for markets, materials and land, carve up the world into empires. Colonies are not accidents — they are the system expanding.

## Sub lesson 2 — An order built on rivalry

But an order carved up between rivals carries its crisis inside it. Each empire guards its share and envies the others'. The imperial order is armed, jealous and unstable from the start.

## Sub lesson 3 — Crisis — and world war

The rivalry between the empires brings the imperial order to crisis — and the crisis breaks open as world war. The book teaches the war not as a sudden accident but as the imperial system's own contradiction arriving due.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of Imperialism and its crisis in your own words, part by part, using the sub lesson headings as your beats. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
