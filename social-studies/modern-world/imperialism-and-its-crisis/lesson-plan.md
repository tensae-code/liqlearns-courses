# Lesson plan — Imperialism and its crisis

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Imperialism and its crisis
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Brazil, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Capitalist states carve up the world into empires, and rivalry between them brings the imperial order to crisis and world war.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Capitalist states reach outward** — The book teaches imperialism as capitalism reaching outward: the capitalist states, hungry for markets, materials and land, carve up the world into empires. Colonies are not accidents — they are the system expanding.
2. **An order built on rivalry** — But an order carved up between rivals carries its crisis inside it. Each empire guards its share and envies the others'. The imperial order is armed, jealous and unstable from the start.
3. **Crisis — and world war** — The rivalry between the empires brings the imperial order to crisis — and the crisis breaks open as world war. The book teaches the war not as a sudden accident but as the imperial system's own contradiction arriving due.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Effects of industrialisation — cities, classes, empires; The First World War — causes. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the capitalist states did to the world, in the book's teaching of imperialism.
2. Say what rivalry existed inside the imperial order.
3. Say what the imperial order's crisis broke open as.
