# Video script — Papua New Guinea in the two world wars

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Papua New Guinea's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Two world wars started far away arrive in PNG — the colonisers fight each other in WWI, and in WWII whole provinces feel the fighting; children research and document the impacts on communities, individuals and groups.

Japan takes Rabaul in 1942 and makes it its South-West Pacific headquarters; ANGAU runs the unoccupied territory with PNG carriers, stretcher-bearers and interpreters; American abundance — and black soldiers with white soldiers' privileges — astonishes villagers, while bombs destroy gardens, homes and lives.

Three jungle tracks become lifelines — Kokoda, Black Cat and Bulldog — up near-vertical slopes through rivers, forest and mosquitoes, with PNG carriers hauling supplies and ammunition to exhausted soldiers; today trekkers walk Kokoda while the other two fall silent.

On the Owen Stanley Track, PNG carriers — "impromptu angels with the fuzzy-wuzzy hair" — carry badly wounded Australians down the mountain "as gentle as a nurse", and mothers in Australia pray for them by name; students map every battle site with dates and participants.
