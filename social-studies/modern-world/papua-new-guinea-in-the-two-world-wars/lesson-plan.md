# Lesson plan — Papua New Guinea in the two world wars

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Papua New Guinea in the two world wars
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Papua New Guinea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Two world wars started far away arrive in PNG — the colonisers fight each other in WWI, and in WWII whole provinces feel the fighting; children research and document the impacts on communities, individuals and groups

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **World War I / World War II / Impacts of World War II** — two world wars started far away arrive in PNG — the colonisers fight each other in WWI, and in WWII whole provinces feel the fighting; children research and document the impacts on communities, individuals and groups
2. **Impact of World War II** — Japan takes Rabaul in 1942 and makes it its South-West Pacific headquarters; ANGAU runs the unoccupied territory with PNG carriers, stretcher-bearers and interpreters; American abundance — and black soldiers with white soldiers' privileges — astonishes villagers, while bombs destroy gardens, homes and lives
3. **Significance of War Tracks during World War II** — three jungle tracks become lifelines — Kokoda, Black Cat and Bulldog — up near-vertical slopes through rivers, forest and mosquitoes, with PNG carriers hauling supplies and ammunition to exhausted soldiers; today trekkers walk Kokoda while the other two fall silent
4. **Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels** — on the Owen Stanley Track, PNG carriers — "impromptu angels with the fuzzy-wuzzy hair" — carry badly wounded Australians down the mountain "as gentle as a nurse", and mothers in Australia pray for them by name; students map every battle site with dates and participants
