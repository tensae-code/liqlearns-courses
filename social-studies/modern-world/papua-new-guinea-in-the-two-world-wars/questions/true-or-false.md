# Papua New Guinea in the two world wars — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Two world wars started far away arrive in PNG — the colonisers fight each other in WWI, and in WWII whole provinces feel the fighting; children research and document the impacts on communities, individuals and groups.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in World War I / World War II / Impacts of World War II

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Social Science Teacher Guide — Primary Grade 8, Standards Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about World War I / World War II / Impacts of World War II

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Japan takes Rabaul in 1942 and makes it its South-West Pacific headquarters; ANGAU runs the unoccupied territory with PNG carriers, stretcher-bearers and interpreters; American abundance — and black soldiers with white soldiers' privileges — astonishes villagers, while bombs destroy gardens, homes and lives.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Impact of World War II

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Social Science Junior High Grade 9 Teacher Guide, Standards-Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Impact of World War II

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Three jungle tracks become lifelines — Kokoda, Black Cat and Bulldog — up near-vertical slopes through rivers, forest and mosquitoes, with PNG carriers hauling supplies and ammunition to exhausted soldiers; today trekkers walk Kokoda while the other two fall silent.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Significance of War Tracks during World War II

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Social Science Junior High Grade 9 Teacher Guide, Standards-Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Significance of War Tracks during World War II

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** On the Owen Stanley Track, PNG carriers — "impromptu angels with the fuzzy-wuzzy hair" — carry badly wounded Australians down the mountain "as gentle as a nurse", and mothers in Australia pray for them by name; students map every battle site with dates and participants.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *History — Upper Secondary Teacher Guide (Grades 11–12, Outcomes Based Education)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels
