# Papua New Guinea in the two world wars — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: World War I / World War II / Impacts of World War II.
**Answer: Two world wars started far away arrive in PNG — the colonisers fight each other in WWI, and in WWII whole provinces feel the fighting; children research and document the impacts on communities, individuals and groups (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Papua New Guinea's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Social Science Teacher Guide — Primary Grade 8, Standards Based* (Papua New Guinea), World War I / World War II / Impacts of World War II, printed p. 76.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Impact of World War II.
**Answer: Japan takes Rabaul in 1942 and makes it its South-West Pacific headquarters; ANGAU runs the unoccupied territory with PNG carriers, stretcher-bearers and interpreters; American abundance — and black soldiers with white soldiers' privileges — astonishes villagers, while bombs destroy gardens, homes and lives (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Papua New Guinea's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Social Science Junior High Grade 9 Teacher Guide, Standards-Based* (Papua New Guinea), Impact of World War II, printed pp. 95–97.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: Significance of War Tracks during World War II.
**Answer: Three jungle tracks become lifelines — Kokoda, Black Cat and Bulldog — up near-vertical slopes through rivers, forest and mosquitoes, with PNG carriers hauling supplies and ammunition to exhausted soldiers; today trekkers walk Kokoda while the other two fall silent (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** In Papua New Guinea's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Social Science Junior High Grade 9 Teacher Guide, Standards-Based* (Papua New Guinea), Significance of War Tracks during World War II, printed p. 127.**

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Retell this part in your own words: Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels.
**Answer: On the Owen Stanley Track, PNG carriers — "impromptu angels with the fuzzy-wuzzy hair" — carry badly wounded Australians down the mountain "as gentle as a nurse", and mothers in Australia pray for them by name; students map every battle site with dates and participants (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** In Papua New Guinea's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *History — Upper Secondary Teacher Guide (Grades 11–12, Outcomes Based Education)* (Papua New Guinea), Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels, printed p. 31.**
