# Jacksonian democracy, 1820–1840

## Do it first

Andrew Jackson widened democracy for white men, broke the national bank — and drove the eastern Native nations west on the Trail of Tears. Widening and removal, in one presidency's shadow. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — A rough new political style widens democracy for white men

Andrew Jackson's rough new political style widened democracy for white men — more of them could vote, hold office and feel the government was theirs. The book holds the limit inside the praise: the widening stopped at race and sex.

In your mind's eye: the world of this lesson as the book shows it. Say what changed in this part of the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nullification crushed, the national bank crushed

Jackson crushed nullification — a state's claim to void federal law — and crushed the national bank in his war on it. The presidency itself grew stronger by the fights it won.

## Sub lesson 3 — Indian Removal and the Trail of Tears

The same energy drove the eastern Native nations west in the Indian Removal. Forced from their lands, peoples walked the Trail of Tears — thousands suffering and dying on the road west. The book sets removal beside the democracy: both are Jackson's America.
