**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the course ends by looking forward — what the pupils' own century may become is left as their question to answer
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§22 Prospects for civilisation's development in the 2000s** — the course ends by looking forward — what the pupils' own century may become is left as their question to answer
2. **Theme 5, Lessons 65–66: prospects for the development of civilisation in the 21st century** — pupils close the course by weighing what science gives humanity against the new dangers it creates
3. **Theme 5 §24: prospects of civilisation's development in the 21st century** — the year's argument peaks — can one civilisation of mankind grow without flattening the many cultures inside it?
4. **Theme 6 §29: the civilisation achievements of the newest period** — the book closes by summing the newest age's achievements and asking what they are worth
