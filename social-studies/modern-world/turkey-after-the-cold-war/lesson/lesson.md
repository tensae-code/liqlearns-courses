# Turkey after the Cold War

## Do it first

The wall falls in Berlin, and Turkey's whole map of the world falls with it: the enemy the alliances were built against dissolves, wars catch fire in the Balkans and the Gulf, and a republic between Europe and its region must renegotiate its place from first principles. Turkey's book ends here, on the threshold. Before you read on, decide what a country should hold onto when its world changes overnight. Hold your answer; it is the book's last question too.

## Sub lesson 1 — Turkey and the world on the threshold of the 21st century

Turkey's Grade 12 book ends its eight units on a threshold. The Cold War's end dissolves the order Turkey had joined the West inside: the blocs are gone, and with them the map that told the republic where it stood. Around it, the neighbourhood catches fire — the Balkans and the Gulf burn in the 1990s — while new states and new questions rise on every border.

The republic's answer, as the book leaves it, is a renegotiation: Turkey after 1990 must find its place again between Europe and its region, no longer as a flank of someone else's alliance but as a country deciding for itself. The book's closing beat: the wall falls, the neighbourhood catches fire, and the republic renegotiates its place between Europe and its region.

In your mind's eye: the threshold years — the fallen Berlin Wall's painted fragments in the foreground, and behind it a map of Turkey between Europe and the Middle East redrawn in new colours. The student points to Turkey on the map and says what has changed around it.
