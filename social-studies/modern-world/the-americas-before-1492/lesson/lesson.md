# The Americas before 1492 — three worlds on the eve of contact

## Do it first

Before any European ship crossed the Atlantic, three worlds were already full of peoples, cities, farms and trade. This lesson meets all three on the eve of contact. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The diverse Native societies of the Americas

Long before 1492 the Americas held many different Native societies — farming peoples, city builders, hunters and traders, each with its own way of life. The book opens here to make one point clear: the Americas were not empty land waiting to be found; they were lived-in worlds with histories of their own.

## Sub lesson 2 — A Europe on the brink of change

Across the ocean, Europe stood on the brink of change — its kingdoms, churches and towns were shifting, and its sailors and rulers were beginning to look outward. The Europe that would soon sail west was itself mid-transformation.

## Sub lesson 3 — West Africa — and slavery before the Atlantic trade

The third world is West Africa, where slavery already existed in forms of its own. The book is careful here: what already existed was not what came next. The Atlantic trade would transform slavery beyond recognition — in scale, in race, and in the buying and selling of people across an ocean.
