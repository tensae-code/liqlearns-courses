# Lesson plan — Stalinism Industrialisation And Collectivisation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Stalinism Industrialisation And Collectivisation
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Stalin's five-year plans force factories up and peasants into collective farms at a terrible human cost
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§21 I. V. Stalin and totalitarianism in the USSR; industrialisation; collectivisation** — Stalin's five-year plans force factories up and peasants into collective farms at a terrible human cost
2. **§21 Stalin and totalitarianism in the USSR; industrialisation; collectivisation** — five-year plans and forced collectivisation at a terrible human cost
3. **§6.1 How did the Stalinist political regime change the country?** — one party, one leader, terror as routine government
4. **§6.2 How did the USSR's economy develop in the 1920s–30s?** — from NEP markets to command plans and collective farms
5. **§6.3 "Engineers of human souls": how did art serve power?** — socialist realism conscripts writers and painters into the plan
