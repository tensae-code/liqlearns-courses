# Lesson plan — Iceland's road to independence

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Iceland's road to independence
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Iceland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Jón Sigurðsson leads Iceland's peaceful independence struggle; home rule and sovereignty in 1918 follow — and at last, in 1944, the republic is proclaimed at Þingvellir.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Jón Sigurðsson and the Fjallkona** — Jón Sigurðsson leads Iceland's peaceful independence struggle, and the Fjallkona becomes the nation's face (Verkefnablöð 19–20: Var Jón Sigurðsson frelsishetja?; Fjallkonan as national symbol)
2. **Home rule and sovereignty in 1918** — home rule, then sovereignty in 1918, mark the road's middle steps (Þjóðfrelsi og lýðræði, student book pp. 132–151)
3. **The republic at Þingvellir, 1944** — at last the republic is proclaimed at Þingvellir in 1944 (Leiðin til lýðveldis, student book pp. 146–151)
