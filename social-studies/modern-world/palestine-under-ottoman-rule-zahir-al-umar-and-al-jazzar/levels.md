# Palestine under Ottoman rule — Zahir al-Umar and al-Jazzar — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Palestine in the Ottoman era — its rulers
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Palestine is governed as sanjaks (Jerusalem's first among them), but local power keeps breaking away — Fakhr al-Din al-Ma'ni ruling Lebanon and northern Palestine from 1603, Zahir al-Umar al-Zaydani (1689–1775) building his Galilee state, and Ahmad Pasha al-Jazzar installed at Acre after crushing Zahir in 1775.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2 Lesson 4: Ottoman building works in Palestine
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Suleiman the Magnificent rebuilds Jerusalem's wall, restores the al-Qibli mosque and its sanctuary gates and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre and monasteries, and the era leaves mosques and clock towers across Palestine.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 2 Lesson 1: Palestine under the late Ottoman administration
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Tanzimat reach Palestine — the Humayun edict of 1856 grants the sects equal treatment and their own courts, the 1876 constitution follows — farming booms on European demand for olives, grapes, wheat and citrus before taxes drag it down; after Egypt's 1840 withdrawal al-Sham is re-divided, and Jerusalem becomes a mutasarrifate (1874) answering directly to Istanbul, partly to keep it from colonial rivalry and Zionist immigration.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 2 Lesson 2: foreign influence in nineteenth-century Palestine
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — a weakening empire lets the powers in — capitulations, consulates (Britain's foreign minister instructs his Jerusalem consul in 1858 to protect incoming Jews), Protestant then Catholic and Orthodox missions, the Russian Compound and Haifa's German colony, mission schools and hospitals doubling as cultural invasion.
