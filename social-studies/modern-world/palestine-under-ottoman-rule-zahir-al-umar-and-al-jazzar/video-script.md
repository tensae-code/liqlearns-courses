# Video script — Palestine under Ottoman rule — Zahir al-Umar and al-Jazzar

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Palestine's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Palestine is governed as sanjaks (Jerusalem's first among them), but local power keeps breaking away — Fakhr al-Din al-Ma'ni ruling Lebanon and northern Palestine from 1603, Zahir al-Umar al-Zaydani (1689–1775) building his Galilee state, and Ahmad Pasha al-Jazzar installed at Acre after crushing Zahir in 1775.

Suleiman the Magnificent rebuilds Jerusalem's wall, restores the al-Qibli mosque and its sanctuary gates and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre and monasteries, and the era leaves mosques and clock towers across Palestine.

The Tanzimat reach Palestine — the Humayun edict of 1856 grants the sects equal treatment and their own courts, the 1876 constitution follows — farming booms on European demand for olives, grapes, wheat and citrus before taxes drag it down; after Egypt's 1840 withdrawal al-Sham is re-divided, and Jerusalem becomes a mutasarrifate (1874) answering directly to Istanbul, partly to keep it from colonial rivalry and Zionist immigration.

A weakening empire lets the powers in — capitulations, consulates (Britain's foreign minister instructs his Jerusalem consul in 1858 to protect incoming Jews), Protestant then Catholic and Orthodox missions, the Russian Compound and Haifa's German colony, mission schools and hospitals doubling as cultural invasion.
