# Video script — Cotton and slavery — the antebellum South, 1800–1860

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told straight, in the order it happened. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

One machine made cotton pay — and made slavery pay harder. This lesson follows the money, the lives it was built on, and the culture it built.

The cotton gin made cleaning cotton fast — and made slavery more profitable than ever. Demand for enslaved labour grew with every bale. The book begins with the economics: cotton was king, and the king ruled through slavery.

Behind the economics stand lives: the book follows enslaved African Americans — their work, families and resistance under the system — and free African Americans, living free in law but fenced by racism in a slave society.

Cotton built a planter culture: great houses, defended slavery, and a ruling class that preached its way of life as civilisation. At its edge, filibusters hunted new slave states in the Caribbean — private adventurers trying to expand slavery by force.
