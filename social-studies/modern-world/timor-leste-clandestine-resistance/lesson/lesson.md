# Timor-Leste — the clandestine resistance

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of a country whose whole modern story fits inside one person's lifetime — grandparents who were there at the proclamation, children who learn it from their own family's memory. Hold that thought: this lesson is one chapter of exactly such a story, as Timor-Leste's own Grade 6 book teaches it. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Couriers under the occupier's nose

The resistance does not live only in the mountains. Inside the towns, secret — clandestine — networks carry letters to Falintil in the bush, and carry food and medicine too. The couriers are often schoolchildren and students: young people moving messages under the occupier's nose, at real risk, as organisers of a hidden war.

In your mind's eye: the key scene of this lesson as the book teaches it. Name the people and the place in the picture and say what happened there.

## Sub lesson 2 — Renetil and Solidamor — resistance inside Indonesia

The clandestine front reaches inside Indonesia itself. Student networks such as Renetil organise Timorese students studying there, and — the book insists children notice this — Indonesians themselves organise in solidarity groups such as Solidamor, supporting Timor-Leste's cause from inside the occupying country. The enemy, the lesson shows, was a government and an army, never a whole people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of this lesson in your own words, keeping its story beat: Resistance inside the towns and inside Indonesia itself: clandestine letters to Falintil in the bush, student networks such as Renetil, and Indonesian solidarity groups such as Solidamor — schoolchildren and students become couriers and organisers, carrying food, medicine and messages under the occupier's nose. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
