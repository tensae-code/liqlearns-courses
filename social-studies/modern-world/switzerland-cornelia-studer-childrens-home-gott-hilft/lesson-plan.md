# Lesson plan — Switzerland — Cornelia Studer and the Gott hilft children's home

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Switzerland — Cornelia Studer and the Gott hilft children's home
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Switzerland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Seven-year-old Cornelia Studer was sent with her brother to the Gott hilft children's home at Zizers for "rest and order" — and found eight years of work before dawn, beatings and hunger punishments behind a facade that inspectors praised; she wrote it down fifty years later.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sent for rest and order** — Cornelia is seven when she and her brother are sent to the "Gott hilft" home at Zizers, for "rest and order". What they find is eight years of pre-dawn labour, beatings, hunger punishments and abuse nobody reports. Cornelia writes it all down only fifty years later.
2. **A home network built on children's work** — The companion shows the system behind the home: a Salvation-Army couple founds the self-financing "Gott hilft" network in 1916, running on children's farm labour and unpaid carers. Graubünden counts 121 children's homes in 1955 — and when cantonal inspection finally arrives that year, it still gives "Gott hilft" top marks. (Teacher guide — evidence.)
3. **Memory, inspection and an overwhelmed carer** — At the upper level pupils read the 1955 inspection in full: it praises the home's people and passes over its punishments. Studer's own preface warns that memory mixes lived episodes with others' tales. And carer Werner Würmli's life shows untrained, tubercular staff overwhelmed by the work — including a castration he later confessed as a terrible misjudgement. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell Cornelia Studer's story: sent at seven for "rest and order", eight years of labour and punishment, written down fifty years later.
2. Say how the Gott hilft network paid for itself, and what the 1955 inspection gave it.
3. Say what the full 1955 inspection did, and what Studer's preface warns about memory.
