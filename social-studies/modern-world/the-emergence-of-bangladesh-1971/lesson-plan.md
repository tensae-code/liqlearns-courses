# Lesson plan — The emergence of Bangladesh, 1971

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The emergence of Bangladesh, 1971
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Bangladesh, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one long arc: from the ancient janapadas through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British extraction, and the 1947 partition that made East Pakistan a colony of the West — to Bangabandhu's movement, independence in 1971, the homecoming of 10 January 1972, and a name that grows from Banga to Bangladesh.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told plainly, part by part; it needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The long journey of millennia to independence** — from the ancient janapadas through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British extraction and indigo, the 1947 partition that made East Pakistan a colony of the West, to Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's movement and independence in 1971
2. **The long arc — janapada to 1971, told through economics** — Banga janapada becomes Bangal, Bangala, Bengal; Deva, Chandra, Pala and Sena dynasties, Mauryas, Guptas, Sultanate and Mughals, then Portuguese, Dutch, French and English, and finally Pakistani exploitation from 1947 — all ending in 1971
3. **The spirit, the homecoming, the rebuilding** — freed from a Pakistani prison by international pressure, Bangabandhu lands in independent Bangladesh on 10 January 1972 and turns the war's spirit — freedom of language, culture and destiny, an end to exploitation — into students farming, building and teaching literacy across the country
4. **From Banga to Bangladesh, from Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu** — the land's name grows from Banga to Bangladesh as its people's friend, Bangabandhu, becomes a symbol of all oppressed peoples and receives the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace on 23 May 1973

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the arc's stages: janapadas, Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British rule, East Pakistan, independence 1971.
2. Say what the 1947 partition made of East Pakistan in the book's words: a colony of the West.
3. Name the leader of the final movement: Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
4. Tell the name's growth: Banga janapada becomes Bangal, Bangala, Bengal.
5. Name the Europeans who came in order: Portuguese, Dutch, French and English.
6. Say what the Grade 7 telling adds: each stage is also a story of exploitation, ending in 1971.
7. Say when Bangabandhu landed in independent Bangladesh: 10 January 1972.
8. Name the war's spirit as the book gives it: freedom of language, culture and destiny; an end to exploitation.
9. Say how students carried the spirit: farming, building and teaching literacy.
10. Tell both growths: Banga to Bangladesh; Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu.
11. Say what Bangabandhu received on 23 May 1973: the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace.
12. Say who gave the 'Bishwabandhu' honour's context: the World Peace Council, in the Grade 9 book's telling.
