# Hands-on — The emergence of Bangladesh, 1971

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The long road

Draw the arc as a road with seven stops — janapadas, Pala, Sena, Muslim rulers, Mughals, British, East Pakistan — ending at a flag marked 1971.

## Not sudden

Write two sentences explaining the book's claim: why 1971 is the end of a long road, not a sudden beginning.

## Name chain

Write the name chain — Banga, Bangal, Bangala, Bengal, Bangladesh — as five links, with one ruler or power beside each link.

## Who took the wealth?

For three stages of the arc, write one sentence on who held Bengal and what the book's economic lens asks about them.

## Homecoming date

Add 10 January 1972 to your long-road drawing as the homecoming, and write what Bangabandhu returned to.

## Spirit into work

List the spirit's four parts — language, culture, destiny, end of exploitation — and beside each, one rebuilding job from the lesson.

## Two growing names

Draw two ladders side by side: Banga → Bangladesh, and Bangabandhu → Bishwabandhu. Write one event on the rung where each name changes.

## A medal's meaning

Write two sentences: what the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace honours, and why the book gives it a place in Bangladesh's story.
