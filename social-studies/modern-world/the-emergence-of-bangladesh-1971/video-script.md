# Video script — The emergence of Bangladesh, 1971

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Bangladesh book tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The Grade 6 book ends with a journey of millennia. From the ancient janapadas, Bengal passes through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers.

Then British extraction and indigo; then 1947, when partition made East Pakistan a colony of the West.

At the road's end stands Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's movement — and independence in 1971.

Grade 7 retells the arc by its name and its wealth. Banga the janapada becomes Bangal, Bangala, Bengal.

Dynasties pass — Deva, Chandra, Pala, Sena; Mauryas, Guptas; Sultanate and Mughals. Then the Europeans: Portuguese, Dutch, French, English. Then Pakistani exploitation from 1947.

All of it ends in 1971.

The Grade 8 book goes past victory day. Freed from a Pakistani prison by international pressure, Bangabandhu lands in independent Bangladesh on 10 January 1972.

Then the war's spirit becomes work — freedom of language, culture and destiny, an end to exploitation.

Students farm, build and teach literacy across the country. Independence is a rebuilding, and everyone has a hand in it.

Grade 9 joins two names. The land's name grows — Banga to Bangladesh — across a thousand years.

And the man's name grows with it: Bangabandhu, Friend of Bengal, becomes a symbol of all oppressed peoples.

On 23 May 1973 he receives the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace — honoured as Bishwabandhu, Friend of the World.
