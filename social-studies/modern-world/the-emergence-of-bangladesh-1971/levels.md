# The emergence of Bangladesh, 1971 — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — The long journey of millennia to independence
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: List the arc's stages: janapadas, Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British rule, East Pakistan, independence 1971. Say what the 1947 partition made of East Pakistan in the book's words: a colony of the West. Name the leader of the final movement: Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.

## Sub lesson 2 — The long arc — janapada to 1971, told through economics
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tell the name's growth: Banga janapada becomes Bangal, Bangala, Bengal. Name the Europeans who came in order: Portuguese, Dutch, French and English. Say what the Grade 7 telling adds: each stage is also a story of exploitation, ending in 1971.

## Sub lesson 3 — The spirit, the homecoming, the rebuilding
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Say when Bangabandhu landed in independent Bangladesh: 10 January 1972. Name the war's spirit as the book gives it: freedom of language, culture and destiny; an end to exploitation. Say how students carried the spirit: farming, building and teaching literacy.

## Sub lesson 4 — From Banga to Bangladesh, from Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tell both growths: Banga to Bangladesh; Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu. Say what Bangabandhu received on 23 May 1973: the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace. Say who gave the 'Bishwabandhu' honour's context: the World Peace Council, in the Grade 9 book's telling.
