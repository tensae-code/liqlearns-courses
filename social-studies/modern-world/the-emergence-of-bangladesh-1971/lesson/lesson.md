# The emergence of Bangladesh, 1971

## Do it first

Bangladesh is young — born in 1971. But its books insist its story is thousands of years old. Before you read on, guess how a book joins a brand-new country to an ancient land. Hold your guess; the millennia are coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — The long journey of millennia to independence

Bangladesh's Grade 6 book closes with the whole arc in one sweep. From the ancient janapadas, Bengal passes through Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers; then British extraction and indigo; then the 1947 partition, which made East Pakistan a colony of the West; and finally Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's movement and independence in 1971. The book's claim is its title: independent Bangladesh emerges after a journey of millennia — 1971 is the end of a very long road, not a sudden beginning.

In your mind's eye: a long timeline ribbon unrolling from ancient Bengal janapadas through sultans and Mughals to a green-and-red flag rising in 1971. The student traces the ribbon and names three stops on the way.

## Sub lesson 2 — The long arc — janapada to 1971, told through economics

The Grade 7 book tells the same arc through its economic lens — and through the land's changing name: the Banga janapada becomes Bangal, Bangala, Bengal. The rulers pass: Deva, Chandra, Pala and Sena dynasties; Mauryas and Guptas; the Sultanate and the Mughals. Then come the Europeans — Portuguese, Dutch, French and English — and finally Pakistani exploitation from 1947. Every stage, in this telling, is also a story of who took Bengal's wealth — until 1971 ends the taking.

## Sub lesson 3 — The spirit, the homecoming, the rebuilding

The Grade 8 book carries the story past victory. Bangabandhu, freed from a Pakistani prison by international pressure, lands in independent Bangladesh on 10 January 1972. Then the book turns the war's spirit into work: freedom of language, culture and destiny, and an end to exploitation become students farming, building and teaching literacy across the country. Independence, in this telling, is not a finish line — it is a rebuilding that everyone, students included, has a hand in.

## Sub lesson 4 — From Banga to Bangladesh, from Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu

The Grade 9 book joins the land's name and the man's name. The land's name grows from Banga to Bangladesh across the millennia of the earlier lessons. And its people's friend — Bangabandhu, 'Friend of Bengal' — becomes, in this telling, a symbol of all oppressed peoples: on 23 May 1973 he receives the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace, and the book honours him as 'Bishwabandhu', Friend of the World. One name grows from a region to a nation; the other grows from a nation's friend to the world's.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 4 beats: The long journey of millennia to independence; The long arc — janapada to 1971, told through economics; The spirit, the homecoming, the rebuilding; From Banga to Bangladesh, from Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory: list the arc's stages: janapadas, Pala, Sena, Muslim and Mughal rulers, British rule, East Pakistan, independence 1971.
2. Write, from memory: say what the 1947 partition made of East Pakistan in the book's words: a colony of the West.
3. Write, from memory: name the leader of the final movement: Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
4. Write, from memory: tell the name's growth: Banga janapada becomes Bangal, Bangala, Bengal.
5. Write, from memory: name the Europeans who came in order: Portuguese, Dutch, French and English.
6. Write, from memory: say what the Grade 7 telling adds: each stage is also a story of exploitation, ending in 1971.
7. Write, from memory: say when Bangabandhu landed in independent Bangladesh: 10 January 1972.
8. Write, from memory: name the war's spirit as the book gives it: freedom of language, culture and destiny; an end to exploitation.
9. Write, from memory: say how students carried the spirit: farming, building and teaching literacy.
10. Write, from memory: tell both growths: Banga to Bangladesh; Bangabandhu to Bishwabandhu.
11. Write, from memory: say what Bangabandhu received on 23 May 1973: the Joliot-Curie Medal for Peace.
12. Write, from memory: say who gave the 'Bishwabandhu' honour's context: the World Peace Council, in the Grade 9 book's telling.
