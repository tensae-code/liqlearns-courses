# The Bolsheviks in power, 1917 to 1924

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From 1917 to 1924, the Bolsheviks seize power, win a civil war and build a one-party state. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — 2.1 Early consolidation of power, 1917–18

Trotsky signs away land at Brest-Litovsk to keep the promise of peace, the Constituent Assembly is dispersed and the Romanov family is murdered.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — 2.2 The Civil War, 1918–21

Reds against Whites, Greens and foreign intervention armies; war communism, requisitioning and famine follow, and the Reds' unity and control of the railways win through.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — 2.3 Moves towards totalitarianism

Within weeks the new rulers ban rival newspapers and create the Cheka; show trials and terror turn a revolutionary government into a one-party dictatorship.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — 2.4 Economic and social change, 1918–24

War communism collapses into the NEP retreat, while society is remade — women declared equal (1917), free compulsory education, legal abortion (1920) and Church land nationalised.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: 2.1 Early consolidation of power, 1917–18; 2.2 The Civil War, 1918–21; 2.3 Moves towards totalitarianism; 2.4 Economic and social change, 1918–24. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
