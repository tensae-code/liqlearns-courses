# Lesson plan — Lebanon — from Greater Lebanon to Taif

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Lebanon — from Greater Lebanon to Taif
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Cut from Syria in 1920, Lebanon wins independence by 1946, then survives civil wars and invasions to the Taif agreement of 1989.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lebanon — from Greater Lebanon to Taif** — France cut Lebanon from Syria and Gouraud proclaimed Greater Lebanon in 1920; after the 1926 constitution and the national government of Bishara al-Khoury and Riad al-Solh in 1943, France arrested the president and ministers, the people rose, and Lebanon won independence, completed on 31 December 1946; the lesson follows the civil wars of 1958 and 1975 — the Syrian army's entry, the Israeli invasions and the Sabra and Shatila massacre — to the Taif agreement of 1989.
