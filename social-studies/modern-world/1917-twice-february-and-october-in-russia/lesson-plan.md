# Lesson plan — 1917 twice — February and October in Russia

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** 1917 twice — February and October in Russia
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — the age of world wars unit; the Grade 10 book's Russian Revolution lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in one year, 1917, Russia revolted twice: February threw out the Czar, and October brought the Marxists to power — the world's first communist state, born out of the First World War.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the First World War lessons — the war is the furnace this revolution was forged in. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Czar's Russia and its secret opponents** — autocracy above, and below it liberals, Social Revolutionaries and Marxists organising in secret.
2. **February, then October** — the February Revolution ends the Czar; the October Revolution brings the Marxists to power and founds the first communist state.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The First World War — causes. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the Czar's Russia and the three opposition currents.
2. Tell February and October apart: who fell, who rose.
3. Name Karl Marx's followers and what they built.
4. Say why October mattered to the whole world.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep the two revolutions separate with two questions: February — who fell? (The Czar.) October — who rose? (The Marxists, the Bolsheviks.) Every other fact in the lesson answers one of those two.
