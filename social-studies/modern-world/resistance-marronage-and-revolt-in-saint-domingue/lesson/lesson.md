# Resistance, marronage and revolt in Saint-Domingue

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From everyday resistance and flight, through named risings and maroon communities, to Vodou gatherings banned again and again — the enslaved of Saint-Domingue resist, and the maroon who runs becomes a whole hidden counter-society. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — To resist and to maroon

Starting from albert mangonès' statue of the unknown maroon, pupils trace everyday resistance and flight — the maroon who runs becomes a whole hidden counter-society (Séance 1, Résister et marronner)

[Picture slot — a picture of Albert Mangonès' statue of the Unknown Maroon in Port-au-Prince — the student says what marronage was and why the statue honours it]

## Sub lesson 2 — From the masters' vision to the historians' analysis

The same revolt is read twice — in the masters' frightened gazettes and in historians' reconstructions — and the two stories do not match (Séance 2, De la vision des maîtres à l'analyse des historiens)

## Sub lesson 3 — Revolts and maroon societies

Named risings — quartier-morin 1697, maribaroux 1720, makandal's poison network in the 1750s — grow into maroon communities that negotiate, raid and survive (Séance 3, Révoltes et sociétés marronnes)

## Sub lesson 4 — Vodou and syncretism as resistance

Banned again and again by law from the code noir onward, vodou gatherings persist — the repeated bans themselves prove the faith was a meeting-place of resistance (Séance 4, Le vaudou et le syncrétisme, moyens et formes de la résistance)
