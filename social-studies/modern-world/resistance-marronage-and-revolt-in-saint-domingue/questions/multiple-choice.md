# Resistance, marronage and revolt in Saint-Domingue — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) To resist and to maroon  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) To resist and to maroon** — starting from Albert Mangonès' statue of the Unknown Maroon, pupils trace everyday resistance and flight — the maroon who runs becomes a whole hidden counter-society.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In the guide this part is —
a) Séance 1, Résister et marronner  b) A geography map exercise only  c) A maths drill
**Answer: a) Séance 1, Résister et marronner** — the guide's own heading for this part.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) From the masters' vision to the historians' analysis  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) From the masters' vision to the historians' analysis** — the same revolt is read twice — in the masters' frightened gazettes and in historians' reconstructions — and the two stories do not match.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of the lesson teaches —

**Answer: a) Revolts and maroon societies** — named risings — Quartier-Morin 1697, Maribaroux 1720, Makandal's poison network in the 1750s — grow into maroon communities that negotiate, raid and survive.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of the lesson teaches —
a) Vodou and syncretism as resistance  b) Something unrelated  c) Nothing at all
**Answer: a) Vodou and syncretism as resistance** — banned again and again by law from the Code Noir onward, Vodou gatherings persist — the repeated bans themselves prove the faith was a meeting-place of resistance.
