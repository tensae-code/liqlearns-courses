# Lesson plan — Resistance, marronage and revolt in Saint-Domingue

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Resistance, marronage and revolt in Saint-Domingue
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Haiti, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From everyday resistance and flight, through named risings and maroon communities, to Vodou gatherings banned again and again — the enslaved of Saint-Domingue resist, and the maroon who runs becomes a whole hidden counter-society.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **To resist and to maroon** — starting from Albert Mangonès' statue of the Unknown Maroon, pupils trace everyday resistance and flight — the maroon who runs becomes a whole hidden counter-society (Séance 1, Résister et marronner)
2. **From the masters' vision to the historians' analysis** — the same revolt is read twice — in the masters' frightened gazettes and in historians' reconstructions — and the two stories do not match (Séance 2, De la vision des maîtres à l'analyse des historiens)
3. **Revolts and maroon societies** — named risings — Quartier-Morin 1697, Maribaroux 1720, Makandal's poison network in the 1750s — grow into maroon communities that negotiate, raid and survive (Séance 3, Révoltes et sociétés marronnes)
4. **Vodou and syncretism as resistance** — banned again and again by law from the Code Noir onward, Vodou gatherings persist — the repeated bans themselves prove the faith was a meeting-place of resistance (Séance 4, Le vaudou et le syncrétisme, moyens et formes de la résistance)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
