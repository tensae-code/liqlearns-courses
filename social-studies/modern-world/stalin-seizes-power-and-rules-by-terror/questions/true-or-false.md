# Stalin seizes power and rules by terror — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Lenin dies leaving a Testament that warns against Stalin; Stalin poses as Lenin's loyal disciple, outmanoeuvres Trotsky and the party wings in turn, and ends the NEP.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.1 The struggle for power, 1924–29

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Russia and the Soviet Union, 1917–1941* is about 3.2 The use of terror in the 1930s.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.1 The struggle for power, 1924–29

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The NKVD works to arrest quotas, so people are seized for a scribble on Stalin's photograph or for nothing at all; purges, show trials and the gulag follow.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.2 The use of terror in the 1930s

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Russia and the Soviet Union, 1917–1941* is about 3.1 The struggle for power, 1924–29.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.2 The use of terror in the 1930s

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Socialist realism conscripts art, novels and music — 'Woman, get on the locomotive!' — as Stalin's 'engineers of souls' set out to manufacture a new kind of person.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.3 Propaganda and censorship

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History: Russia and the Soviet Union, 1917–1941* is about 3.1 The struggle for power, 1924–29.
**Answer: False** — it is about 3.3 Propaganda and censorship

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Posters, paintings, poems and songs rewrite the past itself, showing Stalin as Lenin's intimate friend and October's planner — roles that were not his.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in 3.4 The cult of Stalin
