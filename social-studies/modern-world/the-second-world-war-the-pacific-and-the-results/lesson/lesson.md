# The Second World War — the Pacific and the results

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — the pacific — sweep, check, and the bombs is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Pacific — sweep, check, and the bombs

**Japan's 1942 invasion took the Philippines, Malaysia, Indonesia and New Guinea.** The sweep stopped when the **American navy checked the advance, defeated Japan repeatedly and took many islands** — and from those islands the **American air force struck Japan** itself. Then the end: on **August 6 and 9, 1945**, the American air force dropped **atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki**. **Unable to withstand the attack, Japan's government accepted defeat, and the war ended in Asia.** The most destructive war in history closed with its most destructive weapon — a fact the results list now weighs.

## Sub lesson 2 — The results

The book's results list: **heavy loss of life and property**; **communism spread**; **America and Russia emerging as great powers** — the two victors who would face each other next; **the Cold War set off** between them; **states' borders changed**; the **anti-colonial struggle quickened** — peoples under colonial rule now pushed harder for freedom; and the **United Nations founded**, replacing the weak League with a stronger attempt at world peace. Compare this list with the First World War's results in the earlier lesson: again two powers rise and a world body is founded — but this time the two powers face each other, and the next lesson is their Cold War.
