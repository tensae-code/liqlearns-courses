# Colombia's independence — Morillo, Angostura and Gran Colombia — short answer

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Guía 2: The birth of a nation.
**Answer: The early acts still name Fernando VII while demanding criollo power; Pablo Morillo besieges Cartagena from 18 August 1815 in the Spanish reconquest; on 17 December 1819 the Congress of Angostura proclaims the Republic of Colombia, with Santander vice-president of Cundinamarca, Bolívar's union dream fails at the Congress of Panama, and bolivarianos and santanderistas split (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Colombia's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Ciencias Sociales — Primera Cartilla, Grado Quinto, Escuela Nueva* (Colombia), Guía 2: The birth of a nation, printed p. 21.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Módulo 2 / Guía 4: Why did we become independent?.
**Answer: The causes of New Granada's independence are built from sources — Captain Antonio Baraya's company guards the council houses as the movement opens, and a merchant lover of liberty asks whether Europe will let an old serpent devour the fairest part of the globe (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Colombia's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Ciencias Sociales — Grado 8, Postprimaria Rural (Sociales)* (Colombia), Módulo 2 / Guía 4: Why did we become independent?, printed p. 59.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: Unidad 4 / Tema 19: Formation of the Colombian State, 1819–1849.
**Answer: Nariño's authority is disowned and war declared on Santafé; Bolívar's arrival forces Viceroy Sámano to flee; Gran Colombia forms, Quito separates in May 1830, haciendas harden social relations, and the Guerra de los Supremos follows (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
