# Lesson plan — Greeks Under Ottoman Rule 1453 1821

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Greeks Under Ottoman Rule 1453 1821
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Greece, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** after 1453 the Ottomans complete the conquest of the Greek lands, ending the long Byzantine decline in Turkish and Latin lordship
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit B, ch. 1 The conquest of the Greek peninsula** — after 1453 the Ottomans complete the conquest of the Greek lands, ending the long Byzantine decline in Turkish and Latin lordship
2. **Unit B, ch. 2 The living conditions of the enslaved** — great Byzantine families flee west while the rayah peasants pay the head tax and carry the conqueror's burdens
3. **Unit B, ch. 3 The religious and political organisation of the Greeks** — the Ottomans abolish Byzantine rule but tolerate the Church; the Patriarch becomes the nation's keeper under the millet order
4. **Unit B, ch. 4 The Klephts and the Armatoloi** — mountain klephts rob and resist the Turks while armatoloi police the passes — the two faces of armed Greek life that folk song celebrates, Nikotsaras among them
5. **Unit B, ch. 5 Economic life** — an agrarian empire of wheat and barley slowly fills with Greek merchants, shipowners and guilds trading from Smyrna to Vienna
6. **Unit B, ch. 6 The Greeks of the diaspora communities and the Danubian Principalities** — emigrant Greeks build churches, schools and printing houses abroad, and Phanariots like Alexandros Mavrokordatos rise to rule Moldavia and Wallachia
7. **Unit B, ch. 7 The Teachers of the Nation** — cleric-teachers, many schooled in the West, multiply schools — Kosmas the Aetolian preaching from village to village — and wake a sleeping nation
8. **Unit B, ch. 9 The main revolutionary movements** — Maniots, Cretans and islanders rise again and again through three centuries, keeping alive the hope of liberation the sources record
9. **Unit B, ch. 10 The struggles of the Souliots** — the shepherd-warriors of Souli defy Ali Pasha for years, write him defiant letters, and end in exile and the dance of Zalongo's shadow
10. **Unit B exercise dossier (Greeks under Ottoman and Latin rule)** — term-matching, map place-names from Moschopolis to Smyrna, a research task on Kosmas the Aetolian and a timeline from Küçük Kaynarca to the Revolution
11. **Ch. 7.II Hellenism under Venetian and Ottoman rule** — Crete blooms under Venice while the mainland lives under the Porte — Greek culture survives in two empires until the age of revolutions
12. **§5 Hellenism from the mid-18th to the early 19th century** — merchants, schools, the diaspora and Rigas's and Korais's Enlightenment prepare a nation inside an empire
