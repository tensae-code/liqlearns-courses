## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theme 3: religious conflicts in Europe** — Charles V cannot crush the Protestant princes, so Augsburg lets each ruler choose his land's faith — "cuius regio, eius religio"; France's wars end with Henry IV's Edict of Nantes; and the Thirty Years War (1618–1648) burns Germany until Westphalia lets Protestants worship freely
2. **Theme 2: Europe's wars of religion** — Charles V cannot shoot Europe back into one faith — Augsburg settles for "whose land, his religion", France massacres its Huguenots on St Bartholomew's Day yet later shelters them at Nantes, and only after the Thirty Years' War does Westphalia (1648) let every state choose its own faith
