# Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — A new picture of nature

Between Copernicus and Newton, Europeans rebuilt their picture of nature. Copernicus set the Sun, not the Earth, at the centre; observers and experimenters tested the heavens and the falling stone alike; Newton bound it all in laws of motion and gravity.

The method was the revolution: observe, measure, experiment — and let the result overrule the old book.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reason turns to government

The Enlightenment took that method and aimed it at society. Locke argued government rests on the consent of the governed; Montesquieu, that power must be divided to stay safe; Rousseau, that society's contract can be remade; Voltaire mocked intolerance and defended free thought.

None of them ran a state. All of them armed the revolutions that close this course.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why this lesson is a hinge

Stand back and see the hinge: a new science of nature, then a new science of society, then — within a generation — revolutions in America and France built from these very arguments.

The Grade 11 book ends its long road here for a reason. Ideas, tested like experiments, become events.
