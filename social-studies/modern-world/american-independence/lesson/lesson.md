# American independence

## Do it first

Imagine a shopkeeper in a port town far across the sea from the king who rules her. A new tax arrives on every chest of tea, every legal paper, every newspaper — voted by a parliament she cannot vote for, sitting in a city she will never see. Her neighbours dump the tea into the harbour. This lesson is what happened next.

## Sub lesson 1 — Taxed without a voice

Britain's thirteen colonies along America's Atlantic coast had grown prosperous — and governed much of their own daily life through their own assemblies. After expensive wars, Britain looked to the colonies for revenue and laid tax upon tax on them. The colonists' answer became a slogan of the age: no taxation without representation. They paid, but they had never voted for a single member of the Parliament that taxed them.

Protest sharpened into defiance — most famously when colonists, some disguised, boarded ships in Boston harbour and threw the taxed tea into the water. Britain answered with punishment; the colonies answered by meeting together. The argument about taxes had become an argument about who had the right to rule.

## Sub lesson 2 — 1776 — a new country declares itself

In 1776 the colonies took the step words had been walking toward: they declared independence. The Declaration of Independence, drafted chiefly by Thomas Jefferson, announced that the thirteen colonies were now free and independent states, and grounded the break in the claim that governments rule only by the consent of the governed — a claim aimed at every throne in Europe, not only Britain's.
[Picture 23103 — map of the thirteen colonies]

Declaring was the easy half. Britain sent its army and navy, among the strongest in the world, against farmers and tradesmen turned soldiers. The colonists endured early defeats, held on, and won the help that changed the war's balance: France, Britain's old rival, joined them. With French arms, money and sea power beside them, the Americans forced Britain to accept the fact the Declaration had announced — a new republic stood on the map.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: a tax with no say; a declaration in 1776; a war won with French help. Then say the claim the Declaration made about where a government's right to rule comes from.
