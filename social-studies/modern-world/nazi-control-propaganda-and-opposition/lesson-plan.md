# Lesson plan — Nazi control, propaganda and opposition

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Nazi control, propaganda and opposition
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (United Kingdom, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Nazi regime controls Germany through a police state, propaganda and the arts — and some Germans resist.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **8.1 The development of the Nazi police state** — SS, Gestapo and SD watch the whole population and concentration camps hold the regime's enemies; fear, not consent alone, keeps the dictatorship safe
2. **8.2 The Nazi control of the legal system** — Judges join the Nazi lawyers' league, the People's Court tries treason without appeal, and law becomes whatever serves the regime
3. **8.3 Nazi policies towards the Church** — A Concordat with the Vatican (1933) buys quiet while the regime builds its own Reich Church; pastors who resist, like Niemöller, end in camps
4. **8.4 Goebbels and the Ministry of Propaganda** — Goebbels' ministry and Reich Chamber of Culture licence every journalist, actor and musician; cheap radios, rallies and film carry one message into every home
5. **8.5 The Nazi control of the Arts** — Jazz and modern art are banned as degenerate while folk music, classical giants and monumental building glorify the regime — culture itself becomes propaganda
6. **8.6 The Nazi control of sport** — Sport is Nazified through clubs and the 1936 Berlin Olympics are staged as a showcase of Aryan Germany for the world's cameras
7. **9.1 The extent of support for the Nazi regime** — Many Germans conform for jobs, order and pride restored; the book weighs real popularity against fear to judge how far support was freely given
8. **9.2 Opposition from young people** — Edelweiss Pirates and swing gangs grow their hair, play banned music and beat up Hitler Youth patrols — small refusals that the regime answers with prison and, later, the gallows
9. **9.3 Opposition from the Churches** — Niemöller's Confessional Church and Bishop Galen's sermons defend faith against Nazi doctrine; Niemöller spends 1937–45 in prison and camps for preaching obedience to God over man
