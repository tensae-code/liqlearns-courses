## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Teema 2 background: 420,000 people leave home** — after the Winter War Finland cedes about a tenth of its land and 420,000 Karelians and others must rebuild their lives elsewhere; the 1940 quick-settlement law shares out land, but housing is wretched and friction flares over dialect, customs and Orthodox faith — alongside warm welcomes that Finland is judged to have managed well
2. **Tehtävä 1 (yläkoulu): letter from Säkylä, 26 Feb 1941** — a Karelian front-line soldier writes that locals in his new home village abuse the evacuees with an ethnic slur, and asks whether law and decency no longer rule in the land he fought for
3. **Tehtävä 2 (yläkoulu): letter from Rovaniemi, 27 Feb 1941** — a poor evacuee who has hunted three months for a home asks who the much-praised "gift houses" donated from Sweden are really for, since even two poor families together cannot afford one
4. **Tehtävä 3 (yläkoulu): letter from Suonenjoki, 20 Sep 1941** — a poor evacuee family in one tiny stove-room watches its rent climb from under 100 to 150 marks as the rich landlord passes his cession tax onto them, with no rent board to appeal to
5. **Tehtävä 4 (lukio): letter from Oulu, 21 Apr 1941** — a reservist corporal from Pitkäranta, who lost everything he owned, is refused an advance on his 34,000-mark compensation claim and cannot clothe his family of three on his small wage
6. **Tehtävä 5 (lukio): letters from Säkylä (1941) and Reposaari, 17 Oct 1944, with the Laukko crofter eviction of 1906–07 as comparison** — a family in Kangasala is bundled into a kitchen corner while spare rooms stand empty, officials give no help, and pupils compare the evacuees' lot with the crofter families evicted into the frost at Laukko a generation earlier
7. **Tehtävä 6 (lukio): a 1944 letter and Jahvetti's office's reply (Kauhava letter)** — an evacuee wife pleads her husband's case for leave from the front; the office's reply refuses, and pupils must find the 1944 battle situation behind the refusal and weigh the women's experience of evacuation
8. **introduction: cession and resettlement** — Finland cedes a tenth of its land; 420,000 people leave home, the 1940 rapid-resettlement law buys land for them, yet housing stays desperate and friction follows
9. **Task 1, Säkylä letter 1941** — a Karelian front-line veteran writes that locals call Karelians 'ryssä' — he fought for the home he no longer has
10. **Task 2, Rovaniemi letter 1941** — a poor evacuee who has hunted a home for three months asks who the fairy-tale-expensive Swedish 'gift houses' are really for
11. **Task 3, Suonenjoki letter 1941** — a rich landlord raises a poor evacuee family's rent for one tiny stove-room, blaming his cession tax; no rent board exists to appeal to
12. **Task 4, Oulu letter 1941** — a reservist sergeant from Pitkäranta, everything left behind, is refused a 3,000 mk advance on his 34,000 mk compensation claim
13. **Task 5, Säkylä repeated and Reposaari 1944 letters** — a family is dumped into a kitchen corner while rooms stand empty; the writer recalls the 1906–07 Laukko crofter evictions as the same wound
14. **Task 6, 1944 letter and reply** — an evacuee wife argues her husband should get leave; the reply refuses as the 1944 offensive burns — women's war inside the resettlement story
15. **Task 3, the evacuee photographs** — evacuees sit on a barge deck remembering home (June 1944) and a father and daughter bid Suistamo church farewell (August 1944) — pupils split the experience by men, women and children
