# Finland's Karelian evacuees, 1939–1945

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: After the Winter War Finland cedes about a tenth of its land and 420,000 Karelians and others must rebuild their lives elsewhere; the 1940 quick-settlement law shares out land, but housing is wretched and friction flares over dialect, customs and Orthodox faith — alongside warm welcomes that Finland is judged to have managed well As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tehtävä 1 (yläkoulu): letter from Säkylä, 26 Feb 1941

A Karelian front-line soldier writes that locals in his new home village abuse the evacuees with an ethnic slur, and asks whether law and decency no longer rule in the land he fought for.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tehtävä 2 (yläkoulu): letter from Rovaniemi, 27 Feb 1941

A poor evacuee who has hunted three months for a home asks who the much-praised "gift houses" donated from Sweden are really for, since even two poor families together cannot afford one.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — Tehtävä 3 (yläkoulu): letter from Suonenjoki, 20 Sep 1941

A poor evacuee family in one tiny stove-room watches its rent climb from under 100 to 150 marks as the rich landlord passes his cession tax onto them, with no rent board to appeal to.

## Sub lesson 5 — Tehtävä 4 (lukio): letter from Oulu, 21 Apr 1941

A reservist corporal from Pitkäranta, who lost everything he owned, is refused an advance on his 34,000-mark compensation claim and cannot clothe his family of three on his small wage.
