# Lesson plan — Micronesia — the Japanese mandate years

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Micronesia — the Japanese mandate years
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Micronesia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Japan seized Germany's islands in 1914 and ruled them as a League of Nations Mandate, building towns, industries and a cash economy that made islanders a minority in their own land — Okinawan and Japanese immigrants poured in, island children passed through kogakko schools toward civil-service and trade jobs, exports such as katsuobushi and phosphate tied the islands to the Japanese Empire, and chiefs found no real place in the system, sitting in 'yes-yes councils' that only agreed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told in order, part by part, as Micronesia's books tell it. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Japan seizes the islands and builds a mandate** — say that Japan seized Germany's islands in 1914, ruled them as a League of Nations Mandate, and built towns, industries and a cash economy that made islanders a minority in their own land.
2. **Schools, immigrants and island work** — say that Okinawan and Japanese immigrants poured in, island children passed through kogakko schools toward civil-service and trade jobs, and exports such as katsuobushi and phosphate tied the islands to the Japanese Empire.
3. **Chiefs with no place: the yes-yes councils — and Kapingamarangi's Japanese time** — say that under Japanese rule chiefs found no real place in the system and sat in 'yes-yes councils' that only agreed, and that Kapingamarangi's Japanese time ran 1914–1945.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
