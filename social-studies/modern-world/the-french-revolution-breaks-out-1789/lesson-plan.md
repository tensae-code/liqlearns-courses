# Lesson plan — 1789 — the French Revolution breaks out

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** 1789 — the French Revolution breaks out
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — the age of revolutions unit; the Grade 11 book's French Revolution opening lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** France's old regime — a society of three estates, an empty treasury and dear bread — broke in 1789: the Estates-General, the Tennis Court Oath, the fall of the Bastille, and the Declaration of the Rights of Man.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Enlightenment ideas the learner has met — that government should answer to reason and to the people. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The old regime — three estates, empty coffers, dear bread** — a society stacked in three orders, the Third carrying the taxes, a king in debt and a people hungry.
2. **1789 — the oath, the Bastille, the rights of man** — the Third Estate refuses to part, Paris storms the royal fortress, and the Assembly declares a new order of rights.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: American independence — the first of the age's revolutions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the old regime: the three estates and who carried the taxes.
2. Tell why the king called the Estates-General in 1789.
3. Retell the Tennis Court Oath and 14 July 1789.
4. Say what the Declaration of the Rights of Man announced.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep the year's four beats in order: the king calls the Estates-General for money; the Third Estate swears not to part; Paris takes the Bastille; the Assembly declares the rights of man. Every detail hangs on one of those four hooks.
