## Sub lesson 1 — Why the break came

The **Reformation** was the movement to **separate from the Roman Catholic Church**. Its driver was the **popes' and clergy's unlimited influence over political, economic and religious matters** — one church reaching into every part of life. Attempts at protest had stirred earlier, in **14th-century England and Bohemia**, but the break was **formally founded in 1517** by a German: **Martin Luther**, a **Catholic monk and professor of theology at Wittenberg University**. What Luther denounced was the money the church collected from individuals as '**indulgence**' — payment **for the pardon of sins**. Against it he preached that people are **freed from sin by good deeds, not by giving money**.

## Sub lesson 2 — October 31, 1517 — and the spread

On **October 31, 1517**, Luther posted his **95 protest theses on the Wittenberg church door** — the Reformation's birthday. Efforts to **silence him** and return him to Catholicism **failed**; he won **many followers**, and the new faith spread country by country. In **France**, **John Calvin's Calvinist** Protestantism grew, whose followers are called the **Huguenots**. In **England**, the **Anglican Church** formed. In **Switzerland**, the **Swiss Reformed Church**. The map of European Christianity was redrawn — and the book adds the cost plainly: in many European countries the Reformation **brought religious war**.
