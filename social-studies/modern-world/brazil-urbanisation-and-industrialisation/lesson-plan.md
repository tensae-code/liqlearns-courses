# Lesson plan — Brazil — urbanisation and industrialisation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Brazil — urbanisation and industrialisation
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Brazil, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From the first cities in history to Brazil's explosive 20th-century urban growth, industry pulls country people into cities that grow faster than homes, water and work can follow.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Cities in history** — The book begins far back, with the first cities in history — the places where people first gathered in large numbers to live and work together. A city is not just a big village: it is a new way of living, and it has a history of its own.
2. **Brazil's cities explode** — Then the story lands in Brazil. In the 20th century, industry pulled country people into Brazil's cities in enormous numbers. The cities grew explosively — faster than homes, water and work could follow. People arrived seeking the factory's wage, and the city struggled to hold them.
3. **Growth faster than the city can follow** — That gap — between the people arriving and the homes, water and work ready for them — is the heart of the lesson. Urbanisation and industrialisation arrive together, but they do not arrive at the same speed, and ordinary families live inside that gap.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Effects of industrialisation — cities, classes, empires; Capitalism — private ownership, factories, new classes. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say where the book begins the story of cities, and what makes a city a new way of living.
2. Say what pulled country people into Brazil's cities in the 20th century.
3. Say what Brazil's explosive urban growth outran — homes, water and work.
