# Brazil — urbanisation and industrialisation

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From the first cities in history to Brazil's explosive 20th-century urban growth, industry pulls country people into cities that grow faster than homes, water and work can follow. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cities in history

The book begins far back, with the first cities in history — the places where people first gathered in large numbers to live and work together. A city is not just a big village: it is a new way of living, and it has a history of its own.

## Sub lesson 2 — Brazil's cities explode

Then the story lands in Brazil. In the 20th century, industry pulled country people into Brazil's cities in enormous numbers. The cities grew explosively — faster than homes, water and work could follow. People arrived seeking the factory's wage, and the city struggled to hold them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Growth faster than the city can follow

That gap — between the people arriving and the homes, water and work ready for them — is the heart of the lesson. Urbanisation and industrialisation arrive together, but they do not arrive at the same speed, and ordinary families live inside that gap.
