# Realism — painting ordinary life

## Do it first

Before you read on, picture the scene this lesson is about, from its title alone: Realism — painting ordinary life. What do you expect to happen, and what do you expect to change? Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Backs turned on heroes and gods

The realists turn their backs on heroes and gods.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ordinary life painted

They paint stone-breakers, funerals in provincial towns and peasants at work.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 3 — A scandal and a claim

It is a scandal at the salons — and a claim that ordinary life is worthy of the biggest canvases.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: The realists turn their backs on heroes and gods. Then: They paint stone-breakers, funerals in provincial towns and peasants at work. And finally: It is a scandal at the salons — and a claim that ordinary life is worthy of the biggest canvases. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
