# Rococo — the aristocracy's playful art

## Do it first

Before you read on, picture the scene this lesson is about, from its title alone: Rococo — the aristocracy's playful art. What do you expect to happen, and what do you expect to change? Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Glory traded for pleasure

After the Sun King's death the aristocracy trades glory for pleasure.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 2 — Salons, shells and scrolls

Rooms shrink to salons, and curves turn to shells and scrolls.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 3 — A party on the eve of revolution

Painting fills with silk, flirtation and garden parties — on the eve of a revolution nobody at the party expects.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: After the Sun King's death the aristocracy trades glory for pleasure. Then: Rooms shrink to salons, and curves turn to shells and scrolls. And finally: Painting fills with silk, flirtation and garden parties — on the eve of a revolution nobody at the party expects. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
