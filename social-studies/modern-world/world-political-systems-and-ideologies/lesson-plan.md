# Lesson plan — World political systems and ideologies

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** World political systems and ideologies
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Papua New Guinea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Power, authority and legitimacy defined and sorted — monarchs, feudal units and every system by which a society's collective decisions get made, enforced and reproduced
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **World political systems and ideologies** — power, authority and legitimacy defined and sorted — monarchs, feudal units and every system by which a society's collective decisions get made, enforced and reproduced
2. **Ideology and hegemony** — doctrine against domination — political and epistemological ideologies sorted, and hegemony named as one group's authority over a society or one nation's over others, and how it works on the less powerful

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
