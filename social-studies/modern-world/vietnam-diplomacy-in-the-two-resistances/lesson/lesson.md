## Do it first

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Bài 13 Diplomacy in the anti-French resistance, 1945–1954: the 25-11-1945 directive defines the diplomatic art as making the country "few enemies and many allied friends"; the infant DRV petitions the UN and the great powers, conciliates the Chinese occupation while fighting France in the South, signs the 1946 Preliminary Agreement and Modus Vivendi to buy time, keeps a peace offer open through the war, wins recognition and diplomatic relations from China, the USSR and the socialist countries in 1950, helps found the Việt–Miên–Lào People's Alliance (1951), and at Geneva (May–21-7-1954) turns Điện Biên Phủ into a signed international recognition of Indochina's basic national rights — every military phase has its paper twin, and the war is finally ended at a conference table in Switzerland. Bài 13 Diplomacy in the anti-American resistance, 1954–1975: Hanoi demands the Geneva elections through the International Control Commission, balances USSR and China while mobilising socialist aid, fights and talks at once — publishing the evidence of America's unjust war, winning world opinion, campaigning for recognition of the Liberation Front — until at Paris the four-party fight over the very shape of the table (rectangle, divided circle, or one big round table) ends with the big round table and the 27-1-1973 Agreement committing the US to respect Vietnam's independence, sovereignty, unity and territorial integrity — the last battle of the war is over furniture, because the table's shape is the war's verdict in miniature.
