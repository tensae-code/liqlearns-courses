# Vietnam Diplomacy In The Two Resistances — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bài 13 Diplomacy in the anti-French resistance, 1945–1954: the 25-11-1945 directive defines the diplomatic art as making the country "few enemies and many allied friends"; the infant DRV petitions the UN and the great powers, conciliates the Chinese occupation while fighting France in the South, signs the 1946 Preliminary Agreement and Modus Vivendi to buy time, keeps a peace offer open through the war, wins recognition and diplomatic relations from China, the USSR and the socialist countries in 1950, helps found the Việt–Miên–Lào People's Alliance (1951), and at Geneva (May–21-7-1954) turns Điện Biên Phủ into a signed international recognition of Indochina's basic national rights
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — every military phase has its paper twin, and the war is finally ended at a conference table in Switzerland

## Sub lesson 2 — Bài 13 Diplomacy in the anti-American resistance, 1954–1975: Hanoi demands the Geneva elections through the International Control Commission, balances USSR and China while mobilising socialist aid, fights and talks at once — publishing the evidence of America's unjust war, winning world opinion, campaigning for recognition of the Liberation Front — until at Paris the four-party fight over the very shape of the table (rectangle, divided circle, or one big round table) ends with the big round table and the 27-1-1973 Agreement committing the US to respect Vietnam's independence, sovereignty, unity and territorial integrity
