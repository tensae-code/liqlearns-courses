# Suriname — immigration makes a plural society

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From a plantation colony in 1651 the population is remade group by group — including Portuguese Jews arriving to empty promises of houses, land and tools — until Suriname holds many peoples As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Onze samenleving verandert

From a plantation colony in 1651 the population is remade group by group — including Portuguese Jews arriving to empty promises of houses, land and tools — until Suriname holds many peoples (Onze samenleving verandert)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Onze samenleving verandert — From a plantation colony in 1651 the population is remade group by group — including Portuguese Jews arriving to empty promises of houses, land and tools — until Suriname holds many peoples; Hoe wij hier ook samenkwamen — Taking its title from the anthem, the lesson recounts how each population group came to Suriname, building one multicultural society; Ons land, een smeltkroes van culturen — Suriname as melting pot: cultural exchange, respect and national unity across the groups, voiced by poet Robin Ravales (Dobru). Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
