# Lesson plan — Vietnam Ho Chi Minh Life Story

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Ho Chi Minh Life Story
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a boy from Kim Liên leaves in 1911 to find a way to save the country, finds Lenin's theses in 1920, founds the Communist Party in 1930 and returns in 1941 to lead independence
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 18, story of Nguyễn Ái Quốc – Hồ Chí Minh** — a boy from Kim Liên leaves in 1911 to find a way to save the country, finds Lenin's theses in 1920, founds the Communist Party in 1930 and returns in 1941 to lead independence
2. **Bài 25, Nguyễn Tất Thành leaves Bến Nhà Rồng, 1911** — unlike elders who looked East, the young man sails West as kitchen hand Văn Ba on the Latouche-Tréville on 5 June 1911 to learn how France and the West work
3. **Bài 4, story box: the love of Tân Trào's peoples for Bác Hồ** — at the Tân Trào base the Tày, Nùng, Dao and Cao Lan villagers shelter the revolution in secret and call the simply-dressed Hồ Chí Minh "Ông Ké", the kind old grandfather
4. **Bài 14, story of Hồ Chí Minh and the Declaration of Independence** — he leaves Tân Trào, types the Declaration draft himself at 48 Hàng Ngang street, takes colleagues' comments, and on 2 September 1945 reads it at Ba Đình square, proclaiming the Democratic Republic of Vietnam
5. **Bài 19, Nguyễn Tất Thành takes the third road, 1911–1917** — Nguyễn Tất Thành, born 19 May 1890 into a patriotic scholar's family at Kim Liên, admires the older patriots but will not follow either of their roads; on 5 June 1911, at Nhà Rồng wharf, he signs on as a kitchen hand on a French merchantman and sails west through Europe, Africa and America; in 1917 he settles in France, works at many trades, throws himself into the Association of Patriotic Vietnamese and the French workers' movement — and the victory of the Russian October Revolution strikes deep into his thinking; these years, the book concludes, are the condition for his finding the right road of national salvation
6. **Bài 6, Nguyễn Ái Quốc's road, 1919–1930** — in France he joins the Socialist Party (1919), signs the Demands of the Annamese People at Versailles (June 1919), and in July 1920 reads Lenin's Theses — the book quotes Chế Lan Viên's poem of his tears of joy; at Tours (December 1920) he co-founds the French Communist Party, then builds the Union of Colonies (1921) and the paper Le Paria (1922); in the USSR he sits on the Peasant International Council (1923) and the Comintern's Fifth Congress (1924); in Canton he trains 75 young revolutionaries — Lê Hồng Phong, Trần Phú, Phạm Văn Đồng among them — and writes Đường Kách mệnh (1927)
7. **Bài 12 Nguyễn Ái Quốc's foreign apprenticeship, 1911–1930: in France he meets Europe's political and cultural activists, co-founds the French Communist Party (1920) and the Union of Colonial Peoples (1921), and as head of the Indochina research board ties communists to colonial peoples; in the USSR (from June 1923) he argues the colonial question at the Comintern's own forums, and at Guangzhou (from late 1924) trains Vietnam's patriotic youth while preaching that revolution needs international forces united — founding the League of Oppressed Peoples of Asia in July 1925** — before he leads a revolution at home, he spends two decades building its foreign support network
8. **Bài 15 What made Hồ Chí Minh: a civilisation of patriotism, solidarity and humanity forged over thousands of years, and a nation stripped of independence from the mid-1800s (his own indictment: Indochina exploited, its people poisoned with alcohol and opium, kept in ignorance); a homeland — Kim Liên, Nghệ An — of harsh land, studious tradition and uprisings from Mai Thúc Loan to Cần Vương, with the Vinh–Bến Thủy industrial centre nearby; and a family — Phó bảng Nguyễn Sinh Sắc, an upright Confucian scholar, and Hoàng Thị Loan, steeped in folk culture — whose upbringing through a hard childhood formed his character and sympathy for working people** — the biography is taught as geology — country, homeland and family as three strata under one life
9. **Bài 15 The life in outline, 1890–1969: born Nguyễn Sinh Cung at Làng Sen (19-5-1890), schooled at Huế and Quy Nhơn, in the 1908 anti-tax movement, teaching at Dục Thanh (1910), he sails from Nhà Rồng wharf as Văn Ba on 5-6-1911; thirty years abroad — the 1919 Versailles demands signed Nguyễn Ái Quốc, Lenin's theses (July 1920), the French Communist Party's founding (Dec 1920), the Revolutionary Youth League at Guangzhou (1925), the Party unified at Kowloon (early 1930) — end with his return on 28-1-1941; then Pác Bó, Việt Minh, the name Hồ Chí Minh (Aug 1942), the August Revolution and the Declaration of Independence (2-9-1945), two resistances led to 1969, and his death at Hanoi on 2-9-1969** — the school timeline runs the whole national epic through one man's itinerary
10. **Bài 16 Finding the road: from 5-6-1911 Nguyễn Tất Thành crosses continents, works, studies and grasps colonialism's essence and the world's bourgeois revolutions; Lenin's theses in July 1920 confirm the road — national liberation bound to class liberation, independence to socialism — and at Tours (Dec 1920) he votes for the Third International, becoming a founder of the French Communist Party and Vietnam's first communist; his own summary: at first it was patriotism, not yet communism, that led him to Lenin** — the country's future line is chosen by one reader in Paris recognising his own question in Lenin's theses
11. **Bài 16 Leading the two resistances, 1945–1969: the helmsman of his own image steers the infant republic through famine and illiteracy campaigns, the 1946 general election, the conciliation of China and the two 1946 agreements, then issues the 19-12-1946 Appeal; in the nationwide resistance he sets the line, chairs the 1951 Congress, works the diplomatic front and follows campaigns at the front (Đông Khê, 1950); against America he chairs the 1959 conference that reopens the Southern revolution, the 1960 Congress of building socialism and unification, and in 1965–68 his Tết poems serve as fighting orders while he receives American anti-war intellectuals (1967) and holds the independent line between Moscow and Beijing** — one biography is made to carry the statecraft of twenty-four years of war
