# Lesson plan — Decolonisation of Asia and the Commonwealth

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Decolonisation of Asia and the Commonwealth
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Papua New Guinea, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** 1945–1960 the empires let go, each colony its own way — peaceful and orderly in some, revolution in Indonesia and India, long civil war in French Indo-China — while the new UN and the Cold War pull the newborn states in opposite directions
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Foreign influence on governance in Asia and Commonwealth countries** — 1945–1960 the empires let go, each colony its own way — peaceful and orderly in some, revolution in Indonesia and India, long civil war in French Indo-China — while the new UN and the Cold War pull the newborn states in opposite directions
2. **Decolonisation and Independence** — WWII breaks the empires; students map Africa, Asia and the Pacific with independence dates, interrogate USA and UN involvement, and build five case studies — colonisation, the strategy that won freedom, the key people, and progress since
3. **Factors involved in systems of power and authority globally** — why the empires fell after 1945 — the Depression starved the colonies, Japan conquered Europe's Asia and broke its prestige, the exhausted Europeans could no longer afford empire, and nationalism rose like a tide
