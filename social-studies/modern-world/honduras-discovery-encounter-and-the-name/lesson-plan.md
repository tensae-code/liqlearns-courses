# Lesson plan — Honduras Discovery Encounter And The Name

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Honduras Discovery Encounter And The Name
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Honduras, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two unlike worlds meet — armour, horses, gunpowder and monarchy against maize, nature-linked spirituality and hunting weapons; storm-tossed Spaniards thank God they escaped "estas Honduras", Guaymuras and Higueras give way to the name Honduras
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lección 22: encuentro de dos culturas y el nombre Honduras** — two unlike worlds meet — armour, horses, gunpowder and monarchy against maize, nature-linked spirituality and hunting weapons; storm-tossed Spaniards thank God they escaped "estas Honduras", Guaymuras and Higueras give way to the name Honduras
2. **Lección 23: consecuencias del descubrimiento** — Europe gains gold, silver, tobacco and empires and the Atlantic becomes the highway; America gains colonists, mestizaje, slave sales, mine extraction, an imposed culture, language and religion, unknown diseases and a falling population
3. **the "encounter of cultures" debate** — 1492 was chance, not plan: Columbus miscalculated, and the book insists a populated continent cannot be "discovered" — two worlds met, with Galeano's "Las venas abiertas" assigned as the radiography of what followed
