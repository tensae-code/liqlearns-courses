# Iceland's old society

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: In old Iceland most people own nothing: tenant farmers work other men's land, servants are tied to farms by the vistarband, and in 1703 a census counts them all. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tenant farmers, servants and the vistarband

in old Iceland most people own nothing; the vistarband ties servants to farms (Gamla samfélagið, student book pp. 4–9)

In your mind's eye: a tenant farm in old Iceland, with the farmer's family and servants, and a census writer at the door. Say who owns the farm and who is counted.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1703 census counts them all

the census counts the tenant farmers and servants of old Iceland — a whole people listed farm by farm (Gamla samfélagið, student book pp. 4–9)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the lesson as its beats: Tenant farmers, servants and the vistarband — in old Iceland most people own nothing; the vistarband ties servants to farms (Gamla samfélagið, student book pp. 4–9); The 1703 census counts them all — the census counts the tenant farmers and servants of old Iceland — a whole people listed farm by farm (Gamla samfélagið, student book pp. 4–9). Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
