# Lesson plan — Vietnam Border Wars And Gac Ma

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Border Wars And Gac Ma
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on the southwestern border the Pol Pot government occupies Phú Quốc and Thổ Chu islands and massacres civilians in An Giang and Tây Ninh; Vietnam protests and offers peaceful negotiation, Pol Pot refuses and cuts relations, and in December 1978 sends 19 infantry divisions with artillery and tanks against Tây Ninh — from 23 December 1978 Vietnam's general counteroffensive defeats the invasion and opens the way for the Cambodian revolution; on the northern border, after China ends its aid in 1978, some 600,000 Chinese troops attack along the whole frontier on 17 February 1979 — Lạng Sơn, Cao Bằng and Lào Cai fight fiercely, China declares withdrawal on 5 March, but tension lasts to the late 1980s, above all at Vị Xuyên, where the soldiers' oath "live clinging to the rock, die turned to stone" is carved at Hill 468; at sea Vietnam administers Bạch Long Vĩ, Hoàng Sa and Trường Sa districts and protests at the United Nations, and in March 1988, at Gạc Ma in the Trường Sa archipelago, 64 Vietnamese naval soldiers die defending the reefs against a Chinese attack
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 20, defending the borders and the sea, 1975–1989** — on the southwestern border the Pol Pot government occupies Phú Quốc and Thổ Chu islands and massacres civilians in An Giang and Tây Ninh; Vietnam protests and offers peaceful negotiation, Pol Pot refuses and cuts relations, and in December 1978 sends 19 infantry divisions with artillery and tanks against Tây Ninh — from 23 December 1978 Vietnam's general counteroffensive defeats the invasion and opens the way for the Cambodian revolution; on the northern border, after China ends its aid in 1978, some 600,000 Chinese troops attack along the whole frontier on 17 February 1979 — Lạng Sơn, Cao Bằng and Lào Cai fight fiercely, China declares withdrawal on 5 March, but tension lasts to the late 1980s, above all at Vị Xuyên, where the soldiers' oath "live clinging to the rock, die turned to stone" is carved at Hill 468; at sea Vietnam administers Bạch Long Vĩ, Hoàng Sa and Trường Sa districts and protests at the United Nations, and in March 1988, at Gạc Ma in the Trường Sa archipelago, 64 Vietnamese naval soldiers die defending the reefs against a Chinese attack
2. **Bài 13 Defending the islands by arms: the timeline of the struggle — French garrisons demanding the Republic of China's withdrawal in 1946–47, the January 1974 loss of Hoàng Sa to China's attack, and March 1988, when Vietnamese naval soldiers fall defending Gạc Ma, Cô Lin and Len Đao** — sovereignty written in flotilla logs ends up defended in blood on three reefs in 1988
3. **Bài 9 The Southwest border war: Pol Pot's forces raid from May 1975 — Phú Quốc and Thổ Chu seized, An Giang attacked (30-4-1977), over 3,000 civilians massacred at Ba Chúc in twelve days of April 1978 — and Vietnam answers in two stages, diplomacy alongside defence, then a general counter-offensive; at the Cambodian National United Front's request, Vietnamese volunteers join Cambodia's army and people to topple Pol Pot, and Phnom Penh is liberated on 7-1-1979** — the neighbour Vietnam helped liberate turns its guns on Vietnamese villages, and the war ends in the neighbour's own capital
4. **Bài 9 The Northern border war: relations sour from mid-1975 as China cuts aid and provokes along the frontier; on the morning of 17-2-1979 hundreds of thousands of Chinese troops attack along more than 1,000 km from Móng Cái to Phong Thổ, and six border provinces' army and people defeat the "quick attack, quick victory" aim at Lạng Sơn, Cao Bằng and Lào Cai; China declares withdrawal from 5-3-1979, yet the frontier stays tense to late 1989 — at Vị Xuyên alone some 5,000 soldiers fall in 1984–1989** — the ally of the resistance years becomes the invader of 1979, and the border war's tail lasts a full decade
