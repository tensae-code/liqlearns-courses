**Answer: on the southwestern border the Pol Pot government occupies Phú Quốc and Thổ Chu islands and massacres civilians in An Giang and Tây Ninh; Vietnam protests and offers peaceful negotiation, Pol Pot refuses and cuts relations, and in December 1978 sends 19 infantry divisions with artillery and tanks against Tây Ninh — from 23 December 1978 Vietnam's general counteroffensive defeats the invasion and opens the way for the Cambodian revolution; on the northern border, after China ends its aid in 1978, some 600,000 Chinese troops attack along the whole frontier on 17 February 1979 — Lạng Sơn, Cao Bằng and Lào Cai fight fiercely, China declares withdrawal on 5 March, but tension lasts to the late 1980s, above all at Vị Xuyên, where the soldiers' oath "live clinging to the rock, die turned to stone" is carved at Hill 468; at sea Vietnam administers Bạch Long Vĩ, Hoàng Sa and Trường Sa districts and protests at the United Nations, and in March 1988, at Gạc Ma in the Trường Sa archipelago, 64 Vietnamese naval soldiers die defending the reefs against a Chinese attack (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
