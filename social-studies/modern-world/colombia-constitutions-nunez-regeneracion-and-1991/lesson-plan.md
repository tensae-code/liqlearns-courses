# Lesson plan — Colombia's constitutions — Núñez, the Regeneración and 1991

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Colombia's constitutions — Núñez, the Regeneración and 1991
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Colombia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Colombia rebuilds its state on paper again and again — Rionegro 1863, Núñez and 1886, the 1936 reform, and the 1991 charter born of a student-led Constituent Assembly
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Guía 5: National identity / Núñez and the constitutions** — Rafael Núñez's project of change is debated from his own discourse, the liberal Constitution of 1863 written at Rionegro is read as one of Colombians' many attempts, and learners tabulate Colombia's constitutions
2. **Guías 15–16: The Constitution of 1991 / The Constitutions of 1886 and 1991** — a timeline runs from 1843, when Pedro Alcántara Herrán is elected mid-civil-war, to 1990, when university students' initiative makes the National Constituent Assembly possible; the 1991 charter ends the Church–State settlement of 1886, replaces the state of siege with graduated emergencies, and is reformed 26 times in 17 years
3. **Tema 20: Formation of the Colombian State, 1849–1886** — the law of 15 June 1853 separates Church and State, the Liberal party splits, a constitution consolidates nine sovereign states, Núñez presides from 8 April 1880 for four periods, the Concordat of 1887 follows, and the Constitution of 1886 is reformed down to 1958 and 2005
4. **Tema 21: Political-administrative organisation of Colombia in the 19th century** — the Republic of New Granada (1830–1858), the Granadine Confederation, the United States of Colombia and the Republic from 1886 — war-haunted to 1910 — succeed each other, each with its own symbols, the centralist regime dating from 5 August
5. **Tema 17: Liberal dominance, 1930–1946** — after four decades of conservative government the liberals rule; the Constitutional Reform of 1936 under López provokes immediate controversy, and Eduardo Santos is elected in 1937
