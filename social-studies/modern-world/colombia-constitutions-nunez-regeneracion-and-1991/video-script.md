# Video script — Colombia's constitutions — Núñez, the Regeneración and 1991

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Colombia's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Colombia rebuilds its state on paper again and again — Rionegro 1863, Núñez and 1886, the 1936 reform, and the 1991 charter born of a student-led Constituent Assembly.

Rafael Núñez's project of change is debated from his own discourse, the liberal Constitution of 1863 written at Rionegro is read as one of Colombians' many attempts, and learners tabulate Colombia's constitutions.

A timeline runs from 1843, when Pedro Alcántara Herrán is elected mid-civil-war, to 1990, when university students' initiative makes the National Constituent Assembly possible; the 1991 charter ends the Church–State settlement of 1886, replaces the state of siege with graduated emergencies, and is reformed 26 times in 17 years.

The law of 15 June 1853 separates Church and State, the Liberal party splits, a constitution consolidates nine sovereign states, Núñez presides from 8 April 1880 for four periods, the Concordat of 1887 follows, and the Constitution of 1886 is reformed down to 1958 and 2005.

The Republic of New Granada (1830–1858), the Granadine Confederation, the United States of Colombia and the Republic from 1886 — war-haunted to 1910 — succeed each other, each with its own symbols, the centralist regime dating from 5 August.

After four decades of conservative government the liberals rule; the Constitutional Reform of 1936 under López provokes immediate controversy, and Eduardo Santos is elected in 1937.
