# Lesson plan — The Greek Revolution 1821 1830

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Greek Revolution 1821 1830
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Greece, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** three merchants in Odessa found a secret society in 1814, swear members by oath, and spin a web of initiates across the diaspora
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit C, ch. 1 The Filiki Etaireia** — three merchants in Odessa found a secret society in 1814, swear members by oath, and spin a web of initiates across the diaspora
2. **Unit C, ch. 2 The uprising in Moldavia-Wallachia** — Alexandros Ypsilantis crosses the Prut calling the Balkans to rise; the Sacred Band dies at Dragatsani and the northern rising is crushed
3. **Unit C, ch. 3 The revolution in the Peloponnese** — the Morea rises in March 1821 — Kalamata first — while Europe's congresses condemn the revolt they cannot ignore
4. **Unit C, ch. 4 The revolution in Central Greece** — chieftains swear the rising at Levadeia, Odysseas Androutsos holds the inn at Gravia, and Athanasios Diakos dies impaled after Alamana
5. **Unit C, ch. 5 The revolution in the Aegean islands** — Spetses rises first under Bouboulina; Hydra, Psara and Samos turn merchant fleets into a navy under Miaoulis and Papanikolis
6. **Unit C, ch. 6 The revolution in Epirus, Thessaly and Macedonia** — returned Souliots strike in Epirus while the Karatasos father and son raise Olympus and Chalkidiki, and a young Macedonian exile writes home of the struggle
7. **Unit C, ch. 7 The fall of Tripolitsa** — Kolokotronis rings the Ottoman capital of the Morea; after months of siege the city falls in a storm of killing that shadows the victory
8. **Unit C, ch. 8 The struggles of Kanaris** — with only merchant ships against great war fleets, Greek captains send in fireships — Kanaris burns the Turkish flagship at Chios and becomes a hymn
9. **Unit C, ch. 9 Dramali's campaign — Dervenakia** — Dramali's great army pours into the Morea and starves in the Argos plain; Kolokotronis destroys it in the Dervenakia passes
10. **Unit C, ch. 10 Markos Botsaris** — after the disaster at Peta, Botsaris leads his Souliots in a night attack at Karpenisi and falls at the moment of victory
11. **Unit C, ch. 11 Ibrahim in the Peloponnese — Papaflessas** — Egypt's Ibrahim lands with a modern army and burns the Morea; the monk Papaflessas refuses his offers and dies fighting at Maniaki
12. **Unit C, ch. 12 The second siege of Missolonghi — Dionysios Solomos** — starving Missolonghi holds a year against Kioutachis and Ibrahim, then makes its Exodus by night; Solomos turns its free besieged into the national hymn
13. **Unit C, ch. 13 The struggles of Karaiskakis** — after Missolonghi falls, Karaiskakis campaigns to save Athens, retakes Attica town by town, and is killed before the Acropolis he tried to relieve
14. **Unit C, ch. 14 Philhellenism** — over 1,200 Europeans — Byron's generation — come to fight, pay and write for Greece: "We are all Greeks"
15. **Unit C, ch. 15 The intervention of the Great Powers and the Battle of Navarino** — Britain, France and Russia, pushed by philhellene opinion, send the fleets that sink the Ottoman-Egyptian navy at Navarino in 1827
16. **Unit C, ch. 16 The National Assemblies and the political organisation of the Struggle** — while fighting, the Greeks argue constitutions at Epidaurus, Astros and Troezen — a state invented in assembly tents
17. **Unit C, ch. 17 Ioannis Kapodistrias and his work** — the Corfiot ex-foreign minister of Russia lands as Governor, builds schools, an orphanage, a currency and an army — and is assassinated at Nafplio
18. **Unit C, ch. 18 The end of the Revolution and Greek independence** — the London Protocol of 1830 recognises a small independent Greece — the first new nation-state born of a national revolution
19. **Unit C exercise dossier (The Great Revolution)** — gap-fill on Moldavia's rising, fighters sorted by theatre, Alamana set beside ancient Thermopylae, Ibrahim's route mapped from Egypt, and the Revolution's own acrostic
20. **§7 The Filiki Etaireia and the proclamation of the Greek Revolution in the Danubian Principalities** — the secret Society crosses the Prut with Ypsilantis in February 1821 and the Greek rising begins — badly, gloriously
21. **§8 The course of the Greek Revolution (1821–1827)** — Tripolitsa, Dervenakia, Missolonghi, civil wars and Ibrahim — victory, catastrophe and rescue in six years
22. **§9 The revolutionary Greeks' first efforts to build a state** — assemblies at Epidaurus and Astros draft constitutions while the war still rages
23. **§10 The Greek Revolution and Europe** — governments condemn, peoples volunteer — Byron dies at Missolonghi and the fleets settle it at Navarino
24. **The course of the Greek revolution (1821–1827)** — island fleets and mountain fighters beat Ottoman armies, fall into civil war, face Ibrahim's Egyptians — and survive
25. **The struggle for independence: outline of the Revolution** — 1821's rising spreads from the Peloponnese and the islands; eight years of war end in a small free state
26. **The forces of the belligerents and their war tactics** — guerrilla bands and island fire-ships face Ottoman regulars and Ibrahim's Egyptian army
27. **The Greek Revolution of 1821: a message of freedom for Europe (the course of the war)** — Ypsilantis crosses the Prut, the Peloponnese and the islands rise, Missolonghi falls in a heroic exodus — and a small nation fights an empire for eight years
