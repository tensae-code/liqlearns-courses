# Video script — Palestine after 1948 — from the Nakba to the Palestinian Authority

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Palestine's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The Absentees' Property Law hands the state the lands of Palestinians driven out in 1948, and the "present absentee" rule bars villagers like those of Ein Hawd — who hid in the Carmel forest from the Haganah and still live nearby — from ever returning to their own village.

At the Palestinian Arab congress in Jerusalem on 28 May 1964 the Palestine Liberation Organization is founded under Ahmad Shuqairi, to fight the battle of liberation and affirm the Palestinian Arab's right in his homeland.

In 1948 Palestinians proclaim the All-Palestine Government in Gaza under Haj Amin al-Husseini — with its own flag and passports — refusing a mere civil administration, until international pressure aborts it.

Inside the new state, laws do the demographic work — army land confiscation (1949), the Absentees' Property Law and the Law of Return (both 1950); the Jericho Conference (1 December 1948) resolves to join the West Bank to Jordan, and Gaza (365 km²) passes under Egyptian administration.

Gaza is occupied during the attack on Egypt and pays in massacres — the lesson tabulates the martyrs by town (Gaza City 256, Khan Yunis and its surroundings 415, Rafah 197 among them) — and on the aggression's eve, 29 October 1956, the Kafr Qasim massacre is committed inside the state.

The first Palestinian National Council meets in Jerusalem on 28 May 1964 with King Hussein present and founds the PLO under Ahmad Shuqairi; Fatah launches on 1 January 1965 with the Eilabun operation, Yahya Hammuda holds the chair in between, and Yasser Arafat takes the leadership in 1969.

On the morning of Thursday 21 March 1968 the Palestinian resistance and the Jordanian army together repel the assault on Karameh; Arafat calls it the turning point between despair and hope, and the 1969 Cairo National Council rejects all settlement projects at odds with Palestinian rights and elects him PLO chairman.

The old legal tools are reused — absentee-property and "state land" declarations — settlements climb the hilltops under plans like Allon's, the wall (begun 2002) follows, and the lesson sets the law against it: Geneva IV Article 49(6), The Hague 1907, and the International Court of Justice's 2004 advisory opinion.

Driven from Jordan in 1971, the PLO rebuilds in Lebanon; Israel assassinates Kamal Nasser, Kamal Adwan and Abu Yusuf al-Najjar in Beirut in 1973, and the National Council's twelfth session (Cairo, 1974) adopts the Ten-Point phased programme — an independent national authority on any liberated part.

Sadat's 1977 Jerusalem speech and the Camp David framework leave the West Bank and Gaza to staged negotiations; the Arab world erupts in rejection and the Steadfastness Front forms at the 1977 Tripoli summit — Syria, Libya, Algeria, Yemen and the PLO.

In 1982 the invasion reaches Beirut — the stand at Beaufort Castle, then an 80-day siege of West Beirut with power, food, medicine and fuel cut — until the revolution's fighters evacuate the city.

On 8 December 1987 an Israeli truck kills four workers from Jabalia; the funerals turn to stone-throwing and a six-year popular uprising begins, women at its heart, while Israel assassinates its leaders abroad — Abu Jihad in Tunis (1988), Abu Iyad and Abu al-Hol (1991), Fathi Shaqaqi in Malta (1995) — and the 1988 Declaration of Independence proclaims the democratic state.

At Madrid (1991) Palestinians sit inside the Jordanian delegation; Oslo (1993) brings mutual recognition and an interim self-government of at most five years; the army withdraws from Gaza and Jericho in 1994, Arafat enters Gaza on 1 July 1994 as president of the new Authority, Oslo II is signed at Taba (28 September 1995), and a Legislative Council is elected in 1996.

Sharon's entry into the al-Aqsa courtyards sparks the second intifada, more militarised than the first, answered with bulldozed farmland and olive trees, closures and the annexation wall; after Arafat's death come the 2005 presidential elections, the unilateral Gaza disengagement evacuating the settlements (2005), full UNESCO membership (31 October 2011) and UN observer-state status.
