# Palestine after 1948 — from the Nakba to the Palestinian Authority

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: the Absentees' Property Law hands the state the lands of Palestinians driven out in 1948, and the "present absentee" rule bars villagers like those of Ein Hawd — who hid in the Carmel forest from the Haganah and still live nearby — from ever returning to their own village As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Unit 1 Lesson 4: the laws that keep refugees out

The Absentees' Property Law hands the state the lands of Palestinians driven out in 1948, and the "present absentee" rule bars villagers like those of Ein Hawd — who hid in the Carmel forest from the Haganah and still live nearby — from ever returning to their own village (Unit 1 Lesson 4: the laws that keep refugees out)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unit 2 Lesson 3: the Palestinian national movement organises

At the Palestinian Arab congress in Jerusalem on 28 May 1964 the Palestine Liberation Organization is founded under Ahmad Shuqairi, to fight the battle of liberation and affirm the Palestinian Arab's right in his homeland (Unit 2 Lesson 3: the Palestinian national movement organises)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 3 — Unit 4 Lesson 2: the All-Palestine Government

In 1948 Palestinians proclaim the All-Palestine Government in Gaza under Haj Amin al-Husseini — with its own flag and passports — refusing a mere civil administration, until international pressure aborts it (Unit 4 Lesson 2: the All-Palestine Government)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 4 — Unit 4 Lesson 3: Palestine divided after 1948

Inside the new state, laws do the demographic work — army land confiscation (1949), the Absentees' Property Law and the Law of Return (both 1950); the Jericho Conference (1 December 1948) resolves to join the West Bank to Jordan, and Gaza (365 km²) passes under Egyptian administration (Unit 4 Lesson 3: Palestine divided after 1948)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 5 — Unit 4 Lesson 4: Palestine in the 1956 Tripartite Aggression

Gaza is occupied during the attack on Egypt and pays in massacres — the lesson tabulates the martyrs by town (Gaza City 256, Khan Yunis and its surroundings 415, Rafah 197 among them) — and on the aggression's eve, 29 October 1956, the Kafr Qasim massacre is committed inside the state (Unit 4 Lesson 4: Palestine in the 1956 Tripartite Aggression)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 6 — Unit 4 Lesson 5: the PLO and the contemporary revolution

The first Palestinian National Council meets in Jerusalem on 28 May 1964 with King Hussein present and founds the PLO under Ahmad Shuqairi; Fatah launches on 1 January 1965 with the Eilabun operation, Yahya Hammuda holds the chair in between, and Yasser Arafat takes the leadership in 1969 (Unit 4 Lesson 5: the PLO and the contemporary revolution)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 7 — Unit 5 Lesson 2: the Battle of Karameh 1968

On the morning of Thursday 21 March 1968 the Palestinian resistance and the Jordanian army together repel the assault on Karameh; Arafat calls it the turning point between despair and hope, and the 1969 Cairo National Council rejects all settlement projects at odds with Palestinian rights and elects him PLO chairman (Unit 5 Lesson 2: the Battle of Karameh 1968)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 8 — Unit 5 Lesson 3: settlement colonisation after 1967

The old legal tools are reused — absentee-property and "state land" declarations — settlements climb the hilltops under plans like Allon's, the wall (begun 2002) follows, and the lesson sets the law against it: Geneva IV Article 49(6), The Hague 1907, and the International Court of Justice's 2004 advisory opinion (Unit 5 Lesson 3: settlement colonisation after 1967)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 9 — Unit 6 Lesson 1: the Palestinian revolution 1970–1976

Driven from Jordan in 1971, the PLO rebuilds in Lebanon; Israel assassinates Kamal Nasser, Kamal Adwan and Abu Yusuf al-Najjar in Beirut in 1973, and the National Council's twelfth session (Cairo, 1974) adopts the Ten-Point phased programme — an independent national authority on any liberated part (Unit 6 Lesson 1: the Palestinian revolution 1970–1976)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 10 — Unit 6 Lesson 2: Palestine in the 1978 Egyptian–Israeli peace

Sadat's 1977 Jerusalem speech and the Camp David framework leave the West Bank and Gaza to staged negotiations; the Arab world erupts in rejection and the Steadfastness Front forms at the 1977 Tripoli summit — Syria, Libya, Algeria, Yemen and the PLO (Unit 6 Lesson 2: Palestine in the 1978 Egyptian–Israeli peace)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 11 — Unit 6 Lesson 3: the invasions of Lebanon 1978–1982

In 1982 the invasion reaches Beirut — the stand at Beaufort Castle, then an 80-day siege of West Beirut with power, food, medicine and fuel cut — until the revolution's fighters evacuate the city (Unit 6 Lesson 3: the invasions of Lebanon 1978–1982)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 12 — Unit 6 Lesson 4: the 1987 Intifada

On 8 December 1987 an Israeli truck kills four workers from Jabalia; the funerals turn to stone-throwing and a six-year popular uprising begins, women at its heart, while Israel assassinates its leaders abroad — Abu Jihad in Tunis (1988), Abu Iyad and Abu al-Hol (1991), Fathi Shaqaqi in Malta (1995) — and the 1988 Declaration of Independence proclaims the democratic state (Unit 6 Lesson 4: the 1987 Intifada)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 13 — Unit 6 Lesson 5: the peace process and the Palestinian Authority

At Madrid (1991) Palestinians sit inside the Jordanian delegation; Oslo (1993) brings mutual recognition and an interim self-government of at most five years; the army withdraws from Gaza and Jericho in 1994, Arafat enters Gaza on 1 July 1994 as president of the new Authority, Oslo II is signed at Taba (28 September 1995), and a Legislative Council is elected in 1996 (Unit 6 Lesson 5: the peace process and the Palestinian Authority)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

[Picture 40792 — madridoslo]

## Sub lesson 14 — Unit 6 Lesson 6: the al-Aqsa Intifada (2000) and after

Sharon's entry into the al-Aqsa courtyards sparks the second intifada, more militarised than the first, answered with bulldozed farmland and olive trees, closures and the annexation wall; after Arafat's death come the 2005 presidential elections, the unilateral Gaza disengagement evacuating the settlements (2005), full UNESCO membership (31 October 2011) and UN observer-state status (Unit 6 Lesson 6: the al-Aqsa Intifada (2000) and after)

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.
