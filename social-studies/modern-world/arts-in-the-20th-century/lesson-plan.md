# Lesson plan — Arts In The 20th Century

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Arts In The 20th Century
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Greece, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** painting breaks with realism, cinema is born and becomes the century's popular art
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Arts in the 20th-century world** — painting breaks with realism, cinema is born and becomes the century's popular art
2. **Poetry and prose in the 20th-century world** — two wars and broken empires drive writers to new forms for a fractured world
3. **The arts in the 20th century** — art questions itself, cinema and the new arts democratise it, postwar art goes global
4. **Arts of the 20th century: movements, cinema and mass culture** — fauvism, cubism, Dada, surrealism and pop art follow cinema, jazz and rock into a mass-culture century
5. **Chapter 16: The decades 1900–1930 (part one)** — Expressionism, Fauvism, the Blue Rider, Cubism and Futurism break the figure and the canvas in twenty years
6. **Chapter 17: The decades 1900–1930 (part two)** — De Stijl, Suprematism, Constructivism, Dada and Surrealism, the Bauhaus and the great masters of architecture remake art between the wars
7. **Chapter 18: Postwar art in America and Europe — the New York School and Abstract Expressionism** — Pollock's drips move art's capital across the Atlantic
8. **Chapter 19: The 1960s to the 1980s–90s** — Pop, Op and Kinetic art, Minimalism, Conceptual art and Photorealism argue over what art even is

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
