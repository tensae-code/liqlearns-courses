# The Renaissance — its great figures

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — the playwright and the artists is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — The playwright and the artists

**William Shakespeare (1564–1616)**, the English playwright, wrote plays still performed everywhere: **Hamlet, Romeo and Juliet, Julius Caesar and Macbeth**. **Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519)** was a master of **many trades — painter, sculptor, musician, engineer, scientist** — known above all for two paintings: the **Mona Lisa** and the **Last Supper**. **Michelangelo (1475–1564)** contributed the **Creation of Adam**, the reaching hands nearly touching, among the most famous images in the world. Notice the Renaissance pattern in one lesson: one man writes the inner life, one man does everything, one man paints creation itself — human nature and the visible world, exactly as the age promised.

## Sub lesson 2 — The men who moved the Earth

**Nicolaus Copernicus (1473–1543)**, the Polish astronomer, established that the **Sun is the centre of the solar system** and the **Earth, like the other planets, revolves around it** — the Earth dethroned from the centre. **Galileo Galilei (1564–1642)**, the Italian astronomer and physicist, **improved the telescope's use**, turning it on the heavens. **Isaac Newton (1642–1727)**, the English mathematician and physicist, **developed calculus** and gave gravity its **law** — the formula that describes how the Earth and all masses pull. Keep Galileo and Newton distinct, as this lesson teaches them: Galileo's instrument opened the sky; Newton's mathematics described its pull.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the whole lesson as a story, without looking: Five names carry the Renaissance: Shakespeare the playwright, Leonardo and Michelangelo the artists, and Copernicus, Galileo and Newton, who moved the Earth and measured its pull.
