# Video script — Argentina — the conquest of indigenous territories, and who progress left out

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the other side of the building of the Argentine state, as Argentina's Grade 6 teacher guide tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The new Argentine state had two faces, and this book refuses to show only one of them.

The same state that opened offices in Europe to welcome immigrants sent its army south, and into the Chaco. There it fought the indigenous peoples who had lived on those lands, hunting and fishing there, long before the state existed. The conquest took their hunting and fishing lands — the ground their whole way of life stood on.

And in the cities, the newcomers the state had fetched found progress crowded. Immigrant families packed into conventillos — tenement houses where many families shared small rooms and a common courtyard, in the middle of the booming capital.

So the book ends its story of building a country with a question, and it asks the children reading it to answer: progress came — but who did progress leave out?
