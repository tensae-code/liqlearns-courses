# Lesson plan — Muslim reform movements after 1857

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Muslim reform movements after 1857
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Pakistan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** After the catastrophe of 1857, Muslim India's leaders answer two ways — Sir Syed's Aligarh turns to modern education and the Two-Nation Theory, while Deoband and Nadva guard the faith — and together they shape the road to Pakistan.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** — After the catastrophe of 1857, Sir Syed argues the Muslims of India must learn the new knowledge or be left behind — he opens schools at Muradabad and Ghazipur, founds the Scientific Society in 1863, raises the college at Aligarh that becomes a university, and plants the two-nation idea from which Pakistan grows

2. **After 1857 — a community punished** — A defeated community is punished and shut out, and its leaders must decide whether to fight the British or learn from them

3. **Sir Syed and the Two-Nation Theory** — Sir Syed tells Muslims to stop mourning the Mughals, take English education, and recognise Hindus and Muslims as two nations

4. **Aligarh builds schools and colleges** — Reform becomes bricks and classrooms, as community-funded colleges carry a generation of Muslims into modern professions

5. **Deoband and Nadva answer from the faith** — While Aligarh looks West, Deoband guards the faith, and the two answers to 1857 shape Muslim India together
