# Lesson plan — Saudi Arabia — unification under Abdulaziz: the brave courier

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Saudi Arabia — unification under Abdulaziz: the brave courier
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Saudi Arabia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** King Abdulaziz, having secured the roads and unified the country, must get a secret letter to his men in another city while enemies watch the road between — told in the Grade 4 book's story activity "The Brave Man", from the Stories I Belong To series.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on a single contrast a child can hold: a king who has united a whole country still cannot trust its roads — and one brave courier carries the letter the kingdom depends on. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A country made one** — King Abdulaziz secured the roads and unified the country — the kingdom the story stands on.
2. **The secret letter** — The king must get a secret letter to his men in another city, while enemies watch the road between.
3. **The brave courier** — One brave man carries the letter the kingdom depends on — the story the book calls "The Brave Man".

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what King Abdulaziz had done — secured the roads and unified the country.
2. Say why a secret letter had to reach his men in another city, and what made the road dangerous.
3. Retell the story's beats in order: a united country, a watched road, a brave courier.
4. Explain, in their own words, why one letter could matter to a whole kingdom.
