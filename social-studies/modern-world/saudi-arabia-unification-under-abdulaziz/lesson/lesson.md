# Saudi Arabia — unification under Abdulaziz: the brave courier

## Do it first

A king has done the hardest thing: he has made many towns and tribes into one country, and made its roads safe. But enemies still watch one road — and a letter that must reach his men in another city cannot be seen travelling it. Before you read on, decide: who would you send, and what would make you trust them? Hold your answer; the book's story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A country made one

The Saudi Arabia Grade 4 book tells this story inside Unit 2, as a story activity — "The Brave Man", from the قصص أنتمي إليها, the Stories I Belong To series: stories a Saudi child is told as their own inheritance.

The story stands on what King Abdulaziz had already done: he had secured the roads and unified the country. The kingdom exists. Its towns are one country now, under one king.

In your mind's eye: a desert road between two Saudi towns at dusk, a single courier on horseback carrying a sealed letter, watchers hidden far off among the rocks. The student points to the courier and says what he carries and why it must arrive.

## Sub lesson 2 — The secret letter

But a united country is not yet a safe one everywhere. The king must get a secret letter to his men in another city — and enemies watch the road between.

A letter seen is a plan known. So the message that the kingdom depends on cannot travel openly, in daylight, by an ordinary messenger. It needs someone the road itself cannot stop.

## Sub lesson 3 — The brave courier

So the letter goes with a brave man — the courier whose courage gives the story its name.

That is the beat the book leaves with its nine-year-olds: a king who has united a country still cannot trust its roads, and one brave courier carries the letter the kingdom depends on. The story is filed here as a plot summary in the course's own words only — never transcribed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: King Abdulaziz had secured the roads and unified the country; he had to get a secret letter to his men in another city while enemies watched the road; and one brave courier carried the letter the kingdom depended on. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write, from memory, what King Abdulaziz had already done before the story begins.
2. Write why the letter had to be secret, and who was watching the road.
3. Write two sentences explaining how a king who has united a country can still depend on one brave courier.
