# Lesson plan — Micronesia — a leap into the modern world

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Micronesia — a leap into the modern world
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Micronesia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In the 1960s–70s money, radios, new schools, hospitals, roads and airfields transformed the islands, Peace Corps volunteers and Civic Action Teams arrived, and Micronesianization put islanders in charge of more of the Trust Territory government.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself, told in order, part by part, as Micronesia's books tell it. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Money, radios, schools and hospitals** — say that in the 1960s–70s money, radios, new schools and hospitals transformed the islands.
2. **Roads, airfields, Peace Corps and Civic Action Teams** — say that roads and airfields were built, and Peace Corps volunteers and Civic Action Teams arrived.
3. **Micronesianization: islanders take charge** — say that Micronesianization put islanders in charge of more of the Trust Territory government.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
