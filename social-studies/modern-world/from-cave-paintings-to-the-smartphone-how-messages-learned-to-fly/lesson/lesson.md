# From cave paintings to the smartphone — how messages learned to fly

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Every generation's message travels faster and farther than the last — from paintings on cave walls and post cards to Marconi's wireless, television, the telephone in the pocket and the computer's global village. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cave walls to the computer

The first messages are paintings on cave walls; then come letters and the post card, Marconi's wireless of 1901, Baird's television (Pakistan's own TV starts in 1964), the telephone in the pocket and Babbage's computer — every generation's message travels faster and farther than the last.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — The global village

The Class 4 story continues into the age of mass media — newspapers and magazines, radio and television, then the computer, the internet and social media shrink the world into what the book, quoting Marshall McLuhan, calls a global village.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Cave walls to the computer; The global village. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
