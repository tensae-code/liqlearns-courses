# Malaysia after 1963 — Singapore's separation, 13 May and the New Economic Policy — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Singapore separates
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Tun Abdul Razak's negotiations with Lee Kuan Yew fail across 29 June–1 July 1965; the Tunku directs Razak to settle separation with Dr. Ismail Abdul Rahman, Tan Siew Sin and V.T. Sambanthan in early August 1965, and Singapore leaves Malaysia

## Sub lesson 2 — The communist threat after 1963
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: in Sarawak the army's operations — Hammer (1965), RASCOM's and Sri Aman among them — break the communist movement division by division, while the peninsula faces its own renewed insurgency

## Sub lesson 3 — The inter-ethnic tragedy
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the race riots of 13 May 1969 force an emergency; the Yang di-Pertuan Agong consents to the National Operations Council (MAGERAN) under Tun Abdul Razak Hussein, which governs from 16 May 1969 to 23 February 1971 to restore order

## Sub lesson 4 — Rebuilding unity and the economy
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: the Rukun Negara is proclaimed as the nation's unity charter; education is re-knit (the Cabinet Committee of September 1974 reviews the national system with Bahasa Melayu at its centre); and the New Economic Policy (Dasar Ekonomi Baru, 1970–1990), launched through the First Outline Perspective Plan, sets out to eradicate poverty and restructure society's economic imbalances
