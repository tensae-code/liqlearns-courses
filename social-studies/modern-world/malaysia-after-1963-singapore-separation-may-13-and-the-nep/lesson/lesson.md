# Malaysia after 1963 — Singapore's separation, 13 May and the New Economic Policy

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: After 1963 Malaysia is stress-tested — Singapore separates in 1965, the communists renew their fight, and the riots of 13 May 1969 force an emergency — then rebuilt through the Rukun Negara and the New Economic Policy. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Singapore separates

Tun Abdul Razak's negotiations with Lee Kuan Yew fail across 29 June–1 July 1965; the Tunku directs Razak to settle separation with Dr. Ismail Abdul Rahman, Tan Siew Sin and V.T. Sambanthan in early August 1965, and Singapore leaves Malaysia (Bab 6.2: Singapore separates)

## Sub lesson 2 — The communist threat after 1963

In Sarawak the army's operations — Hammer (1965), RASCOM's and Sri Aman among them — break the communist movement division by division, while the peninsula faces its own renewed insurgency (Bab 6.3: the communist threat after 1963)

## Sub lesson 3 — The inter-ethnic tragedy

The race riots of 13 May 1969 force an emergency; the Yang di-Pertuan Agong consents to the National Operations Council (MAGERAN) under Tun Abdul Razak Hussein, which governs from 16 May 1969 to 23 February 1971 to restore order (Bab 6.5: the inter-ethnic tragedy)

## Sub lesson 4 — Rebuilding unity and the economy

The Rukun Negara is proclaimed as the nation's unity charter; education is re-knit (the Cabinet Committee of September 1974 reviews the national system with Bahasa Melayu at its centre); and the New Economic Policy (Dasar Ekonomi Baru, 1970–1990), launched through the First Outline Perspective Plan, sets out to eradicate poverty and restructure society's economic imbalances (Bab 7–8: rebuilding unity and the economy)
