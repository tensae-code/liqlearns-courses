# Lesson plan — Haiti — independence debt and neocolonialism by debt

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Haiti — independence debt and neocolonialism by debt
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Haiti, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** France offers recognition at a price in 1825 — an indemnity to former slave-owners that becomes a double debt, draining Haiti's treasury for decades; in 2003 President Aristide demands the money back.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **International recognition and debt** — France offers recognition at a price — the 1825 ordinance turns independence itself into a bill Haiti must pay (Séance 7, Reconnaissance internationale et dette (developed in the companion debt sequence))
2. **The debt and Haiti's development — a contemporary debate** — in 2003 President Aristide demands France give the money back — pupils open the file and ask what a 19th-century debt did to Haiti's development (Séance 1, La dette et le développement d'Haïti, un débat contemporain ?)
3. **The independence debt — negotiation and imposition** — French warships anchor off Port-au-Prince in 1825; the ordinance offers recognition in exchange for a huge indemnity to former slave-owners, softened only by the 1838 treaties (Séance 2, La dette de l'indépendance, négociation et imposition)
4. **From the debt to the double debt (1)** — to pay the first instalments Haiti must borrow from French banks — now it owes the indemnity and the loans taken to pay it (Séance 3, De la dette à « la double dette » (1))
5. **From the debt to the double debt (2)** — decades of payments and fresh loans drain the treasury — pupils follow the money and judge the book's charge: neocolonialism by debt (Séance 4, De la dette à « la double dette » (2))

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
