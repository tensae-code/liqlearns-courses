# Lesson plan — Kazakhstan The Start Of Russian Annexation

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Kazakhstan The Start Of Russian Annexation
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Abulkhair's oath of 1731, taken under Dzhungar pressure, opens the long road from alliance to annexation
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§§9–10 The beginning of the Kazakh Khanate's joining to the Russian Empire** — Abulkhair's oath of 1731, taken under Dzhungar pressure, opens the long road from alliance to annexation
2. **§9 The causes and consequences of Abulkhair's acceptance of Russian subjecthood** — Abulkhair swears the oath of 1731 seeking Russian guns against the Dzhungars — and opens the door to empire
3. **§10 The Russian Empire's policy in Kazakhstan in the 1730s–40s** — Orenburg is founded and the empire turns a defensive alliance into administration
4. **§2.2 What were the reasons for Abulkhair Khan's letter to Empress Anna Ioannovna?** — one letter of 1730 is cross-examined — plea for help, or surrender of sovereignty?
5. **§2.3 How did the Middle Zhuz join Russia?** — the Middle Zhuz's oaths and embassies follow a different road to the same empire
6. **Lesson plans §§9–14: Abulkhair's oath; Russia's policy in the 1730s–50s; Abylai's rise and policy** — the oath is analysed for causes and aims, then Abylai is built up lesson by lesson as the khanate's restorer

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
