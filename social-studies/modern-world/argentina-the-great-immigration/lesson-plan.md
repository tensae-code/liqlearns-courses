# Lesson plan — Argentina — the great immigration

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Argentina — the great immigration
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Argentina, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Millions of Europeans crossed the Atlantic to Argentina — pushed by crisis and crowding at home, pulled by Argentina's promise of land and work, and actively recruited by the Argentine state under the 1876 Immigration and Colonisation Law, until by 1914 nearly a third of Argentina's people were foreign-born, mostly from Italy and Spain.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on push and pull — why people leave a home and why they choose a new one — and on a state that did not wait for newcomers but went out to fetch them. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pushed from Europe, pulled to Argentina** — crisis and crowding in Europe push millions to leave home; Argentina's promise of land and work pulls them across the Atlantic.
2. **The state goes out to fetch them** — Alberdi's "to govern is to populate" becomes the 1876 Immigration and Colonisation Law: the state opens offices in Europe and pays passages, and by 1914 nearly a third of Argentina's people are foreign-born, mostly from Italy and Spain.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Argentina — building the state, 1860–1916. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what pushed millions out of Western Europe and what pulled them to Argentina.
2. Say what "to govern is to populate" means, and whose words they were.
3. Say what the 1876 Immigration and Colonisation Law made the state do.
4. Say how large the foreign-born share of Argentina's people had become by 1914, and which two countries most of them came from.
