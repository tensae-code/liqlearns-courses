# The Second World War — causes

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — dictators and racist nationalism is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — Dictators and racist nationalism

The book's basic causes begin at home, inside three states. **(a) Fascist-natured dictatorial governments took power in Italy and Germany**, and a **military government in Japan**. **(b) Absolute racist nationalism spread in those three states** — nationalism, which the First World War lesson defined as love and loyalty to country, here hardened into a doctrine that one race or nation stands above all others and may rule them. Where the League of Nations was meant to settle quarrels by talk, these governments glorified force — and prepared their peoples to use it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Axis, appeasement and the weak League

**(c)** The three states **spent heavily to produce arms, build their war forces and prepare their peoples' minds for war** — and formed the **Berlin–Rome–Tokyo Axis**, which **frightened other countries' security**. **(d)** Against this stood two failures: the **failure of Britain's and France's appeasement policy** — giving in to the dictators' demands in hope of peace — and the **weakness of the League of Nations**, the body founded after the First World War precisely to prevent this. Aggression grew; the restraints did not hold. The next lesson follows the war the Axis launched.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the whole lesson as a story, without looking: The Second World War grew from fascist dictatorships in Italy and Germany and a military government in Japan, from racist nationalism and a new arms build-up joined in the Axis — while appeasement failed and the League stood weak.
