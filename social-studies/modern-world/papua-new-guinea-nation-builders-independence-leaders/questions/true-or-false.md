# Papua New Guinea — nation builders: the independence leaders — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Grand Chief Michael Somare, Sir Maori Kiki, Vincent Eri and Sir John Guise — what each man did in the events of self-government and Independence, and what it built for the nation.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Important persons or people – nation

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Social Science Teacher Guide — Primary Grade 5, Standards Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Important persons or people – nation

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Sir Michael Somare, Dame Josephine Abaijah, and Sir Tei Abal — the Enga medical assistant with no formal schooling who reached Parliament in 1964, led the Opposition at Independence in 1975, and said moka talks were "much the same as meetings of Parliament" — each leader's contribution to independence is profiled and their home province mapped.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Sir Tei Abal

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Social Science Teacher Guide — Primary Grade 8, Standards Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Sir Tei Abal

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The named changemakers — Somare, Julius Chan, Maori Kiki, Tei Abal, John Momis, WWII hero Yauwiga of Sepik, Kondom Agaundo, the Bully Beef Club and the Pangu Party — many already half-forgotten, their legacies still shaping the country.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Agents of change in PNG

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Social Science Junior High Grade 9 Teacher Guide, Standards-Based* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Agents of change in PNG

**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Prominent Papua New Guineans
