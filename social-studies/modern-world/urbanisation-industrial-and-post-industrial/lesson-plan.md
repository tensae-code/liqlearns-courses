# Lesson plan — Urbanisation Industrial And Post Industrial

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Urbanisation Industrial And Post Industrial
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** urbanisation in the industrial age begins in the late 18th century with the first industrial revolution — machines raise productivity, production shifts from mechanised to electrified, and the world's urban share climbs from 13.6% (1900) to 36.6% (1970) in the book's UN table, with Manchester and Chicago as typical industrial cities and New York and Paris as service cities, the biggest of all Tokyo at 23.3 million (1970) — while uncontrolled growth eats farmland and breeds pollution and social evils; in the post-industrial age (from the late 20th century) the internet and knowledge economy reorganise the city: by 2024 the world counts 33 megacities, 27 of them in developing countries, city-regions merge into belts like Tokyo's and the Boston–Washington corridor, and green and smart cities become the goal; Vietnam's own urbanisation, tied to industrialisation and modernisation, reaches 38.5% in 2024 with Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City as the twin growth poles
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
