# Lesson plan — The Third Industrial Revolution Computers And Space

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Third Industrial Revolution Computers And Space
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the third industrial revolution, also called the digital revolution, begins in the 1940s of the 20th century on the foundation of early-century science and technique, and its first important achievement is the computer — ENIAC (by John Mauchly and Presper Eckert, 1946), the first electronic computer; the Altair 8800 (1975), the first personal computer, with software by Paul Allen and Bill Gates; and the laptop produced in 1994; then the internet connects the world's regions and lets devices share information easily and effectively — Tim Berners-Lee's invention of the World Wide Web (1990) is honoured on the pictured stamp, and wireless internet connection (wifi), invented by a team of scientists and engineers led by John Sullivan and patented in 1996, follows; automation and robot technology free human labour power and raise productivity and product quality; biotechnology breaks through in genetic, cell, micro-organism and enzyme technology, and the "Green Revolution" in agriculture creates many new pest-resistant varieties; and the revolution is bound up with the conquest of space by the two superpowers, the USSR and the USA — the Soviet artificial satellite Sputnik 1 in orbit in 1957, and the American astronaut Neil Armstrong, the first man to set foot on the Moon, in 1969
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.
