# Lesson plan — Vietnam Nationwide Resistance 1946

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Nationwide Resistance 1946
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** France breaks the 1946 agreements — occupying Hanoi's Finance Department and the former Governor-General's Palace (25 June 1946), proclaiming an "Autonomous Republic of Nam Kì", attacking Hải Phòng, Lạng Sơn and Đà Nẵng (November) and massacring in Hàng Bún and Yên Ninh streets (17 December); the book photographs Hồ Chí Minh at Normandy touching the hot cannon barrel — "Keep peace! Prevent war!" — but on 18–19 December the Standing Committee decides on nationwide resistance, and his Appeal of 19 December 1946 rings out: "Who has a gun, use a gun; who has a sword, use a sword; no sword, use hoe, pick, stick…"; the resistance line — all-people, all-sided, prolonged, self-reliant — is set out in the 12 December directive and Trường Chinh's "Resistance Will Surely Win" (1947), and the first years bring the government safe to Việt Bắc, defence factories in the free zones, some 10 million people made literate by June 1950 and recognition from China, the USSR and Eastern Europe in early 1950
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bài 14, the nationwide resistance begins** — France breaks the 1946 agreements — occupying Hanoi's Finance Department and the former Governor-General's Palace (25 June 1946), proclaiming an "Autonomous Republic of Nam Kì", attacking Hải Phòng, Lạng Sơn and Đà Nẵng (November) and massacring in Hàng Bún and Yên Ninh streets (17 December); the book photographs Hồ Chí Minh at Normandy touching the hot cannon barrel — "Keep peace! Prevent war!" — but on 18–19 December the Standing Committee decides on nationwide resistance, and his Appeal of 19 December 1946 rings out: "Who has a gun, use a gun; who has a sword, use a sword; no sword, use hoe, pick, stick…"; the resistance line — all-people, all-sided, prolonged, self-reliant — is set out in the 12 December directive and Trường Chinh's "Resistance Will Surely Win" (1947), and the first years bring the government safe to Việt Bắc, defence factories in the free zones, some 10 million people made literate by June 1950 and recognition from China, the USSR and Eastern Europe in early 1950
2. **Bài 7 The road back to war: the new republic, "hanging by a hair" between Allied armies, 600,000 Japanese awaiting disarmament and France's new expeditionary corps, still negotiates — the Preliminary Agreement (6-3-1946) and Modus Vivendi (14-9-1946) — while France fires on Saigon's Independence Day crowd (2-9-1945) and attacks on the night of 22–23-9-1945 open the second invasion; the South resists and "Southward advance" units march to join it; when France's ultimatum of 18-12-1946 demands disarmament, Hồ Chí Minh issues the Appeal for National Resistance on 19-12-1946 — guns, swords, hoes, sticks** — a government that signs two peace agreements in one year ends it calling every citizen to fight with farm tools
