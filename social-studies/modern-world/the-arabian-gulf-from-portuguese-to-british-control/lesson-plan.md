# Lesson plan — The Arabian Gulf — from Portuguese to British control

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Arabian Gulf — from Portuguese to British control
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Syria, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Portuguese, then British, then American power circles the Arab Gulf — until Kuwait, the Emirates and Oman stand independent and the Gulf Cooperation Council is founded in 1981.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Arabian Gulf — from Portuguese to British control** — The Portuguese came round the Cape and seized Muscat and Bahrain, and the Arabs of Oman freed Muscat in 1650 and drove them out; Britain then bound the Gulf rulers by treaties (1800, the 1820 treaty after the Qawasim resistance, Kuwait 1899), split its emirates, and after oil was found faced American rivalry; Kuwait won independence in 1961, the Trucial states united as the Emirates in 1971, Oman united in 1970, and the Gulf Cooperation Council was founded in 1981.
