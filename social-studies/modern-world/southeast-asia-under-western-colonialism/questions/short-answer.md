# Southeast Asia Under Western Colonialism — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 4, how the Western powers entered Southeast Asia.
**Answer: the book's table runs country by country — Portugal takes Malacca in 1511 (its ship, the Flor do Mar, is now a museum there) and the eastern Indonesian islands; Spain seizes most of the Philippines by the mid-16th century and rules 350 years; the Dutch complete the conquest of Indonesia by the mid-19th century; Britain, France and the Netherlands contest Malaya, Burma and Indochina; Siam is entered by traders in the 16th century and pressed only after Britain has taken India and parts of Malaya and Burma (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In Vietnam's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Lịch sử và Địa lí 8 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* (Vietnam), Bài 4, how the Western powers entered Southeast Asia, printed p. 20.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 4, Southeast Asia under colonial rule.
**Answer: native rulers surrender, collaborate or reign as puppets while colonial officials govern; the economy is plundered — land seized for plantations, no heavy industry allowed, railways and ports built for extraction and repression; Western culture erodes traditions and "enslavement" policies aim to assimilate; and a quoted Vietnamese history of the region shows the method — the British first merged Malaya, Singapore and Brunei into one unit, then split them into four separate regimes, so that each piece could be ruled more easily (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In Vietnam's book, where is this part taught?
**Answer: In *Lịch sử và Địa lí 8 (Kết nối tri thức với cuộc sống)* (Vietnam), Bài 4, Southeast Asia under colonial rule, printed p. 21.**

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Retell this part in your own words: Bài 4, the peoples resist, 16th–mid-19th centuries.
**Answer: on Mactan island in 1521 Lapu-Lapu's people resist the Spanish landing; Indonesia rises against the Dutch under Trunojoyo (1675), Surapati (1683–1719) and Diponegoro (1825–1830); Burma's army under General Bandula fights the first British invasion (1824–1826) until Bandula falls in 1825 — all these risings fail, the book notes, but none of the peoples submits quietly (any close wording — summary only, never the book's words).**
