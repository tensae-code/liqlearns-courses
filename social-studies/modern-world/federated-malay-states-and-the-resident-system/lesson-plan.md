# Lesson plan — The Federated Malay States and the Resident system

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Federated Malay States and the Resident system
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Malaysia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Tin makes the west-coast states rich; Britain binds their rulers by treaty from Pangkor in 1874, installs Residents whose advice must be followed, and federates Perak, Selangor, Negeri Sembilan and Pahang
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Federated Malay States** — tin makes the west-coast states rich — Larut's mines from the 1840s and Chinese miners raise Perak, Selangor and Negeri Sembilan into world tin leaders; Britain binds the rulers by treaty, beginning with the Pangkor Treaty of 1874 in Perak, installs Residents whose "advice" must be followed, and federates Perak, Selangor, Negeri Sembilan and Pahang (Bab 3: the Federated Malay States)
