# Lesson plan — Vietnam Unesco Intangible Heritage

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Vietnam Unesco Intangible Heritage
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the book's timeline runs from Nhã nhạc, Huế court music, in 2003 through the Central Highlands gong culture (2005), Quan họ folk songs and Ca trù (2009), the Gióng festival (2010), Hùng Kings worship (2012), Đờn ca tài tử (2013), Ví Giặm songs (2014), tug-of-war rituals (2015), the Tam phủ Mother Goddess practice (2016), Bài chòi and Hát Xoan (2017), Then practice (2019), Xoè Thái dance (2021) and Chăm pottery (2022) to the Bà Chúa Xứ festival (2024); Hát Xoan of Phú Thọ is the rescue story — in 2017 UNESCO moves it off the urgent-safeguarding list onto the Representative List after ancient songs are collected, more than 30 Xoan clubs founded, and artists over 80 keep performing and teaching the young; the case studies go deep: Nhã nhạc, present from the Lý–Trần age, systematised under the Lê and refined under the Nguyễn (Phạm Quỳnh's awestruck account of a Huế performance is quoted); Quan họ, the antiphonal singing of the Kinh Bắc villages, whose age scholars dispute between the 11th and 17th centuries; and Đờn ca tài tử, born in the late 19th century among the settlers of the southern frontier
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Chuyên đề 2, III.1, Vietnam's intangible heritages and their stories** — the book's timeline runs from Nhã nhạc, Huế court music, in 2003 through the Central Highlands gong culture (2005), Quan họ folk songs and Ca trù (2009), the Gióng festival (2010), Hùng Kings worship (2012), Đờn ca tài tử (2013), Ví Giặm songs (2014), tug-of-war rituals (2015), the Tam phủ Mother Goddess practice (2016), Bài chòi and Hát Xoan (2017), Then practice (2019), Xoè Thái dance (2021) and Chăm pottery (2022) to the Bà Chúa Xứ festival (2024); Hát Xoan of Phú Thọ is the rescue story — in 2017 UNESCO moves it off the urgent-safeguarding list onto the Representative List after ancient songs are collected, more than 30 Xoan clubs founded, and artists over 80 keep performing and teaching the young; the case studies go deep: Nhã nhạc, present from the Lý–Trần age, systematised under the Lê and refined under the Nguyễn (Phạm Quỳnh's awestruck account of a Huế performance is quoted); Quan họ, the antiphonal singing of the Kinh Bắc villages, whose age scholars dispute between the 11th and 17th centuries; and Đờn ca tài tử, born in the late 19th century among the settlers of the southern frontier
2. **Chuyên đề 1 Mother Goddess worship: the agricultural people's goddess cult, layered with Chinese Daoism into the Tam phủ and Tứ phủ systems — Liễu Hạnh and Mẫu Thượng Ngàn in the North, Thiên Y A Na in the Centre, Bà Chúa Xứ in the South — inscribed by UNESCO in 2016 as a Representative Intangible Heritage of humanity** — the rice farmer's mother-goddess ends up on the world's heritage list
