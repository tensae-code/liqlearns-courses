# The early American republic, 1790–1820

## Do it first

The republic survived its birth. Now it had to survive its arguments — and, in 1812, a second war with Britain. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Federalists and Democratic-Republicans fight over the republic's soul

Two visions of the republic collided: Federalists and Democratic-Republicans fought over the republic's soul — how strong the centre should be, and whose republic it would be.

[Picture slot — a picture of the world of this lesson as the book shows it: point to the picture and say what changed in this part of the story.]

## Sub lesson 2 — Parties are born in the fighting

The fighting organised itself. Parties were born in the fighting — lasting camps with names, newspapers and loyalties, arguing the republic's future election after election.

## Sub lesson 3 — 1812 — back to war with Britain, and survival

In 1812 the young nation went back to war with Britain. It was a hard, uneven war — and the republic survived it. Survival itself was the proof: the new nation could take a great power's blow and stand.
