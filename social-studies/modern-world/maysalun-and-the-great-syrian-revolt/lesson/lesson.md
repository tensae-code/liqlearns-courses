# Maysalun and the Great Syrian Revolt

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: At Maysalun on 24 July 1920 Yusuf al-Azma falls defending Damascus — and the revolts his stand begins grow into the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925–1927. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Maysalun — Yusuf al-Azma and the fall of the Arab government

The Syrian Congress rejected the Faisal–Clemenceau agreement and San Remo put Syria under French mandate; General Gouraud's ultimatum of 14 July 1920 demanded the army's dissolution, and though Faisal accepted, France marched; War Minister Yusuf al-Azma led the volunteers to Maysalun and was martyred there on 24 July 1920, and the French entered Damascus and ended the Arab government.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Great Syrian Revolt, 1925–1927

France split Syria into small statelets and looted it, and the revolts answered — Saleh al-Ali on the coast, Ibrahim Hananu in the north, Hawran in 1920, the Golan ambush of Gouraud's convoy in 1921 — until the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925–1927 under Sultan al-Atrash: al-Kafr, the siege of Suwayda, al-Mazraa where the French lost thousands, then the Ghouta and Damascus itself, which France bombarded and burned (the al-Hariqa quarter).

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.
