# Video script — Suriname — El Dorado and the Dutch colony, 1667

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Suriname's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Spaniards chase the golden king El Dorado by Lake Parima and leave the "Wild Coast" disappointed; around 1650 Willoughby's English plant tobacco and sugar and build Fort Willoughby, until Abraham Crijnssen's Zeeland fleet takes Suriname in 1667 and the fort becomes Fort Zeelandia.

Columbus coasts Venezuela on his third voyage but never lands in Suriname; later Spaniards pushing up the Amazon are shot at with poisoned arrows and chase golden legends along the Wild Coast.

In the first half of the 1600s English settlers lay out some 60 plantations shipping tobacco and sugar to England, making Suriname an English plantation colony.

In the Second Anglo-Dutch War Crijnssen takes Suriname (1667); the West India Company rules it, and in 1683 Cornelis van Aerssen van Sommelsdijck becomes governor.
