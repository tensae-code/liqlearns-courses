# Turkey's War of Independence — the fronts

## Do it first

A new state with no regular army must fight on three fronts at once: in the east, in the south where cities defend themselves street by street, and in the west where the decisive war will be fought — and feed that army by levies that reach into every home. Before you read on, guess which front decides the war. Hold your guess; Sakarya and the Great Offensive are coming.

## Sub lesson 1 — East, South and West — İnönü, Sakarya and the Great Offensive

Turkey's Grade 12 book fights the War of Independence front by front. In the east, the front closes with Kars secured. In the south, there is no regular line at all — the cities defend themselves: Maraş, Urfa, Antep and Adana fight as towns, and earn their honour-names doing it.

The decisive war is in the west. The İnönü battles stop the advance; Kütahya-Eskişehir forces a retreat that the nation answers with the Tekâlif-i Milliye levies, taking a share of every home's goods for the army; Sakarya is the turning stand on the river. Then, in August 1922, the Great Offensive — the Commander-in-Chief's battle of 30 August — breaks the front, and a hundred-mile offensive ends the war in a fortnight. The book's beat: three fronts, one regular army built from nothing.

In your mind's eye: the Western Front in 1922 — the regular army advancing across the Anatolian plain in the Great Offensive, dust rising over the columns. The student points to the advance and says which way the war is moving, and why.
