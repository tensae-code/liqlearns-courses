# Lesson plan — The Internationals And May Day

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The Internationals And May Day
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the First International (1864–1876), founded by Marx and Engels, carries scientific socialism into the movement and wins resolutions for strikes, unions and the eight-hour day; on 1 May 1886 some 400,000 Chicago workers strike for that eight-hour day and, though repressed, over 50,000 win it — from 1889 the day is International Labour Day; workers' parties follow (German Social Democrats 1875, French Workers' Party 1879, Russia's Emancipation of Labour group 1883), the Second International is founded in Paris on 14 July 1889, and after Engels' death, as revisionism creeps in, Lenin takes up Marx and Engels' cause
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
