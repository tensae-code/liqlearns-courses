# Syria's struggle for independence

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: From Faisal's Arab government of 1918, through mandate and revolt, to the French evacuation of 17 April 1946 — Syria's long road to independence. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Faisal's Arab government in Damascus, 1918–1920

Faisal's Arab government in Damascus (1918–1920) spread schools, founded the Arab Academy and made Arabic the language of administration; Faisal demanded Arab rights at Paris in 1919; the American King–Crane commission toured Bilad al-Sham, the Syrian General Congress declared Syria's complete independence including Palestine and Lebanon, and the commission recommended independence for Syria and Iraq and no mandate — and its report was shelved until 1922.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Sub lesson 2 — The political struggle to independence, 1928–1946

The 1928 constitution and the 1932 parliament elected Muhammad Ali al-Abid first president; the sixty-day strike forced the 1936 treaty project; in the Second World War the nationalists won the 1943 elections under Shukri al-Quwatli; France bombarded the parliament on 29 May 1945 and Arab and world opinion turned, and the French forces evacuated on 17 April 1946.

In your mind's eye: the place and people of this part of the story. Name two things that changed in the story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Faisal's Arab government in Damascus, 1918–1920; The political struggle to independence, 1928–1946. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
