# Timor-Leste — the referendum of 1999 and the restoration of 2002

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of a country whose whole modern story fits inside one person's lifetime — grandparents who were there at the proclamation, children who learn it from their own family's memory. Hold that thought: this lesson is one chapter of exactly such a story, as Timor-Leste's own Grade 6 book teaches it. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The vote — 30 August 1999

In 1998, political change in Indonesia opens a door that 24 years of resistance had pushed against. Timor-Leste's resistance unites in the CNRT, and on 30 August 1999 the people finally vote. They choose independence by a landslide. The book works this through a class timeline, 1984–1999, so children place the vote at the end of the long years they have studied.

## Sub lesson 2 — Scorched earth, then the flag again — 20 May 2002

The answer to the vote is fire: as Indonesia leaves, scorched-earth destruction burns the country the people have just chosen. But the story the book tells does not end in ashes. On 20 May 2002, independence is restored — the republic proclaimed for nine days in 1975 is raised again, this time to stay.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of this lesson in your own words, keeping its story beat: Political change in Indonesia in 1998 and the founding of the CNRT open the way; the people vote in the referendum of 30 August 1999 and choose independence by a landslide; Indonesia's scorched-earth destruction follows as it leaves; and independence is restored on 20 May 2002 — worked through a class timeline, 1984–1999: after 24 years the people finally vote, watch their country burn for it, and raise their flag again in 2002. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
