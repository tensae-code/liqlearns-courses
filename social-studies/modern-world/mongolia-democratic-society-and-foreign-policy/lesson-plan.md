# Lesson plan — Mongolia — democratic society and foreign policy

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Mongolia — democratic society and foreign policy
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Mongolia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** free speech, free religion and free travel return; monasteries reopen and a new, plural society argues with itself
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ch. 4.6 Changes in Mongols' thinking and social life** — free speech, free religion and free travel return; monasteries reopen and a new, plural society argues with itself
2. **Ch. 4.7 The change in Mongolia's foreign policy** — the "third neighbour" policy reaches beyond Russia and China to the US, Japan, Korea and the EU
3. **Ch. IV.4 Revolutionary change in the sphere of thought** — free press and free faith return; Chinggis is honoured again, the Secret History's 750th anniversary fills Naadam 1990, the repressed are rehabilitated and 10 September becomes their memorial day
4. **Ch. IV.5 Mongolia's foreign relations expand and develop** — the third-neighbour policy, nuclear-weapon-free status, peacekeepers abroad, the 2004 Russian debt settlement, and ties with 180 states
