# Switzerland — Ruedi Hofer, the Verdingkinder and reparation

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Ruedi Hofer was passed to over 30 farm places as a hired-out child labourer and lost half a hand at eight; his lesson asks the question his whole case asks — can such a wrong ever be made good, and what does the state's answer, a "solidarity contribution" of at most 25,000 francs, really say? As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Passed from farm to farm

Ruedi Hofer is passed to over 30 farm places as a child labourer — a Verdingkind, a hired-out child. At eight he loses half a hand in an unexplained explosion. He is exploited and abused, and never gets a steady job. His gift finds another road: he rescues people with his dogs.

## Sub lesson 2 — One child among a hundred thousand

The companion widens the frame: over 100,000 Swiss children are hired out. Grisons children herd seasonally, or walk a week to labour in Upper Swabia as Schwabengänger. The 1955 foster ordinance leaves the seasonal children out. Only the 2017 federal law (AFZFG) names the wrong and pays the solidarity contribution. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Can money undo a childhood?

The source pages let Hofer answer himself: his drawings show the scythe strapped to his ruined hand, and he writes that money cannot undo a childhood. The victims' counsellor explains the state's careful word: its 25,000 francs are a "solidarity contribution", deliberately not called compensation.

## Sub lesson 4 — Initiative against law

At the upper level pupils judge the politics of repair: Guido Fluri's initiative demands a 500-million fund; parliament answers with the 2017 law and a 300-million fund paying at most 25,000 francs. Minister Sommaruga apologises in 2013 — looking away, she says, is also an act — and a 2016 postage stamp carries the remembrance into every post office. (Teacher guide — evidence.)

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of Switzerland — Ruedi Hofer, the Verdingkinder and reparation in your own words, part by part, using the sub lesson headings as your beats. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Tell Hofer's story: over 30 farm places, half a hand lost at eight, no steady job, rescuing people with his dogs.
2. Say how many Swiss children were hired out, and what the 2017 federal law did.
3. Say what Hofer writes about money and childhood, and why the state calls its payment a solidarity contribution, not compensation.
4. Say what the initiative demanded, what parliament's law gave instead, and what Sommaruga's apology said.
