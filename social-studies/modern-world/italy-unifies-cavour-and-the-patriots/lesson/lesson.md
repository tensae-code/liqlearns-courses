# Italy unifies — Cavour and the patriots

## Do it first

Look at the title and the dates. This is a story before it is a list: people act, other people answer, and the map changes. As you read, keep asking — who acted, what did they want, and what changed because of it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Many states, one people

Open the map of Italy around 1850 and it looks like a jigsaw spilled on a table — many smaller states, each with its own ruler. But look at the people inside the pieces: they share a **language**, a **culture**, a **history** and a **religion**. By the nationalism lesson's own test, they are one people living in many states. The question of the age was whether the jigsaw could be locked together into one kingdom.

## Sub lesson 2 — Different patriots, one kingdom — 1861

The unification came in the second half of the 19th century, and it did not come one way — **different patriotic approaches worked together**: diplomacy and war, kings and volunteer armies. At the centre of the design stands **Cavour**, named the **architect of the united Italy** for his central part in the process. Piece by piece the states clicked together — watch them in the GIF — until in **1861 the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed**.

The new kingdom inherited hard problems with its crown: **tension between the industrialising north and the agrarian south** — be careful, the book's exercise corrects the claim that reverses them — and a **weakened papacy**, whose lands the new state had absorbed. One people had their state; governing it fairly was the next story.
