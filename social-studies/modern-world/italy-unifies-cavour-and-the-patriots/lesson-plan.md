# Lesson plan — Italy unifies — Cavour and the patriots

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Italy unifies — Cavour and the patriots
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Development of Capitalism and Nationalism 1815–1914 (Grade 10 History).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Italy, broken into many states whose people shared language, culture, history and religion, was unified in the second half of the 19th century — **Cavour**, the architect, and the patriots working by different approaches together; the **Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the nationalism lesson just before it — Italy is nationalism's first great success story in this unit. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Many states, one people** — Describe Italy before unification: many smaller states, one people sharing language, culture, history and religion.
2. **Different patriots, one kingdom — 1861** — Explain that unification came from different patriotic approaches working together, name Cavour as the architect, date the Kingdom of Italy to 1861, and name the new state's inherited problems: north–south tension and a weakened papacy.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
