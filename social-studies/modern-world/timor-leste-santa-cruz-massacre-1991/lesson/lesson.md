# Timor-Leste — the Santa Cruz massacre, 1991

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of a country whose whole modern story fits inside one person's lifetime — grandparents who were there at the proclamation, children who learn it from their own family's memory. Hold that thought: this lesson is one chapter of exactly such a story, as Timor-Leste's own Grade 6 book teaches it. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — 12 November 1991 — the cemetery shooting

On 12 November 1991, young mourners march to the Santa Cruz cemetery. Soldiers open fire on them there, among the graves. The book names this for what it was: a massacre — the Santa Cruz massacre — and teaches it as a turning point children must know by its date.

In your mind's eye: the key scene of this lesson as the book teaches it. Name the people and the place in the picture and say what happened there.

## Sub lesson 2 — The tape that turned the world's eyes

What makes Santa Cruz different from earlier killings is a camera. Foreign journalists film the shooting, and the tape travels: the occupation can no longer be hidden, and the massacre's impact reaches the whole world. The book also recalls the Kraras massacre alongside it — an earlier massacre the world did not see — so children learn both what happened, and what difference being seen made.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of this lesson in your own words, keeping its story beat: The Santa Cruz massacre of 12 November 1991 and its impact on the world, with the Kraras massacre recalled: young mourners march to a cemetery, soldiers open fire, and foreign journalists film the shooting — one cameraman's tape turns the world's eyes onto Timor, and the occupation can no longer be hidden. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
