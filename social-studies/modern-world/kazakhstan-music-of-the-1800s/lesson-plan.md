**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the kuishi composers turn the dombra into the steppe's orchestra
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **§50 The musical art of Kazakhstan in the 19th century** — the kuishi composers turn the dombra into the steppe's orchestra
2. **§51 Song art of Kazakhstan in the 19th century** — Birzhan-sal and Akan-seri make the singing poet a public figure
3. **§52 The Kazakh people's national musical instruments** — dombra, kobyz and sybyzgy — each instrument with its legend
4. **§6.2 Why is the 19th century considered the flourishing age of the Kazakhs' traditional music art?** — Kurmangazy, Tattimbet and Birzhan-sal make the century a golden age of kui and song
5. **Lesson plans §§51–53: kui art; song art; national instruments** — the dombra kui is played and read as a historical text
