# Lesson plan — Pacific regionalism — the Forum and the USP

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Pacific regionalism — the Forum and the USP
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Samoa, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pacific nations build their own institutions together — the South Pacific Forum for regional politics and policy, and the University of the South Pacific, established in 1968 and jointly owned by twelve island governments with its main campus in Fiji and the School of Agriculture at Alafua in Samoa
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unit 4, Topics 2–3, regional organisations** — Pacific nations build their own institutions together — the South Pacific Forum for regional politics and policy, and the University of the South Pacific, established in 1968 and jointly owned by twelve island governments with its main campus in Fiji and the School of Agriculture at Alafua in Samoa
2. **Part 4, Units 2–3** — shut out of politics at the South Pacific Commission, the new island leaders found the South Pacific Forum at Wellington in 1971 (Sāmoa among the seven founders), build SPEC (1973), the Forum Fisheries Agency and SOPAC, win the 1989 driftnet ban, and crown regional co-operation with the 1985 Rarotonga Treaty making the South Pacific a nuclear free zone
