# Video script — Westward expansion and the Mexican-American War, 1800–1860

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told straight, in the order it happened. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Lewis and Clark crossed a continent. Then Texas broke away, war took half of Mexico — and every new acre asked the question that would break the Union: free soil or slave soil?

Lewis and Clark crossed the continent on their famous expedition — mapping, naming and claiming a West the young republic had only just bought and barely knew. The nation's eyes turned west for good.

Expansion immediately struck the slavery question. Missouri's admission cracked the Union's balance between free and slave states. Further south, Texas broke from Mexico and joined the United States — carrying the same question with it.

The Mexican-American War (1846–1848) took half of Mexico. Victory doubled the question's size: all that new land left the unanswerable question — free soil or slave soil? — sitting at the centre of American politics.
