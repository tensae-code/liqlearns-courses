# Video script — Slavery and the plantation economy of Saint-Domingue

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story as the Haiti guide tells it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Europeans seize the island, clear it and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by africans — a chronology told through local remains

Pupils dig the plantation's traces — mill, boiling house, quarters — and read domination itself out of stones and layouts

Through engravings and texts, the enslaved day is rebuilt — buying, work, food, punishment — until the system's violence is unmistakable

The enslaved appear mostly through masters' pens and pictures — pupils hunt for their real voice and image behind the distortions

Novels from émeric bergeaud's stella to madison smartt bell's trilogy retell saint-domingue — literature becomes a second archive of slavery

Hold that story: Europeans seize the island and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans — a system read through its remains, its work, its voices and its stories until its violence is unmistakable.
