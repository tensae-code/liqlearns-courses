# Slavery and the plantation economy of Saint-Domingue

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: Europeans seize the island and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans — a system read through its remains, its work, its voices and its stories until its violence is unmistakable. As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — From conquest to the plantation economy

Europeans seize the island, clear it and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by africans — a chronology told through local remains (Séance 1, De la conquête et de la colonisation européennes à l'économie de plantation)

In your mind's eye: a Saint-Domingue sugar plantation — mill, boiling house and quarters. Name the three places and says what each one proves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Archaeology of a domination

Pupils dig the plantation's traces — mill, boiling house, quarters — and read domination itself out of stones and layouts (Séance 2, Archéologie d'une domination)

## Sub lesson 3 — Slave work and living conditions (1)

Through engravings and texts, the enslaved day is rebuilt — buying, work, food, punishment — until the system's violence is unmistakable (Séance 3, Travail servile et conditions de vie des esclaves dans les plantations, le Code noir (1))

## Sub lesson 4 — The voice of the enslaved

The enslaved appear mostly through masters' pens and pictures — pupils hunt for their real voice and image behind the distortions (Séance 5, La parole des esclaves, leur image dans la réalité et dans les textes et les images)

## Sub lesson 5 — Slavery in literature

Novels from émeric bergeaud's stella to madison smartt bell's trilogy retell saint-domingue — literature becomes a second archive of slavery (Séance 7, L'impact de l'histoire et de la mémoire des esclaves et des esclavages dans la littérature)
