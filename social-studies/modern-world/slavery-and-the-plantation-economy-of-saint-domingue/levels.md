# Slavery and the plantation economy of Saint-Domingue — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — From conquest to the plantation economy
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Europeans seize the island, clear it and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by africans — a chronology told through local remains (Séance 1, De la conquête et de la colonisation européennes à l'économie de plantation)

## Sub lesson 2 — Archaeology of a domination
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Pupils dig the plantation's traces — mill, boiling house, quarters — and read domination itself out of stones and layouts (Séance 2, Archéologie d'une domination)

## Sub lesson 3 — Slave work and living conditions (1)
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Through engravings and texts, the enslaved day is rebuilt — buying, work, food, punishment — until the system's violence is unmistakable (Séance 3, Travail servile et conditions de vie des esclaves dans les plantations, le Code noir (1))

## Sub lesson 4 — The voice of the enslaved
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: The enslaved appear mostly through masters' pens and pictures — pupils hunt for their real voice and image behind the distortions (Séance 5, La parole des esclaves, leur image dans la réalité et dans les textes et les images)

## Sub lesson 5 — Slavery in literature
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: Novels from émeric bergeaud's stella to madison smartt bell's trilogy retell saint-domingue — literature becomes a second archive of slavery (Séance 7, L'impact de l'histoire et de la mémoire des esclaves et des esclavages dans la littérature)
