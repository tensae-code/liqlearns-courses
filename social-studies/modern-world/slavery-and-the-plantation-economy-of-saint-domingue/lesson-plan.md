# Lesson plan — Slavery and the plantation economy of Saint-Domingue

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Slavery and the plantation economy of Saint-Domingue
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Haiti, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Europeans seize the island and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by enslaved Africans — a system read through its remains, its work, its voices and its stories until its violence is unmistakable.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else. It stands on teacher-guide evidence only (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **From conquest to the plantation economy** — Europeans seize the island, clear it and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by Africans — a chronology told through local remains (Séance 1, De la conquête et de la colonisation européennes à l'économie de plantation)
2. **Archaeology of a domination** — pupils dig the plantation's traces — mill, boiling house, quarters — and read domination itself out of stones and layouts (Séance 2, Archéologie d'une domination)
3. **Slave work and living conditions (1)** — through engravings and texts, the enslaved day is rebuilt — buying, work, food, punishment — until the system's violence is unmistakable (Séance 3, Travail servile et conditions de vie des esclaves dans les plantations, le Code noir (1))
4. **The voice of the enslaved** — the enslaved appear mostly through masters' pens and pictures — pupils hunt for their real voice and image behind the distortions (Séance 5, La parole des esclaves, leur image dans la réalité et dans les textes et les images)
5. **Slavery in literature** — novels from Émeric Bergeaud's Stella to Madison Smartt Bell's trilogy retell Saint-Domingue — literature becomes a second archive of slavery (Séance 7, L'impact de l'histoire et de la mémoire des esclaves et des esclavages dans la littérature)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
