# Slavery and the plantation economy of Saint-Domingue — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Europeans seize the island, clear it and rebuild it as sugar and coffee plantations worked by africans — a chronology told through local remains
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 1, De la conquête et de la colonisation européennes à l'économie de plantation).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Pupils dig the plantation's traces — mill, boiling house, quarters — and read domination itself out of stones and layouts
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 2, Archéologie d'une domination).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Through engravings and texts, the enslaved day is rebuilt — buying, work, food, punishment — until the system's violence is unmistakable
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 3, Travail servile et conditions de vie des esclaves dans les plantations, le Code noir (1)).

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** The enslaved appear mostly through masters' pens and pictures — pupils hunt for their real voice and image behind the distortions
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 5, La parole des esclaves, leur image dans la réalité et dans les textes et les images).

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Novels from émeric bergeaud's stella to madison smartt bell's trilogy retell saint-domingue — literature becomes a second archive of slavery
**Answer: True** — as the guide teaches it (Séance 7, L'impact de l'histoire et de la mémoire des esclaves et des esclavages dans la littérature).
