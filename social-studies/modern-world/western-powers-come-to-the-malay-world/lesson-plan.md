# Lesson plan — Western powers come to the Malay world

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Western powers come to the Malay world
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Malaysia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The West comes to a prosperous Malay world because industrial Europe demands markets and raw materials, the Suez Canal and the steamship shorten the road east, and the Western powers race one another with trade, treaty and force
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why the West came** — the book first shows a prosperous country — strategic ports trading its minerals, fertile soil and sea produce to the world, with a flourishing intellectual life — then the pressure builds: Britain's Industrial Revolution (and by the 1870s Germany's and France's) demands markets and raw materials, the Suez Canal and the steamship shorten the road east, and the Western powers race one another with different strategies of trade, treaty and force (Bab 1 Kedatangan Kuasa Barat: why the West came)
