# Dutch Golden Age — art of a republic of traders

## Do it first

Before you read on, picture the scene this lesson is about, from its title alone: Dutch Golden Age — art of a republic of traders. What do you expect to happen, and what do you expect to change? Hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — One land splits in two

One Low Countries split in two — Catholic Flanders and the Protestant Dutch Republic.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 2 — Flanders paints big, Holland paints its own life

Flanders paints big and sensuous for church and court with Rubens, while the Dutch Republic, rich on trade, buys small pictures of its own homes, ships, landscapes and faces.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rembrandt turns light inward

Rembrandt turns light inward — onto faces and feelings rather than display.

This is a real turn in the story, not a detail at its edge: hold it by its place in the order — what came before it, and what it makes possible next.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as three beats: One Low Countries split in two — Catholic Flanders and the Protestant Dutch Republic. Then: Flanders paints big and sensuous for church and court with Rubens, while the Dutch Republic, rich on trade, buys small pictures of its own homes, ships, landscapes and faces. And finally: Rembrandt turns light inward — onto faces and feelings rather than display. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
