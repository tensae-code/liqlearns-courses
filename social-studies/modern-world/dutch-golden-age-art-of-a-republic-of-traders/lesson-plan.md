# Lesson plan — Dutch Golden Age — art of a republic of traders

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** Dutch Golden Age — art of a republic of traders
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Poland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** One Low Countries split in two — Catholic Flanders paints big and sensuous for church and court with Rubens, while the Protestant Dutch Republic, rich on trade, buys small pictures of its own homes, ships, landscapes and faces, and Rembrandt turns light inward.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **One land splits in two** — One Low Countries split in two — Catholic Flanders and the Protestant Dutch Republic.
2. **Flanders paints big, Holland paints its own life** — Flanders paints big and sensuous for church and court with Rubens, while the Dutch Republic, rich on trade, buys small pictures of its own homes, ships, landscapes and faces.
3. **Rembrandt turns light inward** — Rembrandt turns light inward — onto faces and feelings rather than display.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
