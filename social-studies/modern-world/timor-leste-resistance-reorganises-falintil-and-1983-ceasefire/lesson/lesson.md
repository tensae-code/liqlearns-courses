# Timor-Leste — the resistance reorganises: Falintil and the 1983 ceasefire

## Do it first

Before you read on, think of a country whose whole modern story fits inside one person's lifetime — grandparents who were there at the proclamation, children who learn it from their own family's memory. Hold that thought: this lesson is one chapter of exactly such a story, as Timor-Leste's own Grade 6 book teaches it. Say in your mind what you already know of it; the story will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beaten as an army, rebuilt as guerrillas

Beaten as a conventional army, the resistance does not end — it reorganises. Falintil, its armed force, rebuilds as guerrillas, and the resistance learns to work on three fronts at once, so that no single defeat can finish it.

In your mind's eye: the key scene of this lesson as the book teaches it. Name the people and the place in the picture and say what happened there.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1983 ceasefire — Kontaktu Dame

In 1983 comes a ceasefire — Kontaktu Dame. The book teaches it honestly: even a ceasefire becomes a weapon, a pause each side uses. When it collapses, uprisings follow. The lesson is that a people's resistance survives not only battles, but also the peace that fails.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of this lesson in your own words, keeping its story beat: The resistance reorganises itself — Falintil guerrilla war on three fronts — then comes the 1983 ceasefire, Kontaktu Dame, and the uprisings that followed its collapse: beaten as a conventional army, the resistance rebuilds as guerrillas and a clandestine network, and even a ceasefire becomes a weapon. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.
