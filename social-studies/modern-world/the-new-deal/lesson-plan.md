# Lesson plan — The New Deal, 1932–1941

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The New Deal, 1932–1941
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (United States, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Depression lesson ended with breadlines. This one is the answer: a president who rebuilt banks, jobs and farms — then rebuilt the government's place in American life for good.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the story itself — people, places, what happened, what changed. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The First New Deal stops the fall** — Roosevelt's First New Deal attacked the collapse head-on: banks stabilised, jobs programmes set the unemployed to work, farm programmes steadied agriculture.
2. **The Second New Deal goes further** — The Second New Deal went further: Social Security for old age and unemployment, labour rights for unions, and great works programmes.
3. **The federal government's place is remade for good** — Together the New Deals remade the federal government's place in American life for good.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
