# The New Deal, 1932–1941

## Do it first

The Depression lesson ended with breadlines. This one is the answer: a president who rebuilt banks, jobs and farms — then rebuilt the government's place in American life for good. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The First New Deal stops the fall

Roosevelt's First New Deal attacked the collapse head-on: banks stabilised, jobs programmes set the unemployed to work, farm programmes steadied agriculture. Its first job was to stop the fall — and it did.

[Picture slot — a picture of the world of this lesson as the book shows it: point to the picture and say what changed in this part of the story.]

## Sub lesson 2 — The Second New Deal goes further

The Second New Deal went further: Social Security for old age and unemployment, labour rights for unions, and great works programmes. Relief became structure — protections built into the nation's frame.

## Sub lesson 3 — The federal government's place is remade for good

Together the New Deals remade the federal government's place in American life for good. After Roosevelt, Americans expected Washington to act in a crisis — an expectation every later president inherited.
