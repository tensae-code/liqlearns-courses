# Video script — The New Deal, 1932–1941

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told straight, in the order it happened. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The Depression lesson ended with breadlines. This one is the answer: a president who rebuilt banks, jobs and farms — then rebuilt the government's place in American life for good.

Roosevelt's First New Deal attacked the collapse head-on: banks stabilised, jobs programmes set the unemployed to work, farm programmes steadied agriculture. Its first job was to stop the fall — and it did.

The Second New Deal went further: Social Security for old age and unemployment, labour rights for unions, and great works programmes. Relief became structure — protections built into the nation's frame.

Together the New Deals remade the federal government's place in American life for good. After Roosevelt, Americans expected Washington to act in a crisis — an expectation every later president inherited.
