# Lesson plan — Science Technology And Arts 18th 19th

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Science Technology And Arts 18th 19th
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Vietnam, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Fulton's steamship (1807) and more than 500 steamers in English ports by 1836, the blast furnace and new steel and aluminium, electricity's motors, telephone and radio, the internal combustion engine behind cars and aircraft; literature gives Balzac's Human Comedy, Hugo's Les Misérables (1862, "a hymn to love" for the utterly poor), Tolstoy's War and Peace, Pushkin, Goethe, Dickens; music runs from Bach and Mozart to the romantic storm of Beethoven, Chopin and Tchaikovsky, painting from David and Delacroix to Goya, Levitan and Van Gogh — an art that, the book says, condemns the age's injustices and wakes the poor to fight for a free and happy life
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.
