# Video script — Suriname — resistance: Maroons and uprisings

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Story-style: the story told as Suriname's own books tell it. Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Indigenous fighters (Kaikoesie), enslaved rebels and Maroons resist in turn; the Cottica Maroons under Baron, Boni and Joli Coeur hold swamp Fort Buku until betrayal reveals its underwater path, and Boni fights on until 1792; later contract workers rise at Zoelen, Alliance and Mariënburg under leaders like Ramdjanee.

Resistance runs from stories and songs through sabotage of work to open joint revolt on the plantation and flight to camps in the forest.

Maroon villages deep inland organise well-planned attacks on plantations to free people and seize supplies, while forest patrols hunt the escapees — the Tempati uprising is studied for its consequences.

Peace with the Ndyuka (10 October 1760) and two more Maroon groups follows, but the Boni wars go on around Fort Boekoe and the Cordonpad under Governor Nepveu's counter-measures.

Cheated of the free housing and fair treatment their contracts promised, contract workers resist; in 1902 the great Mariënburg uprising sees a plantation director killed.
