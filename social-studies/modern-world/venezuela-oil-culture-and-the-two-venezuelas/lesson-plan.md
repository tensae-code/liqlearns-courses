# Lesson plan — Venezuela Oil Culture And The Two Venezuelas

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Venezuela Oil Culture And The Two Venezuelas
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Venezuela, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** oil money remakes values and empties the countryside into the cities, leaving two deeply different Venezuelas inside one state-capitalist country
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Oil culture, country-to-city migration and the two Venezuelas** — oil money remakes values and empties the countryside into the cities, leaving two deeply different Venezuelas inside one state-capitalist country
2. **When the oil economy changed customs and even the spatial structure** — oil does not only pay bills; it changes what people eat, want and build, and rearranges the country's very space
3. **Impact of the oil economy on the population** — oil pulls country people to the camps and cities, new social classes appear, and customs change with the new work
