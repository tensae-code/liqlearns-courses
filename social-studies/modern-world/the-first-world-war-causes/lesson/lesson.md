# The First World War — causes

## Do it first

Before you read a word of explanation, test what you already carry: say out loud, in one sentence, what you think this lesson is about — imperialism and nationalism is where it starts. Then read on and check your sentence against the story. History here is told story-first: people, dates and deeds, in the order they happened.

## Sub lesson 1 — Imperialism and nationalism

The book calls the war in character **an imperialists' war**: capitalism's growth in Europe raised political and economic rivalry to its height, organising Europe's states under **two military camps**. Its basic causes begin with two. **(a) Imperialism and colonial clashes** — the European great powers' **intense rivalry to scramble for Africa and Asia**, breeding **conflict, distrust and fear**. The colonies the earlier lessons described — seized in the age of exploration and supplied by industrial power — now set their holders against each other. **(b) Nationalism** — peoples' **patriotic feeling, love and loyalty to country** — a **main source of conflict in 19th-century Europe**: every nation's pride measured itself against its neighbours.

## Sub lesson 2 — Blocs and the arms race

**(c) Bloc-building and the arms race.** Parliaments **debated military budgets** while each state **raced to out-arm the other**, and suspicion drove the states into **military blocs**. On one side, the **Triple Alliance — later the Central Powers — under Germany, with Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Turkey, Italy (which left in 1915) and Bulgaria (which joined in 1915)**. Against it stood the **1907 Triple Entente of Britain, France and Russia**. Learn the camps as they stood at the war's start, with the book's two switches noted: Italy walked out of the Alliance in 1915; Bulgaria walked into the Central Powers the same year. Two armed camps, each fearing the other, each armed to the teeth — the next lesson shows the spark that set them off.
