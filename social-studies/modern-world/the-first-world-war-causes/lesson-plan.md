# Lesson plan — The First World War — causes

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** The First World War — causes
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 13 — The Modern World (CURRICULUM.md path; TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The First World War grew from imperialism and colonial rivalry, from nationalism, and from bloc-building and an arms race that split Europe into two armed camps.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner has followed the early-modern and modern world in this title's order. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or being made — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Imperialism and nationalism** — Rivalry for colonies breeds distrust; love of country turns to a source of conflict.
2. **Blocs and the arms race** — Parliaments vote military budgets, each state out-arms the next, and the two camps harden.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this title, in the title's order — most nearest, the lesson just before this one. Recommended, never forced.
- A learner may jump straight in here: the lesson stands alone, and names are explained where they first appear.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Characterise the war as the book does: an imperialists' war, from capitalism's rivalry organised into two camps.
2. Explain imperialism/colonial clashes and nationalism as causes.
3. List both blocs with members, noting Italy left in 1915 and Bulgaria joined in 1915, and the Entente's 1907 date.
