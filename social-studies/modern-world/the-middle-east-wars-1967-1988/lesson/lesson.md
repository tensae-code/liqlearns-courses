# The Middle East wars, 1967–1988

## Do it first

South of Turkey, the second half of the twentieth century is a drumbeat of wars: Arab against Israeli in round after round, then Iraq against Iran in an eight-year agony — while oil turns the region's quarrels into the world's business. Turkey's book teaches them as the neighbourhood's story. Before you read on, guess what a watching neighbour learns. Hold your guess; Ankara watched all of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Arab-Israeli wars and the Iran-Iraq War

Turkey's Grade 12 book turns, in its seventh unit, to the world of Turkey's southern neighbours. Its story is war in generations: the Arab-Israeli wars, round after round across the decades, and then the Iran-Iraq War, eight years of attrition on Turkey's eastern doorstep.

Through all of it runs oil: the region's wealth under the ground redraws its weight in the world, pulling great powers into every quarrel. The book's beat is the neighbour's view: Turkey's southern neighbours fight three generations of wars while oil redraws their weight in the world — and Turkey, bordering the storm on two sides, must live as its watchful neighbour.

In your mind's eye: the region in the war years — a map-like view of the Middle East with Israel and its Arab neighbours, and the Iraq-Iran front to the east. The student points to Turkey at the map's top and says what it means to watch wars on two of your borders.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as 1 beat: The Arab-Israeli wars and the Iran-Iraq War. Then let your companion ask you one hard question about it — answer from the lesson.

3. Write, from memory: say what redrew the region's weight in the world through these wars: oil.
