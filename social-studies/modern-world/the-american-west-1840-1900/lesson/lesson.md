# The American West, 1840–1900

## Do it first

'Go West' was the slogan. The story is homesteaders and gold, cattle and railroads — and a generation-long assault on American Indian life, from Sand Creek to Wounded Knee. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Homesteaders, gold and cattle build boom economies

Homesteaders chased free land across the plains, farming where rain was uncertain. Gold strikes and the cattle trade built boom economies — towns that exploded overnight and emptied as fast.

[Picture slot — a picture of the world of this lesson as the book shows it: point to the picture and say what changed in this part of the story.]

## Sub lesson 2 — Chinese immigrants and Hispanic citizens pushed to the margins

The West's promise was rationed. Chinese immigrants who built its railroads and Hispanic citizens who had lived there before the flag changed were pushed to the margins — taxed, excluded and attacked.

## Sub lesson 3 — The assault on American Indian life — Sand Creek to Wounded Knee

The United States waged a generation-long assault on American Indian life and culture — wars, broken treaties, forced removals and boarding schools, from Sand Creek to Wounded Knee. The book ends the West's story there: the conquest underneath the boom.
