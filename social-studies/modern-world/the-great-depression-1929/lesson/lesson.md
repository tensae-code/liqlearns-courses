# The Great Depression — 1929 and the world it broke

## Do it first

October 1929. In New York, men who were rich on Monday queue to sell shares for anything by Thursday. Within four years, one worker in four in the world's richest country has no job at all. This lesson follows the crash as it walks around the world.

## Sub lesson 1 — The crash — October 1929

The Great Depression, the worst downturn in the industrialised world's history, began with the New York Wall Street crash of October 1929. Share prices, swollen by speculation, collapsed; fortunes built on borrowed money vanished in days. The panic in the stock market became a collapse in the real economy: banks failed, credit froze, and companies that could not sell could not keep their workers.

The collapse fed on itself in a spiral. Personal income, tax revenue, profits and prices all dropped together. Failing companies laid off workers in steepening spirals of falling output and employment — each layoff cutting the spending that might have kept the next company alive. By 1933 the industrial world was producing and employing at a fraction of its 1929 level.

## Sub lesson 2 — A world broken — trade, jobs, farms

The Depression was worldwide because the industrial world was connected. International trade plunged by more than 50% as countries raised barriers and buyers vanished. In the United States unemployment rose to 25% — one worker in four — and in some countries it reached 33%. Construction halted almost entirely: no one builds houses for families who cannot pay rent.

Farm communities suffered their own version of the fall: crop prices dropped about 60%, so the harvest that had fed a family now bought a third of what it had. City breadlines and ruined farms are the two faces of the same collapse. And misery on this scale does political work: the next lesson shows how it lifted dictators who promised jobs, bread and blame.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the crash in numbers: October 1929; trade down by more than half; one American worker in four jobless; crop prices down about 60%. End with the political consequence.
