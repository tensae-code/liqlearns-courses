# Lesson plan — The Great Depression — 1929 and the world it broke

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The Great Depression — 1929 and the world it broke
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — the interwar unit; the Grade 10 book's economic crisis lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Wall Street crash of October 1929 opened the worst economic collapse of the industrial age: trade halved, a quarter of America's workers lost their jobs, farm prices crashed — and the misery prepared the ground for the dictators.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the industrialisation lessons — only an industrial world could crash in an industrial way. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The crash — October 1929** — Wall Street's collapse and the spiral of falling output and employment that followed to 1933.
2. **A world broken — trade, jobs, farms** — international trade plunges by more than half, unemployment reaches 25% in the US and a third elsewhere, construction halts, crop prices fall about 60%.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Effects of industrialisation — cities, classes, empires. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Date the crash and name where it began.
2. Give the collapse's measures: trade, unemployment, farm prices.
3. Tell the spiral: falling output, layoffs, falling demand.
4. Say why the Depression matters politically — the road to Fascism and Nazism.

## If the learner is stuck
Hold the numbers as four pegs: crash October 1929; world trade down by more than half; US unemployment 25%; crop prices down about 60%. The political peg: misery made dictators' promises sound like rescue.
