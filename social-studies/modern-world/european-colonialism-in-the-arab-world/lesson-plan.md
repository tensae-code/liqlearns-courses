# Lesson plan — European colonialism in the Arab world

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** European colonialism in the Arab world
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Jordan, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pope Alexander VI splits the world between Spain and Portugal in 1493; the Arab navigator Ibn Majid, born at Ras al-Khaimah in 1432, pilots Vasco da Gama to India in 1498 and the Portuguese seize the Arabian coasts; Napoleon storms Egypt and exposes Ottoman weakness; foreign capitulations and Orientalist scholarship pry the empire open; Britain garrisons Kuwait in 1793 while the Qawasim fight her in the Gulf; Dey Hussein's fly-whisk insult hands France her excuse, and Algiers falls in 1830
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lesson 2: colonialism in the Arab homeland before the First World War** — Pope Alexander VI splits the world between Spain and Portugal in 1493; the Arab navigator Ibn Majid, born at Ras al-Khaimah in 1432, pilots Vasco da Gama to India in 1498 and the Portuguese seize the Arabian coasts; Napoleon storms Egypt and exposes Ottoman weakness; foreign capitulations and Orientalist scholarship pry the empire open; Britain garrisons Kuwait in 1793 while the Qawasim fight her in the Gulf; Dey Hussein's fly-whisk insult hands France her excuse, and Algiers falls in 1830
2. **Lesson 3: colonialism in the Arab homeland after the First World War** — France works to erase Arabic in the Maghreb, seizes the awqaf and fans Arab against Berber; Italy whispers "peaceful penetration" over Libya; in the East, Britain issues the Balfour Declaration on 2 November 1917 promising a Jewish national home in a Palestine it does not own; Damascus answers with the Syrian General Congress of March 1920 crowning Faysal king of a united Syria — and San Remo tears the country into mandates, France splitting Syria into statelets and handing Alexandretta to Turkey in 1939
