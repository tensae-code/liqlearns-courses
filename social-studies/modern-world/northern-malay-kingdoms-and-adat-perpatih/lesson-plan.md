# Lesson plan — The northern Malay kingdoms and Adat Perpatih

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** Modern World · **Lesson:** The northern Malay kingdoms and Adat Perpatih
**Place in the curriculum:** Modern World — built in the History expansion (Malaysia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The northern kingdoms — Kedah, Kelantan, Perlis — keep a living heritage of poems, kites, batik and wayang kulit, while Negeri Sembilan orders itself by Adat Perpatih, the matrilineal custom where descent and inheritance run through the mother
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kedah, Kelantan and Perlis and their heritage** — the northern kingdoms deal with their Malay neighbours and leave a living heritage — Kedah's war poem Syair Sultan Maulana from the reign of Sultan Ahmad Tajuddin Halim Shah II (1804–1845), the Hikayat Seri Kelantan (1914), Kelantan's wau kites and batik, and wayang kulit raised to national heritage (Bab 8–9.1: Kedah, Kelantan and Perlis and their heritage)
2. **Negeri Sembilan's Adat Perpatih** — Minangkabau migrants settle Rembau, Sungai Ujong, Johol and Naning bringing Adat Perpatih, the matrilineal custom: descent and inheritance run through the mother, a husband joins his wife's suku as orang semenda, and the people — Perut, Suku and Luak — appoint their adat chiefs (Ibu Soko, Buapak, Lembaga), whose four Undang elect the Yamtuan Besar (Bab 9.2: Negeri Sembilan's Adat Perpatih)
