**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Mongolia after the Empire — fragmentation
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Mongolia, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** after the empire breaks, khans rise and fall in quick succession and the aimags pull apart while Ming China presses from the south
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Part II, Ch. I.1: Mongolia in the 15th–18th centuries** — after the empire breaks, khans rise and fall in quick succession and the aimags pull apart while Ming China presses from the south
2. **Ch. I.2: The struggle to restore united power** — the seven-year-old Dayan Khan is raised on Chinggis's throne before the Eight White Gers and reunites the Mongols; a century later Altan Khan founds Khökh Khot and in 1577 receives the title Dalai Lama from Sonam Gyatso
3. **Ch. I.4: Mongolia's foreign environment in the 15th–18th c.** — a divided Mongolia is squeezed from three sides — Ming China, the rising Manchu, and Russia pushing across Siberia to Lake Baikal
4. **Ch. 4.1 Mongolia centred on its homeland** — after Yuan falls in 1368 the khans return to the steppe; the Northern Yuan fights Ming and itself in turn
5. **Ch. 4.2 The Mongolian khanate states** — Dayan Khan's reunification and his sons' appanages — Khalkha, Chakhar, Ordos, Tümed — set the map the Manchu will conquer piece by piece
