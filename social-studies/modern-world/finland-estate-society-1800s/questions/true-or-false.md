# Finland — estate society in the 1800s — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** 1800s Finland sorts everyone into ranked estates, and your birth decides your work, dress and prospects before you can walk.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Sääty-yhteiskunta 1800-luvun Suomessa (the estate society: into what groups were people divided, and how did each group live?)

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *Suomalainen sääty-yhteiskunta (History deck, FI/SV)* is about Johdanto: the Jahvetin kirjelaatikko programme and its archive.
**Answer: False** — it is about Sääty-yhteiskunta 1800-luvun Suomessa (the estate society: into what groups were people divided, and how did each group live?)

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Pupils check name registers and old-name lists to learn what a child of their estate would actually have been called.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Etu- ja sukunimet 1800-luvun Suomessa (first names and surnames; did everyone even have a surname?)

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *Suomalainen sääty-yhteiskunta (History deck, FI/SV)* is about Jahvetin kirjelaatikko (the programme, 1940–1945).
**Answer: False** — it is about Etu- ja sukunimet 1800-luvun Suomessa (first names and surnames; did everyone even have a surname?)

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Each pair or small group draws one rung of the ladder, from noble to landless lodger, and must learn to live on it.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Ryhmäjako (the seven groups: aateliset nobles, papit clergy, porvarit burghers, talonpojat peasants, torpparit crofters, piika ja renki maid and farmhand, loiset the landless lodgers)

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** This part of *Suomalainen sääty-yhteiskunta (History deck, FI/SV)* is about Opettajalle: the four theme packages and how the letter tasks are built.
**Answer: False** — it is about Ryhmäjako (the seven groups: aateliset nobles, papit clergy, porvarit burghers, talonpojat peasants, torpparit crofters, piika ja renki maid and farmhand, loiset the landless lodgers)

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Armed with the estate and name research, each pupil invents one believable 1800s Finn and writes their plan.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Oman roolihahmon luominen (creating your own 1800s role character: name, age, family, home place, work — or a child's life)

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** This part of *Suomalainen sääty-yhteiskunta (History deck, FI/SV)* is about Teema 2 background: 420,000 people leave home.
**Answer: False** — it is about Oman roolihahmon luominen (creating your own 1800s role character: name, age, family, home place, work — or a child's life)

**9. (Sub lesson 5)** Partners question each other in role — name, family, home, work — and the 1800s world has to hold up under questioning.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Haastattelu (interviewing each other's role characters)

**10. (Sub lesson 5)** This part of *Suomalainen sääty-yhteiskunta (Mother-tongue deck, FI/SV)* is about Tehtävä 1 (yläkoulu): letter from Säkylä, 26 Feb 1941.
**Answer: False** — it is about Haastattelu (interviewing each other's role characters)

**11. (Sub lesson 6)** Each pupil writes home-news from inside the 1800s — mood, recent life, worries and one piece of good news — signs with the character's name, and reads the partner's letter to see another estate's life.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Kirje roolihahmon äänellä (writing, sending and reading a letter in your character's voice; model letter headed Mäntyharju, 5 Oct 1889)

**12. (Sub lesson 6)** This part of *Suomalainen sääty-yhteiskunta (Mother-tongue deck, FI/SV)* is about Tehtävä 3 (yläkoulu): letter from Suonenjoki, 20 Sep 1941.
**Answer: False** — it is about Kirje roolihahmon äänellä (writing, sending and reading a letter in your character's voice; model letter headed Mäntyharju, 5 Oct 1889)
