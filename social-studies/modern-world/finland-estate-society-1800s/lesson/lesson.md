# Finland — estate society in the 1800s

## Do it first

Before you read on, hold this lesson's one thing in mind: 1800s Finland sorts everyone into ranked estates, and your birth decides your work, dress and prospects before you can walk As you read, keep asking where each part of that sentence is proved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sääty-yhteiskunta 1800-luvun Suomessa (the estate society: into what groups were people divided, and how did each group live?)

1800s Finland sorts everyone into ranked estates, and your birth decides your work, dress and prospects before you can walk.

[Picture slot — a picture of the place and people of this part of the story — the student names two things that changed in the story]

## Sub lesson 2 — Etu- ja sukunimet 1800-luvun Suomessa (first names and surnames; did everyone even have a surname?)

Pupils check name registers and old-name lists to learn what a child of their estate would actually have been called.

[Picture slot — a picture of the place and people of this part of the story — the student names two things that changed in the story]

## Sub lesson 3 — Ryhmäjako (the seven groups: aateliset nobles, papit clergy, porvarit burghers, talonpojat peasants, torpparit crofters, piika ja renki maid and farmhand, loiset the landless lodgers)

Each pair or small group draws one rung of the ladder, from noble to landless lodger, and must learn to live on it.

[Picture slot — a picture of the place and people of this part of the story — the student names two things that changed in the story]

## Sub lesson 4 — Oman roolihahmon luominen (creating your own 1800s role character: name, age, family, home place, work — or a child's life)

Armed with the estate and name research, each pupil invents one believable 1800s Finn and writes their plan.

[Picture slot — a picture of the place and people of this part of the story — the student names two things that changed in the story]

## Sub lesson 5 — Haastattelu (interviewing each other's role characters)

Partners question each other in role — name, family, home, work — and the 1800s world has to hold up under questioning.

[Picture slot — a picture of the place and people of this part of the story — the student names two things that changed in the story]

## Sub lesson 6 — Kirje roolihahmon äänellä (writing, sending and reading a letter in your character's voice; model letter headed Mäntyharju, 5 Oct 1889)

Each pupil writes home-news from inside the 1800s — mood, recent life, worries and one piece of good news — signs with the character's name, and reads the partner's letter to see another estate's life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story as its beats, in order: Sääty-yhteiskunta 1800-luvun Suomessa (the estate society: into what groups were people divided, and how did each group live?); Etu- ja sukunimet 1800-luvun Suomessa (first names and surnames; did everyone even have a surname?); Ryhmäjako (the seven groups: aateliset nobles, papit clergy, porvarit burghers, talonpojat peasants, torpparit crofters, piika ja renki maid and farmhand, loiset the landless lodgers); Oman roolihahmon luominen (creating your own 1800s role character: name, age, family, home place, work — or a child's life); Haastattelu (interviewing each other's role characters); Kirje roolihahmon äänellä (writing, sending and reading a letter in your character's voice; model letter headed Mäntyharju, 5 Oct 1889).
