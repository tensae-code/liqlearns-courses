# Lesson plan — Finland — estate society in the 1800s

**Course:** Social Studies · **Title:** The Modern World · **Lesson:** Finland — estate society in the 1800s
**Place in the curriculum:** The Modern World — built in the History expansion (Finland, 2026-11-15).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** 1800s Finland sorts everyone into ranked estates, and your birth decides your work, dress and prospects before you can walk
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own story, told in full inside the lesson. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. History is taught as story: people, places, what happened, what changed.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sääty-yhteiskunta 1800-luvun Suomessa (the estate society: into what groups were people divided, and how did each group live?)** — 1800s Finland sorts everyone into ranked estates, and your birth decides your work, dress and prospects before you can walk
2. **Etu- ja sukunimet 1800-luvun Suomessa (first names and surnames; did everyone even have a surname?)** — pupils check name registers and old-name lists to learn what a child of their estate would actually have been called
3. **Ryhmäjako (the seven groups: aateliset nobles, papit clergy, porvarit burghers, talonpojat peasants, torpparit crofters, piika ja renki maid and farmhand, loiset the landless lodgers)** — each pair or small group draws one rung of the ladder, from noble to landless lodger, and must learn to live on it
4. **Oman roolihahmon luominen (creating your own 1800s role character: name, age, family, home place, work — or a child's life)** — armed with the estate and name research, each pupil invents one believable 1800s Finn and writes their plan
5. **Haastattelu (interviewing each other's role characters)** — partners question each other in role — name, family, home, work — and the 1800s world has to hold up under questioning
6. **Kirje roolihahmon äänellä (writing, sending and reading a letter in your character's voice; model letter headed Mäntyharju, 5 Oct 1889)** — each pupil writes home-news from inside the 1800s — mood, recent life, worries and one piece of good news — signs with the character's name, and reads the partner's letter to see another estate's life

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
