# Crime and punishment since 1900 — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — 4.1 Crime and definitions of crime, c1900–present
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Changing attitudes, above all in the 1960s, decriminalise acts such as homosexuality (1967) while new technology keeps creating new crimes

## Sub lesson 2 — 4.2 Law enforcement, c1900–present
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — 200 separate local forces with no shared records give way to fingerprint branches (1901), the 999 number, forensic science and community policing

## Sub lesson 3 — 4.3 Changes in punishment, c1900–present
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Execution shrinks to murder and treason, separate treatment for young offenders begins (Children's Act 1908), and punishment turns toward rehabilitation on the road to abolition

## Sub lesson 4 — 4.4 Case study: conscientious objectors in the First and Second World Wars
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Conscription (1916) makes refusing to fight a crime for the first time; objectors are sorted, imprisoned or given front-line non-combat work, and treated differently again in 1939–45

## Sub lesson 5 — 4.5 Case study: the Derek Bentley case and the abolition of capital punishment
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Derek Bentley is hanged despite doubts and a plea from 200 MPs; the scandal drives the 1957 Homicide Act's diminished-responsibility defence and the abolition campaign
