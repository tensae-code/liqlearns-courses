# Effects of the geographical discoveries on the Arab homeland — true or false

Say True or False, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** the Arabs were there first — Yemeni ships crossing to India, China and East Africa a thousand years before Christ, Egyptian voyages to Punt, Hanno of Carthage rounding Africa, the Abbasid travellers Ibn Fadlan, al-Masudi and Ibn Battuta, and the Yemeni masters Ibn Majid and Sulayman al-Mahri, whom this book credits with guiding the Portuguese themselves — yet the discoveries turn the world's trade away from the Arab seas to the Atlantic, drain the Arab ports of their revenues, strangle the Mamluks until they fall in 1517, and bring Portuguese guns blockading Aden, al-Shihr and the Gulf.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Effects of the geographical discoveries on the Arab homeland

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** This part of *تاريخ العرب الحديث والمعاصر للصف التاسع من مرحلة التعليم الأساسي — الجزء الأول (Modern and Contemporary History of the Arabs for Grade 9, Basic Education — Part 1)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Effects of the geographical discoveries on the Arab homeland

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** the book first credits Arab sailors of al-Andalus with crossing the Atlantic before Spain and Portugal used their maps; then da Gama reaches Calicut in 1498, and at Diu in 1509 the Mamluk fleet sent to defend Arab trade is betrayed by Diu's own ruler — the signal that the Indian Ocean, and thirty million enslaved Africans' homelands, pass under Western hegemony.
**Answer: True** — the book tells this in Effects of the European geographical discoveries (Unit 1 Lesson 2)

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** This part of *تاريخ العالم الحديث والمعاصر — تاريخ للصف الثالث الثانوي (History: Modern and Contemporary History of the World, for the Third Secondary Grade)* is about a different part of this story, told in another lesson of this expansion.
**Answer: False** — it is about Effects of the European geographical discoveries (Unit 1 Lesson 2)
