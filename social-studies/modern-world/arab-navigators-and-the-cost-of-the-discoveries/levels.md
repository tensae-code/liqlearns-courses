# Effects of the geographical discoveries on the Arab homeland — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — Effects of the geographical discoveries on the Arab homeland
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — the Arabs were there first — Yemeni ships crossing to India, China and East Africa a thousand years before Christ, Egyptian voyages to Punt, Hanno of Carthage rounding Africa, the Abbasid travellers Ibn Fadlan, al-Masudi and Ibn Battuta, and the Yemeni masters Ibn Majid and Sulayman al-Mahri, whom this book credits with guiding the Portuguese themselves — yet the discoveries turn the world's trade away from the Arab seas to the Atlantic, drain the Arab ports of their revenues, strangle the Mamluks until they fall in 1517, and bring Portuguese guns blockading Aden, al-Shihr and the Gulf
