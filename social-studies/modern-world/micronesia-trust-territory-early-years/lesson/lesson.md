# Micronesia — the Trust Territory's early years

## Do it first

The war ends — and a new foreign ruler arrives promising trust, not colony. Before you read on, decide: what would you watch to tell the difference between a trustee and a ruler? Hold your answer; the municipalities will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The US Navy rebuilds — and the Trust Territory begins, 1947

*Over Seas and Time* calls the chapter Early Years of American Occupation. The US Navy rebuilt the war-torn islands, and repatriation — people sent home from wartime postings — reshaped villages across Micronesia.

In 1947 the arrangement was given a legal name: the United Nations Trust Territory, thirty more years of foreign rule — but a new kind, with municipalities, magistrates and district congresses giving islanders elected local office.

[Picture slot — a picture of a small island council house in the 1950s, with a magistrate, chiefs and villagers meeting under an American flag and a UN notice on the wall — the student points to the chiefs and the council seats and says what double demand the meeting had to meet.]

## Sub lesson 2 — From municipalities to the Congress of Micronesia

The Grade 11 book follows the islanders' side of those years. In the 1940s–60s, islanders built municipalities and district congresses — new democratic forms that had to do two things at once: respect traditional leadership, and satisfy American demands for democracy.

Out of those district congresses grew the Congress of Micronesia, the first body speaking for all the islands together — the seed of self-government.

## Sub lesson 3 — Kapingamarangi: the beginning of the American time

The Kapingamarangi reader names the era plainly: The Beginning of the American Time. After Japan's defeat the Americans — Baalangi — took charge, and the atoll entered the Trust Territory world of municipalities and elected councils.

On a small atoll, world arrangements arrive as small things: a council, a magistrate, a vote. The reader records them in that order — the island's scale first.
