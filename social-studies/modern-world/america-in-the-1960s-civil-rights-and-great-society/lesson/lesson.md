# America in the 1960s — civil rights and the Great Society

## Do it first

Sit-ins at lunch counters, freedom rides, Birmingham, a march on Washington — and a president's Great Society. The 1960s contested America's future on every front at once. Before you read on, say in your mind what you already think happened — hold your ideas; the story will test them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kennedy's promise and Johnson's Great Society

Kennedy's promise opened the decade — a young president calling the nation forward. Johnson answered with the Great Society: a programme to end poverty and racial injustice and rebuild American life through federal power.

[Picture slot — a picture of the world of this lesson as the book shows it: point to the picture and say what changed in this part of the story.]

## Sub lesson 2 — The civil rights movement marches on

The civil rights movement marched on: sit-ins, freedom rides, Birmingham, the March on Washington. Ordinary people, many of them young, put their bodies where the Constitution's promises were denied — and forced the nation to look.

## Sub lesson 3 — A wider generation challenges the status quo

Beyond civil rights, a wider generation challenged the status quo on every front — war, women's place, the universities, the culture itself. The book's title names the decade: contesting futures — rival Americas arguing over which one would win.
