# The Pakistan Movement — from Congress to the Muslim League — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its sub lessons. Practice is tied to the learner's skill number. The questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view. Parts are ordered as the book teaches them, as stocked.

## Sub lesson 1 — The provinces create Pakistan
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — After the failed war of 1857 the Muslims of India organise; the Lahore Resolution of 23 March 1940 demands a separate homeland; and each province plays its part — Punjab passes the resolution's spirit at the Minar-e-Pakistan's ground, Sindh, the land where Islam first entered with Muhammad bin Qasim in 712 and which the book calls Bab-ul-Islam, becomes the first province to vote for Pakistan in 1943, Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa join by their own decisions and referendum, until 14 August 1947

## Sub lesson 2 — Minorities share in the making
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — The new country is not built by Muslims alone — minority citizens, from the Christians, Hindus, Sikhs and Parsis who chose Pakistan to servants of the sick like Dr Ruth Pfau, share in its making, and the chapter closes with the heroes decorated with the Nishan-e-Haider

## Sub lesson 3 — The organisers and the name-giver
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — Begum Rana Liaquat Ali Khan builds the women's movement and founds APWA in 1949; Begum Jahan Ara Shahnawaz organises the Punjab women's Muslim League from 1937; Chaudhry Rahmat Ali, a student at Cambridge, coins the very name "Pakistan" in his 1933 pamphlet Now or Never; and Sir Abdullah Haroon carries the demand in Sindh, whose assembly votes to join Pakistan on 26 June 1947

## Sub lesson 4 — The provinces complete the map
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: retell this part in your own words — The map is completed piece by piece — Punjab, the movement's centre under leaders like Sir Mian Muhammad Shafi, is divided by the 3 June 1947 plan; Khyber Pakhtunkhwa votes in its referendum; Balochistan's Shahi Jirga and Quetta municipality decide for Pakistan; Mirza Hasan Khan's Gilgit scouts raise Pakistan's flag in Gilgit-Baltistan in November 1947; and Sardar Ibrahim's Azad Jammu and Kashmir joins its future to Pakistan
